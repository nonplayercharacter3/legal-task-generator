## Issue 1 – Non-Competition Covenant Is Far Broader Than the LOI and Would Gut Seller's Retained Business

**Where:** SPA § 6.4; compare LOI ¶ 8; KEA § 1.1 (definition of "Competing Business").

**Explanation:**

The LOI agreed to a three-year non-compete limited to "the development or publishing of tactical role-playing games for console and PC only," with express carve-outs for (i) the Bikas Pocket mobile casual game publishing business, (ii) Seller's continued ownership, development and licensing of the Cinderloom engine, and (iii) passive ownership of less than 5% of publicly traded securities.

The SPA replaces all of this with a prohibition on "own[ing], manag[ing], operat[ing] or engag[ing] in any Competing Business anywhere in the world," where "Competing Business" is defined by cross-reference to KEA § 1.1 as "the development, publishing or distribution of any video game in any genre, on any platform." There is no carve-out for Bikas Pocket. There is no carve-out for the Cinderloom engine. The only exception is the 5% passive ownership threshold.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- Seller retains the Cinderloom engine and licenses it to the Company at Closing (SPA Recital C; § 6.6). Under the SPA's non-compete, Seller could not develop, improve, or license the Cinderloom engine to any other customer for three years. This would destroy the economic rationale for retaining the engine.
- Seller retains the Bikas Pocket mobile casual game publishing business (SPA Recital D). Under the SPA's non-compete, Seller could not develop, publish, or distribute *any* video game on *any* platform, including mobile casual titles. This would force Seller to shut down or sell Bikas Pocket.
- The LOI is the agreed commercial framework. The SPA's departure is not a drafting error; it is a substantive renegotiation of a core economic term.

**Recommendation:**

Propose to conform § 6.4 to the LOI: limit the restricted activity to "development or publishing of tactical role-playing games for console and PC," and add express carve-outs for (a) the Seller Retained Business (Bikas Pocket), (b) Seller's ownership, development, improvement and licensing of the Cinderloom engine (including to the Company under the Retained Engine License and to third parties), and (c) passive ownership of less than 5% of publicly traded securities. Remove the cross-reference to the KEA definition.

## Issue 2 – Uncapped, Perpetual IP Indemnity

**Where:** SPA § 9.2(d).

**Explanation:**

Section 9.2(d) requires Seller to indemnify Buyer against "any third-party claim alleging that any Company IP, or the development, publishing or exploitation of any game title of the Company, infringes, misappropriates or otherwise violates any Intellectual Property of any Person, whether known or unknown, and whether arising before or after the Closing." The provision expressly states that Seller's obligations under this clause "apply without regard to the Cap, the Basket or the survival periods in Section 9.1."

The LOI contemplated only a standard 18-month survival with a 10% cap for Seller's general indemnification (LOI ¶ 5). It did not provide for a separate, uncapped, perpetual IP indemnity.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- The indemnity is uncapped and has no survival limit. Seller could face unlimited liability indefinitely for any IP claim, including claims that arise years after Closing from post-closing development decisions made by Buyer.
- The Company's games use the Cinderloom engine (licensed from Seller) and the Kestrel Motion toolkit (licensed from Vasquez-Ilunga). If a third party asserts that the engine or toolkit infringes its IP, Seller would be on the hook under § 9.2(d) even though Seller does not control the Company's use of the tools post-Closing.
- "Whether arising before or after the Closing" means Buyer could make changes to the games post-Closing that create new infringement exposure, and Seller would still indemnify.

**Recommendation:**

- Delete § 9.2(d) as a standalone indemnity. IP infringement claims should be addressed through the representation in § 4.8, subject to the standard cap, basket, and 18-month survival period.
- If Buyer insists on a separate IP indemnity, limit it to (a) claims based on facts existing prior to Closing, (b) a cap no greater than the Base Purchase Price, and (c) a survival period no longer than the applicable statute of limitations.
- In no event should the IP indemnity cover claims arising from post-Closing modifications, new titles, or new uses of the Cinderloom engine or Kestrel Motion that are directed by Buyer.

## Issue 3 – Buyer's Indemnification Cap Omits Carve-Outs Agreed in the LOI

**Where:** SPA § 9.5; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Excluded Buyer Obligations"); compare LOI ¶ 6.

**Explanation:**

The LOI provided that Buyer's liability cap would be the same as Seller's ($8.4M) but that three categories were excluded from the cap: (i) fraud, (ii) Earnout payment obligations, and (iii) breaches of the Retained Engine License.

The SPA defines "Excluded Buyer Obligations" (used in § 9.5(b)) as only "any Losses arising from fraud by Buyer." Earnout payment obligations and breaches of the Retained Engine License (governed by § 6.7) are not excluded from the cap.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- The Maximum Earnout Amount is $30M. If Buyer wrongfully withholds or disputes an earnout payment, Seller's recovery under the indemnity would be capped at $8.4M (less the $840K basket). Seller would have no uncapped remedy for non-payment of the earnout.
- If Buyer or the Company breaches the Cinderloom engine restrictions in § 6.7 (e.g., reverse-engineers the engine, sublicenses it, or uses it to build a competing engine), Seller's indemnification recovery would be capped at $8.4M. Given that the Cinderloom engine is Seller's retained asset and a core part of its ongoing business, the damages from such a breach could far exceed $8.4M.

**Recommendation:**

Amend the definition of "Excluded Buyer Obligations" in § 1.1 to include: (a) fraud by Buyer; (b) Buyer's obligations under § 2.5 (Earnout); and (c) Buyer's and the Company's obligations under § 6.7 (Restrictions on Use of Cinderloom Engine). Conforming changes to § 9.5(b) will follow.

## Issue 4 – Kestrel Motion License Terminates Automatically on Change of Control; SPA Does Not Address This

**Where:** KEA § 7.3(d); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control" in KEA § 1.1); SPA § 4.8; SPA § 7 (Conditions to Closing); SPA Schedule 4.10, item 1.

**Explanation:**

KEA § 7.3(d) provides: "The Kestrel Motion License will terminate automatically upon a Change of Control of the Company, unless before the Change of Control Executive has consented in writing to the continuation of the Kestrel Motion License following that Change of Control."

The sale of 100% of the Company's shares to Douvend is unambiguously a "Change of Control" under KEA § 1.1(a) (sale of a majority of outstanding equity securities). Therefore, at Closing, the Kestrel Motion license will terminate automatically unless Vasquez-Ilunga has provided written consent beforehand.

The SPA does not:
- Make Vasquez-Ilunga's consent a condition to Closing (Article 7 is silent);
- Include a covenant requiring Buyer or Seller to obtain such consent before Closing;
- Address the consequence of the license terminating (the Company would lose its right to use Kestrel Motion in all Company Games, including Ashen Vanguard);
- Reflect the consent requirement in Schedule 4.4 (which states "None").

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- If the Kestrel Motion license terminates at Closing, the Company's games lose a core animation technology. This directly impairs the value of the asset Buyer is purchasing and could reduce Ashen Vanguard's revenue during the Earnout Period, which would reduce Seller's earnout payments.
- Seller's representation in § 4.8 that "the Company owns, or has a valid right to use, all Intellectual Property used in the conduct of the Business" will be false at Closing if the Kestrel Motion license has terminated. This could trigger a post-closing indemnification claim by Buyer against Seller (subject to the cap), creating an unnecessary dispute.
- Seller's representation in § 4.4 (No Conflicts; Consents) is qualified by Schedule 4.4, which says "None." If Vasquez-Ilunga's consent is required to maintain the Kestrel Motion license, the schedule is incomplete.

**Recommendation:**

- Add a condition to Closing in Article 7 (either as a condition to both parties or to Buyer) that Vasquez-Ilunga has delivered written consent under KEA § 7.3(d) to the continuation of the Kestrel Motion License following the Change of Control.
- Alternatively, add a covenant requiring Seller to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain such consent before Closing, and a covenant requiring Buyer to cooperate.
- Update Schedule 4.4 to disclose that Vasquez-Ilunga's consent is required under the KEA for the Kestrel Motion License to survive the transaction.
- **Action needed from client:** Confirm whether Vasquez-Ilunga has been approached about the consent and whether he is amenable to continuing in his role post-Closing. The LOI (¶ 7) states his continued involvement is "important to the Transaction," and the email thread (Message 2, point 4) confirms Douvend expects him to remain as Studio Head. We should ask the client whether there is any indication he might not consent.

## Issue 5 – No Covenant or Condition Requiring Buyer to Retain Vasquez-Ilunga

**Where:** SPA (absent); LOI ¶ 7; KEA §§ 5.3, 7.3(d).

**Explanation:**

The LOI states that "Buyer considers the continued involvement of the Company's studio head, Teodor Vasquez-Ilunga, important to the Transaction" and contemplates that Buyer will "discuss with Mr. Vasquez-Ilunga the terms of his continued role with the Company following Closing." The SPA contains no covenant requiring Buyer to retain Vasquez-Ilunga, no condition to Closing based on his continued employment, and no requirement that Buyer negotiate in good faith with him.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- If Buyer terminates Vasquez-Ilunga immediately after Closing (other than for Cause), he would be entitled to 12 months' severance under KEA § 5.5, but the Kestrel Motion license would still terminate under § 7.3(d) (the change of control has already occurred). The Company would lose both its Studio Head and its animation technology.
- Without his continued involvement, the quality and revenue trajectory of Ashen Vanguard could suffer, directly impacting the earnout.
- Seller has no contractual lever to prevent this.

**Recommendation:**

- Add a covenant requiring Buyer to use commercially reasonable efforts to retain Vasquez-Ilunga for a specified period post-Closing (e.g., 12–18 months), or at minimum to negotiate in good faith with him regarding his continued role.
- Alternatively, make his written consent to the Kestrel Motion License continuation (see Issue 4) a condition to Closing, which effectively requires Buyer to secure his cooperation before it can close.
- **Action needed from client:** Ask whether the client wants to push for a retention covenant or whether the Kestrel Motion consent condition is sufficient leverage. Also ask whether the client has any direct relationship with Vasquez-Ilunga that could facilitate the consent.

## Issue 6 – Earnout Acceleration Does Not Cover a Stock Sale of the Company

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(f).

**Explanation:**

Section 2.5(f) accelerates the unpaid earnout if "Buyer sells all or substantially all of the assets of the Company relating to Ashen Vanguard." It does not trigger if Buyer sells the Company's stock to a third party. In a stock sale, the earnout obligation remains with Buyer (the contractual counterparty), but the new owner of the Company would control Ashen Vanguard's operations and could suppress revenue without triggering acceleration.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- If Buyer sells the Company to a competitor or a passive investor who has no interest in growing Ashen Vanguard, the new owner could reduce marketing spend, delay updates, or otherwise depress revenue during the Earnout Period. Seller would have no acceleration right and would have to rely on the good-faith covenant in § 2.5(e)(i), which is difficult to enforce.

**Recommendation:**

Amend § 2.5(f) to add: "or if Buyer sells, transfers or otherwise disposes of all or a majority of the outstanding equity securities of the Company to any Person that does not assume Buyer's obligations under this Section 2.5 in writing."

## Issue 7 – "Working Capital" Is Not Defined

**Where:** SPA §§ 2.3, 2.4.

**Explanation:**

The SPA references "Closing Working Capital" and "Target Working Capital" ($3,250,000) but never defines the term "Working Capital." The LOI stated the target would be "agreed in the Definitive Agreement" (¶ 2(a)), implying the definition would be negotiated.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- Without a definition, the parties could dispute whether items such as deferred revenue, accrued bonuses, or the value of the Kestrel Motion license (if it were on the balance sheet) should be included or excluded.
- The $3.25M target is new (not in the LOI). We need to confirm with the client's controllers whether this target is achievable and whether the Estimated Closing Statement will show a favorable or unfavorable adjustment. A target set too high could result in a purchase price reduction at Closing.

**Recommendation:**

- Add a definition of "Working Capital" in Article 1 (typically: current assets minus current liabilities, with specified exclusions such as deferred revenue, taxes payable, and accrued compensation).
- **Action needed from client:** Ask the client's finance team to confirm whether the $3,250,000 target is appropriate based on the Company's current working capital position and whether they expect the Estimated Closing Statement to show an overage or shortage.

## Issue 8 – Buyer's Unfettered Assignment Right

**Where:** SPA § 11.4.

**Explanation:**

Section 11.4 permits Buyer to assign the entire Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement "to any Buyer Designee" without Seller's consent. Upon assignment and the designee's written assumption, "Buyer will be relieved of its obligations." There is no requirement that the designee have comparable financial strength, be in a comparable line of business, or be acceptable to Seller.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- Seller's earnout (up to $30M) and indemnification rights are only as good as the credit of the obligor. If Buyer assigns to a shell company or a financially weaker entity, Seller's recovery for unpaid earnouts or indemnification claims could be impaired.
- The combination of this assignment right with the limited carve-outs from Buyer's indemnification cap (see Issue 3) compounds the risk.

**Recommendation:**

- Require that any Buyer Designee be an entity with net worth or creditworthiness no less than Buyer's at the time of assignment, or require Seller's reasonable consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) to any assignment.
- Alternatively, require that Buyer remain jointly and severally liable with any designee for at least the duration of the Earnout Period.

## Issue 9 – Ancillary Agreements (Exhibits A and B) Not Yet Drafted

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definitions of "Retained Engine License" and "Transition Services Agreement"); SPA Exhibits A and B (marked "to be attached"); email thread, Message 3, point 4.

**Explanation:**

The Retained Engine License and the Transition Services Agreement are integral to the transaction. The SPA's operative provisions (§§ 6.6, 6.7) and closing deliverables (§§ 3.2(b), 3.3(b)) depend on their terms. Owen Batarseh confirmed in the email thread (Message 3, point 4) that first drafts will be circulated with the SPA markup.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- The Retained Engine License defines the scope of the Company's right to use Cinderloom. If the license is too broad, it undermines Seller's ability to license the engine to other customers (compounding the non-compete issue in Issue 1). If it is too narrow, the Company's games may not function properly.
- The Transition Services Agreement defines what services Seller must provide post-Closing and at what cost. Unclear terms could create ongoing operational entanglement.
- SPA § 11.9 provides that the SPA prevails over any Ancillary Agreement. This means if there is a conflict, the SPA controls. We need to ensure the SPA's references to the ancillary agreements are consistent with the intended terms.

**Recommendation:**

- Review Exhibits A and B carefully when they are circulated. Key points for the Retained Engine License: confirm the license is limited to Ashen Vanguard and titles under development at Closing (as stated in § 6.6); confirm no right to use Cinderloom for new, unrelated titles; confirm the term and termination rights; confirm the fee structure (if any).
- For the Transition Services Agreement: confirm the scope is limited and time-bounded; confirm fees are at cost with no margin; confirm Seller has no obligation to provide services beyond the agreed scope.

**Explanation:**

Schedule 4.4 states "None" in response to the representation in § 4.4 that no consent is required under any Material Contract. However, the KEA is listed as a Material Contract (Schedule 4.10, item 1), and KEA § 7.3(d) requires Vasquez-Ilunga's written consent for the Kestrel Motion License to survive the Change of Control. This should be done in conjunction with the condition to Closing recommended in Issue 4.

## Issue 11 – Earnout Operating Covenants Are Thin

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(e); compare LOI ¶ 3.

**Explanation:**

Section 2.5(e) requires Buyer to (i) act in good faith and not take action with the primary purpose of avoiding or reducing the earnout, (ii) continue making Ashen Vanguard available on current platforms, and (iii) maintain separate books. The LOI (¶ 3) used similar language ("operate the title in good faith and will not take any action with the primary purpose of avoiding or reducing the Earnout").

The "primary purpose" standard is a high bar. A buyer could argue that a decision to reduce marketing spend or delay a content update was driven by legitimate business reasons (e.g., pivot to a new title) even if it had the effect of reducing Ashen Vanguard's revenue.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- The earnout represents up to $30M (36% of total consideration). The operating covenants are the primary protection against buyer behavior that suppresses Ashen Vanguard's revenue.
- Without a requirement to maintain a minimum level of development or marketing investment, Buyer could effectively starve the title while technically complying with the "primary purpose" standard.

**Recommendation:**

- Add a requirement that Buyer use commercially reasonable efforts to support the ongoing development and marketing of Ashen Vanguard during the Earnout Period, consistent with the level of support provided in the 12 months prior to Closing.
- Add a requirement that Buyer maintain a development team of comparable size and skill to the team in place at Closing, at least for the first Earnout Year.
- These are harder to negotiate, but given the size of the earnout relative to the base price, they are worth proposing.

## Issue 12 – Section 6.1 (Conduct of Business) Could Create Circularity on KEA Amendments

**Where:** SPA § 6.1; KEA (generally); SPA Schedule 4.10, item 1.

**Explanation:**

Section 6.1 requires Buyer's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for the Company to "enter into, amend or terminate any Material Contract." The KEA is a Material Contract. If the Company needs to amend the KEA to (a) document Vasquez-Ilunga's consent to the Kestrel Motion License continuation, or (b) adjust his role or compensation post-Closing, Buyer's consent would be required under § 6.1.

**Why it matters for Bikas:**

- This creates a potential circularity: Seller needs Buyer's consent to allow the Company to amend the KEA, but the purpose of the amendment is to facilitate the very transaction Buyer is acquiring. While the consent standard is "not to be unreasonably withheld," it gives Buyer leverage.
- More practically, if Vasquez-Ilunga wants to negotiate new terms as a condition of his consent (e.g., a retention bonus, equity in the new parent, or a revised role), the Company would need Buyer's consent to amend the KEA to reflect those terms.

**Recommendation:**

- Add an exception to § 6.1 for amendments to the KEA that are necessary to effectuate the transactions contemplated by the SPA or to obtain Vasquez-Ilunga's consent under KEA § 7.3(d), provided such amendments do not increase the Company's compensation obligations by more than a de minimis amount.
- Alternatively, address this in the condition to Closing (Issue 4) by requiring that the consent and any related amendment be executed before Closing, when § 6.1 is still in effect but the parties are working toward a common goal.
