## Issue 1: Exclusivity Scope – MSA §3.4 vs. Order Form No. 4 §2.1

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §3.4 (Exclusivity)
- MSA §1.3 (Order of Precedence)
- Order Form No. 4 §2.1 (Exclusive Services Scope)

**The problem.** MSA §3.4 provides that during the term of the Agreement, Staidaix and its Affiliates "shall procure all genotyping, sequencing and trait-screening services for any crop species in any territory exclusively from Provider, and shall not engage any third-party laboratory or service provider, or perform those services internally, except with Provider's prior written consent." This is extraordinarily broad: all crops, all territories, all genotyping/sequencing/trait-screening, and it even bars Staidaix from performing these services in-house.

Order Form No. 4 §2.1 narrows the exclusive scope to "Soybean marker-assisted genotyping services performed for Customer in the United States." However, MSA §1.3 states that an Order Form term prevails over a conflicting MSA term "only if the Order Form expressly identifies, by section number, the provision of this Agreement that it is intended to override." Order Form No. 4 §2.1 does not cite MSA §3.4 by section number. On its face, the narrower scope in the Order Form does not validly override the broader MSA provision.

**Why it matters.** If MSA §3.4 controls, Staidaix is locked into Prupox for all genotyping, sequencing, and trait-screening services globally for the entire MSA term (initial 5 years to February 2030, plus auto-renewals). It cannot use another lab for corn, cannot use a lab outside the U.S., and cannot even run its own genotyping in-house. Post-merger with Fenwyck, this could restrict Fenwyck's and its other subsidiaries' ability to source genotyping services. This is likely not what the business deal intended.

**Recommended fix.** Before or at the next amendment, add a sentence to Order Form No. 4 §2.1 (or a side letter) that expressly states: "This Section 2.1 overrides Section 3.4 of the Agreement solely with respect to the Services under this Order Form." Alternatively, amend MSA §3.4 to conform to the intended scope. If the parties cannot agree on a narrow scope, Staidaix should negotiate a carve-out for (a) services not covered by any active Order Form, (b) internal performance, and (c) Affiliates that are not in the plant-breeding business.

## Issue 2: Change of Control – MSA §14.2 and the Pending Fenwyck Merger

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control," clause (d))
- MSA §14.1 (Assignment)
- MSA §14.2 (Change of Control deemed assignment)
- MSA §12.3 (Termination by Provider for Assignment)
- Order Form No. 4, Recital B (acknowledgment of Merger Agreement)
- Order Form No. 4 §5.2 (Acceleration on Termination for Customer's Breach)

**The problem.** Order Form No. 4 Recital B acknowledges that Staidaix Holdings, Inc. signed a Merger Agreement on October 12, 2026 under which it will be acquired by Fenwyck AgriSciences Corp., expected to close in Q1 2027. MSA §1.1 defines "Change of Control" to include, in clause (d), "any event described in clauses (a) to (c) occurring for any parent entity that directly or indirectly controls that entity, such that control of that entity changes indirectly." The acquisition of Staidaix Holdings by Fenwyck will almost certainly constitute a Change of Control of Staidaix Seed Genetics, LLC.

Under MSA §14.2, a Change of Control of Customer "is deemed an assignment of this Agreement and each Order Form by Customer and requires Provider's prior written consent." Provider "may withhold or condition its consent in its discretion." If Provider withholds consent, MSA §12.3 allows Provider to terminate immediately, and that termination "is a termination for Customer's breach for all purposes."

**Why it matters.** This is the single largest financial risk in the contract set. If Provider withholds consent and terminates under §12.3, Order Form No. 4 §5.2 accelerates the entire remaining Minimum Annual Commitment: $1,850,000 × remaining Contract Years, less fees already invoiced. If the merger closes in, say, February 2027 (approximately 4 months into the first Contract Year), Staidaix could owe roughly $1.85M (remainder of Year 1) + $1.85M (Year 2) + $1.85M (Year 3) ≈ **$5.55M**, less any fees already paid. The Order Form was signed on October 20, 2026—eight days after the Merger Agreement—yet it contains no consent, waiver, or condition relating to the Change of Control. The parties clearly knew about the merger but did not address the consent requirement.

**Recommended fix.** Staidaix should seek Provider's written consent to the Change of Control before the merger closes. Given that Provider already acknowledged the merger in Recital B and signed the Order Form with full knowledge of it, there is a strong equitable argument that consent was implied or that Provider is estopped from withholding it. However, the contract language is explicit and gives Provider discretion. I recommend:
1. Open a dialogue with Prupox immediately (before closing) to obtain written consent under §14.2.
2. If Prupox resists, negotiate a condition (e.g., Fenwyck assumes the Agreement, or a reduced commitment) in exchange for consent.
3. As a fallback, document that Prupox's execution of Order Form No. 4 with full knowledge of the pending merger constitutes implied consent or a waiver, to support a post-hoc argument if Prupox later attempts to terminate.
4. Confirm with the Fenwyck deal team whether the Merger Agreement addresses this consent requirement and who bears the risk if Prupox withholds consent.

## Issue 3: Lot SG-26-117 – Nonconformance Notice Timeliness

**Where it appears:**
- Quality Agreement §7.2 (Notice Period – 10 Business Days)
- Quality Agreement §7.3 (Content and Delivery)
- Order Form No. 4 §6.1–6.4 (Lot SG-26-117)
- Q3 2026 Meeting Notes §5.1

**The problem.** Lot SG-26-117 (18,432 soybean samples, SoyCore-384) had a Data Release Date of September 14, 2026 (a Monday). Quality Agreement §7.2 requires a Nonconformance Notice "within ten (10) Business Days after the Data Release Date." Counting from September 15, the 10th Business Day is Monday, September 28, 2026. Staidaix's Nonconformance Notice is dated October 2, 2026—four Business Days late.

Order Form No. 4 §6.2 records the parties' disagreement: Prupox asserts the notice was untimely and the batch is deemed accepted; Staidaix disputes timeliness and reserves all rights.

**Why it matters.** If the notice is untimely, Quality Agreement §7.2 deems the batch accepted and strips Staidaix of "any right to a Re-run, replacement data, service credit under Section 4.2 of the Service Level Schedule, or refund in respect of that Batch." The batch had Call Rate and Concordance Rate below Service Levels on 23 of 48 plates. At $3.15 per sample, the fees are approximately $58,060. But the bigger concern is whether the sub-standard data has already been used in breeding decisions. If the data is flawed and Staidaix cannot get a re-run, the downstream cost to the breeding program could far exceed the fees.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Assess whether there is a factual basis for the timeliness dispute (e.g., portal access issues, the data not actually being posted on September 14 despite the meeting notes saying "the week of September 14," or a system glitch). The Q3 notes (§5.1) say Provider "expects to post final data to the Customer Portal the week of September 14"—this is a forward-looking statement made on September 10, not confirmation that posting occurred on the 14th. If there is any evidence the data was not actually accessible on September 14, the effective Data Release Date may be later.
2. If the notice was in fact late, consider whether Prupox's own conduct (e.g., failure to send the Data Release Notification under SLS §2.4, or a portal issue) contributed, which could support an equitable estoppel or waiver argument.
3. Regardless of the timeliness question, press for a substantive resolution: request Prupox's investigation records under Quality Agreement §8.1 and MSA §6.3, and consider whether the data is usable despite the quality metrics being below threshold.
4. Keep the invoice disputed under MSA §4.4 (which Staidaix has done per Order Form No. 4 §6.3) to preserve leverage.

## Issue 4: Acceleration Clause – Order Form No. 4 §5.2

**Where it appears:**
- Order Form No. 4 §5.2 (Acceleration on Termination for Customer's Breach)
- MSA §12.3 (Termination for Assignment)
- MSA §14.2 (Change of Control)

**The problem.** Order Form No. 4 §5.2 provides that if Prupox terminates for Customer's breach, "the unpaid Minimum Annual Commitment for the then-current Contract Year and each remaining Contract Year of the Order Term... becomes immediately due and payable, without further notice or demand." This is a full-acceleration clause covering all remaining years, not just the current year. It is triggered by any termination for Customer's breach, including the deemed-breach termination under MSA §12.3 for an unconsented Change of Control.

**Why it matters.** As noted in Issue 2, this creates a potential ~$5.55M exposure if the Fenwyck merger triggers a Change of Control and Prupox withholds consent. The clause gives Prupox enormous leverage: it can effectively hold the merger hostage or extract a premium for consent. Even if the merger proceeds and Prupox does not terminate, the existence of this clause means Staidaix has limited ability to walk away from the Order Form without triggering full acceleration.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Negotiate a cap on acceleration (e.g., limited to the current Contract Year only, or a percentage of remaining commitment).
2. Add an explicit carve-out: acceleration does not apply if the termination results from a Change of Control that Provider consented to in writing.
3. If Prupox will not amend the clause, ensure written consent to the Change of Control is obtained before closing (see Issue 2) to eliminate the trigger entirely.

## Issue 5: Asymmetric Assignment Rights – MSA §14.2 vs. §14.3

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §14.1 (General assignment restriction)
- MSA §14.2 (Customer Change of Control requires consent)
- MSA §14.3 (Provider may assign genotyping business without consent)

**The problem.** Customer cannot undergo a Change of Control without Provider's discretionary consent (§14.2), but Provider can assign the entire Agreement to a successor of its genotyping laboratory business without Customer's consent (§14.3), subject only to the successor agreeing in writing to be bound and Provider giving notice within 10 Business Days after the assignment.

**Why it matters.** Staidaix is locked in: it cannot change ownership without Prupox's permission, but Prupox can sell or transfer its genotyping business to anyone (including a competitor of Fenwyck post-merger) and Staidaix must accept the new provider. There is no right of termination if the assignee is unsuitable, no quality-of-successor standard, and no advance notice (only post-assignment notice within 10 Business Days).

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate an amendment to §14.3 requiring (a) advance written notice (e.g., 30 days) before the assignment, (b) the successor to meet minimum qualifications (accreditation, financial stability), and (c) Customer to have a termination right (without acceleration) if the successor does not meet those standards. At minimum, add a provision that Customer's consent to a Change of Control of Customer (under §14.2) is not contingent on Provider's own assignment rights.

## Issue 6: MSA Auto-Renewal and Long-Term Lock-In – MSA §12.1

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §12.1 (Term and auto-renewal)
- MSA §3.4 (Exclusivity runs "during the term of this Agreement")

**The problem.** The MSA has a 5-year initial term (March 1, 2025 – February 28, 2030) and auto-renews for successive 1-year periods unless either party gives notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. The first non-renewal notice deadline is approximately September 1, 2029. Because MSA §3.4 exclusivity runs "during the term of this Agreement," the (arguably overbroad) exclusivity obligation continues through each auto-renewal period.

**Why it matters.** If Staidaix misses the 180-day notice window, it is locked in for another year with the same exclusivity terms. Post-merger, Fenwyck may want flexibility to consolidate genotyping across its portfolio. The auto-renewal combined with broad exclusivity creates a compounding lock-in risk.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (180 days before February 28, 2030 ≈ September 1, 2029) and set internal reminders well in advance.
2. When negotiating the exclusivity fix (Issue 1), also consider adding a provision that auto-renewal periods are subject to re-negotiation of the exclusivity scope, or that the exclusivity applies only during periods when an active Order Form is in effect.

## Issue 7: Provider's Capacity Obligation is Conditional – MSA §3.5 and SLS §5(c)

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §3.5 (Capacity)
- Service Level Schedule §5(c) (Exclusion for volumes above capacity limits)
- Order Form No. 4 §2.3 (Capacity for Exclusive Services)

**The problem.** MSA §3.5 requires Provider to maintain capacity sufficient for forecast volumes, "provided that actual volumes do not exceed one hundred twenty percent (120%) of the most recent forecast." SLS §5(c) excludes Provider from service level responsibility for failures caused by "Batch volumes above the capacity limits in Section 3.5." So if Staidaix's actual volumes exceed 120% of its most recent quarterly forecast, Prupox has no obligation to meet turnaround times or data quality standards for the excess, and no service credits apply.

**Why it matters.** Staidaix's forecast for the first Contract Year is ~410,000 soybean and ~65,000 corn samples (Order Form No. 4 §1.3). The 120% threshold is ~492,000 soybean and ~78,000 corn. If breeding program needs surge (e.g., a new trait-screening initiative or a large backcross program), Staidaix could quickly exceed the threshold and lose service level protection. The forecast must be updated quarterly (MSA §2.3), but if Staidaix under-forecasts to avoid appearing to over-commit, it risks exceeding 120%.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Ensure the quarterly forecast process is robust and that the program team flags volume increases early so the forecast can be updated before the 120% threshold is breached.
2. Negotiate to raise the threshold to 130% or 150%, or to make the capacity obligation apply to the Minimum Annual Commitment volume rather than the forecast.
3. Clarify in the next Order Form that the 120% cap applies per Service Line, not in aggregate, so a surge in one area does not erode capacity for another.

## Issue 8: Service Credit Cap May Be Inadequate – SLS §4.4

**Where it appears:**
- Service Level Schedule §4.1–4.3 (Credit percentages)
- Service Level Schedule §4.4 (Monthly cap of 25%)

**The problem.** Total service credits in any calendar month are capped at 25% of the Fees invoiced under the applicable Order Form for that month (SLS §4.4). In a bad month with multiple late batches and data quality failures, the credits could be well below the actual value of the service degradation.

**Why it matters.** For a month with, say, $200,000 in invoiced fees, the maximum credit is $50,000. If multiple batches are significantly late and below quality thresholds, the true cost to Staidaix's breeding timeline could exceed that. The cap limits Staidaix's recovery for service failures short of a material breach.

**Recommended fix.** The 25% cap is not unreasonable on its face, but Staidaix should be aware of it when assessing whether to pursue a material breach claim (MSA §12.2) or a chronic failure claim (SLS §4.6) rather than relying on service credits. If performance deteriorates, escalate quickly to the chronic failure provision, which converts repeated misses into a termination right.

## Issue 9: Data Ownership and Use Restrictions – MSA §7.2

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §7.2 (Customer Data and Deliverables)
- MSA §7.5 (Improvements)
- MSA §11.3(c) (Liability cap exception for breach of §7.2)

**The problem.** MSA §7.2 assigns all Customer Data and Deliverables to Staidaix and prohibits Provider from using Customer Data "for any purpose other than performing the Services" or from "aggregat[ing], de-identif[y]ing or combin[ing] Customer Data with other data for its own purposes." This is strong protection. However, MSA §7.5 allows Provider to retain "Improvements to Provider Technology of general application" that it develops in performing the Services, so long as they "do not incorporate or reveal Customer Data or Customer Confidential Information."

**Why it matters.** The boundary between a "general application" improvement and one that "incorporates or reveals" Customer Data can be fuzzy in genomics. If Prupox develops a new calling algorithm or marker panel optimization using patterns it observes across Staidaix's data (even without using specific data points), Staidaix may have difficulty proving the improvement "incorporates or reveals" its data. The uncapped liability in §11.3(c) for breach of §7.2 is helpful, but proving the breach may be challenging.

**Recommended fix.**
1. In the next amendment or Order Form, add a specific prohibition: Provider shall not use any statistical patterns, allele frequencies, or genomic architecture derived from Customer Data to develop products or services for third parties, even if the specific data points are removed.
2. Require Provider to certify annually (e.g., at the quarterly review) that no Provider Technology improvements have been derived from Customer Data.
3. Ensure the audit right (Quality Agreement §10.1, MSA §5.3) is used periodically to verify compliance.

## Issue 10: Quality Agreement – Plate Sealer Firmware Update

**Where it appears:**
- Quality Agreement §11.1 (Change Notification – 30 days' prior notice for material changes)
- Q3 2026 Meeting Notes §4.3

**The problem.** At the Q3 review, Prupox's Quality Director announced a planned firmware update to the plate sealers for November 2026. Prupox does not regard it as a "material change" under Quality Agreement §11.1, so it is not providing the 30-day prior notice or validation summary that §11.1–11.2 would require. Prupox will circulate "a short summary" (Action Item 2, due October 15, 2026).

**Why it matters.** Plate sealers affect sample integrity (cross-contamination, evaporation, seal failure). A firmware change could alter sealing pressure, temperature, or timing. If Prupox is correct that it is not a "material change," Staidaix has no contractual right to require validation or concordance testing before the update is applied to Staidaix batches. If it is in fact material, Prupox is in breach of §11.1 by not giving 30 days' notice (the update is planned for November; the notice was given September 10, which is roughly 60 days, so the timing may actually be sufficient—but the characterization as non-material means no validation is required).

**Recommended fix.**
1. When the "short summary" arrives (due October 15), review it carefully. If the firmware change affects any parameter that could impact seal integrity, argue that it is a material change and require validation under §11.2, including concordance testing on Staidaix check lines.
2. If Prupox resists, use the annual quality audit (already scheduled for Q1 2027 per Action Item 4) to review the change and its validation.
3. Consider whether to request that the next batch after the firmware update include additional check lines to verify no degradation.

## Issue 12: Order of Precedence – Structural Risk

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §1.3 (Order of Precedence)

**The problem.** The MSA prevails over all other documents unless an Order Form "expressly identifies, by section number" the MSA provision it overrides. This is a high bar. Any term in an Order Form that is inconsistent with the MSA but does not include an express cross-reference is subordinate to the MSA. This creates a structural trap: business teams negotiating Order Forms may believe they have agreed to a term that actually does not override the MSA.

**Why it matters.** As shown in Issue 1 (exclusivity), this has already produced a gap between what the parties likely intended (narrow exclusivity in Order Form No. 4) and what the contract language achieves (broad MSA exclusivity still in force). The same risk applies to any future Order Form or amendment.

**Recommended fix.**
1. For Order Form No. 4, immediately add express override language for §2.1 (exclusivity) and any other terms that differ from the MSA.
2. For all future Order Forms, adopt a drafting convention: any term that deviates from the MSA must include the sentence "This Section [X] overrides Section [Y] of the Agreement."
3. Consider amending MSA §1.3 to a simpler rule (e.g., later-dated documents prevail) to reduce the risk of inadvertent non-override.

## Issue 13: Liability Cap – MSA §11.2

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §11.1 (Exclusion of indirect damages)
- MSA §11.2 (Cap at 12 months of fees)
- MSA §11.3 (Exceptions)

**The problem.** The liability cap is "FEES PAID AND PAYABLE BY CUSTOMER UNDER ALL ORDER FORMS DURING THE TWELVE (12) MONTHS BEFORE THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO THE CLAIM." At the current commitment level (~$1.85M/year), the cap is approximately $1.85M. The indirect damages exclusion (§11.1) bars lost profits, lost revenue, and consequential damages.

**Why it matters.** If Prupox's data errors cause Staidaix to make incorrect breeding decisions (e.g., selecting a line that lacks a key trait), Staidaix's actual loss (cost of the breeding cycle, lost time, opportunity cost) could far exceed $1.85M, but the indirect damages exclusion and cap would bar recovery. The exceptions in §11.3 (confidentiality breach, data ownership breach, gross negligence, fraud, willful misconduct) are helpful but narrow.

**Recommended fix.**
1. The cap is not unusual for this type of agreement, but Staidaix should be aware of it when assessing risk. Ensure that data quality issues are caught early (through the Nonconformance process and quarterly reviews) so that the cost of correction (re-run) is covered by the Quality Agreement remedies rather than by a damages claim subject to the cap.
2. Consider negotiating to raise the cap to 18 or 24 months of fees, or to add a super-cap (e.g., 2× the general cap) for data quality failures that require re-runs.
3. Confirm that the insurance requirements (MSA §13: $5M E&O) provide a backstop. Request current certificates of insurance.

## Issue 14: Termination Rights – Limited for Customer

**Where it appears:**
- MSA §12.2 (Termination for material breach, 30-day cure)
- MSA §12.4 (Termination for insolvency)
- SLS §4.6 (Chronic failure = material breach)
- Order Form No. 4 §5.3 (Customer termination for Provider's breach – commitment falls away)

**The problem.** Staidaix's termination rights are limited to: (a) Provider's material breach (with 30-day cure period), (b) Provider's insolvency, and (c) chronic service level failure (3 misses in 6 months = material breach under SLS §4.6). There is no convenience termination, no termination for Change of Control of Provider, and no termination if Provider assigns under §14.3.

**Why it matters.** If the relationship sours or if Fenwyck's post-merger strategy does not align with Prupox, Staidaix has no clean exit. It must either (a) prove a material breach and wait 30 days for cure, or (b) serve a non-renewal notice 180 days before the MSA term ends (which is not until ~September 2029 for the initial term). The Order Form commitment ($1.85M/year × 3 years) means that even if the MSA is not renewed, any active Order Form continues (MSA §12.1, last sentence).

**Recommended fix.**
1. Negotiate a convenience termination right for Customer on 12 months' notice, with a buyout formula (e.g., 50% of remaining commitment) rather than full acceleration.
2. Add a termination right for Customer if Provider assigns under §14.3 to a successor that Customer reasonably objects to.
3. Ensure the chronic failure provision (SLS §4.6) is monitored: if Prupox misses the same service level in 3 of 6 months, Staidaix should be prepared to issue a material breach notice immediately.

## Issue 15: Fenwyck's Role – Not a Contract Party

**Where it appears:**
- Order Form No. 4, Recital B
- MSA §1.1 (definition of "Affiliate")

**The problem.** Fenwyck AgriSciences Corp. is not a party to any of the agreements. It is mentioned only in Recital B of Order Form No. 4 as the acquirer of Staidaix Holdings. Post-merger, Fenwyck will be an Affiliate (parent) of Staidaix Seed Genetics, LLC. MSA §3.4 exclusivity binds "Customer and its Affiliates." So post-merger, Fenwyck and all its subsidiaries would be bound by the exclusivity obligation (to the extent MSA §3.4 controls, per Issue 1).

**Why it matters.** Fenwyck likely has its own genotyping operations or uses other providers across its portfolio. If MSA §3.4 applies to Affiliates, the merger could impose Prupox exclusivity on Fenwyck's entire genotyping operation. This is a potential deal issue that should be resolved before closing.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Confirm with the Fenwyck deal team whether the Merger Agreement addresses this (e.g., a condition that Prupox consents, or an indemnity for acceleration costs).
2. When negotiating the exclusivity fix (Issue 1), ensure the carve-out covers Affiliates that are not engaged in plant breeding, or limit the exclusivity to Staidaix Seed Genetics, LLC only (not its Affiliates).
3. If the broad exclusivity cannot be narrowed, consider whether Fenwyck should be joined as a party to the Agreement (with its own consent) to clarify the scope of obligations.
