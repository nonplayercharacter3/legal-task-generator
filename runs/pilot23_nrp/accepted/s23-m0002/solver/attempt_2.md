## Issue 1 – Change of Control Gives Prupox an Unfettered Right to Block the Fenwyck Transaction

**Where:** MSA §14.2; MSA §12.3; OF4 Recital B; OF4 §9.2; OF4 §5.2.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §14.2 provides that a Change of Control of Customer "is deemed an assignment of this Agreement and each Order Form by Customer and requires Provider's prior written consent under Section 14.1." Critically, it adds: **"Provider may withhold or condition its consent in its discretion."** There is no standard (e.g., "not to be unreasonably withheld") and no obligation on Prupox to deal.
- MSA §12.3 provides that if Customer assigns in breach of §14, Prupox may terminate immediately, and that termination "is a termination for Customer's breach for all purposes."
- OF4 Recital B acknowledges that Staidaix Holdings signed a Merger Agreement with Fenwyck on October 12, 2026, and that closing is expected in Q1 2027.
- OF4 §9.2 states: "Nothing in this Order Form constitutes Provider's consent under any provision of the Agreement, or a waiver by either Party of any right or remedy, except as expressly stated in this Order Form."
- OF4 §5.2 provides that if Prupox terminates for Customer's breach, the full unpaid Minimum Annual Commitment for the then-current and all remaining Contract Years accelerates and becomes immediately due.

**Why it matters:**

Prupox signed OF4 eight days after the Merger Agreement was executed, with full knowledge of the pending Change of Control, yet expressly disclaimed any consent or waiver. This means Prupox retains the right, at any time before or after the Fenwyck closing, to refuse consent to the Change of Control. If it does:

1. The refusal constitutes a deemed assignment in breach of MSA §14.
2. Prupox can terminate the MSA and all Order Forms under §12.3.
3. The termination is treated as Customer's breach.
4. Under OF4 §5.2, the full remaining Minimum Annual Commitment accelerates. At $1,850,000 per year for a three-year term (October 20, 2026 – October 19, 2029), Staidaix's maximum acceleration exposure is approximately **$5,550,000**, less any fees already invoiced and counted.
5. Staidaix would lose its genotyping service provider mid-stream, with no contractual right to transition.

This is the single most dangerous provision in the contract set for Staidaix's current situation.

**Recommended fix:**

Before the Fenwyck transaction closes, Staidaix should negotiate a written consent (or a consent letter with conditions acceptable to Fenwyck) from Prupox under MSA §14.2. The negotiation should address:

- Whether Prupox will consent unconditionally or seek conditions (e.g., a guarantee from Fenwyck, a price adjustment, or a shortened Order Form term).
- Whether the parties will amend OF4 §5.2 to exclude Change-of-Control-driven terminations from the acceleration clause, or to provide a transition period.
- Whether the MSA and Order Forms will be novated to or assumed by the post-merger entity.

If Prupox refuses to consent, Staidaix needs to understand its fallback: the MSA has no termination right for Customer in the absence of Provider breach, and the auto-renewal structure (MSA §12.1) means the relationship continues by default. Staidaix should also assess whether it can operationally absorb a termination (see Issue 3 on Lot SG-26-117 and the capacity/exclusivity issues below).

**Flag for client discussion:** Staidaix needs to confirm whether it gave Prupox the written notice of the proposed Change of Control required by MSA §14.2 ("as soon as reasonably practicable and, where lawful, before it occurs"). The Merger Agreement was signed October 12, 2026, and OF4 (which references it) was signed October 20, 2026. If no separate notice was sent, Prupox could argue that Staidaix is already in breach of the notice obligation, strengthening its hand.

## Issue 2 – Exclusivity Scope: Direct Conflict Between MSA §3.4 and OF4 §2.1

**Where:** MSA §3.4; OF4 §2.1; MSA §1.3 (Order of Precedence).

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §3.4: "During the term of this Agreement, Customer and its Affiliates shall procure **all genotyping, sequencing and trait-screening services for any crop species in any territory** exclusively from Provider, and shall not engage any third-party laboratory or service provider, or perform those services internally, except with Provider's prior written consent."
- OF4 §2.1: "The Parties agree that the exclusive services scope for this Order Form is: **Soybean marker-assisted genotyping services performed for Customer in the United States** (the 'Exclusive Services')."
- MSA §1.3 (Order of Precedence): "A term in an Order Form will prevail over a conflicting term of this Agreement **only if the Order Form expressly identifies, by section number, the provision of this Agreement that it is intended to override**, and then only for the Services under that Order Form."

OF4 §2.1 does not cite MSA §3.4 by section number.

**Why it matters:**

On a plain reading of MSA §1.3, OF4 §2.1 does not effectively override MSA §3.4 because it fails to identify the MSA provision by section number. This means the broader MSA exclusivity (all crops, all territories, all service types, all Affiliates) likely still governs, and OF4 §2.1 is either (a) a redundant narrowing that has no legal effect, or (b) an attempt to narrow that fails under the precedence rule.

The practical consequence is significant:

- If MSA §3.4 controls, Staidaix cannot use any third party for corn genotyping, soybean sequencing, trait-screening in other countries, or any internal laboratory work without Prupox's consent.
- If OF4 §2.1 controls (which the documents don't clearly support), Staidaix is free to use third parties for everything except soybean marker-assisted genotyping in the US.
- The ambiguity gives Prupox an argument that any use of a third party for, say, corn GBS sequencing or international soybean genotyping is a breach of MSA §3.4, potentially triggering termination under §12.2 and acceleration under OF4 §5.2.

This issue becomes even more acute post-merger (see Issue 5 below).

**Recommended fix:**

Staidaix should seek a clarifying amendment to OF4 (or a side letter) that either:

(a) Expressly states that OF4 §2.1 overrides MSA §3.4 for the Services under OF4, citing §3.4 by number as MSA §1.3 requires; or

(b) Amends MSA §3.4 to conform to the narrower scope the parties actually intended.

Until resolved, Staidaix should operate on the assumption that the broader MSA §3.4 exclusivity applies and should not engage any third-party genotyping, sequencing, or trait-screening provider without first obtaining Prupox's written consent under §3.4.

**Flag for client discussion:** We need to confirm with Staidaix what the commercial intent was when OF4 §2.1 was negotiated. The Q3 Notes §6.2 reference the "Exclusive Services scope of soybean marker-assisted genotyping performed for Customer in the United States" as a term in the working draft, suggesting this was a negotiated point. If the intent was to narrow exclusivity, the drafting failed to satisfy MSA §1.3, and the broader MSA language likely still applies.

## Issue 3 – Lot SG-26-117: Nonconformance Notice Likely Untimely, Triggering Deemed Acceptance

**Where:** QA §7.2; OF4 §6.1–6.4; SLS §2.2; Q3 Notes §5.1.

**What the documents say:**

- OF4 §6.1: Lot SG-26-117 is a Batch of 18,432 soybean Samples (SoyCore-384). The Data Release Date was **September 14, 2026**. Customer's Nonconformance Notice is dated **October 2, 2026**, reporting Call Rate and Concordance Rate below Service Levels across 23 of 48 plates.
- QA §7.2: "Customer shall give Provider a Nonconformance Notice for a Batch within **ten (10) Business Days** after the Data Release Date for that Batch. If Customer does not give a Nonconformance Notice for a Batch within that period, the Batch and its Deliverables are **deemed accepted**, and Customer will have **no right to a Re-run, replacement data, service credit under Section 4.2 of the Service Level Schedule, or refund** in respect of that Batch."
- OF4 §6.2: Prupox's position is that the notice was untimely and the lot is accepted. Customer disputes this.
- OF4 §6.3: Customer has disputed the invoice under MSA §4.4. Fees for the lot do not count toward the Minimum Annual Commitment.
- Q3 Notes §5.1 (September 10, 2026): Provider stated the lot was "on schedule" and expected to post data "the week of September 14."

**Business Day calculation:**

September 14, 2026 is a Monday. The 10 Business Days after the Data Release Date are:

| Business Day | Date |
|---|---|
| 1 | Sept 15 (Tue) |
| 2 | Sept 16 (Wed) |
| 3 | Sept 17 (Thu) |
| 4 | Sept 18 (Fri) |
| 5 | Sept 21 (Mon) |
| 6 | Sept 22 (Tue) |
| 7 | Sept 23 (Wed) |
| 8 | Sept 24 (Thu) |
| 9 | Sept 25 (Fri) |
| 10 | **Sept 28 (Mon)** |

The Nonconformance Notice dated October 2, 2026 (a Friday) was sent **4 Business Days after the deadline**.

**Why it matters:**

If the notice is untimely, QA §7.2 operates as a strict deemed-acceptance clause. Staidaix loses:

- The right to a Re-run (QA §8.2);
- The right to replacement data;
- The right to a data quality service credit under SLS §4.2 (10% of Batch fees);
- The right to a refund under QA §8.4.

For a Batch of 18,432 Samples at $3.15 per Sample (OF4 §4.1), the fees are approximately **$58,061**. The service credit alone would be ~$5,806, but the Re-run and potential refund are the more valuable remedies. More importantly, if the data quality is genuinely deficient (23 of 48 plates below threshold), Staidaix may be forced to use unreliable genotype calls in its breeding program, with no contractual recourse.

Staidaix's dispute of the invoice under MSA §4.4 (OF4 §6.3) does not override the deemed-acceptance clause. MSA §4.4 allows withholding of disputed amounts, but QA §7.2 specifically extinguishes the underlying rights (Re-run, credit, refund) upon deemed acceptance. The dispute may preserve the right to withhold payment, but it does not restore the quality remedies.

**Potential counterarguments for Staidaix (to be assessed with the full Nonconformance Notice):**

- **QA §6.4 (Deviations):** If Prupox had a Deviation that may have affected the Batch and failed to notify Customer within 3 Business Days, there may be an argument that the deemed-acceptance period should be tolled or that Prupox is estopped from relying on it. We do not have evidence of any Deviation notice for this lot.
- **Course of dealing / waiver:** The Q3 Notes show the parties discussed the lot in a routine context. If Prupox knew or should have known about the quality issues before September 14 (e.g., through its own release review under QA §6.3), it might be inequitable to enforce the deadline. However, QA §6.3 requires Prupox to notify Customer of pre-release Nonconformances, and there is no evidence of such a notice.
- **The notice may satisfy §7.3 content requirements** (identifying the Batch, affected plates, and describing the Nonconformance with supporting data), which would at least show good faith. But content compliance does not cure a timing failure.

**Recommended fix:**

1. **Immediately** obtain and review the full Nonconformance Notice to confirm its date, content, and delivery method (QA §7.3 requires email to Provider's Quality Contact with a copy to the account manager).
2. Assess whether there is any basis to argue the notice was timely (e.g., if delivery was by a method that didn't constitute effective notice until later, or if there was a Prupox Deviation that tolled the period).
3. If the notice was genuinely late, consider whether Prupox can be persuaded to waive the deadline given the scale of the quality issue (23 of 48 plates). This is a commercial negotiation, not a legal right.
4. In the meantime, Staidaix should independently validate the genotype data for the affected plates to quantify the actual impact on its breeding program. If the data is unusable, the commercial cost of re-genotyping 23 plates (~9,168 Samples) at $3.15 each is approximately $28,879, plus the time cost.
5. Preserve the invoice dispute under MSA §4.4 regardless of the deemed-acceptance question.

**Flag for client discussion:** We need to understand why the Nonconformance Notice was 4 Business Days late. Was it a internal process failure at Staidaix? If so, this raises a broader concern about Staidaix's ability to meet the 10-Business-Day deadline for future Batches, particularly as volumes scale up under OF4. We should recommend that Staidaix implement an internal tracking system for Data Release Dates and Nonconformance Notice deadlines.

## Issue 4 – Minimum Annual Commitment: Structure and Risk

**Where:** OF4 §3.1–3.5; OF4 §5.2; MSA §11.2.

**What the documents say:**

- OF4 §3.1: Minimum Annual Commitment of **$1,850,000 per Contract Year** for a three-year term (total commitment: ~$5,550,000).
- OF4 §3.3: Fees with a Data Release Date in a Contract Year count toward the commitment. Service credits do not reduce the amount counted.
- OF4 §3.4: If actual fees fall short, Prupox can invoice the Shortfall Amount within 30 days after year-end.
- OF4 §3.5: No carry-forward of excess fees.
- OF4 §5.2: On termination for Customer's breach, the full remaining commitment accelerates.
- OF4 §5.3: On termination for Provider's breach, the commitment does not apply for the termination year or later.
- MSA §11.2: General liability cap is fees paid/payable in the 12 months before the claim event.

**Why it matters:**

- The $1.85M annual commitment is a significant fixed cost. At the OF4 per-Sample rates (OF4 §4.1), Staidaix would need to process approximately 475,000+ Samples per year (consistent with the forecast in OF4 §1.3) to meet the commitment organically.
- The no-carry-forward rule (§3.5) means that if Staidaix over-delivers in one year, the excess is wasted.
- The acceleration clause (§5.2) converts the commitment from an annual shortfall mechanism into a lump-sum liability upon a Customer-breach termination. This is the financial engine behind the Change of Control risk (Issue 1).
- The liability cap (MSA §11.2) is approximately $1.85M (12 months of fees), which means that even if Staidaix wins a breach claim against Prupox, its recovery is limited to roughly one year's fees. However, the cap does not apply to Prupox's breach of data/germplasm ownership (MSA §11.3(c)), indemnification obligations (§11.3(a)), or gross negligence/fraud (§11.3(d)).

**Recommended fix:**

- In any renegotiation triggered by the Change of Control (Issue 1), Staidaix should seek to:
  - Reduce or eliminate the acceleration clause (§5.2) for Change-of-Control-driven terminations.
  - Add a carry-forward provision (e.g., up to 50% of excess fees in one year can be applied to the next).
  - Clarify that the Shortfall Amount (§3.4) is subject to the liability cap in MSA §11.2 (it currently isn't expressly addressed).
- Staidaix should model its expected Sample volumes for each Contract Year to assess the probability of a shortfall. If volumes are likely to fall below the commitment (e.g., due to breeding program changes post-merger), the commitment should be renegotiated.

## Issue 5 – Post-Merger Affiliate Exclusivity: Potential Dramatic Expansion of Obligations

**Where:** MSA §3.4; MSA §1.1 (definition of "Affiliate"); OF4 §2.1.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §3.4 binds "Customer and its **Affiliates**" to the exclusivity obligation.
- MSA §1.1 defines "Affiliate" as any entity that controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with a Party.
- Post-merger, Fenwyck will be the parent of Staidaix. All of Fenwyck's other subsidiaries will become Affiliates of Staidaix under the MSA definition.

**Why it matters:**

If MSA §3.4 applies as written (see Issue 2 on the scope conflict), then post-merger, **all of Fenwyck's subsidiaries** would be obligated to procure all genotyping, sequencing, and trait-screening services exclusively from Prupox. If Fenwyck has other agricultural businesses with breeding programs (which is likely, given it is an "AgriSciences" company), this could:

- Impose a company-wide exclusivity obligation on Fenwyck that was never negotiated.
- Create a conflict between Fenwyck's existing laboratory relationships and the MSA.
- Give Prupox a leverage point to extract concessions from the combined entity.
- Potentially constitute a breach if any Fenwyck subsidiary continues to use its existing genotyping providers.

Even if OF4 §2.1's narrower scope were to apply (soybean marker-assisted genotyping in the US only), the "Affiliates" language in MSA §3.4 could still pull in Fenwyck's other US-based soybean genotyping activities.

**Recommended fix:**

- In the Change of Control consent negotiation (Issue 1), Staidaix/Fenwyck should seek to:
  - Limit the Affiliate exclusivity obligation to Staidaix and its direct subsidiaries engaged in soybean/corn breeding, excluding Fenwyck's other business units.
  - Alternatively, amend MSA §3.4 to remove the "Affiliates" language or to define a narrower group.
  - At minimum, obtain a written confirmation from Prupox that the exclusivity obligation will not be enforced against Fenwyck entities that are not engaged in the same breeding programs as Staidaix.
- **Flag for client discussion:** We need to understand the corporate structure of Fenwyck and what genotyping/sequencing activities its other subsidiaries currently conduct. This is essential to assessing the scope of the problem.

## Issue 7 – Plate Sealer Firmware Update: Potential Unnoticed Material Change

**Where:** Q3 Notes §4.3; QA §11.1–11.2.

**What the documents say:**

- Q3 Notes §4.3: "PMT gave notice that a firmware update to the plate sealers is planned for November 2026. Provider does not regard it as a material change under Section 11.1 of the Quality Agreement, but will circulate a short summary for GAL's review."
- Action Item 2: Owner PMT, Due October 15, 2026.
- QA §11.1: "Provider shall give Customer at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice of any material change to the validated assay protocols, marker panels, **instrument platforms**, genotype calling software or processing sites used for the Services."
- QA §11.2: Provider must validate each material change before implementing it for Customer Batches.

**Why it matters:**

A firmware update to plate sealers could affect sample integrity (seal quality, evaporation, cross-contamination) and therefore data quality. Prupox has determined it is not a "material change" under QA §11.1, but this is Prupox's unilateral determination. If the update is in fact material (e.g., it changes sealing pressure, temperature profile, or timing), then:

- Prupox was required to give 30 days' prior notice (the update is planned for November 2026; the Q3 meeting was September 10, 2026, so the notice period may be adequate if the update is after mid-October).
- Prupox was required to validate the change before implementing it for Customer Batches (QA §11.2).
- If validation is not performed or is inadequate, and data quality degrades, Staidaix may have a claim under QA §6.2 (Acceptance Criteria) and the SLS data quality service levels.

**Recommended fix:**

- Follow up on Action Item 2 (due October 15, 2026) to confirm the firmware summary was received and reviewed.
- If the summary indicates any change to sealing parameters that could affect DNA integrity or Sample Failure Rate, request that Prupox treat it as a material change under QA §11.1 and provide validation data (concordance with pre-update Batches on Customer check lines, per QA §11.2).
- Monitor the Sample Failure Rate and Concordance Rate for Batches processed after the November 2026 update. If there is any degradation, raise a Nonconformance Notice within the 10-Business-Day window (see Issue 3 lessons learned).

## Issue 8 – Capacity Guarantee Threshold: 120% of Forecast

**Where:** MSA §3.5; OF4 §2.3; SLS §5(c).

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §3.5: Provider shall maintain capacity sufficient for forecast volumes, "provided that actual volumes do not exceed one hundred twenty percent (120%) of the most recent forecast."
- SLS §5(c): Provider is not responsible for Service Level failures to the extent caused by "Batch volumes above the capacity limits in Section 3.5 of the Agreement."
- OF4 §1.3: First-year forecast is approximately 410,000 soybean + 65,000 corn = ~475,000 Samples.

**Why it matters:**

The capacity guarantee (and the associated Service Level protections) only applies if actual volumes stay within 120% of the most recent forecast. 120% of 475,000 is ~570,000 Samples. If Staidaix's breeding programs expand post-merger (e.g., Fenwyck integrates additional lines into the Staidaix program), volumes could exceed this threshold, and:

- Prupox would no longer be contractually required to maintain sufficient capacity.
- Service Level failures due to volume overruns would be excluded (SLS §5(c)).
- Turnaround times could slip without triggering service credits.

**Recommended fix:**

- Ensure that rolling quarterly forecasts (MSA §2.3) are updated promptly if volumes are expected to increase, so the 120% threshold moves up.
- In any renegotiation, seek to raise the threshold (e.g., 130% or 150%) or to tie it to the Minimum Annual Commitment rather than the forecast.
- If the Fenwyck merger is expected to increase Sample volumes, notify Prupox in the next forecast and confirm capacity.

## Issue 9 – Service Level Performance: Generally Good, but Monitor the July Exception

**Where:** Q3 Notes §3.2–3.4; SLS §4.1; SLS §4.6.

**What the documents say:**

- June–August 2026 performance was generally strong: all data quality metrics met or exceeded Service Levels; portal availability was above 99.5% in all three months.
- One July corn batch (ZeaSelect-192) was 2 Business Days late due to a scanner fault. A 5% Turnaround Time credit was applied (SLS §4.1).
- No chronic failures under SLS §4.6 (which requires the same Service Level to be missed in 3 of 6 rolling months to constitute a material breach).

**Why it matters:**

Performance is currently acceptable. However:

- The scanner fault is a hardware/reliability issue that could recur. If it affects multiple Batches in a rolling 6-month period, it could trigger the chronic failure provision (SLS §4.6) and give Staidaix a termination right under MSA §12.2.
- The 5% credit is modest. For a batch of, say, 5,000 corn Samples at $2.85 each, the credit is only ~$712. The real cost to Staidaix is the 2-day delay in its breeding timeline.
- As volumes scale under OF4, the probability of isolated delays increases.

**Recommended fix:**

- No immediate action required. Track monthly reports (SLS §3.2) for any pattern of late Batches.
- If the same Service Level is missed in 2 of the next 6 months, be prepared to invoke SLS §4.6 (chronic failure = material breach) as leverage in any renegotiation.
- In the Q4 2026 quarterly review (scheduled for December 2026 per Q3 Notes §9), confirm that the scanner fault was addressed and request a CAPA summary.

- In the Change of Control negotiation (Issue 1), consider whether the parties should align the MSA term with the post-merger strategic plan.

## Issue 11 – Liability Cap Is Low Relative to Staidaix's Exposure

**Where:** MSA §11.1–11.3.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §11.1: Mutual exclusion of indirect, consequential, special, and punitive damages, and lost profits/revenue.
- MSA §11.2: Cap on total liability at fees paid/payable in the 12 months before the claim event (~$1.85M based on the MAC).
- MSA §11.3: Carve-outs for indemnification (§10), confidentiality breach (§8), Provider's breach of sample/data ownership (§3.3, §7.2) or loss of Customer Germplasm, gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and Customer's payment obligations.

**Why it matters:**

- If Prupox breaches a Service Level or the Quality Agreement (other than the carved-out provisions), Staidaix's recovery is capped at ~$1.85M.
- The exclusion of lost profits is significant for a breeding company: if bad genotype data leads to the wrong selection decisions, the cost is in lost breeding progress, which is precisely the kind of "lost profit" or "consequential damage" that §11.1 excludes.
- The carve-outs for Germplasm and data ownership (§11.3(c)) are important and should be protected.
- The cap is mutual, so Staidaix's liability to Prupox (e.g., for the MAC shortfall or accelerated commitment) is also theoretically capped at 12 months of fees. However, OF4 §5.2 (acceleration) is a payment obligation, which is carved out of the cap by MSA §11.3(e). So the acceleration is **not** subject to the cap.

**Recommended fix:**

- The cap structure is standard for this type of agreement and difficult to change. However, in any renegotiation:
  - Seek to increase the cap to 24 months of fees or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $3M).
  - Clarify that the cap does not apply to Prupox's obligation to perform Re-runs under QA §8.2 (this is arguably a service obligation, not a damages claim, but the interaction is unclear).
  - Ensure the Germplasm/data ownership carve-out (§11.3(c)) is preserved in any amendment.

## Issue 12 – Order Form No. 3 Transition: Lot SG-26-117 Governed by Expired Order Form

**Where:** OF4 §9.3; OF4 §6.

**What the documents say:**

- OF4 §9.3: "Order Form No. 3 continues to govern Services for Batches received by Provider before the Order Form Effective Date, including Lot SG-26-117."
- OF4 §6 addresses Lot SG-26-117 in the context of OF4, including the invoice dispute and the parties' positions.

**Why it matters:**

There is a structural tension: OF4 §9.3 says OF3 governs Lot SG-26-117, but OF4 §6 sets out the parties' positions on the lot and the invoice dispute within OF4 itself. This could create ambiguity about which Order Form's terms (pricing, service levels, termination provisions) apply to the lot.

In practice, this is likely not a significant issue because:
- The SLS and QA apply across all Order Forms.
- The MSA applies to both.
- The per-Sample fee for SoyCore-384 is likely the same under OF3 and OF4 (we don't have OF3 to confirm).

However, if the dispute escalates, Prupox could argue that OF3's terms (which we don't have) govern, potentially with different pricing or commitment structures.

**Recommended fix:**

- Obtain a copy of Order Form No. 3 for the file.
- In the Lot SG-26-117 resolution, confirm in writing which Order Form's terms govern the fee and any credit or refund.
