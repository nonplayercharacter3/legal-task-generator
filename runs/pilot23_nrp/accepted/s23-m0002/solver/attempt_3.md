## I. CRITICAL ISSUES REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ATTENTION

### 1. Lot SG-26-117 – Nonconformance Notice Likely Untimely; Batch May Be Deemed Accepted

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 7.2; Order Form No. 4 § 6.1–6.2; Service Level Schedule § 1 (definition of "Data Release Date")

**Facts.** Lot SG-26-117 is a Batch of 18,432 soybean Samples (SoyCore-384). The Data Release Date was **September 14, 2026** (Order Form No. 4, § 6.1). Staidaix's Nonconformance Notice is dated **October 2, 2026** (Order Form No. 4, § 6.1). That is 18 calendar days, or approximately 12–14 Business Days (depending on federal holidays in the period), after the Data Release Date.

**The problem.** Quality Agreement § 7.2 provides:

> "Customer shall give Provider a Nonconformance Notice for a Batch within **ten (10) Business Days** after the Data Release Date for that Batch. If Customer does not give a Nonconformance Notice for a Batch within that period, the Batch and its Deliverables are **deemed accepted**, and Customer will have **no right to a Re-run, replacement data, service credit** under Section 4.2 of the Service Level Schedule, **or refund** in respect of that Batch."

On the plain language, the October 2 notice is outside the 10-Business-Day window. Provider has taken this position (Order Form No. 4, § 6.2). Staidaix disputes it and has reserved its rights, but a reservation of rights in a commercial document does not override a contractual deemed-acceptance clause.

**Why it matters.** This is Staidaix's largest single Batch to date (Meeting Notes § 5.1). The reported failures affect 23 of 48 plates (Call Rate and Concordance Rate below Service Levels). If the Batch is deemed accepted, Staidaix loses:
- the right to a no-charge Re-run (Quality Agreement § 8.2);
- the 10% data quality service credit (Service Level Schedule § 4.2);
- any refund for affected plates (Quality Agreement § 8.4);
- and the extended sample retention that would preserve evidence (Quality Agreement § 9.3).

Staidaix has also disputed the invoice under MSA § 4.4 (Order Form No. 4, § 6.3), which is procedurally proper, but the substantive right to withhold may be undermined if the Batch is deemed accepted.

**Recommended fix.**
- **Immediately** confirm the exact date the final data was posted to the Customer Portal. The Service Level Schedule defines Data Release Date as the date of posting, "regardless of when the notice is sent or opened" (§ 2.4). If the portal timestamp shows a date later than September 14, the 10-day clock may not have started when Staidaix believes. Pull the portal logs.
- If the Data Release Date is confirmed as September 14, assess whether there is any basis to argue the notice was timely (e.g., the Data Release notice under § 2.4 was not actually sent, or was sent with incorrect Batch identification, giving Staidaix a later actual-knowledge date). This is a weak argument given the express "regardless of when the notice is sent" language, but worth exploring.
- If the notice was in fact late, consider whether Provider's own conduct (e.g., failure to send the § 2.4 notice, or a Deviation under Quality Agreement § 6.4 that Provider should have disclosed) provides an equitable or estoppel-based argument.
- As a commercial fallback, negotiate a resolution: a partial re-run of the 23 affected plates at reduced or no cost, in exchange for Staidaix withdrawing the deemed-acceptance dispute. Document any agreement in a written amendment.
- **Do not** allow the extended retention period to lapse. Quality Agreement § 9.3 extends retention until 60 days after the matter is "closed." Keep the matter formally open until resolved to preserve the DNA and tissue.

**Flag for client discussion:** We need to understand *why* the notice was 4+ Business Days late. Was it a internal process failure? If so, Staidaix should implement a tracking protocol for Data Release Dates going forward, particularly for large Batches.

### 2. Change of Control – Fenwyck Merger Triggers Consent Requirement; Failure to Obtain Consent Risks Termination and Full Commitment Acceleration

**Where:** MSA §§ 14.1, 14.2, 12.3; Order Form No. 4 Recital B, §§ 5.2, 9.2

**Facts.** Staidaix Holdings, Inc. (Staidaix's parent) signed a Merger Agreement with Fenwyck AgriSciences Corp. on October 12, 2026. Closing is expected in Q1 2027 (Order Form No. 4, Recital B).

**The problem.** MSA § 14.2 provides:

> "A Change of Control of Customer is **deemed an assignment** of this Agreement and each Order Form by Customer and **requires Provider's prior written consent** under Section 14.1. … Provider may **withhold or condition its consent in its discretion**."

MSA § 12.3 provides that if Customer assigns in breach of § 14, Provider may terminate immediately, and the termination "is a termination for Customer's breach for all purposes."

Order Form No. 4 § 5.2 then accelerates the **entire remaining Minimum Annual Commitment** on a termination for Customer's breach:

> "the unpaid Minimum Annual Commitment for the then-current Contract Year and **each remaining Contract Year** of the Order Term … becomes **immediately due and payable**."

With a $1,850,000 annual commitment over 3 years (Order Form No. 4, § 3.1), the maximum acceleration exposure is approximately **$5,550,000** (less Fees already invoiced and counted in the then-current year).

Critically, Order Form No. 4 § 9.2 states: "Nothing in this Order Form constitutes Provider's consent under any provision of the Agreement, or a waiver by either Party of any right or remedy, **except as expressly stated** in this Order Form." The acknowledgment of the merger in Recital B is **not** an express consent. It is background only.

**Why it matters.** If the Fenwyck merger closes without Provider's written consent:
- Provider can terminate the MSA and all Order Forms immediately (§ 12.3);
- The termination is treated as for Staidaix's breach;
- The full remaining Minimum Annual Commitment accelerates (§ 5.2);
- Staidaix loses access to all Services, including the GlyMax 6K array (see Issue 6 below);
- Staidaix's germplasm and data are subject to the wind-down provisions of § 12.5, but there is a 30-day window for data delivery (§ 12.5(c)).

**Recommended fix.**
- **Before the merger closes**, Staidaix must obtain Provider's written consent to the Change of Control under § 14.2. The MSA requires notice "as soon as reasonably practicable and, where lawful, before it occurs."
- In the consent negotiation, Staidaix should seek:
  - Unconditional consent (or consent conditioned only on the successor assuming the Agreement in writing);
  - A waiver or reduction of the § 5.2 acceleration if consent is later revoked or if the transaction structure changes;
  - Confirmation that the exclusivity scope will be narrowed (see Issue 3) as a condition of consent, since post-merger the "Affiliate" definition will capture Fenwyck's other entities.
- If Provider withholds consent, Staidaix should evaluate whether to (a) restructure the transaction to avoid a Change of Control of Staidaix Seed Genetics specifically (e.g., if Fenwyck acquires only the parent and Staidaix Seed Genetics remains a separate subsidiary, a Change of Control still occurs under § 1.1(d) because "control of that entity changes indirectly"), or (b) negotiate a termination with a capped buyout rather than full acceleration.

**Flag for client discussion:**
- What is the expected closing date? If it is before we can secure consent, we need a contingency plan.
- Has Staidaix given Provider the § 14.2 notice yet? If not, do it immediately.
- Does Staidaix want to make consent a condition to closing the merger, or is it willing to risk termination?
- Post-merger, will Fenwyck's other subsidiaries be conducting genotyping? If so, the exclusivity issue (below) becomes even more acute.

### 3. Exclusivity Scope – MSA § 3.4 Is Far Broader Than Order Form No. 4 § 2.1, and the MSA Prevails Under the Order of Precedence

**Where:** MSA §§ 1.3, 3.4; Order Form No. 4 §§ 2.1, 2.2, 9.2

**The problem.** MSA § 3.4 provides:

> "During the term of this Agreement, Customer and its Affiliates shall procure **all genotyping, sequencing and trait-screening services for any crop species in any territory** exclusively from Provider, and shall not engage any third-party laboratory or service provider, or perform those services internally, except with Provider's prior written consent."

Order Form No. 4 § 2.1 narrows the scope:

> "The Parties agree that the exclusive services scope for this Order Form is: **Soybean marker-assisted genotyping services performed for Customer in the United States**."

These provisions are in direct conflict. Under MSA § 1.3 (Order of Precedence), the MSA prevails over an Order Form **unless** the Order Form "expressly identifies, by section number, the provision of this Agreement that it is intended to override." Order Form No. 4 § 2.1 does not cite MSA § 3.4. Therefore, **the broader MSA exclusivity controls.**

**Why it matters.**
- The MSA exclusivity covers **all crops** (soybean, corn, and any future crop), **all territories** (global), and **all of Customer's Affiliates**.
- The Order Form's "Exclusive Services" definition (soybean, US only) governs only the § 2.2 right-to-quote procedure and § 2.3 capacity reservation. It does **not** limit the underlying § 3.4 obligation.
- Post-merger, "Affiliate" (MSA § 1.1) will include Fenwyck and its other subsidiaries (entities under common control with Staidaix). This could mean **Fenwyck's entire global genotyping operation** is locked into Prupox.
- Staidaix is also prohibited from performing genotyping **internally** without Provider's consent, which constrains its own R&D flexibility.

**Recommended fix.**
- Negotiate an amendment to MSA § 3.4 (or a new Order Form provision that expressly identifies § 3.4 as overridden) to align the exclusivity scope with what was commercially agreed: soybean marker-assisted genotyping in the United States.
- If the broader exclusivity was intentional (e.g., Prupox gave up pricing in exchange for global, all-crop exclusivity), Staidaix needs to understand the commercial trade-off and decide whether it is acceptable, particularly post-merger.
- In the Change of Control consent negotiation (Issue 2), make narrowing the exclusivity scope a condition of consent.

**Flag for client discussion:**
- Was the broad MSA exclusivity a deliberate commercial term, or a drafting oversight that the Order Form was meant to fix?
- Post-merger, what genotyping does Fenwyck currently perform internally or through other labs? If significant, the MSA exclusivity as written creates a major operational constraint on the combined entity.
- Does Staidaix intend to expand into new crops or geographies where it would need its own genotyping capability?

## II. SIGNIFICANT COMMERCIAL AND OPERATIONAL ISSUES

### 4. Minimum Annual Commitment – Asymmetric Termination Economics

**Where:** Order Form No. 4 §§ 3.1, 3.4, 5.2, 5.3

**The issue.** The 3-year commitment totals $5,550,000. The termination economics are asymmetric:
- If **Provider** breaches and Staidaix terminates (§ 5.3): the commitment falls away for the current and all remaining years. Staidaix walks free.
- If **Staidaix** breaches and Provider terminates (§ 5.2): the full remaining commitment accelerates immediately.

Combined with the Change of Control deemed-assignment rule (Issue 2), Staidaix's largest termination risk is not a performance failure but a corporate transaction.

**Recommended fix.**
- In the Change of Control consent, negotiate that a consented Change of Control will not trigger § 5.2 acceleration, or that the acceleration will be capped at a reasonable breakage fee (e.g., 6–12 months of the commitment).
- Consider whether the 3-year term is appropriate given the pending merger. If the merger closes in Q1 2027, Staidaix will have committed to ~2.75 years of service under a corporate structure that no longer exists.

### 5. Auto-Renewal – 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice Deadline

**Where:** MSA § 12.1

**The issue.** The MSA has a 5-year initial term (March 1, 2025 – March 1, 2030) and then renews automatically for successive 1-year periods unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least **180 days** before the end of the then-current term. The first non-renewal notice deadline is approximately **September 2, 2029**.

**Why it matters.** If Staidaix misses this deadline, it is locked in for another year. Given the pending merger and the exclusivity issues, Staidaix may want the flexibility to exit or renegotiate at the end of the initial term.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the September 2, 2029 deadline (and subsequent annual deadlines) immediately. Build the decision into the post-merger integration planning.

### 6. Loss of Access to Prupox GlyMax 6K Array on Termination

**Where:** MSA §§ 7.1, 7.5; Order Form No. 4 § 1.1

**The issue.** The GlyMax 6K mid-density soybean array is "Provider Technology" (MSA § 1.1) and remains Provider's property (§ 7.1). Staidaix has no license to use it outside the Agreement. If the relationship terminates, Staidaix loses access to this array immediately.

**Why it matters.** Staidaix's breeding program is built around specific marker panels. Switching to a different 6K array would require:
- re-validation of marker effects on the new platform;
- re-genotyping of reference populations;
- potential loss of historical data comparability.

This creates significant switching costs and gives Provider leverage in any renegotiation.

**Recommended fix.**
- Assess whether Staidaix has alternative 6K soybean arrays available (e.g., from other providers) and what the validation timeline would be.
- In the Change of Control consent or a renegotiation, seek a transition license: the right to use the GlyMax 6K array for a defined period (e.g., 12–24 months) post-termination at agreed rates, to allow for an orderly transition.
- Ensure that all historical data generated on the GlyMax 6K is delivered in full at termination (MSA § 12.5(c); Order Form No. 4 § 7.2 includes raw intensity files).

### 7. Capacity Limit at 120% of Forecast

**Where:** MSA § 3.5; Service Level Schedule § 5(c)

**The issue.** Provider's obligation to meet turnaround times applies only if actual volumes do not exceed 120% of the most recent forecast. Above that threshold, Provider is excused from Service Level failures (Service Level Schedule § 5(c)) and has no capacity obligation.

**Why it matters.** Staidaix is locked into exclusivity (Issue 3) but Provider can effectively refuse to meet SLAs if volumes spike. If Staidaix has a breeding deadline and submits more than 120% of forecast, it has no contractual remedy for delayed data and may not be able to use another lab (due to exclusivity).

**Recommended fix.**
- Maintain accurate and conservative forecasts (MSA § 2.3 requires rolling quarterly forecasts 15 days before each quarter).
- Negotiate a higher capacity threshold (e.g., 150%) or a mechanism for Staidaix to give advance notice of volume increases and have Provider confirm capacity in writing.
- Clarify that the MSA § 15.5 force majeure carve-out (which allows Staidaix to use another provider if delay exceeds 30 days) also applies to capacity-related delays.

### 8. Plate Sealer Firmware Update – Provider's "Not Material" Characterization

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 11.1; Meeting Notes § 4.3, Action Item 2

**The issue.** Provider announced (September 10, 2026) a planned firmware update to plate sealers for November 2026. Provider does not regard it as a "material change" under Quality Agreement § 11.1, which requires 30 days' prior written notice of changes to "validated assay protocols, marker panels, **instrument platforms**, genotype calling software or processing sites."

A firmware update to plate sealers arguably modifies an "instrument platform." If it is material, Provider needed 30 days' notice (i.e., by early October 2026 for a November update) and must validate the change before implementing it for Customer Batches (§ 11.2).

**Why it matters.** Plate sealers affect well-to-well cross-contamination and sample integrity. A firmware change could alter sealing pressure, timing, or temperature profiles. If the change causes data quality issues (e.g., increased Sample Failure Rate or reduced Concordance Rate), Staidaix would want to have objected to the change beforehand.

**Recommended fix.**
- Hold Provider to Action Item 2: receive the firmware summary by October 15, 2026.
- Review the summary with Dr. Voskuijlen and Gareth Lindqvist. If the update changes any parameter that affects sealing performance, push back on the "not material" characterization and request:
  - 30 days' formal notice under § 11.1;
  - a validation summary under § 11.2 showing concordance with prior process on Staidaix check lines;
  - the right to reject the update for Staidaix Batches if validation is unsatisfactory.
- Document Staidaix's position in writing to create a record if data quality issues arise after the update.

### 9. Expedited Volume Cap at 15% per Quarter

**Where:** Order Form No. 4 § 4.3

**The issue.** Expedited Batches may not exceed 15% of Customer's Samples in any calendar quarter without Provider's written agreement.

**Why it matters.** In a breeding program, there are often compressed windows (e.g., pre-planting genotyping, rapid backcross confirmation). If Staidaix needs to expedite more than 15% of a quarter's volume, it must get Provider's agreement, which Provider can withhold or condition.

**Recommended fix.**
- Assess historical expedited usage. If Staidaix regularly exceeds 15% in peak quarters, negotiate a higher cap or a pre-agreed expedited allocation for specific quarters.
- Build the 15% cap into forecasting and scheduling so that expedited needs are planned in advance.
