## I. CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. IP Assignment Agreement Excludes Trade Secrets, Know-How, Process Parameters, and Software

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Recycling Process Technology"); SPA § 6.6; SPA § 11.4; LOI § 3(b)

**Issue:** The SPA defines "Recycling Process Technology" broadly to include "patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software used in the Company's hydrometallurgical metal recovery" (SPA § 1.1). Section 6.6 covenants that Seller assign "all Recycling Process Technology" to the Company via the IP Assignment Agreement. However, the IP Assignment Agreement redefines "Recycling Process Technology" in § 1.1 to mean only "the Patents listed on Exhibit A." Section 2.1(a) then assigns "the Recycling Process Technology" using that narrow definition. Trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software are not assigned anywhere in the IP Assignment Agreement.

This is compounded by SPA § 11.4 (Order of Precedence), which provides that "each Ancillary Agreement controls as to its own subject matter." Because the IP Assignment Agreement is the ancillary agreement governing IP, its narrower definition likely controls over the SPA's broader definition.

The LOI § 3(b) confirms the parties' original intent: "assign to the Company, by an intellectual property assignment agreement, all patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software owned by Seller or its Affiliates."

**Why it matters:** For a hydrometallurgical battery recycling business, the process know-how (leach chemistry, solvent extraction parameters, precipitation conditions, process optimization data) is often more valuable than the patents. Without an assignment of trade secrets and process parameters, Mouloul would own the Company but the Company would lack the operational knowledge needed to run the business. Seller could potentially retain or re-use that knowledge for a competing venture (subject to the non-compete, but the non-compete is only 5 years and covers "lithium-ion battery recycling or black-mass processing" — not adjacent applications).

**Recommended fix:** Amend IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 to adopt the SPA's broader definition of "Recycling Process Technology" (patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software). Add a specific assignment clause covering trade secrets, technical data, process documentation, software source code and object code, and any other intangible information used in the Business. Consider adding a representation that Assignor has identified and scheduled all such items. Alternatively, add a separate schedule to the IP Assignment Agreement listing all trade secrets and know-how being assigned.

### 2. Environmental Termination Right Has Already Expired

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(e); LOI § 6(c)

**Issue:** SPA § 9.1(e) gives Buyer the right to terminate if the Phase II Assessment reports estimated Remediation Costs exceeding $1,500,000, but only if notice is delivered "no later than forty-five (45) days after the date of the Letter of Intent." The LOI is dated February 9, 2026. Forty-five days after that date is March 26, 2026. The SPA itself is dated May 11, 2026 — nearly seven weeks after the deadline has passed.

The LOI § 6(c) contemplated a different (and more reasonable) deadline: "no later than thirty (30) days following execution of the Definitive Agreement" (i.e., 30 days after May 11, 2026 = June 10, 2026). The SPA deviated from the LOI in a way that is materially adverse to Buyer.

**Why it matters:** As of the SPA signing date, Buyer appears to have already forfeited its right to walk away from the deal based on unacceptable environmental conditions at the Facility. Given that the Facility handles electrolyte solvents, lithium hexafluorophosphate, heavy metals, process liquors, leach residues, and black mass (all defined as Hazardous Materials in SPA § 1.1), the environmental risk is significant. Without the termination right, Buyer's only recourse for pre-closing contamination is the indemnity in § 8.2(d) (subject to the $648,000 basket and $8,640,000 cap in § 8.4) and the 5-year survival of the § 4.9 environmental representation.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SPA § 9.1(e) to restore the LOI's deadline: "no later than thirty (30) days after the Closing Date" or "no later than thirty (30) days following execution of this Agreement." If Seller resists, consider extending to 60 days after the SPA date to allow time for the Phase II to be completed. This should be negotiated before the SPA is treated as final.

**Flag:** I cannot confirm from the documents whether a Phase II Assessment has already been performed or whether notice was given before March 26, 2026. If the Phase II was completed and notice was timely given, this issue is moot. Please confirm the status of the Phase II.

## II. SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 3. Bill of Sale Schedule 1 – Asset Identification Incomplete

**Where:** Bill of Sale, Schedule 1

**Issue:** Every row in Schedule 1 has blank fields ([●]) for Manufacturer/Model and Serial Number. The schedule lists 14 categories of equipment by description only (e.g., "Primary twin-shaft shredder, with hydraulic power unit") without identifying the specific units.

**Why it matters:** Without serial numbers and model identifiers, there is no objective way to verify at closing that the correct equipment has been conveyed. If Seller later claims that a particular unit was not part of the Shredding Line Assets, or if a unit fails and the Company discovers it was not the model represented, the lack of identification creates dispute risk. The representation in Bill of Sale § 4.1 (good title, free of liens) is only as useful as the ability to identify the assets to which it applies.

**Recommended fix:** Require Seller to complete Schedule 1 with manufacturer, model, and serial number for each item before closing. If serial numbers are genuinely unavailable for some items (e.g., the spare parts inventory in Item 14), require a detailed inventory list with quantities, specifications, and condition. Consider making completion of Schedule 1 a closing condition under SPA § 7.2(f).

### 4. IP Assignment Agreement Exhibit A – Patent Numbers Not Filled In

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement, Exhibit A

**Issue:** All six listed patents/applications have blank patent numbers, filing dates, and (for the PCT application) publication numbers. The record owner is listed as "Porert Industrial Holdings, LLC" for all six.

**Why it matters:** Without patent numbers, we cannot verify: (a) that the patents actually exist and are maintained; (b) that Porert is the true record owner (as opposed to the Company or an affiliate); (c) whether there are any liens or security interests on the patents; or (d) the scope of claims. The representation in IP Assignment § 5.1(d) that all maintenance fees have been paid is unverifiable without patent numbers. Additionally, the fact that all patents are recorded in Porert's name (not the Company's) confirms that the Company has been operating without owning its core IP — making the assignment at closing even more critical.

**Recommended fix:** Require Seller to complete Exhibit A with all patent/application numbers, filing dates, and current status before closing. Conduct a USPTO (and foreign office) search to verify ownership, maintenance fee status, and absence of liens. Consider making delivery of a completed Exhibit A a closing condition.

### 5. Transition Services Agreement – Provider's Unilateral Right to Delegate

**Where:** TSA § 12.3

**Issue:** "Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate any of its obligations under it, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient." Recipient, by contrast, may not assign without Provider's consent.

**Why it matters:** The Services include environmentally sensitive and operationally critical functions: process control system support (Schedule A, Service 2), environmental compliance reporting (Service 3), laboratory and assay services (Service 4), and feedstock logistics including DOT hazardous materials shipping documentation (Service 5). If Seller delegates these to an unknown third party without quality controls or Recipient's approval, the Company's operations, regulatory compliance, and safety could be compromised. Post-closing, Seller has reduced incentive to maintain service quality, and the delegation right gives Seller an easy exit from performance obligations while retaining the fee revenue.

**Recommended fix:** Amend TSA § 12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment or delegation. Alternatively, permit delegation only to Provider's Affiliates and only with notice to Recipient. Add a requirement that any delegatee assume the same obligations and be bound by the same Service Standard.

### 6. Transition Services Agreement – Governing Law Inconsistent with SPA

**Where:** TSA § 12.7; SPA § 11.9; SPA § 11.4

**Issue:** The TSA is governed by Texas law with exclusive jurisdiction in Harris County, Texas. The SPA, Bill of Sale, and IP Assignment Agreement are all governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. SPA § 11.4 provides that each Ancillary Agreement "controls as to its own subject matter," which could be read to validate the different governing law, but the practical effect is that disputes involving the intersection of the SPA and TSA (e.g., whether a TSA breach constitutes a SPA covenant breach triggering indemnification under SPA Article 8) could be subject to conflicting choice-of-law analyses.

**Why it matters:** Creates litigation complexity and potential for inconsistent outcomes. If the Company needs to sue Seller for both a TSA breach and a SPA indemnification claim, the claims may need to be brought in different forums under different laws.

**Recommended fix:** Conform TSA § 12.7 to New York law and New York County jurisdiction, consistent with the SPA and the other Ancillary Agreements. If Seller insists on Texas law (perhaps because its personnel and systems are in Texas), at minimum add a provision that the TSA is interpreted consistently with the SPA and that any dispute involving both agreements shall be brought in the forum specified in the SPA.

### 7. Transition Services Agreement Schedule A – Fees Not Specified

**Where:** TSA, Schedule A

**Issue:** All seven monthly Service Fees are blank ([●]).

**Why it matters:** The Company (now Mouloul's subsidiary) will be paying these fees for 9 months post-closing. Without knowing the amounts, Mouloul cannot assess the total cost of the transition or budget for it. The at-cost standard in TSA § 5.1 provides a framework but not a number. The audit right in § 5.5 (once during the term, once within 6 months after) is helpful but reactive.

**Recommended fix:** Require completion of Schedule A with specific monthly fees before closing. If Seller resists fixed fees, agree on a not-to-exceed cap for the total 9-month Service Fees and a mechanism for true-up.

### 8. Insurance – Mouloul Not Named as Additional Insured

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 2.3

**Issue:** The broker confirms that Mouloul is listed only as "certificate holder" and has "not [been] added as an additional insured or loss payee under any policy."

**Why it matters:** Post-closing, Mouloul will be the sole shareholder of the Company. While the Company is a separate legal entity, Mouloul could face: (a) derivative or direct liability for the Company's operations (e.g., if a court pierces the corporate veil); (b) loss of the investment if a major environmental or liability event destroys the Company; and (c) practical exposure if the Company's insurance is insufficient. Being a mere certificate holder provides no direct right to make claims under the policies.

**Recommended fix:** Before closing, request that Mouloul be added as an additional insured under the CGL, Auto, Umbrella, and Pollution Legal Liability policies. At minimum, request that Mouloul be added as a loss payee under the Property policy. If the insurers require the Company's instruction (as the broker notes), ensure the Company (under Mouloul's control post-closing) provides that instruction. Alternatively, negotiate a covenant in the SPA requiring Seller to use commercially reasonable efforts to have Mouloul added as additional insured before closing.

### 9. Insurance – Five-Year Loss Runs Not Yet Delivered

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 4.1

**Issue:** The broker states that loss runs "have been requested from the carriers" and will be provided "once they are received, if the Company authorizes us to do so." They have not been delivered as of the letter date (May 12, 2026).

**Why it matters:** Without loss runs, Mouloul cannot assess the Company's claims history, identify patterns of losses, evaluate whether the current insurance program is adequate, or determine whether any insurer might be inclined to non-renew post-closing. For a facility handling hazardous materials, a history of environmental or property claims could signal underlying operational problems.

**Recommended fix:** Make delivery of complete 5-year loss runs a condition to closing (or at minimum, require them before the Closing Date). Add to SPA § 7.2 as a Buyer condition, or require delivery as part of the closing deliveries under § 3.2(a).

## III. MODERATE ISSUES

### 10. SPA § 8.1 – IP Representation Survival Period May Be Inadequate

**Where:** SPA § 8.1; SPA § 4.7

**Issue:** The IP representation in § 4.7 (that the Recycling Process Technology constitutes all IP necessary to conduct the Business, and that the Business does not infringe third-party IP) survives for only 18 months (the general survival period). It is not a "Fundamental Representation" (which are limited to §§ 4.1, 4.2, and 4.13) and does not benefit from the 5-year environmental survival.

**Why it matters:** IP infringement claims or discovery that critical know-how was not transferred may not surface within 18 months. Patent challenges, third-party infringement claims, or discovery that a key process parameter was not documented could emerge later. Given Issue #1 above (the IP Assignment Agreement's narrow scope), the risk of an incomplete IP transfer is elevated, and 18 months may not be enough time to discover the gap.

**Recommended fix:** Extend survival of § 4.7 to at least 3 years, or add it to the list of Fundamental Representations (which survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations). If Seller resists, consider a specific indemnity for IP infringement claims that is not subject to the general cap.

### 11. SPA § 6.10 – Post-Closing Insurance Covenant Uses "Commercially Reasonable Efforts" Standard

**Where:** SPA § 6.10

**Issue:** "After the Closing, Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to give the Company the benefit of those policies for pre-Closing occurrences." This is an efforts standard, not a guarantee.

**Why it matters:** If an insurer cancels, non-renews, or modifies a policy post-closing (which is within the insurer's rights at the end of the policy period on January 1, 2027), Seller may argue it used "commercially reasonable efforts" but was unable to secure continued coverage. The Company could then be without pollution legal liability coverage for a facility that handles hazardous materials, with no clear remedy against Seller.

**Recommended fix:** Strengthen to: "Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to [and shall be liable for Losses resulting from its failure to] give the Company the benefit of those policies for pre-Closing occurrences." Alternatively, require Seller to procure a tail policy or extended reporting period for the pollution legal liability coverage at Seller's expense.

### 12. Landlord Estoppel – No Guaranty of the Lease

**Where:** Landlord Estoppel § 3.3

**Issue:** "Porert has not guaranteed the Lease. No guaranty of the Lease by any Person is in effect."

**Why it matters:** The Company is solely liable for the lease obligations (base rent of $118,750/month plus ~$21,400/month additional rent = ~$140,150/month, or approximately $1.68 million annually). Post-closing, Mouloul as parent may be asked by the landlord for a guaranty, particularly given the change of control. The absence of an existing guaranty means the Company's credit stands alone. This is not a defect in the documents per se, but Mouloul should be aware of the ongoing lease commitment and whether the landlord might request a parent guaranty in the future (the estoppel consent in § 3.4 is limited to this Transaction only).

**Recommended fix:** No immediate fix required, but Mouloul should budget for the lease obligations and be prepared for a potential landlord request for a parent guaranty at the first lease renewal or option exercise. Confirm that the two 5-year options (§ 2.3) do not require a landlord consent or credit re-underwriting.

---

### 13. SPA § 4.9 – Environmental Representation Lookback Period

**Where:** SPA § 4.9

**Issue:** The representation covers compliance "since January 1, 2021" — approximately 5.3 years before the SPA date.

**Why it matters:** For a facility that has been operating since at least 2021 (the lease commenced June 1, 2021), this lookback appears to cover the entire operational history. However, if the Facility or the underlying real property was used for other purposes before 2021 (e.g., industrial manufacturing with potential contamination), pre-2021 conditions are not covered by the representation. The Phase II Assessment should help identify such conditions, but the representation does not address them.

**Recommended fix:** If the Phase II identifies pre-2021 contamination, rely on § 8.2(d) (indemnity for pre-Closing Releases) and the general indemnity. Consider adding a specific representation that, to Seller's knowledge, there has been no Release of Hazardous Materials at the Facility at any time (not just since January 1, 2021). If Seller resists, the Phase II and the § 8.2(d) indemnity should provide adequate protection.
