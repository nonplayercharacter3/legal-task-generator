## Issue 1 – CRITICAL: Environmental Termination Right Deadline Has Already Expired

**Where:** SPA §9.1(e); LOI §6(b)–(c)

**Problem.** SPA §9.1(e) gives Buyer the right to terminate if the Phase II Assessment reports estimated Remediation Costs exceeding $1,500,000, but requires notice "no later than forty-five (45) days after the date of the Letter of Intent." The LOI is dated **February 9, 2026**. Forty-five days after that date is **March 26, 2026**. The SPA itself is dated **May 11, 2026**. The termination deadline therefore expired nearly seven weeks before the SPA was signed. Buyer has no enforceable environmental walk-away right under the SPA as drafted.

The LOI contemplated a different structure: §6(b) provides a "30-day environmental review period following execution of the definitive stock purchase agreement," and §6(c) ties the termination notice to "thirty (30) days following execution of the Definitive Agreement." The SPA deviated from the LOI by anchoring the deadline to the LOI date rather than the SPA date, producing an already-lapsed right.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The entire business is built on hydrometallurgical processing of hazardous materials at a leased facility. If the Phase II Assessment (which Buyer is still entitled to conduct under SPA §6.2) reveals contamination exceeding $1.5 million in remediation costs, Buyer would be locked into the transaction with no contractual exit. Buyer's only protection would be the indemnity in §8.2(d) for pre-Closing Releases, which is subject to the general exclusive-remedy provision in §8.7 and does not give Buyer the right to walk away.

**Recommended fix.** Amend SPA §9.1(e) to state that the notice deadline is "no later than forty-five (45) days after the date of this Agreement" (i.e., 45 days after May 11, 2026 = June 25, 2026), or alternatively "thirty (30) days after the date of this Agreement" to conform to the LOI. If the Phase II has not yet been performed, consider extending the deadline to a fixed date that allows adequate time for fieldwork, laboratory analysis, and report delivery (e.g., 60 or 75 days after the SPA date).

**Can this be resolved from the documents alone?** No. We need to confirm with the client whether the Phase II fieldwork has already been performed and whether a report is in hand. If the report already exists and shows costs below the threshold, the issue is moot. If not, this must be fixed before Closing.

## Issue 2 – CRITICAL: IP Assignment Agreement Omits Trade Secrets, Know-How, Process Parameters, and Software

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement §1.1; SPA §1.1 (definition of "Recycling Process Technology"); SPA §6.6; LOI §3(b)

**Problem.** The SPA defines "Recycling Process Technology" broadly: "patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software used in the Company's hydrometallurgical metal recovery" (SPA §1.1). SPA §6.6 covenants that Seller "shall assign to the Company all Recycling Process Technology owned by Seller or any of its Affiliates" and specifies that "this assignment shall be made by Seller's execution and delivery of the IP Assignment Agreement." The LOI §3(b) confirms the parties' understanding: Seller will assign "all patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software."

However, the IP Assignment Agreement §1.1 defines "Recycling Process Technology" as only "the Patents listed on Exhibit A." Section 2.1 then assigns the "Assigned Rights," which are defined by reference to that narrowed definition. Trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software are **not** assigned under the IP Assignment Agreement.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** For a hydrometallurgical recycling business, the proprietary leach chemistry, solvent extraction circuit parameters, precipitation conditions, and process control software are likely the most valuable intangible assets — more valuable than the patents themselves, which cover only specific claimed methods. If these are not assigned, Seller retains ownership of the core operational know-how after Closing. The Company (now owned by Mouloul) could be operating with technology it does not own, creating IP ownership disputes, potential infringement claims by Seller, and loss of the competitive advantage that justified the purchase price.

**Recommended fix.** Amend IP Assignment Agreement §1.1 to adopt the SPA's full definition of "Recycling Process Technology" (patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software). Add a new section (or expand §2.1) to expressly assign all trade secrets, technical data, process documentation, software source code and object code, and related know-how. Include a covenant that Seller will deliver all such materials in a usable format within a specified period after Closing (similar to the 30-day file delivery in §4.3, but expanded beyond patent prosecution files).

**Can this be resolved from the documents alone?** No. We need to confirm with the client's technical team what specific trade secrets, process parameters, and software are used in the Business and whether any of them are held by Seller or its Affiliates (as opposed to the Company itself). The SPA representation in §4.7 states that the Recycling Process Technology "together with the intellectual property owned by the Company, constitutes all intellectual property necessary to conduct the Business" — but we cannot verify from the documents what the Company already owns versus what Seller holds.

## Issue 3 – HIGH: Bill of Sale Schedule 1 Lacks Equipment Identification

**Where:** Bill of Sale, Schedule 1

**Problem.** Schedule 1 lists 14 categories of Shredding Line Assets (discharge tanks, shredders, separators, sieving stations, etc.) but every "Manufacturer / Model" and "Serial No." column is marked [●]. The SPA §1.1 defines "Shredding Line Assets" as the equipment "as listed on Schedule 1 to the Bill of Sale." Without manufacturer, model, and serial numbers, the schedule identifies equipment only by generic description.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The Shredding Line Assets are a critical component of the Business — they are the physical equipment that discharges and shreds batteries and separates black mass. If the schedule is too vague, Seller could argue that specific machines (e.g., a particular shredder or separator) were not part of the conveyance. In a post-Closing dispute, Mouloul would bear the burden of identifying which specific assets were supposed to be transferred. The SPA §4.1 representation that Seller holds "good and valid title" (Bill of Sale §4.1) is only as good as the identification of the assets to which it applies.

**Recommended fix.** Require Seller to complete all [●] fields in Schedule 1 before Closing. At minimum, each item should identify manufacturer, model number, and serial number (or asset tag). For the spare parts inventory (Item 14), a detailed parts list or inventory count should be attached as a sub-schedule. Consider adding a representation that the schedule is complete and that no other equipment used in the shredding line is owned by Seller.

## Issue 4 – HIGH: IP Assignment Exhibit A Lacks Patent Numbers and Filing Dates

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement, Exhibit A

**Problem.** Exhibit A lists six patents and applications by title and jurisdiction, but every "Patent / Application No." and "Filing Date" field is marked [●]. While the titles are descriptive enough to identify the general subject matter, patent numbers are the legally operative identifiers for recording assignments with the USPTO and foreign patent offices.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** Without patent numbers, the Company cannot record the assignment with the Patent Offices (see IP Assignment §3.1). Unrecorded assignments are vulnerable — if Seller files for bankruptcy or assigns the same patents to a third party, an unrecorded assignment may not provide constructive notice under 35 U.S.C. §261. Additionally, if any of the listed applications are still pending, the absence of filing dates makes it impossible to verify priority claims or confirm that the applications are actually owned by Seller.

**Recommended fix.** Require Seller to complete all [●] fields in Exhibit A before Closing. Verify each patent number against USPTO and foreign office records to confirm (a) the patents are in fact owned by Porert Industrial Holdings, LLC (as shown in the "Record Owner" column), (b) no liens or security interests are of record, and (c) all maintenance fees are current (as represented in IP Assignment §5.1(d)).

## Issue 5 – HIGH: TSA Permits Provider to Assign or Delegate Without Recipient Consent

**Where:** TSA §12.3

**Problem.** TSA §12.3 provides: "Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate any of its obligations under it, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient." The reciprocal restriction on Recipient is one-way: "Recipient may not assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Provider."

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The TSA covers seven critical operational services (ERP/IT, process control, environmental compliance, laboratory/assay, feedstock logistics, payroll, and accounting) for the first 9 months after Closing. These services are essential to keeping the facility operating and in regulatory compliance. If Porert delegates, for example, the environmental compliance service (air emissions reports, hazardous waste manifests, Tier II reports) to an unknown third party without the Company's consent, Mouloul could lose continuity of compliance reporting, face regulatory gaps, or receive lower-quality service. The one-sidedness is particularly concerning because Porert is a seller that will have no ongoing economic stake in the Company's success after Closing.

**Recommended fix.** Amend TSA §12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment or delegation. Alternatively, at minimum, require that any delegatee have equivalent qualifications and that Recipient may terminate the affected Service without penalty if the delegatee fails to meet the Service Standard. Given the environmental compliance service, consider requiring that any delegatee for Service 3 (EHS) be a licensed environmental professional familiar with the Facility's specific permits.

## Issue 6 – MEDIUM: TSA Governing Law and Jurisdiction Inconsistent with SPA

**Where:** TSA §12.7; SPA §11.9; SPA §11.4

**Problem.** SPA §11.9 provides that "this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement are governed by the laws of the State of New York." The Bill of Sale (§6.2) and IP Assignment Agreement (§7.4) both conform to New York law. However, TSA §12.7 provides: "This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas... Each party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in Harris County, Texas."

SPA §11.4 (Order of Precedence) states: "This Agreement controls, except that each Ancillary Agreement controls as to its own subject matter." This creates ambiguity: does the TSA's Texas governing law clause "control as to [the TSA's] own subject matter," or does the SPA's New York clause control because it expressly encompasses "any Ancillary Agreement"?

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** In a dispute over the TSA (e.g., Provider's failure to meet the Service Standard, or a data security breach), the applicable law and forum could differ from all other transaction documents. Texas law may interpret service-level obligations, limitation of liability, or indemnification differently than New York law. Splitting the governing law across documents also increases litigation complexity and cost.

**Recommended fix.** Amend TSA §12.7 to conform to New York law and New York County jurisdiction, consistent with the SPA, Bill of Sale, and IP Assignment. If Seller resists (perhaps because it is a Texas entity and the services are performed in Texas), at minimum add a clarifying sentence to SPA §11.4 stating that the SPA's governing law and jurisdiction provisions control over any inconsistent provision in an Ancillary Agreement.

## Issue 7 – MEDIUM: TSA Service Fees Are Unspecified

**Where:** TSA, Schedule A (all seven fee entries are $[●])

**Problem.** Every monthly fee in Schedule A is blank. TSA §5.1 states that fees "are intended to equal Provider's actual cost of providing the Services, without mark-up," but without a stated amount, there is no benchmark against which to measure compliance.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The Company (now owned by Mouloul) will be paying these fees for 9 months. Without agreed-upon amounts, Provider could invoice at levels Mouloul considers excessive, and the dispute mechanism (TSA §5.3, §4.3) would be the only recourse. The "at cost" standard in §5.1 helps but is subjective — what counts as an "allocable share of personnel costs" is open to interpretation. TSA §5.5 gives Recipient one audit right during the term and one post-term, but this is reactive rather than preventive.

**Recommended fix.** Complete all fee entries in Schedule A before execution. If the parties cannot agree on specific numbers, at minimum include a formula or cap (e.g., "not to exceed $X per month in the aggregate") and define what "actual cost" includes and excludes (e.g., whether it includes Provider's overhead, profit center allocations, or only direct labor and third-party costs).

## Issue 8 – MEDIUM: Insurance – Mouloul Not Named as Additional Insured; Limited Cancellation Visibility

**Where:** Insurance Cover Letter §2.3, §3.2; SPA §6.10

**Problem.** Two related concerns:

(a) **No additional insured status.** The cover letter confirms that Mouloul is listed only as "certificate holder" — not as an additional insured or loss payee under any policy. The Company (Kessrin) is the first named insured. While the Company will be owned by Mouloul after Closing, Mouloul itself (as parent) is not covered. If Mouloul is sued derivatively (e.g., for the Facility's environmental conditions under a "corporate veil" or "alter ego" theory), the Company's policies would not respond for Mouloul's own defense costs.

(b) **Limited cancellation visibility.** Cover letter §3.2 states the broker will forward any cancellation notice "to the Company and Porert" and will copy Mouloul only "if the Company authorizes us to do so." Post-Closing, the Company is owned by Mouloul, so Mouloul can give that authorization — but there is a gap between Closing and the point at which Mouloul's management is in control of the Company and can direct the broker.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The SPA §6.10 covenant only requires Seller to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to preserve the policies post-Closing. If a policy is cancelled or non-renewed before Mouloul is in a position to intervene, the Company could lose coverage for pre-Closing occurrences (the most likely claims in an environmental business).

**Recommended fix.** (a) Request that the Company (with Mouloul's direction pre-Closing, via SPA §6.1) instruct the broker to add Mouloul as an additional insured under the CGL, umbrella, and pollution legal liability policies, at least through the end of the current policy period. (b) Amend the SPA or add a side letter requiring Seller to cause the Company to authorize the broker to copy Mouloul on any cancellation, non-renewal, or material change notice. (c) Confirm that the pollution legal liability policy has no sub-limits or exclusions for specific hazardous substances used in the Business (electrolyte solvents, LiPF₆, heavy metals) — this requires reviewing the actual policy, not just the certificate.

**Can this be resolved from the documents alone?** No. We need the actual policy forms and endorsements to confirm coverage scope, limits, and exclusions. The certificates of insurance are informational only (Cover Letter §2.1).

## Issue 9 – LOW: SPA §3.2(a)(iii) – Ambiguity in Director/Officer Resignation Requirement

**Where:** SPA §3.2(a)(iii)

**Problem.** The provision requires delivery of "written resignations of the directors and officers of the Company designated by Buyer." The phrase "designated by Buyer" is ambiguous. It could mean:
- (a) the directors and officers who are being replaced by Buyer's designees (i.e., all current directors and officers must resign), or
- (b) only those specific directors and officers that Buyer affirmatively designates must resign (implying Buyer could choose to leave some in place).

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** In a 100% stock purchase, Buyer typically wants full control of the board and management at Closing. If the provision is read as (b), Seller could argue that Buyer must affirmatively identify which individuals must resign, and any individual not identified remains in office. This could create a governance gap at Closing.

**Recommended fix.** Amend to: "written resignations of all directors and officers of the Company, effective as of the Closing." Alternatively, attach a schedule identifying the specific individuals whose resignations are required.

## Issue 10 – LOW: No TSA Extension Mechanism

**Where:** TSA §3.1

**Problem.** The Service Term is fixed at 9 months after Closing with no built-in extension. If migration of any service (particularly the ERP system or process control support) is not complete by the end of the term, the Company could lose critical operational support with no contractual right to continue.

**Why it matters to Mouloul.** The facility is a continuous-process chemical operation. Loss of process control system support (TSA Schedule A, Service 2) or ERP/IT (Service 1) mid-migration could disrupt operations, affect product quality, or create safety or compliance issues.

**Recommended fix.** Add a provision allowing either party to request a good-faith extension of up to 3 months for any Service not yet fully migrated, on the same terms and fees. Alternatively, add a "tail" provision requiring Provider to remain available for emergency support for 30 days after the Service Term expires, at cost.

## Conclusion

The two critical issues (environmental termination deadline and IP assignment scope) should be raised with Seller immediately.
