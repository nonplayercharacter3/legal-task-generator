## Issue 1 – IP Assignment Does Not Cover Trade Secrets, Know-How, Process Parameters, or Software

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 ("Recycling Process Technology" definition); SPA § 6.6; IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1; LOI § 3(b)

**Description:** The SPA defines "Recycling Process Technology" as "patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software used in the Company's hydrometallurgical metal recovery" (SPA § 1.1). Section 6.6 covenants that Seller will assign all Recycling Process Technology to the Company "by Seller's execution and delivery of the IP Assignment Agreement." However, the IP Assignment Agreement defines "Recycling Process Technology" as only "the Patents listed on Exhibit A" (IP Assignment § 1.1), and its assignment clause (§ 2.1) covers only those patents and related rights. Trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software are assigned nowhere in the document set.

The LOI confirms the parties' original intent: § 3(b) states Seller will assign "all patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters and software owned by Seller or its Affiliates and used in the Company's hydrometallurgical metal recovery."

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** The Company's competitive advantage in hydrometallurgical processing likely resides as much in process know-how, proprietary parameters, and control software as in the six listed patents. Without assignment of those assets, the Company may lack the right to operate the Business as currently conducted, and Seller could retain (or license to a competitor) the very process knowledge that makes the Business valuable. SPA § 4.7 represents that the Recycling Process Technology "constitutes all intellectual property necessary to conduct the Business," but that representation is only as good as the actual assignment—and the assignment is incomplete.

**Recommended fix:** Amend the IP Assignment Agreement to (a) redefine "Recycling Process Technology" to match the SPA definition (patents, trade secrets, know-how, process parameters, and software), and (b) expand § 2.1 to assign all such rights. Alternatively, add a separate general IP/know-how assignment as an additional exhibit. Confirm that SPA § 6.6's reference to the IP Assignment Agreement as the exclusive mechanism for the assignment is updated to reflect the broader scope.

## Issue 2 – Environmental Termination Right Has Already Expired

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(e); LOI § 6(c)

**Description:** The LOI provided that Buyer's right to terminate based on Phase II remediation costs exceeding $1,500,000 must be exercised "no later than thirty (30) days following execution of the Definitive Agreement" (LOI § 6(c)). The SPA instead provides that the notice must be delivered "no later than forty-five (45) days after the date of the Letter of Intent" (SPA § 9.1(e)). The LOI is dated February 9, 2026; 45 days later is March 26, 2026. The SPA is dated May 11, 2026. The deadline has therefore already passed before the SPA was even signed.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** Buyer has lost its contractual right to walk away from the deal if the Phase II Assessment reveals significant contamination. Given that the Facility handles electrolyte solvents, lithium hexafluorophosphate, heavy metals, process liquors, leach residues, and black mass (SPA § 1.1, "Hazardous Materials"), this is a material exposure. Buyer is now contractually committed to close regardless of what the Phase II finds (subject only to the general MAE condition in § 7.2(e), which is a high bar).

**Recommended fix:** Amend SPA § 9.1(e) to restore the LOI formulation: the deadline should be 30 days after execution of the Definitive Agreement (i.e., 30 days after May 11, 2026 = June 10, 2026), or alternatively 30 days after delivery of the Phase II Assessment report, whichever is later. If Seller resists, consider negotiating a specific environmental indemnity with no basket or cap (though § 8.2(d) already provides one for pre-Closing Releases, it does not cover conditions that existed pre-Closing but were not "Releases" per se—see also the survival point in Issue 11 below).

## Issue 3 – Bill of Sale Schedule 1 Is Entirely Unpopulated

**Where:** Bill of Sale, Schedule 1; SPA § 1.1 ("Shredding Line Assets" definition)

**Description:** Schedule 1 lists 14 categories of equipment but every "Manufacturer / Model" and "Serial No." field is blank ([●]). The SPA defines "Shredding Line Assets" by reference to "Schedule 1 to the Bill of Sale" (SPA § 1.1). With the schedule empty, the defined term has no operative content, and the conveyance in Bill of Sale § 2.1 is effectively a transfer of an unidentified set of assets.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** Without identified serial numbers and manufacturers, Mouloul cannot (a) verify that the assets actually exist and are in the condition represented, (b) perfect any security interest or record title, (c) confirm that the assets are the same ones used in the Business during due diligence, or (d) enforce the title representation in Bill of Sale § 4.1. In a dispute, Seller could argue that specific machines were not part of the "Shredding Line Assets."

**Recommended fix:** Require Seller to populate Schedule 1 with complete manufacturer, model, and serial number information for all 14 line items before Closing. Make delivery of a completed Schedule 1 a condition to Closing under SPA § 7.2(f) (which requires delivery of "each item listed in Section 3.2(a)," including the Bill of Sale).

## Issue 4 – IP Assignment Exhibit A Patent Numbers and Filing Dates Are Blank

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement, Exhibit A

**Description:** All six entries in Exhibit A have blank patent/application numbers and blank filing dates ([●]). Only the titles and jurisdictions are filled in.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** The entire IP Assignment operates by reference to Exhibit A. Without patent numbers, the assignment is not operative as to any specific patent. The record owner is listed as "Porert Industrial Holdings, LLC" for all six, which is useful, but Mouloul cannot confirm these are the correct and only patents, or verify their status (issued vs. pending, abandoned, etc.) without the numbers.

**Recommended fix:** Require Seller to complete Exhibit A with all patent and application numbers, filing dates, and current status before Closing. Add a representation that Exhibit A is complete and accurate as of the Closing Date.

## Issue 5 – TSA Permits Provider to Delegate Services Without Recipient Consent

**Where:** Transition Services Agreement § 12.3

**Description:** "Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate any of its obligations under it, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient." There is no reciprocal right for Recipient.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** The transition services include process control system support for the leach/solvent extraction/precipitation circuits (Schedule A, Item 2), environmental compliance reporting (Item 3), and laboratory/assay services (Item 4). These are operationally critical. If Provider delegates to an unknown or less-capable third party, the Company's production and compliance could be compromised during the 9-month transition. The Service Standard (§ 2.2) requires performance "in substantially the same manner" as the prior 12 months, but a new delegate may not meet that standard, and enforcement would be difficult.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment or delegation. Alternatively, limit delegation to Provider's Affiliates and require that the delegatee assume all obligations under the TSA in writing.

## Issue 6 – TSA Service Fees Are Unspecified

**Where:** Transition Services Agreement, Schedule A (all fee columns); § 5.1

**Description:** Every monthly fee in Schedule A is blank ([●]). Section 5.1 states fees are "intended to equal Provider's actual cost of providing the Services, without mark-up," but no dollar amounts are agreed.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** Without agreed fees, there is no benchmark against which to dispute invoices under § 5.2 or audit under § 5.5. "At cost" is subjective—what counts as an allocable share of personnel costs? What third-party costs are included? This creates a built-in dispute mechanism for the entire 9-month term.

**Recommended fix:** Populate Schedule A with agreed monthly fees for each of the seven services before Closing. If the parties cannot agree on specific numbers, at minimum define the cost methodology in § 5.1 (e.g., fully loaded personnel cost at specified rates, pass-through of third-party costs at actual with no markup, and a cap on annual fee increases).

## Issue 7 – TSA Governing Law and Jurisdiction Inconsistent with SPA and Other Ancillary Agreements

**Where:** TSA § 12.7; SPA § 11.9–11.10; Bill of Sale § 6.2–6.3; IP Assignment § 7.4–7.5

**Description:** The TSA is governed by Texas law with exclusive jurisdiction in Harris County, Texas. The SPA, Bill of Sale, and IP Assignment are all governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** If a dispute arises that implicates both the SPA and the TSA (e.g., a claim that Seller's failure to provide transition services constitutes a breach of SPA § 6.8, triggering the SPA indemnity), the parties could end up litigating in two different jurisdictions under two different bodies of law. This increases cost and creates strategic complications.

**Recommended fix:** Conform TSA § 12.7 to New York law and New York County jurisdiction, consistent with the SPA and the other Ancillary Agreements. If Seller insists on Texas law (perhaps because the services are performed in Texas), at minimum add a provision permitting joinder or coordination with any SPA-related action.

## Issue 8 – TSA Liability Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** TSA § 8.3

**Description:** "Provider's aggregate liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the total Service Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." Because the fees are at cost (and currently unspecified), the cap is likely to be a modest number—perhaps a few hundred thousand dollars over 9 months. The carve-outs (breach of data/confidentiality obligations, gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud, indemnification under Article 9) are helpful but narrow.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** If a failure in process control support (Schedule A, Item 2) causes a production shutdown or product contamination, the damages could far exceed the total Service Fees. The cap would limit Mouloul's recovery to that modest amount unless the failure rises to gross negligence or willful misconduct—a high bar.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the cap to a fixed dollar amount (e.g., 2–3× the total expected Service Fees) or tie it to a percentage of the Purchase Price. At minimum, add a carve-out for failure to meet the Service Standard that results in a material adverse impact on the Company's operations.

## Issue 9 – Mouloul Is Not an Additional Insured Under the Company's Policies

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 2.3; SPA § 6.10

**Description:** The broker confirms: "We have listed Mouloul Energy Materials, Inc. as certificate holder. We have not added it as an additional insured or loss payee under any policy." A certificate of insurance "confers no rights on the certificate holder" (§ 2.1).

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** Post-Closing, the Company is Mouloul's wholly-owned subsidiary. If a pre-Closing occurrence gives rise to a claim that exceeds the Company's policy limits or that names Mouloul directly (e.g., as a parent entity with alleged supervisory liability), Mouloul has no direct right to the insurance proceeds. SPA § 6.10 only requires Seller to "use commercially reasonable efforts to give the Company the benefit of those policies for pre-Closing occurrences"—a weak standard that does not protect Mouloul as parent.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Require the Company (pre-Closing, at Seller's direction) to request that Mouloul be added as an additional insured under the CGL, umbrella, and pollution legal liability policies for pre-Closing occurrences, or (b) negotiate a specific indemnity in the SPA from Seller for any loss that would have been covered had Mouloul been an additional insured. Also consider strengthening § 6.10 from "commercially reasonable efforts" to a firm covenant that Seller will cause the policies to remain in force and available to the Company for pre-Closing occurrences through the end of the policy period.

## Issue 10 – SPA § 6.10 Post-Closing Insurance Covenant Is Weak

**Where:** SPA § 6.10

**Description:** "After the Closing, Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to give the Company the benefit of those policies for pre-Closing occurrences."

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** "Commercially reasonable efforts" is a low standard. If an insurer cancels or non-renews a policy (perhaps triggered by the change of control, despite the policies being occurrence-based), Seller could argue it made "commercially reasonable efforts" by, say, sending a letter to the insurer. Mouloul would then have no specific remedy beyond a general indemnity claim under § 8.2(b) (breach of covenant), which is subject to the $648,000 basket and $8,640,000 cap in § 8.4.

**Recommended fix:** Replace "commercially reasonable efforts" with a firm covenant: "Seller shall cause the Company's occurrence-based insurance policies in effect on the Closing Date to remain available to the Company for pre-Closing occurrences through the expiration of the applicable policy period." Add that a breach of this covenant is not subject to the basket or cap in § 8.4.

## Issue 11 – Environmental Representation Survival May Be Inadequate Given Latent Contamination

**Where:** SPA § 8.1; SPA § 4.9

**Description:** The environmental representation (§ 4.9) survives for five years. However, § 4.9 represents that "there has been no Release of Hazardous Materials at the Facility that requires investigation or remediation under Environmental Laws." In a battery recycling operation handling the substances listed in the "Hazardous Materials" definition, contamination may be latent—present in soil or groundwater but not yet identified or required to be remediated. Five years may not be sufficient to discover and quantify all latent conditions, particularly if the Phase II Assessment (which Buyer is conducting pre-Closing) does not sample all areas.

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** If contamination is discovered in year six or seven, the § 4.9 representation has expired and Mouloul's only recourse is the specific environmental indemnity in § 8.2(d) (for "any Release of Hazardous Materials at the Facility occurring before the Closing Date"). That indemnity is not subject to the basket or cap, which is helpful, but it requires proving that a "Release" occurred pre-Closing—a factual question that becomes harder over time as records are lost and personnel change.

**Recommended fix:** Extend the survival of § 4.9 to the full statute of limitations for environmental claims (which can be 10+ years under CERCLA and state analogues), or at minimum 7–10 years. Alternatively, add a specific environmental indemnity in § 8.2 that covers all pre-Closing environmental conditions (not just "Releases") and is expressly excluded from the basket, cap, and survival limitations.

## Issue 12 – Landlord Estoppel Confirms No Lease Guaranty

**Where:** Landlord Estoppel § 3.3

**Description:** "Porert has not guaranteed the Lease. No guaranty of the Lease by any Person is in effect."

**Why it matters to Mouloul:** Post-Closing, the Company (a subsidiary of Mouloul) is the sole obligor on a lease with $118,750/month base rent (approximately $1.42M/year) plus ~$21,400/month additional rent, running through at least 2031 (with two 5-year options). The Company's creditworthiness as a newly acquired subsidiary may be insufficient to support this obligation without a parent guaranty. While this is not Seller's problem post-Closing, Mouloul should be aware that it is stepping into a lease with no credit enhancement and should consider whether to provide a parent guaranty or negotiate a lease amendment.

**Recommended fix:** This is not a defect in the transaction documents per se, but Mouloul should (a) confirm the Company's financial capacity to support the lease obligations independently, and (b) decide pre-Closing whether a Mouloul parent guaranty will be required by the Landlord (which could become an issue at the first lease renewal or option exercise). No change to the estoppel is needed, but flag for post-Closing integration planning.
