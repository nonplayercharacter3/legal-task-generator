### Issue 1 – Tavinor change-of-control: no consent, no condition, thin remedy

**Where:** MSA §14.2, §13.5, §2.2, §4.2, §13.1, Sched. A; SPA §4.4(c), Annex 7.2(d), §5.3, §7.2(a) and (d), §1.1 ("Material Adverse Effect" cl. (c)), §9.4.

**What the documents say**
- Any person acquiring more than 50% of the Company's voting equity, directly or indirectly, is a deemed assignment requiring Tavinor's prior written consent (MSA §14.2).
- If we close without consent, Tavinor has 120 days after it receives written notice of the closing to choose one of two remedies.
  - **(a)** Terminate on 30 days' notice.
  - **(b)** Replace the Contract Price with the **List Price** for all later orders. Sections 6.1 and 6.2 then cease to apply, and §13.5 transition supply does not apply.
- SPA §4.4(c) represents that no Material Contract consent is required, other than items on Annex 7.2(d). Annex 7.2(d) lists only the lender payoff and lien release.
- Tavinor is almost certainly a Material Contract, and a Material Supplier under SPA §4.7(b). I cannot confirm this without Disclosure Schedule §§4.7 and 4.8.

**Why it matters**
- **Option (b) is the worse risk.** The Company would stay bound to buy at least 85% of its requirements from Tavinor (MSA §2.2) and to its binding 90-day forecasts (§4.2). It would pay uncapped List Price through the initial term ending January 31, 2029 (§13.1). Non-renewal notice is due by January 31, 2028.
- **Option (a) is less severe on its face.** §13.5 excludes only terminations under §13.2 or §13.3, and §14.2 disapplies §13.5 only for repricing. A §14.2(a) termination therefore arguably still gives up to 12 months of transition supply at Contract Price. Tavinor would likely choose (b) instead.
- **Replacing Tavinor is slow.** Lead times run 10–16 weeks (Sched. A). Tavinor's melt routes are qualified with the Company's customers (Recital C). Item A-4 is aerospace-grade. A-1 and A-4 use the Company's proprietary parameters.
- **The SPA gives little protection today.**
  - There is no closing condition, because §7.2(d) covers only Annex items.
  - There is no efforts covenant, because §5.3 covers only Annex items.
  - A claim on the §4.4 rep would be subject to the 18-month survival (§9.1), the $485,000 Deductible (§9.4(a)), and a cap equal to the $4.85M escrow (§9.4(b)). It would also face the escrow "Losses" problem (Issue 4).
  - The MAE carve-out for "announcement of the transactions" (§1.1, cl. (c)) is not subject to the disproportionate-effect exception. Seller will argue it excludes a Tavinor reaction triggered by the deal.
  - The SPA has no express reservation of rights or pro-sandbagging clause. Because we now know of §14.2, Seller may argue waiver. I have not researched the New York law point (SPA §11.8), and it should be checked.
- **Seller probably cannot cure by supplement.** The MSA dates from February 2024, so it is not a matter "first arising" after signing (SPA §5.7(a)). But if Seller supplements anyway and we do not respond within 10 Business Days, §5.7(c) deems the rep cured (Issue 2).

**Recommended fix (SPA amendment)**
1. Add to Annex 7.2(d): Tavinor's written consent under MSA §14.2, in form satisfactory to Buyer. It should also waive §14.2(a) and (b) for this transaction, with no pricing or other amendment to the MSA.
2. Make Seller's §5.3 efforts obligation specific to Tavinor. Seller should bear any consent cost, and the Company should not agree to MSA changes without Buyer's approval (see §5.1(d)).
3. Add a first-dollar special indemnity for Losses from any exercise of §14.2 rights. It should be outside the Deductible and the §9.4(b) cap, with a longer survival period. Consider a separate holdback if the consent is not in hand at closing.
4. Give Buyer a right to extend the Outside Date (SPA §8.1(b), November 30, 2026). The date is tight if Tavinor is slow.
5. Carve loss of, or adverse action by, a counterparty because of the transaction out of MAE clause (c).
6. Ask that the consent also cover future internal reorganizations, a lender's pledge of the Company stock (SPA §11.6), and indirect changes of control above the Company. Each could re-trigger §14.2.

**Open / client input:** annual Tavinor spend and the List Price premium over Contract Price (Exhibit B-1 is not in the folder). Also whether Harrowgate has alternative qualified sources, and whether Tavinor already knows of the deal. Finally, whether Harrowgate wants Seller or the Company to approach Tavinor (MSA §11.2 permits disclosure to bona fide acquirers under confidentiality).

### Issue 2 – Schedule Supplement mechanics: deemed cure, and the October 27 deadline

**Where:** SPA §5.7(a)–(c), §8.1(e), §1.1 ("Disclosure Schedule"), §11.2, §11.5; Second Supplement Letter §§2–4.

**What the documents say**
- Seller may supplement at any time before Closing for matters "first arising, or of which Seller first becomes aware" after signing (§5.7(a)).
- Buyer may terminate only if a supplement would cause the §7.2(a) condition to fail, and only within 10 Business Days after receipt (§5.7(b)).
- If Buyer does not terminate in that period, two things happen under §5.7(c).
  - Buyer waives termination for those matters.
  - The supplement is deemed to cure any rep inaccuracy "for purposes of Article IX" and for §7.2(a).

**Why it matters**
- **The cure applies even to supplements that do not meet the §5.7(b) threshold.** The practical effect is that every post-signing supplement wipes out indemnity unless Buyer terminates. Termination is unavailable for small items, which is the trap.
- **The deadline.** The Second Supplement is dated October 13 (Tuesday), sent by email and courier.
  - Counting New York Business Days (SPA §1.1), 10 Business Days ends **Tuesday, October 27, 2026**. That assumes email receipt on October 13 before 5:00 p.m. ET (§11.2).
  - If the email arrived after 5:00 p.m., deemed receipt is October 14 and the deadline is October 28. I would work to October 27.
  - **Notice mechanics cannot be resolved from the documents.** §11.2 sends notices to "addresses set forth on the signature pages," and the SPA signature pages in the folder contain no addresses. We should confirm the actual notice address and when and how the email arrived.
- **A reservation-of-rights letter does not stop the cure.** Only a signed amendment or waiver (§11.5) does.
- **Do not send a protective termination notice casually.** An invalid §8.1(e) notice risks a wrongful-termination or willful-breach claim (§8.2(b)) and a specific-performance action (§11.10). On Annex A alone, I do not think the §7.2(a) threshold is met (see Issue 5).
- **The Letter overreaches in two places.**
  - Letter §3 claims cross-section disclosure wherever "relevance is reasonably apparent." The SPA has no such standard.
  - Letter §2 says Seller will treat §4.8, as supplemented, as operative "for all purposes." That goes beyond the "Article IX and §7.2(a)" scope of §5.7(c). It could reach §4.6/MAE, §7.2(c), and §8.1(c).
- **There is no cut-off.** A supplement near closing leaves the 10-Business-Day period unresolved, since closing occurs three Business Days after the conditions are satisfied (§3.1).

**Recommended fix**
- **By October 27**, send a written response that does the following.
  - Rejects Letter §3 cross-disclosure and the "all purposes" statement in Letter §2.
  - Disputes the §5.7(a) eligibility of Annex A items 1 and 2, whose underlying events predate signing.
  - Reserves rights on item 4 as a §5.1(d) covenant matter (Issue 6).
- **Ask Seller to extend the §5.7(b) period** in a signed writing while we negotiate. The counsel call Seller proposed for the week of October 19 (Letter §6) is the venue.
- **Seek an SPA amendment**: supplements may give a walk right but may not cure for Article IX purposes. If Seller refuses, ask for line-item special indemnities (Issues 3 and 6). Also add a no-supplement window before Closing.
- **Set up a docket and a standing procedure** for every future supplement. The September deadline passed without action (Issue 3).

### Issue 4 – Escrow Agreement defines "Losses" narrower than the SPA

**Where:** Escrow §1.1 ("Losses"), §4.3, §4.2(a), §4.6; SPA §1.1 ("Losses"), §9.6, §11.11.

**What the documents say**
- SPA "Losses" include diminution in value, interest, penalties, and reasonable attorneys' fees.
- Escrow "Losses" are "actual out-of-pocket" amounts "paid or incurred." They exclude diminution in value, lost profits, consequential damages, and attorneys' fees and costs of investigation, defense, or enforcement.
- Escrow §4.3 allows disbursement to Buyer "only [for] amounts constituting Losses."
- SPA §11.11 says the SPA prevails in a conflict, except that the Escrow Agreement controls "escrow disbursement mechanics." Seller will argue the definition is a "mechanic."

**Why it matters**
- It could cut recoveries from the escrow, which is the first source of recovery under SPA §9.6(a). It would exclude defense costs of third-party claims (such as the forklift suit) and value-based losses (such as a Tavinor repricing).
- The "paid or incurred" language also conflicts with Escrow §4.2(a), which allows claims for Losses a Buyer Indemnitee "reasonably expects to incur."
- The Escrow Agent pays on non-objection (§4.2(c)), so the mismatch bites mainly when Seller objects.

**Recommended fix**
- Delete the Escrow definition and replace it with "has the meaning given in the Purchase Agreement."
- Amend SPA §11.11 so the Escrow Agreement controls only the agent's procedural steps and the SPA governs the scope and amount of indemnifiable Losses.
- Conform SPA §9.6(a). It refers only to joint instruction or final order, and omits the non-objection path in Escrow §4.2(c).
- Attach the agreed escrow form as an SPA exhibit. Today the SPA does not attach it, but §3.2(b) requires Seller to deliver it executed.

### Issue 5 – Indemnity economics and credit

**Where:** SPA §9.1, §9.4(a)–(e), §9.6, §9.7, §2.4; Escrow §4.1.

**Points**
- **Deductible and cap.** The Deductible is $485,000 (1% of price). Indemnity under §9.2(a) for non-Fundamental reps is capped at the escrow amount, $4.85M (10%). Fundamental reps and the §4.12 tax rep are outside both limits (§9.4(a)–(b)). The overall cap is the Purchase Price.
- **Effect on Annex A.** The four Annex A items are modest dollar items and would sit largely inside the Deductible even without cure (Issue 6). Their significance is mostly precedent and the combined effect with Issue 2.
- **Materiality scrape is single only.** §9.4(e) disregards materiality "for purposes of calculating Losses" only. It does not apply to determining whether a breach occurred. Reps such as §4.5 and §4.11 are materiality-qualified, so breach is still tested against materiality. Seek a double scrape.
- **Collection risk.** Seller is a fund (Meridale Capital Partners II, L.P.). The escrow is released at 18 months (Escrow §4.1), but Fundamental and tax rep claims survive to the statute of limitations plus 60 days (SPA §9.1). Recovery then depends on a direct claim against a fund that may have distributed proceeds. Ask for a Seller parent or fund guaranty, a distribution restriction, or an extended escrow for tax and Fundamental claims.
- **Thin rep package.** The Article IV reps do not include IP, employee or labor, insurance, or environmental (beyond §4.11), and there is no product-warranty rep. Given the aerospace-grade products and the Annex A subjects, consider asking for additions or specific indemnities.
- **Indebtedness definition is narrow.** It covers borrowed money only (SPA §1.1). Closing payments are net of the payoff and escrow (§2.3), and unpaid Seller and Company transaction expenses are not deducted. Consider adding transaction expenses and similar debt-like items.

**Client input:** appetite to push for a guaranty or longer escrow, and whether R&W insurance is in play (not mentioned in the folder).
