## Summary

The most significant items, in order, are:

1. **Tavinor change-of-control (MSA §14.2) is not covered by the SPA.** The deal is a >50% equity sale. It is deemed an assignment requiring Tavinor's prior consent. Without consent, Tavinor can terminate on 30 days' notice or reprice to List Price and cut off transition supply. Annex 7.2(d) lists only lender items, so no consent is a closing condition or covenant target.
2. **Schedule Supplement mechanism (SPA §5.7) is very seller-favorable, and the Second Supplement (Oct. 13) puts a clock on Buyer.** Failure to terminate within 10 Business Days is a deemed waiver and cure for indemnity. Based on the letter's October 13 date, the window likely runs to about October 27, 2026. This must be confirmed against actual receipt.
3. **The First Supplement (Ferrovane, about 18% of revenue, non-renewal) has already been waived and cured.** Seller's letter says so, and on the face of §5.7 it appears right. Harrowgate has lost indemnity and walk-right protection for a major revenue loss. This must be addressed in price or a special indemnity.
4. **Escrow Agreement conflicts with SPA indemnity (Losses definition; §4.3).** The Escrow's narrower "Losses" definition excludes diminution in value and attorneys' fees, and §11.11 of the SPA gives the Escrow Agreement control over disbursement mechanics.
5. **Cap and basket mechanics, and other SPA gaps.**
6. **Lease: no issue found on change of control; a few minor points.**

## 1. Tavinor Master Supply Agreement – Change of Control

**Where:** MSA §14.2 (and §13.5, §2.2, §13.1); SPA §4.4(c), §4.7, §5.3, §7.2(d), Annex 7.2(d).

**Issue.**
- MSA §14.2 provides that any transaction by which a Person acquires beneficial ownership of more than 50% of Coldbrook's voting equity is a deemed assignment requiring Tavinor's *prior written consent*. Harrowgate's purchase of 100% of the Shares (SPA §2.2) triggers it.
- If the deal closes without consent, Tavinor has 120 days after receiving notice of consummation to elect either of two remedies:
  - (a) terminate on 30 days' notice; or
  - (b) reprice all later orders at List Price, with MSA §§6.1/6.2 (the negotiated pricing and surcharge) ceasing to apply and the §13.5 12-month transition supply not applying.
- Section 14.1 makes the assignment void, but §14.2 supplies the specific remedies.

**Why it matters.**
- Tavinor supplies four alloy grades, including A-4 (aerospace-grade) and A-1, which incorporate Coldbrook's proprietary process parameters (MSA Sched. A).
- Lead times run 10–16 weeks (Sched. A).
- Coldbrook must buy at least 85% of its requirements from Tavinor (MSA §2.2).
- Qualification by Coldbrook's customers is involved (Recitals, MSA §7.4).
- Termination, or a switch to List Price, with transition supply unavailable would threaten supply continuity and margins. Replacement would require requalification.
- The MSA runs to January 31, 2029, then auto-renews (§13.1). That is a valuable term to lose.

**SPA gaps.**
- Annex 7.2(d) lists only the Lender payoff and lien release. Tavinor consent is not a closing condition (§7.2(d)), and §5.3's efforts covenant covers only Annex items.
- §4.4(c) represents that the deal does not require any consent under a Material Contract or give rise to termination rights. §4.4 also says no consents are needed other than those on Annex 7.2(d). On its face the Tavinor MSA makes this representation inaccurate. This is a Material Contract under §4.7(a)/(b) (supply contract with a Material Supplier, presumably, and likely over $250,000 per year; we should confirm from Disclosure Schedule 4.7/4.8).
- Even so, a breach of §4.4 is subject to the 18-month survival, the $485,000 Deductible, and the escrow cap (§9.1, §9.4(a)–(b)). Indemnity would be a poor substitute for the contract itself. Section 5.7 also lets Seller try to cure it by supplement. Nothing we have seen shows that it has disclosed or tried to.
- Section 8.1(c) and §7.2(a) would help Buyer only if the inaccuracy is material, and the Closing Date bring-down is "in all material respects."

**Recommended fix.**
- Add Tavinor's written consent (and waiver of §14.2 remedies) to Annex 7.2(d) as a closing condition, in form reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.
- Add a specific Seller covenant to seek the consent promptly, with Buyer participation in communications.
- Add a special indemnity (outside the Deductible and cap) for Losses from exercise of the §14.2 remedies.
- Ask Seller to confirm whether the Disclosure Schedule lists the Tavinor MSA and whether Tavinor has been approached. Request the Disclosure Schedule (not in the folder).

**Open points / client input.**
- We do not have the Disclosure Schedule, so we cannot confirm what Seller disclosed on §4.4, §4.7 or §4.8.
- Client should tell us how critical Tavinor is, whether alternative qualified sources exist, what the List Price premium to the Contract Price is, and whether it will accept a closing without consent.
- Timing: the MSA's notice clause requires consent *before* closing. Any delay by Tavinor could push against the November 30, 2026 Outside Date (SPA §8.1(b)). Consider an extension right.
- The MSA's 120-day election period runs from Tavinor's receipt of written notice of consummation. This could be long after closing, so Buyer would carry the risk in the interim.
- The Tavinor consent request may itself give Tavinor leverage to renegotiate. The client should decide how to approach this.
- MSA §11.2 permits disclosure to bona fide prospective acquirers under confidentiality, so diligence disclosure of the MSA is likely fine, but we have not verified how our copy was obtained.
- The MSA signature block shows Coldbrook's address as "1180 Elm Ridge Road, Suite 300", consistent with the HQ lease. The Tavinor address on the signature page has no suite for Coldbrook beyond that, which is not an issue.

## 2. Schedule Supplement Mechanism and the Pending 10-Business-Day Clock

**Where:** SPA §1.1 ("Disclosure Schedule"), §5.7(a)–(c), §8.1(e), §7.2(a), §9.2(a); Second Schedule Supplement Letter §§2–4, Annex A.

**Issue.**
- Section 5.7(a) lets Seller supplement the Disclosure Schedule for matters "first arising, or of which Seller first becomes aware" after signing.
- Under §5.7(b), Buyer may terminate only if the supplement would cause the §7.2(a) condition to fail, and only within 10 Business Days after receipt.
- Under §5.7(c), failure to terminate within that period is an irrevocable waiver and cures the inaccuracy "for purposes of Article IX" and the condition.
- Effectively, every supplement either gives Buyer a walk right or wipes out the indemnity. Buyer must make a binary decision in 10 Business Days, often without full information.

**Timing for the Second Supplement.**
- The Second Supplement is dated October 13, 2026 and was sent by email and overnight courier. Under SPA §11.2, email notice sent before 5:00 p.m. Eastern on a Business Day is effective that day. Otherwise it is effective the next Business Day. Courier notice is effective one Business Day later.
- We do not know the time of transmission or when Harrowgate's General Counsel actually received it. The period is measured from Buyer's "receipt" (§5.7(b)).
- If effective October 13, 10 Business Days would end about October 27 (Business Day defined by New York bank closures, §1.1; October 13, 2026 is a Tuesday, and Columbus Day/Indigenous Peoples' Day is October 12, so I have assumed no further holidays in the period). If effective October 14, the deadline is October 28. Please confirm the dates before relying on these.
- **Treat October 27 as the working deadline.**

**Substance of the Second Supplement (Annex A).**
- §4.10: forklift injury suit, $175,000 claimed, insurer tendered subject to a reservation of rights, $25,000 retention.
- §4.11: two recordkeeping deficiencies under the air permit; records submitted; no notice of violation yet.
- §4.6: Plant Controller resigned October 1; interim cover; no severance.
- §4.7: renewal of CMM maintenance contract at $268,000 per year (above the $250,000 Material Contract threshold).
- Individually these look modest. Only if they rise to a failure of §7.2(a) ("all material respects") would Buyer have a termination right. As far as one can tell from the documents, they probably do not. That means the practical effect of §5.7(c) is mainly to cure them for indemnity purposes. This should be traded for something.
- Items of concern to run down:
  - The incident occurred May 6, 2026, before signing on August 3. It was therefore not a matter "first arising" after signing. Whether Seller "first became aware" after signing is uncertain, but the Company presumably knew of the incident. Supplement eligibility under §5.7(a) arguably fails for item 1 (and possibly item 2, since the March and April 2026 recordkeeping lapses pre-date signing). Seller's letter asserts only that Seller "first became aware" after signing. That is a factual claim to test. Note §4.11's reference to compliance "since January 1, 2023" and §4.6's no-ordinary-course-exceptions.
  - Insurance reservation of rights: the coverage position is unresolved.
  - Air permit: possible penalties. Note the supplement says none "as of the date."
  - Controller departure: the unaudited financials and the §2.5 working capital true-up (Closing Working Capital determined under GAAP) depend on finance staff continuity. This is a practical risk.
  - The CMM contract renewal on September 18 occurred between signing and closing. Under §5.1(d), the Company may not "enter into, materially amend or terminate any Material Contract" without Buyer's consent. A renewal at $268,000 per year is a new Material Contract. Seller says it is ordinary course, but §5.1(d) has no ordinary-course exception. The letter at §5 nonetheless confirms ordinary-course compliance with §5.1. This is a technical covenant issue worth noting, and it affects the §7.2(b) condition and certificate. It is a low-dollar item, but it is a useful bargaining point.
- **Cross-disclosure language (Letter §3).** Seller asserts that each Annex A matter is disclosed against every section "to which its relevance is reasonably apparent on the face." Nothing in the SPA gives Seller that standard for supplements. The SPA's Article IV lead-in says "Except as set forth in the Disclosure Schedule" and does not address cross-references. Buyer should reject this in writing, or the risk is a broad cure.

**Why it matters.** The folder shows the structure favors Seller in two ways: (i) the supplement right is unlimited in number, (ii) cure is for *all* purposes, including indemnity, with no Buyer remedy except termination.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Immediately calendar the deadline. Send a written reservation-of-rights letter now stating that Buyer (a) does not accept the supplement's eligibility under §5.7(a) for pre-signing matters, (b) does not accept the cross-disclosure standard, and (c) disputes the "no admission" and cure positions. A letter alone may not prevent a deemed waiver under §5.7(c). Consider also getting a tolling or extension agreement from Seller signed by both parties (§11.5), or be prepared to terminate. **Termination is available only if the condition would fail.** A termination notice that is wrong could itself be a breach.
2. In the markup/amendment, revise §5.7:
   - limit supplements to matters first arising after signing (not just first discovered);
   - provide that supplements cure for the closing condition only, never for Article IX;
   - extend Buyer's period to 15–20 Business Days and make receipt formal (e.g., to named counsel);
   - give Buyer a termination right for any supplement with Losses above a stated threshold;
   - cap the number of supplements and bar them in the last 5 Business Days before closing.
3. Negotiate a specific indemnity for the forklift claim (SPA §9.2 as new clause), the air permit matter, and the Ferrovane loss (see item 3).

**Open points.**
- Which exact day Harrowgate received the Second Supplement, and by what means.
- The Disclosure Schedule as currently supplemented.
- Whether Buyer had any knowledge of these matters before signing. The SPA has no anti-sandbagging or pro-sandbagging clause. New York law on this point is not simple, and I have not researched it. This is a gap and should be fixed.

## 4. Escrow Agreement Conflicts with the SPA

**Where:** Escrow Agreement §1.1 ("Losses"), §4.2, §4.3, §4.6, §3.3, §4.1; SPA §1.1 ("Losses"), §2.4, §9.4, §9.6, §11.11.

**4.1 "Losses" definitions conflict.**
- SPA "Losses" includes reasonable attorneys' fees and diminution in value.
- Escrow "Losses" is "actual out-of-pocket losses ... excluding diminution in value, lost profits, consequential damages, and attorneys' fees and other costs of investigation, defense or enforcement."
- Escrow §4.3 allows disbursement to Buyer "only amounts constituting Losses" (as defined in the Escrow Agreement), and each Claim Notice and instruction must state the amount of "Losses."
- SPA §11.11 says the SPA prevails in a conflict, "except that the Escrow Agreement shall control with respect to escrow disbursement mechanics." The definition of Losses is arguably not a "mechanic," but Seller could argue that §4.3 is a mechanic. Ambiguity is the problem. Expect a dispute at the very point Buyer needs cash.
- **Fix:** conform the Escrow "Losses" definition to refer to the SPA's definition, or delete the definition and §4.3's limiter. Also add to SPA §11.11 that the SPA governs the scope of recoverable Losses.

**4.2 Claims for "reasonably expected" Losses.** Escrow §4.2(a) allows a Claim Notice for Losses incurred "or reasonably expects to incur," but §4.3 pays only "amounts constituting Losses." Pending third-party claims (like the forklift suit) will therefore be held, not paid. Clarify that the Pending Claim Amount is held until resolution (this appears to be the intent of §4.1).

**4.3 Release timing vs. survival.** The Escrow Period (18 months, Escrow §4.1) matches general survival (SPA §9.1). The Fundamental Representations and §4.12 survive longer, and §9.2(c)–(d) claims have no time limit, but the Escrow only secures against claims for 18 months. After release Buyer relies on a direct claim against Seller, a private equity fund (Meridale Capital Partners II, L.P.). Fund distribution and wind-down risk is real (SPA §9.6(b)). **Fix:** extend escrow for tax and fundamental claims, or require a Seller guaranty or a creditworthy parent/GP backstop. Ask the client for the fund's term and remaining life.

**4.4 Escrow cap equals the general cap, but the Escrow is Buyer-funded from the Purchase Price.** SPA §2.3(c) deducts the escrow from Seller's proceeds, so the Purchase Price is not increased, which is fine. But the Escrow Agreement §3.3 treats Buyer as tax owner of the funds, reports all earnings as Buyer's income, and has the Escrow Agent pay Buyer a quarterly tax distribution at an assumed 25%. Buyer's income taxes on income that may ultimately go to Seller is unusual, and the 25% rate is arbitrary. Commonly Seller is the tax owner of the escrow in a stock deal, or the parties treat it as an installment sale. This is a point for tax review. **Fix:** consider moving tax ownership to Seller. Needs tax advice. Also, SPA §1.1 "Escrow Funds" includes earnings and §3.3 adds earnings to principal, so the cap in SPA §9.4(b) (Indemnity Escrow Amount) is arguably the principal only. Clarify.

**4.5 Fees.** Escrow §6.1 splits fees 50/50 and says they are not paid from the Escrow Funds, but Schedule 3 has fees "payable at Closing" and annually. SPA §11.1 is silent. Low priority but align. Also the joint and several indemnity of the Escrow Agent (§5.4) means Buyer could be liable for 100% if Seller fails to pay.

**4.6 Dispute resolution gap.** Escrow §4.2(e) refers to "Section 11.9 of the Purchase Agreement" for disputes. That clause is jurisdiction, so it works. No problem, but if the SPA is assigned under §11.6, Escrow §10.4(a) permits Buyer's assignment. Fine.

**4.7 Blanks.** Escrow date, Schedules 1 and 2 are blank. Ensure KYC (§8.1) is started now, since the Account cannot be opened until the information is provided. Escrow Agent notices are effective only on actual receipt (§9.1), so Buyer must follow up Claim Notices.

**4.8 Objection mechanics.** Seller has 30 days to object (§4.2(b)), then 30 days to negotiate (§4.2(e)), then court. The SPA §9.6(a) mentions "final non-appealable order" but allows joint instructions. A Final Order requires counsel's certificate that the order is final and non-appealable (Escrow §1.1), a slow path. Consider arbitration or expedited process for escrow disputes. This is a business point to raise with the client.

**4.9 Interaction with SPA §9.4.** The SPA says Seller's liability for general rep breaches is capped at the escrow amount ($4.85M, 10% of price) with a $485,000 Deductible (true deductible, not a tipping basket) (§9.4(a)–(b)). The deductible plus the narrow Losses definition effectively reduces recoverable amounts further.

## 5. Other SPA Issues for the Markup

Listed in rough order of importance.

**5.1 No Buyer closing condition or covenant for third-party consents or key customer/supplier retention.** Annex 7.2(d) contains only lender items. See item 1. Also consider a condition tied to Ferrovane or to a minimum revenue level.

**5.2 Section 4.8 representation is narrow.** It covers only termination or reduction "or written notice." It does not cover oral notice or reasonably anticipated reductions. Expand it.

**5.3 Cap and basket structure.**
- Deductible of $485,000 (1% of price) is acceptable but is a deductible rather than a tipping basket. Consider a first-dollar tipping basket.
- Cap equals escrow (10%) for general reps (§9.4(b)). The carve-outs are only Fundamental Representations and §4.12. Fundamental Representations are defined in §1.1 as §§4.1, 4.2, 4.3 and 4.13 and *Buyer's* §§6.1 and 6.2. That formulation makes the Buyer's reps "Fundamental" too, which is unobjectionable in itself, but it omits §4.4(a) (conflict with charter) and title.
- Section 9.4(c) caps Seller's liability at the Purchase Price. But, oddly, the definition of Fundamental Representations includes §4.13 (Brokers) and not §4.5 (financial statements) or §4.4.
- No separate cap or carve-out for breach of covenants (§9.2(b)) — §9.4(b) caps only §9.2(a), so covenants and §9.2(c)–(d) go up to the Purchase Price. That is good for Buyer and should be kept.
- Section 9.4(e) disregards materiality for calculating Losses only, not for determining whether there is a breach (a "single" materiality scrape). Buyer should seek a double scrape.

**5.4 Fraud definition.** §9.4(d) and §9.7 refer to "fraud" with no definition. Seller will likely ask for a narrow one. Buyer should be careful, and may want fraud to include recklessness (a position for the client).

**5.5 Sandbagging.** No express clause. Add a pro-sandbagging provision (reliance and knowledge do not affect indemnity), especially since Buyer now has knowledge from the supplements.

**5.6 Seller credit support.** Seller is a fund. Add a guaranty, or covenant to maintain net assets or not to distribute proceeds until the survival period ends.

**5.7 Section 5.1 conduct covenants.**
- Clause (e) allows "cash dividends paid before the Closing" — no limit. That can strip cash before closing. Because "Closing Working Capital" excludes cash (§2.5(c)), and there is no cash adjustment and no debt-like items beyond Indebtedness, Seller may sweep cash. Nothing in the SPA adds cash to the price, so this is presumably a cash-free/debt-free construct on the Indebtedness side only. Confirm the commercial deal with the client. If the intent was cash-free debt-free, the price mechanic is incomplete (no cash credit). If cash stays in the Company, restrict dividends.
- Clause (c) permits borrowing under the Loan Agreement in the ordinary course, but those borrowings are Indebtedness paid off at closing from the Purchase Price (§2.3(a)). §2.3(c) deducts the payoff from the price paid to Seller. Fine.
- "Indebtedness" definition (§1.1) is thin: borrowed money only, no capital leases, deferred purchase price, unpaid taxes, transaction expenses, change of control payments, or accrued bonuses. Expand.
- Transaction expenses of the Company/Seller are not addressed, apart from the brokers rep (§4.13).

**5.8 Working capital.**
- Target is fixed at $6.2M. The estimate adjustment in §2.5(a) is dollar-for-dollar without collar.
- The post-closing process in §2.5(b) says "Any resulting payment" with no mention of who pays whom, no cap, and no security. Buyer has 90 days, which is good. Add express payment mechanics and Buyer's right to recover from the Escrow (which is intended for indemnity). Also, GAAP "applied consistently" with 2025 audit might conflict with accounting policy.
- Section 9.7 carves §2.5 claims out of exclusivity. Good.
- With the Plant Controller gone, closing statements may be harder. Make sure there is a transition plan.

**5.9 Termination and Outside Date.** The Outside Date is November 30, 2026 (§8.1(b)). The Second Supplement letter proposes conferring about a Closing Date in the week of October 19. The Closing is the third Business Day after the conditions are met (§3.1), and the Payoff Letter is due three Business Days before (§5.8). If Tavinor consent is added, Buyer should have a unilateral extension right.

**5.10 §8.2 and remedies on termination.** No termination fee or expense reimbursement. Only willful breach liability survives. Consider the client's views if the client has incurred material costs.

**5.11 Disclosure Schedule definition and "as supplemented."** The definition (§1.1) bakes supplements into the Disclosure Schedule, which compounds §5.7. If §5.7 is revised, conform the definition.

**5.12 Seller's "knowledge."** Used in §§4.7, 4.10 but not defined. Add a definition, with named individuals and a duty of inquiry. The Company's officers, not just the fund, should be included, since the fund likely has no operating knowledge.

**5.13 Representations missing.** The SPA lacks reps on employees and benefits, environmental (relevant given the air permit matter), insurance, IP, product warranty, related-party transactions, inventory, and the sufficiency of assets, and has a thin tax rep (§4.12). Consider whether the client has done diligence on these; if not, add them. Also, 4.11 refers to compliance in "all material respects."

**5.14 Section 4.1 and Company as a party.** The Company is a party "solely for purposes of Article IV and Article V." After the Closing the Company will be Buyer-owned, so recourse against the Company for its reps is circular. Ensure the indemnity is solely Seller's and that Seller waives contribution rights against the Company. Add this waiver.

**5.15 R&W insurance.** Not addressed. Ask the client whether it is considering it.

**5.16 Drafting points.**
- Section 7.2(e) mentions that the certificate covers the "other deliverables set forth in Section 3.2" — awkward, and §3.2(e) cross-refers to §7.2(e) in a loop. Clean up.
- Section 4.4 says the consents required are "set forth on Annex 7.2(d)," but Annex 7.2(d) lists lender deliverables, not consents. Tidy.
- Section 3.2 does not require delivery of the Tavinor consent (see item 1), or landlord estoppel.
- Section 5.7(b) references §7.2(a), and §8.1(e) is cross-referenced consistently. No error.
- Section 9.2(a) covers the Company's reps "in any certificate," good.
- Section 10.3: transfer taxes borne equally. Typically Seller bears them. Raise only if the client cares.
- Section 2.6 withholding. Fine.
- Section 6.3: Buyer's financing is not conditional. Ask the client whether financing is in place (a "no financing out" position and the specific performance clause at §11.10 means Seller could force closing).
- Section 11.6: collateral assignment to financing sources is permitted only "after the Closing." Lenders typically want this at closing. Raise if financed.

## 6. Headquarters Office Lease

**Where:** Lease §§1.1, 3.2, 4.1, 5, 8.3, 11.1–11.4, 12, 14.2, 16.5.

**Change of control.** Lease §11.3 expressly provides that a sale of Tenant's stock or of any direct or indirect owner "is not a Transfer, and requires neither the consent of nor notice to Landlord." The stock purchase therefore does not need landlord consent. **No lease consent is needed and none should be added as a closing condition.** This is useful to know: the Tavinor MSA and the Lease are treated differently and Seller may point to the Lease as the "model."

**Items worth noting (lower priority):**
- **Governing law is the law of "the State in which the Property is located" (§16.6),** but the lease never states the state. The address is "1180 Elm Ridge Road" with no city or state, and the MSA signature page also gives no city or state. I could not resolve the governing law or the court from the documents. This matters for the enforceability of the remedies in §12.2 and the interpretation of other provisions, but is not a deal issue. Confirm from diligence.
- **Permitted Use is office use only (§1.1(i)).** SPA §4.9 and the Second Supplement refer to a "machining facility" where the forklift incident and the air permit inspection occurred. The HQ lease is limited to office use and is clearly not the machining facility. The SPA says the Company "does not own any real property" (§4.9), so the machining facility must be leased. Section 4.9 requires the Disclosure Schedule to list each lease. **The machining facility lease is not in our folder.** Request it and check its assignment/change-of-control terms. Forklifts and a machining plant implies the plant lease may be more critical than HQ, and may contain change-of-control restrictions. Also request any environmental permits and the air permit.
- **Term and economics.** The term runs through May 31, 2032, with a five-year extension at 95% of Fair Market Rent (§3.2). The notice window is 15–9 months before expiry (that is, roughly February to August 2031), so it is not near term. Base Rent for the current lease year (Lease Year 5, June 1, 2026–May 31, 2027) is $384,892.50 per year. That is above the $250,000 threshold, so the lease is a Material Contract in any case, and it is a lease of real property (§4.7(d)).

Lease does not provide for gross-up. This could lead to higher pass-throughs. Ask the Company for the history of §5.3 statements and whether any audit rights (§5.5, 180 days) are running.
