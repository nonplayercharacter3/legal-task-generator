## 3. Issues, most significant first

### Issue 1: Tavinor change-of-control consent is not covered by the SPA

**Where**
- MSA §14.2 (change of control), §14.1 (assignment "by operation of law"), §13.5 (transition supply), §2.2 (85% requirements), §13.1 (term).
- SPA §4.4(c) and its last sentence, §4.7(b), Annex 7.2(d), §5.3, §7.2(d).

**What the documents say**
- MSA §14.2 deems any transaction giving a person more than 50% of Coldbrook's voting equity to be an assignment. That requires Tavinor's *prior* written consent.
- If the transaction closes without consent, Tavinor has 120 days from written notice of consummation to elect either:
  - (a) termination effective 30 days after its notice, or
  - (b) replacement of the Contract Price with the **List Price** for all later orders. This switches off MSA §§6.1–6.2 and §13.5 (the 12-month transition supply).
- The SPA gives Harrowgate nothing here:
  - Annex 7.2(d) lists only the payoff letter and lien releases.
  - §5.3 obliges Seller to chase only Annex items.
  - §7.2(d) conditions closing only on Annex items.
- SPA §4.4(c) represents that the deal does not require consent under, or create a termination right under, any Material Contract. The last sentence of §4.4 says no consent is needed beyond Annex 7.2(d). On its face that is inaccurate if Tavinor is a Material Contract, which is very likely under §4.7(a)/(b). The rep is subject to the Disclosure Schedule, which we have not seen.

**Why it matters**
- *Repricing trap.* Under option (b), only MSA §§6.1, 6.2 and 13.5 fall away. The Company stays bound to buy at least 85% of its requirements from Tavinor (§2.2) and to honor the binding 90-day Firm Period (§4.2). It would do so at an uncapped List Price with no transition right, and the MSA runs to January 31, 2029 (§13.1).
- *Termination risk.* A 30-day termination leaves no transition supply. Lead times are 10–16 weeks (Schedule A). Items A-1 and A-4 embed Company process parameters. Recital C and MSA §7.4 show Tavinor's routes are qualified with the Company's customers, so requalifying a new source is slow.
- *Weak walk right.* The MAE definition excludes effects of "the announcement of the transactions" (SPA §1.1 clause (c)), with no disproportionality exception. Seller will say a Tavinor loss triggered by the deal is not an MAE.
- *Indemnity is a poor substitute.*
  - Recovery for a §4.4 breach is subject to the $485,000 Deductible (§9.4(a)), the Escrow-sized cap (§9.4(b)) and the 18-month survival period (§9.1).
  - The escrow definition problem in Issue 3 compounds this.
  - The SPA is silent on whether Buyer's pre-closing knowledge limits recovery. That is a risk under New York law, which governs (SPA §11.8).
- *Cure risk.* §5.7(a) lets Seller supplement for matters "of which Seller first becomes aware" after signing. Seller could try to "cure" the Tavinor breach by supplement, claiming it only recently noticed §14.2. That would restart the §5.7 clock and risk another automatic cure.

**Recommended fix**
1. Add the Tavinor consent to Annex 7.2(d) as a Buyer closing condition (§7.2(d)), and extend §5.3 so Seller must use *reasonable best* efforts. The consent should be in form and substance satisfactory to Buyer.
2. The consent should:
   - waive MSA §14.2 for this transaction;
   - confirm the MSA is in full force with no default;
   - confirm that §§6.1, 6.2 and 13.5 are unaffected.
3. Add a Seller covenant that the Company will not amend the MSA or concede terms to obtain consent without Buyer's written approval (see SPA §5.1(d)).
4. Add a first-dollar special indemnity (outside the Deductible and the general cap) for Losses from any Tavinor termination or repricing tied to the transaction. Consider a dedicated holdback if closing without consent.
5. Add an express pro-sandbagging clause.
6. In §5.7(a), limit supplements to matters first *arising* after signing and delete "or of which Seller first becomes aware."
7. Any outreach to Tavinor should be coordinated with the Company, and Harrowgate should decide who leads it.

**Open / client input**
- Annual Tavinor spend, share of cost of goods, and the likely gap between Contract Price and List Price.
- Whether Tavinor is on Disclosure Schedule §4.7/§4.8 and whether any change-of-control language was disclosed.
- Whether the Company has alternative qualified sources (MSA §2.2 allows up to 15%).
- Harrowgate's post-closing plans. A merger or drop-down of Coldbrook would be an assignment "by operation of law" (MSA §14.1) and would need consent too. A consent obtained now should cover those steps.

### Issue 2: SPA §5.7 gives Seller an automatic cure right, and Seller's letter overreaches

**Where**
- SPA §5.7(a)–(c), §1.1 ("Disclosure Schedule"), §8.1(e), §9.2(a), §7.2(a), §7.2(c), §11.5.
- Seller letter (second_schedule_supplement_letter.md) §§2, 3, 5 and Annex A.

**What the documents say**
- Under §5.7(c), the 10-Business-Day window is a one-way ratchet. Silence waives the termination right and cures the inaccuracy *for Article IX*, so there is no indemnity for disclosed matters. This is a seller-friendly structure.
- **Ferrovane.**
  - Under the First Supplement, Ferrovane, a Material Customer at about 18% of revenue, gave written notice of non-renewal (Seller letter §1).
  - That is a direct inaccuracy of the §4.8 rep ("failed to renew... or given written notice").
  - Harrowgate did not terminate within the window, so the matter is cured for indemnity and for §7.2(a) (Seller letter §2).
  - The price is unchanged at $48.5M (SPA §2.1).
- **Cross-disclosure language.** Seller's letter §3 says each Annex A item is also disclosed against any other section "to which its relevance is reasonably apparent." The SPA has no such standard. §5.7 and the Article IV lead-in provide none, and a unilateral letter cannot amend the SPA (§11.5 requires a writing signed by Buyer and Seller).
- **"For all purposes."** Letter §2 says Seller will treat Disclosure Schedule §4.8, as supplemented, as operative "for all purposes of the Agreement." That is broader than §5.7(c), which is limited to Article IX and §7.2(a).
- **Ambiguity on the MAE condition.** §5.7(c) waives the right to terminate "with respect to the matters disclosed." Seller may argue this also bars relying on §7.2(c) (MAE) or the Outside Date as to Ferrovane. The text is ambiguous on whether it reaches the §7.2(c) condition.

**Why it matters**
- Ferrovane's loss is a purchase-price problem that Harrowgate has probably already absorbed contractually. Remaining leverage is limited to the open Tavinor issue, any other §7.2(a) failure, and Seller's wish to close before the **November 30, 2026** Outside Date (§8.1(b)).
- Each Second Supplement item will be cured for indemnity after October 27 (see Issue 5).
- Seller's cross-disclosure language, if left unanswered, will be cited later to defeat claims.

**Recommended fix**
- Send a short response letter before the window closes, so the record is clean. It should:
  - reject the cross-disclosure language and the "all purposes" language, citing §11.5;
  - reserve all rights, including as to §7.2(a)–(c), §5.1 and Article IX;
  - state that nothing in the response waives Buyer's rights on Tavinor or on any matter not disclosed.
  
  A reservation letter cannot defeat §5.7(c), which is automatic, so also negotiate the points below.
- In the markup, rewrite §5.7:
  - supplements should not cure for Article IX or the §7.2(a) condition (at most, they should inform the Buyer's termination right);
  - limit supplements to matters first arising after signing;
  - add a Buyer walk right if cumulative supplements are materially adverse.
- Open price discussions on Ferrovane: a purchase price reduction, or a specific indemnity or holdback tied to Ferrovane's wind-down.

**Open / client input**
- First Supplement text (not in the folder), Ferrovane's expiry date, Ferrovane contract and margin contribution, reason for non-renewal, and whether the client wants to retain it.
- Whether the client reserved rights in September, and the client's view on repricing.

### Issue 3: Escrow Agreement conflicts with the SPA on "Losses" and on release mechanics

**Where**
- Escrow Agreement §1.1 ("Losses"), §1.2, §4.2(c)–(e), §4.3, §4.6, §5.4; SPA §1.1 ("Losses"), §9.4(b), §9.6, §11.11.

**What the documents say**
- **Definition mismatch.**
  - SPA "Losses" includes *diminution in value* and *reasonable attorneys' fees*.
  - Escrow "Losses" is limited to actual out-of-pocket amounts and *excludes* diminution in value, lost profits, consequential damages, and attorneys' fees and costs of investigation, defense and enforcement.
  - Escrow §4.3 lets the Agent pay Buyer "only amounts constituting Losses" as defined in the Escrow Agreement.
  - Escrow §1.2 imports SPA definitions only for terms not defined in the Escrow Agreement. "Losses" is defined there, so the narrow version governs.
  - SPA §11.11 says the Escrow Agreement controls "escrow disbursement mechanics" and otherwise the SPA prevails. Seller will say a definition is part of the mechanics. Others will say it is not.
- **Release trigger mismatch.**
  - SPA §9.6(a) pays from escrow only on a joint written instruction or a final non-appealable order.
  - Escrow §4.2(c) releases on Seller's silence after 30 days, which is favorable to Buyer.

**Why it matters**
- If the narrow definition governs, a legitimate indemnity claim for legal fees or diminution in value could be partly unrecoverable from the escrow. Anything left over must be chased from Seller directly under §9.6(b), with a cap for general reps. The escrow cap of $4.85M (§9.4(b)) is effectively the whole fund for general-rep claims.
- Seller is a fund limited partnership. The documents contain no guarantee, no parent support and no distribution restriction. Direct recovery after the escrow is released is only as good as Seller's solvency.

**Recommended fix**
- Replace the Escrow "Losses" definition with "has the meaning given in the Purchase Agreement."
- Revise SPA §11.11 to say the Escrow Agreement controls only the Agent's procedural steps and never limits what Buyer may recover under Article IX.
- Conform SPA §9.6(a) to accept Escrow §4.2(c) (deemed acceptance after the Objection Period).
- Consider a Seller creditworthiness fix (parent or fund guarantee, or a covenant not to distribute proceeds below a floor for 18 months).

**Other escrow clean-up (lower priority)**
- **KYC.** Escrow §8.1 says the Agent may not open the account until KYC is complete. Start now so closing isn't delayed.
- **Blank schedules.** Schedules 1–2 and the date are blank. Schedule 1 (authorized signatories and call-back numbers) and Schedule 2 (wires) must be done before closing.
- **Joint and several indemnity.** §5.4 makes Buyer liable for 100% of the Agent's indemnity claims, with a 50/50 split only between Buyer and Seller. Confirm that is acceptable.
- **Tax treatment.** §3.3 treats Buyer as owner of the escrow funds and taxes it on earnings, with quarterly tax distributions at an assumed 25%. That is favorable to Buyer but may draw Seller pushback. Our tax group should confirm.
- **Fees.** SPA §11.1 says each party bears its own expenses, and Escrow §6.1 splits Agent fees 50/50. Confirm that is the intended allocation.

### Issue 6: Other SPA drafting points for the markup

1. **§4.10 scope.** As drafted, "against the Company or against Seller relating to the Shares or the transactions" may limit the rep to litigation about the Shares or the deal, so the forklift suit might not be a breach at all. Broaden it to any action involving the Company.
2. **Undefined "knowledge."** "Seller's knowledge" is used in §4.7 and §4.10 but not defined in §1.1. Define it as actual and constructive knowledge of named individuals, including Company management and Meridale deal team members.
3. **Materiality scrape (§9.4(e)).** It applies only to *calculating Losses*. Extend it to determining whether a breach occurred.
4. **Working capital true-up (§2.5(b)).** Seller's payment is unsecured and not clearly recoverable from the escrow (§2.4 limits it to Article IX amounts). Provide a separate adjustment escrow, or allow set-off against the indemnity escrow.
5. **Cash and leakage.** §5.1(e) permits "cash dividends paid before the Closing," and §2.5(c) excludes cash from working capital. Confirm that the deal is intended to be cash-free and debt-free. "Indebtedness" (§1.1) covers only borrowed money and doesn't pick up unpaid transaction expenses or accrued income taxes.
6. **MAE definition.** Add a carve-back so that the announcement exclusion in clause (c) does not apply to the §4.4 no-consent rep or any condition tied to consents.
7. **Rep coverage.** The rep set is thin for an industrial manufacturer. Consider adding undisclosed liabilities, environmental/EHS, employee/benefits/workers' compensation, insurance, title to assets and affiliate transactions. The Annex A items point to exposures in this area.
8. **Notices.** §11.2 refers to "addresses set forth on the signature pages," but the SPA signature pages contain none. Insert addresses so notice and the §5.7 clock aren't disputed.
9. **Post-closing recourse.** §9.6(b) relies on Seller's credit once the escrow is released. See the Issue 3 fix.
10. **Financing.** §6.3 has no financing condition and §11.10 allows specific performance. Client to confirm financing certainty.

### Issue 7: HQ office lease: no deal issue, with housekeeping items

**Where:** Lease §11.3, §11.1, §14.2, §3.2, §4.1, §5.

- **No consent needed.** §11.3 states that a sale or transfer of Tenant's stock "or of any direct or indirect owner" is not a Transfer and needs neither consent nor notice. Do **not** add the Landlord to Annex 7.2(d).
- **Post-closing mergers.** §11.3(b) permits assignment on a merger or sale of substantially all assets without consent, if Tenant gives notice within 30 days after the Transfer and the assignee assumes in writing. Useful for integration planning, but compare Tavinor (Issue 1).
- **Estoppel.** Lease §14.2 lets Tenant request an estoppel certificate, due within 10 Business Days. It is a cheap request and gives comfort that rent is current, no default exists and there are no amendments. Make it a closing deliverable if the lease matters to Harrowgate.
- **Rent schedule.** I recomputed each year's rent in §4.1 (rate × 14,250 RSF, and monthly = annual ÷ 12). All figures tie. Tenant's Share (23.75%) also ties (14,250 ÷ 60,000).
- **Term and renewal.** The term ends May 31, 2032, with one five-year option at 95% of Fair Market Rent. The notice window is roughly 15 to 9 months before expiration (§3.2). Nothing near-term.
- **Open point.** The lease's governing-law clause (§16.6) and the address do not name a state, so we can't determine governing law from the document alone.
- **Low priority.** The Base Year is calendar 2022 (§5.1), a partial year with no gross-up clause, so the pass-through base may be low. Confirm the 2023–2025 reconciliations are paid, and ask whether the audit window (§5.5, 180 days) is relevant.
