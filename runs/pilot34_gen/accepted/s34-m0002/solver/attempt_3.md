## Issue 1 – Set-Off Right Gutted by Conflict Between APA and Seller Note

**Where:** APA § 8.7 (Right of Set-Off); Seller Note § 4.2 (Set-Off) and § 1 (definition of "Losses"); APA § 11.4 (Order of Precedence).

**Why it matters:** APA § 8.7 permits Tipul to set off "the amount of any Losses that Buyer claims in good faith" against principal or interest on the Seller Note. This is a critical self-help remedy: it lets Tipul withhold note payments while an indemnification claim is pending, without having to litigate first.

However, the Seller Note defines "Losses" (Note § 1) as only "amounts that have been finally determined by a court of competent jurisdiction, in a judgment that is no longer subject to appeal." Note § 4.2 then limits set-off to those judicially determined amounts. APA § 11.4 provides that "the Seller Note controls on matters concerning the indebtedness it evidences." Set-off against the note is squarely a matter concerning the indebtedness it evidences. The Note's narrow definition therefore prevails, and Tipul's good-faith set-off right under APA § 8.7 is effectively inoperative.

In practice, this means that if Tipul has a valid indemnification claim against Nilur (e.g., for an Excluded Liability), Tipul must continue making full note payments and then pursue the claim separately—losing the leverage and cash-flow protection the set-off was designed to provide.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Note § 4.2 (and the "Losses" definition in Note § 1) to conform to APA § 8.7. Proposed language for Note § 4.2:

> "Maker may set off against any amount of principal or interest due under this Note the amount of any Losses that Maker claims in good faith under Article VIII of the Purchase Agreement, by written notice to Holder describing the claim in reasonable detail. If the claim is later finally resolved for less than the amount set off, Maker shall pay the difference to Holder within ten (10) Business Days after that resolution, together with interest at the Interest Rate. The exercise of this right in good faith does not constitute a default under this Note."

Alternatively, if Nilur resists, add a carve-out in APA § 11.4: "…except that the Seller Note controls on matters concerning the indebtedness it evidences, **provided that Section 8.7 of this Agreement shall control over Section 4.2 of the Seller Note**."

## Issue 2 – 12-Month Survival for Excluded Liability Indemnification Is Inadequate for Implanted Devices

**Where:** APA § 8.2(c) (indemnification for Excluded Liabilities); APA § 8.1(a) (12-month survival for non-Fundamental Representations); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Pre-Closing Product Liabilities"); APA § 2.4(c) (Pre-Closing Product Liabilities are Excluded Liabilities).

**Why it matters:** Section 8.2(c) requires Seller to indemnify Buyer for "any Excluded Liability," which includes all Pre-Closing Product Liabilities (defined in § 1.1 as liabilities arising from Products "designed, manufactured, sold, distributed or implanted before the Closing Date…regardless of when the claim is asserted"). The indemnity is therefore intended to cover claims that surface years after implantation.

But § 8.2(c) then states: "Any claim for indemnification under Section 8.2(c) shall be asserted by written notice to Seller within the survival period set forth in Section 8.1(a)." Section 8.1(a) is 12 months. So Tipul has only 12 months post-closing to assert a claim for a product liability injury from a knee or hip implant placed before closing.

For orthopedic implants, this is commercially unreasonable. Revision surgeries, loosening, infection, and fracture claims routinely arise 2–5+ years after implantation. A 12-month window means that most Pre-Closing Product Liability claims will fall outside the indemnification period, leaving Tipul bearing the loss with no recourse against Nilur.

**Recommended fix:** Decouple the Excluded Liability survival from the general 12-month period. Add a new sentence to § 8.2(c) or a new subsection:

> "Notwithstanding Section 8.1(a), any claim for indemnification under this Section 8.2(c) arising from a Pre-Closing Product Liability shall be asserted within the later of (i) twelve (12) months after the Closing Date, or (ii) the statutory limitations period applicable to such claim in the jurisdiction where the Product was implanted, plus six (6) months."

At minimum, extend the survival for § 8.2(c) claims to six years (matching the Fundamental Representation survival in § 8.1(b) and the product liability tail in § 6.7).

## Issue 3 – No Evidence of Required Product Liability Tail Insurance

**Where:** APA § 6.7 (Seller Product Liability Insurance); Ostrander Pike cover letter (addresses only property/inventory insurance under APA § 6.6).

**Why it matters:** Section 6.7 requires Seller to "maintain, or purchase extended reporting period coverage under, product liability insurance covering the Products sold before the Closing Date for not less than six years after the Closing Date, with limits not less than those in effect on the date of this Agreement, and shall cause Buyer to be named as an additional insured."

The only insurance documentation in the folder is the Ostrander Pike cover letter, which evidences property and inland marine coverage for inventory (the § 6.6 requirement). There is no certificate, no binder, no evidence that Nilur has arranged—or even committed to arrange—the § 6.7 product liability tail.

This is not a theoretical concern. Given Issue 2 (12-month indemnification survival), the § 6.7 tail is Tipul's primary protection against Pre-Closing Product Liabilities that surface after the indemnification window closes. If Nilur has not secured the tail, and the indemnification period expires, Tipul could be left holding the bag for a multi-year product liability exposure with no insurance and no indemnitor.

Additionally, § 6.7 is a pre-closing covenant (it says Seller "shall maintain" until the Closing), but the obligation to carry coverage for six years post-closing is a post-closing covenant. It survives under § 8.1(c), but a breach would be subject to the $4.85M cap in § 8.4(b) (since § 6.7 is not a Fundamental Representation).

**Recommended fix:**
- Before signing, require Nilur to deliver a certificate of product liability insurance (or a binder for an extended reporting period / "tail" policy) evidencing: (i) coverage for Pre-Closing Products; (ii) a six-year reporting period post-closing; (iii) limits no lower than current; and (iv) Tipul named as additional insured.
- Add this as a Seller closing deliverable in § 3.2 (e.g., new § 3.2(i): "a certificate of product liability insurance or extended reporting period coverage complying with Section 6.7").
- Make delivery of such certificate a condition to Buyer's closing obligation in § 7.1.
- Flag for discussion with Nilur's counsel: whether the tail is already in place or whether a premium payment is needed, and who bears that cost.

**Unresolved from documents alone:** We cannot confirm whether Nilur has actually purchased or can purchase the required tail. This should be raised with Nilur's counsel immediately.

## Issue 4 – Seller Note Governed by Minnesota Law and Subject to Minnesota Exclusive Jurisdiction

**Where:** Seller Note §§ 10.1–10.2; compare APA § 11.8 (Delaware governing law) and § 11.9 (Delaware exclusive forum); IP Assignment § 6.5 (Delaware governing law) and § 6.6 (Delaware exclusive forum).

**Why it matters:** Every other document in the package is governed by Delaware law and subject to Delaware exclusive jurisdiction. The Note alone is governed by Minnesota law and must be litigated in Hennepin County, Minnesota. This creates:

- **Interpretive risk:** Cross-referenced terms (e.g., "Losses," "Event of Default," "Senior Indebtedness") may be construed differently under Minnesota law than under the Delaware-law APA.
- **Forum conflict:** A dispute that implicates both the APA (e.g., an indemnification claim) and the Note (e.g., whether a set-off is permissible) could be subject to competing exclusive jurisdiction clauses, forcing Tipul to litigate in two forums or face a motion to dismiss.
- **Practical burden:** Tipul is a Delaware corporation; requiring it to defend a note dispute in Minnesota is an unnecessary cost and inconvenience.

There is no apparent business reason for the Minnesota choice (Nilur is also a Delaware LLC). This may be a drafting error or a holdover from a prior form.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Note §§ 10.1 and 10.2 to conform to the APA:

> § 10.1: "…governed by the laws of the State of **Delaware**…"
> § 10.2: "…exclusive jurisdiction of the Court of Chancery of the State of **Delaware** (or, only if that court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware or the United States District Court for the District of Delaware)…"

If Nilur resists, at minimum add a provision that in the event of a conflict between the Note and the APA on any cross-referenced term, the APA's Delaware-law interpretation controls.

## Issue 6 – Insurance Certificate: Policy Terms Not Accessible; Short Cancellation Notice for Non-Payment

**Where:** Ostrander Pike cover letter, § 4 (Nature of the Certificate) and § 3 (Notice of Cancellation).

**Why it matters:**
- **§ 4:** "Copies of the policy forms and endorsements are available on request, **subject to Nilur's consent**." Tipul is named as loss payee and certificate holder but cannot review the actual policy to confirm exclusions, sublimits, or conditions that could negate coverage. For a $48.5M transaction where inventory is a key purchased asset, this is an unacceptable information gap.
- **§ 3:** The notice period for cancellation due to non-payment of premium is only **10 days**. The policy period runs to January 1, 2027, and the closing is April 15, 2026. If Nilur has a cash-flow problem and lets the premium lapse, Tipul could lose coverage on the inventory with only 10 days' warning.

**Recommended fix:**
- Require Nilur to deliver copies of the full policy forms, endorsements, and declarations page (not just the certificate) as a condition to closing or before signing. Remove the "subject to Nilur's consent" qualifier.
- Negotiate the non-payment notice period to 30 days (matching the general cancellation notice), or require Nilur to prepay the premium through the Closing Date.
- Add to APA § 3.2 (Seller Closing Deliverables): "certified copies of the property insurance policies and all endorsements in effect under Section 6.6."

## Issue 7 – Broad Information and Inspection Rights in the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note § 6.2 (Information and Inspection Rights).

**Why it matters:** The Note requires Tipul to provide Nilur with:
- Monthly unaudited financial statements (certain by CFO);
- Annual audited financials;
- Quarterly Product Line reports including **unit sales and net revenue by Product and by region, average selling prices, and a list of hospital, ASC, and distributor customers with revenue attributable to each** (§ 6.2(c)); and
- Inspection rights with five days' notice.

This is unusually intrusive for a seller note. The customer-level revenue data in § 6.2(c) gives Nilur a detailed, ongoing picture of Tipul's commercial operations in the exact product line Nilur is contractually barred from entering (APA § 6.5 non-compete). While this could be framed as monitoring for non-compete compliance, it also provides Nilur with competitive intelligence that could be used in adjacent markets, licensing discussions, or a future re-entry strategy. Combined with Note § 9 (Nilur can transfer the Note to any third party without Tipul's consent), this information could end up in the hands of an unknown party.

**Recommended fix:**
- Narrow § 6.2(c) to aggregate Product Line revenue and unit volume by region, removing the customer-level detail. If Nilur insists on customer-level data for non-compete monitoring, limit it to top-20 customers and add a confidentiality restriction specific to the Note.
- Add a provision that any transferee of the Note under § 9 must agree in writing to be bound by the confidentiality obligations of APA § 6.4 before receiving any information under § 6.2.
- Limit inspection rights to once per year (rather than the current "upon five Business Days' prior notice" with no frequency cap), and to the Product Line facilities only.

## Issue 8 – Indemnification Cap Is Low and Key Reps Are Not "Fundamental"

**Where:** APA § 8.4(b) (cap); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations").

**Why it matters:** The cap for non-Fundamental representation breaches is $4,850,000 (10% of the Purchase Price). The Fundamental Representations are limited to Organization, Authority, Title, Taxes, and Brokers. Critically, the following are **not** Fundamental:
- § 4.6 (Intellectual Property – ownership and non-infringement);
- § 4.7 (Regulatory Compliance – FDA, QSR, recalls, 483s);
- § 4.8 (Product Liability – no known defects);
- § 4.9 (Inventory – shelf life, quality);
- § 4.11 (Material Contracts).

A material IP ownership defect, an undisclosed FDA enforcement action, or a systemic product defect would all be subject to the $4.85M cap. For a product line involving implanted medical devices, a single undisclosed design defect could generate liabilities well in excess of $4.85M.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add §§ 4.6 (IP), 4.7 (Regulatory), and 4.8 (Product Liability) to the definition of "Fundamental Representations" in § 1.1, so that breaches of those reps are subject to the Purchase Price cap ($48.5M) rather than the $4.85M cap.
- Alternatively, if Nilur resists reclassifying the reps, increase the general cap to at least 20–25% of the Purchase Price ($9.7M–$12.1M) and add a "super-cap" of the full Purchase Price for IP and regulatory reps.

## Issue 9 – Note Transferability Without Maker Consent

**Where:** Seller Note § 9 (Transfer).

**Why it matters:** "Holder may sell, assign, pledge or otherwise transfer this Note and all of its rights under this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker." This means Nilur can sell the $9M note to a hedge fund, a distressed-debt buyer, or—worst case—an affiliate of a Tipul competitor. The transferee would inherit all information and inspection rights under § 6.2 (see Issue 7) and all enforcement rights under § 8.

There is no restriction on who can receive the Note, no notice requirement beyond payment instructions, and no requirement that the transferee assume confidentiality obligations.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a permitted-transferee limitation: "Holder may transfer this Note only to (a) an Affiliate of Holder, or (b) a financial institution or investment fund that agrees in writing to be bound by the confidentiality provisions of Section 6.4 of the Purchase Agreement and the information restrictions of Section 6.2 of this Note."
- Require 10 Business Days' prior written notice to Maker of any proposed transfer, identifying the proposed transferee.
- Add that a transfer in violation of this Section does not relieve Maker of its payment obligations but constitutes a breach giving Maker the right to prepay the Note without penalty.
