I have reviewed the Asset Purchase Agreement (APA), the Subordinated Seller Note (Note), the IP Assignment Agreement (IP Assignment), and the Ostrander Pike insurance certificate cover letter (Cover Letter). ## Issue 1 – 12-Month Survival Period for Excluded Liability Indemnification

**Where:** APA § 8.2(c) (last sentence) read with APA § 8.1(a).

**What it says:** Section 8.2(c) obligates Seller to indemnify Buyer for "any Excluded Liability." The final sentence of § 8.2 then provides: "Any claim for indemnification under Section 8.2(c) shall be asserted by written notice to Seller within the survival period set forth in Section 8.1(a)." Section 8.1(a) sets that period at **12 months** after the Closing Date.

**Why it matters:** Excluded Liabilities include all Pre-Closing Product Liabilities (APA § 2.4(c)), defined as liabilities "arising out of or relating to any Product designed, manufactured, sold, distributed or implanted before the Closing Date, including Liabilities for death, personal injury, revision surgery, product defect, breach of warranty, failure to warn, recall, field correction or market withdrawal, **regardless of when the claim is asserted**" (APA § 1.1). Orthopedic implant failures, revision surgeries, and associated product liability claims routinely surface three to ten or more years after implantation. A 12-month notice window means that if a patient implanted in 2024 files a malpractice or product defect claim in 2028, Tipul will have no indemnification recourse against Nilur. The "regardless of when the claim is asserted" language in the definition of Pre-Closing Product Liabilities confirms the parties contemplated late-arising claims, making the 12-month cutoff internally inconsistent and commercially unreasonable.

**Recommended fix:** Carve Excluded Liability indemnification claims out of the 12-month survival period. Proposed language for a new sentence at the end of § 8.2:

> "Notwithstanding the foregoing, any claim for indemnification under Section 8.2(c) arising from a Pre-Closing Product Liability shall be asserted by written notice to Seller within the later of (i) twelve (12) months after the Closing Date, or (ii) the date on which the underlying claim, action or proceeding is first asserted against Buyer or any Buyer Indemnitee, provided that in no event shall such claim be asserted more than six (6) years after the Closing Date."

The six-year outer limit aligns with the Fundamental Representations survival period (§ 8.1(b)) and with typical medical device product liability statutes of repose.

**Flag:** This is the single most commercially significant issue in the package. I recommend we make this a condition to signing.

## Issue 2 – Set-Off Conflict Between APA § 8.7 and Note § 4.2

**Where:** APA § 8.7 (Right of Set-Off) vs. Note § 4.2 (Set-Off) and Note § 1 (definition of "Losses"); APA § 11.4 (Order of Precedence).

**What it says:**

- **APA § 8.7** permits Buyer to set off against the Seller Note "the amount of any Losses that Buyer **claims in good faith** under this Article VIII, by written notice to Seller describing the claim in reasonable detail."
- **Note § 4.2** permits Maker to set off "only **Losses**," which Note § 1 defines as "amounts that have been **finally determined by a court of competent jurisdiction, in a judgment that is no longer subject to appeal**, to be owed by Holder to Maker under Article VIII of the Purchase Agreement."
- **APA § 11.4** provides that the APA controls over the Ancillary Agreements, "except that the Seller Note controls on matters concerning the indebtedness it evidences."

**Why it matters:** Set-off against the Note is unambiguously a matter "concerning the indebtedness it evidences," so the Note's stricter standard governs. Under the Note, Tipul cannot set off a good-faith indemnification claim until it has obtained a final, non-appealable court judgment against Nilur. In practice, this means Tipul must litigate to judgment (potentially years, given the exclusive forum provisions) before it can reduce its Note payments. The APA's broader set-off right is effectively nullified. This removes Tipul's principal self-help lever for collecting indemnification and converts the Note into a pure cash-outflow obligation with no offset for known or likely losses.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Note § 4.2 (and the Note's definition of "Losses") to conform to APA § 8.7. Proposed replacement for Note § 4.2:

> "Maker may set off against any amount of principal or interest due under this Note the amount of any Losses that Maker claims in good faith under Article VIII of the Purchase Agreement, by written notice to Holder describing the claim in reasonable detail. If the claim is later finally resolved for less than the amount set off, Maker shall pay the difference to Holder within ten (10) Business Days after that resolution, together with interest at the Interest Rate. The exercise of this right in good faith does not constitute a default under this Note."

Alternatively, if Nilur resists, we should at minimum lower the threshold from "final, non-appealable judgment" to "amount agreed in writing by the Parties or determined by a binding arbitration or court order that is no longer subject to appeal."

## Issue 3 – No Evidence of Required Product Liability Insurance

**Where:** APA § 6.7 (Seller Product Liability Insurance); Cover Letter (entire document).

**What it says:** APA § 6.7 requires Seller to "maintain, or purchase extended reporting period coverage under, product liability insurance covering the Products sold before the Closing Date for not less than six years after the Closing Date, with limits not less than those in effect on the date of this Agreement, and shall cause Buyer to be named as an additional insured." The Cover Letter and the enclosed Certificate address only **commercial property and inland marine transit coverage** for inventory. No product liability policy, certificate, or evidence of extended reporting period (tail) coverage appears anywhere in the matter folder.

**Why it matters:** Product liability insurance is the primary funding source for pre-closing product claims. If Seller does not actually maintain the required coverage—or if the policy has a short tail that expires before the six-year period—Tipul's indemnification claim under § 8.2(c) (already hampered by the 12-month survival issue) will be an unsecured claim against a seller that may have distributed its assets. For an orthopedic implant product line, a single catastrophic failure or class action could generate liabilities well in excess of the $4.85M non-fundamental cap or even the full Purchase Price cap.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Require Seller to deliver, as a closing condition (add to APA § 3.2), a certificate of product liability insurance (or extended reporting period endorsement) evidencing: (a) coverage for pre-closing Products for at least six years post-closing; (b) limits not less than those in effect on the APA date; and (c) Buyer named as an additional insured.
2. Add to APA § 7.1 (Conditions to Buyer's Obligations) a new sub-section: "(f) Seller has delivered a certificate of product liability insurance or extended reporting period endorsement substantially in the form of Exhibit [●], evidencing the coverage required by Section 6.7."
3. If Seller does not currently carry product liability insurance, require it to purchase such coverage (or a tail) as a condition to closing, with the premium for Seller's account.

**Flag:** We need to ask Nilur's counsel immediately whether product liability coverage exists and what the limits are. This cannot be resolved from the documents alone.

## Issue 4 – Inconsistent Governing Law: Note Governed by Minnesota Law

**Where:** Note § 10.1 (Governing Law) vs. APA § 11.8 (Governing Law) and IP Assignment § 6.5 (Governing Law).

**What it says:** The APA and IP Assignment are both governed by Delaware law. The Note is governed by Minnesota law, with exclusive jurisdiction in Hennepin County state courts or the U.S. District Court for the District of Minnesota (Note § 10.2).

**Why it matters:** This creates a three-document, two-law, two-forum structure. Any dispute that touches both the APA (e.g., whether an indemnification obligation was triggered) and the Note (e.g., whether a set-off is proper) will raise threshold choice-of-law and forum questions. Minnesota law may interpret set-off rights, subordination, or default provisions differently than Delaware law. It also forces Tipul to litigate in Minnesota for Note-related disputes while litigating in Delaware for APA-related disputes, increasing cost and complexity. There is no apparent reason for the Minnesota selection—Tipul is a Delaware corporation and the APA contemplates Delaware jurisdiction.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Note § 10.1 to provide that the Note is governed by Delaware law, and § 10.2 to submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Delaware Court of Chancery (or Superior Court / D. Del.), consistent with APA § 11.9. If Nilur insists on Minnesota for some business reason, at minimum add a provision that any dispute requiring interpretation of the APA or its indemnification provisions shall be resolved under Delaware law and in the Delaware forum.

## Issue 5 – Unrestricted Transfer Right in the Note

**Where:** Note § 9 (Transfer).

**What it says:** "Holder may sell, assign, pledge or otherwise transfer this Note and all of its rights under this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker."

**Why it matters:** Nilur can sell or pledge the $9M Note to any third party—a distressed debt fund, a competitor, or an entity with no relationship to the transaction—without Tipul's consent. A new holder would step into Nilur's shoes with full rights to demand monthly financials (Note § 6.2), inspect Tipul's books (Note § 6.2), and enforce the Note. This creates a confidentiality risk (the monthly and quarterly reports include customer-level revenue data) and a strategic risk (an aggressive holder may be less willing to negotiate set-offs or forbearance).

**Recommended fix:** Add a restriction to Note § 9:

> "Holder may not transfer this Note to (a) any competitor of Maker or the Product Line (as determined by Maker in good faith), (b) any Person that is a direct or indirect competitor in the orthopedic implant market, or (c) any Person designated by Maker in writing as a prohibited transferee, in each case so long as the transfer would reasonably be expected to result in the disclosure of Maker's confidential information to such Person. Any transfer in violation of this sentence shall be void."

Alternatively, require Holder to give Maker 30 days' prior notice of any proposed transfer and to covenant that the transferee will be bound by the confidentiality provisions of the APA.

## Issue 6 – Aggressive Information and Inspection Rights in the Note

**Where:** Note § 6.2 (Information and Inspection Rights).

**What it says:** Maker must deliver: (a) **monthly** unaudited financial statements certified by the CFO within 30 days of month-end; (b) annual audited financials within 120 days; (c) **quarterly** Product Line reports including unit sales, net revenue by product and region, average selling prices, and a **list of hospital, ASC and distributor customers with revenue attributable to each**; and (d) such other information as Holder "may reasonably request." Additionally, Holder may inspect books, records and properties on five Business Days' notice.

**Why it matters:** Monthly CFO-certified financials are unusual for a seller note and impose a significant administrative burden. The customer-level revenue disclosure is particularly concerning: if Tipul has other product lines or strategic relationships, this data could be competitively sensitive. The "such other information as Holder may reasonably request" language is open-ended and could be used to extract information well beyond what is necessary to monitor credit risk. If the Note is transferred (see Issue 5), this information flows to a third party.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Reduce monthly reporting to quarterly (consistent with the Product Line report cadence).
2. Delete or narrow the customer-level revenue list. Proposed replacement: "a summary of the top ten customers by revenue for the Product Line, without identifying individual customers, unless required for Holder's reasonable credit monitoring."
3. Add a confidentiality proviso: "All information delivered under this Section 6.2 shall be treated as confidential information of Maker subject to Section 6.4 of the Purchase Agreement, and Holder shall not disclose such information to any third party except to its professional advisors under obligations of confidentiality."
4. Narrow the inspection right to financial records and Product Line records only, and increase notice to 10 Business Days.

## Issue 7 – IP Assignment Schedules Are Placeholders

**Where:** IP Assignment, Schedule A (Assigned Patents) and Schedule B (Assigned Marks).

**What it says:** Both schedules contain only placeholder entries: "[●]" for every field, with a footnote stating they are "[t]o conform to the patents and patent applications listed on Schedule 2.1(b) of the Purchase Agreement."

**Why it matters:** We cannot verify what IP is actually being assigned. The definition of "Assigned Patents" (IP Assignment § 1.2) is broad and includes continuations, divisionals, foreign counterparts, and "all inventions and improvements disclosed or claimed in any of the foregoing." But without the actual patent and application numbers, we cannot confirm: (a) that all relevant IP is captured; (b) that the patents are not subject to liens or security interests; (c) that there are no pending oppositions or inter partes reviews; or (d) that the schedule is consistent with APA Schedule 2.1(b). If a key patent is omitted, Tipul may discover the gap only after closing, when the 12-month survival period for the IP representation (APA § 4.6) has already run.

**Recommended fix:** Require fully populated Schedules A and B as a condition to closing. Cross-reference each entry against APA Schedule 2.1(b). Add a closing deliverable requiring Seller to confirm in writing that the schedules are complete and accurate.

## Issue 8 – Retained Design History Files and Complaint Files

**Where:** IP Assignment § 3.2 (Retained Copies).

**What it says:** Assignor may retain "one archival copy of the Design History Files and complaint files solely to the extent required by Law for Assignor's regulatory record-retention obligations for Products sold before the Closing Date."

**Why it matters:** Design History Files contain detailed manufacturing processes, design rationale, verification and validation data, and risk management information. Complaint files contain patient injury reports, root cause analyses, and corrective action records. Even limited to pre-closing products, these files give Nilur a detailed understanding of Tipul's manufacturing processes and product weaknesses. While APA § 6.5 imposes a five-year global non-compete on knee and hip implants, the non-compete does not cover other medical device categories, and the confidentiality obligation (APA § 6.4) is only as strong as its enforcement. The phrase "required by Law" is vague—FDA record retention rules (21 C.F.R. § 820.180) require retention for the life of the device plus two years, but do not necessarily require retention of the *complete* Design History File in a form usable for product development.

**Recommended fix:** Narrow the retention right:

> "Assignor may retain one archival copy of the complaint files and Medical Device Reports solely to the extent required by 21 C.F.R. § 803.24 or other applicable Law for Assignor's regulatory obligations with respect to Products sold before the Closing Date. Assignor shall not retain any Design History Files, device master records, or manufacturing process documentation. Any retained complaint files shall be redacted to remove any information relating to Products manufactured or sold by Assignee after the Closing Date."

If Nilur resists deleting all DHFs, at minimum require a written certification from Nilur's regulatory counsel specifying exactly which records are "required by Law" and in what form.

## Issue 9 – Knowledge Qualifier on Product Liability Representation

**Where:** APA § 4.8 (Product Liability).

**What it says:** "To Seller's **knowledge**, there is no design or manufacturing defect in any Product."

**Why it matters:** The knowledge qualifier means Seller is not liable for defects it did not actually know about. For a product line with multiple generations of implants, it is plausible that a design flaw exists that was not identified by Seller's engineering or quality teams. Combined with the 12-month survival period (Issue 1) and the $4.85M cap (APA § 8.4(b)), Tipul's recourse for an unknown pre-closing design defect is severely limited. The representation also covers only the five-year lookback for the disclosure schedule listing of claims, not for the defect representation itself.

**Recommended fix:** Delete the knowledge qualifier from the second sentence of § 4.8, or at minimum change it to "to Seller's knowledge after reasonable inquiry." Proposed language:

> "There is no known or, after reasonable inquiry by Seller's engineering, quality and regulatory personnel, suspected design or manufacturing defect in any Product that would reasonably be expected to result in a recall, field correction, market withdrawal or product liability claim."

If Nilur resists, make this a Fundamental Representation so it benefits from the six-year survival period and the full Purchase Price cap.

## Issue 10 – Tipul Is Loss Payee, Not Additional Insured, on Pre-Closing Property Policy

**Where:** Cover Letter § 2(e) (Certificate Holder and Loss Payee) and § 4 (Nature of the Certificate).

**What it says:** Tipul is designated as "Certificate Holder and Loss Payee: Tipul Orthopedics, Inc., as its interest may appear." Section 4 states the Certificate "confers no rights on the certificate holder other than those provided by the loss payee designation and the notice of cancellation endorsement."

**Why it matters:** As a loss payee, Tipul has a right to receive insurance proceeds for loss of the inventory, but it does not have the right to make or control claims, negotiate with the insurer, or receive the full scope of policy defenses. If a transit loss occurs just before closing and the insurer disputes coverage (e.g., arguing the loss was due to an exclusion), Tipul as loss payee has a weaker position than Tipul as additional insured. Additionally, the "as its interest may appear" language is standard but can be contested by insurers in subrogation or coverage disputes.

**Recommended fix:** Request that the insurer endorse the policy to name Tipul as an **additional insured** (not merely loss payee) for the pre-closing period, or alternatively require Seller to assign the policy to Tipul at closing (consistent with APA § 6.6's assignment of insurance proceeds). If the policy cannot be amended, require Seller to deliver a signed waiver of subrogation in Tipul's favor.

## Issue 11 – Short Cancellation Notice for Non-Payment

**Where:** Cover Letter § 3 (Notice of Cancellation).

**What it says:** "For non-payment of premium, the notice period is 10 days."

**Why it matters:** The policy period runs through January 1, 2027, and the Closing is April 15, 2026. If Nilur fails to pay a premium installment and the insurer gives only 10 days' notice of cancellation, there is a risk that coverage could lapse in the window between cancellation and Closing, leaving the inventory uninsured. APA § 6.6 places risk of loss on Seller until Closing, but if the policy is cancelled, Seller may not have the financial wherewithal to make Tipul whole, and Tipul's loss payee rights (Issue 10) would be of no use if there is no policy in force.

**Recommended fix:** Add to APA § 6.6: "Seller shall provide Buyer with immediate written notice (and in any event no later than the date of cancellation) if Seller receives any notice of cancellation, non-renewal or material modification of the property insurance required by this Section 6.6. If such insurance is cancelled or lapses before the Closing Date, Seller shall, at its sole cost and expense, restore coverage or provide equivalent coverage before the Closing Date, and the cancellation shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement giving rise to Buyer's right to terminate under Section 9.1(c)."

## Issue 12 – Selling Price Valuation for Finished Goods

**Where:** Cover Letter § 2(d) (Valuation).

**What it says:** "Selling price for finished goods. Replacement cost for raw materials and work in process."

**Why it matters:** "Selling price" for finished orthopedic implants is typically lower than the full replacement cost (which includes manufacturing overhead, profit margin, and the cost of re-sterilizing and repackaging). If a significant inventory loss occurs (fire, transit accident, contamination), the insurance recovery may not be sufficient to replace the inventory at cost, leaving Tipul with a gap. For sterile-packaged implants, "replacement" also requires re-validation of the sterilization process, which may not be captured by a simple selling-price valuation.

**Recommended fix:** Request that the policy be endorsed to provide replacement cost valuation for all inventory categories, or at minimum that the selling price be defined to include the full cost of replacement (manufacturing, sterilization validation, packaging, and regulatory re-submission costs). If the policy cannot be amended, require Seller to carry an additional policy or increase limits to account for the valuation gap.

**Flag:** We do not have the actual Certificate of Insurance (only the cover letter), so we cannot verify the policy limits, sublimits, deductibles, or exclusions. We need to request the full certificate and, ideally, the policy form.

## Issue 13 – Event of Default for Sale of Product Line

**Where:** Note § 8.1(e).

**What it says:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells or otherwise transfers all or substantially all of the Product Line to any Person other than an Affiliate of Maker, unless the transferee assumes Maker's obligations under this Note in writing."

**Why it matters:** If Tipul later wishes to restructure its business—e.g., spin off the orthopedic line into a subsidiary, sell it as part of a larger transaction, or transfer it to an acquisition vehicle—the Note triggers a default unless the transferee assumes it. The "substantially all" standard is not defined and could be interpreted broadly. This could complicate future M&A activity or internal restructurings and give Nilur (or its assignee, see Issue 5) leverage to demand a premium or consent fee.

**Recommended fix:** Add clarifying language: "For purposes of this Section 8.1(e), 'all or substantially all of the Product Line' means a transfer of assets or equity representing more than seventy percent (70%) of the fair market value of the Product Line. A transfer to an Affiliate of Maker, or a transfer in connection with a merger, consolidation or reorganization of Maker in which Maker's existing stockholders hold at least fifty percent (50%) of the surviving entity, shall not constitute an Event of Default."
