## Summary

I have reviewed the four documents in the matter folder. I identify eleven issues ranging from critical to moderate. The three most significant are: (1) a direct conflict between the APA and the Seller Note that effectively eliminates Tipul's set-off remedy; (2) a 12-month survival period for Excluded Liability indemnification that is grossly inadequate for medical device product liability; and (3) the absence of any evidence that Seller will maintain the six-year post-closing product liability insurance required by the APA. Details and recommended fixes follow.

## Issue 1 – Set-Off Conflict Between APA and Seller Note

**Where:** APA § 8.7; Seller Note § 1 (definition of "Losses") and § 4.2; APA § 11.4 (order of precedence).

**Why it matters:** APA § 8.7 permits Tipul to set off "any Losses that Buyer claims in good faith under this Article VIII" against the Seller Note. The Seller Note, however, defines "Losses" (in § 1) as only "amounts that have been finally determined by a court of competent jurisdiction, in a judgment that is no longer subject to appeal," and § 4.2 limits set-off to that narrow definition. In practical terms, Tipul cannot exercise the set-off right in the APA unless it first obtains a final, non-appealable court judgment against Nilur. Because APA § 11.4 provides that "the Seller Note controls on matters concerning the indebtedness it evidences," the Note's restrictive definition will likely prevail. This strips Tipul of its principal self-help remedy for post-closing indemnification claims and forces it to litigate to judgment before it can reduce its note payments.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Seller Note § 1 (definition of "Losses") and § 4.2 to conform to APA § 8.7. Proposed language for Note § 4.2:

> "Maker may set off against any amount of principal or interest due under this Note any Losses that Maker claims in good faith under Article VIII of the Purchase Agreement. Maker shall give Holder written notice of any set-off not later than the Payment Date on which it is applied, describing the claim in reasonable detail. If the claim is later finally resolved for less than the amount set off, Maker shall pay the difference to Holder within 10 Business Days after that resolution, together with interest at the Interest Rate. The exercise of this right in good faith does not constitute a breach of this Note or a default hereunder."

Delete the "finally determined by a court" language from the Note's definition of "Losses" or add a separate definition for set-off purposes.

## Issue 2 – Inadequate Survival Period for Excluded Liability Indemnification

**Where:** APA § 8.2(c) (last sentence); APA § 8.1(a); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Pre-Closing Product Liabilities").

**Why it matters:** Section 8.2(c) requires that "Any claim for indemnification under Section 8.2(c) shall be asserted by written notice to Seller within the survival period set forth in Section 8.1(a)." Section 8.1(a) is 12 months. Pre-Closing Product Liabilities are defined to include claims "regardless of when the claim is asserted," yet Tipul has only 12 months post-closing to assert its indemnification claim for them. In orthopedic implants, product liability claims (revision surgery, device failure, infection) routinely surface three to ten years after implantation. A 12-month window means Tipul will almost certainly lose its indemnification right before most claims arise. The six-year survival for Fundamental Representations (§ 8.1(b)) does not help because title, organization, authority, taxes, and brokers are not the reps at issue here.

**Recommended fix:** Add a separate survival period for Excluded Liabilities. Proposed amendment to APA § 8.1:

> "(e) The indemnification obligations of Seller under Section 8.2(c) survive the Closing until the later of (i) six (6) years after the Closing Date, or (ii) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations for the underlying claim, plus thirty (30) days."

Conforming change to the last sentence of § 8.2(c): replace "within the survival period set forth in Section 8.1(a)" with "within the survival period set forth in Section 8.1(e)."

## Issue 3 – No Evidence of Post-Closing Product Liability Insurance

**Where:** APA § 6.7; Ostrander Pike Cover Letter (entire document).

**Why it matters:** Section 6.7 obligates Nilur to maintain (or purchase extended reporting period coverage under) product liability insurance for the pre-closing Products for at least six years post-closing, with limits no less than those in effect on the agreement date, and to name Tipul as an additional insured. The only insurance document in the folder is a *property* insurance certificate covering inventory. It says nothing about product liability. We have no policy, no binder, no quote, and no confirmation that the required coverage exists or will be placed. If Nilur is an LLC selling its assets and potentially dissolving, its ability to satisfy a six-year insurance obligation is uncertain. Without this coverage, Tipul bears the full economic risk of pre-closing product liability claims to the extent Nilur's indemnification is exhausted or uncollectible.

**Recommended fix:**
- Require Nilur to deliver, as a closing condition (add to APA § 3.2), a certificate of product liability insurance (or extended reporting period endorsement) evidencing: (i) the required six-year tail; (ii) limits no less than those currently in force; and (iii) Tipul named as additional insured.
- Add to APA § 7.1 (conditions to Buyer's closing obligation): "Seller has delivered a certificate of product liability insurance or extended reporting period coverage in compliance with Section 6.7."
- Consider requiring the premium to be paid at closing or escrowed.

**Flag for discussion with Nilur's counsel:** Confirm what product liability policy Nilur currently carries, what the limits are, and whether the insurer will agree to a six-year extended reporting period. If Nilur is planning to dissolve post-closing, confirm the policy will remain in force and that the insurer has acknowledged the additional insured endorsement.

## Issue 4 – Governing Law and Jurisdiction Mismatch

**Where:** Seller Note §§ 10.1–10.2; APA §§ 11.8–11.9.

**Why it matters:** The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware courts. The Seller Note is governed by Minnesota law with exclusive jurisdiction in Minnesota state and federal courts. Both parties are Delaware entities. The Minnesota choice is unexplained and creates at least three problems: (1) the set-off dispute in Issue 1 may be governed by different law depending on which document "controls"; (2) Tipul may need to litigate in two forums; and (3) Minnesota law may interpret subordination, acceleration, or set-off provisions differently than Delaware law.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Seller Note §§ 10.1 and 10.2 to conform to the APA. Proposed language:

> "**10.1 Governing Law.** This Note, and all claims and causes of action arising out of or relating to it, are governed by the laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law provision that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction.
>
> **10.2 Jurisdiction.** Maker and Holder each irrevocably submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, only if that court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware or the United States District Court for the District of Delaware) for any action arising out of or relating to this Note…"

**Flag for discussion with Nilur's counsel:** Confirm whether the Minnesota forum was negotiated intentionally (e.g., for a Senior Lender's requirement) or is a drafting error. If it was negotiated, understand the rationale before conceding the point.

## Issue 5 – Excessively Broad Information and Inspection Rights in Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note § 6.2.

**Why it matters:** The Note requires Tipul to deliver: (a) monthly unaudited financial statements certified by the CFO; (b) annual audited financials; (c) quarterly Product Line reports including "a list of the hospital, ambulatory surgery center and distributor customers of the Product Line with the revenue attributable to each"; and (d) any other information Holder "may reasonably request," plus on-site inspection rights with only five Business Days' notice. Customer-level revenue data is competitively sensitive and could be used by Nilur (or a transferee under Note § 9) to target Tipul's key accounts. The monthly reporting burden is disproportionate for a $9M note that amortizes over three years. The "any other information" catch-all and inspection right are open-ended.

**Recommended fix:**
- Delete or narrow § 6.2(c): replace customer-level revenue detail with aggregate Product Line revenue, unit volumes, and gross margin by product category.
- Change monthly reporting (§ 6.2(a)) to quarterly, consistent with the amortization schedule.
- Add a confidentiality obligation: "All information delivered under this Section 6.2 shall be treated as confidential information of Maker subject to Section 6.4 of the Purchase Agreement."
- Limit inspection rights to once per fiscal year and to the Product Line facilities only.
- Delete or narrow § 6.2(d): "upon written request stating the specific purpose, Maker shall provide such additional information as is reasonably necessary for Holder to assess Maker's ability to perform under this Note."

## Issue 6 – Holder May Transfer the Note Without Tipul's Consent

**Where:** Seller Note § 9.

**Why it matters:** Nilur may "sell, assign, pledge or otherwise transfer this Note and all of its rights under this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker." Tipul's $9M creditor could become an unknown third party—a distressed-debt fund, a competitor, or an entity with aggressive collection practices. Combined with the broad information rights in § 6.2, a transferee could gain detailed commercial intelligence about Tipul's Product Line. There is no restriction on transfer to a competitor or to a Person affiliated with a competitor.

**Recommended fix:** Add consent and restriction language to Note § 9:

> "Holder may sell, assign, pledge or otherwise transfer this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person other than (a) a competitor of Maker in the orthopedic implant market, (b) an Affiliate of any such competitor, or (c) any Person designated by Maker in writing as an unacceptable transferee (not to be unreasonably withheld), in each case without Maker's consent. Any other transfer requires Maker's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. Any permitted transferee shall assume in writing the confidentiality obligations applicable to information received under Section 6.2."

## Issue 7 – IP Assignment Schedules Are Unpopulated

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement, Schedule A (Assigned Patents) and Schedule B (Assigned Marks).

**Why it matters:** Both schedules contain only placeholder brackets "[●]" with a note to "conform to" Schedule 2.1(b) of the Purchase Agreement. We cannot verify the scope of IP being transferred, confirm that all relevant patents and marks are included, or assess whether any material IP has been omitted. The APA representation in § 4.6 that "Schedule 2.1(b) lists all registered and applied-for Transferred IP" is only as good as the schedule itself.

**Recommended fix:** Require Nilur to deliver completed Schedules A and B (and confirm Schedule 2.1(b) of the APA) as a condition to closing. Add to APA § 7.1: "Schedules A and B of the IP Assignment Agreement have been completed and conform to Schedule 2.1(b) of this Agreement." Do not sign off on the package until the schedules are populated and reviewed.

## Issue 8 – Named Inventor Availability Limited to Current Employees

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 4.2 (second sentence).

**Why it matters:** The provision states: "Assignor shall make available its current employees who are named inventors on any Assigned Patent to execute declarations, assignments and other documents reasonably required in connection with the prosecution or enforcement of the Assigned Patents." If a named inventor has already left Nilur (which is common in a business being sold), Tipul has no contractual right to compel that person to execute documents. Without proper inventor assignments, patent enforceability can be compromised.

**Recommended fix:** Expand the obligation:

> "Assignor shall use reasonable best efforts to cause all Persons who are named inventors on any Assigned Patent (whether or not currently employed by Assignor) to execute such declarations, assignments and other documents as Assignee reasonably requires. Assignor shall indemnify Assignee for any Losses arising from Assignor's failure to use such reasonable best efforts."

Also, add a specific representation to APA Article IV: "All inventors of the Transferred IP have executed valid assignments of their interest therein to Seller, and no inventor's assignment is missing, defective or subject to challenge."

## Issue 9 – Property Insurance Certificate: Limited Protections and Short Non-Payment Notice

**Where:** Ostrander Pike Cover Letter §§ 3–4; APA § 6.6.

**Why it matters:** Two sub-issues:
- *10-day non-payment notice (§ 3):* If Nilur fails to pay its premium, the insurer need only give Tipul 10 days' notice of cancellation. Given that closing is April 15, 2026 and the policy runs to January 1, 2027, a premium payment due date falling in the pre-closing window could leave inventory uninsured with minimal warning.
- *No rights beyond loss payee (§ 4):* The certificate explicitly states it "confers no rights on the certificate holder other than those provided by the loss payee designation and the notice of cancellation endorsement." Tipul is not a named insured. If there is a dispute over coverage, Tipul has no direct privity with the insurer.

**Recommended fix:**
- Request that the insurer endorse the policy to name Tipul as an *additional insured* (not merely loss payee) for the pre-closing period, or require Nilur to provide a direct covenant that it will not allow the policy to lapse and will notify Tipul immediately of any premium delinquency.
- Negotiate the non-payment notice period to 30 days (matching the general cancellation notice) or require Nilur to prepay the premium through the Closing Date.
- Confirm the policy covers all inventory locations listed in the APA, including consignment and in-transit inventory.

## Issue 10 – Cap on Non-Fundamental Representation Indemnification

**Where:** APA § 8.4(b).

**Why it matters:** The cap for non-Fundamental representation breaches is $4,850,000 (10% of the Purchase Price). The representations most likely to be relevant in a medical device acquisition—regulatory compliance (§ 4.7), product liability (§ 4.8), IP ownership and non-infringement (§ 4.6), and inventory quality (§ 4.9)—are all non-Fundamental. A single significant regulatory issue (e.g., an unreported FDA enforcement action or a batch of adulterated inventory) could easily generate losses exceeding $4.85M, at which point Tipul's recovery is capped. The Fundamental Representations (organization, authority, title, taxes, brokers) are unlikely to be the source of material economic loss in this transaction.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) increase the cap to 20–25% of the Purchase Price ($9.7M–$12.1M), or (b) carve out the regulatory compliance (§ 4.7) and product liability (§ 4.8) representations from the cap and subject them to the Purchase Price cap instead. Proposed language for § 8.4(b):

> "The aggregate liability of Seller under Section 8.2(a) for the Fundamental Representations, for breaches of Sections 4.7 and 4.8, and under Sections 8.2(b) and 8.2(c) shall not exceed the Purchase Price. The aggregate liability of Seller under Section 8.2(a) for all other representations and warranties shall not exceed $4,850,000."

## Issue 11 – Exclusive Remedy Provision Limits Post-Closing Recourse

**Where:** APA § 8.8.

**Why it matters:** After closing, the indemnification provisions are the "sole and exclusive remedy" for any claim arising out of the Agreement, except for fraud, specific performance/injunctive relief, and the tax allocation provision (§ 2.6). This means that if a representation is breached and the loss exceeds the cap, or falls outside the survival period, Tipul has no remedy at all (short of proving fraud, which is a high bar). Combined with Issues 2 and 10, the exclusive remedy clause amplifies the risk of the short survival period and low cap.

**Recommended fix:** This is a standard provision and difficult to eliminate entirely. However, ensure that the fixes in Issues 2 and 10 are implemented so that the exclusive remedy structure does not operate to Tipul's detriment. Additionally, confirm that the fraud carve-out in § 8.8 is not itself subject to the survival period (it should not be; § 8.4(c) already provides that limitations do not apply to fraud, but make this express in § 8.1 to avoid ambiguity).
