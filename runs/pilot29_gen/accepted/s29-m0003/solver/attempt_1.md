This likely conflicts with Ostrander's existing logistics arrangements.

## DETAILED ISSUES

### 1. Change of Control Requires Provider Consent; Termination Right Without It

**Where:** MSA §15.2; Side Letter Recital B and §3.2.

**Issue.** §15.2 deems any Change of Control of Customer to be an assignment requiring Provider's prior written consent. If the Change of Control occurs without consent, Provider may terminate for cause and recover the Early Termination Fee under §14.4. The Side Letter acknowledges the pending Ostrander acquisition (Recital B) but §3.2 states the Side Letter "does not constitute a consent, approval or waiver by either Party under any provision of the MSA, including Section 15."

**Why it matters.** The acquisition closes January 29, 2027. If Poubis has not obtained Provider's consent by that date, Provider can terminate the MSA immediately. Poubis (now an Ostrander subsidiary) would lose its exclusive refrigerated warehousing, cross-docking, and reefer transportation provider with no contractual right to continue service beyond the 90-day transition period in §14.5.

**Recommended fix.** Make Provider's written consent to the Change of Control a condition to closing in the Ostrander acquisition agreement, or obtain the consent as a standalone deliverable before closing. In the consent negotiation, bundle the exclusivity and liability issues below.

**Open item / ask client:** Has any consent or waiver been discussed or agreed informally with Gibeis? Is there a term sheet or letter of intent for the consent?

### 2. Exclusivity Obligation Sweeps in Acquirer's Affiliates

**Where:** MSA §3.1; MSA §1.1 (definition of "Affiliate" and "Control").

**Issue.** §3.1 requires Customer to purchase *exclusively* from Provider all temperature-controlled logistics services "required by Customer or any Affiliate of Customer anywhere in North America." "Affiliate" includes any entity under common control. Post-acquisition, Ostrander Foods Group will control Poubis, making all Ostrander subsidiaries Affiliates of Poubis.

**Why it matters.** Ostrander likely has its own cold-chain logistics providers and existing contracts. As drafted, §3.1 would require Ostrander to migrate all of its temperature-controlled logistics to Gibeis, or at minimum Poubis would be in breach if its new sister companies use other providers. This is a material operational and cost issue for the combined entity.

**Recommended fix.** In the Change of Control consent negotiation, seek to amend §3.1 to (a) limit exclusivity to Poubis's standalone operations as conducted pre-acquisition, (b) carve out Ostrander's existing logistics arrangements and facilities, or (c) convert the exclusivity to a right-of-first-refusal or volume-commitment structure.

**Open item / ask client:** What is Ostrander's existing cold-chain logistics footprint? Are there current contracts with other 3PLs that would conflict? What volumes does Ostrander move through temperature-controlled channels?

### 3. Auto-Renewal Locks In Three More Years

**Where:** MSA §2.1, §2.2.

**Issue.** The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. The MSA renews automatically for successive three-year terms unless a party gives non-renewal notice between 120 and 90 days before expiration (i.e., November 1–30, 2026). The acquisition closes January 29, 2027—after the notice window closes.

**Why it matters.** If no non-renewal notice is given by November 30, 2026, the MSA renews through February 28, 2030. The acquirer would be locked into Gibeis's services (and the exclusivity in §3.1) for another three years with limited leverage to renegotiate. Conversely, if Poubis gives non-renewal notice, the MSA ends February 28, 2027—one month after closing—creating a transition gap.

**Recommended fix.** The client needs to make a strategic decision before November 30, 2026:
- If the intent is to keep Gibeis: allow the renewal, but use the Change of Control consent negotiation to amend key terms (exclusivity scope, fee structure, liability) effective at the renewal date.
- If the intent is to exit or compete: give non-renewal notice now and plan a transition using the §14.5 90-day bridge, or negotiate a voluntary early termination with Gibeis as part of the consent package.

**Open item / ask client:** What is Ostrander's strategic view on Gibeis as a long-term provider? Does Ostrander have its own cold-chain infrastructure that would make Gibeis redundant? Has any non-renewal notice already been given?

### 4. Side Letter Narrows Cargo Loss Liability to Gross Negligence / Willful Misconduct Only

**Where:** Side Letter §2.1–2.3; MSA §11.1, §11.2, §11.3(b) (as amended).

**Issue.** Original MSA §11.3(b) excluded *all* of Provider's Cargo Loss liability from both the consequential damages bar (§11.1) and the 12-month-fee cap (§11.2). The Side Letter amends §11.3(b) to apply only "to the extent caused by Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct." For Cargo Loss caused by ordinary negligence (e.g., a reefer unit failure due to inadequate maintenance, a driver's failure to check temperatures), the claim is now subject to the 12-month-fee cap and the consequential damages exclusion.

**Why it matters.** For a food distributor, a single Temperature Excursion affecting a full trailer or warehouse zone can result in six- or seven-figure spoilage. The 12-month-fee cap may be well below actual losses. The consequential damages bar could also exclude downstream customer claims, recall costs, and lost profits attributable to spoiled product. This materially weakens Poubis's position compared to what the original MSA provided.

**Recommended fix.**
- If the Side Letter has not yet been countersigned by Poubis, do not sign without negotiating a higher cap for Cargo Loss (e.g., 24 months of fees, or a fixed dollar amount tied to inventory value in Provider's care).
- If already signed, address in the Change of Control consent negotiation: seek to restore the original §11.3(b) or at minimum raise the cap.
- Confirm that the Parent Guaranty still backstops this (see Issue 5 below)—but note the Guaranty only covers frozen goods.

**Open item / ask client:** Has Poubis already countersigned the Side Letter? What is the average monthly fee under the current SOWs (to gauge the 12-month cap)? What is the typical value of a full trailer or warehouse zone of product?

### 5. Parent Guaranty Covers Only Frozen Goods (≤ −18°C), Not Chilled Goods

**Where:** Guaranty §1.1 (definition of "Covered Goods"); MSA §1.1 (definition of "Cargo").

**Issue.** The Guaranty defines "Covered Goods" as "goods stored or transported by Provider at or below −18°C." The MSA defines "Cargo" to include both chilled (0–4°C) and frozen goods. The Guaranty's "Guaranteed Obligations" are limited to obligations "to the extent they relate to Covered Goods" (Guaranty §1.1).

**Why it matters.** The press release and MSA recitals confirm Poubis distributes dairy, fresh produce, and meat—products typically stored in the chilled range (0–4°C). If a Cargo Loss event involves chilled product (e.g., a compressor failure in a chilled zone, a reefer breakdown on a dairy shipment), the Parent Guaranty does not backstop Gibeis Cold Logistics's obligations. Poubis's only recourse is against the LLC itself, which may have limited assets.

**Recommended fix.** In the Change of Control consent negotiation, require Gibeis Holdings to amend the Guaranty to expand "Covered Goods" to all Cargo (i.e., remove the −18°C threshold or replace it with "at or below 4°C" to capture both chilled and frozen). Alternatively, require Provider to maintain a higher insurance limit for cargo in the chilled range (see Issue 8).

**Open item / ask client:** What percentage of Poubis's inventory value is chilled vs. frozen? Are there any SOWs that specify temperature ranges that would clarify which products are "Covered Goods"?

### 6. Early Termination Fee Calculation May Be Nominal but Termination Right Is the Real Risk

**Where:** MSA §14.4; MSA §15.2.

**Issue.** The Early Termination Fee equals average monthly Fees × the lesser of (a) 6 months and (b) full months remaining in the then-current term. If the Change of Control occurs at closing (January 29, 2027) and the Initial Term ends February 28, 2027, there are zero *full* months remaining. The ETF would be zero. However, if the MSA has already renewed (see Issue 3), the "then-current term" is the new three-year Renewal Term, and the ETF could be up to 6 months of fees.

**Why it matters.** The ETF itself may be small or zero, but the termination right is the critical issue. Provider can walk away from the relationship entirely. The fee is not the protection; the consent is.

**Recommended fix.** Same as Issue 1—obtain consent. Do not rely on the ETF being low as a reason to forgo consent.

### 7. Press Release May Have Breached the Publicity Restriction

**Where:** MSA §9.4; Partnership Press Release (March 5, 2024).

**Issue.** §9.4 prohibits either party from issuing a press release or public statement about the Agreement without the other party's prior written approval. The joint press release was issued March 5, 2024—four days after the Effective Date. There is no document in the matter folder evidencing prior written approval by either party.

**Why it matters.** This is a minor issue in isolation, but it could be raised by Provider as a technical breach to support a termination-for-cause argument under §14.1 (if Provider is being adversarial about the Change of Control). It also sets a precedent that the parties have been informal about §9.4.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm whether written approval was exchanged (email, signed release draft, etc.). If not, document mutual ratification. Low priority, but clean up the file.

**Open item / ask client:** Was there an email or other written approval for the March 2024 press release?

### 8. Insurance Limits May Be Inadequate Given Reduced Cargo Loss Recovery

**Where:** MSA §8.1(c); Side Letter §2 (as discussed in Issue 4).

**Issue.** Provider must maintain warehouse legal liability and motor truck cargo insurance of at least $1.5 million per location and per conveyance. With the Side Letter narrowing uncapped Cargo Loss recovery to gross negligence/willful misconduct only, ordinary-negligence Cargo Loss claims are capped at 12 months of fees. The $1.5M per-conveyance insurance is the practical backstop for a single-trailer loss.

**Why it matters.** A single reefer trailer of premium frozen product (e.g., lobster, specialty dairy) can easily exceed $1.5M in replacement cost. If the cap is 12 months of fees and that is less than $1.5M, Poubis could be under-protected even with the insurance.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm the actual 12-month fee cap amount. If it is below $1.5M per conveyance, negotiate either (a) a higher Cargo Loss cap in the MSA, or (b) an increased insurance requirement in §8.1(c).

**Open item / ask client:** What are the current monthly fees under all active SOWs? What is the highest-value single-trailer load Poubis typically ships?

### 9. Side Letter Fuel Surcharge Reset – Favorable but Note for Acquirer

**Where:** Side Letter §1.1–1.4; MSA §5.3 (as replaced).

**Issue.** The Side Letter raises the base price from $3.75 to $4.10 and widens the increment from $0.05 to $0.06 per gallon. This is favorable to Poubis (the surcharge kicks in later and in larger steps). No action needed for Poubis, but the acquirer should model transportation costs using the revised formula.

**Why it matters.** Budget accuracy for the combined entity. The original §5.3 would have produced a higher surcharge at current diesel prices.

**Recommended fix.** None for Poubis. Flag to Ostrander's integration team for cost modeling.

### 10. Subcontracting Consent Right (MSA §15.3)

**Where:** MSA §15.3.

**Issue.** Provider may not subcontract without Customer's prior written consent, except for overflow spot transportation. If Provider is using subcontractors (common in reefer transportation), Poubis should have a current list and confirmed consents.

**Why it matters.** Post-acquisition, Ostrander may want to audit Provider's subcontractor network for food safety compliance. Without consent records, there may be technical breaches.

**Recommended fix.** Request from Provider a current list of all subcontractors and copies of consent records. Include in the Change of Control consent negotiation as a representation.

**Open item / ask client:** Is Poubis aware of any subcontractors Gibeis uses? Have any consent requests been made or denied?

---

### 11. Audit Rights Limited to Twice per Year (MSA §6.5)

**Where:** MSA §6.5.

**Issue.** Customer may audit Provider no more than twice in any 12-month period (excluding audits following a Temperature Excursion or food safety incident). Post-acquisition, Ostrander will likely want to conduct integration due diligence audits of Gibeis's facilities and food safety practices.

**Why it matters.** The acquirer may want more than two audits in the first year post-closing to verify compliance, insurance, and operational readiness. The existing cap could be a constraint.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate an additional audit right (or a one-time integration audit) as part of the Change of Control consent.

---

### 12. Guaranty Does Not Require Provider Consent for Customer's Change of Control

**Where:** Guaranty §10; MSA §15.

**Issue.** The Guaranty permits Customer to assign its rights to any person to whom the MSA is assigned under §15. This is consistent with the MSA but means the Guaranty is only as useful as the MSA's assignability. If Provider withholds consent under §15.2, the MSA cannot be assigned, and the Guaranty's benefit to the acquirer is moot.

**Why it matters.** The acquirer may be relying on the Parent Guaranty as a credit enhancement. It is only effective if the MSA survives the Change of Control.

**Recommended fix.** Same as Issue 1—secure consent. Additionally, consider requiring Gibeis Holdings to confirm in writing that the Guaranty will remain in full force following the Change of Control (even though §3.2(c) of the Guaranty says it is not affected by changes in ownership of Provider, there is no analogous express provision for changes in ownership of Customer beyond the assignment clause in §10).
