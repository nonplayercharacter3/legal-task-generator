## DETAILED ISSUES

### 1. Change of Control – Consent Not Given; Termination Right and Early Termination Fee

**Documents:** MSA §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), §15.1, §15.2, §14.4; Side Letter Recital B, §3.2.

**What the documents say:**
- §15.2 provides that any Change of Control of Customer "is deemed an assignment" and "requires the prior written consent of Provider." If it occurs without consent, Provider may terminate for cause and recover the Early Termination Fee.
- Side Letter Recital B acknowledges that on October 1, 2026, Customer notified Provider of the Ostrander acquisition (closing expected January 29, 2027).
- Side Letter §3.2 states: "This Side Letter does not constitute a consent, approval or waiver by either Party under any provision of the MSA, including Section 15 of the MSA."

**Why it matters:**
The parties have been in active discussions about this acquisition (evidenced by the Side Letter's recital), yet Provider has deliberately not consented. At closing, if no separate consent is in place, Provider has an immediate right to terminate and claim the ETF. Even if the ETF is small (see below), termination would disrupt Poubis's entire cold chain logistics at the worst possible moment.

**ETF exposure:** Under §14.4, the ETF equals average monthly Fees in the preceding 12 months multiplied by the lesser of (a) 6 and (b) the number of full months remaining in the then-current term. The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. If closing occurs January 29, 2027, there are zero or one full months remaining (ambiguous on the face of the documents). The ETF is therefore likely modest, but the termination right itself is the real risk.

**Recommendation:**
- Obtain Provider's written consent to the Change of Control **before** closing. Make it a condition to closing in the acquisition agreement.
- In the consent, seek a waiver of the "deemed assignment" language so the MSA continues in Poubis's name without further action.
- Confirm the deal structure: if Poubis survives as the contracting entity (e.g., stock purchase), the MSA and Guaranty remain in place. If Poubis merges into an Ostrander subsidiary, §15.1's anti-assignment clause is triggered by operation of law, and a novation or consent is needed.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- What is the expected deal structure (stock purchase, merger, asset purchase)?
- Has any consent or side agreement regarding §15.2 been discussed or drafted separately from the October 2026 Side Letter?
- What are the average monthly Fees under the MSA (to quantify ETF exposure)?

### 2. Exclusivity Scope – "Any Affiliate… Anywhere in North America"

**Documents:** MSA §3.1; MSA §1.1 (definition of "Affiliate"); Press Release §2.

**What the documents say:**
- §3.1: "During the Term, Customer shall purchase exclusively from Provider all temperature-controlled logistics services… required by Customer or any Affiliate of Customer anywhere in North America. Customer shall cause its Affiliates to comply with this Section 3.1."
- "Affiliate" is defined to include any entity that "now or hereafter" controls, is controlled by, or is under common control with Customer.
- The March 2024 Press Release describes the exclusivity as limited to "Poubis's Mid-Atlantic distribution network."

**Why it matters:**
Post-closing, Ostrander will control Poubis. Every Ostrander subsidiary and affiliate that requires temperature-controlled logistics "anywhere in North America" must use Gibeis. If Ostrander operates in other regions (e.g., the Midwest, the Southeast) with its own cold chain logistics, those operations are swept into the exclusivity. This could be a material operational and cost constraint for the acquirer that is not apparent from the press release.

The discrepancy between the press release (Mid-Atlantic only) and the contract (all of North America, all Affiliates) is a disclosure issue: if the acquirer's diligence relied on the press release, it has been misled on scope.

**Recommendation:**
- Flag to the acquirer that the exclusivity is far broader than the public statement suggests.
- Negotiate a carve-out or amendment in the consent to the Change of Control: limit exclusivity to Poubis's existing Mid-Atlantic network, or at minimum exclude Ostrander's pre-existing operations.
- Alternatively, if the acquirer is comfortable with the scope, document that understanding in the consent so there is no later dispute.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- Does Ostrander or any of its affiliates currently use temperature-controlled logistics providers other than Gibeis? If so, in which regions and for what volumes?
- Did the acquirer's diligence team review §3.1, or did they rely on the press release?

### 3. Side Letter §2 – Cargo Loss Liability Now Capped for Ordinary Negligence

**Documents:** MSA §11.1, §11.2, §11.3(b) (original); Side Letter §2.1, §2.2, §2.3.

**What the documents say:**
- **Original MSA §11.3(b):** Provider's liability for Cargo Loss was entirely excluded from the consequential damages waiver (§11.1) and the 12-month fee cap (§11.2). In other words, for any Cargo Loss, Poubis could recover full replacement cost plus consequential damages (lost profits, customer claims, etc.) with no cap.
- **Side Letter §2.2 (amended §11.3(b)):** The exclusion now applies "solely to the extent caused by Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct."
- **Side Letter §2.3:** Applies to all Cargo Loss events on or after October 15, 2026.

**Why it matters:**
This is a significant reduction in Poubis's protection. For a food distributor, the most common Cargo Loss scenarios—minor temperature excursions, handling errors, equipment malfunctions short of gross negligence—will now be subject to:
- A cap of 12 months' Fees (likely a few million dollars at most, depending on volume), and
- An exclusion of consequential damages (so Poubis cannot recover lost profits from spoiled goods, costs of customer claims, or brand damage).

Given that the press release touts "cold chain integrity from dock to delivery," this Side Letter provision undercuts the commercial story. For the acquirer, this means the post-closing entity has less recourse if Gibeis's performance is merely negligent rather than grossly negligent.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate to revert §11.3(b) to its original form as part of the Change of Control consent. Provider's leverage is highest now (pre-consent); once the consent is given, it will be harder to reopen.
- If Provider resists, seek at minimum: (a) a higher cap for Cargo Loss (e.g., 24 months' Fees or a fixed dollar amount), and (b) an exception to the consequential damages waiver for Customer's downstream customer claims and recall costs.
- Document the business rationale: the Side Letter was signed in the context of the acquisition discussions, and the acquirer should not inherit a reduced standard of protection without compensation.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- What is the average monthly Fee under the MSA (to quantify the cap)?
- Has Poubis experienced any Cargo Loss events since the Side Letter's effective date (October 15, 2026)?
- What was the commercial rationale Poubis gave for agreeing to Side Letter §2? Was it traded for the fuel surcharge reset in §1?

### 4. Parent Guaranty – Frozen Goods Only

**Documents:** Guaranty §1.1 (definition of "Covered Goods"); MSA §1.1 (definition of "Cargo"); Press Release §1.

**What the documents say:**
- Guaranty §1.1: "Covered Goods" means goods stored or transported by Provider "at or below −18 °C."
- The Guaranteed Obligations (§1.1) extend only to obligations "to the extent they relate to Covered Goods."
- MSA §1.1 defines "Cargo" to include both chilled (0–4 °C) and frozen goods.
- The Press Release identifies Poubis's product categories as "dairy, fresh produce, meat and frozen goods."

**Why it matters:**
Dairy, fresh produce, and most meat are chilled goods (0–4 °C), not frozen. The Guaranty provides no backstop for the majority of Poubis's product categories. If Gibeis Cold Logistics, LLC (a subsidiary) fails to perform or becomes insolvent, Poubis can look to Gibeis Holdings Corp. only for frozen goods claims. For chilled goods, Poubis's sole recourse is against the LLC itself.

This is a structural gap that the acquirer should understand. The "parent guarantee" is materially narrower than it appears.

**Recommendation:**
- As part of the Change of Control consent negotiation, request that Gibeis Holdings amend the Guaranty to extend "Covered Goods" to all Cargo (i.e., remove the −18 °C threshold).
- If Provider resists, at minimum confirm that the LLC's balance sheet and insurance (MSA §8: $10M umbrella, $1.5M per-location warehouse legal liability) are sufficient to cover a worst-case chilled goods spoilage event.
- Confirm that the insurance certificates required by MSA §8.2 are current and that Customer is named as additional insured.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- What percentage of Poubis's logistics volume (by value) is chilled vs. frozen?
- Has Gibeis Holdings' creditworthiness been assessed? (The Guaranty is only as good as the parent.)
- Are current insurance certificates on file?

### 5. Auto-Renewal and the Non-Renewal Notice Window

**Documents:** MSA §2.1, §2.2.

**What the documents say:**
- Initial Term: March 1, 2024 – February 28, 2027.
- §2.2: Auto-renews for successive 3-year terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal "no earlier than one hundred twenty (120) days and no later than ninety (90) days before the expiration of the then-current term."
- 120 days before February 28, 2027 = **November 1, 2026**.
- 90 days before February 28, 2027 = **December 1, 2026**.
- A notice given outside that window "has no effect."

**Why it matters:**
If the acquirer wants the option to replace Gibeis post-closing (or to renegotiate terms with leverage from the expiration), it needs to give non-renewal notice between November 1 and December 1, 2026. If it misses that window, the MSA auto-renews for three more years (through February 2030), and the next opportunity to exit is 2029–2030.

This interacts with the Change of Control issue: if Provider consents to the acquisition, the MSA continues and will auto-renew. If Provider does not consent and terminates, the auto-renewal is moot. The acquirer needs a clear strategy.

**Recommendation:**
- Determine now whether the acquirer intends to continue with Gibeis or transition to a different provider (or to Gibeis on renegotiated terms).
- If there is any chance of non-renewal, calendar the November 1 – December 1, 2026 window and prepare the notice.
- Note: giving non-renewal notice is not the same as terminating. It simply prevents auto-renewal. The MSA continues through February 28, 2027, and §14.5's 90-day transition period would apply.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- Has the acquirer expressed a preference to continue with Gibeis or to transition?
- Is the acquisition agreement expected to be signed before or after the non-renewal notice window?
- If the acquirer plans to continue with Gibeis, is it willing to accept the current terms (including the Side Letter's reduced Cargo Loss protection) for another three years?

### 6. No Termination for Convenience

**Documents:** MSA §14 (all subsections).

**What the documents say:**
Customer may terminate only: (a) for Provider's material breach with 30-day cure period (§14.1); (b) for Provider's insolvency (§14.2); or (c) for three or more Temperature Excursions causing Cargo Loss at the same Facility or on the same lane in a rolling six-month period (§14.3). There is no termination for convenience.

**Why it matters:**
Combined with the exclusivity (§3.1) and auto-renewal (§2.2), Poubis is locked in for the duration of the Term unless Provider materially underperforms. The acquirer cannot simply decide post-closing that it wants a different logistics provider and walk away. The only exit levers are: (a) Provider's breach, (b) the §14.3 temperature-failure trigger, or (c) waiting for the non-renewal window.

**Recommendation:**
- Ensure the acquirer's integration plan accounts for this lock-in.
- If the acquirer wants flexibility, negotiate a termination-for-convenience right (with a reasonable transition period and fee) as part of the Change of Control consent.
- Track Temperature Excursions carefully post-closing to preserve the §14.3 right if performance deteriorates.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- Has Poubis experienced any Temperature Excursions in the past six months? (To assess whether §14.3 is currently available.)
- Does the acquirer's business plan assume any change in logistics providers?

### 9. Guaranty Assignment – Follows MSA

**Documents:** Guaranty §10; MSA §15.

**What the documents say:**
- Guaranty §10: "Customer may assign its rights under this Guaranty to any person to whom the MSA is assigned in accordance with Section 15 of the MSA."
- MSA §15.1: No assignment without consent.

**Why it matters:**
The Guaranty is not independently assignable. It moves only if and when the MSA is validly assigned. If the Change of Control is not consented to (see Issue 1), the MSA is not assigned, and the Guaranty stays with Poubis. If Poubis merges out of existence in the deal structure, the Guaranty's beneficiary may be in limbo.

**Recommendation:**
- In the Change of Control consent, include language confirming that the Guaranty remains in full force for the benefit of Poubis (or its successor) and that no separate assignment of the Guaranty is required.
- If the deal structure involves a merger, address the Guaranty's survival explicitly.

**Facts to confirm with client:**
- Confirm deal structure (see Issue 1).

### 10. Insurance – Confirm Current Certificates

**Documents:** MSA §8.1, §8.2.

**What the documents say:**
- Provider must maintain CGL ($2M/$5M), auto liability ($5M CSL), warehouse legal liability and motor truck cargo ($1.5M per location/conveyance, including reefer breakdown and spoilage), workers' comp, and umbrella ($10M).
- Customer must be named additional insured (CGL, auto, umbrella) and loss payee (warehouse legal liability / motor truck cargo).

**Why it matters:**
If a Cargo Loss event occurs and the Provider's LLC is undercapitalized, Poubis's first line of recovery (after the contractual claim) is the insurance. If certificates are lapsed or Customer is not properly endorsed, recovery could be delayed or denied.

**Recommendation:**
- Request current certificates of insurance from Provider as part of pre-closing diligence.
- Confirm that all five policies in §8.1 are in force and that Customer is properly named.

### 11. Transition Period – 90 Days at Current Fees

**Documents:** MSA §14.5.

**What the documents say:**
On expiration or termination, at Customer's request, Provider must continue performing for up to 90 days at then-current Fees, cooperate in transferring Cargo to a successor provider, and release all Cargo on payment of undisputed amounts.

**Why it matters:**
This is a useful safety net for the acquirer if it decides to transition providers. It ensures 90 days of continuity. However, it is at Customer's request—Poubis must affirmatively invoke it.

**Recommendation:**
- Include in the acquirer's integration plan: if a transition is contemplated, issue the §14.5 request promptly upon termination or expiration to lock in the 90-day window.
- Note that the 90-day period is at "then-current Fees"—no price increase during transition.
