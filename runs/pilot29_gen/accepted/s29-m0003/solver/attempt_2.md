## MATTERS REQUIRING ATTENTION BEFORE CLOSING

1. **Obtain Gibeis's written consent to the Change of Control before closing.** MSA §15.2 makes any Change of Control of Customer a deemed assignment requiring Provider's prior written consent. Without it, Gibeis may terminate for cause and claim the Early Termination Fee (§14.4). Side Letter Recital B confirms Gibeis is aware of the transaction, but Side Letter §3.2 expressly disclaims any consent under §15. This is the single most urgent item.

2. **Negotiate the scope of the exclusivity obligation (§3.1) before or as a condition to consent.** Post-closing, every Ostrander affiliate anywhere in North America becomes an "Affiliate" of Poubis under the §1.1 definition, and §3.1 requires Poubis to cause all Affiliates to purchase temperature-controlled logistics exclusively from Gibeis. This likely far exceeds what Poubis or Ostrander intended. Gibeis will have leverage to demand this in exchange for consent.

3. **The auto-renewal non-renewal window closes before the expected closing date.** The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. Effective non-renewal notice must be given between October 31 and November 30, 2026 (§2.2). Expected closing is January 29, 2027 (Side Letter Recital B). After November 30, 2026, neither party can prevent the three-year auto-renewal effective March 1, 2027. If Ostrander wants the option to exit or renegotiate post-closing, that window will have passed.

## DETAILED ISSUES

### 1. Change of Control Consent and Termination Right

**Document / Section:** MSA §15.2; Side Letter Recital B and §3.2

**Issue:** Any Change of Control of Customer (defined in §1.1 to include stock purchase, merger, or sale of substantially all assets) is "deemed an assignment" requiring Gibeis's prior written consent. If it occurs without consent, Gibeis may terminate for cause and recover the Early Termination Fee under §14.4. The Side Letter acknowledges the pending Ostrander acquisition (Recital B) but expressly states it does not constitute consent under §15 (§3.2).

**Why it matters:** If the deal closes on January 29, 2027 without Gibeis's consent, Gibeis can terminate the MSA. Poubis would lose its exclusive cold-chain provider mid-integration. The acquirer would face immediate operational disruption and the cost of onboarding a replacement provider.

**Recommendation:** Make Gibeis's written consent a condition to closing in the Ostrander–Poubis acquisition agreement. Begin consent negotiations now. In exchange for consent, Poubis should seek to resolve the exclusivity issue (below) and confirm the Parent Guaranty survives.

**Open question for client:** Is the Ostrander transaction a stock purchase, merger, or asset purchase? The §1.1 definition of Change of Control covers all three, but the mechanics of consent and assignment differ. If it is an asset deal, the MSA must actually be assigned to the surviving entity, which raises additional issues under §15.1 and Guaranty §10.

### 2. Exclusivity Expands to All Ostrander Affiliates North America-Wide

**Document / Section:** MSA §3.1; §1.1 (definition of "Affiliate" and "Control")

**Issue:** §3.1 requires Customer to purchase "exclusively from Provider all temperature-controlled logistics services… required by Customer or any Affiliate of Customer anywhere in North America." "Affiliate" is defined to include any entity under common control. Post-closing, Ostrander Foods Group will control Poubis, and all other Ostrander subsidiaries will be under common control with Poubis, making them Affiliates. Poubis must "cause" them to comply.

**Why it matters:** This could obligate Poubis to migrate all of Ostrander's temperature-controlled logistics—across all subsidiaries, all of North America—onto Gibeis. That is a dramatic expansion of scope that Poubis did not have when it signed the MSA as a standalone company. It may be operationally infeasible or economically unreasonable, and it gives Gibeis significant leverage in any consent negotiation.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate a carve-out or cap as part of the consent process. Options include: (a) limiting the Affiliate exclusivity to entities that actually require the same product categories Poubis handles; (b) limiting geography to the Mid-Atlantic or to facilities Poubis actually operates; (c) providing a phase-in period; or (d) making the Affiliate obligation subject to a volume or revenue threshold. At minimum, seek a side letter or amendment clarifying that the Affiliate obligation does not extend to Ostrander's existing cold-chain arrangements in other regions without separate negotiation.

**Open question for client:** How many Ostrander subsidiaries currently require temperature-controlled logistics, in what geographies, and with what volumes? What are their existing providers? This will determine the practical scope of the problem and the negotiating position.

### 3. Early Termination Fee – Timing and Calculation

**Document / Section:** MSA §14.4

**Issue:** The ETF equals average monthly Fees for the preceding 12 months multiplied by the lesser of (a) 6 and (b) the number of full months remaining in the then-current term. If Gibeis terminates effective at or near the January 29, 2027 closing, the Initial Term (ending February 28, 2027) has less than one full month remaining, so the multiplier would be zero and the ETF would be $0. However, if termination is effective later (e.g., after the auto-renewal takes effect March 1, 2027), the "then-current term" would be the new three-year Renewal Term, and up to six months of average Fees would be at stake.

**Why it matters:** The ETF exposure depends heavily on timing. If Gibeis waits until after the auto-renewal to exercise its termination right, the fee could be substantial. Poubis and Ostrander should understand this dynamic in planning the consent negotiation.

**Recommendation:** In the consent agreement, specify the effective date of the Change of Control and confirm that no ETF is owed (or cap it at a negotiated amount). Alternatively, structure the consent so that Gibeis's termination right under §15.2 is expressly waived.

**Open question for client:** What are the average monthly Fees under the current SOWs? This is needed to model the ETF exposure.

### 4. Cargo Loss Liability Now Capped for Ordinary Negligence

**Document / Section:** Side Letter §§2.1–2.3; MSA §11.2, §11.3(b) (as amended)

**Issue:** Originally, §11.3(b) excluded all Cargo Loss from both the consequential-damages waiver (§11.1) and the liability cap (§11.2), meaning Poubis could recover uncapped replacement cost for any Cargo Loss. The Side Letter narrows this: the exclusion from the cap and consequential-damages waiver now applies "solely to the extent caused by Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct." For Cargo Loss caused by ordinary negligence (e.g., a minor temperature excursion from equipment malfunction short of gross negligence), Poubis's recovery is now subject to the 12-month-Fees cap in §11.2 and the consequential-damages exclusion in §11.1.

**Why it matters:** For a food distributor, spoilage from an ordinary-negligence temperature excursion can be costly. The cap (12 months of Fees) may be insufficient to cover a major spoilage event. The acquirer is inheriting this reduced protection. This was negotiated in October 2026—confirm whether Poubis's board or the acquirer's deal team was aware of this trade-off.

**Recommendation:** Flag to the acquirer in the disclosure schedule. If Poubis has leverage in the consent negotiation, consider seeking to revert §11.3(b) to its original form or at least raise the cap for Cargo Loss. Alternatively, confirm that the insurance program (§8.1(c), warehouse legal liability and motor truck cargo with reefer breakdown and spoilage, minimum $1.5M per location/conveyance) provides adequate backstop, and verify current policy limits.

**Open question for client:** What is the 12-month trailing Fees figure (i.e., the current cap amount)? What is the historical frequency and cost of Cargo Loss events? What are the current insurance policy limits?

### 5. Parent Guaranty Covers Only Frozen Goods (≤ −18°C)

**Document / Section:** Guaranty §1.1 (definition of "Covered Goods")

**Issue:** The Guaranty defines "Covered Goods" as "goods stored or transported by Provider at or below −18°C." All Guaranteed Obligations are limited to those relating to Covered Goods. The MSA covers both chilled (0–4°C) and frozen Cargo (§1.1). The Guaranty therefore does not backstop Poubis's claims for chilled product losses, chilled-product-related indemnification, or chilled-product service failures.

**Why it matters:** If Gibeis Cold Logistics, LLC (the subsidiary) is undercapitalized or becomes insolvent, Poubis has no recourse to Gibeis Holdings Corp. for any loss involving chilled goods—which for a grocery distributor handling dairy and fresh produce may represent a substantial portion of the business. The acquirer should understand this gap.

**Recommendation:** In the consent negotiation, request that Gibeis amend the Guaranty to cover all Cargo (not just frozen), or at minimum to cover chilled goods in the 0–4°C range. If Gibeis resists, assess the creditworthiness of Gibeis Cold Logistics, LLC on a standalone basis and consider requiring additional security (e.g., a letter of credit or escrow) for chilled-goods exposure.

**Open question for client:** What percentage of Poubis's volume and revenue is chilled versus frozen? What is the standalone financial condition of Gibeis Cold Logistics, LLC? Has Gibeis Holdings provided financial statements or a credit support package?

### 6. Guaranty Assignment Tied to MSA Assignment

**Document / Section:** Guaranty §10

**Issue:** Customer may assign its rights under the Guaranty only "to any person to whom the MSA is assigned in accordance with Section 15 of the MSA." Because §15 requires consent for any assignment (and §15.2 treats a CoC as a deemed assignment), the Guaranty cannot be freely transferred to the acquirer. In a stock deal, Poubis remains the customer and the Guaranty continues to benefit it—no assignment is needed. In an asset deal, the MSA must be assigned with Gibeis's consent, and only then can the Guaranty follow.

**Why it matters:** If the transaction is structured as an asset purchase or if the acquirer wants to novate the MSA to a different Ostrander entity, the Guaranty does not automatically transfer. The acquirer could lose the (limited) frozen-goods credit support.

**Recommendation:** Confirm deal structure. If it is a stock deal, this is a non-issue. If it is an asset deal or involves a novation, require Gibeis to consent to the assignment of both the MSA and the Guaranty as a condition to closing, and require Gibeis Holdings to reaffirm the Guaranty in favor of the successor entity.

**Open question for client:** What is the expected structure of the Ostrander acquisition (stock, asset, merger)?

### 7. Press Release Understates the Scope of Exclusivity

**Document / Section:** Press Release §2; MSA §3.1

**Issue:** The press release states Gibeis will be "the exclusive provider of temperature-controlled logistics services for Poubis's Mid-Atlantic distribution network." The MSA actually requires exclusivity for all of North America and for all Affiliates. The public statement materially narrows the contractual obligation.

**Why it matters:** If Ostrander's deal team or board relied on the public description of the relationship when underwriting the acquisition, they may not appreciate the true scope of the exclusivity lock-in. Additionally, MSA §9.4 requires prior written approval for press releases about the Agreement. There is no evidence in the folder that such approval was obtained (though the bilateral quotes suggest cooperation). If Gibeis later claims the release was unauthorized, it could be used as leverage.

**Recommendation:** Disclose the discrepancy in the acquisition due diligence. Confirm whether §9.4 approval was documented. If the acquirer relies on the press release's narrower description, that should be corrected in the disclosure schedules or the purchase agreement.

**Open question for client:** Was written approval for the press release exchanged between the parties? Is there a copy?

### 8. Auto-Renewal Locks in a Three-Year Commitment

**Document / Section:** MSA §2.1–2.2

**Issue:** The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. The agreement auto-renews for successive three-year terms unless non-renewal notice is given between 120 and 90 days before expiration (i.e., between October 31 and November 30, 2026). The expected closing date (January 29, 2027) is after that window closes. The agreement will renew on March 1, 2027 for a new three-year term through February 28, 2030.

**Why it matters:** Post-closing, Ostrander is locked into a three-year commitment (plus the exclusivity and Affiliate issues above) with no contractual right to exit before February 28, 2030, absent a material breach by Gibeis. If the integration does not go as planned, or if Ostrander has its own preferred logistics provider, the exit cost could be significant.

**Recommendation:** As part of the consent negotiation, seek either (a) a break right for the acquirer at a specified date post-closing (e.g., 12 months) upon payment of a negotiated fee; or (b) a mutual amendment resetting the term or adding a non-renewal option. At minimum, model the cost of staying through 2030 versus the cost of a negotiated early exit.

### 9. Fuel Surcharge Reset – Confirm Favorable Terms Are Preserved

**Document / Section:** Side Letter §1.1; MSA §5.3 (as replaced)

**Issue:** The Side Letter raised the base price from $3.75 to $4.10 and increased the step from $0.05 to $0.06 per gallon. This is favorable to Poubis (higher threshold, fewer steps). The Side Letter is effective November 2, 2026 and applies to loads tendered on or after that date (§1.4).

**Why it matters:** This is not a problem for Poubis, but it should be confirmed that the Side Letter is properly executed and that the revised §5.3 will carry through any consent or amendment process. If the MSA is amended to address the CoC, the Side Letter's priority under §16.4 (Side Letters control over the MSA) should be preserved.

**Recommendation:** No action needed beyond confirming execution. Ensure any CoC consent letter or amendment does not inadvertently revert §5.3.

### 10. Transition Period May Be Insufficient

**Document / Section:** MSA §14.5

**Issue:** Upon termination, Provider must continue services for up to 90 days at current Fees and cooperate in transferring Cargo to a successor. For a cold-chain operation handling perishable goods, 90 days to qualify a new provider, transfer inventory, re-paper food safety certifications, and re-route logistics may be tight.

**Why it matters:** If Gibeis terminates post-closing (with or without consent), Poubis has a limited window to transition. The acquirer should have a backup provider identified and a transition plan in place before closing.

**Recommendation:** Include a transition plan in the acquisition integration workstream. Consider negotiating an extension of the transition period (e.g., 120–180 days) as part of the consent agreement, or at least confirm that the 90-day period is workable given the volume and complexity of Poubis's cold-chain operations.

**Open question for client:** What is the volume of Cargo in Gibeis's facilities at any given time? How long would it take to transfer that volume to an alternative provider? Are there any product-specific handling requirements that would extend the transition?

### 11. Audit Rights May Be Insufficient Post-Acquisition

**Document / Section:** MSA §6.5

**Issue:** Customer may audit Gibeis's facilities and records no more than twice in any 12-month period (unlimited after a Temperature Excursion or food safety incident). Post-acquisition, Ostrander's internal audit or food-safety team may want more frequent access, particularly during integration.

**Why it matters:** If Ostrander has a corporate food-safety audit program that requires more than two site visits per year, the MSA limits Poubis's ability to comply without Gibeis's agreement.

**Recommendation:** Low priority, but if a consent amendment is being negotiated anyway, consider increasing the audit frequency or adding a provision for acquirer-designated auditors.

### 12. "Deemed Assignment" Language Creates Ambiguity

**Document / Section:** MSA §15.1 (void assignments); §15.2 (CoC deemed an assignment)

**Issue:** §15.1 provides that any assignment in breach of §15 is "void." §15.2 provides that a CoC is "deemed an assignment" requiring consent. Read together, one could argue that an unconsented CoC renders the agreement (or the CoC's effect on it) void ab initio, rather than merely giving Gibeis a termination right. This creates uncertainty about whether the agreement continues in effect after an unconsented CoC or whether it is automatically void.

**Why it matters:** If the agreement is void, Poubis may lose all contractual protections (insurance, indemnification, transition rights) immediately upon the CoC, before Gibeis even sends a termination notice. Alternatively, if it is merely a termination right, the agreement continues until Gibeis acts. The practical difference is significant for operational continuity.

**Recommendation:** In the consent letter, have Gibeis confirm that the CoC does not render the agreement void and that the agreement continues in full force and effect. This eliminates the ambiguity contractually.
