## 1. Objection Notice Deadline Has Likely Expired

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 2.4(b); Gliluv Board Written Consent, Recital B and Resolution 2.2

**Issue.** Section 2.4(b) of the SPA requires Buyer to deliver an Objection Notice "within thirty (30) days after receipt of the Closing Statement." Seller delivered the Closing Statement on February 9, 2026 (Board Consent, Recital B). The 30-day deadline therefore expired on **March 11, 2026**. The Board's unanimous written consent authorizing the Objection Notice is dated **March 20, 2026**—nine days after the deadline.

If the Objection Notice has not in fact been delivered to Seller on or before March 11, Section 2.4(b) provides that "the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Working Capital will be final and binding on the parties" and "[a]ny item not specifically disputed in a timely Objection Notice will likewise be final and binding." The $1,900,000 working-capital overstatement identified by management (Recital C) would be lost.

**Why it matters.** This is the single most time-sensitive issue in the file. The Board has authorized a remedy that may no longer be available. If the notice was in fact delivered before March 11 (e.g., by management acting under prior authority before the formal Board consent was executed), the issue is moot, but that needs to be confirmed immediately.

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm with the CEO and CFO whether an Objection Notice was physically or electronically delivered to Seller on or before March 11, 2026, and obtain the delivery confirmation.
- If it was not delivered, assess whether Seller can be persuaded to waive the deadline (unlikely, but worth raising). Alternatively, evaluate whether Seller's preparation of the Closing Statement in bad faith or with known errors could support an equitable tolling argument under New York law, though this is a long shot.
- If the notice was timely delivered, confirm the content matches the three categories of overstatement in Recital C (stale receivables, prepaid fees for terminated agreements, omitted accrued liabilities) and that each is stated with "reasonable detail" as § 2.4(b) requires.

## 2. Escrow Agreement Eliminates Direct Recourse from Seller for All Indemnification Claims, Including Environmental

**Document / Section:** Escrow Agreement § 5.3; SPA § 9.6(b), § 9.5(b), § 11.8

**Issue.** SPA § 9.6 provides a two-tier source of recovery: (a) first from the Escrow Fund, and (b) "thereafter, with respect to Losses that are not subject to the cap in Section 9.5(a), directly from Seller." Section 9.5(b) confirms that the cap does not apply to Fundamental Representations, Fraud, or the Environmental Indemnity (§ 9.2(c)). Thus, under the SPA, Gliluv should be able to pursue Seller directly for environmental losses exceeding the $6.4M escrow.

Escrow Agreement § 5.3, however, states: "The Escrow Fund shall be the **sole and exclusive source of recovery** for all claims for indemnification by the Buyer Indemnitees under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, **including claims under Section 9.2(c)** of the Purchase Agreement. Once the Escrow Fund has been fully disbursed under this Agreement, **no Buyer Indemnitee shall have any further recourse against Seller or its Affiliates** in respect of any such claim."

SPA § 11.8 (Order of Precedence) resolves conflicts in favor of the SPA, "except that the Escrow Agreement shall govern with respect to recovery from the Escrow Fund." Whether § 5.3 is a rule about "recovery from the Escrow Fund" (and thus controls) or a substantive waiver of all recourse against Seller (and thus conflicts with the SPA's two-tier structure) is ambiguous on its face.

**Why it matters.** The Environmental Indemnity survives until the fifth anniversary of Closing (SPA § 9.1(c)), i.e., January 15, 2031. The Escrow Fund releases to Seller on July 15, 2027 (18 months, Escrow Agreement § 3.1). If § 5.3 is read to eliminate all recourse once the fund is disbursed, Gliluv would have **no remedy** for environmental liabilities discovered between July 2027 and January 2031—a three-year window during which OPA 90, MARPOL, or ballast-water claims could surface. The $6.4M escrow would be the absolute ceiling on all indemnification, regardless of the SPA's uncapped provisions.

**Recommendation.**
- Raise with Dreisend's counsel before any claim is made. Seek a written amendment to Escrow Agreement § 5.3 clarifying that it governs only the mechanics of recovery *from the Escrow Fund* and does not limit or waive the right to seek recovery directly from Seller under SPA § 9.6(b) for claims excluded from the cap (Fundamental Representations, Fraud, Environmental Indemnity).
- If Seller resists, consider whether the ambiguity can be resolved in Gliluv's favor under New York contract-interpretation principles (the SPA, as the principal agreement, expresses the parties' intent for a two-tier structure; the Escrow Agreement's recitals identify it as a mechanism for holding and disbursing the escrow, not as a substantive limitation on liability).
- In the interim, treat the $6.4M escrow as the only reliable source of recovery for all Article IX claims and factor that into any reserve analysis.

## 3. Transition Services Agreement: Recipient Indemnifies Provider for Provider's Own Negligence, Without Limit

**Document / Section:** TSA § 8.3, § 8.4, § 8.2

**Issue.** Section 8.3 provides that Gliluv (Recipient) "shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Provider... from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to the Services or the use of the Services by Recipient Group. **This includes Losses arising from the negligence of Provider or any Provider Personnel**, except to the extent those Losses are caused by the gross negligence or willful misconduct of Provider." The final sentence adds: "The obligations of Recipient under this Section 8.3 **shall not be subject to Section 8.4 or any other limitation**."

Meanwhile, § 8.4 caps Provider's aggregate liability at "total Service Fees actually paid by Recipient under this Agreement" (maximum approximately $3.43M over the full service periods, per Schedule A). And § 8.2 limits Provider's indemnity to Gliluv to only (a) gross negligence or willful misconduct, or (b) breach of the data/confidentiality articles.

The practical effect: if Provider's crew-management office negligently fails to track a seafarer's certification expiry and the vessel is detained at port, Gliluv bears the detention costs and must also indemnify Provider against any third-party claim arising from that negligence. Provider's exposure to Gliluv for the same event is zero (ordinary negligence is not covered by § 8.2), and in any event is capped at fees paid.

**Why it matters.** This inverts the normal risk allocation in a services agreement. Gliluv is paying for services and simultaneously assuming the service provider's operational risk. The unlimited nature of Gliluv's indemnity (expressly carved out of the cap) means a single serious incident—crew injury, vessel detention, regulatory fine attributable to Provider's negligence—could generate liability well in excess of the total value of the services.

**Recommendation.**
- This is a closed agreement (effective January 15, 2026), so renegotiation is difficult. However, if a dispute arises or if Gliluv exercises its extension right under § 7.2, use that as leverage to amend § 8.3.
- Seek to delete the sentence "This includes Losses arising from the negligence of Provider or any Provider Personnel" or, at minimum, subject § 8.3 to the same cap in § 8.4.
- In the short term, ensure Gliluv's insurance program (including the Company's existing policies) explicitly covers liability assumed under TSA § 8.3. Confirm with the broker that the policy responds to indemnification obligations of this type.
- Going forward, if any Additional Services are added under § 2.4, make the correction to § 8.3 a condition of signing the supplement.

## 4. Tax Representation Survival Period Is Only Six Months

**Document / Section:** SPA § 9.1(b), § 9.2(d)

**Issue.** Section 9.1(b) provides that the Tax Representations (§ 4.11) survive "until six (6) months after the Closing Date"—i.e., until **July 15, 2026**. Section 9.2(d) makes the tax indemnity "subject to the survival period applicable to the Tax Representations under Section 9.1." Thus, any claim for pre-closing Taxes must be noticed before July 15, 2026, or it is time-barred.

The federal income-tax statute of limitations is generally three years (IRC § 6501(a)). The Company is subject to U.S. gross transportation tax under § 887 and potentially to tonnage taxes, state income taxes, and foreign taxes. A tax audit or assessment could easily arise after July 2026.

**Why it matters.** If the IRS (or a state or foreign authority) assesses additional tax for a Pre-Closing Tax Period after July 15, 2026, Gliluv has no indemnification recourse against Seller. Given that the Company's last audited financials are as of December 31, 2024 (SPA § 4.5) and the tax representation covers all periods "ending on or before the Closing Date," there is meaningful exposure for 2024 and 2025 tax years that will not be fully examined within six months.

**Recommendation.**
- This is a closed agreement and the survival period cannot be unilaterally extended. However:
  - Confirm that the Company's tax returns for 2024 and the stub period of 2025 have been filed and that no extensions are outstanding. If a filing extension is in place, the six-month survival may expire before the return is even filed.
  - Review the Disclosure Schedule (not in the folder) for any tax-related disclosures that might indicate known issues.
  - Ensure that the working-capital dispute (if timely raised) captures any tax accruals or reserves that were improperly excluded from the Closing Statement.
  - For future deals, negotiate tax survival to match the applicable statute of limitations plus a six-month tail (the market standard).

## 5. Environmental Indemnity: Gap Between Escrow Release and Survival Expiry

**Document / Section:** SPA § 9.1(c), § 9.2(c); Escrow Agreement § 3.1, § 5.3

**Issue.** The Environmental Indemnity (§ 9.2(c)) survives until the fifth anniversary of Closing (January 15, 2031). The Escrow Fund is released to Seller on July 15, 2027. As discussed in Issue 2, Escrow Agreement § 5.3 may eliminate all recourse once the fund is disbursed. Even if § 5.3 is read narrowly (governing only escrow mechanics), the practical reality is that Seller is a Bermuda exempted company, and collecting a judgment against it after the escrow is gone may be difficult.

**Why it matters.** Maritime environmental liabilities (OPA 90 removal costs, MARPOL fines, natural resource damages) often surface with a lag—contamination may not be detected for years, and ballast-water or fuel-oil issues can emerge after vessel delivery. A three-year window (July 2027 – January 2031) during which the indemnity is technically alive but the escrow is gone and the Seller is offshore creates real collection risk.

**Recommendation.**
- In conjunction with the amendment sought in Issue 2, consider requesting that the escrow release date for environmental claims be extended to the fifth anniversary, or that a portion of the escrow (e.g., $1M) be ring-fenced for environmental claims until that date.
- If Seller will not agree, assess whether a parent guarantee or letter of credit from a Dreisend affiliate could substitute for the escrow as security for the environmental tail.
- Review the Company's vessel-management agreements and any P&I club cover to understand what environmental liabilities are already insured versus what would fall to the Company (and thus to Gliluv) directly.

## 6. Provider's Indemnity Does Not Cover Ordinary Negligence

**Document / Section:** TSA § 8.2

**Issue.** Provider indemnifies Gliluv only for (a) gross negligence or willful misconduct, or (b) breach of the data-security and confidentiality articles. Ordinary negligence in performing crew management, vessel-performance data, ERP, or chartering services is not covered. Combined with Issue 3 (where Gliluv indemnifies Provider for Provider's ordinary negligence), the risk allocation is entirely one-directional.

**Recommendation.** Address as part of the § 8.3 amendment discussed in Issue 3. At minimum, seek to add "negligence" (without the "gross" qualifier) to § 8.2(a).

## 7. Lease: Subordination, Non-Disturbance and Attornment Agreement (SNDA) – Status Unconfirmed

**Document / Section:** Saltreach Office Lease § 17.1

**Issue.** The lease is subordinate to any mortgage on the building, conditioned on the mortgagee delivering a "commercially reasonable subordination, non-disturbance and attornment agreement" (SNDA). Landlord was required to deliver the SNDA from its existing mortgagee within 60 days of the Effective Date (by approximately May 1, 2022). The folder does not include the SNDA or any confirmation of delivery.

**Why it matters.** Without a valid SNDA, if the mortgagee forecloses, the new owner (often the mortgagee itself) can terminate the lease or refuse to attorn to it. Saltreach's Stamford headquarters (9,800 sq ft, the Company's primary shore-based office) could be lost.

**Recommendation.** Request from Seller (or the Company's file) a copy of the executed SNDA. If it was not delivered, raise as a post-closing item with Landlord. Confirm the identity of the current mortgagee and whether the building has been refinanced since 2022.

## 8. Lease: Renewal Option Notice Window

**Document / Section:** Saltreach Office Lease § 3.3

**Issue.** The one-time five-year renewal option requires written notice "at least twelve (12) months, and not more than eighteen (18) months, before the Expiration Date" (March 31, 2029). The notice window is therefore **September 30, 2027 through March 31, 2028**. The option is available only if "no Event of Default exists when the notice is given."

**Why it matters.** If Gliluv wants the option, it must decide by September 30, 2027—before the escrow releases and before the TSA service periods have all expired. The decision will need to be made with knowledge of whether the Company's Stamford footprint is still appropriate.

**Recommendation.** Calendar the notice window. Begin internal planning no later than Q2 2027. Verify that no Event of Default exists (or is curable) before the notice date.

## 9. TSA: No Dispute-Resolution Mechanism for the Migration Plan

**Document / Section:** TSA § 3.2

**Issue.** Section 3.2 requires the parties to "agree" a written Migration Plan within 45 days after the Effective Date (by approximately March 1, 2026). The folder does not include the Migration Plan. If the parties have not agreed, there is no escalation path—no independent expert, no arbitration, no court-specific remedy. The only general dispute-resolution clause is § 12.2 (litigation in Manhattan), which would be slow and expensive for a data-migration disagreement.

**Why it matters.** The Migration Plan governs the transfer of crew records, charter data, ERP data, and vessel-performance data—assets critical to the Company's operations. If Provider delays or withholds cooperation on migration, Gliluv has no contractual lever short of a breach claim (which, per Issues 3 and 6, is difficult to monetize against Provider).

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm whether the Migration Plan was agreed by March 1, 2026. If not, escalate immediately through the service managers (Schedule A) and, if needed, via formal notice under § 12.3.
- If the plan is stalled, consider whether Provider's failure to agree constitutes a material breach supporting termination under § 7.4 (though termination would accelerate the need to find replacement providers).

## 10. Financial Statement Gap: No Q4 2025 Statements

**Document / Section:** SPA § 4.5, § 4.6

**Issue.** The most recent financial statements delivered are unaudited interim statements as of September 30, 2025. The Closing occurred on January 15, 2026. There is a nearly four-month gap. The "Absence of Changes" representation (§ 4.6) runs from December 31, 2024, but is not supported by any financial statement for Q4 2025.

**Why it matters.** The working-capital calculation is as of January 14, 2026 (the day before Closing). If the Q4 2025 period included unusual items (year-end accruals, bonus payments, vessel-delivery costs), these would not be visible in the September 30 statements. This gap may have contributed to the $1.9M overstatement in the Closing Statement.

**Recommendation.** If the Objection Notice was timely (see Issue 1), use the Q4 gap to support the position that Seller's Closing Statement was not prepared in accordance with the Accounting Principles. Request Q4 2025 trial-balance detail under § 2.4(d) (access to books and records).

## 12. Non-Solicitation Limited to Shore-Based Employees

**Document / Section:** SPA § 6.5

**Issue.** The two-year non-solicit covers only "shore-based employees of the Company." Crew members, who are supplied through crew-supply arrangements (SPA § 4.12), are not covered. Seller or its affiliates could poach the Company's crew-supply relationships or approach individual master mariners and chief engineers.

**Recommendation.** For a ship-management company, crew relationships are a key asset. Consider whether the Disclosure Schedule identifies any crew-supply agreements that are exclusive or that contain their own non-solicit provisions. If not, this is a gap that cannot be cured post-closing (the SPA is executed), but it should inform Gliluv's retention strategy for key crewing personnel in the first 24 months.
