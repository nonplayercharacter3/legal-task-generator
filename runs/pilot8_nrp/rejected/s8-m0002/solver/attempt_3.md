## Issue 1: Survival Period Reduced from 18 Months to 6 Months

**Documents:** APA § 9.1; LOI ¶ 6

**What the documents say:**

- LOI ¶ 6: "Seller's representations and warranties will survive for **18 months** after closing. This includes the healthcare billing and payer compliance representations."
- APA § 9.1: "The representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement, **including the representations and warranties in Section 4.12**, shall survive the Closing until the date that is **six (6) months** following the Closing Date."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

This is the single most consequential discrepancy in the deal documents. The healthcare billing and payer compliance representation (APA § 4.12) covers a **six-year lookback period** and is the primary protection against pre-closing Medicaid, CHIP, and private payer audit exposure. Payer audits in the dental industry routinely take 12–36 months to initiate, complete, and result in a recoupment or overpayment determination. With only 6 months of survival (expiring approximately **September 2, 2026**), Saitouth will have no contractual indemnification recourse for the vast majority of pre-closing billing issues that surface after that date.

Because the APA is the operative agreement, the 6-month period controls. The LOI is non-binding (LOI ¶ 14) and cannot be used to extend survival.

**Status:** If the current date is on or after September 2, 2026, the survival period for non-Fundamental Representations (including § 4.12) has **already expired**. No Claim Notice can be delivered for these reps after expiration (APA § 9.1, final sentence).

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Determine immediately whether Saitouth has received or is aware of any pending or threatened payer audit, recoupment demand, or overpayment notice relating to pre-closing services. If so, assess whether a Claim Notice was delivered before September 2, 2026.
2. If no claim was timely made, evaluate whether there is a basis to argue that Seller breached its covenant in APA § 6.5 (Payer Transition) or that the short survival period renders the indemnification provision unconscionable or a material deviation from the parties' agreement as evidenced by the LOI. This is a weak position but worth assessing.
3. For any future transactions, ensure the APA survival period matches the LOI and that healthcare compliance reps carry a survival period of no less than the applicable statute of limitations (typically 6 years for FCA claims).

**Proposed language for future deals (APA § 9.1):**

> "The representations and warranties of Seller contained in Section 4.12 (Healthcare Billing and Payer Compliance) shall survive the Closing until the later of (a) eighteen (18) months following the Closing Date or (b) the date on which any audit, investigation, recoupment or overpayment demand relating to pre-Closing services is resolved in a final, non-appealable determination."

## Issue 2: IP Assignment Caps IP-Related Claims at $840,000, Overriding the Fundamental Representation Cap

**Documents:** IP Assignment § 7; APA § 9.5(a)–(b); APA § 11.8; APA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations")

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 1.1 defines "Fundamental Representations" to include **Section 4.9 (Intellectual Property)**.
- APA § 9.5(b): "The Cap shall **not apply** to Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of the Fundamental Representations, **including Section 4.9** … Seller's aggregate liability for such Losses, other than in the case of fraud, shall not exceed the **Purchase Price** [$8,400,000]."
- IP Assignment § 7: "Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, **whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement**, shall not exceed **the Cap** [$840,000]."
- APA § 11.8 (Order of Precedence): "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall control, **except that the IP Assignment shall control as to the Assigned IP**."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

The IP Assignment, by its own terms and by the APA's order-of-precedence clause, **controls** for all matters relating to the Assigned IP. Section 7 of the IP Assignment expressly pulls Section 4.9 claims into the $840,000 Cap, even though Section 4.9 is a Fundamental Representation that the APA would otherwise cap at $8.4 million. This is a **tenfold reduction** in maximum recovery for IP breaches.

The IP includes registered trademarks (TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL, TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL & Tooth Design), domain names, social media accounts, and proprietary software (Trolind RecallPro, Trolind Patient Portal). If Seller's representation that it is the "sole and exclusive owner" (APA § 4.9) turns out to be inaccurate—for example, if a third party has a prior claim to a mark or the software was developed under a restrictive license—Saitouth's maximum recovery is $840,000 rather than $8.4 million.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm whether Saitouth has conducted post-closing IP due diligence (e.g., USPTO assignment records, domain registrant checks, software license review) and whether any issues have surfaced.
2. If no IP issues have been identified, the practical risk is lower, but the structural problem remains. Negotiate an amendment to IP Assignment § 7 to carve out Fundamental Representation claims from the Cap, consistent with APA § 9.5(b).
3. If an IP issue has been identified, assess the value of the IP against the $840,000 cap to determine whether the limitation is material.

**Proposed amendment to IP Assignment § 7:**

> "Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, shall not exceed the Cap; **provided, that** this limitation shall not apply to Losses arising from any inaccuracy in or breach of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement to the extent such Section constitutes a Fundamental Representation, which shall be subject to the limitations set forth in Section 9.5(b) of the Purchase Agreement."

## Issue 3: 150-Day Set-Off Window Has Likely Expired

**Documents:** Note § 5.2; APA § 9.6; LOI ¶ 4(b)

**What the documents say:**

- Note § 5.1: Maker (Saitouth) may set off indemnifiable Losses against Note payments.
- Note § 5.2: "Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 **only if** Maker delivers to Holder a written notice (a 'Set-off Notice') **within one hundred fifty (150) days after the Issue Date**."
- The Issue Date is March 2, 2026. 150 days later is approximately **July 30, 2026**.
- LOI ¶ 4(b): "Buyer will have the right to set off amounts owed to it for indemnifiable losses against payments due under the Seller Note, **on terms to be set out in the Definitive Agreement and the Seller Note**." The LOI did not impose a 150-day deadline.

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

The set-off right is Saitouth's primary self-help remedy against the $1.8 million Seller Note. If the 150-day window has passed without a Set-off Notice being delivered, Saitouth has **lost its contractual right to set off** indemnifiable losses against Note payments. Saitouth would still be obligated to make quarterly installments of $150,000 (Note § 3.1) and would have to pursue indemnification separately through the APA's Claim Notice procedure (APA § 9.4)—a process that requires actual payment of Losses first (see Issue 4 below).

This is particularly acute because the survival period for most reps also expired around the same time (Issue 1). Saitouth may be in a position where it can neither set off nor claim indemnification.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm immediately whether a Set-off Notice was delivered on or before July 30, 2026. If not, the window has closed.
2. If the window has closed, evaluate whether Seller's agreement to the 150-day limitation (which was not in the LOI) constitutes a material deviation that could support a claim for reformation or equitable estoppel. This is a difficult argument but should be assessed.
3. For future deals, the set-off window should be tied to the survival period, not a fixed short deadline.

**Proposed language for future deals (Note § 5.2):**

> "Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 at any time during the survival period of the applicable representation, warranty or covenant under Section 9.1 of the Purchase Agreement, by delivering to Holder a written Set-off Notice."

## Issue 4: Claim Notice Requires Actual Payment of Losses (vs. LOI's "Reasonably Anticipated" Standard)

**Documents:** APA § 9.4(b); LOI ¶ 7(e)

**What the documents say:**

- LOI ¶ 7(e): "A claim notice … may be given for losses that have been incurred **or are reasonably anticipated, including losses arising from a pending audit or recoupment demand**."
- APA § 9.4(b): "A Claim Notice shall … include invoices, remittance records or **other documentation of Losses actually paid** by the Indemnified Party. A Claim Notice that does not satisfy this Section 9.4(b) **shall not be valid**."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

The APA requires Saitouth to have **actually paid** the loss before it can deliver a valid Claim Notice. This means:

- If a payer audit is pending and a recoupment demand has been issued but not yet paid, Saitouth cannot claim indemnification until it pays the recoupment.
- If Saitouth is required to post a bond or make a payment under protest to maintain its payer enrollment, it must first absorb the cost and then seek reimbursement.
- Combined with the 6-month survival period (Issue 1), Saitouth may be forced to pay a recoupment after the survival period has expired, at which point no Claim Notice can be delivered.

The LOI's "reasonably anticipated" standard would have allowed Saitouth to preserve its claim when an audit was initiated, even before the final determination and payment.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm whether any payer audit or recoupment demand is currently pending. If so, the practical impact of this provision is immediate.
2. If the survival period has already expired (see Issue 1), this issue is largely moot for non-Fundamental Reps but remains relevant for Fundamental Reps (which survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations expires).
3. For future deals, restore the LOI standard.

**Proposed language for future deals (APA § 9.4(b)):**

> "A Claim Notice shall describe in reasonable detail the facts giving rise to the claim and the provision of this Agreement under which indemnification is sought. A Claim Notice may be delivered for Losses that have been actually paid or that are reasonably anticipated, including Losses arising from a pending audit, investigation or recoupment demand. For Losses actually paid, the Claim Notice shall include invoices, remittance records or other documentation of payment. For reasonably anticipated Losses, the Claim Notice shall include a good-faith estimate of the amount and the basis for the anticipation."

## Issue 5: Missing Closing Conditions (Due Diligence Satisfaction, MAC, Payer Transition)

**Documents:** APA § 7.1; LOI ¶ 10

**What the documents say:**

LOI ¶ 10 listed the following conditions to closing:
- "completion of Buyer's legal, financial, billing and operational due diligence **to Buyer's satisfaction**"
- "the absence of any **material adverse change** in the Business"
- "arrangements **satisfactory to Buyer** for transition of payer enrollment"
- "receipt of all required approvals"

APA § 7.1 conditions Buyer's obligation to close to only:
- (a) accuracy of Seller's reps in all material respects;
- (b) Seller's performance of covenants in all material respects;
- (c) receipt of landlord consents; and
- (d) delivery of closing items.

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

The omission of the **MAC condition** and the **due diligence satisfaction condition** means Saitouth was contractually obligated to close even if it discovered material adverse changes or was unsatisfied with its due diligence findings (subject only to the rep accuracy condition). The omission of the **payer enrollment transition** condition means Saitouth closed without a contractual guarantee that payer transitions were arranged.

Given that the press release (Section 3) states Saitouth is still "working with" Medicaid, CHIP, and private payers to transition enrollment, this gap is practically significant. If payer enrollment is delayed, patients may lose coverage, revenue may be disrupted, and Saitouth has no contractual lever to compel Seller to accelerate the transition beyond the general cooperation covenant in APA § 6.5.

**Status:** Because the deal has closed, these omitted conditions cannot be invoked to walk away. However, they are relevant if Saitouth seeks to argue that Seller breached its covenants (e.g., § 6.5 Payer Transition) or that the closing was not properly conditioned.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm the current status of payer enrollment transitions for all four clinics. Identify any payers where enrollment is delayed or where Saitouth has not yet been approved.
2. If payer transitions are stalled, send a formal notice under APA § 6.5 requesting Seller's cooperation and documenting the delay. This preserves the record for a potential covenant breach claim (which survives in accordance with its terms under APA § 9.1).
3. For future deals, ensure all LOI conditions are carried into the APA.

## Issue 6: IP Assignment Definition of "Business" Omits Orthodontic Services

**Documents:** IP Assignment § 1.1; APA § 1.1

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 1.1: "**Business** means general dentistry, hygiene **and orthodontic services** conducted at the Clinics."
- IP Assignment § 1.1: "**Business** means general dentistry and hygiene services conducted at the Clinics." (No reference to orthodontics.)

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

The IP Assignment's definition of "Assigned IP" is tied to IP "owned by Assignor and used **primarily in the Business**" (IP Assignment § 1.1). By omitting "orthodontic services" from the definition of Business, there is an argument that IP used primarily in the orthodontic practice (e.g., orthodontic-specific software modules, orthodontic patient-recall lists, orthodontic marketing materials, or orthodontic-specific trade names) falls outside the scope of the Assigned IP.

The LOI ¶ 11 expressly contemplated transfer of "the general dentistry, hygiene **and orthodontic brands** and the patient-recall software." The APA's definition of Intellectual Property Assets (APA § 1.1) is not limited by the "Business" definition—it covers all IP "used in the Business" as defined in the APA (which includes orthodontics). But the IP Assignment, which controls for Assigned IP matters (APA § 11.8), uses its own narrower definition.

Schedule A lists only TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL marks. If there are separate orthodontic brands (e.g., a "Trolind Orthodontics" mark or a distinct orthodontic logo), they may not be captured.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm whether the orthodontic practice operates under any separate brand, trade name, logo, or marketing identity distinct from the general "TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL" marks. Review the orthodontic clinics' websites, social media, and in-office signage.
2. If separate orthodontic IP exists, negotiate a supplemental assignment or an amendment to the IP Assignment to correct the "Business" definition and add the orthodontic IP to the schedules.
3. Even if no separate orthodontic brand exists, correct the definition to avoid ambiguity.

**Proposed amendment (IP Assignment § 1.1):**

> "**Business** means general dentistry, hygiene **and orthodontic services** conducted at the Clinics, as defined in Section 1.1 of the Purchase Agreement."

## Issue 7: No Deadline for Delivery of the IP Assignment

**Documents:** APA § 6.6; IP Assignment (dated October 12, 2026)

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 6.6: "Following the Closing, each Party shall execute and deliver such additional instruments as the other Party reasonably requests … Without limiting the foregoing, Seller shall execute and deliver the IP Assignment and such short-form recordable assignments as are necessary to record the transfer of the Intellectual Property Assets …"
- No specific deadline is stated.
- The IP Assignment was not executed until **October 12, 2026**—approximately seven months after the March 2, 2026 Closing.

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

Between the Closing and the execution of the IP Assignment, Saitouth did not have a recorded assignment of the trademarks, domain names, or software. During this period:

- The USPTO records still showed Seller as the owner of the registered marks.
- Domain names were still registered in Seller's name (or at least not yet transferred).
- If Seller had encountered financial distress or entered bankruptcy during this window, the IP could have been at risk.
- Third parties (e.g., a competitor or a patient) could have challenged Saitouth's right to use the marks.

While the IP Assignment confirms the transfer was "effective as of the Closing Date" (IP Assignment § 2.1), the delay in recordation creates a gap in the chain of title that could complicate enforcement against third parties.

**Recommendation:**

1. Confirm that the short-form trademark assignments (Exhibit 1 to the IP Assignment) have been recorded with the USPTO and that the domain name transfers have been completed with the registrars (IP Assignment §§ 4.2–4.3).
2. For future deals, include a specific deadline (e.g., 30 days post-closing) for delivery of the IP Assignment and recordation of assignments, with a liquidated damages or specific performance remedy for delay.

**Proposed language for future deals (APA § 6.6):**

> "Seller shall execute and deliver the IP Assignment no later than thirty (30) days following the Closing Date and shall cause the short-form recordable assignments to be filed with the USPTO and domain name registrars no later than sixty (60) days following the Closing Date. Seller's failure to comply with these deadlines shall constitute a breach of covenant for which Buyer shall be entitled to specific performance and, if the failure continues for more than thirty (30) days after written notice, a liquidated payment of $500 per day until compliance."

## Issue 8: Note § 7(c) Change-of-Control Event of Default

**Documents:** Note § 7(c)

**What the documents say:**

> "(c) Maker sells or otherwise transfers all or substantially all of the Purchased Assets to a person that is not an affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is repaid in full at or before the completion of that transfer."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

If Saitouth later sells the acquired clinics (or substantially all of the Purchased Assets) to a third party that is not an affiliate, the Note becomes immediately due and payable. This could:

- Restrict Saitouth's ability to sell underperforming clinics or restructure its portfolio.
- Create a liquidity event if Saitouth is acquired by a larger DSO (the acquirer would not be an "affiliate" of Saitouth at the time of the transfer).
- Complicate any future sale of the clinics to a strategic buyer.

The exception for "affiliates" provides some flexibility, but a sale to a non-affiliate (including a parent company in a reorganization or a strategic acquirer) triggers default.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm Saitouth's corporate structure and whether any near-term reorganization, upsizing, or sale of assets is being contemplated. If Saitouth is itself a subsidiary or may be acquired, this provision needs to be addressed.
2. Negotiate an amendment to Note § 7(c) to add an exception for (a) a sale to a parent or affiliate of Maker, (b) a sale in connection with a change of control of Maker where the acquirer assumes the Note, or (c) a sale where the proceeds are sufficient to repay the Note.

**Proposed amendment (Note § 7(c)):**

> "(c) Maker sells or otherwise transfers all or substantially all of the Purchased Assets to a person that is not an affiliate of Maker, unless (i) this Note is repaid in full at or before the completion of that transfer, (ii) the transferee assumes this Note in writing and is not in default under this Note, or (iii) the sale is made to a parent entity of Maker or to an acquirer of all or substantially all of Maker's equity interests."

## Issue 9: Press Release Creates Public Commitments That May Exceed Contractual Obligations

**Documents:** Press Release §§ 2–3; APA §§ 6.3, 6.5

**What the documents say:**

- Press Release § 2: "Existing appointments, treatment plans and **orthodontic treatment in progress** will continue as scheduled. Patient records will remain available at each clinic in accordance with applicable privacy law."
- Press Release § 3: "Saitouth is **working with** Medicaid, the Delaware Children's Health Insurance Program and private dental benefits plans to transition payer enrollment for the clinics. Patients should continue to present their current insurance information at their next visit."
- Press Release § 4: Trolind President's quote: "This **partnership** positions our clinics and our staff for the **next chapter of growth**."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

1. **Payer transition:** The press release publicly states that patients should "continue to present their current insurance information." If payer enrollment is not completed and patients are denied coverage, this public statement could create patient complaints, regulatory scrutiny, or even a claim of misleading publicity. APA § 6.5 requires Seller to "cooperate" but does not guarantee a specific timeline or outcome.

2. **Orthodontic treatment continuity:** The press release commits to continuation of "orthodontic treatment in progress." If orthodontic staff do not accept employment offers (APA § 6.3 requires Buyer to "offer" employment, not guarantee acceptance), or if orthodontic equipment is inadequate (APA § 4.6 is a rep that may have expired), Saitouth could be in breach of its public commitment.

3. **Non-compete tension:** The Trolind President's characterization of the relationship as a "partnership" for "the next chapter of growth" could be cited by Seller in a future non-compete dispute to argue that the restrictive covenant (APA § 6.2) should be construed narrowly or that the relationship was collaborative rather than adversarial.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm the current status of payer enrollment. If any payer has not yet approved Saitouth, assess the risk of patient denials and consider whether a patient communication is needed.
2. Monitor orthodontic staff retention. If key orthodontists decline employment offers, assess the impact on in-progress treatment plans and the press release commitment.
3. No immediate action needed on the non-compete point, but be aware of the language if a dispute arises.

## Issue 10: Non-Competition Covenant – Enforceability and Scope

**Documents:** APA § 6.2; LOI ¶ 9

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 6.2: "For **five years** after the Closing Date, Seller shall not, directly or indirectly, **own, operate or provide dental services** through any dental practice located within **ten miles** of any Clinic, or **solicit** any Transferred Employee or any patient of the Clinics."

**Why it matters for Saitouth:**

- **Duration:** Five years is at the outer boundary of what Delaware courts will enforce for a dental practice non-compete. Delaware courts generally favor 1–3 year restrictions. A five-year restriction may be challenged as overbroad, particularly as to the non-solicitation of patients (which is arguably less justified than a non-compete on the practice itself).
- **Scope:** The restriction covers "own, operate or provide dental services." If the Seller's doctors (individual dentists) are not parties to the APA, the restriction binds only the corporate entity (Trolind Family Dental, P.A.). Individual dentists who were shareholders or officers of Seller may not be personally bound unless they are also parties.
- **Geography:** Ten miles from each of four clinics in New Castle County could cover a very large area, potentially encompassing most of the county. This breadth supports enforceability (it is tailored to the market) but could be challenged if found to be broader than necessary.

**Recommendation:**

1. **[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]** Confirm whether the individual doctors/shareholders of Trolind Family Dental, P.A. signed the APA or any separate non-compete. If not, the covenant binds only the P.A. entity, which may be a shell after the asset sale.
2. If individual doctors are not bound, consider whether to seek individual non-compete agreements as a condition of the Transferred Employee offers (APA § 6.3) or as a separate post-closing matter.
3. The five-year duration is aggressive. If challenged, a court may blue-pencil it to three years. Be prepared for this possibility.
