## Issue 1 – Survival Period Reduced from 18 Months to 6 Months

**Documents and Sections:**
- LOI ¶ 6: "Seller's representations and warranties will survive for 18 months after closing. This includes the healthcare billing and payer compliance representations."
- APA § 9.1: "The representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement, including the representations and warranties in Section 4.12, shall survive the Closing until the date that is six (6) months following the Closing Date."

**Why It Matters:**
This is the single most consequential deviation from the LOI. Section 4.12 of the APA contains a healthcare billing and payer compliance representation with a **six-year lookback period** covering claims accuracy, coding, Anti-Kickback Statute compliance, False Claims Act compliance, and Delaware Medicaid program rules. Payer audits and recoupment demands routinely surface 12–36 months after the services were rendered. With only 6 months of survival (expiring approximately September 2, 2026), Saitouth will lose its indemnification right for the vast majority of pre-closing billing issues before they are discovered. The 6-year lookback in § 4.12 is rendered largely illusory if Saitouth has only 6 months to act on it.

Additionally, APA § 9.1 states that "No claim may be made in respect of any representation or warranty after its survival period expires, unless a valid Claim Notice was delivered under Section 9.4 before that expiration." Combined with § 9.4(b)'s requirement that a Claim Notice include "documentation of Losses actually paid" (see Issue 5), Saitouth must both discover and *pay* a loss within 6 months of closing to preserve its claim.

**Recommended Fix:**
Restore the 18-month survival period for all Seller representations and warranties, or at minimum for § 4.12 (Healthcare Billing and Payer Compliance). If Seller resists a blanket 18-month period, a tiered approach is acceptable:

> **Proposed § 9.1 (revised):** "The representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement shall survive the Closing as follows: (a) the representations and warranties in Section 4.12 (Healthcare Billing and Payer Compliance) shall survive until the date that is the later of (i) twenty-four (24) months following the Closing Date or (ii) sixty (60) days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations for any claim or audit that could arise from the period covered by Section 4.12; (b) the Fundamental Representations shall survive until sixty (60) days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations; and (c) all other representations and warranties of Seller shall survive until the date that is eighteen (18) months following the Closing Date."

**⚑ CLIENT INPUT NEEDED:** The client should confirm whether it is willing to accept 18 months across the board (per the LOI) or whether it would prefer to negotiate a longer period specifically for § 4.12 (e.g., 24–36 months) given the six-year lookback. The client should also confirm whether it has received any audit notices or recoupment demands to date that would make this issue urgent.

## Issue 2 – IP Assignment Caps IP-Related Liability at $840,000, Overriding Fundamental Representation Treatment

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1: "Fundamental Representations" includes "Section 4.9 (Intellectual Property)."
- APA § 9.5(b): "The Cap shall not apply to Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of the Fundamental Representations, including Section 4.9, or to fraud. Seller's aggregate liability for such Losses, other than in the case of fraud, shall not exceed the Purchase Price."
- APA § 11.8: "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall control, **except that the IP Assignment shall control as to the Assigned IP**."
- IP Assignment § 7: "Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, **whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement**, shall not exceed the Cap."
- IP Assignment § 1.1: "'Cap' has the meaning given in Section 9.5(a) of the Purchase Agreement" (i.e., $840,000).

**Why It Matters:**
Under the APA alone, a breach of § 4.9 (IP) would be treated as a Fundamental Representation breach, carrying a liability cap of the full Purchase Price ($8,400,000) and no basket. However, IP Assignment § 7 expressly subordinates § 4.9 to the $840,000 Cap for all IP-related claims, and APA § 11.8 gives the IP Assignment controlling effect "as to the Assigned IP." The net result is that Saitouth's maximum recovery for an IP problem—such as discovering that Seller did not own the patient-recall software, that a third party holds a prior license, or that the trademarks are subject to an undisclosed encumbrance—is $840,000 rather than $8,400,000. This is a tenfold reduction in protection for a category of risk that the parties themselves classified as "fundamental."

**Recommended Fix:**
Amend IP Assignment § 7 to conform to the APA's Fundamental Representation framework:

> **Proposed IP Assignment § 7 (revised):** "Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, shall be subject to the limitations set forth in Section 9.5 of the Purchase Agreement, including the treatment of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement as a Fundamental Representation. For the avoidance of doubt, the Cap set forth in Section 9.5(a) of the Purchase Agreement shall not apply to Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement or of the representations in Section 6.1 of this Assignment."

Alternatively, if Seller will not agree to remove the cap entirely for IP claims, Saitouth should at minimum negotiate to raise the IP-specific cap to 25–50% of the Purchase Price and to exclude IP claims from the $84,000 basket.

**⚑ CLIENT INPUT NEEDED:** The client should assess the value it places on the IP assets (particularly the patient-recall software and the TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL trademark registrations) relative to the $840,000 cap. If the software is a proprietary platform with significant ongoing value, the current cap is inadequate.

## Issue 3 – 150-Day Set-Off Deadline in the Note Effectively Eliminates the Set-Off Remedy

**Documents and Sections:**
- LOI ¶ 4(b): "Buyer will have the right to set off amounts owed to it for indemnifiable losses against payments due under the Seller Note, on terms to be set out in the Definitive Agreement and the Seller Note."
- Note § 5.1: "Maker may set off against any installment of principal or interest under this Note the amount of any Losses for which Maker is entitled to indemnification under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, as provided in Section 9.6 of the Purchase Agreement."
- Note § 5.2: "Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 **only if** Maker delivers to Holder a written notice (a 'Set-off Notice') **within one hundred fifty (150) days after the Issue Date**."
- Note § 1 (Issue Date): March 2, 2026.

**Why It Matters:**
The Issue Date is March 2, 2026. One hundred fifty days later is approximately **July 30, 2026**. After that date, Saitouth loses its right to set off indemnifiable losses against the Note entirely. The LOI contemplated a set-off right "on terms to be set out in the Definitive Agreement" but did not impose (or suggest) a 150-day outside date. Given that:

- The survival period for most Seller reps is only 6 months (i.e., until ~September 2, 2026) (see Issue 1);
- Healthcare billing audits can take 12+ months to resolve; and
- A valid Claim Notice under APA § 9.4(b) requires documentation of "Losses actually paid" (see Issue 5);

Saitouth may not be in a position to deliver a conforming Set-off Notice before July 30, 2026, even for losses that are clearly indemnifiable. The 150-day deadline converts what should be a practical self-help remedy into a right that is likely to expire unused.

**Recommended Fix:**
Delete the 150-day outside date in Note § 5.2 and replace it with a deadline tied to the applicable survival period:

> **Proposed Note § 5.2 (revised):** "Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 only if Maker delivers to Holder a written notice (a 'Set-off Notice') within thirty (30) days after Maker delivers a valid Claim Notice under Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement in respect of the related Losses, and in any event before the expiration of the applicable survival period for the representation, warranty or covenant giving rise to the Losses."

If Seller insists on an outside date, it should be no shorter than the survival period for the relevant representation (i.e., at least 18 months from the Closing Date, or 24 months for § 4.12 if Issue 1 is resolved as recommended).

**⚑ CLIENT INPUT NEEDED:** Confirm whether Saitouth has already identified any specific losses or pending audits for which it would want to exercise the set-off before the July 30, 2026 deadline. If so, this becomes time-critical.

## Issue 4 – IP Assignment Omits Orthodontic Services from the Definition of "Business"

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1: "'Business' means general dentistry, **hygiene and orthodontic services** conducted at the Clinics."
- IP Assignment § 1.1: "'Business' means general dentistry **and hygiene services** conducted at the Clinics." (Orthodontic services omitted.)
- LOI ¶ 1: "Buyer's proposal covers the Business as a whole, **including the orthodontic practice and its related brands, equipment and patient-recall systems**."
- IP Assignment § 1.1, "Assigned IP": "all intellectual property owned by Assignor and **used primarily in the Business**."

**Why It Matters:**
The "Assigned IP" is defined by reference to IP "used primarily in the Business." Because the IP Assignment's definition of "Business" excludes orthodontic services, any IP used primarily in the orthodontic practice—such as orthodontic-specific branding, marketing materials, patient education content, or software modules dedicated to orthodontic treatment planning—may fall outside the scope of the assignment. The LOI expressly included the orthodontic practice and its related brands and systems in the deal. Saitouth could find itself operating an orthodontic practice without clear title to the IP supporting it.

Looking at the schedules: Schedule A lists only "TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL" marks (no separate orthodontic brand); Schedule C lists "Trolind RecallPro" (described as having "hygiene recall scheduling modules" but no orthodontic-specific module) and "Trolind Patient Portal." If the orthodontic practice uses any distinct IP, it is not captured.

**Recommended Fix:**
Amend IP Assignment § 1.1 to conform the "Business" definition to the APA:

> **Proposed IP Assignment § 1.1 (revised, first sentence of "Business" definition):** "'Business' means general dentistry, hygiene **and orthodontic** services conducted at the Clinics, as defined in Section 1.1 of the Purchase Agreement."

Additionally, confirm with the client whether the orthodontic practice uses any IP (brands, software, patient materials) not listed on Schedules A, B, or C, and if so, add those items to the appropriate schedule.

**⚑ CLIENT INPUT NEEDED:** The client should identify whether the orthodontic practice has its own brand name, logo, software, or patient-facing materials that are distinct from the general dentistry/hygiene IP listed in the schedules. If yes, those items need to be added to the IP Assignment.

## Issue 5 – Claim Notice Requires "Losses Actually Paid," Eliminating the LOI's Provision for Anticipated Losses

**Documents and Sections:**
- LOI ¶ 7(e): "A claim notice will describe the claim in reasonable detail. **It may be given for losses that have been incurred or are reasonably anticipated, including losses arising from a pending audit or recoupment demand.**"
- APA § 9.4(b): "A Claim Notice shall describe in reasonable detail the facts giving rise to the claim and the provision of this Agreement under which indemnification is sought, and **shall include invoices, remittance records or other documentation of Losses actually paid by the Indemnified Party.** A Claim Notice that does not satisfy this Section 9.4(b) shall not be valid."

**Why It Matters:**
The APA requires that a Claim Notice include documentation of losses *actually paid*. A Claim Notice that fails to meet this requirement "shall not be valid." This means:

1. Saitouth cannot file a protective claim when it receives a payer audit notice or recoupment demand—it must wait until it has actually paid the recoupment.
2. If the survival period expires (6 months under current § 9.1) before Saitouth pays the loss, the claim is time-barred even though the underlying event (the audit notice) occurred well within the survival period.
3. Saitouth must front all funds for pre-closing billing issues with no assurance of timely recovery.

This is particularly acute for Medicaid and DHHS audits, where the process from initial notice to final determination and payment can take 12–24 months.

**Recommended Fix:**
Amend APA § 9.4(b) to restore the LOI's language:

> **Proposed APA § 9.4(b) (revised):** "A Claim Notice shall describe in reasonable detail the facts giving rise to the claim and the provision of this Agreement under which indemnification is sought. For losses that have been incurred, the Claim Notice shall include invoices, remittance records or other documentation of Losses actually paid by the Indemnified Party. **For losses that are reasonably anticipated, including losses arising from a pending audit, investigation or recoupment demand, the Claim Notice shall include a copy of the audit notice, recoupment demand or other communication from the Payer or Governmental Authority, together with a reasonable estimate of the anticipated Losses.** A Claim Notice that does not satisfy this Section 9.4(b) shall not be valid."

Pair this with the extended survival period recommended in Issue 1.

## Issue 6 – APA Omits Material Adverse Change and Due Diligence Satisfaction Conditions from the LOI

**Documents and Sections:**
- LOI ¶ 10 (Conditions to Closing): Includes "(i) completion of Buyer's legal, financial, billing and operational due diligence to Buyer's satisfaction" and "(v) the absence of any material adverse change in the Business."
- APA § 7.1 (Conditions to Obligations of Buyer): Lists only (a) accuracy of Seller's reps; (b) Seller's performance of covenants; (c) receipt of landlord consents; and (d) delivery of closing items. No MAC condition. No due diligence satisfaction condition.

**Why It Matters:**
Because the APA was signed and closed on the same day (March 2, 2026), the practical impact of omitting these conditions is reduced—there was no intervening period during which a MAC could occur or additional due diligence could be completed. However, the omission is still notable for two reasons:

1. If any post-closing dispute arises about whether Saitouth "should have" discovered an issue during due diligence, the absence of a DD-satisfaction condition removes a potential defense for Saitouth (i.e., that it would not have closed had DD revealed the problem).
2. The absence of a MAC condition means that if the transaction had been structured with a signing-to-closing gap, Saitouth would have had no walk-right for deterioration.

Given the same-day close, this is a lower-priority issue, but it should be documented for the file.

**Recommended Fix:**
No amendment is practical at this point (the transaction has closed). If the client is pursuing any post-closing claims, the absence of these conditions does not bar the claims but may be raised by Seller as a defense (i.e., "Buyer had the opportunity to investigate and chose to close"). Be prepared for this argument.

**No client input needed** – this is a note for the file.

## Issue 7 – IP Assignment Dated Seven Months Post-Closing Creates a Documentation Gap

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 3.1: Closing Date is March 2, 2026.
- APA § 2.3(e): Intellectual Property Assets are included in the Purchased Assets transferred at Closing.
- APA § 6.6: "Following the Closing, each Party shall execute and deliver such additional instruments as the other Party reasonably requests to give effect to the transactions contemplated by this Agreement. Without limiting the foregoing, Seller shall execute and deliver the IP Assignment..."
- IP Assignment: Dated October 12, 2026.
- IP Assignment § 2.1: "Assignor hereby irrevocably sells, assigns, transfers, conveys and delivers to Assignee, **and confirms its prior sale, assignment, transfer and conveyance to Assignee effective as of the Closing Date**, all of Assignor's right, title and interest in and to the Assigned IP."

**Why It Matters:**
The IP Assignment is structured as a confirmatory document—it confirms a transfer that was supposed to have occurred at Closing under APA § 2.3(e). However, between March 2, 2026 and October 12, 2026 (seven months), there was no executed assignment instrument in place. If a third party (e.g., a trademark licensee, a software vendor, or a domain registrar) had asserted a claim against the IP during that interval, or if Seller's creditors had made a claim on the IP before the confirmatory assignment was executed, Saitouth's position would have been weakened. The APA's transfer language is sufficient as a matter of contract between the parties, but it is not recordable with the USPTO or domain registrars without a formal assignment instrument.

Additionally, IP Assignment § 4.1 requires delivery of source code and credentials "within ten (10) business days after the date of this Assignment" (i.e., by ~October 26, 2026). If the software was not delivered at Closing (as part of the Purchased Assets under APA § 2.3), Saitouth has been operating for seven months without the source code, build instructions, administrator credentials, and license keys.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Confirm that the software (source code, object code, credentials, license keys) was in fact delivered at Closing as part of the Purchased Assets. If not, demand immediate delivery under APA § 2.3 and § 6.6 (Further Assurances).
2. Ensure that short-form recordable assignments (per IP Assignment Exhibit 1) are filed with the USPTO for the registered trademarks (TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL word mark and design mark) promptly. The registration numbers are left blank in Exhibit 1—obtain them and complete the filings.
3. Confirm that domain name transfers (Schedule B) were completed within the 5-business-day window in IP Assignment § 4.2.

**⚑ CLIENT INPUT NEEDED:** Confirm whether (a) the software source code and credentials were delivered at Closing or only in October 2026; (b) the USPTO recordable assignments have been filed; and (c) the domain name transfers are complete.

## Issue 8 – Cross-Reference Error in Closing Conditions (Landlord Consents)

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 7.1(c): "receipt of the landlord consents listed on **Schedule 4.3**."
- APA § 4.3 (No Conflicts; Consents): "The execution and performance of this Agreement by Seller do not violate Seller's organizational documents, any applicable law or any material contract, **except as set forth on Schedule 4.3**."
- APA § 4.7 (Real Property): "Schedule 4.7 lists each lease for the Clinics."

**Why It Matters:**
Landlord consents to lease assignments are most naturally tracked on the real property schedule (Schedule 4.7) or a dedicated lease consent schedule. Placing them on Schedule 4.3 (which is the "exceptions" schedule for the No Conflicts representation) is unconventional and creates ambiguity: are the landlord consents a condition to closing because their absence would constitute a conflict with a material contract (the lease), or are they a separate operational requirement? If a landlord consent is missing from Schedule 4.3 but the lease itself is listed on Schedule 4.7, it is unclear whether the closing condition in § 7.1(c) has been satisfied.

This is a lower-severity issue because the transaction has already closed, but it could create confusion if a dispute arises about whether all required consents were obtained.

**Recommended Fix:**
No amendment is practical post-closing. For the file, confirm that all four landlord consents (Wilmington, Newark, Middletown, Bear) were in fact received and are documented. If any consent was not obtained, assess whether the lease assignment is effective without it (some leases provide that assignment is effective upon notice even if consent is not granted within a specified period).

**No client input needed** – verify the consents are in the closing file.
