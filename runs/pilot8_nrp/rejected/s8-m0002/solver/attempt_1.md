## Issue 1: Survival Period Reduced from 18 Months to 6 Months

**Documents and Sections:**
- LOI ¶ 6: *"Seller's representations and warranties will survive for 18 months after closing. This includes the healthcare billing and payer compliance representations."*
- APA § 9.1: *"The representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement, including the representations and warranties in Section 4.12, shall survive the Closing until the date that is six (6) months following the Closing Date."*

**Why It Matters:**
This is the single most significant deviation from the LOI. The parties negotiated an 18-month survival period in the LOI, with express confirmation that it applies to the healthcare billing and payer compliance representation (§ 4.12). The APA cuts this to 6 months. The practical effect is severe:

- Section 4.12 covers a **six-year lookback** period for billing and coding compliance. Medicaid and other payer audits routinely take 12–36 months to initiate and resolve. A 6-month survival period means that if a recoupment demand or audit finding surfaces in month 7 or later, Buyer has no rep-based indemnification recourse.
- The 6-year lookback in § 4.12 becomes largely illusory: Seller represented compliance going back to 2020, but Buyer can only enforce that representation through September 2, 2026.
- While Excluded Liabilities under § 9.2(c) are not subject to the survival period or the cap, there will be characterization disputes. A payer recoupment for pre-closing claims is likely an Excluded Liability, but a finding that Seller's § 4.12 rep was false (e.g., Seller stated no overpayments existed when they did) is a rep breach subject to the 6-month window and the $840,000 cap.

**Recommended Fix:**
Restore the 18-month survival period for all Seller representations, and extend the survival period for § 4.12 specifically to the later of (a) 18 months after the Closing Date or (b) the date on which the applicable statute of limitations for the underlying claim expires.

**Proposed Language for APA § 9.1 (first sentence):**

> "The representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement shall survive the Closing until the date that is eighteen (18) months following the Closing Date; provided, that (i) the representations and warranties in Section 4.12 (Healthcare Billing and Payer Compliance) shall survive until the later of (A) eighteen (18) months following the Closing Date or (B) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations for the underlying claim; and (ii) the Fundamental Representations shall survive until sixty (60) days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations."

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** Confirm whether the client is willing to accept 18 months as the floor for the § 4.12 survival period, or whether it wants the full statute of limitations (which for FCA claims can be 6–10 years). The longer the survival, the more leverage we have in negotiating other terms.

## Issue 2: IP Assignment Caps IP-Related Claims at $840,000, Overriding Fundamental Representation Treatment

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1: Defines "Fundamental Representations" to include **Section 4.9 (Intellectual Property)**.
- APA § 9.5(b): *"The Cap shall not apply to Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of the Fundamental Representations, including Section 4.9… Seller's aggregate liability for such Losses, other than in the case of fraud, shall not exceed the Purchase Price."*
- IP Assignment § 7: *"Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement, shall not exceed the Cap."*
- APA § 11.8: *"In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall control, except that the IP Assignment shall control as to the Assigned IP."*

**Why It Matters:**
The APA treats Section 4.9 (IP) as a Fundamental Representation, meaning the $840,000 Cap does not apply and recovery is available up to the full Purchase Price ($8,400,000). However, the IP Assignment—through § 7—expressly caps all IP-related claims (including those arising under § 4.9 of the APA) at "the Cap" ($840,000). Because APA § 11.8 provides that the IP Assignment controls as to the Assigned IP, the $840,000 cap in IP Assignment § 7 overrides the $8,400,000 Fundamental Representation cap in APA § 9.5(b) for any IP-related claim.

This is a 10-fold reduction in maximum recovery for IP issues. If, for example, a third party has a prior claim to the TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL trademark or the patient-recall software, Buyer's maximum recovery would be $840,000 rather than $8,400,000.

**Recommended Fix:**
Amend IP Assignment § 7 to carve out Fundamental Representations from the Cap, consistent with APA § 9.5(b).

**Proposed Language for IP Assignment § 7:**

> "Assignor's aggregate liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of its representations and warranties with respect to the Assigned IP, whether under this Assignment or the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement, shall not exceed the Cap; provided, that the Cap shall not apply to Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement (which is a Fundamental Representation under the Purchase Agreement) or to fraud. Assignor's aggregate liability for such Losses, other than in the case of fraud, shall not exceed the Purchase Price. Claims under this Section 7 shall be made in accordance with the procedures in Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement."

## Issue 3: IP Assignment Definition of "Business" Omits Orthodontic Services

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1: *"'Business' means general dentistry, hygiene and **orthodontic** services conducted at the Clinics."*
- IP Assignment § 1.1: *"'Business' means general dentistry and hygiene services conducted at the Clinics."*
- IP Assignment § 1.1 (Assigned IP): *"'Assigned IP' means all intellectual property owned by Assignor and used primarily in the Business…"*
- LOI ¶ 1: *"Buyer's proposal covers the Business as a whole, including the orthodontic practice and its related brands, equipment and patient-recall systems."*
- LOI ¶ 2(a): References "all intellectual property used in the Business" including "the TROLIND FAMILY DENTAL and related marks, domain names, social media accounts, and Seller's patient-recall and appointment-reminder software."
- Press Release: Confirms the clinics provide "general dentistry, hygiene and **orthodontic** services."

**Why It Matters:**
Because "Assigned IP" is defined by reference to IP "used primarily in the Business," and the IP Assignment's "Business" definition excludes orthodontics, any IP used primarily in the orthodontic practice falls outside the assignment. This could include:

- Orthodontic-specific software modules (Schedule C lists "hygiene recall scheduling modules" but does not explicitly list orthodontic treatment planning or progress-tracking software);
- Orthodontic-specific patient education materials or marketing content;
- Any separate orthodontic brand or trade name;
- Orthodontic patient-recall lists or databases.

The LOI expressly contemplated that the acquisition includes "the orthodontic practice and its related brands, equipment and patient-recall systems." The IP Assignment as drafted fails to deliver on that commitment.

**Recommended Fix:**
Conform the IP Assignment's "Business" definition to the APA.

**Proposed Language for IP Assignment § 1.1 (second paragraph):**

> "'Business' means general dentistry, hygiene and orthodontic services conducted at the Clinics, as defined in Section 1.1 of the Purchase Agreement."

Additionally, confirm that Schedule C captures all orthodontic-specific software, and that Schedule A captures any orthodontic-specific marks.

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** Please confirm with the client whether there are any orthodontic-specific brands, software, or patient databases beyond what is listed in Schedules A, B, and C of the IP Assignment. If so, those items need to be added.

## Issue 4: Seller Note Set-Off Notice Deadline of 150 Days Severely Limits Security

**Documents and Sections:**
- Seller Note § 5.2: *"Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 only if Maker delivers to Holder a written notice (a 'Set-off Notice') within one hundred fifty (150) days after the Issue Date."*
- Issue Date: March 2, 2026 → Set-off deadline: approximately **July 30, 2026**.
- APA § 9.1: Survival period for Seller's reps ends **September 2, 2026** (6 months).
- LOI ¶ 4(b): *"Buyer will have the right to set off amounts owed to it for indemnifiable losses against payments due under the Seller Note, on terms to be set out in the Definitive Agreement and the Seller Note."* (No deadline specified.)

**Why It Matters:**
The 150-day deadline means Saitouth must identify and assert all set-off claims by approximately July 30, 2026—less than five months after closing. This creates several problems:

1. **Gap with survival period:** Claims arising between July 30 and September 2 (the end of the 6-month survival period) cannot be set off against the note. They can still be pursued as indemnification claims, but without the security of the note.
2. **Gap with Excluded Liabilities:** Payer recoupments and audits for pre-closing services (Excluded Liabilities under APA § 9.2(c)) often surface 12+ months after closing. The 150-day deadline means these cannot be set off against the note, even though they are uncapped and not subject to the survival period.
3. **Inconsistent with LOI:** The LOI contemplated set-off "on terms to be set out in the Definitive Agreement" without specifying a short deadline. The 150-day limit was introduced in the definitive documents without apparent client approval.
4. **Practical impossibility:** It is unrealistic to identify all potential healthcare billing issues within 150 days of closing, particularly when the § 4.12 lookback extends six years.

**Recommended Fix:**
Extend the set-off notice deadline to align with the survival period (or longer for Excluded Liabilities), and permit set-off for Excluded Liabilities without a fixed deadline.

**Proposed Language for Seller Note § 5.2:**

> "Maker may exercise its right under Section 5.1 only if Maker delivers to Holder a written notice (a 'Set-off Notice') within the applicable survival period for the related claim under Section 9.1 of the Purchase Agreement; provided, that with respect to Losses arising from any Excluded Liability, Maker may deliver a Set-off Notice at any time before the Maturity Date. Each Set-off Notice shall state the amount to be set off, identify the installment or installments against which the set-off will be applied, and attach or refer to the Claim Notice delivered under Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement in respect of the related Losses."

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** The client should confirm whether it is comfortable with a set-off window tied to the (restored) 18-month survival period, or whether it wants the ability to set off Excluded Liabilities at any time before maturity. The latter is more protective but may be harder to negotiate.

## Issue 5: No Escrow or Holdback to Secure Indemnification Obligations

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 2.2: Purchase Price paid as $6,600,000 cash at closing + $1,800,000 Seller Note. No escrow or holdback.
- LOI ¶ 4: Same structure. No escrow contemplated.

**Why It Matters:**
The $1,800,000 Seller Note is the only security for Seller's indemnification obligations. However, as noted in Issue 4, the 150-day set-off deadline limits its practical utility. Additionally:

- The Note matures in three years (March 2, 2029) and is being paid down quarterly ($150,000 per quarter). By the time a payer audit surfaces (often 12–24 months post-closing), a significant portion of the Note will have been paid down, reducing the available security.
- The Note is subordinated to Senior Indebtedness (Seller Note § 6). If Saitouth incurs senior debt, the Note's payments are subordinated, but more importantly, if Saitouth is in default on senior debt, the Note's value as security for indemnification claims is further diminished.
- There is no general security interest in the Purchased Assets securing the Note. If Saitouth seeks to set off a large Excluded Liability (e.g., a $2M Medicaid recoupment) but the Note balance has been paid down to $500,000, the set-off is limited to the remaining balance.

**Recommended Fix:**
Establish an escrow account funded from the cash portion of the Purchase Price (e.g., $500,000–$1,000,000) to be held for the duration of the survival period (or longer for § 4.12). Release from escrow would be conditioned on the expiration of the applicable survival period without unresolved claims.

**Proposed Language (new APA § 2.2(c)):**

> "(c) Escrow. From the cash portion of the Purchase Price otherwise payable under Section 2.2(a), the sum of $[●] shall be deposited into an escrow account (the 'Escrow Account') with [●] as escrow agent, to be held and disbursed in accordance with the terms of an escrow agreement in the form attached as Exhibit [●] (the 'Escrow Agreement'). The Escrow Account shall secure Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX and shall be released in accordance with the Escrow Agreement."

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** The client needs to decide (a) whether to pursue an escrow, given that the LOI did not contemplate one and Seller may resist; (b) the appropriate escrow amount; and (c) whether the escrow should be funded from the cash portion (reducing Seller's day-one cash) or from the Note (reducing the Note principal). This is a commercial decision that will affect the negotiation dynamic.

## Issue 6: Non-Compete Binds Only the Seller Entity, Not Individual Practitioners

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 6.2: *"For five years after the Closing Date, Seller shall not, directly or indirectly, own, operate or provide dental services through any dental practice located within ten miles of any Clinic, or solicit any Transferred Employee or any patient of the Clinics."*
- LOI ¶ 9: Same scope.
- APA signature block: Seller is "Trolind Family Dental, P.A." signed by its "President."

**Why It Matters:**
The non-compete binds only Trolind Family Dental, P.A.—a professional association. After the asset sale, it is common for the P.A. to dissolve or become a shell entity with no active operations. The individual dentists and practitioners who are the true source of competition (and patient relationships) are not personally bound by the covenant. If the selling dentist(s) form a new P.A. or PLLC and open a practice within 10 miles of a Clinic, Saitouth would need to argue that the new entity is the same as or controlled by the old P.A.—a fact-intensive and uncertain analysis.

Additionally, the non-solicitation of "any patient of the Clinics" is broad and valuable, but it is only as enforceable as the entity's continued existence and the court's willingness to attribute the individual practitioners' actions to the P.A.

**Recommended Fix:**
Add the individual practitioners (or at minimum the President/principal dentist) as parties to the non-compete, or add a separate restrictive covenant agreement with the individuals.

**Proposed Language (new APA § 6.2, second sentence):**

> "Each individual who is a shareholder, member, or officer of Seller as of the Closing Date (each, a 'Restricted Individual') shall be bound by the restrictions in this Section 6.2 as if set forth in a separate agreement executed by such individual, and Seller shall cause each Restricted Individual to execute and deliver a restrictive covenant agreement in the form attached as Exhibit [●] (each, a 'Restricted Individual Agreement') as a condition to the Closing."

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** The client should identify which individual practitioners are the key competitive threats (e.g., the principal dentist, any orthodontist). We will need their names and entity relationships to draft the Restricted Individual Agreements. Also confirm whether the client wants a 5-year non-compete from individuals (which some courts may find overbroad) or whether a shorter period (e.g., 3 years) with the same geographic scope would be acceptable.

## Issue 7: IP Assignment Uses "Used Primarily in the Business" Standard (Narrower Than APA)

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1 (Intellectual Property Assets): *"...all trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos, domain names, social media accounts, copyrights, software (including Seller's patient-recall and appointment-reminder software), databases and other intellectual property owned by Seller and **used in the Business**…"*
- IP Assignment § 1.1 (Assigned IP): *"'Assigned IP' means all intellectual property owned by Assignor and **used primarily in the Business**…"*

**Why It Matters:**
The addition of "primarily" in the IP Assignment introduces a material qualifier not present in the APA. IP that is used in the Business but also used in other contexts (e.g., software used both for the dental practice and for Seller's administrative functions, or a domain name that hosts both practice content and personal content of the owner) could be argued to not be "used primarily" in the Business. This creates an unnecessary gap between what the APA says is being purchased and what the IP Assignment actually transfers.

**Recommended Fix:**
Delete "primarily" from the IP Assignment to conform to the APA.

**Proposed Language for IP Assignment § 1.1 (Assigned IP, opening):**

> "'Assigned IP' means all intellectual property owned by Assignor and used in the Business, including:…"

## Issue 8: IP Assignment Lacks Explicit Survival Period for Its Own Representations

**Documents and Sections:**
- IP Assignment § 6.1: Contains Assignor's representations (authority, bring-down of APA § 4.9, no outstanding licenses).
- IP Assignment § 7: References "the procedures in Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement" for claims but does not specify a survival period.
- APA § 9.1: Survival periods are stated for "representations and warranties of Seller contained in this Agreement" (i.e., the APA). It is unclear whether the IP Assignment's own representations are covered.

**Why It Matters:**
If a dispute arises over whether the IP Assignment's § 6.1 representations survive (and for how long), the absence of an explicit survival period creates uncertainty. Seller could argue that the IP Assignment's reps have no survival period (and thus expired at closing) or that they are governed by the APA's 6-month period. Either interpretation is less protective than the Fundamental Representation survival period (60 days after statute of limitations) that should apply to § 4.9.

**Recommended Fix:**
Add an explicit survival provision to the IP Assignment.

**Proposed Language (new IP Assignment § 7, final sentence):**

> "The representations and warranties in Section 6.1 shall survive in accordance with Section 9.1 of the Purchase Agreement, and the representations in Section 6.1(b) (relating to Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement) shall survive as Fundamental Representations."

## Issue 9: Press Release Publicly Confirms Payer Transition Is Incomplete

**Documents and Sections:**
- Press Release § 3: *"Saitouth is working with Medicaid, the Delaware Children's Health Insurance Program and private dental benefits plans to transition payer enrollment for the clinics."*
- APA § 6.5: *"Following the Closing, Seller shall cooperate with Buyer in Buyer's enrollment with each Payer…"*
- APA § 4.8: Seller represented that all Payer participation agreements are "valid and binding."

**Why It Matters:**
The press release, issued the day after closing, publicly states that payer enrollment has not been completed. This creates several risks:

1. **Operational risk:** If there is a gap in payer coverage (even a short one), patients may be unable to use their insurance, leading to lost revenue, patient dissatisfaction, and potential patient attrition.
2. **Evidentiary risk:** In any subsequent dispute about the condition of the Business at closing or Seller's cooperation under § 6.5, the press release is a public, dated admission that the transition was incomplete. Seller could argue that the incomplete transition was a known, accepted condition at closing.
3. **Reputational risk:** If the payer transition takes longer than expected, the public statement creates an expectation of prompt resolution that may be difficult to meet.

**Recommended Fix:**
This is not a document-drafting fix but a practical matter. We should:
- Confirm with the client the current status of payer enrollment for each of the four clinics and each major Payer (Medicaid, CHIP, and the major private plans).
- If enrollment gaps exist, assess whether Seller is in breach of § 6.5 (cooperation covenant) and whether the press release statement limits Buyer's ability to claim that the gap is Seller's responsibility.
- Consider whether a follow-up communication or patient notice is warranted to manage expectations.

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** Please provide the current status of payer enrollment for each clinic and Payer. We need to know whether any gaps exist, how long they are expected to last, and whether Seller has been responsive under § 6.5.

## Issue 11: Section 4.12(d) Knowledge Qualifier on Overpayments

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 4.12(d): *"no credit balances or **known** overpayments are owed by Seller to any Payer."*

**Why It Matters:**
The word "known" limits this representation to overpayments that Seller was actually aware of. If Seller's billing system generated a credit balance or overpayment that no one at Seller noticed, the representation is not breached even though the overpayment exists. This is less protective than an absolute representation ("no credit balances or overpayments are owed by Seller to any Payer").

In practice, this may not be a major issue because overpayments that surface later would likely be characterized as Excluded Liabilities (uncapped, no survival limit) rather than rep breaches. But the knowledge qualifier weakens the rep-based protection and could be used by Seller to argue that a discovered overpayment was not "known" at closing.

**Recommended Fix:**
Delete "known" from § 4.12(d).

**Proposed Language for APA § 4.12(d):**

> "(d) no credit balances or overpayments are owed by Seller to any Payer as of the Closing Date."

## Issue 12: IP Assignment Dated Seven Months After Closing Creates Confirmation Gap

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 3.1: Closing Date is March 2, 2026.
- APA § 2.3(e): At Closing, Seller transfers "the Intellectual Property Assets."
- IP Assignment: Dated October 12, 2026.
- IP Assignment § 2.1: *"Assignor hereby irrevocably sells, assigns, transfers, conveys and delivers to Assignee, and **confirms its prior sale, assignment, transfer and conveyance to Assignee effective as of the Closing Date**…"*

**Why It Matters:**
The IP Assignment is confirmatory, and it states that the transfer was effective as of the Closing Date. However, for seven months (March 2 through October 12, 2026), there was no executed, recordable assignment document in place. During that gap:

- The IP was not recorded with the USPTO or domain registrars;
- A third party could have queried the chain of title and found no record of transfer;
- If Seller entered bankruptcy or had other creditors during that period, the unrecorded assignment could be challenged;
- If Seller granted a license to a third party during the gap (arguing it still "owned" the IP pending formal assignment), Buyer would have a difficult position.

While the APA § 2.3(e) transfer at closing should be effective between the parties, the absence of a recordable instrument for seven months is a avoidable risk.

**Recommended Fix:**
This is now a historical fact (the IP Assignment has been executed). Going forward:
- Confirm that recordation with the USPTO and domain registrars has been completed (per IP Assignment §§ 4.2 and 4.3).
- Confirm that source code and administrator credentials for the Assigned Software have been delivered (per IP Assignment § 4.1, due within 10 business days of October 12, 2026, i.e., by approximately October 26, 2026).
- If any of these items have not been completed, send a notice of default under IP Assignment § 5.1 and invoke the power of attorney in § 5.2.

**[CLIENT INPUT NEEDED]:** Please confirm whether (a) the USPTO recordation has been filed, (b) the domain name transfers are complete, and (c) the software source code and credentials have been delivered. If any are outstanding, we should send a demand letter promptly.

## Additional Observations (Lower Priority)

**A. Basket Structure (True Deductible).** APA § 9.5(d) provides a true deductible (Buyer recovers only amounts *in excess of* $84,000), not a tipping threshold (where once $84,000 is crossed, Seller is liable for all losses from the first dollar). The LOI uses the same "only for the excess" language, so this is consistent with the negotiated deal. However, if the client wants more protection, we could propose changing to a tipping basket. This is a lower-priority item given the other issues.

**B. Seller Note Subordination.** The subordination in Seller Note § 6 is standard and protective of Saitouth's senior lenders. No action needed unless the client's financing structure changes.

**C. APA § 10.1(b) Outside Date.** The termination outside date is March 31, 2026—only 29 days after the Closing Date. Since the deal has already closed, this is moot.

**D. Press Release Forward-Looking Statements.** The disclaimer in § 7 of the press release is standard. No action needed.
