## URGENT ITEMS

### 1. Escrow Claim Notice Missed the Initial Release Date – $1.3M Claim May Be Unsecured

**Location:** Escrow Agreement §4.2; Side Letter §5(a)–(c)

The Initial Release Date under Escrow Agreement §4.2 is 180 days after the Closing Date of April 15, 2026, which is **October 12, 2026**. Buyer's Claim Notice for $1,300,000 (Saint Odile overbilling) was delivered on **October 19, 2026** – seven days *after* the Initial Release Date. Section 4.2 provides that the Escrow Agent releases the Initial Release Amount ($2,425,000) "less the aggregate Disputed Amount under all Claim Notices delivered **before** the Initial Release Date." Because the Claim Notice postdates the Initial Release Date, the $1,300,000 does not reduce the Initial Release Amount. The Escrow Agent was obligated to release the full $2,425,000 to Seller on October 12 without further instruction from either party.

The Side Letter (§5(b)–(c)) confirms the dispute: Seller has demanded joint written instructions for release of the Initial Release Amount "without reduction," and Buyer has refused. However, under the plain terms of §4.2, the Escrow Agent was not required to obtain joint instructions for the Initial Release – it was to release automatically. The Escrow Agent may be sitting on the funds under §6.5 (Conflicting Demands), but if it releases per §4.2, Buyer's $1.3M claim is secured only by the remaining $2,425,000 (the balance subject to the Final Release on April 15, 2027).

**Why it matters:** Buyer's primary security for the overbilling claim is being eroded. If the Initial Release goes out in full, Buyer's recovery depends on the remaining escrow balance and Seller's general creditworthiness. The claim is for a breach of §§4.7 and 4.9 (undisclosed liabilities / material contracts), which are subject to the $4.85M cap and $485K deductible under SPA §9.5(a)–(b).

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately confirm with Fernhill Trust whether the Initial Release has been made. If it has, assess whether the release was proper under §4.2 or whether the Escrow Agent erred by waiting.
- If the release has not yet been made, consider whether Buyer should acquiesce to the release of the Initial Release Amount in exchange for Seller's agreement to a specific resolution timeline for the $1.3M claim, or whether Buyer should seek a Final Order under §4.1(b) to compel the correct treatment.
- Preserve all rights under the Side Letter §5(d) reservation.

### 2. Uncapped Buyer Indemnity for Retained Credit Support (Side Letter §3(b))

**Location:** Side Letter §3(b)–(c); SPA §9.3(b), §9.5(c)

The Side Letter requires Buyer to indemnify Seller against **all** Losses arising from the Retained Credit Support – the Yonkers lease guaranty (base rent ~$1.15M/year, lease runs through **June 30, 2034**) and the $3,000,000 Saint Odile performance bond. Critically, this indemnity is "deemed an obligation of Buyer under Section 9.3(b)" of the SPA. Under SPA §9.5(c), the Cap ($4.85M) and Deductible ($485K) apply **only** to claims under §§9.2(a) and 9.3(a). Therefore, Buyer's indemnity under Side Letter §3(b) is **uncapped and has no deductible**.

**Why it matters:** In the worst case, Buyer is exposed to the full remaining term of the Yonkers lease (nearly 8 years × ~$1.15M base rent + additional rent, taxes, and operating expenses) plus the $3M bond, with no limit. This is a contingent liability that could exceed $12M. The release deadline has been extended to April 30, 2027 (Side Letter §3(a)), and as of the Side Letter date (October 26, 2026), neither release has been obtained. The landlord is still reviewing Buyer's financials, and Saint Odile has not consented to the bond substitution.

**Recommendation:**
- Treat obtaining the two releases as the top operational priority. Assign specific owners and track weekly.
- For the Yonkers lease: engage the landlord directly to expedite the substitute guaranty review. If Buyer's credit profile is the issue, explore providing a letter of credit or cash collateral as an interim measure.
- For the Saint Odile bond: follow up with Saint Odile on the consent to substitution. If consent is being withheld, determine whether there is a contractual right to compel it or whether a new bond can be posted directly by Buyer (bypassing the need for Saint Odile's consent to release Seller).
- Consider negotiating with Seller to (a) cap the indemnity, (b) make it subject to the Deductible, or (c) limit it to the period through the Extended Release Date only (i.e., delete "whether the claim arises before or after the Extended Release Date").
- If the releases are not obtained by April 30, 2027, assess whether Buyer is in breach of the "commercially reasonable efforts" standard and what the consequences would be.

### 3. Tax Indemnity Survival Period Materially Shortened from Prior Draft

**Location:** SPA §9.1(c) (final) vs. Prior Draft §9.1(c); Disclosure Schedule 4.16, Item 1

The final SPA §9.1(c) provides that Seller's obligation under §9.2(c) (Pre-Closing Taxes) survives "until the end of the survival period applicable to the representations in Section 4.16." Section 4.16 is not a Fundamental Representation, so its survival period is **12 months after the Closing Date** (SPA §9.1(a)) = **April 15, 2027**.

The prior draft (February 18, 2026) §9.1(c) provided that the tax indemnity survives "until **60 days after expiration of the statute of limitations** applicable to the assessment of the underlying Tax, giving effect to any waiver or extension." For New York sales and use tax, the statute of limitations is generally three years from the due date of the return. For the periods at issue (March 2023 – November 2024), the statute would not expire until at least 2027–2028, meaning the prior draft would have provided survival well beyond April 2027.

The Disclosure Schedule (Schedule 4.16, Item 1) discloses a **pending** NY State sales and use tax field audit (Case No. X-418827364) covering March 1, 2023 through November 30, 2024. No assessment has been issued as of the Disclosure Schedule date. If the audit results in an assessment after April 15, 2027, Buyer's indemnity claim against Seller will have expired.

**Why it matters:** The Company's revenue is $41.6M, and the audit covers the taxability of linen rental charges and equipment/chemical purchases – potentially a multi-million-dollar exposure. Buyer has paid $48.5M for the Company and may be left holding the bag on a pre-Closing tax issue that was already identified in diligence.

**Recommendation:**
- This is a post-closing issue, so the SPA cannot be amended retroactively without Seller's consent. However, Buyer should:
  - Immediately notify Seller in writing that the NY audit is a Pre-Closing Tax matter under §9.2(c) and that Buyer expects Seller to bear any resulting assessment, interest, and penalties.
  - Request that Seller confirm in writing its continued obligation under §9.2(c) with respect to the audit, or negotiate a side letter extending the survival period for this specific audit to 60 days after the statute of limitations expires (matching the prior draft language).
  - If Seller refuses, assess the likely tax exposure and reserve accordingly. The audit has been pending since at least January 2026 (information document request issued January 12, 2026; response February 9, 2026), so an assessment could come at any time.
  - Monitor the audit timeline closely. If an assessment is issued before April 15, 2027, assert the claim immediately under §9.4(b) to preserve it under §9.1(d).

## SIGNIFICANT ITEMS

### 4. Key Employee Agreement "Business" Definition Is Much Narrower Than the SPA – Non-Compete Gap

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement §1 ("Business" definition) vs. SPA §1.1 ("Business" definition)

The SPA defines "Business" as "commercial laundry, linen rental and textile processing services for **healthcare, hospitality and industrial** customers."

The Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" as "commercial laundry and linen services provided to **hotels, resorts and restaurants**, including linen rental, laundering, pressing and the delivery and collection of linens and related textile products."

The KEA definition excludes healthcare and industrial customers entirely. Per the Disclosure Schedule (Schedule 4.10), healthcare represents **61.9%** of Company revenue ($25.78M) and industrial represents **14.4%** ($5.98M). The non-compete in KEA §7.1 restricts Szabo-Whitlock only from competing in the hospitality segment.

**Why it matters:** Renata Szabo-Whitlock is the Company's President and has "substantial knowledge of the Company's operations, customers, pricing, employees and confidential information" (KEA Recital B). If she leaves, she could immediately compete for the Company's healthcare and industrial customers – the vast majority of its revenue – without breaching the KEA. The SPA's Seller non-compete (§6.5) covers all three segments, but that binds Seller, not the key employee.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend the KEA (by written instrument signed by Szabo-Whitlock and an authorized officer of the Company, per KEA §9.4) to conform the "Business" definition to the SPA definition. This should be done promptly while the employment relationship is stable.
- If Szabo-Whitlock resists, consider whether the confidentiality provisions (KEA §6) and the customer non-solicitation (KEA §7.2, which uses the same narrow "Business" definition) provide any residual protection. They likely do not, for the same reason.
- Document the rationale for the narrower definition (if any) for the file.

As of the Side Letter date (October 26, 2026), there is no indication in the folder that Buyer delivered the calculation.

**Why it matters:** If Buyer failed to deliver the calculation within 90 days, it may have waived its right to a working capital adjustment under §2.4. The Target Working Capital is $3,200,000. If actual Closing Working Capital was below target, Buyer is owed a reduction in the Purchase Price that it may no longer be able to claim.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm immediately whether Buyer delivered the §2.4(b) calculation by July 14, 2026.
- If it was not delivered, assess whether Seller has affirmed the right to adjust (e.g., by discussing it) or whether the failure is excusable. Under New York law, waiver requires intent, but the contractual deadline is clear.
- If the calculation was delivered, confirm Seller's response timeline (30 days to object) and whether any dispute has arisen.

### 9. Workers' Compensation Self-Insured Deductible

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule 4.7, Item 2

The Company has a $250,000 per-occurrence deductible on its workers' compensation program. As of February 15, 2026, there were six open claims with aggregate reserves of $214,600.

**Why it matters:** The reserves ($214,600) are below the per-occurrence deductible, suggesting the Company is self-insuring these claims. If any single claim exceeds $250,000, the Company absorbs the excess up to the deductible. With six open claims in a labor-intensive business (laundry plants), the aggregate exposure could be material. This was disclosed, so it is not a breach, but it is a known cost item.

**Recommendation:** Obtain an updated workers' comp loss run from the broker/insurer. Model the expected cost of the six open claims and any new claims through the first policy renewal. Consider whether the deductible level is competitive.

### 10. Escrow Tax Treatment – Buyer Pays Tax on Escrow Earnings

**Location:** Escrow Agreement §7.2

"Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds, and all earnings on them are reported as income of Buyer, until released."

**Why it matters:** Buyer is currently paying income tax on earnings generated by the Escrow Funds (up to $4.85M, or $2.425M if the Initial Release has been made), even though those funds are security for Seller's obligations and may ultimately be released to Seller. This is a timing mismatch – Buyer bears the tax cost now but may never receive the underlying funds.

**Recommendation:** Quantify the tax cost of escrow earnings to date. If the amounts are significant, consider whether the Purchase Price or the escrow terms should be adjusted (though this would require Seller's agreement). At minimum, ensure the tax is properly reflected in Buyer's financial modeling for the investment.

**Location:** Side Letter §4

Until each item of Retained Credit Support is released, Buyer must give Seller, within five Business Days after receipt, copies of: (a) any written notice of default under the Yonkers lease or Saint Odile agreement, and (b) any written demand or claim under the guaranty or bond.

**Recommendation:** Implement an internal protocol so that any notice of default or demand received by the Company is flagged to legal within 24 hours to ensure the five-Business-Day deadline is met. Designate a single owner for this obligation.
