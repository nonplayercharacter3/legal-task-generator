## URGENT ITEMS

### 1. Escrow Security Compromised by Claim Notice Timing

**Location:** Escrow Agreement § 4.2; Side Letter § 5(a)–(c)

The Initial Release Date under the Escrow Agreement is 180 days after the Closing Date of April 15, 2026, which is **October 12, 2026**. Section 4.2 provides that on that date the Escrow Agent releases the Initial Release Amount ($2,425,000) "less the aggregate Disputed Amount under all Claim Notices delivered **before** the Initial Release Date."

Buyer's Claim Notice for $1,300,000 (Saint Odile overbilling) was delivered **October 19, 2026** — seven days *after* the Initial Release Date. Under the plain language of § 4.2, the Claim Notice does not reduce the Initial Release Amount. Seller is now demanding Joint Written Instructions to release the Initial Release Amount without reduction (Side Letter § 5(b)), and Buyer has refused (Side Letter § 5(c)).

**Why it matters:** If the Escrow Agent releases (or has already released) the full $2,425,000 Initial Release Amount to Seller, Buyer's security for the $1,300,000 claim is reduced to the remaining $2,425,000 (plus earnings). More critically, the Final Release Date is **April 15, 2027** (12 months post-closing). If the claim is not resolved by then, § 4.3 releases all remaining Escrow Funds less the Disputed Amount — meaning only $1,300,000 would be withheld and $1,125,000 would go to Seller. After that, Buyer's recovery depends entirely on Seller's creditworthiness, with no escrow backstop.

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately confirm with Fernhill Trust whether the Initial Release Amount was in fact wired to Seller on or about October 12, 2026. If it was, assess whether a constructive-trust or unjust-enrichment argument is available under Delaware law (governing the Escrow Agreement, § 8.2) to claw back the portion attributable to the known claim.
- If the Escrow Agent has not yet released the Initial Amount (i.e., it is holding under § 6.5 due to conflicting demands), argue that the Claim Notice, though delivered after the Initial Release Date, was based on facts known to Buyer before that date, and that equitable principles should prevent Seller from receiving the full Initial Release.
- In either scenario, prepare to seek a Final Order (injunction) in the Delaware Court of Chancery to enjoin release of at least $1,300,000 from the Escrow Funds pending resolution of the claim.
- Accelerate resolution of the underlying overbilling dispute. Consider whether a partial settlement or stipulated accounting could be reached before April 15, 2027.

### 2. Uncapped, Open-Ended Indemnity for Retained Credit Support

**Location:** Side Letter § 3(b); SPA § 9.3(b), § 9.5(c); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 6.6

Side Letter § 3(b) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller against "all Losses arising out of or resulting from the Retained Credit Support," including any amount paid under the Yonkers lease guaranty or the Saint Odile performance bond, "whether the claim arises before or after the Extended Release Date" (April 30, 2027). The indemnity is expressly deemed an obligation under SPA § 9.3(b) (breach of covenant).

SPA § 9.5(c) provides that the Cap ($4,850,000) and Deductible ($485,000) apply **only** to claims under § 9.2(a) and § 9.3(a) — i.e., representation and warranty claims. The Side Letter indemnity, as a § 9.3(b) obligation, is **uncapped and has no deductible**.

The Yonkers Lease runs through **June 30, 2034** (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.12). Base rent alone is approximately $1,150,000 per year, plus additional rent, taxes, and operating expenses. Over the remaining ~8 years, Seller's guaranty exposure exceeds $9 million in base rent before add-ons. The Saint Odile performance bond is $3,000,000 and renews annually (Schedule 6.6, Item 2).

**Why it matters:** Giraisk has assumed unlimited financial exposure for Seller's credit support obligations that extend well beyond the Extended Release Date. If the Company defaults on the Yonkers lease in 2029, the landlord can call the guaranty, and Giraisk must indemnify Seller for the full amount — with no cap, no deductible, and no time limit. The Side Letter also grants Seller information rights (Side Letter § 4) requiring Buyer to forward any notice of default or demand under those agreements within five Business Days, giving Seller early warning and leverage.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a cap on the Side Letter indemnity, or at minimum limit it to the Extended Release Date (April 30, 2027). The current language explicitly covers claims arising *after* that date, which is unreasonable given that Buyer's obligation is to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain release by that date, not to guarantee the outcome indefinitely.
- If Seller insists on post-release coverage, require that it be limited to claims arising from events occurring before the Extended Release Date, or that it be subject to a cap equal to the Escrow Amount or some other defined maximum.
- Accelerate the release process. The landlord is "reviewing Buyer's financial information" (Side Letter § 2(a)) — push for a definitive timeline. For the Saint Odile bond, obtain consent to substitution from Saint Odile Health System as a condition of any further extension.
- Model the worst-case exposure (full lease guaranty through 2034 + bond) and confirm with the client that it is comfortable with the risk or that additional protection (e.g., a parent guaranty from a stronger affiliate, or a dedicated reserve) is needed.

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 3. Tax Indemnity Survival Period Dramatically Shortened (Draft-to-Executed Change)

**Location:** SPA § 9.1(c) (executed) vs. Prior Draft § 9.1(c) (February 18, 2026)

The prior draft provided: *"The obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(c) survive until 60 days after expiration of the statute of limitations applicable to the assessment of the underlying Tax, giving effect to any waiver or extension."*

The executed SPA provides: *"The obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(c) survive until the end of the survival period applicable to the representations in Section 4.16."*

Section 4.16 (Taxes) is a non-fundamental representation, so under § 9.1(a) it survives only **12 months after the Closing Date** — i.e., until **April 15, 2027**.

**Why it matters:** The Disclosure Schedule (Schedule 4.16, Item 1) discloses a pending New York State sales and use tax field audit covering periods March 1, 2023 through November 30, 2024. The audit was still open as of the Disclosure Schedule (information document request issued January 12, 2026; Company responded February 9, 2026; no assessment yet). NYS sales tax assessments can be made within the applicable statute of limitations (generally 3 years from filing, or 6 years for certain understatements). If the audit results in an assessment after April 15, 2027, the tax indemnity in § 9.2(c) will have expired, and Giraisk will bear the full cost with no recourse against Seller.

This appears to be a negotiated concession that was traded for something else, or an error. Either way, it is a material adverse change from the buyer's draft.

**Recommendation:**
- Determine the statute of limitations for the audited periods (March 2023 – November 2024). If returns were filed by the applicable deadlines, the NYS assessment period likely runs to 2027 or 2028.
- If the audit is not resolved before April 15, 2027, consider whether the pending claim can be asserted as a direct claim under § 9.1(d) ("A claim asserted in writing with reasonable specificity before expiration of the applicable survival period survives until finally resolved"). File a protective claim notice before April 15, 2027, identifying the NYS audit as a potential Pre-Closing Tax exposure under § 9.2(c).
- Going forward, in any amendment or side letter, restore the tax indemnity survival to the statute-of-limitations formulation.

### 4. Key Employee Agreement "Business" Definition Excludes Healthcare and Industrial Segments

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement § 1 (definition of "Business"); compare SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Business")

The Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" as: *"commercial laundry and linen services provided to **hotels, resorts and restaurants**, including linen rental, laundering, pressing and the delivery and collection of linens and related textile products."*

The SPA defines "Business" as: *"commercial laundry, linen rental and textile processing services for **healthcare, hospitality and industrial** customers."*

The non-compete (§ 7.1), customer non-solicit (§ 7.2), and employee non-solicit (§ 7.3) in the Key Employee Agreement all key off the "Business" definition.

**Why it matters:** Per Schedule 4.10, healthcare customers account for **61.9%** of Company revenue ($25,780,000 of $41,620,000) and industrial customers for **14.4%** ($5,980,000). The Key Employee Agreement's restrictive covenants cover only the hospitality segment (23.7% of revenue). If Renata Szabo-Whitlock departs and joins a competitor serving hospitals, medical centers, or industrial clients — the Company's primary revenue sources — she would not be in breach of § 7.1 or § 7.2.

This is likely a drafting error. The SPA's broader definition was presumably the intended scope, and the Key Employee Agreement should conform.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend the Key Employee Agreement (or execute a corrective side agreement) to replace the "Business" definition with the SPA's definition, or to add "healthcare and industrial customers" to the existing definition.
- If Szabo-Whitlock has not yet been asked to re-sign, do so promptly. The agreement is still within its Initial Term (through April 15, 2029), and § 9.4 permits amendment by written instrument signed by Executive and an authorized officer of the Company (other than Executive).
- In the interim, note that the SPA's own non-compete against Seller (§ 6.5) uses the broader "Business" definition and covers all three segments, so Seller-side protection is intact. The gap is only on the employee side.

### 5. Pending NYS Sales and Use Tax Audit — Limited Protection

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.16, Item 1; SPA § 9.2(c), § 9.1(c)

As noted in Issue 3, the NYS audit is pending and the tax indemnity survival is only 12 months. Additionally, § 9.2(c) indemnifies only for Pre-Closing Taxes "to the extent not reflected as a liability in Closing Working Capital as finally determined." If the working capital calculation (SPA § 2.4) already includes a reserve for this audit, the indemnity may be unavailable.

**Why it matters:** The audit covers the taxability of linen rental charges and certain equipment and chemical purchases. If NYS determines that linen rental charges are subject to sales tax (or that certain purchases were not properly exempted), the exposure could be significant given the Company's $41.6 million revenue base. The buyer has no clear path to recovery if the assessment arrives after April 15, 2027 and the working capital calculation did not reserve for it.

**Recommendation:**
- Review the Closing Working Capital calculation (due within 90 days after closing, i.e., by July 14, 2026) to confirm whether a reserve for the NYS audit was included. If not, the § 9.2(c) indemnity should be available — but only if asserted before April 15, 2027.
- File a protective claim under § 9.1(d) before the survival period expires.
- Engage NYS tax counsel to assess the likelihood and magnitude of an adverse determination.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 6. Governing Law Mismatch: SPA (New York) vs. Escrow Agreement (Delaware)

**Location:** SPA § 11.9; Escrow Agreement § 8.2

The SPA is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County courts. The Escrow Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery.

**Why it matters:** The pending $1,300,000 escrow claim will require litigation in Delaware (for the escrow mechanics) while any underlying SPA breach claim would be in New York. This creates procedural complexity, potential for inconsistent rulings, and increased costs. The Escrow Agent's duties are "solely ministerial" (Escrow Agreement § 6.1), so the Chancery Court may be reluctant to interpret the underlying SPA representations to determine whether a claim is valid.

**Recommendation:**
- In any settlement or stipulation regarding the escrow, ensure the Delaware proceeding addresses only the release mechanics and does not adjudicate the merits of the SPA claim.
- If litigation becomes necessary, consider whether the parties can agree to a consolidated or sequential proceeding to avoid conflicting outcomes.

### 7. Side Letter Information Rights Give Seller Operational Visibility

**Location:** Side Letter § 4

Until the Retained Credit Support is fully released, Buyer must give Seller, within five Business Days after receipt, copies of (a) any written notice of default under the Yonkers lease or Saint Odile agreement, and (b) any written demand or claim under the Retained Credit Support.

**Why it matters:** This gives Seller early notice of any operational problems at the Company that could trigger the credit support. Combined with the uncapped indemnity (Issue 2), Seller can monitor the Company's performance and potentially time its claims. It also creates a channel for Seller to assert that Buyer is not using "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain releases if the Company is in difficulty.

**Recommendation:**
- Limit the information rights to actual demands or calls on the guaranty/bond, not mere notices of default under the underlying agreements (which may be curable and never result in a claim on Seller).
- Alternatively, require that any such notice be accompanied by Seller's written acknowledgment that it does not constitute a demand under the Retained Credit Support unless and until the landlord or surety actually calls.

### 8. Restriction on Amending Yonkers Lease and Saint Odile Contract

**Location:** SPA § 6.6 (as extended by Side Letter § 3(a))

Until the Retained Credit Support is released (now through April 30, 2027), Buyer must cause the Company not to amend, extend or renew the Yonkers plant lease or the Saint Odile contract "in any manner that increases Seller's obligations under the Retained Credit Support without Seller's prior written consent."

**Why it matters:** This could prevent the Company from entering into a lease amendment that, for example, adds square footage (increasing the guaranteed rent) or extends the Saint Odile contract (increasing the period during which the bond is required). Even if the amendment is commercially beneficial to the Company, it may be blocked if it technically increases Seller's exposure.

**Recommendation:**
- Before negotiating any amendment to the Yonkers lease or Saint Odile contract, obtain a written determination (from counsel or from Seller) that the amendment does not increase Seller's obligations under the Retained Credit Support.
- In the alternative, negotiate a carve-out for amendments that do not increase the *aggregate* guaranteed amount or the *term* of the bond.

### 10. Mutual NDA Clean Team Limitation on Diligence

**Location:** Mutual NDA § 2.5

Because Giraisk and the Company compete in certain markets, Pinaix was entitled to designate customer-specific pricing, contract terms, and similar information as "Clean Team Only," accessible only to outside counsel, advisors, and limited non-commercial employees of Giraisk.

**Why it matters:** This may have limited the commercial team's ability to fully evaluate customer relationships, pricing dynamics, and contract terms during diligence. If the $1,300,000 Saint Odile overbilling claim (Side Letter § 5(a)) relates to pricing or invoicing information that was Clean Team Only, there may be an argument that the representation in § 4.9 (Material Contracts in full force and effect) or § 4.7 (Undisclosed Liabilities) was qualified by the Clean Team restriction. However, this is a weak argument post-closing and should not be relied upon.

**Recommendation:**
- No immediate action required, but note for the file that the Clean Team restriction was in effect during diligence. If the overbilling claim turns out to involve information that was specifically designated Clean Team Only, document that fact for potential use in the escrow dispute.
