### 1. Escrow Claim Filed Seven Days After Initial Release Date – $2,425,000 Already Released to Seller

**Location:** Escrow Agreement § 4.2; Side Letter § 5(a)

The Initial Release Date under the Escrow Agreement is 180 days after the April 15, 2026 Closing – i.e., **October 12, 2026**. Section 4.2 provides that the Escrow Agent releases the Initial Release Amount ($2,425,000) to Seller "less the aggregate Disputed Amount under all Claim Notices **delivered before the Initial Release Date**."

Buyer's Claim Notice for $1,300,000 (Saint Odile overbilling credits) was delivered **October 19, 2026** – seven days after the Initial Release Date. As a result, the full $2,425,000 was released to Seller on October 12, 2026, without reduction for Buyer's claim.

**Why it matters:** Buyer's $1.3M claim can now only be recovered from the remaining $2,425,000 in escrow (the Final Release portion, due April 15, 2027) or by direct action against Seller. Because Seller has disputed the claim (Side Letter § 5(b)), the $1.3M is a "Disputed Amount" under Escrow Agreement § 5.4 and will be withheld from the Final Release on April 15, 2027. However, if the dispute is not resolved by then through Joint Written Instructions or a Final Order, the Escrow Agent may interplead the funds (Escrow Agreement § 6.5), and Buyer would need to litigate in the **Delaware Court of Chancery** (Escrow Agreement § 8.2) – a different forum and under a different governing law (Delaware) than the SPA (New York).

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately assess whether the $1.3M claim can be resolved before the Final Release Date (April 15, 2027) to avoid interpleader and multi-forum litigation.
- If resolution is unlikely, prepare to file in the Delaware Court of Chancery to obtain a Final Order preserving the disputed funds.
- Evaluate whether Seller's creditworthiness supports a direct recovery action if the escrow is interpleaded or exhausted.

### 2. Tax Indemnity Survival Period Drastically Shortened from Prior Draft – Pending NY Sales Tax Audit Likely to Exceed the New Period

**Location:** SPA § 9.1(c) (final) vs. Prior Draft § 9.1(c); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.16, Item 1

**Prior Draft § 9.1(c):** "The obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(c) survive until **60 days after expiration of the statute of limitations** applicable to the assessment of the underlying Tax, giving effect to any waiver or extension."

**Final SPA § 9.1(c):** "The obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(c) survive until **the end of the survival period applicable to the representations in Section 4.16**."

Section 4.16 is not a Fundamental Representation, so its survival period is **12 months after the Closing Date** (SPA § 9.1(a)) – i.e., **April 15, 2027**.

**Why it matters:** The Disclosure Schedule (Schedule 4.16, Item 1) discloses a pending New York State sales and use tax field audit (Case No. X-418827364) covering **March 1, 2023 through November 30, 2024**. The audit addresses the taxability of linen rental charges and equipment/chemical purchases. The auditor issued an information document request on January 12, 2026; the Company responded February 9, 2026. No assessment has been issued as of the Disclosure Schedule date.

The NY sales tax statute of limitations is generally five years from the due date of the return. For the 2023–2024 periods at issue, the limitations period would not expire until **2028–2029**. Under the prior draft, Buyer's tax indemnity would have been protected through approximately 2029–2030. Under the final SPA, **all tax indemnity protection expires April 15, 2027** – likely well before the audit concludes and any assessment is issued.

If the NY audit results in a material assessment for pre-closing periods after April 15, 2027, Buyer will have **no recourse against Seller** under the indemnification provisions. The claim would also be time-barred under the Section 4.16 representation (same 12-month survival).

**Recommendation:**
- This is the single most valuable provision lost in negotiation. If the deal has not yet closed (confirm status), this must be restored. If the deal has closed (the side letter confirms Closing on April 15, 2026), the practical steps are:
  - Monitor the NY audit closely. If an assessment is issued before April 15, 2027, file a Claim Notice against the escrow immediately.
  - If the assessment is likely to come after April 15, 2027, explore whether the overassessment could be framed as a breach of the Section 4.16 representation (rather than a Pre-Closing Tax under § 9.2(c)) – though the survival period is the same.
  - Consider whether Seller's continued involvement in the tax matter (SPA § 6.7 requires Seller to prepare pre-closing tax returns and cooperate in audits) creates a separate covenant-based claim under § 9.2(b), which is **not** subject to the cap or deductible (SPA § 9.5(c)) and survives "in accordance with its terms" (§ 9.1(c)). If Seller fails to cooperate in the audit, that could be a covenant breach with longer survival.
  - Quantify the potential exposure: the audit covers ~21 months of linen rental revenue. At 2025 revenue of $41.6M, the audited periods likely represent $7–8M in revenue. If even a portion is reassessed as taxable, the exposure could be substantial (NY sales tax is 4–8.875% depending on jurisdiction).

### 3. Side Letter Imposes Uncapped, Open-Ended Indemnity for Retained Credit Support

**Location:** Side Letter § 3(b); SPA § 6.6, § 9.3(b), § 9.5(c)

Side Letter § 3(b) provides:

> "Buyer shall indemnify Seller and the other Seller Indemnitees against, and hold them harmless from, **all Losses arising out of or resulting from the Retained Credit Support**. This includes any amount paid or payable by Seller or any of its Affiliates under the Yonkers lease guaranty, and any amount paid or payable to the surety under the Saint Odile performance bond or any related indemnity agreement, **in each case whether the claim arises before or after the Extended Release Date**."

This indemnity is "deemed an obligation of Buyer under Section 9.3(b)" of the SPA. Under SPA § 9.5(c), the Cap ($4.85M) and Deductible ($485K) apply **only** to claims under § 9.2(a) and § 9.3(a). Therefore, this indemnity is **uncapped and has no deductible**.

**Why it matters:**

| Item | Duration | Maximum Exposure |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| Yonkers Lease Guaranty | Lease runs through **June 30, 2034** (~8.25 years) | ~$1.15M/yr base rent + taxes, operating expenses, other charges. Conservative estimate: **$12–15M+** |
| Saint Odile Performance Bond | Bond renews annually while the agreement requires it. Agreement runs through Dec 31, 2027 + two 1-yr auto-renewals = potentially **Dec 31, 2029** | **$3,000,000** (bond face amount) |
| **Combined maximum** | | **~$15–18M+** |

The phrase "whether the claim arises before or after the Extended Release Date" (April 30, 2027) means that even if Seller is never released, and a claim under the guaranty or bond arises in 2028, 2029, or later, Buyer must indemnify Seller. This converts what was originally a "commercially reasonable efforts" obligation (SPA § 6.6) into an **absolute financial guarantee** of Seller's credit support obligations for the remaining life of the underlying agreements.

As of the side letter date (October 26, 2026), **neither release has been obtained**: the landlord is still "reviewing" Buyer's financial information, and Saint Odile has "not yet consented" to the bond substitution (Side Letter § 2).

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a cap on the § 3(b) indemnity (e.g., limited to the face amount of the bond plus a defined multiple of annual rent).
- Add a "drop-dead" date after which the indemnity terminates regardless of whether the release has been obtained.
- Require Seller to use its own influence with the landlord and Saint Odile to facilitate the releases (the current "cooperation" obligation in § 3(d) is weak – Seller is not required to pay any fee or incur any cost).
- If the releases are not obtained by the Extended Release Date (April 30, 2027), consider whether Buyer can terminate the indemnity or require Seller to post alternative security.
- Ensure the Company's insurance program covers lease liability and performance bond obligations to the maximum extent possible.

### 4. Key Employee Agreement Non-Compete Excludes the Company's Primary Business Segment

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement § 1 (definition of "Business") vs. SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Business")

**Key Employee Agreement § 1:**
> "**Business** means commercial laundry and linen services provided to **hotels, resorts and restaurants**, including linen rental, laundering, pressing and the delivery and collection of linens and related textile products."

**SPA § 1.1:**
> "**Business** means commercial laundry, linen rental and textile processing services for **healthcare, hospitality and industrial** customers."

The non-compete in Key Employee Agreement § 7.1 restricts Szabo-Whitlock from engaging in "the Business" – defined in the Key Employee Agreement to cover only hospitality (hotels, resorts, restaurants). It **excludes healthcare and industrial customers**.

Per Schedule 4.10, **healthcare represents 61.9% of Company revenue** ($25.78M of $41.62M). The Company's largest customer is Saint Odile Health System (23.7% of revenue).

**Why it matters:** If Szabo-Whitlock departs and joins or starts a competitor serving healthcare facilities (hospitals, nursing homes, dialysis centers, surgical centers), she would **not** be in breach of the non-compete. Given that she is the Company's President with "substantial knowledge of the Company's operations, customers, pricing, employees and confidential information" (Key Employee Agreement, Recital B), this is a significant gap in protection.

The customer non-solicitation (§ 7.2) and employee non-solicitation (§ 7.3) are also keyed to "the Business" as narrowly defined, so they likely suffer from the same defect.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend the Key Employee Agreement to conform the definition of "Business" to the SPA definition (healthcare, hospitality, and industrial).
- If Szabo-Whitlock is amenable, execute a revised agreement now while she is still within the Initial Term (through April 15, 2029).
- In the interim, ensure the confidentiality provisions (§ 6) are strictly enforced, as they are not limited by the "Business" definition.

### 5. Multiemployer Pension Plan Withdrawal Liability – CBA Expires February 28, 2027

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.17(b)(2); SPA § 4.17, § 9.1(a)

Schedule 4.17(b)(2) discloses that the Company contributes to the **Local 412 Pension Fund**, a multiemployer plan, under the CBA with Hudson Textile Workers Union, Local 412. The CBA runs through **February 28, 2027**.

Under ERISA § 4201, if an employer withdraws from a multiemployer plan, it becomes liable for its proportionate share of the plan's unfunded vested benefits. This "withdrawal liability" can be substantial and is triggered by various events, including cessation of participation in the plan.

**Why it matters:**
- The CBA expires in approximately 4 months (from the side letter date). If the Company and the union cannot agree on a successor CBA, and the Company stops contributing to the pension fund, a withdrawal event could be triggered.
- The Section 4.17 representation (employee benefit plans) is **not** a Fundamental Representation, so it survives only **12 months after Closing** – i.e., until **April 15, 2027**. That is only **6 weeks** after the CBA expires.
- Withdrawal liability assessments from multiemployer plans can take considerable time to calculate and deliver. If the assessment arrives after April 15, 2027, Buyer may have no indemnification recourse.
- The potential withdrawal liability is **not disclosed on Schedule 4.7** (Undisclosed Liabilities), so it is not carved out of the "no liabilities" representation. However, it is a contingent liability that may not be "reflected on the December 31, 2025 balance sheet" (SPA § 4.7(a)).

**Recommendation:**
- Before the CBA expires, determine the Local 412 Pension Fund's funded status and estimate the Company's potential withdrawal liability share. Request this information from the plan (ERISA § 4021 entitles participants and employers to annual funding notices).
- If the CBA is not renewed, ensure the Company continues to make required contributions through the transition period to avoid an inadvertent withdrawal trigger.
- If a material withdrawal liability is identified, assess whether it constitutes a breach of the Section 4.7 (Undisclosed Liabilities) representation and file a claim before the survival period expires (April 15, 2027).
- Consider whether the SPA's tax indemnity survival issue (Item 2 above) creates a pattern of inadequate protection for contingent liabilities that crystallize after 12 months.

### 6. Split Governing Law and Jurisdiction – Escrow Disputes in Delaware, SPA Claims in New York

**Location:** Escrow Agreement § 8.2; SPA § 11.9, § 11.10

The SPA is governed by **New York law** with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County** courts. The Escrow Agreement is governed by **Delaware law** with exclusive jurisdiction in the **Delaware Court of Chancery** (or Delaware Superior Court / D. Del. if Chancery lacks subject-matter jurisdiction).

**Why it matters:** The current $1.3M escrow dispute sits at the intersection of both agreements. Buyer's underlying claim is for breach of SPA representations (Sections 4.7 and 4.9), which would be litigated in New York. But the mechanism for recovering from escrow is governed by the Escrow Agreement, which requires Delaware litigation. The Escrow Agent's duties are "solely ministerial" (Escrow Agreement § 6.1) – it will not resolve the dispute. Buyer may need to:
- File in Delaware to compel the Escrow Agent to hold the disputed funds (or to interplead), AND
- File in New York to establish the underlying breach of representation.

This creates risk of inconsistent rulings, increased cost, and delay. Delaware law may interpret the SPA's indemnification provisions differently than New York law would.

**Recommendation:**
- If pursuing the $1.3M claim, file in the Delaware Court of Chancery first to establish that the disputed amount should be held (or to obtain a Final Order). Simultaneously prepare the New York action on the merits.
- For any future escrow disputes, consider whether the parties can agree to consolidate in one forum (though the executed agreements likely cannot be amended without all three parties' consent – Escrow Agreement § 8.5).

### 9. SPA § 9.5(e) – Drafting Error: Heading/Text Mismatch on Consequential Damages

**Location:** SPA § 9.5(e)

The heading reads "**No Consequential Damages**" but the operative text states: "No party is liable for **punitive damages**, except to the extent paid to a third party."

**Why it matters:** Under SPA § 1.2, headings are "for convenience only," so the text controls. As drafted, the provision limits only punitive damages – it does **not** exclude consequential damages (lost profits, lost business opportunities, etc.). This is actually favorable to Buyer (it preserves the ability to claim consequential damages for Seller's breaches), but it creates ambiguity that Seller could exploit in a dispute. Seller might argue that the heading reflects the parties' intent to exclude consequential damages, and that the text is a scrivener's error.

**Recommendation:**
- If Buyer is the one asserting a claim for consequential damages (e.g., lost profits from a customer lost due to a pre-closing breach), be prepared to defend the provision as written.
- If the opportunity arises to amend (e.g., in connection with resolving the escrow dispute), clarify the provision to state the parties' actual intent.
- Note that this provision does not affect the exclusive remedy clause (§ 9.7) or the cap/deductible (§ 9.5(a)–(b)).

### 11. NDA Expiration and Post-Closing Confidentiality

**Location:** NDA § 8.1; SPA § 6.4

The NDA expires **September 8, 2027** (two years from the September 8, 2025 Effective Date). After that date, the NDA's confidentiality obligations lapse (except for retained copies under NDA § 4.2). However, SPA § 6.4 provides that "From and after the Closing, Seller shall, and shall cause its Affiliates to, hold in confidence all confidential information concerning the Company and the Business." This is a covenant that survives "in accordance with its terms" (SPA § 9.1(c)) – and since no term is specified, it survives until "fully performed," which is arguably indefinite.

This is adequate protection, but note that the SPA § 6.4 covenant is subject to the exclusive remedy provision (§ 9.7). If Seller breaches confidentiality after the NDA expires, Buyer's remedy is limited to indemnification under Article IX (subject to the cap if framed as a § 9.2(a) claim, though a covenant breach under § 9.2(b) would be uncapped).
