## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Transition Services Agreement: Buyer Indemnifies Seller for Seller's Own Negligence (Uncapped)

**Document/Section:** Transition Services Agreement, § 7.1, read with § 8.3.

**Issue:** Section 7.1 provides that *Recipient* (Buyer) "shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Provider [Seller]... from and against any and all Losses arising out of or relating to the Services... **including Losses arising from Provider's negligence in performing the Services**, except to the extent such Losses result from Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct." Section 8.3 then carves out "Recipient's obligations under Section 7.1" from the liability cap in § 8.2. The net effect: Buyer has **uncapped** indemnity exposure for Seller's **ordinary negligence** in performing the transition services.

**Why it matters:** During the six-month TSA term, Seller operates the Monitoring Platform, the customer call center, permitting support, and billing. A negligent error—missed permit deadline, misrouted warranty claim, data breach from inadequate monitoring—would generate Losses that Buyer must indemnify Seller against, with no cap. Given that the call center answers "in the name of the Business" (TSA Schedule A, Service A-3) and handles 6,842 residential and 87 commercial customer relationships, the exposure is material and ongoing.

**Recommendation:** Reverse the indemnity. Seller (as Provider) should indemnify Buyer for Losses arising from Seller's negligence in performing the Services. Buyer's indemnity should be limited to Losses arising from Buyer's own negligence or direction. Delete the reference to Section 7.1 from the § 8.3 cap exceptions.

### 2. Section 4.9 (Installed Systems) Survival Period Reduced to 90 Days

**Document/Section:** APA (executed), § 9.1(b); APA (executed), § 1.1 ("Specified Warranty Period"); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9 Part A. Compare APA (prior draft), § 9.1.

**Issue:** The prior draft provided a uniform 18-month survival for all representations and warranties (with longer survival only for Fundamental Representations). The executed APA adds § 9.1(b): "the representations and warranties in Section 4.9 shall survive until the expiration of the Specified Warranty Period." Schedule 4.9 Part A defines "Specified Warranty Period" as "the period beginning on the Closing Date and **ending ninety (90) days after the Closing Date**."

Thus, Seller's representations regarding the condition, code compliance, and absence of defects in all 6,929 Installed Systems (6,842 residential + 87 commercial) expire **90 days** after Closing—rather than 18 months as in the prior draft.

**Why it matters:** Solar installation defects (roof penetrations, flashing failures, structural issues, code violations) are often latent and may not manifest for months or years. The Disclosure Schedule itself identifies 112 historical roof-leak reports, 23 systems without final inspection sign-off, and 18 pending racking retrofits. If a systemic installation defect is discovered at month four, Buyer will have no indemnity recourse under Section 4.9. The customer warranty obligations (10–25 years per Schedule 4.8 Part A) remain with Buyer as an Assumed Liability under § 2.3(b), but the rep that the systems were properly installed is gone.

**Recommendation:** Restore the 18-month survival for Section 4.9 reps, or extend the Specified Warranty Period to a duration that aligns with the nature of the risk (e.g., 12–18 months). Alternatively, negotiate a specific survival period for Section 4.9(c) (roof-penetration and structural defects) of no less than 24 months, given the historical claim pattern disclosed.

### 3. Key Employee Agreement: "Business" Defined Too Narrowly for Non-Compete Protection

**Document/Section:** Key Employee Agreement, § 1.1 ("Business"); compare APA, § 1.1 ("Business").

**Issue:** The KEA defines "Business" as "the installation of residential solar photovoltaic systems." The APA defines "Business" as "the design, sale, installation, maintenance and monitoring of residential and commercial solar energy and battery storage systems." The KEA's restrictive covenants (non-compete in § 7.1(a), non-solicitation in §§ 7.1(b)–(d)) all key off the KEA's narrower definition.

**Why it matters:** Tobias Renfeld is the founder, CEO, and majority shareholder of Seller. He possesses intimate knowledge of the Business's commercial solar operations, battery storage offerings, monitoring platform, design processes, and customer relationships. Under the current KEA definition, Renfeld could lawfully:
- Launch a **commercial** solar installation or EPC business;
- Enter the **battery storage** market (design, sale, installation, or maintenance);
- Provide **monitoring** services (the Monitoring Platform is a key acquired asset);
- Engage in **design** or **sale** of residential solar systems (as long as not "installation");
- Operate in any solar segment so long as it is not the "installation of residential solar photovoltaic systems."

The APA's § 6.6 non-compete binds Seller (the entity) for five years, but post-Closing Seller will be an asset-less shell. The KEA is the operative personal restriction on Renfeld, and it is materially underinclusive.

**Recommendation:** Conform the KEA's definition of "Business" to the APA's definition, or at minimum expand it to cover "the design, sale, installation, maintenance and monitoring of residential and commercial solar energy and battery storage systems." This is the covenant that protects the $38.5M purchase price and the goodwill acquired under APA § 2.1(h).

## HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 4. Side Letter Excluded from "Ancillary Agreements" Definition

**Document/Section:** APA (executed), § 1.1 ("Ancillary Agreements"); compare APA (prior draft), § 1.1.

**Issue:** The executed APA defines "Ancillary Agreements" to include the Bill of Sale, Assignment and Assumption Agreement, TSA, Escrow Agreement, and KEA, "but **excluding the Side Letter**." The prior draft had no such exclusion (and no Side Letter existed).

**Why it matters:**
- **Authority rep gap (APA § 4.2):** Seller represents it has authority to enter into "this Agreement and the Ancillary Agreements to which it is a party." The Side Letter is not covered. If there is any authority defect in the Side Letter (e.g., a shareholder approval requirement under the Shareholders Agreement listed in Disclosure Schedule 2.2, item 6), Buyer has no rep to rely on.
- **Indemnity gap (APA § 9.2(b)):** Seller indemnifies Buyer for "any breach or non-fulfillment of any covenant or agreement to be performed by Seller **under this Agreement**." The Side Letter's covenants (e.g., § 3.2—no settlement without Buyer's consent) are performed under the Side Letter, not "this Agreement." A breach of Side Letter § 3.2 does not clearly trigger APA indemnity.
- **Tension with Order of Precedence (APA § 11.10):** The new § 11.10 lists "the concurrent Side Letter" first in the precedence order, then "the other Ancillary Agreements" last. The word "other" implies the Side Letter is in the same family but technically excluded—a drafting inconsistency that could generate ambiguity.

**Recommendation:** Either (a) delete "but excluding the Side Letter" from the Ancillary Agreements definition, or (b) add express language to the Side Letter stating that (i) Seller's covenants in the Side Letter are covered by APA § 9.2(b), and (ii) the authority reps in APA §§ 4.2 and 5.1 extend to the Side Letter. Option (a) is cleaner.

### 5. Installer Classification Matter: Capped and Basketed Recovery May Be Inadequate

**Document/Section:** Side Letter, § 2; APA, §§ 9.5(a)–(b); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.13(c) item 1.

**Issue:** Side Letter § 2 channels all Installer Classification Matter Losses through the Section 4.13(c) rep, making them subject to:
- The **15% cap** (APA § 9.5(a)): $5,775,000;
- The **$385,000 basket** (APA § 9.5(b)): no recovery until aggregate Losses exceed this threshold;
- The **18-month survival period** (APA § 9.1): the DOL audit is ongoing with no findings yet; if the assessment is not issued within 18 months of Closing, the claim may be time-barred.

The Disclosure Schedule states that 137 individuals were engaged as independent contractors since January 1, 2023. A DOL misclassification finding for 137 workers over 3.5 years could produce back wages, overtime, FLSA liquidated damages (100% premium), civil money penalties, and state-law claims easily exceeding $5.775M.

**Why it matters:** If the ultimate assessment exceeds the cap, Buyer absorbs the excess with no recourse. If the DOL process extends beyond 18 months (common for multi-worker audits), Buyer may lose the claim entirely before the assessment is final.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate an **exception to the cap** for the Installer Classification Matter (similar to the existing Fundamental Rep and fraud exceptions in § 9.5(c)), or raise the cap specifically for this matter.
- Negotiate an **extended survival period** for Section 4.13(c) (e.g., until 60 days after the final resolution of the DOL matter, or a fixed period of 3–4 years), given the audit is still pending.
- Confirm that the basket does not apply (or is reduced to zero) for this matter, given it is a known, quantifiable risk rather than an unknown rep breach.

### 6. TSA: Seller May Delegate Services Without Buyer's Consent

**Document/Section:** Transition Services Agreement, § 12.3.

**Issue:** "Provider may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, or **delegate the performance of any of the Services, to any Person without the consent of Recipient**."

**Why it matters:** The Services include hosting and operating the Monitoring Platform (which contains sensitive customer data), running the customer call center, and handling permitting and interconnection filings. If Seller delegates these to an unknown third party—particularly during a period when Seller is divesting its core assets and may be financially distressed—Buyer's customers and data could be in the hands of an unvetted entity. There is no quality-control mechanism, no approval right, and no step-in right for Buyer.

**Recommendation:** Require Buyer's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any delegation of Services, or at minimum for Services A-1 (Monitoring Platform) and A-3 (Customer Call Center). Add a provision that any delegatee must assume the TSA's confidentiality, data security, and service standard obligations.

## MODERATE-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 7. Post-Closing Remediation Obligations Not Allocated

**Document/Section:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9 Part B, items 3, 4, and 5.

**Issue:** Three categories of post-Closing work are identified but not assigned:
- **Item 3:** $46,000 commercial warranty remediation (inverter pad drainage/conduit water intrusion), scheduled for September 2026.
- **Item 4:** 23 residential systems lacking final inspection sign-off; re-inspection being scheduled.
- **Item 5:** 18 racking retrofits (manufacturer-funded) to be completed by October 31, 2026.

None of these is expressly addressed in the TSA Schedule A, the APA covenants, or the Side Letter. Under APA § 2.4(a), pre-Closing liabilities are Excluded Liabilities (Seller's responsibility). But practically, the systems are Buyer's assets post-Closing, the customers are Buyer's customers, and Seller (a post-Closing shell) must perform physical work on Buyer's installations.

**Why it matters:** Without a clear allocation and access mechanism, disputes are likely. Seller may lack incentive or capacity to complete the work promptly. Buyer's customers will experience delays.

**Recommendation:** Add a specific post-Closing covenant (in the APA or a schedule to the TSA) requiring Seller to complete items 3, 4, and 5 by their stated dates, with Buyer (or its designee) having the right to perform the work at Seller's cost if Seller fails to do so. Include access rights for Seller's personnel to Buyer's sites.

### 8. "Closing Statement" Referenced but Not Defined

**Document/Section:** APA (executed), § 2.3(a). Compare APA (prior draft), § 2.3(a).

**Issue:** The executed APA adds the qualifier "and that are reflected in the **Closing Statement**" to the Assumed Liabilities for trade payables. The term "Closing Statement" is not defined anywhere in the APA, the Ancillary Agreements, or the Disclosure Schedule.

**Why it matters:** While the qualifier is favorable to Buyer (limiting assumed payables to those on the statement), the absence of a definition creates uncertainty about (a) who prepares it, (b) when it is delivered, (c) what format it takes, and (d) the consequence if a payable is omitted in error. A dispute over whether a particular trade payable is "reflected in the Closing Statement" could delay or complicate the allocation of Excluded vs. Assumed Liabilities.

**Recommendation:** Add a definition of "Closing Statement" (e.g., a schedule of trade payables prepared by Seller and delivered to Buyer no later than [X] Business Days before Closing, in a form reasonably acceptable to Buyer) or a procedural provision governing its preparation and dispute resolution.

### 9. Third-Party Consent Gap After 12-Month Period

**Document/Section:** APA, § 2.9; Bill of Sale, § 4.

**Issue:** APA § 2.9 requires Seller to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain third-party consents for up to 12 months post-Closing. Bill of Sale § 4 provides that assignment occurs automatically when consent is obtained. But neither document addresses what happens if consent is **not** obtained within the 12-month period. The Assigned Contract remains with Seller, but Buyer has already paid for it as part of the Purchase Price.

**Why it matters:** Schedule 4.4 identifies eight contracts requiring consent or notice, including the Master Supply Agreement (photovoltaic modules), the Distribution Agreement (inverters and battery storage), and the Authorized Installer Agreement (residential battery storage). Loss of any of these would materially impair the Business. If consent is not obtained by month 12, Buyer has no further remedy against Seller under the APA (the covenant has expired), and the contract is not assigned.

**Recommendation:** Add a provision stating that if any consent listed in Schedule 4.4 has not been obtained by the end of the 12-month period, (a) the Purchase Price shall be reduced by the allocated value of the unassigned contract, or (b) Seller shall remain obligated to use best efforts (elevated from "commercially reasonable") until consent is obtained, with a specific indemnity for Losses resulting from the failure to obtain consent.

### 10. Board Consent: Broad Waiver Authority Without Board Re-Approval

**Document/Section:** Buyer Board Consent, § 7 (second "RESOLVED FURTHER").

**Issue:** The consent authorizes any Authorized Officer to "exercise or waive any of the Company's rights under the Transaction Documents before or after the Closing," with a carve-out only for amendments "that changes the Purchase Price or the Indemnity Escrow Amount."

**Why it matters:** An Authorized Officer could, without further Board approval, waive the non-compete in APA § 6.6, release indemnification claims under Article IX, accept a shortening of the survival period, or waive a closing condition. The only protected items are the Purchase Price and Escrow Amount.

**Recommendation:** Expand the carve-out to require Board re-approval for any waiver of (a) Article IX indemnification rights, (b) the non-competition covenants in APA § 6.6 or KEA Article VII, (c) any closing condition in Article VII, or (d) any reduction in the survival periods in § 9.1.

## LOWER-PRIORITY / DRAFTING OBSERVATIONS

### 11. TSA Call Center Operates Under Acquired Trade Name

**Document/Section:** TSA, Schedule A, Service A-3; APA, § 2.1(d).

**Issue:** The trade name "Rebous Rooftop Energy" is a Purchased Asset (APA § 2.1(d)). The TSA requires Seller's call center to answer calls "in the name of the Business" for six months post-Closing. This means Seller's personnel will be using Buyer's acquired trade name, creating potential confusion about which entity is responsible for customer interactions and any resulting obligations.

**Recommendation:** Clarify in the TSA that calls are answered "in the name of the Business on behalf of Recipient," and that Provider acts as Recipient's agent for Service A-3 purposes. Add a specific indemnity from Provider for misstatements or unauthorized commitments made by call center personnel.

### 12. Escrow Agreement: Buyer Bears Tax on Escrow Interest

**Document/Section:** Indemnity Escrow Agreement, § 8.1.

**Issue:** "Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds until they are disbursed... All interest and earnings shall be reported as income of Buyer until disbursed."

**Why it matters:** On $3,850,000 in a money market account, even at modest rates, Buyer will recognize taxable interest income over the 18-month escrow period without receiving the cash (it remains in escrow as security). This is a minor economic cost but should be factored into the deal model.

**Recommendation:** Acceptable as a market-standard provision, but confirm with tax counsel that the treatment under Treas. Reg. § 1.468B-8 is appropriate given the indemnity (rather than purchase price adjustment) nature of the escrow. Consider negotiating for Seller to bear the tax or for the escrow to be structured as a "true" escrow where neither party is the tax owner.

### 13. Disclosure Schedule 4.15, Item 3: Awkward Characterization of Receivable

**Document/Section:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.15, item 3.

**Issue:** The disclosure states: "The receivable is an Excluded Asset under Section 2.2(a) of the Agreement only to the extent it is collected before the Closing." Section 2.2(a) excludes "cash and cash equivalents"—not receivables. The intended meaning is that *proceeds* collected pre-Closing are cash (excluded), while the *receivable itself* remains a Purchased Asset. The language as written could be read to suggest the receivable is an Excluded Asset, creating ambiguity.

**Recommendation:** Revise to: "The receivable is a Purchased Asset under Section 2.1(a). Any cash proceeds collected in respect of it before the Closing Date are Excluded Assets under Section 2.2(a)."

**Document/Section:** Referenced in APA §§ 1.1, 2.3, 3.2(b), 3.3(c); Bill of Sale § 5.

**Issue:** The Assignment and Assumption Agreement is a defined Ancillary Agreement and a required closing deliverable, but it is not included in the nine documents provided for review.
