## Summary

Review of the Asset Purchase Agreement (APA), the Side Letter, the form Bill of Sale, the Letter of Intent (LOI), and the Ostrander insurance certificate cover letter reveals several material departures from the commercial terms agreed in the LOI and drafting choices that leave Soupen significantly exposed. The most serious issues are (1) a Side Letter provision that redefines "Losses" for the entire agreement in a way that would strip out remediation, investigation, and legal costs from indemnification recovery; (2) a 12-month survival period for environmental indemnity claims, contrary to the LOI's agreement of statute-of-limitations-plus-60-days; and (3) the omission of the Lakeport Municipal Water District consent from Schedule 4.5, which the LOI identified as a condition to closing. Details and recommended fixes follow, ranked by significance.

---

## Issue 1 – Side Letter Redefines "Losses" for the Entire Agreement

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.2; APA § 1.1 (definition of "Losses"); APA § 11.9 (order of precedence); LOI § 4.3.

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 1.1 defines "Losses" as "damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees."
- LOI § 4.3 confirms: "Indemnifiable losses would include damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees."
- Side Letter § 2.2 states: *"For all purposes of the Agreement and this Side Letter, 'Losses' means only out-of-pocket fines and penalties paid to a Governmental Authority."*
- APA § 11.9 provides that the Side Letter prevails over the APA in the event of conflict.

**Why it matters:**

Read literally, Side Letter § 2.2 overrides the APA's definition of "Losses" for the entire agreement, not just the Pasadena NOV indemnity in Side Letter § 2. If this reading holds, then for *every* indemnification claim under APA Article VIII—breach of representations (§ 8.2(a)), Excluded Liabilities including all Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities (§ 8.2(b)), and breach of covenants (§ 8.2(c))—Soupen could recover only out-of-pocket fines and penalties actually paid to a government authority. Soupen would have no indemnity recourse for:

- Environmental investigation and remediation costs (the most likely and expensive category at a chemical blending facility);
- Attorneys' fees;
- Damages from third-party claims;
- Costs of responding to regulatory inquiries that do not result in a fine or penalty.

Even if the provision was intended to apply only to the Pasadena NOV indemnity in Side Letter § 2, the language as drafted ("For all purposes of the Agreement") is unambiguous and, under the precedence rule in APA § 11.9, controls.

**Recommended fix:**

Strike the words "For all purposes of the Agreement and this Side Letter" and replace with "For purposes of Section 2 of this Side Letter only." Alternatively, if the parties intended a narrower definition for the Pasadena NOV, define a separate term (e.g., "NOV Losses") and use it only in Side Letter § 2.1, leaving the APA's "Losses" definition intact for all other indemnification claims.

## Issue 2 – Environmental Indemnity Survival Reduced to 12 Months

**Where:** APA § 8.1 (Survival); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations"); LOI § 4.4(c).

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 4.4(c): *"Given the nature of operations at a chemical blending facility, the Parties have agreed that Seller's environmental representations and Seller's indemnity for Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities, would survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations."*
- APA § 1.1 defines "Fundamental Representations" as only §§ 4.1 (Organization), 4.2 (Authority), 4.4 (Title), and 4.14 (Brokers). The environmental representation in § 4.12 is *not* a Fundamental Representation.
- APA § 8.1: General representations survive 12 months. Fundamental Representations survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations. Critically, § 8.1 further provides: *"All claims for indemnification under this Article VIII, including claims under Section 8.2(b), must be asserted by written notice delivered before the expiry of twelve (12) months after the Closing Date, other than claims for breach of a Fundamental Representation…"*

**Why it matters:**

Section 8.2(b) is the indemnity for Excluded Liabilities, which expressly includes all Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities (APA § 2.4(a)). Under the APA as drafted, Soupen must assert any environmental indemnity claim within 12 months after closing. For a chemical blending facility, this is dangerously short:

- Latent soil or groundwater contamination may not manifest for years.
- TCEQ investigations routinely extend beyond 12 months.
- The Pasadena NOV itself (involving stormwater exceedances from the tank farm) is still unresolved and could generate follow-on obligations.
- The LOI specifically negotiated a longer period "given the nature of operations at a chemical blending facility."

The only environmental claim with unlimited survival is the Pasadena NOV under Side Letter § 2.3. All other Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities—unknown contamination, prior releases, historical violations—fall within the 12-month window.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend APA § 8.1 to provide that (a) the environmental representation in § 4.12 and (b) the indemnity for Excluded Liabilities under § 8.2(b) (at least to the extent arising from Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities) survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations, consistent with LOI § 4.4(c). Add § 4.12 to the definition of "Fundamental Representations" or create a separate "Environmental Representations" category with its own survival period.

## Issue 3 – Lakeport Consent Omitted from Schedule 4.5

**Where:** APA Schedule 4.5; APA § 4.5; APA § 7.2(d); LOI §§ 3.1–3.3.

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 3.1: The Lakeport Supply Contract is "the Business's largest customer contract by revenue," and Buyer "regards continuation of that contract on its current terms as central to its valuation of the Business."
- LOI § 3.2: The contract "requires the District's prior written consent for assignment or change of control of the supplier."
- LOI § 3.3(b)–(d): The definitive agreement would (b) disclose the consent as a required third-party consent, (c) require Seller to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain it, and (d) make receipt of the consent a condition to Buyer's obligation to close.
- APA Schedule 4.5: **"None."**
- APA § 4.5: Seller represents that no third-party consent is required "except as set out in Schedule 4.5."
- APA § 7.2(d): Buyer's closing condition is that "Seller has obtained each of the consents listed in Schedule 4.5"—which is an empty set.

**Why it matters:**

The LOI treated the Lakeport consent as a deal-critical item. The definitive agreement silently drops it. The consequences:

1. Seller now represents that *no* third-party consent is needed (APA § 4.5), which is factually incorrect if the Lakeport contract truly requires consent for assignment.
2. Buyer has no closing condition protecting it against the risk that the Lakeport contract is not validly assigned.
3. If the assignment is ineffective without consent, Soupen may lose its largest revenue contract post-closing with no indemnity trigger tied to a specific consent failure (though a breach of § 4.5 would technically give rise to a claim under § 8.2(a), subject to the 12-month survival and the Basket).
4. The omission may also affect the "Material Contracts" representation in § 4.7, which states each Assumed Contract is "valid, binding and in full force and effect"—a statement that could be called into question if an assignment condition has not been satisfied.

**Recommended fix:**

Add the Lakeport consent to Schedule 4.5. Confirm that APA § 7.2(d) operates as a condition to Buyer's closing obligation. If the consent has already been obtained, request a copy for the file and note it in the schedule. If it has not been obtained, this should be a condition to closing that cannot be waived without Board approval.

## Issue 4 – Governing Law and Jurisdiction Shifted to Texas

**Where:** Side Letter § 5; APA §§ 11.7–11.8; APA § 11.9; LOI § 12.

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 12: Binding provisions governed by New York law.
- APA § 11.7: New York law governs.
- APA § 11.8: Exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York; jury trial waived.
- Side Letter § 5: *"This Side Letter, and any dispute arising out of or relating to the Agreement or the transactions contemplated by it, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Texas… Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state courts located in Harris County, Texas…"*
- APA § 11.9: The Side Letter prevails over the APA in the event of conflict.

**Why it matters:**

Because the Side Letter prevails (APA § 11.9), the effective governing law for the entire transaction—including disputes about the APA itself—is Texas law, and the effective forum is Harris County, Texas (where Seller is headquartered). This is a significant departure from the LOI and from the APA's own choice of New York. Practical implications include:

- Texas courts may interpret indemnification, survival, and limitation-of-liability provisions differently than New York courts.
- The non-compete in APA § 6.6 may be construed under Texas law, which has its own reasonableness standards for restrictive covenants.
- Litigating in Harris County is less convenient for a New York-based buyer.
- The APA's New York choice-of-law and forum provisions are effectively nullified.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend Side Letter § 5 to conform to APA §§ 11.7–11.8 (New York law, New York County jurisdiction), or at minimum limit the Texas governing law to disputes arising solely under the Side Letter (e.g., the Pasadena NOV indemnity) and preserve New York law for all other APA disputes. If Seller insists on Texas law for the Side Letter, negotiate a carve-out so that the APA's indemnification and survival provisions are interpreted under New York law.

## Issue 5 – Soupen Not Named as Additional Insured; Insurance Policies Excluded

**Where:** Ostrander cover letter §§ 3–4; APA § 2.2 (Excluded Assets); APA § 8.4(d).

**What the documents say:**

- Ostrander cover letter § 4: *"Soupen Water Solutions, Inc. is shown as certificate holder for informational purposes. It is not named as an additional insured or loss payee under any policy."*
- Ostrander cover letter § 3: Certificates "confer no rights on the certificate holder" and "do not create a contract between the issuing insurers and the certificate holder."
- APA § 2.2: Seller's insurance policies are Excluded Assets.
- APA § 8.4(d): Losses are calculated "net of any insurance proceeds actually recovered by the Indemnified Party."

**Why it matters:**

At closing, Soupen will own the Facility but will have no access to Seller's pollution legal liability, CGL, or property policies. The insurance gap creates several risks:

1. **Environmental:** If a pre-closing Release gives rise to a TCEQ enforcement action or third-party claim after closing, Soupen (as facility owner) will be the named respondent. It cannot look to Seller's pollution legal liability policy because it is not an additional insured.
2. **Property:** Between closing and the effective date of Soupen's own property policy, the Facility (including the tank farm and inventory) is uninsured.
3. **Indemnity offset:** APA § 8.4(d) reduces indemnifiable Losses by insurance proceeds "actually recovered by the Indemnified Party." If Soupen cannot access Seller's policies, it recovers nothing from insurance, which is fine for Soupen (no offset). But the reverse problem is that Seller may argue Soupen should have mitigated by obtaining its own insurance, and any proceeds from Soupen's own policies would offset the indemnity.
4. **Pasadena NOV:** The Side Letter requires Seller to respond pre-closing at its own cost (§ 2.4), but post-closing Soupen controls the response (APA § 8.5). If the NOV results in a penalty, Soupen pays it and seeks indemnity from Seller—subject to the narrowed "Losses" definition (Issue 1) and the 12-month claim deadline (Issue 2, though the Side Letter carves out the Pasadena NOV from the deadline).

**Recommended fix:**

- Require Seller to add Soupen as an additional insured on the pollution legal liability and CGL policies (at minimum) through the Closing Date, or to maintain those policies for a tail period post-closing with Soupen as additional insured.
- Alternatively, require Seller to procure a retroactive environmental insurance policy (also called "pollution legal liability – prior owner" coverage) naming Soupen as insured, with premiums paid by Seller.
- Confirm that Soupen's own insurance program is bound and effective as of the Closing Date to eliminate any gap.
- Make delivery of evidence of such coverage a condition to closing under APA § 7.2.

## Issue 6 – Asymmetric Assignment Right

**Where:** APA § 11.4; Side Letter § 6.2.

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 11.4: *"Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights… without the prior written consent of Seller. Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights, or delegate any of its obligations, under this Agreement to any Person without the consent of Buyer."*
- Side Letter § 6.2: "Section 11.4 of the Agreement applies to this Side Letter."

**Why it matters:**

Seller can assign all of its rights and obligations—including its indemnification obligations under APA Article VIII and its obligations under the Side Letter—to any person without Soupen's consent. If Seller assigns to an undercapitalized affiliate or a special-purpose entity, Soupen's recourse for environmental and other Excluded Liabilities could be significantly impaired. The non-compete in § 6.6 could also be assigned to an entity that is a shell with no operations to restrict.

Conversely, Soupen cannot assign without Seller's consent, which could complicate any internal restructuring or sale of the acquired business.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend APA § 11.4 to require Seller's assignment to be to a Person with net worth no less than Seller's at the time of assignment, and to provide that any assignment does not relieve Seller of its indemnification obligations (i.e., the assignee assumes but Seller remains jointly and severally liable). Alternatively, require Buyer's consent for any assignment of Seller's indemnification obligations.

## Issue 7 – Accounts Receivable Cut-Off Creates Working Capital Uncertainty

**Where:** APA § 2.1(f); LOI § 2.1.

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 2.1(f): Purchased Assets include "all accounts receivable of the Business arising after the date of this Agreement" (i.e., after August 14, 2026).
- LOI § 2.1: "The price assumes delivery of the Business on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normal level of working capital."

**Why it matters:**

Accounts receivable that existed as of August 14, 2026 (invoices already issued but not yet collected) are not Purchased Assets. They are retained by Seller. If the LOI's "normal level of working capital" was calculated including existing A/R, then Soupen is paying $48.5 million for a business whose working capital is lower than modeled, because a portion of the receivables stays with Seller. There is no working capital adjustment mechanism in the APA (no target, no true-up formula). The APA simply fixes the price at $48.5 million plus Assumed Liabilities.

**Recommended fix:**

Confirm with the deal team whether the $48.5 million price was modeled to exclude pre-signing A/R. If not, negotiate either (a) inclusion of all A/R (not just post-signing) in the Purchased Assets, or (b) a working capital true-up mechanism with a defined target and post-closing adjustment.

## Issue 8 – Schedule 4.12 Not Provided in the Matter Folder

**Where:** APA § 4.12; Side Letter § 1.2.

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 4.12: Environmental representations are qualified by "Except as set out in Schedule 4.12."
- Side Letter § 1.2: Confirms the Pasadena NOV "is disclosed in Schedule 4.12 to the Agreement and remains unresolved."
- The matter folder does not include Schedule 4.12.

**Why it matters:**

We cannot assess the full scope of disclosed environmental issues. Schedule 4.12 may contain additional notices, violations, or conditions beyond the Pasadena NOV that further qualify the environmental representations. Each disclosed item removes that issue from the scope of the § 4.12 representation (and thus from the § 8.2(a) indemnity for breach of that representation), though it would remain covered by the § 8.2(b) Excluded Liabilities indemnity.

**Open question:**

Request Schedule 4.12 from Seller's counsel. Review it for any additional environmental disclosures that could limit the scope of the environmental representation or indicate known conditions not addressed in the Side Letter.

## Issue 9 – Escrow Release at 12 Months May Precede Resolution of Environmental Claims

**Where:** APA § 8.6; APA § 2.5(a); Side Letter § 2.3.

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 2.5(a): $2,425,000 (5% of Purchase Price) is deposited into escrow.
- APA § 8.6: "Any portion of the Indemnity Escrow Amount not subject to a pending claim shall be released to Seller on the date that is twelve (12) months after the Closing Date."
- Side Letter § 2.3: Buyer may recover Pasadena NOV amounts from the escrow.

**Why it matters:**

The escrow is the primary security for Seller's indemnification obligations. It releases at 12 months. If the Pasadena NOV is still unresolved at 12 months, the portion of the escrow "subject to a pending claim" should be held. However, we do not have the Escrow Agreement in the folder to confirm that it mechanically withholds amounts for pending claims or that it aligns with the Side Letter's unlimited survival for the Pasadena NOV. For non-Pasadena environmental claims, the 12-month claim deadline (Issue 2) means the escrow release is aligned—but only because the claim deadline is itself unreasonably short.

**Open question:**

Request the Escrow Agreement. Confirm that (a) it withholds amounts for any claim noticed before the 12-month release date, (b) it permits recovery for the Pasadena NOV beyond 12 months consistent with Side Letter § 2.3, and (c) the release mechanics do not require Buyer to affirmatively request a holdback.

## Issue 10 – Transitional Supply Forecast: Missing Explicit Confidentiality Label

**Where:** APA § 6.7; LOI § 5.2.

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 5.2: "Any volume forecasts that Buyer provides to Seller for this purpose would be **confidential**. Seller would use those forecasts only to perform the supply arrangement."
- APA § 6.7: "Seller shall use those forecasts only to perform this Section 6.7." The word "confidential" does not appear.

**Why it matters:**

The APA limits Seller's *use* of the forecasts but does not expressly label them as confidential information subject to APA § 6.3 (Confidentiality). Section 6.3 obligates Seller to keep confidential "all information relating to the Business and the Purchased Assets, and all information received from Buyer under this Agreement." One could argue the forecasts are covered by § 6.3's broad language, but the explicit "confidential" designation in the LOI was likely intended to make the point unambiguous, particularly because the forecasts "may be derived from Buyer's customer volume forecasts" (APA § 6.7), which could reveal Soupen's own commercial strategy.

**Recommended fix:**

Add to APA § 6.7: "The forecasts provided under this Section 6.7 shall be treated as confidential information of Buyer subject to Section 6.3."
