## Summary

Review of the Asset Purchase Agreement (APA), Side Letter, form Bill of Sale, Letter of Intent (LOI), and Ostrander insurance certificate cover letter reveals several material issues that leave Soupen significantly more exposed than the LOI contemplated. The two most serious are (1) a Side Letter provision that globally redefines "Losses" to exclude remediation costs, investigation costs, attorneys' fees, and damages from the indemnification package, and (2) a 12-month claims deadline that contradicts the LOI's agreement to extend environmental survival to the applicable statute of limitations. Both issues appear to deviate from the commercial deal struck in the LOI and should be raised immediately.

---

## Issue 1 (Critical): Side Letter Globally Redefines "Losses," Gutting the Indemnification Package

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.2; APA § 1.1 (definition of "Losses"); APA Art. VIII (indemnification); LOI § 4.3.

**Explanation:**

The APA defines "Losses" in § 1.1 as "damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees." The LOI confirms this scope at § 4.3. However, Side Letter § 2.2 provides:

> "For all purposes of the Agreement and this Side Letter, 'Losses' means only out-of-pocket fines and penalties paid to a Governmental Authority."

Because APA § 11.9 gives the Side Letter precedence over the Agreement, this redefinition controls. The phrase "for all purposes of the Agreement" means every reference to "Losses" throughout the APA is now limited to fines and penalties paid to a government body. This affects:

- **§ 8.2(a)** (breach of reps/warranties): Soupen cannot recover investigation costs, remediation costs, attorneys' fees, or consequential damages for any misrepresentation, including the environmental reps in § 4.12.
- **§ 8.2(b)** (Excluded Liabilities / Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities): Even though the *trigger* for indemnity is broad (any Pre-Closing Environmental Liability), the *recoverable amount* is limited to fines and penalties paid to a Governmental Authority. If TCEQ or the EPA orders Soupen to remediate a plume migrating from the Facility, those remediation costs are not "Losses" and are not recoverable.
- **§ 8.2(c)** (breach of covenants): If Seller fails to perform the transitional supply obligation (§ 6.7) and Soupen must buy feedstock at a higher price from a third party, the price differential is not a "fine or penalty paid to a Governmental Authority" and is unrecoverable.

**Why it matters:** For a chemical blending facility with an active TCEQ notice of violation involving stormwater contamination from the tank farm, the most likely and costly exposures are investigation and remediation—not fines. This redefinition strips Soupen of recovery for precisely the costs it is most likely to incur. It also renders the transitional supply covenant (§ 6.7) effectively unenforceable through indemnification.

**Recommended fix:** Delete Side Letter § 2.2 in its entirety. If the parties intended a narrower definition for the Pasadena NOV indemnity specifically, limit it to "For purposes of Section 2.1 of this Side Letter" and restore the APA's full definition of "Losses" for all other purposes. Alternatively, add a carve-out: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, 'Losses' retains the meaning set forth in Section 1.1 of the Agreement for purposes of Sections 8.2(a) and 8.2(c) of the Agreement."

## Issue 2 (Critical): 12-Month Claims Deadline for Environmental Indemnity Contradicts the LOI

**Where:** APA § 8.1 (survival and claims deadline); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations"); LOI § 4.4(c).

**Explanation:**

LOI § 4.4(c) states:

> "Given the nature of operations at a chemical blending facility, the Parties have agreed that Seller's environmental representations and Seller's indemnity for Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities, would survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations."

The APA does not implement this agreement. Instead:

- "Fundamental Representations" (§ 1.1) are limited to § 4.1 (Organization), § 4.2 (Authority), § 4.4 (Title), and § 4.14 (Brokers). The environmental reps in § 4.12 are **not** Fundamental.
- § 8.1 provides that general reps survive 12 months and that "[a]ll claims for indemnification under this Article VIII, **including claims under Section 8.2(b)**, must be asserted by written notice delivered before the expiry of twelve (12) months after the Closing Date," with the only exception being Fundamental Representation claims.

The result: Soupen has 12 months from closing to assert any claim for a Pre-Closing Environmental Liability under § 8.2(b), any breach of the § 4.12 environmental reps under § 8.2(a), or any other non-Fundamental indemnity claim. After 12 months, the right to indemnification is extinguished regardless of when the environmental condition manifests.

**Why it matters:** Environmental contamination at a chemical facility (groundwater plumes, soil contamination, asbestos, legacy wastes) frequently does not become apparent for years. A 12-month window is commercially unreasonable for this asset type and directly contradicts the deal the parties agreed to in the LOI. Combined with Issue 1, Soupen's environmental protection is reduced to: 12 months to claim, and only fines/penalties to a government are recoverable.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 8.1 to provide that (a) the § 4.12 environmental representations survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations, and (b) claims under § 8.2(b) (Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities) may be asserted at any time within that extended survival period. This aligns the APA with LOI § 4.4(c).

## Issue 3 (High): Lakeport Municipal Water District Consent Omitted from Schedule 4.5 and Not a Closing Condition

**Where:** APA Schedule 4.5 ("None"); APA § 7.2(d); APA § 4.5; LOI §§ 3.1–3.3.

**Explanation:**

LOI § 3.1 identifies the Lakeport Supply Contract as "the Business's largest customer contract by revenue" and states that Soupen "regards continuation of that contract on its current terms as central to its valuation of the Business." LOI § 3.2 confirms the contract requires the District's prior written consent for assignment or change of control. LOI § 3.3(b)–(d) expressly provides that the definitive agreement would (b) disclose the consent as a required third-party consent and (d) make receipt of the consent a condition to Buyer's obligation to close.

In the APA, Schedule 4.5 lists "None." Section 7.2(d) (condition to Buyer's closing) requires only that "Seller has obtained each of the consents listed in Schedule 4.5"—which is automatically satisfied when the schedule is empty. Section 4.5 (Seller's rep) states no third-party consent is required "except as set out in Schedule 4.5"—again, none.

**Why it matters:** If the Lakeport contract is void or terminable upon assignment without the District's consent, Soupen could close, pay $48.5 million, and lose its largest revenue stream. Because the consent is not a closing condition, Seller can walk Soupen into closing without it. If the District later asserts the assignment was ineffective, Soupen has no walk-away right and its only recourse is an indemnity claim (itself impaired by Issues 1 and 2).

**Recommended fix:** (a) Add the Lakeport consent to Schedule 4.5; (b) confirm § 7.2(d) operates as a condition to Buyer's closing obligation with respect to that consent; and (c) consider adding a specific condition that the Lakeport Supply Contract remains in full force and effect with no notice of termination or non-renewal as of the Closing Date.

## Issue 4 (High): Irreconcilable Governing Law and Jurisdiction Conflict Between APA and Side Letter

**Where:** APA §§ 11.7–11.8 (New York law; New York County courts); Side Letter § 5 (Texas law; Harris County courts); APA § 11.9 (Side Letter prevails).

**Explanation:**

APA § 11.7 selects New York law and § 11.8 selects exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. Side Letter § 5 selects Texas law and exclusive jurisdiction in Harris County, Texas. Critically, Side Letter § 5 does not limit its scope to the Side Letter itself; it provides that "This Side Letter, **and any dispute arising out of or relating to the Agreement or the transactions contemplated by it**," is governed by Texas law in Harris County.

APA § 11.9 states the Side Letter prevails over the Agreement in case of conflict. Read together, the Side Letter appears to override the APA's New York governing law and jurisdiction for *all* disputes relating to the Agreement, not just the Side Letter.

**Why it matters:** This creates a threshold litigation risk. If a dispute arises (e.g., over the scope of the "Losses" redefinition or the environmental survival period), the parties will first have to litigate whether New York or Texas law applies and which court has jurisdiction. Texas and New York differ on key contract interpretation principles, indemnification enforceability, and environmental regulatory preemption. The uncertainty itself is a negotiating disadvantage for Soupen, which is a New York corporation with its principal office in New York.

**Recommended fix:** Conform Side Letter § 5 to the APA: delete the reference to "the Agreement or the transactions contemplated by it" so that Texas law applies only to interpretation of the Side Letter itself (if that is the commercial intent), or—preferably—conform the Side Letter to New York law and New York County jurisdiction to match the APA. If the parties genuinely want bifurcated governing law, the Side Letter should say "This Side Letter shall be governed by Texas law" without sweeping in the Agreement.

## Issue 5 (Moderate): Seller's Unilateral Right to Assign the Agreement and Side Letter

**Where:** APA § 11.4; Side Letter § 6.2 (incorporates § 11.4 by reference).

**Explanation:**

APA § 11.4 provides: "Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights, or delegate any of its obligations, under this Agreement without the prior written consent of Seller. **Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights, or delegate any of its obligations, under this Agreement to any Person without the consent of Buyer.**"

Side Letter § 6.2 extends this to the Side Letter.

**Why it matters:** Soupen's post-closing protection depends on Seller's ability and willingness to pay indemnification obligations. If Seller assigns the Agreement (including its indemnification obligations under Art. VIII and the Side Letter's Pasadena NOV indemnity) to an affiliate, a buyer of Seller's own assets, or an entity in financial distress, Soupen's recovery could be impaired. The clause permits assignment to "any Person" with no financial-ability requirement, no notice to Buyer, and no consent right.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 11.4 to require that any assignee of Seller's indemnification obligations (a) have net worth of not less than [agreed amount], (b) assume the obligations in writing, and (c) not be in bankruptcy or insolvency proceedings. Alternatively, provide that Seller may not assign or delegate its obligations under Article VIII or the Side Letter without Buyer's prior written consent.

## Issue 6 (Moderate): Soupen Is Not an Additional Insured Under Seller's Existing Policies

**Where:** Ostrander cover letter § 4; APA § 2.2 (insurance policies are Excluded Assets); APA § 8.4(d).

**Explanation:**

The Ostrander letter confirms that "Soupen Water Solutions, Inc. is shown as certificate holder for informational purposes. It is **not named as an additional insured or loss payee** under any policy." The APA excludes Seller's insurance policies from the Purchased Assets (§ 2.2), so they remain with Seller.

**Why it matters:** For any pre-closing environmental condition that manifests after closing, Seller's pollution legal liability policy (identified in the Ostrander letter § 1(b)) is the most likely source of insurance recovery. But Soupen cannot access that policy directly. Soupen must rely on: (a) Seller's indemnity under § 8.2(b) (itself impaired by Issues 1 and 2), or (b) Seller's cooperation in making a claim under its own policy. If Seller becomes insolvent or uncooperative after closing, Soupen has no independent right to the insurance proceeds.

Additionally, § 8.4(d) provides that Losses are calculated "net of any insurance proceeds actually recovered by the Indemnified Party." Because Soupen is not an additional insured, it will not "actually recover" insurance proceeds, so this clause is less of a direct threat—but it does mean Soupen bears the full cost of any loss that Seller's insurance would have covered, with no subrogation or direct access.

**Recommended fix:** Require Seller, as a condition to closing or in the Escrow Agreement, to (a) add Soupen as an additional insured under the pollution legal liability policy for pre-closing conditions, or (b) assign to Soupen the benefit of any insurance recovery for pre-closing environmental losses, or (c) extend the escrow period to match the policy's retention period. At minimum, add a covenant requiring Seller to cooperate in good faith with any insurance claim Soupen directs Seller to make under its pre-closing policies.

## Issue 7 (Moderate): Escrow Release at 12 Months Provides No Security Beyond the Claims Deadline

**Where:** APA § 8.6; APA § 8.1; LOI § 2.2.

**Explanation:**

APA § 8.6 provides that the Indemnity Escrow Amount ($2,425,000) is released to Seller 12 months after closing "not subject to a pending claim." APA § 8.1 requires all non-Fundamental claims to be asserted within 12 months. The two provisions are coextensive: the escrow is released at the same moment the claims window closes.

LOI § 2.2 contemplated the escrow as security for a longer-tail environmental indemnity: "That amount would be held for twelve (12) months as security for Seller's indemnification obligations, but would not limit them." The LOI's "would not limit them" language assumed the indemnity survived longer than 12 months (per LOI § 4.4(c)). In the APA as drafted, the indemnity *does* effectively end at 12 months, making the "would not limit" language moot.

**Why it matters:** After month 12, Soupen has no escrow security, no ability to bring new claims (per § 8.1), and must rely on Seller's general credit for any Fundamental Rep claims only. For environmental exposure, the protection is zero after 12 months.

**Recommended fix:** If Issue 2 is fixed (extending environmental survival), the escrow term should be extended accordingly, or a portion of the escrow (e.g., 50%) should be held for the extended environmental survival period. Alternatively, require Seller to maintain a pollution legal liability policy or provide a letter of credit for the extended period.

## Issue 8 (Moderate): Side Letter Corrective Measures Not Expressly a Closing Condition

**Where:** Side Letter § 3; APA § 7.2 (conditions to Buyer's closing).

**Explanation:**

Side Letter § 3 requires Seller to complete, before closing, (a) repair of the secondary containment liner and (b) update of the stormwater pollution prevention plan. Seller must deliver "reasonable written evidence" of completion "together with the officer's certificate required by Section 7.2(c)."

However, § 7.2 does not list completion of the Side Letter corrective measures as a freestanding condition to Buyer's closing obligation. The only potential hook is § 7.2(b) (Seller has performed in all material respects all covenants required to be performed before or at the Closing). Whether the Side Letter § 3 obligations qualify as "covenants required to be performed before the Closing" under § 7.2(b) is arguable but not certain.

**Why it matters:** If Seller has not completed the liner repair or SWPPP update by the time all other conditions are satisfied, Soupen may lack a clear contractual basis to refuse to close. Given that the liner failure is the subject of the active TCEQ NOV, closing without the repair in place risks (a) continued non-compliance, (b) escalation of the NOV, and (c) potential personal liability for Soupen as the new Facility owner.

**Recommended fix:** Add to § 7.2 a new subsection: "(g) Seller has completed the corrective measures described in Section 3 of the Side Letter and delivered reasonable written evidence of completion to Buyer." This makes the repair an express condition to Buyer's closing obligation.

## Issue 10 (Lower): Transitional Supply Covenant Lacks a Specific Remedy

**Where:** APA § 6.7; APA § 8.2(c); Side Letter § 2.2 (narrowed "Losses").

**Explanation:**

Section 6.7 obligates Seller to supply ferric chloride feedstock for 18 months at cost plus 6%. If Seller fails to supply, Soupen's remedy is an indemnity claim under § 8.2(c) for breach of covenant. However, because Side Letter § 2.2 redefines "Losses" to mean only fines and penalties paid to a Governmental Authority, the price Soupen must pay to source feedstock from an alternative supplier is not a recoverable "Loss." The covenant is therefore difficult to enforce through the indemnification mechanism.

**Recommended fix:** Add to § 6.7 an express remedy: "If Seller fails to deliver feedstock in accordance with this Section 6.7, Seller shall pay Buyer the difference between the price actually paid by Buyer for substitute feedstock and the price that would have applied under this Section 6.7, plus any additional transportation costs." Alternatively, carve § 6.7 breaches out of the Side Letter's "Losses" redefinition.
