## Summary

Review of the Asset Purchase Agreement (APA), Side Letter, form Bill of Sale, Letter of Intent (LOI), and Ostrander insurance certificate cover letter reveals several material issues that leave Soupen exposed. The three most critical are: (1) a Side Letter provision that appears to globally redefine "Losses" to exclude remediation, investigation, and attorney fee recoveries; (2) the omission of the Lakeport Municipal Water District consent from Schedule 4.5, eliminating the closing condition the LOI promised; and (3) a 12-month survival period for environmental representations and Excluded Liability indemnity, contrary to the LOI's agreement of 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations. Details and recommended fixes follow, ranked by significance.

---

## Issue 1 – Side Letter Globally Narrows the Definition of "Losses"

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.2; APA § 1.1 (definition of "Losses"); APA § 11.9 (order of precedence)

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 1.1 defines "Losses" as "damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees."
- LOI § 4.3 confirms: "Indemnifiable losses would include damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees."
- Side Letter § 2.2 states: *"For all purposes of the Agreement and this Side Letter, 'Losses' means only out-of-pocket fines and penalties paid to a Governmental Authority."*
- APA § 11.9 provides: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency, the Side Letter prevails over this Agreement."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

As drafted, Side Letter § 2.2 uses the phrase "for all purposes of the Agreement and this Side Letter," which on its face overrides the APA's broader definition of "Losses" globally. Because the Side Letter prevails under APA § 11.9, a court could hold that for *every* indemnification claim under the APA—breach of representations, Excluded Liabilities, breach of covenants—Soupen may recover only out-of-pocket fines actually paid to a government. This would eliminate recovery for:

- Environmental investigation and remediation costs (potentially the largest category at a chemical blending facility);
- Attorneys' fees;
- Third-party damages (e.g., property damage, personal injury);
- Fines that are assessed but not yet paid.

Even if a court reads § 2.2 narrowly as applying only to the Pasadena NOV indemnity in Side Letter § 2, the practical effect is still severe: the NOV alleges stormwater discharge exceedances and recordkeeping deficiencies. The most likely costs are remediation (containment liner repair, SWPPP compliance, ongoing monitoring) and legal fees—not merely a fine check. Soupen would bear those costs with no indemnity recovery.

**Recommended fix:**

Delete Side Letter § 2.2 in its entirety. If the parties intended a narrower definition for the Pasadena NOV specifically, restate it as: "For purposes of Section 2 of this Side Letter only, 'Losses' means [agreed scope]." Confirm that the APA § 1.1 definition of "Losses" applies to all other claims. Alternatively, if the concern is limiting recovery to government-imposed amounts for the NOV, negotiate a definition that at minimum includes "investigation and remediation costs and reasonable attorneys' fees" in addition to fines and penalties.

## Issue 2 – Lakeport Consent Missing from Schedule 4.5; No Closing Condition

**Where:** APA Schedule 4.5; APA § 4.5; APA § 7.2(d); LOI §§ 3.1–3.3; Bill of Sale § 2.1(c) and Annex 1

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 3.1: The Lakeport Supply Contract is "the Business's largest customer contract by revenue" and "continuation of that contract on its current terms [is] central to [Soupen's] valuation."
- LOI § 3.2: "the Lakeport Supply Contract requires the District's prior written consent for assignment or change of control of the supplier."
- LOI § 3.3(b): "the definitive agreement would disclose the District's consent as a required third-party consent."
- LOI § 3.3(d): "receipt of that consent would be a condition to Buyer's obligation to close."
- APA Schedule 4.5: **"None."**
- APA § 4.5: "No consent of any third party is required … except as set out in Schedule 4.5."
- APA § 7.2(d): Closing condition is "Seller has obtained each of the consents listed in Schedule 4.5" (i.e., none).
- Bill of Sale § 2.1(c) and Annex 1: The Lakeport Supply Contract is listed as an Assumed Contract to be conveyed at Closing.

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

The LOI expressly identified the Lakeport consent as a deal-critical item and required it to be (a) disclosed in the definitive agreement and (b) a condition to closing. The APA omits it entirely. The practical consequences:

1. **No closing condition.** Soupen is obligated to close even if the Lakeport consent has not been obtained. If the District refuses or does not respond, Soupen still must close (absent an MAE or other condition being triggered).
2. **Affirmative misrepresentation risk.** APA § 4.5 represents that no third-party consents are required except as listed in Schedule 4.5. If the Lakeport contract in fact requires consent (as Seller told Soupen in the LOI), this representation is false at signing. However, because it is not a Fundamental Representation, any claim is subject to the 12-month survival and the $485,000 basket.
3. **Ineffective assignment.** If the Lakeport contract prohibits assignment without consent and no consent is obtained, the assignment in the Bill of Sale may be a nullity. Soupen would pay $48.5M for a business whose largest revenue contract it does not actually hold.
4. **No specific indemnity.** The failure to obtain the consent would be a breach of § 4.5 (a general representation), not an Excluded Liability or covenant breach, so it falls within the basket and cap.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add the Lakeport Municipal Water District consent to Schedule 4.5.
- Add a specific closing condition in § 7.2 (new subsection) requiring delivery of the Lakeport consent in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.
- Alternatively, if the parties believe consent is not actually required (e.g., the contract has been amended), require Seller to deliver a legal opinion or the relevant contract provision at Closing confirming no consent is needed.
- Until resolved, treat the absence of this consent as a material open item that should delay Closing.

## Issue 3 – 12-Month Survival for Environmental Reps and Excluded Liabilities (Contrary to LOI)

**Where:** APA § 8.1; APA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations"); LOI § 4.4(c); APA § 2.4(a); APA § 8.2(b)

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 4.4(c): "Given the nature of operations at a chemical blending facility, the Parties have agreed that Seller's environmental representations and Seller's indemnity for Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities, would survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations."
- APA § 1.1: "Fundamental Representations" = §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, and 4.14 only. Section 4.12 (Environmental Matters) is *not* a Fundamental Representation.
- APA § 8.1: General reps survive 12 months. Fundamental Reps survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations. "All claims for indemnification under this Article VIII, **including claims under Section 8.2(b)**, must be asserted by written notice delivered before the expiry of twelve (12) months after the Closing Date, other than claims for breach of a Fundamental Representation …"
- APA § 2.4(a): Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities are Excluded Liabilities.
- APA § 8.2(b): Seller indemnifies for Excluded Liabilities.

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

The LOI specifically negotiated an extended survival for environmental matters given the chemical nature of the business. The APA does not implement this agreement. As drafted:

- Soupen has only **12 months after Closing** to assert any claim for a Pre-Closing Environmental Liability, even though environmental contamination (e.g., migrating groundwater plumes, latent soil contamination) often does not manifest for years.
- After 12 months, Soupen retains direct liability as the current owner/operator under CERCLA, RCRA, Texas Solid Waste Disposal Act, and TCEQ enforcement authority—but has no contractual indemnity from Seller.
- The environmental representation in § 4.12 (compliance since 2016, no releases, no USTs, etc.) also expires at 12 months, even though it covers a 10-year look-back period.
- The escrow (5% / $2.425M) is released at 12 months (APA § 8.6), so there is no security for any environmental claim that surfaces after that date.

For a chemical blending facility with an active TCEQ NOV (see Issue 5), this is the single largest structural gap between what the parties agreed in the LOI and what the APA delivers.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add Section 4.12 (Environmental Matters) to the definition of "Fundamental Representations" in APA § 1.1, *or* create a separate category "Environmental Representations" with survival until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations.
- Amend APA § 8.1 to provide that claims under § 8.2(b) (Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities) may be asserted at any time before the expiry of the applicable statute of limitations (plus 60 days), consistent with LOI § 4.4(c).
- Extend the escrow release date to match the extended survival for environmental claims, or add a separate environmental escrow holdback.
- If Seller resists full statutory-limitations survival, negotiate a minimum of 3–5 years for environmental claims, with the escrow held for that period.

## Issue 4 – Side Letter Imposes Texas Governing Law and Forum, Overriding the APA

**Where:** Side Letter § 5; APA §§ 11.7, 11.8, 11.9; LOI § 12

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 11.7: New York law governs.
- APA § 11.8: Exclusive jurisdiction in New York County; jury waiver.
- LOI § 12: New York law for binding provisions.
- Side Letter § 5: *"This Side Letter, and any dispute arising out of or relating to the Agreement or the transactions contemplated by it, shall be governed by … the laws of the State of Texas … Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state courts located in Harris County, Texas …"*
- APA § 11.9: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency, the Side Letter prevails over this Agreement."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

The Side Letter's governing law clause is not limited to the Side Letter itself—it purports to govern "any dispute arising out of or relating to the Agreement or the transactions contemplated by it." Combined with the precedence rule in APA § 11.9, this means:

- Texas law, not New York law, governs the entire APA, including indemnification, survival, and the interpretation of "Losses."
- Exclusive forum is Harris County, Texas (where Seller is located), not New York County.
- Soupen, a New York corporation, must litigate in Seller's home jurisdiction.
- Texas and New York differ on key issues: interpretation of indemnification clauses, enforceability of survival periods, the effect of disclosure schedules on representations, and the standard for "materiality."

This appears to be a drafting error (the Side Letter likely intended to govern only disputes arising under the Side Letter itself), but as written, with the precedence clause, it controls.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend Side Letter § 5 to limit its scope: "This Side Letter shall be governed by … Texas law. Any dispute arising out of or relating to the Agreement shall be governed by and subject to the jurisdiction set forth in Sections 11.7 and 11.8 of the Agreement."
- Alternatively, conform Side Letter § 5 to New York law and New York County jurisdiction to match the APA.
- If the Texas forum is non-negotiable (e.g., Seller insists), ensure the APA's indemnification and survival provisions are drafted with Texas law in mind and have them reviewed by Texas counsel.

## Issue 5 – Pasadena NOV: Escrow Released at 12 Months But Indemnity Survives Indefinitely

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 2.1, 2.3; APA §§ 8.1, 8.6; Side Letter § 1.2

**What the documents say:**

- Side Letter § 1.2: TCEQ NOV alleges stormwater discharge exceedances (TSS, dissolved iron) and recordkeeping deficiencies at the tank farm containment area, Q1 2026. Unresolved as of the Side Letter date.
- Side Letter § 2.3: "Seller's obligations under this Section 2 survive the Closing until the Pasadena NOV is finally resolved and all amounts payable under this Section 2 have been paid."
- APA § 8.6: "Any portion of the Indemnity Escrow Amount not subject to a pending claim shall be released to Seller on the date that is twelve (12) months after the Closing Date."
- Side Letter § 2.3 also states: "Buyer may recover amounts owed under this Section 2 from the Indemnity Escrow Amount in accordance with Section 8.6 of the Agreement and the Escrow Agreement."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

TCEQ enforcement proceedings commonly take 18–36 months or longer from NOV to final resolution (investigation, administrative penalty hearing, possible judicial review). The escrow is released at 12 months. If the NOV is still pending at month 12:

- If a claim is "pending" at month 12, the portion of the escrow "subject to a pending claim" is retained. But what constitutes a "pending claim" is determined by the Escrow Agreement (not in the folder—see Open Questions). If the Escrow Agreement requires a formal demand before the 12-month date to "hold" escrow funds, and Soupen has not made a formal demand (because the NOV is still in investigation), the full escrow may be released.
- After the escrow is released, Soupen's recovery for the NOV depends entirely on Seller's creditworthiness. If Seller has distributed the purchase price, sold its remaining assets, or become insolvent, the indemnity is an unsecured promise.

Compounded by Issue 1 (narrowed "Losses" definition), even if the NOV results in a $2M remediation order plus a $500K penalty, Soupen may recover only the $500K penalty (and only if actually paid to TCEQ), and only if the escrow hasn't been released.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a carve-out in the escrow release: the portion of the escrow allocable to the Pasadena NOV (or a fixed amount, e.g., $1M) is held until the NOV is finally resolved, regardless of the 12-month date.
- Alternatively, require Seller to post a letter of credit or parent guarantee for the NOV indemnity in lieu of (or in addition to) the escrow.
- Confirm in the Escrow Agreement that a "pending claim" includes any NOV-related matter that has not been finally resolved, even if no formal demand has been made.

## Issue 6 – Asymmetric Assignment Right Favors Seller

**Where:** APA § 11.4

**What the documents say:**

"Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights, or delegate any of its obligations, under this Agreement without the prior written consent of Seller. **Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights, or delegate any of its obligations, under this Agreement to any Person without the consent of Buyer.**"

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

Seller can unilaterally delegate its indemnification obligations (Article VIII, Side Letter § 2) to any third party. If Seller assigns its indemnity obligations to an empty-shell affiliate or a third party with no assets, Soupen's recovery for Excluded Liabilities, environmental claims, or the Pasadena NOV is effectively worthless. The indemnity is only as good as the indemnitor's balance sheet. Meanwhile, Soupen cannot assign its rights (e.g., to a financing affiliate or in a subsequent sale) without Seller's consent.

**Recommended fix:**

- Make the assignment provision mutual: neither party may assign or delegate without the other's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld).
- If Seller insists on assignment flexibility for its *rights* (e.g., to collect the purchase price), limit that to rights and expressly prohibit delegation of indemnification obligations without Buyer's written consent.
- Add a provision that any permitted assignee of Seller's indemnification obligations must have net worth of at least [specified amount] or must provide a guaranty.

## Issue 7 – Soupen Not Additional Insured on Seller's Policies; No Post-Closing Insurance Bridge

**Where:** Ostrander cover letter §§ 3–4; APA §§ 2.2, 8.4(d); APA § 4.13

**What the documents say:**

- Ostrander cover letter § 4: "Soupen Water Solutions, Inc. is shown as certificate holder for informational purposes. **It is not named as an additional insured or loss payee under any policy.**"
- Ostrander cover letter § 3: "The certificates … confer no rights on the certificate holder. They do not amend, extend or alter the coverage provided by the policies."
- APA § 2.2: Seller's insurance policies are Excluded Assets.
- APA § 8.4(d): "Losses shall be calculated net of any insurance proceeds actually recovered by the Indemnified Party in respect of the matter, less the costs of recovery."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

After Closing, Soupen owns and operates the Facility. If a pre-closing environmental condition gives rise to a third-party claim or government enforcement action:

1. Soupen is the named defendant/respondent as current owner.
2. Seller's pollution legal liability policy does not cover Soupen (Soupen is not an additional insured).
3. Soupen's only recourse is the indemnity from Seller (subject to the 12-month assertion deadline per Issue 3, the narrowed "Losses" definition per Issue 1, and Seller's creditworthiness).
4. Section 8.4(d) reduces indemnifiable Losses by insurance proceeds "actually recovered by the Indemnified Party"—but Soupen cannot recover under Seller's policies because it is not an insured. This provision is therefore neutral (no offset), but it confirms the parties contemplated that the Indemnified Party might have its own insurance.
5. Soupen must arrange its own pollution legal liability, CGL, and property insurance effective at Closing. The cost and availability of such coverage for a facility with an active TCEQ NOV may be limited or expensive.

**Recommended fix:**

- Require Seller to add Soupen as an additional insured on the pollution legal liability and CGL policies for a tail period (e.g., 3–5 years post-Closing) to cover pre-closing conditions, at Seller's cost. This is standard in environmental asset purchases.
- Alternatively, require Seller to maintain the existing policies for a specified post-closing period and to name Soupen as additional insured during that period.
- If Seller resists, increase the escrow or require a separate environmental insurance policy (e.g., a cost cap or pollution legal liability policy) placed at Closing with Soupen as the named insured, with the premium paid by Seller or deducted from the purchase price.
- Confirm that Soupen's own insurance program is in place before Closing.

## Issue 8 – No Working Capital Adjustment Mechanism

**Where:** LOI § 2.1; APA §§ 2.3, 2.5; APA § 2.1(f)

**What the documents say:**

- LOI § 2.1: "The price assumes delivery of the Business on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normal level of working capital."
- APA § 2.5: Fixed Purchase Price of $48,500,000. No adjustment mechanism.
- APA § 2.1(f): Only receivables "arising after the date of this Agreement" are Purchased Assets.
- APA § 2.3(b): Assumed Liabilities include "trade accounts payable … incurred in the ordinary course after the date of this Agreement and reflected in the closing statement."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

The LOI contemplated a working-capital-based price, but the APA uses a fixed price with no true-up. Pre-signing receivables are excluded (Seller keeps them), and pre-signing payables are Excluded Liabilities (Seller keeps them). If the business had, for example, $3M in receivables at signing that are excluded from the deal, the effective value transferred to Soupen is less than the $48.5M price implies. Conversely, if inventory levels at Closing are significantly below what was assumed in pricing, Soupen overpays. There is no mechanism to adjust.

The reference to a "closing statement" in § 2.3(b) is unmoored—no section of the APA describes how the closing statement is prepared, what items it includes, or how disputes are resolved.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a closing working capital adjustment: define target net working capital (current assets minus current liabilities, excluding cash and debt) based on the LOI's assumption, and provide for a dollar-for-dollar adjustment to the Purchase Price based on the excess or deficiency at Closing.
- Specify the mechanics: who prepares the closing statement, the timeline, the dispute resolution process (e.g., independent accountant), and the holdback or escrow for disputed amounts.
- Alternatively, if the fixed price is intentional, confirm that the purchase price was negotiated with full knowledge of the working capital position and that both parties accept the risk. Document this in the APA.

## Issue 9 – Ambiguity Over Post-Closing Control of NOV Remediation

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.4; APA § 8.5 (final sentence)

**What the documents say:**

- Side Letter § 2.4: "After the Closing, Seller shall cooperate with Buyer in responding to the Pasadena NOV, and Section 8.5 of the Agreement applies to its defense and settlement."
- APA § 8.5 (final sentence): "Buyer shall control the investigation and remediation of any environmental condition at the Facility that is the subject of a claim under **Section 8.2(b)**."
- The Pasadena NOV indemnity is under Side Letter § 2, not APA § 8.2(b).

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

As the post-Closing owner of the Facility, Soupen needs to control any remediation work (e.g., additional containment repairs, monitoring, SWPPP implementation) to protect its operations and limit liability. If Section 8.5's defense-control provisions apply to the NOV but the remediation-control sentence does not (because it references § 8.2(b) specifically), Seller could assume the "defense" of the NOV and direct remediation in a manner that is inadequate or that imposes operating restrictions on the Facility.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend APA § 8.5 to add: "…or that is the subject of a claim under Section 2 of the Side Letter."
- Alternatively, add to Side Letter § 2.4: "Notwithstanding anything in Section 8.5 of the Agreement to the contrary, Buyer shall control the investigation and remediation of any environmental condition at the Facility that is the subject of a claim under this Section 2."

## Issue 10 – Side Letter Corrective Measures Not an Express Closing Condition

**Where:** Side Letter § 3; APA § 7.2

**What the documents say:**

- Side Letter § 3: Before Closing, Seller shall complete (a) repair of the secondary containment liner and (b) update of the SWPPP, at its own cost, and deliver "reasonable written evidence" of completion.
- APA § 7.2: Closing conditions include (b) "Seller has performed in all material respects all covenants required to be performed by it before or at the Closing."
- The Side Letter is incorporated by reference (APA § 1.1 defines "Side Letter"; APA § 11.9 gives it precedence).

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

The corrective measures are covenants in the Side Letter, so they are arguably covered by the § 7.2(b) closing condition (performance of covenants "in all material respects"). However:

- The "material respects" qualifier means a partial or incomplete repair might not give Soupen a walk right.
- "Reasonable written evidence" is a lower standard than a certified completion report.
- There is no specific condition requiring the liner repair and SWPPP update to be complete and verified.

Given that the liner failure is the subject of the active TCEQ NOV, incomplete repair at Closing could result in immediate re-violation and further enforcement action.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a specific closing condition in APA § 7.2: "Seller has completed the corrective measures described in Section 3 of the Side Letter and has delivered to Buyer a certificate of a licensed engineer or environmental professional confirming completion."
- Raise the evidence standard from "reasonable written evidence" to a professional certification.

## Issue 11 – Bulk Sales Waiver Creates Creditor Exposure

**Where:** APA § 6.9; APA § 2.4 (Excluded Liabilities)

**What the documents say:**

- APA § 6.9: "The Parties waive compliance with any applicable bulk sales Laws. Any liability arising from non-compliance is an Excluded Liability."

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

Texas has a bulk sales statute (Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 1.401 et seq., though significantly revised by the UCC Article 9 amendments). If Seller's creditors argue that the asset transfer constitutes a bulk sale and they were not properly notified, they may have claims against the transferred assets. While the APA makes any such liability an Excluded Liability (indemnifiable by Seller), the practical risk is that a creditor could obtain a lien or attachment on the Purchased Assets in Soupen's hands before Soupen can collect from Seller. For a $48.5M asset purchase, even a modest creditor claim could disrupt operations.

**Recommended fix:**

- Require Seller to provide a bulk sales compliance certificate or, if the Texas bulk sales law no longer applies (post-UCC Article 9 revision), a legal opinion confirming non-applicability.
- Add a specific representation that no creditor claims have been made or are threatened based on the transfer.
- Ensure the Lien release deliverable in § 3.2(a)(vii) includes a search confirming no bulk sales-related filings.

## Issue 12 – No Express Closing Statement Mechanism

**Where:** APA § 2.3(b) (reference to "the closing statement"); no other section addresses preparation or dispute resolution

**What the documents say:**

APA § 2.3(b) references trade payables "reflected in the closing statement," but no section of the APA defines what the closing statement includes, who prepares it, the timeline for preparation, or how disputes are resolved.

**Why it matters for Soupen:**

Without a defined mechanism, there is no process for agreeing on the amount of Assumed Liabilities at Closing. If Seller includes inflated payables in the closing statement, Soupen has no contractual basis to dispute them. Conversely, if the closing statement is ambiguous, the allocation of the Purchase Price (and thus the tax treatment under § 2.6) could be disputed.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a section (e.g., § 3.3) describing the closing statement: items to be included (working capital accounts, assumed payables, prepaid expenses, accrued liabilities), preparation timeline (e.g., Seller delivers draft 5 business days before Closing), dispute resolution (e.g., unresolved items referred to an independent accountant), and effect on the Purchase Price.
