**Re: Letter of Intent: Proposed Acquisition of the Water Treatment Chemicals Business of Faiveirt Chemical Company**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

This letter of intent (this "**Letter**") sets out the principal terms on which Soupen Water Solutions, Inc., a Delaware corporation ("**Buyer**"), proposes to acquire from Faiveirt Chemical Company, a Texas corporation ("**Seller**"), substantially all of the assets of Seller's business of blending and distributing water treatment chemicals (the "**Business**"). The Business includes coagulants, corrosion inhibitors and scale inhibitors, and it is conducted from Seller's blending facility in Pasadena, Texas (the "**Facility**"). The transaction described in this Letter is the "**Proposed Transaction**." Buyer and Seller are each a "**Party**" and together the "**Parties**."

Except for Sections 8 through 13, this Letter is a non-binding expression of the Parties' present intentions. It does not create any obligation to complete the Proposed Transaction.

## 1. Structure of the Transaction

1.1 Buyer would acquire substantially all of the assets used in the Business. These assets would include:

(a) the Facility, including the owned real property, buildings, tank farm, blending and packaging lines, laboratory, warehouse and loading racks;

(b) inventory;

(c) the customer, supplier and other contracts of the Business that Buyer elects to assume (the "**Assumed Contracts**");

(d) transferable permits;

(e) product formulations and other intellectual property used exclusively in the Business; and

(f) the books and records of the Business, including environmental monitoring records.

1.2 Cash, Seller's corporate records, Seller's insurance policies and pre-closing Tax refunds would be excluded assets.

1.3 Buyer would assume only the post-closing performance obligations under the Assumed Contracts and ordinary-course trade payables incurred after signing of the definitive agreement. Seller would retain all other liabilities of the Business. These retained liabilities would include all liabilities arising under environmental laws from the ownership or operation of the Business or the Facility before closing, whether known or unknown at closing ("**Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities**") (together, the "**Excluded Liabilities**").

## 2. Purchase Price

2.1 The purchase price would be $48,500,000, payable in cash at closing. The price assumes delivery of the Business on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normal level of working capital. It is subject to Buyer's satisfactory completion of due diligence.

2.2 An amount equal to five percent (5%) of the purchase price would be deposited with a mutually acceptable escrow agent at closing. That amount would be held for twelve (12) months as security for Seller's indemnification obligations, but would not limit them.

## 3. Key Customer Contracts and Third-Party Consents

3.1 Seller has advised Buyer that its supply contract with Lakeport Municipal Water District for coagulants and corrosion inhibitors (the "**Lakeport Supply Contract**") is the Business's largest customer contract by revenue. Buyer regards continuation of that contract on its current terms as central to its valuation of the Business.

3.2 Seller has advised Buyer that the Lakeport Supply Contract requires the District's prior written consent for assignment or change of control of the supplier.

3.3 The Parties intend the following:

(a) the Lakeport Supply Contract would be an Assumed Contract;

(b) the definitive agreement would disclose the District's consent as a required third-party consent;

(c) Seller would undertake to request and use its commercially reasonable efforts to obtain that consent, in form reasonably satisfactory to Buyer, before closing; and

(d) receipt of that consent would be a condition to Buyer's obligation to close.

3.4 Seller will identify to Buyer during due diligence any other Assumed Contract, permit or registration that requires a third-party or governmental consent, notice or reissuance in connection with the Proposed Transaction.

## 4. Representations, Warranties and Indemnification

4.1 The definitive agreement would contain representations, warranties, covenants and indemnities customary for a transaction of this type. These would include representations on:

(a) organization and authority;

(b) title to and sufficiency of assets;

(c) financial statements;

(d) material contracts;

(e) permits;

(f) litigation;

(g) compliance with laws;

(h) employees;

(i) insurance;

(j) brokers; and

(k) environmental matters relating to the Business and the Facility.

4.2 Seller would indemnify Buyer for the following:

(a) breaches of Seller's representations, warranties and covenants; and

(b) all Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities.

4.3 Indemnifiable losses would include damages, fines, penalties, investigation and remediation costs, and reasonable attorneys' fees.

4.4 Survival would be as follows:

(a) Seller's general representations and warranties would survive for twelve (12) months after closing.

(b) Seller's representations on organization, authority, title and brokers would survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations.

(c) Given the nature of operations at a chemical blending facility, the Parties have agreed that Seller's environmental representations and Seller's indemnity for Excluded Liabilities, including Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities, would survive until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations.

4.5 Indemnity claims for breach of general representations would be subject to the following:

(a) a deductible-to-first-dollar basket equal to one percent (1%) of the purchase price; and

(b) a cap equal to fifteen percent (15%) of the purchase price.

4.6 Neither the basket nor the cap would apply to the following:

(a) claims for breach of the representations described in Section 4.4(b);

(b) claims for fraud; or

(c) claims for Excluded Liabilities.

4.7 Buyer would control the investigation and remediation of any environmental condition at the Facility that is the subject of an indemnity claim.

## 5. Transitional Supply and Non-Competition

5.1 For eighteen (18) months after closing, Seller would supply Buyer at the Facility with ferric chloride feedstock at Seller's cost plus a margin to be agreed, in volumes consistent with the Business's historical consumption.

5.2 Any volume forecasts that Buyer provides to Seller for this purpose would be confidential. Seller would use those forecasts only to perform the supply arrangement.

5.3 For five (5) years after closing, Seller and its affiliates would not engage in the blending or distribution of water treatment coagulants, corrosion inhibitors or scale inhibitors in the United States, or solicit customers of the Business for those products.

## 6. Due Diligence and Conditions

6.1 The Proposed Transaction would be subject to the following:

(a) Buyer's satisfactory completion of business, financial, legal and environmental due diligence, including a Phase I environmental site assessment of the Facility and, if Buyer considers it appropriate, a Phase II assessment;

(b) negotiation and execution of a mutually acceptable definitive asset purchase agreement;

(c) receipt of required third-party consents, including the consent described in Section 3;

(d) the absence of any material adverse change in the Business; and

(e) approval by the board of directors of each Party.

6.2 Seller will give Buyer and its advisors reasonable access to the Facility, the books and records of the Business, and key employees.

## 7. Timing

The Parties intend to use good faith efforts to negotiate and sign a definitive agreement by August 31, 2026. They intend to close as promptly as practicable after the closing conditions are satisfied.

## 8. Exclusivity

From the date of countersignature of this Letter until the earlier of (a) 11:59 p.m. New York time on August 31, 2026 and (b) the date on which Buyer notifies Seller in writing that it is terminating discussions (the "**Exclusivity Period**"), Seller shall not, and shall cause its affiliates and representatives not to, do any of the following:

(a) solicit, encourage, negotiate or enter into any agreement with any person other than Buyer regarding any sale of the Business, the Facility or any material portion of the assets of the Business; or

(b) provide information to any person other than Buyer for that purpose.

Seller shall promptly notify Buyer of any inquiry or proposal it receives during the Exclusivity Period on these matters.

## 9. Confidentiality

The terms of this Letter, and the fact that the Parties are in discussions, are confidential. Neither Party will disclose them except to its directors, officers, employees, lenders and professional advisors who need to know them, or as required by law. Information exchanged in due diligence is also subject to this Section 9.

## 10. Expenses

Each Party will bear its own costs and expenses in connection with this Letter and the Proposed Transaction, whether or not the Proposed Transaction is completed.

## 11. Non-Binding Effect

11.1 Except for Sections 8 through 13, this Letter is not a binding agreement. No Party will have any obligation for the Proposed Transaction unless and until a definitive agreement is executed and delivered, and then only on the terms of that agreement.

11.2 Either Party may terminate discussions at any time, for any reason, by written notice to the other.

## 12. Governing Law

The binding provisions of this Letter are governed by the laws of the State of New York, without regard to conflict of law principles that would require the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction.
