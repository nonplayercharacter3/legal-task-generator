## Issue 1 – Key Employee Agreement Cannot Be Assigned Without Oyelaran's Consent; Restrictive Covenants Terminate on a Void Assignment

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 2.1(c) – lists the Key Employee Agreement as an Assigned Contract
- KEA § 8.3 – prohibits assignment without Executive's prior written consent; any purported assignment without consent is **void**, and the Section 6 restrictive covenants **terminate**
- APA § 2.8 – "Non-Assignable Assets" fallback (hold for benefit, cooperate)

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
The APA treats the KEA as an asset being purchased. But the KEA itself flatly prohibits assignment without Oyelaran's consent and provides that any assignment made without it is void and causes the non-compete (18 months, four-state territory), customer non-solicit, and employee non-solicit to terminate. Oyelaran is the Director of Field Operations – the person who supervises all technicians, schedules safety testing, and interfaces with inspection authorities. She is also one of the three individuals whose knowledge defines "Knowledge of Seller" (APA § 1.1). If Seles closes without her consent:

1. The KEA assignment is void; Seles holds no contractual relationship with her.
2. The restrictive covenants that protect the customer relationships and workforce Seles is paying $18.75 million to acquire **cease to exist**.
3. APA § 2.8's "hold for benefit" mechanism does not cure this – it addresses permits and contracts that need third-party consents, but the KEA's consequence of a non-consented assignment is not merely that the assignment fails; it is that the covenants terminate by their own terms.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Make Oyelaran's written consent to the assignment (or, alternatively, her execution of a new agreement with Seles on substantially the same terms) a **condition to Closing** under APA § 7.2.
- Add a specific closing deliverable in APA § 3.2: Oyelaran's consent or a replacement agreement.
- If Seller resists, consider a specific indemnity in Article IX for Losses arising from the termination of the KEA covenants, funded from the Indemnity Escrow.
- **Flag to client:** Confirm whether Oyelaran has informally agreed to join Seles. If she has not, this is a deal-critical issue that should be raised with Seller before we finalize.

## Issue 2 – Schedule Supplement No. 1: Buyer's Objection Was Likely Timely Defective; $640,000 Special Escrow at Risk

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 6.5 – 5-Business-Day objection window for Schedule Supplements
- Escrow Agreement § 3.3 – recites the dispute; Seller's position (objection late) vs. Buyer's position (objection valid)
- Escrow Agreement § 3.4(b) – on the Indemnity Escrow Release Date (15 months post-Closing), the entire Special Escrow Fund is released to Seller unless Buyer has a Final Order or Joint Written Instructions
- Escrow Agreement § 3.4(c) – safety valve if litigation is pending

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
Schedule Supplement No. 1 (delivered February 9, 2026, a Monday) disclosed open state inspection deficiency notices on 14 Covered Units, directly implicating the representation in APA § 4.12 (no open deficiency notices). Buyer's written objection was delivered February 23, 2026 – **10 Business Days** after receipt, not the 5 required by § 6.5. On the plain text, Seller's position appears correct.

If Seller's position prevails:
- The § 4.12 representation is deemed "cured" for purposes of the closing condition (§ 7.2) **and** the indemnity (§ 9.2(a)).
- Seles has no indemnity recourse for the 14 units' deficiency notices, remediation costs, or any downstream consequences (customer claims, lost contracts, further code violations).
- The $640,000 Special Escrow is released to Seller at month 15 post-Closing absent a Final Order.

**Recommended Fix / Strategy:**
- **Flag to client immediately.** The client needs to understand that its objection was likely 5 Business Days late and that the contractual cure mechanism has probably operated against it. This affects both the Special Escrow and the underlying indemnity for the 14 units.
- If the client still believes it has a merits argument (e.g., the deficiency notices existed before the APA date and should have been in the original Disclosure Schedule, making the "supplement" a mischaracterization), we should assess whether to litigate before the 15-month release date.
- If we litigate, Escrow Agreement § 3.4(c) preserves the Special Escrow pending the action. We must ensure the complaint is filed and notice delivered before the Indemnity Escrow Release Date.
- Consider whether the 90-day survival period for General Representations (Issue 3 below) independently bars the claim regardless of the cure issue – if so, the Special Escrow dispute may be the only remaining avenue.
- **Do not respond to Seller on this point without client direction.**

## Issue 3 – 90-Day Survival for General Representations Is Aggressively Short for an Elevator Business

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 9.1 – General Representations survive **90 days** after Closing; Fundamental Representations survive 6 years
- APA § 1.1 – "General Representations" = all Article IV reps other than Organization, Authority, and Title
- APA § 4.12 – Elevator Code Compliance (a General Representation)
- APA § 4.10 – Litigation (a General Representation)
- APA § 4.11 – Compliance with Laws; Permits (a General Representation)

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
Every substantive representation about the condition of the Business – elevator code compliance, permit status, absence of litigation, financial statement accuracy, employee matters – dies 90 days after Closing. In the elevator industry:

- Periodic safety tests (Category 1, 5, full-load, governor) are on multi-year cycles. A missed test or a test performed but not properly recorded may not surface for months or years.
- Inspection deficiency notices can be issued with lag.
- Latent mechanical issues in Covered Units may not manifest until well after 90 days.
- Customer disputes about service quality or missed maintenance may emerge after the survival period.

The 90-day window means Seles effectively has no post-Closing indemnity for the most operationally important representations. The only reps that survive longer are the "Fundamental" ones (Organization, Authority, Title) – which are unlikely to be the source of real economic loss.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Negotiate to extend General Representation survival to **18 months** (aligning with the Indemnity Escrow Release Date) or at minimum **12 months**.
- Alternatively, carve out § 4.12 (Elevator Code Compliance) and § 4.11 (Permits) for a longer survival period (e.g., 24 months), given the regulatory inspection cycles.
- If Seller will not budge on survival, consider increasing the Indemnity Escrow Amount or extending the Escrow hold period to give Seles more time to discover and assert claims before the 90-day bar.
- **Flag to client:** The client should understand that as drafted, it will have very little post-Closing protection for the condition of the 14+ units it is acquiring.

## Issue 4 – Fenmark Uniform Rental Agreement: Likely Not an Assigned Contract; Operational Gap at Closing

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 2.1(c) – Assigned Contracts are those "listed in Section 2.1(c) of the Disclosure Schedule, including all customer maintenance and service contracts, the vehicle leases for the Business's service fleet, and the Key Employee Agreement"
- APA § 2.2(d) – Excluded Assets include "all contracts that are not Assigned Contracts"
- Fenmark Agreement § 8.2 – permits assignment to a successor without Fenmark's consent, provided the successor assumes in writing
- Fenmark Agreement § 2.4 – minimum 40 Wearers; if below for >2 consecutive months, Fenmark may charge for 40

**Bearing on the Deal:**
The Fenmark agreement is a vendor contract for uniform rental, laundering, and replacement. It is not a customer maintenance contract, not a vehicle lease, and not the KEA. Unless it is specifically listed on the Disclosure Schedule's Section 2.1(c) (which we cannot confirm without seeing the Schedule), it is an **Excluded Asset** under § 2.2(d). This means:

1. At Closing, the uniform rental relationship remains with Tripux. Seles's field technicians (if hired) will be wearing uniforms that are contractually rented to a different entity.
2. The 40-Wearer minimum creates a cost floor. If Seles hires fewer than 40 of Tripux's field staff, it still pays for 40 – but only if it assumes the agreement. If it doesn't assume, it has no uniforms at all.
3. The agreement runs through at least June 2027 (36 months from June 2024) and auto-renews. It is a multi-year operational commitment.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether the Fenmark agreement appears on the Disclosure Schedule as an Assigned Contract.
- If it does not, either (a) add it to the Assigned Contracts list and obtain Fenmark's acknowledgment (easy given § 8.2), or (b) require Seller to assign it as a Closing deliverable under APA § 3.2(f) ("such other customary instruments of transfer as Buyer reasonably requests").
- If Seles expects to hire fewer than 40 field employees, negotiate with Fenmark to reduce the minimum or terminate early (60 days' notice under § 6.4, with the unamortized replacement charge).
- This is a lower-priority item but should be resolved before Closing to avoid a day-one operational gap.

## Issue 5 – TSA Liability Cap Is Low Relative to the Risk of the Services

**Documents and Sections:**
- TSA § 9.1 – Provider's aggregate liability capped at "total fees paid and payable under Section 5.1" (approximately $42,500/month × 6–9 months = ~$255,000–$382,500)
- TSA § 9.1 (exception) – cap does not apply to third-party claims for bodily injury or death caused by Provider's failure to perform After-Hours Emergency Call Answering per § 2.3
- TSA § 2.3(b) – Entrapment Call response: contact on-call technician within 5 minutes; escalate if no acknowledgment in 10 minutes; contact emergency services if injury/distress reported

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
The TSA requires Tripux to answer emergency elevator calls, including entrapments, for up to 9 months post-Closing. The uncapped exception for bodily injury/death is critical and well-drafted. However, the general cap (~$255K–$382K) applies to all other failures – e.g., missed calls that result in customer contract breaches, property damage from delayed response, or regulatory fines for inadequate emergency response. If a series of missed or delayed calls causes Seles to lose a major customer contract or receive a regulatory citation, the TSA cap may not cover the loss.

**Recommended Fix:**
- The bodily injury exception is adequate. Consider whether to add property damage to the uncapped category.
- Alternatively, require Tripux to maintain the $2M CGL and $1M E&O insurance (TSA § 9.3) and name Seles as an additional insured, so that Seles has a direct insurance recovery path outside the TSA cap.
- Confirm that the insurance certificates are delivered at or before Closing.

## Issue 6 – APA § 2.4 Excludes Pre-Closing Inspection Remediation Costs, But § 4.12 Represents No Open Deficiency Notices

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 2.4 – Excluded Liabilities include "any fines, penalties or remediation costs relating to the condition or inspection status of any elevator or escalator serviced by the Business before the Closing"
- APA § 4.12 – represents that there are "no open inspection deficiency notices, violation notices or orders" for any Covered Unit
- Escrow Agreement § 3.3 – 14 units have open deficiency notices (disclosed in Schedule Supplement No. 1)

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
There is a tension between § 2.4 and § 4.12. Section 2.4 says Seles does not assume pre-Closing remediation costs. Section 4.12 says there are no open deficiency notices. If 14 units do have open notices (as Schedule Supplement No. 1 discloses), then:

- The remediation costs are Excluded Liabilities (Seller's responsibility under § 2.4).
- But the indemnity for the § 4.12 breach is subject to the 90-day survival (Issue 3) and the "cure" effect of the Schedule Supplement (Issue 2).
- If the Schedule Supplement cure is effective, Seles may bear the remediation costs in practice (because the units are now its problem operationally) while having no indemnity recourse.

**Recommended Fix:**
- This is subsumed in Issues 2 and 3. If we fix the survival period and the cure issue, this resolves itself.
- Independently, consider adding a specific provision in § 2.4 or a side letter confirming that Seller is responsible for all remediation of the 14 units' deficiency notices, regardless of when the remediation is performed, and that this obligation is not subject to the survival period or the basket.

## Issue 7 – TSA "Substantially Similar" Standard May Lock In Subpar Prior Performance

**Documents and Sections:**
- TSA § 2.2 – Provider shall perform services "in a manner substantially similar in scope, quality, priority and timeliness to that in which Provider performed the same functions for the Business during the 12 months before the Closing"

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
If Tripux's dispatch and emergency call handling in the prior 12 months was slow, error-prone, or below industry standards, this clause sets that subpar level as the contractual benchmark. The response-time metrics in § 2.3 (95% within 30 seconds, etc.) provide some objective floor, but the "substantially similar" language could be invoked by Tripux to argue that a 90% answer rate is acceptable if that is what it historically achieved.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Add language clarifying that the § 2.3 response standards are the minimum and are not subject to the "substantially similar" qualifier.
- Alternatively, delete the first bullet of § 2.2 and rely solely on the objective metrics in § 2.3.

## Issue 8 – Outside Date May Be Tight Given the Municipal License Consent Condition

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 8.1(b) – Outside date: May 15, 2026
- APA § 7.2(c) – Closing conditioned on Seller obtaining the municipal elevator contractor license consent
- APA § 6.3 – Seller to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain consents before Closing
- APA § 4.4 – the only required consent is the municipal licensing authority's approval to transfer the elevator contractor license

**Why It Matters for Seles:**
The APA is signed January 14, 2026. The outside date is May 15, 2026 – approximately 4 months. The municipal license transfer is a condition to Closing. If the municipal authority is slow (common with government agencies), or if there are complications (e.g., the authority requires a site inspection, bonding, or evidence of qualified personnel), the deal could hit the outside date without the consent in hand. Either party could then terminate under § 8.1(b).

**Recommended Fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether the municipal authority has been informally contacted and what its typical processing time is.
- If the timeline is tight, consider extending the outside date by 30–60 days, or adding a mechanism for automatic extension if the delay is caused solely by the Governmental Authority's processing time.
- Ensure the client is prepared to provide whatever information the licensing authority requires (APA § 6.3 obligates Buyer to "cooperate reasonably").
