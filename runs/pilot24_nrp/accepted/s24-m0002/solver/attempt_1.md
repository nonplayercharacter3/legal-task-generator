## Issue 1 (Critical): Section 7.4 of the KEA Removes Intellectual Property from the Fundamental Representations, Directly Contradicting the Board's Approved Risk Allocation

**Where:** KEA § 7.4; APA § 9.5(b); Board Consent § 2.7.

**What happened:** As signed on September 15, 2026, the APA defines "Fundamental Representations" in Section 9.5(b) to include Section 4.9 (Intellectual Property). Fundamental Reps are exempt from the $485,000 basket and the 15% cap, with liability capped only at the full Purchase Price ($48.5 million). The Board Consent at § 2.7 expressly confirms this: it states that "claims relating to title to and non-infringement of the Seller Intellectual Property, including the FieldMind flight-control software and the Sonox drone patents, are not limited by the 15% cap."

At closing on October 30, 2026, Section 7.4 of the KEA amends APA § 9.5(b) to *remove* Section 4.9 from the Fundamental Representations. As amended, the Fundamental Representations consist only of §§ 4.1 (Organization), 4.2 (Authority), and 4.14 (Brokers). The practical effect: all IP-related indemnification claims (title defects, infringement, misappropriation, open-source contamination, missing employee assignments) are now subject to the $485,000 deductible basket and the 15% cap of $7,275,000.

**Why it matters:** The Board identified the FieldMind software and Sonox patents as central to the strategic rationale (Board Consent § 1.3). The entire deal is premised on acquiring this IP. By removing IP from the Fundamental Reps, Nodix's maximum recovery for a total failure of title or a material infringement claim drops from $48.5 million to $7.275 million—a reduction of more than $41 million in protection. If the IP is defective or encumbered, Nodix may be unable to recover its full loss.

**The amendment is technically effective under the APA's own terms.** APA § 11.9(b) provides that an Ancillary Agreement governs "to the extent it expressly amends a specified Section of this Agreement and is signed by both Buyer and Seller." KEA § 7.4 expressly amends § 9.5(b), and Sonox signs the KEA "solely for purposes of Section 7.4" (KEA signature page). So the amendment satisfies the contractual mechanics.

**Recommended fix:** This is the most urgent item. We need to determine whether Nodix can (and should) seek to restore Section 4.9 as a Fundamental Representation. Options include:

- Negotiate a post-closing amendment with Dr. Raskova / Sonox restoring § 4.9 to the Fundamental Reps, potentially in exchange for some other concession (e.g., a reduction in the retention bonus or an extension of her non-compete).
- If the amendment is unauthorized (see Issue 2), assert that it is void or voidable and that APA § 9.5(b) remains as originally signed.
- At minimum, obtain a standalone IP indemnity from Dr. Raskova personally, uncapped and outside the basket, covering title, non-infringement, and employee assignment issues.

**Flag for client discussion:** The client needs to understand that this amendment was executed at closing and that the Press Release (which announces the deal as complete) may make it harder to reopen the term without market sensitivity. We should ask whether anyone at Nodix was aware of § 7.4 before the Board Consent was signed on September 14, and whether the CEO or CFO who executed the KEA at closing understood that it amended the APA's indemnification structure.

## Issue 2 (Critical): The Section 7.4 Amendment Likely Exceeds the Authority Granted by the Board Consent

**Where:** Board Consent §§ 3.1, 3.2, 2.7; KEA § 7.4.

**What the Board authorized:** Board Consent § 3.1 authorizes the CEO and CFO to execute the APA "in substantially the form presented to the Board" and provides that "[a]ny changes approved by the Authorized Officer executing it must be consistent with the material terms described in Article 2." Section 2.7 of Article 2 describes the Fundamental Representations as including § 4.9 (IP) and expressly states that IP claims "are not limited by the 15% cap."

Board Consent § 3.2 authorizes execution of the KEA "on terms consistent with the Purchase Agreement and the material terms described in Article 2."

**The inconsistency:** KEA § 7.4 amends the Purchase Agreement to remove § 4.9 from the Fundamental Representations. This is directly inconsistent with Board Consent § 2.7, which the Board adopted as a material term of the approved deal. An officer who executed the KEA with § 7.4 included did so in a manner inconsistent with the Board's authorization.

**Why it matters:** If the amendment is ultra vires (beyond the officers' authority), it may be voidable. This gives Nodix a potential legal lever: the company could argue that § 7.4 is ineffective because the signatories lacked authority, and that APA § 9.5(b) remains as originally drafted with IP included as a Fundamental Rep. However, this argument cuts both ways—if Nodix later wants to *enforce* the KEA (e.g., the non-compete), the counterparty could argue the entire KEA is tainted. Additionally, Dr. Raskova signed the KEA as both the Executive and as Sonox's Managing Member (for § 7.4 purposes), creating a conflict-of-interest dimension that a court might view unfavorably to Nodix if Nodix tries to selectively enforce or disavow provisions.

**Recommended fix:**

- Determine who specifically inserted § 7.4 and when. Was it in the draft the Board saw on September 14, or was it added between signing and closing?
- If it was added after the Board Consent, assess whether the CEO/CFO had independent authority under the bylaws to make this change (the Board Consent's "substantially the form" language may allow minor changes but almost certainly not a fundamental reallocation of indemnification risk).
- Consider whether a ratification by the Board now would be advisable (if the client wants to keep the amendment) or whether the client should disavow it (if the client wants IP back as a Fundamental Rep).
- Document the analysis carefully. If Nodix disavows § 7.4, it should do so promptly and in writing to avoid waiver.

**Flag for client discussion:** The client should confirm internally who was responsible for the KEA negotiation and whether § 7.4 was discussed with the General Counsel or the Board before closing. This bears on both the legal strategy and any internal accountability questions.

## Issue 3 (High): The KEA's "Business" Definition Is Dramatically Narrower Than the APA's, Creating a Gap in Personal Non-Compete Protection

**Where:** KEA § 1.1 (definition of "Business"); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Business"); KEA § 6.1; APA § 6.4.

**The discrepancy:**

| Document | "Business" Definition |
|----------|----------------------|
| APA § 1.1 | "the design, manufacture, sale and servicing of agricultural drones, payloads and flight-control and agronomic software, including spray, seeding and imaging platforms" |
| KEA § 1.1 | "the crop-imaging drone product line marketed as the Sonox Scout series" |

The KEA's non-compete (§ 6.1) prohibits Dr. Raskova from engaging in any "Competing Business," defined as a business that is "competitive with the Business." Because the KEA's "Business" is limited to the crop-imaging/Scout series, Dr. Raskova could legally join or found a company making *spray drones* or *seeding drones*—two of the three product lines Nodix acquired—without breaching her personal non-compete.

**Why it matters:** The APA's entity-level non-compete (§ 6.4) covers the full "Business" as broadly defined, but it binds Sonox AgroDrone LLC, not Dr. Raskova personally. After the asset sale, Sonox will likely be dissolved or will have no assets or operations. A non-compete against a shell entity is of limited practical value. The *real* protection against Dr. Raskova competing is her personal covenant in the KEA—and that covenant is narrow.

The Press Release and Board Consent both emphasize the full portfolio (spray, seeding, and imaging). The Board's strategic understanding was that Nodix is acquiring all three lines. Yet the personal non-compete protects only one.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate an amendment to the KEA expanding the "Business" definition to match the APA's definition (or at minimum to include spray and seeding platforms).
- Alternatively, add a standalone non-compete provision in the KEA that covers the full agricultural drone business as defined in the APA.
- If Dr. Raskova resists, consider whether the retention bonus or equity vesting can be conditioned on acceptance of the broader definition.

**Flag for client discussion:** Ask the client whether the narrow "Business" definition was a deliberate trade-off (e.g., Dr. Raskova agreed to the § 7.4 IP amendment in exchange for the narrower non-compete scope). If so, the client should understand it is getting neither side of that trade: it gave up IP protection *and* received a narrower non-compete.

## Issue 4 (Medium): FAA Certificates Acquired Only "To the Extent Transferable" – No Confirmation All Key Authorizations Transferred

**Where:** APA § 2.1(e); Board Consent § 2.3; Press Release § 2.

**The issue:** The APA includes "all FAA certificates, waivers and exemptions held by Seller, to the extent transferable" (§ 2.1(e)). The Board Consent and Press Release similarly qualify the acquisition as limited to "transferable" FAA authorizations. None of the four documents confirms which specific FAA certificates, waivers, or exemptions are actually transferable versus which require reapplication by Nodix.

The Disclosure Schedule (referenced at APA § 4.10) would list all permits, certificates, waivers, and exemptions, but it is not in the matter folder.

**Why it matters:** If material FAA waivers (e.g., for beyond-visual-line-of-sight operations, nighttime operations, or operations over populated areas) are not transferable, Nodix must reapply, which can take months or longer. During the gap, the acquired drones may not be legally operable under the conditions growers expect. This could trigger customer attrition and dealer concerns, undermining the integration.

**Recommended fix:**

- Obtain the Disclosure Schedule and identify all FAA authorizations listed.
- Confirm with the FAA (or regulatory counsel) which transferred and which require reapplication.
- If any critical waivers require reapplication, file immediately and assess operational workarounds in the interim.
- Consider whether this gap constitutes a breach of Seller's representation at APA § 4.10 (Compliance with Laws; Permits) or § 4.5 (Condition and Sufficiency of Assets), and whether Nodix should reserve rights under the indemnification provisions.

**Flag:** The Disclosure Schedule is not in the folder. We cannot complete this analysis without it.

## Issue 5 (Medium): The Seller Entity Non-Compete May Be Practically Unenforceable Post-Closing

**Where:** APA § 6.4; APA § 2.2 (Excluded Assets).

**The issue:** APA § 6.4 imposes a 5-year non-compete on Seller (Sonox AgroDrone LLC). However, Sonox has sold substantially all of its assets. The Excluded Assets (§ 2.2) retain only cash, organizational documents, tax records, non-assigned contracts, and employee benefit plans. Sonox has no operations, no products, and no reason to continue existing except to honor its obligations under the APA.

If Sonox is dissolved (a likely outcome for a single-member LLC that has sold all its assets), the non-compete binds a nonexistent entity. While the obligation may survive dissolution under Delaware law (8 Del. C. § 18-32), enforcement against a dissolved entity with no assets is practically difficult. Dr. Raskova, as the former managing member, could argue she has no personal liability under § 6.4 because it binds the entity, not her individually.

**Why it matters:** This reinforces Issue 3. Nodix's practical non-compete protection against Dr. Raskova comes almost entirely from the KEA, which uses the narrow "Business" definition. The 5-year entity non-compete is a backstop that may prove illusory.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm whether Sonox will be dissolved or maintained as a shell. If dissolved, assess the enforceability of § 6.4 against the entity and against Dr. Raskova in her capacity as former managing member.
- Consider obtaining a personal non-compete from Dr. Raskova that matches the scope and duration of APA § 6.4 (5 years, full "Business" definition, same territories), either as an amendment to the KEA or as a standalone agreement.
- If Sonox is to be maintained, ensure it has minimal but sufficient existence to be a viable enforcement target.

## Issue 6 (Low): Potential Section 409A Timing Issue with Retention Bonus Acceleration

**Where:** KEA §§ 3.3, 4.4(b), 8.2.

**The issue:** KEA § 3.3 provides two $375,000 retention bonus installments on the first and second anniversaries. If the Executive is terminated without Cause or resigns for Good Reason before an installment date, § 4.4(b) requires payment "within 60 days after the Termination Date." If the Termination Date falls in late December, the 60-day payment window extends into the following calendar year. Section 8.2 states the parties intend compliance with § 409A and that payments on termination are made "only on a separation from service," but it does not specifically address whether the 60-day window fits within the § 409A short-term deferral exception (which allows payments up to 2½ months after the end of the tax year in which the separation occurs).

**Why it matters:** A § 409A violation triggers a 20% additional tax penalty plus interest on the Executive. While this is the Executive's problem, not Nodix's, it could create a dispute or a claim against the company for failure to structure the agreement properly. It also could complicate the severance negotiation if the Executive's counsel flags the issue.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm with tax counsel that the 60-day payment window in § 4.4(b) fits within the § 409A short-term deferral exception in all realistic scenarios.
- If it does not, amend § 4.4(b) to specify that accelerated retention bonus payments shall be made by the 15th day of the third month following the calendar quarter in which the Termination Date occurs (or another formulation that clearly satisfies § 409A).

## Issue 7 (Low): Press Release Lists Sonox as a Media Contact Post-Closing

**Where:** Press Release § 8.

**The issue:** The Press Release lists "Sonox AgroDrone LLC, Attention: Dr. Raskova" as a media contact. After the closing, Sonox has transferred substantially all of its assets. Listing it as a continuing media contact is confusing and could imply that Sonox retains ongoing operations or that Dr. Raskova has a dual role.

**Why it matters:** Minor, but in the context of the non-compete issues (Issues 3 and 5), any public suggestion that Sonox continues to operate could be used by Dr. Raskova to argue she is "winding up the affairs of Seller" (permitted under KEA § 2.3) rather than competing. It could also create confusion for dealers and customers about whom to contact.

**Recommended fix:** Issue a corrected media contact list directing all inquiries to Nodix only. If Sonox is to be dissolved, ensure the dissolution is filed promptly to eliminate any ambiguity.

Conversely, Nodix could use the conflict to argue that § 7.4 (which she drafted to her own advantage as Seller while also being the Executive) should not be enforced.
