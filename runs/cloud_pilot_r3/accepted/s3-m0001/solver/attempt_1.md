## 2. Issues, Most Significant First

### Issue 1: The Velloran renewal deadline appears to have passed (CRITICAL)

- **Where:**
  - Distribution Agreement ("DA") §3.1 (Initial Term ends Nov. 14, 2026).
  - DA §3.2 (renewal only on Distributor's written notice at least 90 days before expiry; "does not renew automatically"; no notice means expiry).
  - DA §17.1 (notice by hand, courier or certified mail, effective on receipt).
  - MIPA §3.12(c) (Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029").
  - MIPA Sched. 3.12 closing note ("The Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing").
- **Why it matters:**
  - Ninety days before Nov. 14, 2026 is about **Aug. 16, 2026**. If no compliant notice went out by then, the agreement expires on Nov. 14, 2026, two weeks before Target Closing.
  - A notice sent now would be late. Renewal would then depend on Velloran's discretion, which gives Velloran leverage over price, minimums and exclusivity.
  - Velloran is about 91% of purchases. The business has no other main supply source.
  - The representation in MIPA §3.12(c) is untrue as written. It is also made as of the Closing Date (Art. 3 lead-in), by which time the term would have lapsed.
  - The schedule note is thin cover. It does not say the deadline has passed, and it suggests a late notice would work. Buyer could characterize that as misleading, and fraud is carved out of the indemnity limits (MIPA §§8.4, 8.7).
  - The Sellers must promptly notify Buyer of facts likely to cause a closing condition to fail (MIPA §6.5).
  - Exposure includes failure of the bring-down condition (§7.1(a)), Buyer's termination right (§9.1(c)), and joint and several indemnity (§8.2(a)).
- **Recommendation:**
  - (a) Today, establish whether any Renewal Notice was sent. Get the date, method, addressee (VP Channel Sales, copy to General Counsel) and proof of receipt.
  - (b) If none was sent, do not simply mail a late notice. Seek a **signed written waiver or amendment** from Velloran confirming the Renewal Term (DA §§19.2, 19.3 require a writing signed by both Parties). Ideally bundle it with the consent in Issue 2 and an estoppel (agreement in force, no defaults, minimums met).
  - (c) Buyer's written consent is required before the Company amends or waives a material right under a Material Contract (MIPA §6.1(c)), so coordinate with Quillon.
  - (d) Correct the §3.12(c) representation and Sched. 3.12 by written amendment (MIPA §10.3). The MIPA has no schedule-update mechanism.
  - (e) If the renewal is secured, note that Year 4 minimums step up to 105% of Year 3, i.e. **$3,255,000** (DA Exh. B, Pt. 2).

### Issue 2: Velloran's consent to the change of control is required but is not a Required Consent (CRITICAL)

- **Where:**
  - DA §1.3(a) (sale of more than 50% of equity is a Change of Control).
  - DA §14.2 (deemed assignment requiring Supplier's **prior** written consent).
  - DA §14.3 (immediate termination right if no consent).
  - DA §15.4.
  - MIPA §2.1 (100% of interests transfer).
  - MIPA §§3.5(c) and 3.6 (no conflicts or termination rights except Required Consents).
  - MIPA Sched. 3.6 (lists only the Brandywine landlord and Keystone Bank).
  - MIPA §§6.4, 7.1(d), 2.6(c).
- **Why it matters:**
  - The sale is squarely a Change of Control. Without consent, Velloran can terminate immediately, with no cure period and no stated deadline to act.
  - Schedule 3.6 omits the consent, so §§3.5 and 3.6 appear inaccurate on signing.
  - Because Velloran is not a Required Consent, Sellers have no express duty to seek it (§6.4) and Buyer has no express closing condition for it (§7.1(d)).
  - Buyer will likely find the omission. It would then probably demand to add the consent as a condition, or reprice, or assert indemnity or termination rights.
  - A post-closing consent may not cure the issue, because DA §14.2 requires *prior* consent.
- **Recommendation:**
  - (a) Obtain Velloran's written consent **before Closing**. It should expressly waive §14.3 and confirm no default.
  - (b) Raise the omission with Quillon's counsel proactively. Propose amending Sched. 3.6 and the MIPA to add the Velloran consent. Sellers get a clean representation, at the price of a new closing condition (§7.1(d)). Negotiate that condition's terms and the allocation of any Velloran concession costs.
  - (c) The §1.10(c) MAE carve-out for effects of announcing the deal helps on the MAE condition only. It does not solve the representation problem.

### Issue 3: Side Letter non-compete: 18-month tail, trigger on expiry, and reach to Quillon (CRITICAL)

- **Where:**
  - Side Letter ("SL") §1.3 (Restricted Period is the Term plus 18 months after expiration or termination "for any reason").
  - SL §2.1 (bars Competing Products, and requires Distributor to "cause its Affiliates" to comply).
  - SL §1.2 (broad definition including "related accessories or consumables").
  - SL §4.2 (tolling).
  - SL §4.3 (breach is a material breach under DA §15.1).
  - DA §1.1 (Affiliate means control of more than 50%).
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Expiry trigger.** If Issue 1 is not fixed, the DA expires Nov. 14, 2026. Harrow Creek would then be barred from distributing competing instruments in the Territory until about **May 14, 2028**, while it has no Velloran supply. That could cripple the business.
  - **Quillon.** After Closing, Quillon and its controlled companies are Harrow Creek's Affiliates. If any of them sell Competing Products in the Territory, Harrow Creek is in breach, which gives Velloran a termination right and injunctive relief (SL §4.1). The MIPA does not address this. Buyer may not have focused on it.
  - **Existing compliance.** Sched. 3.13 describes Velloran as about 91% of purchases and lists the other suppliers as packaging, freight and office providers. That leaves roughly 9% unexplained. We should confirm Harrow Creek buys and sells no Competing Products, and that it has complied with the notice duty in SL §3.3.
- **Recommendation:**
  - (a) Confirm current compliance and ask Quillon whether any affiliate sells overlapping products.
  - (b) Disclose the Affiliate reach to Quillon's counsel in writing. This protects Sellers on disclosure, and Buyer needs it for its own planning.
  - (c) In the Velloran negotiation, seek to limit the tail to termination for Distributor's breach, or at least exclude expiry and Velloran-initiated termination. Also seek to carve out Quillon affiliates (or existing Quillon businesses).
  - (d) Have Delaware/New York counsel assess enforceability. That is outside the folder and I have not analyzed it.

### Issue 5: Side Letter territory, exclusivity and forum mismatches (HIGH)

- **Where:**
  - SL §1.1 (Territory redefined to add **New York and Virginia**).
  - SL §3.1 ("does not modify Section 2.1").
  - DA §1.12 (four-state Territory).
  - DA §2.2 (no sales outside Territory).
  - DA §19.4 (precedence: First Amendment, then Side Letter, then body).
  - SL §§6.1–6.2 (New York law, New York County courts).
  - DA §§18.1–18.2 (Delaware law, Wilmington courts).
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Territory.** The non-compete covers six states, but exclusivity (per SL §3.1) arguably covers only four. Harrow Creek cannot sell Velloran Products in NY/VA (DA §2.2), yet cannot sell competitors' products there either. It carries the burden without the benefit.
  - **Forum and law.** SL §6.1 covers "any dispute arising out of the exclusivity and non-competition arrangements," which is wide enough to pull exclusivity disputes under DA §2.1 into New York. A single dispute (for example, termination for breach under SL §4.3 and DA §15.1) could split between New York and Delaware.
  - **Precedence.** DA §19.4 ranks the Side Letter above the DA body, so Velloran could use the redefinition to argue the broader Territory.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Seek a clarifying amendment. Either conform the non-compete to the four-state Territory, or extend exclusivity to NY/VA.
  - Conform governing law and forum to Delaware.

### Issue 8: Saltmarsh warehouse agreement (MEDIUM)

- **Where:** Saltmarsh Warehouse Agreement ("WA") §§4.1–4.2, 4.4, 6.4, 7.1–7.2, 8.2, 9.2, 11.1, 2.7.
- **Findings:**
  - **No consent needed.** §11.1 allows assignment on notice, and there is no change-of-control clause. The equity sale needs no Saltmarsh consent. This is correctly left off Sched. 3.6.
  - **Short exit period.** Either party may terminate on **60 days' notice** (§4.2), with only 30 days of post-term transition (§4.4). Saltmarsh could exit quickly after a sale, and the company's inventory sits at its New Castle facility. This is a Material Contract (MIPA §3.12(a)(vi)).
  - **Liability cap.** Saltmarsh's liability for goods is capped at landed cost, up to **$5,000,000 per occurrence** (§9.2), matching its warehouse legal liability insurance (§7.1(b)). The Company must insure Goods at full replacement value (§7.2). Confirm inventory values at the facility against the cap.
  - **Indemnity.** The Company indemnifies Saltmarsh for "any defect in the Goods" (§8.2), uncapped. Velloran's indemnity to Harrow Creek (DA §11.1) is narrower (IP, bodily injury, property damage). The warehouse policy for goods (§7.1(b)) does not list Customer as additional insured.
  - **Weak remedies.** WA §2.7 performance failures only trigger a meeting, with no credits or termination right.
- **Recommendation:** Verify inventory values and the Company's property insurance. Consider asking for a longer notice period, additional-insured status, a narrower §8.2 indemnity, and a service-credit mechanism. Treat the 60-day exit as a continuity risk to flag to Buyer, and prepare a contingency plan.

### Issue 9: Price-change mechanics in the Velloran agreement (MEDIUM)

- **Where:** DA §4.3 (once a year, 60 days' notice, no increase over **5%** in a contract year) against DA Exh. B Pt. 1 (price is Supplier's list price on the PO date less a fixed discount).
- **Why it matters:** Because the price floats with Velloran's list price, Velloran could argue the 5% cap applies only to the discount, not to list changes. That would leave Harrow Creek's margin exposed.
- **Recommendation:** Address it in the renewal amendment. State that the 5% cap applies to the net price and that list changes follow the §4.3 notice requirements.
