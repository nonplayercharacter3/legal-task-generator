## 3. Issues (Most Significant First)

### Issue 1 (CRITICAL): Renewal deadline missed, so the Velloran agreement expires Nov. 14, 2026, and the MIPA rep is wrong

**Where:**
- Dist. Agt. §§ 3.1, 3.2, 17.1, 19.3
- MIPA § 3.12(b)–(c); Schedule 3.12 (closing note)
- MIPA §§ 7.1(a), 7.1(e), 8.2

**Issue:**
- **No auto-renewal.** The Distribution Agreement renews for one 3-year term "only if" Harrow Creek delivers a Renewal Notice at least 90 days before the Initial Term ends. It "does not renew automatically" and "shall expire" otherwise (§ 3.2). Notices must go by hand, overnight courier or certified mail, and email does not count (§ 17.1).
- **Deadline passed.** The deadline was about Aug. 16, 2026. Nothing in the folder shows a notice was sent.
- **Schedule note concedes none was sent.** It says only that "[t]he Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing." A notice delivered now would be late.
- **Rep is inaccurate.** MIPA § 3.12(c) represents that the agreement "remains in effect through November 2029." That is true only if a timely renewal occurred. The rep is made at signing and again at Closing (Art. 3 lead-in), and § 3.12(b) ("in full force and effect") will also be false at Closing.
- **No informal waiver.** Section 19.3 requires waivers in writing, so informal conduct by Velloran will not save the renewal.
- **Maximum term.** Even a valid renewal gives one more 3-year term to Nov. 2029, with no further renewal right (§ 3.2).

**Why it matters:**
- **Business risk.** Harrow Creek would have no supply contract and no exclusivity when it closes. It has only the 90-day sell-off right (§ 16.2) and Velloran's option to repurchase inventory at net landed cost. Velloran also has full leverage to reprice or refuse renewal.
- **Buyer remedies.** Quillon can refuse to close (§ 7.1(a), (e)), terminate after a 20-day cure period (§ 9.1(c)), or seek indemnity (§ 8.2(a)).
- **Fraud exposure.** The schedule note shows the Sellers knew the Initial Term ended Nov. 14. Mr. Esterly signed the MIPA containing a "through November 2029" rep. Quillon could characterize this as fraud, which is uncapped and outside the exclusive-remedy clause (§§ 8.4, 8.7). This is a Seller concern, but it affects the Company's deal.
- **Schedule note is not a safe harbor.** It does not say the deadline passed, and it implies a notice can still be given.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Confirm the facts with the client today.** Was any Renewal Notice sent before Aug. 16 by a permitted § 17.1 method, or any written Velloran communication on renewal?
2. **Go to Velloran now** for a signed written instrument under §§ 19.2 and 19.3 that (a) waives the § 3.2 deadline and accepts a Renewal Notice, or (b) extends or restates the term.
3. **Serve a protective Renewal Notice** by hand or courier, to the VP Channel Sales with a copy to the General Counsel (§ 17.1), reserving rights. This is a fallback only. The terms of § 3.2 and § 19.3 make a late-notice argument weak, and we should research Delaware law on late exercise of renewal options before relying on equitable relief.
4. **Get Quillon's consent first.** MIPA § 6.1(c) bars amending or waiving material rights under a Material Contract without Buyer's consent. Also correct Schedule 3.12 and the § 3.12(c) rep by written amendment (§ 10.3). See Issue 5.

### Issue 2 (CRITICAL): Velloran's change-of-control consent is not a Required Consent

**Where:**
- Dist. Agt. §§ 1.3(a), 14.2, 14.3, 15.4
- MIPA §§ 3.5(c), 3.6, 6.4, 7.1(d); Schedule 3.6

**Issue:**
- **The sale triggers the clause.** Sale of 100% of the membership interests is a "Change of Control" (§ 1.3(a)). It is deemed an assignment requiring Velloran's **prior** written consent (§ 14.2). Without it Velloran may terminate "immediately upon written notice," with no cure period (§§ 14.3, 15.4).
- **Schedule 3.6 omits Velloran.** It lists only the Brandywine Flats landlord and the Keystone credit agreement.
- **Reps are false.** The § 3.6 rep ("[e]xcept for the Required Consents … no consent … is required") and the § 3.5(c) rep (no Material Contract termination right except Required Consents) are inaccurate.
- **Covenants and conditions do not reach Velloran.** Sellers must pursue only Required Consents (§ 6.4), and the closing condition covers only Schedule 3.6 consents (§ 7.1(d)). Quillon is protected through the § 7.1(a) bring-down and indemnity, not the consent condition.
- **Quillon's cooperation is limited.** Quillon need not "pay any fee or grant any accommodation" for a Required Consent (§ 6.4). Velloran may demand credit support or concessions.

**Why it matters:** Closing without consent hands Velloran an immediate termination right over a contract supplying 91% of COGS. It also leaves Sellers exposed on §§ 3.5/3.6. The MAE definition excludes effects from "the announcement of this Agreement" (§ 1.10(c)). A termination triggered by consummation under § 14.3 arguably falls outside that carve-out, so we should not rely on it.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Obtain a written, unconditional Velloran consent before Closing.** It should expressly consent to the Quillon acquisition, waive §§ 14.3 and 15.4 as to this transaction, and be combined with the Issue 1 renewal fix.
2. **Negotiate the clause itself.** Ask Velloran to carve out future affiliate reorganizations and upstream Quillon changes. Section 1.3 reaches "directly or indirectly" and any future sale of Harrow Creek or its parent by Quillon.
3. **Fix the MIPA.** Either (a) add the consent to Schedule 3.6, which makes it a closing condition and obliges the Sellers to pursue it (a Buyer walk right, but accurate), or (b) schedule it as an exception to §§ 3.5/3.6 and negotiate who bears the risk. Quillon will almost certainly insist on (a).
4. **Expect pricing.** Raise what Velloran may require from Quillon (guarantees, assurances) with Quillon's counsel early.

### Issue 3 (CRITICAL): Side Letter non-compete survives expiration or termination "for any reason"

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 1.2, 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 4.2, 5.1, 5.2, 7.2

**Issue:**
- **Scope.** Harrow Creek may not "market, promote, sell, distribute or act as a sales agent" for any Competing Products in the Territory. That covers instruments, balances, calibration standards and related accessories or consumables that are substantially similar to or marketed as substitutes for the Products (§ 1.2).
- **Duration.** The restriction runs through the Term plus 18 months after expiration or termination "for any reason" (§ 1.3). It is tolled during any breach (§ 4.2) and expressly survives (§ 5.2).
- **Even where Velloran is at fault.** The wording would literally apply if the agreement expires through Velloran's refusal to renew, or Velloran terminates after the sale. Harrow Creek would then lose its supplier and be locked out of replacements. Schedule 3.13 suggests Harrow Creek buys essentially only from Velloran, so there is no alternative line to fall back on.
- **Enforcement.** The reasonableness acknowledgment (§ 2.4), injunctive relief without bond (§ 4.1) and the judicial-modification clause (§ 7.2) favor Velloran. Under New York law (§ 6.1) a court may narrow the covenant rather than strike it.

**Why it matters:** Combined with Issues 1 and 2, the downside is no contract, no replacement supplier and an 18-month bar in six states (the Side Letter Territory, see Issue 6). That would undermine the purchase price rationale.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Amend the Side Letter** (§ 7.1, signed by both parties) so the post-term period applies only if Harrow Creek (not Velloran) causes the termination, and falls away on Velloran non-renewal, Velloran breach or termination without cause.
2. **Tie the post-term period to the Territory in which Harrow Creek actually held exclusivity.**
3. **Seek the amendment in the same package as the Issue 1 and 2 fixes.**
4. **Brief Quillon on the covenant.** It affects what Quillon is buying.

### Issue 4 (HIGH): Side Letter non-compete reaches "Affiliates," which after Closing includes Quillon and its group

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 2.1, 2.2, 4.3; Dist. Agt. § 1.1 (Affiliate = controls or is under common control, >50%)

**Issue:**
- **Affiliates are bound.** Harrow Creek must "cause its Affiliates not to" sell Competing Products in the Territory, and may not act "through any Affiliate, member, manager" (§§ 2.1, 2.2).
- **Quillon becomes an Affiliate at Closing.** Quillon, and every entity under its common control, becomes an Affiliate of Harrow Creek. The covenant then binds a business the Company does not control and may not know about.
- **Breach is a termination ground.** A breach during the Term is a material breach for § 15.1 (Side Letter § 4.3), with a 30-day cure period.
- **The only carve-out is narrow.** Section 2.3 permits passive holdings of 2% or less of public securities.
- **The MIPA is silent.** It contains no diligence rep or covenant on this.

**Why it matters:** If any Quillon affiliate sells similar instruments, balances or accessories in the six-state Territory, Velloran could terminate. The Sellers could also be blamed for not flagging it.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Ask Quillon's counsel** to confirm whether any Quillon affiliate sells competing products in the six states.
2. **Negotiate a carve-out** from Side Letter §§ 2.1/2.2 for Quillon and its other affiliates, or limit "Affiliates" to entities Harrow Creek controls. Add it to the Velloran consent package.

### Issue 5 (HIGH): MIPA risk allocation and Seller exposure

**Recommendation:**

2. **Propose a MIPA amendment** that:

### Issue 6 (HIGH): Side Letter Territory, governing law and forum conflict with the Distribution Agreement

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 1.1, 3.1, 6.1, 6.2; Dist. Agt. §§ 1.12, 2.1, 2.2, 18.1, 18.2, 19.4

**Issue:**
- **Mismatched territories.**
  - The Side Letter redefines "Territory" as DE, MD, NJ, PA, **NY and VA**.
  - The Distribution Agreement Territory is DE, MD, NJ and PA only (§ 1.12).
  - The Side Letter says it "does not modify Section 2.1" (§ 3.1), so exclusivity still covers only four states, and Harrow Creek may not sell Products outside them (§ 2.2).
  - Harrow Creek therefore carries non-compete burdens in NY and VA with no exclusivity there.
- **Possible argument that the Side Letter expands the Territory.** The Side Letter ranks above the body of the agreement (§ 19.4), so there is room to argue its definition governs. The point is ambiguous and could be turned against Harrow Creek.
- **Law and forum split.**
  - The Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive Wilmington courts (§§ 18.1, 18.2).
  - The Side Letter selects New York law and exclusive New York County courts for "any dispute arising out of the exclusivity and non-competition arrangements" (§§ 6.1, 6.2). That wording sweeps in exclusivity under § 2.1 of the Distribution Agreement.
  - Disputes over a § 15.1 termination premised on a Side Letter breach could be split between two courts and two bodies of law.

**Recommendation:**
1. **Clarify by amendment** that the Side Letter Territory applies only to Section 2 non-competition, or conform the territories.
2. **Align law and forum** with Delaware, and align the two documents on injunctive relief.
3. **Have Quillon and the Company assess commercial exposure** in NY and VA.

**Where:** Saltmarsh WSA §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 6.4, 7.2, 9.2, 11.1; MIPA §§ 3.6, 3.12

**Issue:**

**Recommendation:** Confirm Harrow Creek has property insurance on Goods held at Saltmarsh. Ask Saltmarsh to extend the termination notice and transition periods, and identify a backup 3PL, with the Conshohocken warehouse as possible fallback capacity.
