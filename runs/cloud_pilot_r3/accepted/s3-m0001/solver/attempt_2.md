## 3. Issues (most significant first)

### Issue 1 – Renewal deadline missed; DA expires before closing; MIPA rep is inaccurate (CRITICAL)

**Where it appears**
- DA §3.1: Initial Term ends Nov. 14, 2026.
- DA §3.2: renews "only if" Distributor delivers a Renewal Notice at least 90 days before expiration. "This Agreement does not renew automatically."
- DA §17.1: notices must be by hand, courier or certified mail, and are effective on receipt.
- MIPA §3.12(c): the DA "is in full force and effect and remains in effect through November 2029."
- MIPA Sched. 3.12, closing note: "The Initial Term … ends November 14, 2026. The Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing."

**Why it matters**
- The deadline was about Aug. 16, 2026. The Schedule note says the notice is still only "intended," so it appears no notice was delivered. A notice sent now is late. Under §3.2 it would not be effective, and Velloran has no obligation to accept it.
- Without renewal, the DA expires on Nov. 14, 2026, before the Nov. 30 target closing.
  - The only post-expiry rights are a 90-day sell-off, or Velloran's option to repurchase inventory at net landed cost (DA §16.2).
  - There is no termination payment or goodwill compensation.
- Even if the expiry date were not reached, the §3.12(c) statement that the DA runs through Nov. 2029 is untrue when made (Oct. 5, 2026).
  - The Schedule note discloses the term end and the intent to renew. It does not disclose that the renewal window had closed.
  - It does not clearly qualify the rep. Under MIPA §10.9, disclosure qualifies another section only if its relevance is "reasonably apparent on the face."
- Consequences for the Sellers and the Company:
  - Quillon can refuse to close because the reps are not true at closing (MIPA §7.1(a)).
  - The §7.1(e) MAE condition may also be implicated. Expiry of the DA with the supplier of 91% of purchases is hard to square with the MAE definition (§1.10), whose only carve-outs are general conditions, law changes, and announcement of the deal.
  - Quillon can terminate after a 20-day cure period (MIPA §9.1(c)).
  - Each Seller must sign a closing certificate confirming the reps are true (MIPA §7.1(c)). Neither can do so truthfully without a fix.
  - Post-closing, the Sellers are jointly and severally exposed to indemnity claims (MIPA §8.2). General-rep claims are capped at the $4.85M escrow (MIPA §8.4(b)). Fraud claims are carved out of the basket and the escrow cap (MIPA §8.4), and the Purchase Price cap in the last sentence of §8.4 still applies. A Seller who signs a rep known to be untrue invites a fraud allegation.
- The DA's first-and-only-renewal structure (§3.2: "one (1) additional term") also means that even a renewal gives only three years of runway, ending Nov. 14, 2029.

**Recommended fix**
1. Confirm the facts (Action A). If a compliant notice was delivered by about Aug. 16, the substantive issue falls away. Even then, correct the Schedule note, which is misleading.
2. If no notice was delivered, obtain a signed written instrument from Velloran under DA §19.2 and §19.3 that confirms the Renewal Term, stating that the Renewal Notice is timely or that Velloran waives the §3.2 deadline. A conduct-based or email-based waiver is not enough. Section 19.3 requires a signed writing and §19.2 requires signatures of authorized representatives. Combine this with the change-of-control consent (Issue 2).
3. Get Quillon's consent under MIPA §6.1(c) before signing any amendment or waiver.
4. Still deliver a protective, formally compliant Renewal Notice now (by hand or courier, to the VP Channel Sales, with a copy to the General Counsel). Do this only with the client's approval, and expect Velloran to treat it as a request for relief.
5. In parallel, negotiate with Quillon's counsel the MIPA treatment if Velloran will not cooperate by closing (see Issue 3).

### Issue 2 – Velloran's prior written consent to change of control is required; not scheduled (CRITICAL)

**Where it appears**
- DA §1.3(a): a Change of Control includes any transfer of more than 50% of Distributor's equity. The MIPA sells 100% (MIPA §2.1).
- DA §14.2: a Change of Control is "deemed an assignment" and "requires the prior written consent of Supplier."
- DA §14.3 and §15.4: if a Change of Control occurs without prior written consent, Velloran "may terminate this Agreement immediately upon written notice." There is no cure period.
- MIPA §3.5(c): no conflict with any Material Contract, or right of termination, except for Required Consents.
- MIPA §3.6: no consents are required beyond those on Schedule 3.6.
- MIPA Sched. 3.6: lists only the Brandywine Flats landlord and Keystone Commonwealth Bank. Velloran is not listed.
- MIPA §6.4: the Sellers have no obligation to obtain any consent that is not a Required Consent.
- MIPA §7.1(d): the closing condition covers only Required Consents.

**Why it matters**
- Closing without Velloran's prior consent gives Velloran an immediate termination right over the contract that supplies about 91% of purchases.
- The "prior" requirement means that consent cannot be cured after closing.
- The reps in §§3.5(c) and 3.6 are inaccurate as written. Same exposure as Issue 1: bring-down failure (§7.1(a)), closing certificate (§7.1(c)), indemnity (§8.2(a)), termination (§9.1(c)).
- The Sellers have no covenant to chase the consent, and Quillon is not required to pay for or concede anything to get one (§6.4). The consent is therefore unmanaged on both sides.
- Velloran has leverage and may use the request to reopen terms.
  - It could ask for a price, minimum-purchase, or term concession.
  - It may object if Quillon or its affiliates carry competing lines (see Issue 4).
- There is no confirmation in the folder that Velloran knows of the transaction.

**Recommended fix**
1. Request Velloran's written consent now, as one package with the renewal fix (Issue 1). The consent should:
   - name Quillon;
   - be unconditional;
   - expressly cover the DA as amended by the FA and supplemented by the SL;
   - confirm no default exists and no shortfall notice has been given.
2. Add the Velloran consent to Schedule 3.6, or amend MIPA §3.5/3.6, so the reps are accurate. This is a client trade-off to discuss.
   - Listing the consent as a Required Consent makes it an express closing condition (§7.1(d)) and a §6.4 efforts covenant. It removes the indemnity exposure for the inaccurate rep.
   - It gives Quillon a clean right to walk if Velloran does not consent. Quillon already has that right through the bring-down condition (§7.1(a)), so the practical downside is limited.
3. Negotiate with Quillon's counsel who bears Velloran's price of consent (§6.4 currently says Quillon bears none).
4. Obtain Velloran's consent to disclosure of DA terms and pricing to Quillon (Issue 7), in the same letter.

### Issue 3 – MIPA mechanics: covenants, conditions and Seller exposure (HIGH)

**Where it appears**
- MIPA §6.1(c): without Buyer's consent, the Sellers cannot cause the Company to "amend, terminate or waive any material right under any Material Contract."
- MIPA §6.5: each Party must "promptly notify" the others of any fact that would reasonably be expected to cause a closing condition to fail by the Outside Date.
- MIPA §§7.1(a), (c), (e); §9.1(b)–(c); §8.2; §8.4; §8.5; §10.9; §5.5.
- MIPA §3.12(d): the Sellers represent that true and complete copies of all Material Contracts, including amendments and side letters, were made available.

**Why it matters**
- Negotiating the Velloran renewal or consent likely requires Buyer's prior written consent (§6.1(c), not to be unreasonably withheld). Working without it risks a covenant breach (§7.1(b), §8.2(b)).
- The known facts in Issues 1 and 2 trigger the §6.5 notice duty. Silence is itself a covenant breach and aggravates any fraud theory.
- The MIPA has no mechanism for updating the Schedules after signing. The reps speak at signing and at closing (Art. 3 lead-in), and the bring-down in §7.1(a) is "in all material respects."
- The MIPA gives the Sellers no price-adjustment or extension mechanism if Velloran delays. The Outside Date (Jan. 29, 2027) is the only backstop. The DA expires first.
- Drafting inconsistencies to fix while the MIPA is open:
  - §8.4(b) caps general-rep liability at the escrow, but §8.5 says claims are satisfied "first" from escrow, implying recourse beyond it.
  - The Sellers are jointly and severally liable (§8.2) even though Ms. Hollister owns only 40%.
  - §5.5 contains a non-reliance statement. The MIPA says nothing about whether Buyer's knowledge limits its indemnity claims. That is relevant to whether Quillon's own diligence knowledge of the renewal gap helps the Sellers.

**Recommended fix**
- Send Quillon's counsel a written request for §6.1(c) consent to negotiate with Velloran, and a §6.5 notice, coordinated with the client's instructions.
- Propose a short MIPA amendment (MIPA §10.3) covering:
  - corrected §3.12(c) language and Schedule 3.12 note;
  - Velloran consent on Schedule 3.6, or a specific closing condition and extension mechanism if Velloran is slow;
  - a Schedule-update right;
  - an express statement that Buyer's pre-closing knowledge does not limit its remedies, or the reverse, depending on which side the client prefers;
  - cleanup of §8.4/§8.5;
  - a Sellers' contribution arrangement between Esterly and Hollister.
- Do not let either Seller sign the §7.1(c) certificate until the reps are accurate or amended.

### Issue 4 – Side Letter non-compete: wider territory, binds Affiliates (incl. Quillon post-closing), 18-month tail "for any reason" (HIGH)

**Where it appears**
- SL §1.1: for SL purposes, "Territory" means DE, MD, NJ, PA, **New York and Virginia**. The DA Territory is DE, MD, NJ and PA only (DA §1.12).
- SL §3.1: exclusivity "continues as set forth in Section 2.1 of the Distribution Agreement." The SL "does not modify Section 2.1."
- SL §2.1: Distributor may not, "directly or indirectly, and shall cause its Affiliates not to," market or sell Competing Products in the Territory.
- SL §2.2: Distributor cannot use an Affiliate, member, manager or employee to avoid the restriction.
- SL §1.2: "Competing Products" means precision measurement instruments, laboratory balances, calibration standards "or related accessories or consumables" that are substantially similar or marketed as substitutes.
- SL §1.3: the Restricted Period is the Term plus 18 months after expiration or termination "for any reason."
- SL §4.2 (tolling) and §4.3: a breach during the Term is a material breach under DA §15.1.
- DA §1.1: "Affiliate" means anyone that controls, or is controlled by or under common control with, the Person.
- DA §19.4: the SL ranks above the body of the DA in a conflict. The First Amendment ranks above the SL.

**Why it matters**
- **One-sided territory.** The non-compete reaches NY and VA, but exclusivity covers only the four DA states. Harrow Creek gets no exclusivity in the two added states and is shut out of competing there. The SL recites only "continued appointment" as consideration (SL preamble).
- **Quillon and its affiliates.** After closing, Quillon controls the Company and becomes an Affiliate. The covenant would require Quillon to cause its affiliates (not just the Company) to stay out of Competing Products across six states for the Term plus 18 months. Quillon's counsel may not have noticed this. If any Quillon portfolio company sells competing instruments, accessories or consumables, closing could put the Company in breach. That would be a termination ground (SL §4.3 and DA §15.1), and Velloran could seek injunctive relief without bond (SL §4.1). It will also affect whether Velloran consents.
- **Tail risk.** The 18-month tail runs after expiration or termination "for any reason" (SL §1.3). If the DA lapses on Nov. 14, 2026 through the missed renewal (Issue 1), the Company would lose the Velloran business and still be barred from competing until about May 2028 in six states. The tail also applies if Velloran terminates for the Company's own shortfall or even in a dispute where Velloran is at fault.
- **Accessories.** The FA added Accessories as Products (FA §2.1, §2.2). The SL's "related accessories or consumables" language means Competing Products now overlaps with a product category the Company must carry exclusively. The Company's other suppliers are packaging, freight and office vendors (Sched. 3.13), so I see no current non-compliance. This should be confirmed with the client.
- Whether a court would enforce the 18-month tail and the NY/VA scope is for separate research. SL §7.2 lets a court narrow the covenant, but we should not rely on that.

**Recommended fix**
- Put the issue squarely in front of Quillon's counsel and ask for its affiliates' product lines (Action D).
- In the Velloran package, seek a short SL amendment (SL §7.1, signed writing) to:
  - limit "Affiliates" to the Company's subsidiaries, or exclude Quillon and its other portfolio companies;
  - conform the non-compete territory to the DA Territory, or extend exclusivity to NY and VA;
  - shorten or eliminate the post-term tail, at least where the DA ends by Velloran's non-renewal, Velloran's breach or Velloran's termination without cause.
- Have the client confirm in writing that it sells no Competing Products today and has given the SL §3.3 notices of competing opportunities.
- Add a MIPA rep or schedule entry disclosing the SL's reach so there is no later argument about concealment (the SL is already listed in Sched. 3.12, item 3, but the affiliate effect is not described).

### Issue 6 – Conflicting governing law and forum; order of precedence (MEDIUM)

**Where it appears**
- DA §18.1–18.2: Delaware law; exclusive Delaware courts in Wilmington.
- SL §6.1–6.2: New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. The clause covers "any dispute arising out of the exclusivity and non-competition arrangements between the parties."
- SL §4.3: an SL breach is a material breach under DA §15.1.
- DA §19.4: the SL outranks the DA body in a conflict.
- FA §5.2: Delaware law and forum apply to the FA.

**Why it matters**
- A single dispute (for example, Velloran terminating for an alleged non-compete breach) could be brought in two states under two laws: the non-compete in New York, the termination in Delaware.
- The SL's forum clause is broad enough to capture disputes about exclusivity under DA §2.1, which belongs in Delaware. That invites a fight over forum before the merits.
- Because the SL ranks above the DA body (§19.4), Velloran can argue the SL's New York provisions control wherever they conflict.
- The Company is a Pennsylvania-based business, and the Sellers are in PA and NJ. Which state's law applies to the non-compete may change its enforceability. That needs research.

**Recommended fix**
- In the SL amendment (Issue 4), conform the SL to Delaware law and forum, or at minimum limit the SL's New York clause to claims under SL §2.
- Add a DA/SL cross-reference stating that exclusivity and termination under the DA remain governed by DA §18.

### Issue 7 – Disclosure of DA terms to Quillon may breach DA §12 (MEDIUM)

**Where it appears**
- DA §12.1: Confidential Information includes non-public business and financial information, including pricing and customer lists.
- DA §12.2: it may be disclosed only to the receiving Party's employees, Affiliates and professional advisers who need to know it and are bound by confidentiality duties.

**Recommended fix**
- Confirm whether a Quillon NDA exists and whether Velloran has been told about the sale.
