## 2. Urgent issues

### Issue 1: Auto-renewal for five years; non-renewal window closes in about two weeks

- **Where:** Agr. §2.1, §2.2, §4.3, §16.1–16.2; Sched. B, Part 2.
- **What it says:**
  - The Initial Term ends June 30, 2027.
  - Absent notice of non-renewal given "not more than 270 days nor less than 240 days" before expiration, the Agreement renews for **five years**.
  - Notice must go by hand, courier or certified mail. Email alone is not notice (§16.1).
  - By my count the window runs about Oct 3 to Nov 2, 2026. The Agreement does not say whether "expiration" falls at the start or end of June 30, 2027, so I would not rely on the last day.
- **Why it matters:**
  - A renewal locks Halvorsen into exclusivity, a non-compete (§3.3), and a purchase minimum for five more years.
  - The minimum steps up 5% over Contract Year 3's $2.4M (§4.3), so about $2.52M.
  - There is no termination for convenience (§14).
  - Prices can be raised once a year with no cap (Issue 3), and disputes go to Delaware (Issue 4).
  - Brightmere can also give non-renewal notice in the same window. If it has, or does, Halvorsen loses the line after June 30, 2027, with only the §14.3 sell-off or repurchase.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Get the client's decision this week on whether it wants five more years on current terms, given the 14% increase.
  - Ask Brightmere now for a **signed written extension of the window** (§18.2 requires a signed writing). Tie it to renegotiating Issues 3 to 7.
  - If there is no signed extension by about Oct 27, advise the client on sending a **protective non-renewal notice** by courier to Brightmere's General Counsel at the §16.1 address. Confirmed delivery should come by Oct 30.
  - A protective notice preserves leverage but risks losing the line, so it is the client's call.
- **Open points:**
  - Has Brightmere sent or threatened non-renewal?
  - Does Halvorsen want to renew at all?
  - §4.3 is ambiguous on whether the 5% compounds each renewal year or is a one-time step-up from the final initial-term year. Clarify it in any amendment.

### Issue 2: The 14% price increase (Notice)

**2A. The objection window appears to have lapsed**

- **Where:** Agr. §5.3; Notice §§2.1, 3.1 and Delivery Confirmation.
- **What it says:**
  - The Notice was delivered by FedEx on **Aug 21, 2026**. That is receipt under §16.2 (deemed received on courier delivery confirmation).
  - The 30-day objection period under §5.3 therefore ended **Sept 20, 2026**.
  - Absent an objection, Halvorsen is "deemed to have accepted" the adjusted Prices.
  - The signature on the courier confirmation is "D. Marchetti". Internal awareness is irrelevant, because the clock ran from delivery.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The §5.3 options (negotiate for 30 days at existing Prices, or remove the affected Products with a proportionate reduction in the minimum) are probably gone.
  - On $2.4M of annual purchases, a 14% increase is roughly **$336,000 a year** if volumes hold. That is an illustration only; we have no purchase data.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - First, confirm with the client whether anyone objected, how, and when.
    - A timely objection delivered by courier preserves the options.
    - An email-only objection is vulnerable under §16.1, though Brightmere's conduct may help.
  - If nothing timely was sent, treat the increase as binding. Ask Brightmere commercially for a phase-in or a price cap, and use the renewal decision (Issue 1) as leverage.
  - Do not assume a legal escape from the increase.
- **What I checked and found fine:**
  - All nine adjusted prices equal the current price × 1.14, rounded (Notice §3.1, §3.2).
  - The Notice gives 72 days' lead time against the 60-day minimum (§5.2).
  - It identifies the Products, the Prices and the effective date (§5.2), and was sent by courier to the §16.1 address.
- **One fact to confirm:** §5.2 allows only one adjustment per Contract Year, and Notice §2.3 says this is the only one in Contract Year 3. Ask the client whether any adjustment was noticed between July 1 and August 20, 2026.

**2B. Pre-increase purchases (time-sensitive)**

- **Where:** Notice §2.2; Sched. C §§3.4, 3.5; Agr. §6.1, §14.3(b).
- **What it says:**
  - Under Notice §2.2, POs **accepted before Nov 1** are invoiced at current Prices. That is more generous than Agr. §5.2.
  - Sched. C §3.4 gives Brightmere 5 Business Days to accept; silence is deemed acceptance.
  - Deemed acceptance of a PO received after about Oct 23 would fall on or after Oct 30 to Nov 2, with a risk it is treated as post-Nov 1.
  - Agr. §6.1 bars unreasonable rejection only of POs "consistent with Distributor's most recent forecast".
- **Why it matters:** Halvorsen can lock in current Prices on forecast-level inventory, subject to cash and storage.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Submit POs by **Oct 23**, consistent with the last forecast.
  - Request express written Order Acknowledgments (Sched. C §3.5) before Oct 30.
  - Ask Brightmere to confirm in writing that the Notice §2.2 treatment applies to any PO received before Nov 1.
- **Risks and unknowns:**
  - Cash flow, and 45-day payment terms from shipment (Agr. §5.4).
  - Whether cartridges have a shelf life (unknown).
  - The Agreement does not say when a purchase "counts" toward the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment (order, shipment or invoice). That matters for Contract Year 3; clarify it.
  - Some comfort: if the Agreement ends, Halvorsen can elect repurchase of saleable, original-packaged stock at Prices paid (§14.3(b)).
  - After Nov 1, audit the first invoices. The 15-day dispute window in §5.4 is short.

**2C. Structural weaknesses in §§5.2–5.3 to fix in any amendment**

- **Where:** Agr. §§5.2, 5.3; §1.1(d).
- **Problems:**
  - No cap or index on increases, and no cost substantiation.
  - §5.3(a) negotiation has no stated outcome if talks fail.
  - The 30-day objection period is short.
  - The minimum is defined as "dollar value at the then-current Prices" (§1.1(d)). It is unclear whether Schedule B's dollar figures are fixed or move with price increases.
- **Fix:**
  - Propose a cap (for example, CPI or a fixed percentage) with documented cost support.
  - Add a default outcome if negotiations fail, such as the increase being limited to the cap.
  - Add a longer objection window.
  - Confirm the Schedule B dollar minimums are fixed and not indexed to Prices.

## 3. Issues in the Amendment and the original terms

### Issue 3: Delaware law and forum slipped into the Amendment

- **Where:** Amendment §6.1–6.2; compare Agr. §17.1–17.2; Amendment §5.1 and Recital C; Agr. §1.4.
- **What it says:**
  - The Agreement said New York law and New York County courts.
  - Amendment §6 says the Amendment "and the Agreement as amended" are governed by **Delaware** law, with exclusive jurisdiction in **Wilmington, Delaware**.
  - Recital C describes only territory, products, prices and "certain conforming changes". Amendment §5.1 says everything not "expressly amended" is unchanged, but §6 does not name §17.
  - Agr. §1.4 ranks amendments first, so Delaware likely prevails. There is an argument that §6 did not "expressly" amend §17, but I would not rely on it.
- **Why it matters:** A New York distributor now litigates in Brightmere's home forum, with added cost and uncertainty. I have not compared Delaware and New York law on any issue here.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Ask the client whether this was discussed.
  - Ask Brightmere to restore New York law and New York County courts (§17), or agree a neutral forum, in the next amendment.
  - Any amendment should expressly amend §17 to remove the ambiguity.

### Issue 4: Brightmere's Recall-cost reimbursement is now capped

- **Where:** Amendment §3.1 (replacing Agr. §12.3); Agr. §9.3, §12.1, §12.2.
- **What it says:**
  - Original §12.3(b) excluded Brightmere's Recall-cost reimbursement (§9.3) from the consequential-damages bar and the cap.
  - The Amendment deletes that carve-out. Halvorsen's own §11.1 indemnity (§12.3(a)) and gross negligence or willful misconduct remain carved out.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Recall reimbursement under §9.3 is now limited to the amounts paid to Brightmere in the 12 months before the claim (§12.2).
  - It is also subject to the lost-revenue exclusion (§12.1). A large recall could exceed the cap.
  - Nothing in the Amendment shows what Halvorsen received for this. The change is not in the recitals.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Restore §12.3(b).
  - Add Brightmere's §11.2 indemnity, its §10.3 warranty remedies and breach of §3.2 (exclusivity) as carve-outs.
- **Questions for the client:**
  - Was this negotiated, and what did Halvorsen get in return?
  - Is any recall pending or threatened? Since March 1, 2026 it would be capped.

### Issue 5: One-sided indemnity

- **Where:** Agr. §11.1 vs §11.2; §12.3(a) (as amended, Amendment §3.1).
- **What it says:**
  - Halvorsen indemnifies Brightmere for **any** third-party claim "arising out of or relating to the distribution, marketing or resale of the Products", with no fault limiter. That is uncapped.
  - Brightmere's indemnity covers only IP infringement and bodily injury or property damage "caused by a manufacturing defect", and is capped.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Read literally, a claim caused by a Brightmere design defect, a false NSF claim (§8.3) or a Brightmere warranty failure could still "relate to" resale.
  - That would put the loss on Halvorsen, uncapped, to protect Brightmere.
  - This is the largest exposure in the Agreement.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Narrow §11.1 to claims caused by Halvorsen's breach, negligence, or unauthorized representations (compare §7.3).
  - Add an express exclusion for claims covered by §11.2.
  - Expand §11.2 to cover design defects, failure to meet §8.3 certifications and Recalls.
  - Make the cap treatment reciprocal.
- **Open point:** I need Halvorsen's insurance details (see Issue 12) to size the practical exposure.

### Issue 6: Lost profits are excluded, which blunts Halvorsen's remedies

- **Where:** Agr. §12.1; §3.2 (exclusivity), §8.1 (supply), §14.
- **Why it matters:** If Brightmere breaches exclusivity or fails to supply, Halvorsen's main loss is lost profit and lost revenue. Those are excluded, and the cap also applies. That makes exclusivity hard to enforce through damages.
- **Fix:**
  - Carve out breach of §3.2 and §8.1 from §12.1.
  - Alternatively, add a liquidated-damages formula or a termination right with a repurchase and compensation mechanism.

### Issue 7: Territory expansion: nothing actually resolves "Existing Accounts"

- **Where:** Amendment §2.1, §2.2; Agr. §3.2, §7.1.
- **What it says:**
  - Dutchess and Orange Counties were added to the Territory.
  - Amendment §2.2 is headed "Existing Accounts", but it only requires Brightmere to refer *inquiries* within 30 days (by March 31, 2026).
  - It says nothing about Brightmere's or other distributors' existing customers there. Agr. §3.2 bars Brightmere only from selling "for resale".
- **Why it matters:**
  - Halvorsen may not actually have exclusivity in the new counties if there are pre-existing relationships.
  - Halvorsen's promotion and sales-organization obligations (§7.1) now extend to the new counties, with no adjustment to the minimum purchase commitments.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Ask the client who sells Aquilon in Dutchess and Orange today.
  - Confirm in writing that Brightmere has ended any other distribution or supply there.
  - If Brightmere has kept direct accounts there, document a transition.
  - Confirm Brightmere made the §2.2 referrals.

### Issue 8: Exclusivity scope gaps

- **Where:** Agr. §1.1(f), §3.2, Sched. A.
- **Problems:**
  - "Products" means only what is listed in Schedule A "as updated by the Parties in writing". A new Aquilon model (as with the RO-750) is outside exclusivity until added.
  - §3.2 bars only sales "for resale", so direct-to-consumer sales or online sales into the Territory are not restricted.
- **Fix:** Add automatic inclusion of new and successor Aquilon products, and extend §3.2 to direct and online sales into the Territory.

### Issue 9: Distributor non-compete is vague and may already be breached

- **Where:** Agr. §3.3, §14.1.
- **What it says:** Halvorsen may not distribute in the Territory any residential water-filtration system that "competes directly" with the Products without Brightmere's written consent. "Competes directly" is undefined, and the Territory has grown.
- **Why it matters:** Recital B shows Halvorsen already distributes water-treatment products. Any competing line could be a breach, with a 30-day cure period.
- **Fix:**
  - Ask the client what other filtration lines it carries.
  - If any, get written consent now or define "competes directly" by product category.

### Issue 10: Minimum purchase commitment: need data

- **Where:** Agr. §4.1, §4.2, §4.3, §1.1(d); Amendment §4.4; Sched. B, Part 2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Minimums are $1.8M (Year 1), $2.1M (Year 2) and $2.4M (Year 3).
  - If Halvorsen misses a year, Brightmere may, within 60 days after year-end, convert the appointment to non-exclusive, or terminate if the shortfall exceeds 25% (§4.2).
  - Contract Year 2 ended June 30, 2026, so that 60-day window ended around Aug 29, 2026.
  - The new products count toward the minimum (Amendment §4.4), but the minimum was not adjusted for the larger Territory.
- **Need from client:**
  - Purchases for Contract Years 1 and 2 and Year 3 to date.
  - Any shortfall notice received.
- **Fix:** Clarify the §1.1(d) and §4.3 ambiguities and the timing of when a purchase "counts".

## 4. Schedule C and housekeeping

### Issue 11: Notice mechanics for operational notices

- **Where:** Agr. §16.1, §5.4, §6.4; Sched. C §5.3, §6.2, §9.3.
- **What it says:**
  - Agr. §16.1 requires "all notices under this Agreement" to go by hand, courier or certified mail, and email "alone shall not constitute notice".
  - Sched. C §9.3 says operational email contacts are for "day-to-day" matters.
  - But the 15-day invoice dispute (§5.4), rejection of non-conforming goods (§6.4 and Sched. C §5.3) and RMA requests (Sched. C §7.1) are all written notices with short windows. Sched. C contemplates email for them.
- **Why it matters:** Brightmere could argue an email-only dispute or rejection was ineffective. Halvorsen could lose a credit.
- **Fix:**
  - Immediately, send a courier copy for anything with a deadline.
  - Longer term, amend §16 to allow email for the §5.4, §6.4 and Sched. C notices.

### Issue 12: Transit damage vs FCA risk allocation

- **Where:** Agr. §6.3 (FCA; risk passes on delivery to the carrier); §6.4; Sched. C §§4.1, 5.1–5.4.
- **What it says:** Risk passes to Halvorsen at the carrier, yet Sched. C §5.3–5.4 has Brightmere replace or credit "damaged or short-shipped" goods. The Agreement body (§6.4) covers only "non-conforming" Products, and the body ranks above schedules (§1.4).
- **Why it matters:** Whether Halvorsen can claim for transit damage from Brightmere or only from the carrier is unclear. The inspection window is a short 15 days (Sched. C §5.2).
- **Fix:**
  - Clarify who bears transit damage.
  - Confirm Halvorsen carries cargo cover.
  - Consider a longer inspection window.

### Issue 13: Schedule C drafting and status

- **Where:** Sched. C §4.4; Agr. §13.2, §1.4, §1.2, Sched. C header.
- **Problems:**
  - Sched. C §4.4 says Brightmere shall not alter labeling "inconsistent with Section 13.2". But §13.2 restricts *Halvorsen*, not Brightmere. The cross-reference is wrong.
  - The Schedule C in the folder is undated, unsigned and has no version identifier. Agr. §1.2 and the Schedule C placeholder in the Agreement say it "forms part" of the Agreement.
  - Under Sched. C §9.4 and Agr. §18.2, changes require a signed amendment.
- **Fix:** Confirm with the client this is the version attached at signing, and correct the cross-reference in the next amendment.

### Issue 14: Traceability compliance ties to recall recovery

- **Where:** Agr. §7.4, §9.3; Sched. C §8.1–8.2.
- **Why it matters:** Halvorsen must keep records by Customer and lot number for three years, and give Brightmere a customer list within 5 Business Days of a request. Recall reimbursement is excluded if the Recall results "solely" from Halvorsen's breach or negligent handling (§9.3). Poor records could give Brightmere a defense.
- **Ask the client:** Does it actually record lot numbers by Customer? If not, fix that now.

### Issue 15: Missing documents and warranty gap

- **Where:** Sched. A, Part 3 (Form AQ-LW-2024, "as attached"); Agr. §10.1, §10.2, §13.1.
- **Problems:**
  - The end-user warranty (Form AQ-LW-2024) is not in the folder. Neither are the brand guidelines (§13.1).
  - Brightmere's warranty to Halvorsen runs 24 months from delivery (§10.1). The end-user warranty may run longer, and Halvorsen must pass it through unmodified (§10.2).
- **Why it matters:** If the end-user warranty outlasts Halvorsen's 24-month recourse, Halvorsen carries the gap.
- **Fix:** Obtain and review both documents before the call.

### Issue 16: Execution copies

- **Where:** Signature blocks in the Agreement, Amendment and Notice.
- **Observation:** All three show blank signature lines (only names and dates are filled in). I assume these are conformed copies.
- **Fix:** Get fully executed copies. Agr. §18.2 requires amendments to be signed.

### Issue 17: Insurance

- **Where:** Agr. §18.4.
- **What it says:** Each party must carry CGL insurance, including products and completed operations coverage, of at least $2M per occurrence and $5M aggregate, for the Term plus two years.
- **Ask the client:** Does it carry this coverage? Get the certificate. Also request Brightmere's certificate (available on request).

### Issue 18: Passing the increase to customers

- **Where:** Agr. §3.4, §15.1.
- **Observation:** Halvorsen sets its own resale prices (§3.4) and can pass the increase on. Prices are Brightmere confidential information (§15.1), so customer communications should announce Halvorsen's new prices without disclosing Brightmere's price list.
- **Ask the client:** Does it have fixed-price commitments to dealers that run past Nov 1?
