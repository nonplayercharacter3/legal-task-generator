## 3. Issues

### A. Time-critical

**Issue 1 – Auto-renewal for five years; window closes about Nov. 2**
- **Where:** Agmt §§2.1, 2.2, 4.3, 16.1–16.2; Sched. B Part 2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Absent notice, the Agreement renews for five years. Halvorsen has no termination-for-convenience right (Agmt §14 covers only breach and insolvency).
  - The minimum becomes the Year 3 figure plus 5%, which is $2.52M if Year 3 is $2.4M (Agmt §4.3).
  - Brightmere has just imposed a 14% increase with no cap on future ones.
  - Notice must go by hand, overnight courier or certified mail and is effective on confirmed delivery. **Email alone is not notice** (Agmt §16.1).
  - Brightmere has the same non-renewal right. Halvorsen should confirm it has not already received one.
- **Fix:** Decide this week, in this order of preference.
  1. Preferably, negotiate a signed amendment under Agmt §18.2 before Nov. 2. It would extend the non-renewal window or Initial Term while terms are renegotiated (see Issues 5–12).
  2. If that cannot be signed in time, send a protective non-renewal notice by courier to Brightmere's General Counsel at 4100 Concord Pike (Agmt §16.1), so it arrives by Oct. 30. Pair it with an offer to negotiate a new term. Obtain a partner and client decision first, because the notice cannot safely be assumed to be withdrawable.
  3. If Halvorsen wants to renew on current terms, no action is needed. It should do so knowingly.
- Non-renewal is not a cliff edge. Halvorsen gets a 180-day sell-off, or can require Brightmere to repurchase saleable stock at the prices Halvorsen paid (Agmt §14.3(b); Sched. C §7.5).

**Issue 2 – 14% price increase: objection right lapsed; mitigate with a pre-buy**
- **Where:** Notice §§2.1–2.3, 3.1; Agmt §§5.2, 5.3, 16.1.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Compliance.** The Notice appears compliant. It was sent by courier to the §16.1 address, gave 72–73 days' notice against the 60 required, and identified products, prices and the effective date. I re-computed all nine adjusted prices and they are arithmetically correct at +14%.
  - **Lapsed objection.**
    - Under Agmt §5.3, Halvorsen had 30 days from receipt to object. The notice had to satisfy §16.1, and Halvorsen had to elect either (a) 30 days of good-faith negotiation at existing Prices or (b) removal of affected Products with a proportionate cut to the minimum. If no objection was made, the increase is "deemed accepted."
    - If Halvorsen did object, §5.3 does not say what happens when the 30-day negotiation fails. Brightmere will say the Nov. 1 prices apply.
  - **Pre-buy.** Notice §2.2 says POs *accepted* before Nov. 1 are invoiced at current prices. Under Sched. C §3.4, a PO is deemed accepted if Brightmere does not respond within 5 Business Days. A PO emailed Fri. Oct. 23 is deemed accepted by Fri. Oct. 30. A PO emailed Mon. Oct. 26 is not deemed accepted until Nov. 2.
  - **Limits on a pre-buy.**
    - Brightmere may reject POs that are not "consistent with Distributor's most recent forecast" (Agmt §6.1).
    - Payment falls due 45 days after invoice (Agmt §5.4).
    - Shelf life of cartridges is unknown.
    - Purchases count toward the CY3 minimum (Agmt §4.1; Amend. §4.4).
    - Stock bought at pre-increase prices is protected by the repurchase right if the relationship ends (Agmt §14.3(b)).
- **Fix:**
  - Confirm with the client whether any objection was sent and how.
  - Have the client place a right-sized pre-buy PO by email to orders@brightmere-instruments.com by Oct. 23. Request a written Order Acknowledgment stating current Prices (Sched. C §§3.1(c), 3.5).
  - Confirm the Q4 forecast (due "the fifteenth day of each calendar quarter," Agmt §4.4; Sched. C §2.1) was sent, and update it to support the PO. That wording is ambiguous, and Oct. 15 may have been the date.
  - Use the renewal window (Issue 1) as leverage to seek a price rollback or phase-in.
  - Seek a cap on future increases (Issue 12).
  - Note the Notice cites "eighteen months" of cost increases (§2.1), yet the RO-750 and RC-7S were first priced on March 1, 2026 (Amend. §4.2). That is a negotiating point for those SKUs, not a contractual right.

### B. Changes made by the First Amendment

**Issue 3 – Delaware law and Wilmington courts replace New York**
- **Where:** Amend. §§6.1–6.2 vs. Agmt §§17.1–17.2; Agmt §1.4; Amend. Recital C.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The Agreement is governed by New York law with exclusive New York County courts.
  - The Amendment states that the "Agreement as amended" is governed by Delaware law and litigated exclusively in Wilmington. Under the precedence clause (Agmt §1.4), the Amendment wins.
  - Recital C describes only Territory, Products, Prices and "certain conforming changes," so this is not a conforming change.
  - Halvorsen now litigates in Brightmere's home state under law its counsel may not know.
  - Section 17 of the Agreement remains in the text and is listed as surviving (Agmt §14.4). The two clauses conflict, which invites a forum fight.
  - **I have not analysed whether any New York statutory protection is lost.** That needs separate review.
- **Fix:**
  - Ask the client whether this was a negotiated trade or simply not noticed.
  - Seek to restore New York law and forum, or at least a neutral forum, in the renewal-driven amendment.
  - Delete the conflicting clause so there is one rule.

**Issue 4 – Recall reimbursement now falls under the liability cap**
- **Where:** Amend. §3.1 (deleting former Agmt §12.3(b)); Agmt §§9.3, 12.1, 12.2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Original §12.3(b) excluded Brightmere's Recall-cost reimbursement (Agmt §9) from both the cap and the consequential-damages bar.
  - Amendment §3.1 deletes it. The Amendment's Recital C does not mention this.
  - Brightmere's §9.3 obligation is now capped at amounts Halvorsen paid in the prior 12 months (Agmt §12.2). Brightmere can also argue that customer credits and lost sales are "consequential" or "lost revenue" under §12.1.
  - Halvorsen's own indemnity under §11.1 remains uncapped (Amend. §3.1(a)). The Amendment thus widened the asymmetry.
  - A whole-home system recall could exceed 12 months' purchases.
- **Fix:** Reinstate the carve-out for Agmt §9 and, ideally, for Brightmere's §11.2 indemnity (see Issue 5). Ask the client who negotiated this and whether anything was given in exchange. Check executed copies (Issue 17).

### C. Original-Agreement terms to renegotiate

**Issue 5 – Indemnities are lopsided and overlap**
- **Where:** Agmt §§11.1, 11.2, 12.2, 12.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Halvorsen indemnifies Brightmere for *any* third-party claim "arising out of or relating to" distribution, marketing or resale (§11.1). That plainly reaches product-defect claims, and it is uncapped.
  - Brightmere indemnifies only for IP infringement and bodily injury or property damage from a *manufacturing* defect (§11.2). It does not cover design defects, failure to warn, or breaches of the NSF/regulatory commitments in §8.3. It is also *capped*, because §12.3 does not carve out §11.2.
  - For a manufacturing-defect injury, both indemnities apply, with no priority rule.
- **Fix:**
  - Narrow §11.1 to claims caused by Halvorsen's breach, negligence, or unauthorised representations (see §7.3).
  - Expressly make §11.1 yield to §11.2.
  - Widen §11.2 to design defects, warnings and regulatory non-compliance.
  - Add §11.2 to the cap carve-outs.
  - Confirm Halvorsen's insurance meets §18.4 ($2M/$5M CGL with products coverage). Request additional-insured status on Brightmere's policy.

**Issue 6 – Lost profits are excluded, which guts remedies for an exclusive distributor**
- **Where:** Agmt §12.1; §§3.2, 8.1.
- **Why it matters:** Halvorsen's real damages from Brightmere's breach of exclusivity or failure to supply are lost profits, which §12.1 bars.
- **Fix:** Carve out breach of §3.2 (exclusivity), wrongful termination, and supply failure from §12.1.

**Issue 7 – No firm supply commitment, yet Halvorsen is bound to a minimum**
- **Where:** Agmt §§4.1, 4.2, 6.1, 8.1, 18.3; Sched. C §§3.3–3.5.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Brightmere must fill only *accepted* POs (§8.1). It may reject POs, subject to a not-unreasonable standard tied to forecasts (§6.1).
  - Sched. C sets only "commercially reasonable efforts" on lead time (§3.3), and there is no remedy for late delivery.
  - Nothing reduces Halvorsen's minimum if Brightmere rejects or delays orders. A shortfall exposes Halvorsen to loss of exclusivity or termination (§4.2).
- **Fix:** Reduce the minimum by the value of POs rejected or delivered late. Add a delivery-performance standard and an allocation rule for shortages.

**Issue 8 – Renewal minimum is ambiguous and does not track price changes**
- **Where:** Agmt §§1.1(d), 4.3, 2.2; Sched. B Part 2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - §4.3 sets each Renewal Year at the "final Contract Year of the immediately preceding term" plus 5%. It is unclear whether that is one flat figure for all five years or a compounding increase.
  - §1.1(d) defines the minimum as a "quantity… expressed in dollar value at the then-current Prices," while Sched. B states fixed dollars. After a 14% increase, it is unclear whether the dollar figure is fixed (fewer units required) or rises.
  - The meaning of "purchase" (ordered, accepted, shipped, invoiced) is undefined. That matters for the timing of a pre-buy across Contract Years.
- **Fix:**
  - Clarify in writing that the minimum is a fixed dollar amount and that renewal years are flat, or that increases are capped.
  - Define "purchase" by Brightmere's acceptance of the PO.
  - Ask for a price-linked reduction in the minimum.

**Issue 9 – The non-compete survives loss of exclusivity**
- **Where:** Agmt §§3.3, 4.2(a).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Under §4.2(a), a shortfall lets Brightmere convert Halvorsen to non-exclusive. Nothing says the §3.3 restriction on carrying competing residential water-filtration systems then falls away. Halvorsen could be left non-exclusive but still restricted.
  - "Competes directly" is undefined. Recital B says Halvorsen distributes water-treatment products broadly, so **there may be an existing conflict**. Ask the client.
- **Fix:**
  - Provide that §3.3 lapses on conversion or termination.
  - Define "competes directly."
  - Schedule any existing carve-outs.

**Issue 10 – Exclusivity is narrower than it sounds**
- **Where:** Agmt §§3.1, 3.2; Amend. §2.2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - §3.2 bars sales "for resale" in the Territory. It does not clearly bar Brightmere's direct sales to end users, e-commerce, or builders.
  - Amendment §2.2 requires only that Brightmere *refer inquiries* from Dutchess and Orange within 30 days (by March 31, 2026). Despite its title ("Existing Accounts"), it does not address accounts Brightmere or another distributor already served there.
- **Fix:**
  - Extend the §3.2 restriction to direct sales and online sales in the Territory.
  - Obtain a list of Brightmere's pre-existing Dutchess/Orange accounts and distributors, and a commitment on their transition.
  - Ask the client what has been referred to date.

**Issue 11 – Shortfall mechanics (positive and negative)**
- **Where:** Agmt §4.2; Sched. B Part 2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The *sole remedy* language is helpful and worth protecting.
  - Brightmere's 60-day window to act on CY2 (minimum $2.1M) ran to about Aug. 29, 2026. Confirm no notice was received.
  - For CY3 (minimum $2.4M), a shortfall of more than 25% allows termination on 90 days' notice. That could land after the Agreement has auto-renewed.
- **Fix:** Get year-to-date purchase figures (Section 4). Keep the sole-remedy wording in any renegotiation.

**Issue 12 – No cap on price increases**
- **Where:** Agmt §5.2; Notice §2.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - §5.2 allows one increase per Contract Year, with no ceiling or justification requirement. The Notice's promise of no further CY3 increase says nothing about CY4.
  - Because the clause counts by notice date, a June 2027 notice could stack on an August 2026 increase.
- **Fix:** Cap annual increases (for example, at a fixed percentage or an index), require cost support, and lengthen the notice period. Restore a workable objection and exit right (the current §5.3(b) can be used only within 30 days).

**Issue 13 – Assignment**
- **Where:** Agmt §18.1.
- **Why it matters:** Either party may assign without consent to a successor to its business. Brightmere could therefore pass the exclusive relationship to an acquirer, including a competitor of Halvorsen's other lines.
- **Fix:** Lower priority. Seek notice and a right to terminate on assignment to a competitor.

### D. Schedule C and operational housekeeping

**Issue 14 – "Formal notice" rules vs. day-to-day email practice**
- **Where:** Agmt §§5.4, 6.4, 16.1; Sched. C §§5.3, 7.1, 9.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - §16.1 says "all notices under this Agreement" must go by hand, courier or certified mail. Sched. C §9.3 treats only "formal notices" that way.
  - Rejection notices (§6.4), invoice-dispute notices (§5.4) and non-conformity notices (Sched. C §5.3) are "written notice" with deadlines of 15 days. Strictly, an email may not satisfy them.
  - Under §1.4, the Agreement body outranks Schedule C.
- **Fix:** Add a sentence to Sched. C (by signed amendment, per Sched. C §9.4 and Agmt §18.2) that operational notices may be given by email to the Section 9 contacts. Until then, send important rejection and dispute notices by courier as well as email.

**Issue 15 – Invoice-dispute window can expire before inspection ends**
- **Where:** Agmt §5.4; Sched. C §§5.2, 5.3, 6.1.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Brightmere invoices on shipment, and the dispute window is 15 days from receipt of the invoice.
  - The inspection period is also 15 days, but it runs from receipt of the goods, which comes later.
  - A defect found on day 14 of inspection may come after the invoice-dispute window has closed.
  - §5.4 does not say whether a late dispute is waived.
- **Fix:** Run the dispute period from the end of the inspection period, and state that a late dispute is not waived. Meanwhile, the client's AP team should dispute proactively.

**Issue 16 – Transit damage under FCA terms**
- **Where:** Agmt §6.3; Sched. C §§4.1, 4.3, 5.1–5.4.
- **Why it matters:** Risk passes to Halvorsen on delivery to the carrier. Yet Sched. C §§5.3–5.4 let Halvorsen report "damaged or short-shipped" goods and require Brightmere to replace them or credit their Price. It is unclear who bears transit loss, and Brightmere may argue Halvorsen must claim against the carrier.
- **Fix:** Clarify that Brightmere bears loss from defective packing or loading and from shortages against the packing list. Have the client note visible damage on the delivery receipt (Sched. C §5.1). Also, Sched. C §7.5 says repurchase returns follow the RMA process, but it does not say who pays return freight on post-term repurchases (compare §7.2). Allocate it to Brightmere.

**Issue 17 – Unsigned copies; missing warranty form; Schedule C status**
- **Where:** Agmt signature page and Schedule A Part 3; Amend. signature page; Notice signature line; Agmt Sched. C.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The Agreement, Amendment and Notice in the folder all show blank signature lines (dates are filled in).
  - Amendment enforceability turns on a signed writing (Agmt §18.2). If the Amendment was never signed, the Delaware clause and the §12.3 change might not bind.
  - Form AQ-LW-2024, the end-user warranty "as attached" (Agmt Sched. A Part 3), is not in the folder. Halvorsen must pass it through unmodified (Agmt §10.2), and Sched. C §7.3 routes end-user claims to it. We cannot assess Halvorsen's exposure for claim handling.
  - The standalone Sched. C has no date, version or signature, so we cannot confirm it is the version in force at signing.
- **Fix:** Get fully executed copies and the warranty form from the client. Confirm there are no other amendments, side letters or Sched. C versions. Note that the executed copies could change the analysis in Issues 3 and 4.

**Issue 18 – Internal notice-handling**
- **Where:** Agmt §§16.1–16.2; Notice, Delivery Confirmation.
- **Why it matters:** The Notice was signed for on Aug. 21 by "D. Marchetti." Receipt is deemed on delivery (§16.2), so any delay in reaching Mr. Halvorsen does not extend deadlines.
- **Fix:** Have all courier and certified-mail deliveries from Brightmere routed to the Managing Member the same day, and calendar every contractual deadline on receipt. Confirm the §16.1 notice address and email remain correct.
