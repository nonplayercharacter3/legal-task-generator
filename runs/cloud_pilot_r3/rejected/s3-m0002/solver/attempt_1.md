- **Mitigating point:** Under §4.2, a shortfall is Brightmere's "sole remedy." The remedies are conversion to non-exclusive, or termination if the shortfall exceeds 25%. There is no damages claim for a shortfall.

## 3. Problems introduced or hidden by the First Amendment

### Issue 3: Governing law and forum flipped from New York to Delaware

- **Where:** Amendment §6.1–6.2, §5.1; Agreement §17.1, §17.2, §1.4, §18.2.
- **What it does:**
  - Agreement §17 selects New York law and the courts in **New York County**.
  - Amendment §6 provides that the Agreement as amended is governed by **Delaware** law, with exclusive jurisdiction in **Wilmington, Delaware** courts.
  - The Amendment's recitals describe only a territory and product update and "certain conforming changes" (Amendment Recital C). A change of law and forum is not a conforming change.
  - §5.1 says everything not expressly amended is unchanged, yet §6 overrides §17 without citing it.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Under the order of precedence in Agreement §1.4, "written amendments" rank first, so Delaware likely wins.
  - Halvorsen is a Port Jefferson, New York company. Delaware is Brightmere's home forum.
  - Disputes would cost more to litigate, and Halvorsen would be under a law its New York counsel has not advised on.
  - The Amendment does not formally amend §17, so there is also drafting uncertainty. Neither side can be sure which clause governs a given dispute.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Ask Brightmere to sign a short corrective amendment deleting Amendment §6 and restoring Agreement §17.
  - At minimum, make it expressly mutual and reasoned (for example, defendant's home forum).
  - Get the negotiation history (see Part 5) to see whether this was a conscious trade.

### Issue 4: Recall-cost carve-out deleted from liability cap

- **Where:** Amendment §3.1 (replacing Agreement §12.3); Agreement §9.3, §12.1, §12.2, original §12.3(b).
- **What changed:**
  - Original §12.3 excluded from both the consequential-damages bar (§12.1) and the cap (§12.2): (a) Halvorsen's §11.1 indemnity, (b) **Brightmere's recall-cost reimbursement under §9**, and (c) gross negligence or willful misconduct.
  - The Amendment deletes (b). Recital C and the heading ("Amendment to Section 12.3") do not flag it as a concession.
- **Why it matters:**
  - §9.3 still obliges Brightmere to reimburse recall costs: notification, retrieval, shipping, storage, disposal, customer refunds and credits, and the Price of recalled inventory.
  - Those payments are now subject to the cap in §12.2 (amounts Halvorsen paid in the prior 12 months, roughly $2.4M or more) and the bar on "lost revenue" and consequential loss in §12.1.
  - Brightmere can argue that downstream customer credits and similar items are consequential.
  - A large recall, especially after a pre-buy, could exceed the cap.
  - The change is **one-way**. Halvorsen's own uncapped indemnity (§12.3(a)) stays.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Restore §12.3(b), or at least carve §9.3 reimbursements out of §12.1 and §12.2.
  - Present it as correcting an unbalanced change.
  - Ask for the same treatment for Brightmere's §11.2 indemnity (Issue 5).

## 4. Structural issues in the Agreement and Schedule C

### Issue 5: Halvorsen's indemnity is broad, uncapped and not fault-based; Brightmere's is capped

- **Where:** Agreement §11.1, §11.2, §12.2, §12.3(a) (original and as amended), §18.4.
- **What it says:**
  - §11.1 requires Halvorsen to defend and indemnify Brightmere against **any** third-party claim "arising out of or relating to the distribution, marketing or resale of the Products." That can include product-defect claims over products Brightmere designs and makes.
  - It is **uncapped** (§12.3(a)).
  - Brightmere's reciprocal indemnity (§11.2) covers only IP infringement and bodily injury or property damage "caused by a manufacturing defect." It is **subject to** the §12.2 cap and the §12.1 exclusions.
- **Why it matters:**
  - This is the largest tail-risk item for a distributor.
  - A claim that a Brightmere product injured someone would fall in the §11.1 net.
  - The defect carve-out in §11.2 leaves design defects and warnings outside Brightmere's indemnity, although §10.1(b) warrants design defects.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Narrow §11.1 to claims caused by Halvorsen's breach, negligence, or unauthorized representations, and expressly exclude claims within §11.2.
  - Carve §11.2 out of the cap (add it to §12.3).
  - Extend §11.2 to design defects and failure to warn.
  - Ask the client to confirm its own insurance (Issue 15).
  - Consider asking for additional-insured (vendor's endorsement) status on Brightmere's policy. §18.4 requires coverage but does not require this.

### Issue 6: Halvorsen's remedies for breach of exclusivity or supply are hollow

- **Where:** Agreement §3.2, §8.1, §12.1, §12.2, §12.3, §17.3, §4.2.
- **What it says:**
  - §12.1 bars "lost profits or lost revenue," which is the main loss from breach of exclusivity or a supply failure.
  - §12.2 caps all liability at 12 months of purchases.
  - Neither §3.2 nor §8.1 is carved out of §12.3.
  - §17.3 preserves injunctive relief only for confidential information and IP, not exclusivity.
  - §3.2 restricts only sales "to any person **for resale** in the Territory." It does not bar direct sales to end users, and it does not address online sales or sales into the Territory by non-Territory resellers.
- **Why it matters:** Exclusivity is the core of Halvorsen's bargain, and the contractual remedy for breach is thin. Brightmere, by contrast, has defined, bounded remedies against Halvorsen under §4.2.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Carve breaches of §3.2 (and ideally §8.1) out of §12.1 and §12.2.
  - Add injunctive relief for breach of exclusivity to §17.3.
  - Broaden §3.2 to cover direct and online sales, and require Brightmere to route Territory inquiries and orders to Halvorsen.

### Issue 7: Territory expansion is vaguely implemented

- **Where:** Amendment §2.1, §2.2 ("Existing Accounts"); Agreement §3.2.
- **What it says:**
  - The Amendment adds Dutchess and Orange counties.
  - §2.2 is headed "Existing Accounts" but only requires Brightmere to refer **inquiries from prospective Customers** within 30 days of March 1, 2026. It does not say what happens to Brightmere's **existing** customers, dealers or other distributors in those counties.
  - Because §3.2 now covers those counties, any existing Brightmere sales there "for resale" may technically breach it.
- **Why it matters:** Unclear ownership of existing accounts invites disputes. The 30-day referral period ended about March 31, 2026. It is unclear whether Brightmere complied or whether Halvorsen has received any referrals.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Ask the client for facts (Part 5).
  - If there are gaps, paper a short side letter: Brightmere to list and transfer existing accounts, to confirm no other distributors in those counties, and to refer all prior inquiries.

### Issue 8: Halvorsen's non-compete is potentially broad

- **Where:** Agreement §3.3, Recital B.
- **What it says:**
  - Halvorsen may not distribute in the Territory any residential water-filtration system that "competes directly" with the Products without Brightmere's prior written consent.
  - Recital B describes Halvorsen as a wholesaler of "water-treatment" products generally.
- **Why it matters:** "Competes directly" is undefined. Any existing or future water-filtration line Halvorsen carries could be challenged, and the Territory is now larger.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Get a list of the client's other filtration lines.
  - Define "competes directly" (for example, same product category and price tier) and schedule existing lines as permitted.
  - Add a deemed-consent mechanism with a response deadline.

### Issue 9: Transit-damage risk under FCA vs. Schedule C inspection and remedy provisions

- **Where:** Agreement §6.3, §6.4; Schedule C §4.1, §4.3, §5.1–5.4, §7.2.
- **What it says:**
  - Under FCA terms, title and **risk of loss pass at delivery to the carrier** (Agreement §6.3; Schedule C §4.1).
  - Yet Schedule C §5.3–5.4 lets Halvorsen reject "damaged or short-shipped" goods and gives it a replacement or credit remedy. Agreement §6.4 refers only to "non-conforming" Products.
  - Under the Agreement's precedence clause (§1.4), the body outranks the Schedules.
- **Why it matters:** Brightmere can argue that transit damage is Halvorsen's risk, to be claimed against the carrier. That conflicts with the practical expectation in Schedule C. The point matters most on large pre-buy shipments.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Clarify in a Schedule C amendment (§9.4 requires a signed written amendment) that Brightmere bears replacement or credit for transit damage and shortage, or else
  - Confirm who files carrier claims, and have Halvorsen carry cargo insurance. In either case, add a short notice-of-loss procedure.

### Issue 10: Invoice-dispute window can expire before inspection is possible

- **Where:** Agreement §5.4; Schedule C §5.2, §5.3, §6.1, §6.2.
- **What it says:**
  - Brightmere invoices **on shipment** and emails the invoice to Halvorsen's accounts-payable contact (Schedule C §6.1–6.2).
  - Halvorsen must dispute an invoice within **15 days after receipt of the invoice** (§5.4).
  - Halvorsen has **15 days after receipt of the goods** to inspect (Schedule C §5.2).
- **Why it matters:** With transit time, the invoice-dispute window can close before the goods arrive or can be inspected. A shortage or defect found on inspection could arrive after the dispute window. Because invoices go to AP by email, the clock may start before the purchasing and receiving teams know.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Amend §5.4, or add to Schedule C §5, that a timely inspection or rejection notice under Schedule C §5.3 also counts as notice of dispute, and that the dispute period runs from the end of the inspection period.
  - Internally, route AP and receiving email through one tracker.

### Issue 11: Post-termination sell-off vs. repurchase is ambiguous; repurchase logistics are thin

- **Where:** Agreement §14.3(b); Schedule C §7.2, §7.5.
- **What it says:**
  - On expiry or termination, Halvorsen may sell off inventory for 180 days "**or**, at Distributor's election, Supplier shall repurchase saleable Products in their original packaging at the Prices paid."
  - It is unclear whether Halvorsen can sell for 180 days and then require repurchase of what is left.
  - Schedule C §7.5 routes repurchases through the RMA process, but §7.2 gives Supplier-paid shipping only for rejected and warranty returns. It is silent on repurchase freight and timing.
- **Why it matters:** This is Halvorsen's exit protection. A pre-buy before November 1 makes it more valuable and the ambiguity more costly.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Clarify that Halvorsen may sell for 180 days and then require repurchase of the remainder.
  - Provide that Brightmere pays freight and pays within a stated number of days.
  - Extend §7.2 of Schedule C to repurchase returns.

### Issue 12: Forecast may be late

- **Where:** Agreement §4.4; Schedule C §2.1–2.3; Agreement §6.1.
- **What it says:**
  - The rolling forecast is due by the 15th day of each calendar quarter, which for this quarter was **October 15, 2026**.
  - Forecasts are "non-binding" (§4.4), but §6.1 ties Brightmere's duty not to unreasonably reject Purchase Orders to Halvorsen's "most recent forecast."
- **Why it matters:**
  - A missing or stale forecast weakens Halvorsen's protection when placing pre-buy orders.
  - A missed forecast may also be cited as a performance lapse in a later negotiation.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Confirm with the client whether the forecast was submitted (by email to the order desk, Schedule C §2.3).
  - If not, send it immediately, sized to support the pre-buy.

### Issue 14: Minor cleanup items

- **Order of precedence (Agreement §1.4).** It lists "notices" below the Agreement body and Schedules. A unilateral Price Adjustment Notice is not a contract document that should sit in the hierarchy. Clarify when the Agreement is next amended.

- **Insurance (Agreement §18.4).** Each party must carry CGL of $2M per occurrence and $5M aggregate, with products coverage, for the Term plus two years. Confirm Halvorsen's actual coverage and consider requesting Brightmere's certificate.
- **Schedule C identity (Agreement §1.2, Schedule C stub).** The Agreement's Schedule C stub refers to a document called "Ordering and Logistics Procedures (Schedule C)." The folder document is titled "Schedule C — Ordering and Logistics Procedures" and is undated and unsigned. Confirm it is the version attached at signing, and whether it was amended since (it can only be amended in a signed writing, Schedule C §9.4).
