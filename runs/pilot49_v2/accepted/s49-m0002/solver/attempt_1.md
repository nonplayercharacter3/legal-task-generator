### 1. Kelbrin Merger Triggers Dronusk's Termination Right Under MSA § 14.3

**Where:** MSA § 1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 14.3; Tanair Letter to Dronusk dated October 5, 2026, §§ 1–3.

**Issue.** The "Change of Control" definition in MSA § 1 captures any transaction in which a person acquires more than 50% of the voting securities of Customer *or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Customer*. Tanair Holdings Corp. is Tanair's parent. The Kelbrin merger (announced September 29, 2026; expected close ~January 15, 2027) will place Tanair Holdings under Kelbrin's control. This is squarely a Change of Control under the definition.

MSA § 14.3 provides: "If Customer undergoes a Change of Control **without Provider's prior written consent**, Provider may terminate this Agreement and all Order Forms upon 30 days' written notice to Customer."

The October 5 letter is styled as a "Notice of Corporate Transaction." It explains the structure, confirms Tanair remains the same legal entity, and states that operations will continue unchanged. **It does not request Dronusk's consent under § 14.3.** It does not acknowledge that § 14.3 applies. It does not address what happens if Dronusk withholds consent.

**Why it matters.** If the merger closes on or about January 15, 2027 and Dronusk has not given written consent, Dronusk can terminate the MSA and all Order Forms on 30 days' notice—i.e., by approximately February 14, 2027. That falls in the middle of Window 3 (January 5–April 30, 2027), when the remaining ~25% of Lines A and B volume (Hawaii and Puerto Rico off-season nurseries) is being processed. Termination would also trigger MSA § 14.5, requiring Dronusk to deliver in-progress Deliverables and return/destroy germplasm, but it would disrupt the breeding season at its most time-sensitive point. Inbred lines from off-season nurseries cannot simply be re-submitted to another lab on the same timeline.

Additionally, the prior draft (Draft v2, § 14) did **not** contain a Change of Control termination right. This provision was added between the draft and the executed MSA, suggesting Dronusk negotiated it deliberately.

**Recommended fix.**

- Before the merger closes, send a supplemental letter (or have Daniel Whitcombe call Viktor Halvorsen) that expressly acknowledges § 14.3, requests Dronusk's prior written consent to the Change of Control, and offers to discuss any conditions Dronusk might want (e.g., a guarantee of payment, a commitment to minimum volume for the next season).
- If Dronusk is unresponsive or signals it will not consent, evaluate whether to (a) seek consent as a condition to closing, (b) negotiate a side letter limiting Dronusk's termination right to a specified date (e.g., no termination before May 31, 2027, the end of Order Form No. 1's term), or (c) begin contingency planning for an alternative genotyping provider for Window 3 volumes.
- Calendar the consent request well in advance of the January 15 expected close.

### 2. Order Form No. 1 § 6 Liability Cap Eliminates MSA § 11.3 Carve-Outs

**Where:** Order Form No. 1 § 6; MSA §§ 2.3, 11.1–11.3.

**Issue.** MSA § 11.2 caps each party's liability at "Fees paid or payable under this Agreement in the 12 months preceding the claim." MSA § 11.3 then carves three categories out of that cap: (a) breach of confidentiality, (b) indemnification obligations, and (c) **loss, contamination or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm**.

Order Form No. 1 § 6 states: "Provider's total liability arising out of or relating to this Order Form, whether in contract, tort (including negligence) or otherwise, shall not exceed the total Fees payable under this Order Form." The total estimated fees are $186,400 (Order Form § 3.1).

MSA § 2.3 provides that "the applicable Order Form shall prevail over the body of this Agreement." Because § 6 is on the same subject as MSA § 11 (limitation of liability), the Order Form cap governs. The Order Form cap contains **no carve-outs**. Read together, the effect is that even if Dronusk loses or contaminates an irreplaceable batch of Tanair's germplasm through negligence, Tanair's recovery is capped at $186,400.

**Why it matters.** Tanair's inbred lines represent years of breeding work and have value far exceeding $186,400. A single contamination event affecting a key recurrent parent or a set of advanced-generation lines could cause losses in the millions. The MSA was clearly negotiated to leave germplasm loss uncapped (§ 11.3(c)), but the Order Form inadvertently—or deliberately—overrides that protection.

**Recommended fix.**

- Amend Order Form No. 1 § 6 to add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the limitations in this Section 6 shall not apply to (a) Provider's breach of Section 9 of the MSA, (b) Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 12 of the MSA, or (c) loss, contamination or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm by Provider, its personnel or its subcontractors."
- If Dronusk resists, at minimum carve out germplasm loss (category (c)), which is the highest-risk scenario.
- This fix should be sought before the next Batch is submitted under Window 2 (currently in progress through December 15, 2026), so the protection is in place for the largest volume period.

### 3. Automatic Renewal – MSA § 14.1

**Where:** MSA § 14.1; compare Draft v2 § 14.1.

**Issue.** The executed MSA auto-renews for successive one-year periods unless either party gives notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the then-current term. The initial three-year term ends March 2, 2029. The first non-renewal notice deadline is **December 1, 2028**.

The prior draft had no auto-renewal; it provided that "the Parties may extend the term by written agreement." The auto-renewal was added in the executed version.

**Why it matters.** If Tanair is unhappy with the relationship (e.g., after a quality issue or if the Kelbrin integration changes Tanair's needs), it could be locked in for another year if it misses the December 1 deadline.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the December 1, 2028 deadline now. No action needed today, but flag for the next contract-management review.

### 4. Dronusk Can Assign to an Acquirer Without Tanair's Consent – MSA §§ 1, 15.2

**Where:** MSA § 1 (definition of "Permitted Assignee"), § 15.2; compare Draft v2 § 15.2.

**Issue.** "Permitted Assignee" includes "any person that acquires all or substantially all of the assets or business to which this Agreement relates." MSA § 15.2 allows either party to assign to a Permitted Assignee "without consent" by written notice. The prior draft required consent for all assignments (subject to a reasonableness standard).

**Why it matters.** If Dronusk is acquired by a competitor of Tanair or by a company with weaker quality systems, Tanair cannot block the assignment. Its germplasm and confidential breeding data would transfer to the new owner. Tanair's only recourse would be to terminate for convenience under § 14.4 (60 days' notice), but that creates a transition gap.

**Recommended fix.** At the next amendment opportunity (e.g., when fixing the Order Form cap in Issue 2 above), negotiate to either (a) delete the "acquirer of substantially all assets" prong from the Permitted Assignee definition, or (b) add a proviso that any assignment to an acquirer is conditioned on the assignee assuming all obligations in writing and Tanair having the right to terminate for convenience on 30 days' notice following the assignment.

### 5. Sample Disposition Conflict – Order Form § 5.3 vs. Quality Agreement § 6.4

**Where:** Order Form No. 1 § 5.3; Quality Agreement § 6.4; MSA § 2.3.

**Issue.** Quality Agreement § 6.4 requires Provider to return or destroy all Customer Germplasm within 30 days after completion of Services, expiration/termination, or Customer's written request. The default under § 6.4(i) is return at Customer's shipping expense.

Order Form § 5.3 tracks this but adds: "If Customer gives no instruction, Provider shall hold the Samples until Customer instructs."

MSA § 2.3 provides that the Quality Agreement prevails "with respect to sample-handling and quality matters." Sample disposition is a sample-handling matter. So the Quality Agreement's 30-day return-or-destroy requirement should control, and the Order Form's "hold indefinitely" language is inoperative.

**Why it matters.** In practice, this is low-risk because Tanair will likely give instructions. But if Tanair is slow to respond (e.g., during the Kelbrin integration), Dronusk could argue it is permitted to hold samples indefinitely under the Order Form, creating storage and contamination risk that Tanair did not intend.

**Recommended fix.** Conform Order Form § 5.3 to Quality Agreement § 6.4 by deleting the "hold until Customer instructs" sentence and replacing it with: "If Customer gives no instruction within 30 days after release of Deliverables, Provider shall return the Samples to Customer at Customer's shipping expense."

### 6. Governing Law and Venue – MSA § 17.5

**Where:** MSA § 17.5; NDA § 12.3.

**Issue.** Both the MSA and the NDA specify New York law and exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. Tanair is in Iowa; Dronusk is in North Carolina. Neither party is in New York.

**Why it matters.** Any dispute will require New York counsel and litigation in Manhattan. This increases cost and creates an unfamiliar forum. It also means Tanair cannot bring a claim in Iowa or North Carolina.

**Recommended fix.** This is a negotiated term and likely not worth reopening absent a dispute. If a dispute arises, evaluate whether the venue clause is enforceable (New York courts generally enforce forum-selection clauses, but a party can argue inconvenience under *Bremen*). For future agreements, consider negotiating for the Customer's home state or a mutually convenient neutral forum.

### 7. NDA/MSA Confidentiality Regime – Overlap and Gaps

**Where:** NDA §§ 2.1, 10.1–10.2; MSA § 9.

**Issue.** The NDA (effective October 14, 2025) has a two-year term, expiring October 14, 2027. Its confidentiality obligations survive for five years after expiration (i.e., until October 14, 2032), and indefinitely for trade secrets. The MSA (effective March 2, 2026) has its own confidentiality regime in § 9, with obligations lasting five years after the MSA term ends (i.e., five years after March 2, 2029, or longer if renewed), plus indefinite protection for trade secrets and Customer Germplasm.

There is no integration clause stating that the MSA supersedes the NDA. Information disclosed between October 2025 and March 2026 (during negotiations) is protected under the NDA. Information disclosed after March 2, 2026 is protected under the MSA.

**Why it matters.** The NDA's definition of Confidential Information (NDA § 2.1(a)) specifically includes "line names and codes, trait targets, marker panel specifications, sample volume forecasts and pricing information." The MSA's definition (MSA § 9.1) is broader ("all non-public information") and includes "breeding pipeline information, and the identity and characteristics of Customer's germplasm." The practical difference is minimal, but if a dispute arose over pre-MSA disclosures, the NDA's shorter survival (5 years from October 2027 = October 2032) could expire before the MSA's survival period (5 years from the MSA's actual end date, which could be 2030 or later with renewals).

**Recommended fix.** Low priority. If Tanair wants certainty, add a sentence to the next amendment: "The Parties' confidentiality obligations with respect to information disclosed before the MSA Effective Date are governed by the NDA and survive in accordance with its terms." Otherwise, the overlapping protection is adequate.

### 8. MSA § 8.4 – Provider Ownership of "Improvements"

**Where:** MSA § 8.4.

**Issue.** "Provider may own improvements to Provider Technology that are of general application, provided that they do not incorporate or disclose Customer Confidential Information or Customer Germplasm."

**Why it matters.** In practice, Dronusk will process data from many customers. If it develops a bioinformatics pipeline improvement "of general application" that was informed by patterns observed across multiple customers' data (including Tanair's), Dronusk could claim ownership under § 8.4. The proviso protects Tanair only if the improvement "incorporates or discloses" Tanair's Confidential Information or Germplasm—a standard that may be difficult to meet for subtle algorithmic improvements.

**Recommended fix.** Consider narrowing § 8.4 at the next amendment to: "Provider may own improvements to Provider Technology that are of general application, provided that they do not incorporate, disclose, or derive from Customer Confidential Information or Customer Germplasm." Adding "derive from" closes the gap.

### 9. Insurance – No Minimum for Property Coverage; No Additional Insured – MSA § 13

**Where:** MSA § 13.

**Issue.** Section 13(c) requires "property insurance covering Customer Germplasm in Provider's custody" but specifies no minimum dollar amount. Sections 13(a) and (b) specify minimums ($2M CGL, $5M professional liability), but (c) does not. There is also no requirement that Tanair be named as an additional insured or loss payee.

**Why it matters.** If a fire or flood destroys Dronusk's storage facility, the property insurance may not adequately cover the value of Tanair's germplasm. Without a specified minimum, Dronusk could carry a policy with a low sub-limit for biological materials.

**Recommended fix.** At the next amendment, add a minimum to § 13(c) (e.g., "of at least $5,000,000 in the aggregate for all Customer Germplasm in Provider's custody") and require that Tanair be named as an additional insured on the property policy or that Dronusk provide a certificate specifying Tanair as loss payee.

### 10. MSA § 4.4 Audit Right – Once Per Year, 15 Days' Notice

**Where:** MSA § 4.4; Quality Agreement § 9.1.

**Issue.** MSA § 4.4 allows Tanair to audit Dronusk's financial records once per calendar year on 15 days' notice. Quality Agreement § 9.1 allows a facility/quality-system audit once per calendar year on 15 *business* days' notice, plus a for-cause audit on 5 business days' notice after a Deviation resulting in loss or contamination.

**Why it matters.** The two audit rights are separate and cumulative (one for financial records under the MSA, one for quality systems under the QA). This is adequate. The 15-business-day notice for the quality audit is reasonable. No action needed, but be aware that the for-cause audit right is the most valuable tool if a Deviation occurs.

**Recommended fix.** None. Just ensure the Quality Contact (Dr. Ruehl) is aware of the for-cause audit trigger so it can be invoked quickly if needed.
