### 1. Change of Control Termination Right – Kelbrin Merger

**Where:** MSA § 14.3 (Termination on Change of Control); MSA § 1 (definition of "Change of Control"); Tanair letter to Dronusk dated October 5, 2026, ¶¶ 1–3.

**Issue.** The MSA defines "Change of Control" to include acquisition of more than 50% of the voting securities of Customer *or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Customer* (MSA § 1). Tanair Holdings Corp. is Tanair's parent. When Kelbrin Agronomics plc completes its merger with Tanair Holdings Corp. (expected January 15, 2027), Kelbrin will indirectly control Tanair. This is a Change of Control under the MSA.

Section 14.3 provides: "If Customer undergoes a Change of Control without Provider's prior written consent, Provider may terminate this Agreement and all Order Forms upon 30 days' written notice to Customer."

The October 5 letter notified Dronusk of the transaction but **did not seek Provider's prior written consent**. It stated that Tanair "will continue as Customer under the MSA" (¶ 3) and framed the letter as informational, not as a consent request.

**Why it matters.** If Dronusk invokes § 14.3 after closing, it can terminate the MSA and Order Form No. 1 on 30 days' notice. Order Form No. 1 covers the 2026-27 breeding season with sample submissions running through April 30, 2027 (Order Form § 2.2(c)) and a term extending to the later of May 31, 2027 or completion of all Deliverables (Order Form § 7). A mid-season termination would strand Tanair's breeding program without a genotyping provider during a critical window. Tanair would also lose the benefit of the MSA's protections (germplasm return, confidentiality, IP ownership of Deliverables in progress) except as preserved by § 14.5 and § 14.6.

Notably, the prior draft (v2, February 9, 2026) contained **no** Change of Control termination right. This provision was added between the draft and the executed MSA.

**Recommended fix.** Before the January 15, 2027 closing, Tanair should:
- (a) Send a formal request for Dronusk's written consent under § 14.3, referencing the October 5 letter; or
- (b) Negotiate a side letter or amendment waiving Dronusk's § 14.3 right for this specific transaction, in exchange for a mutual covenant (e.g., Dronusk agrees not to terminate for convenience during the 2026-27 season).

If Dronusk refuses consent, Tanair should assess whether it can secure a backup genotyping provider for the Window 3 period (January 5 – April 30, 2027) and whether the MSA's § 14.5 transition provisions (delivery of in-progress Deliverables, germplasm return) would be adequate.

### 2. Order Form Liability Cap Eliminates MSA Carve-Outs for Germplasm Loss

**Where:** Order Form No. 1, § 6; MSA § 2.3 (Order of Precedence); MSA § 11.2 (Cap); MSA § 11.3 (Exclusions from Limitation).

**Issue.** Order Form No. 1, § 6 states:

> "Provider's total liability arising out of or relating to this Order Form, whether in contract, tort (including negligence) or otherwise, shall not exceed the total Fees payable under this Order Form."

Total Fees under Order Form No. 1 are $186,400 (Order Form § 3.1).

MSA § 2.3 provides that, subject to the Quality Agreement prevailing on sample-handling and quality matters, "the applicable Order Form shall prevail over the body of this Agreement." Liability allocation is not a "sample-handling or quality matter," so the Order Form's cap controls over MSA § 11.

MSA § 11.3 carves three categories out of the cap: (a) confidentiality breach, (b) indemnification obligations, and (c) "loss, contamination or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm by Provider, its personnel or its subcontractors." Order Form § 6 contains **none of these carve-outs**. Its language ("whether in contract, tort (including negligence) or otherwise") is broader and sweeps in even the germplasm-loss scenario.

**Why it matters.** Customer Germplasm is Tanair's most critical asset. A unique inbred line lost to contamination or a mix-up at Dronusk's lab could represent years of breeding work and a loss far exceeding $186,400. Under the MSA alone, Tanair's recovery for germplasm loss would be uncapped. Under the Order Form, it is capped at $186,400. This is a material and likely unintended erosion of Tanair's protection.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Order Form No. 1, § 6 to add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, this Section 6 shall not limit Provider's liability for (a) loss, contamination or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm; (b) breach of Section 9 of the MSA; or (c) Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 12 of the MSA." Alternatively, add a sentence stating that MSA § 11.3 applies to this Order Form. This should be raised with Dronusk promptly, ideally before the next Batch submission in Window 2 (October 1 – December 15, 2026).

## Other Issues

### 3. Permitted Assignee – Dronusk Can Assign Without Tanair's Consent

**Where:** MSA § 15.2; MSA § 1 (definition of "Permitted Assignee").

**Issue.** MSA § 15.2 permits either Party to assign the Agreement "without consent" to a "Permitted Assignee," defined as (a) any Affiliate of the assigning Party, or (b) any person acquiring all or substantially all of the assets or business to which the Agreement relates. The prior draft (v2 § 15.2) required consent for all assignments, with a "not unreasonably withheld" standard.

**Why it matters.** Dronusk could transfer the MSA (and with it custody of Tanair's germplasm, access to Tanair's confidential breeding data, and the obligation to perform the Services) to an Affiliate or to a successor in a sale of Dronusk's business, without Tanair's approval. Tanair has no veto, no notice requirement beyond the written notice in § 15.2, and no right to terminate if the assignee is a competitor or an entity with weaker quality systems.

**Recommended fix.** Amend MSA § 15.2 to: (a) require 30 days' prior written notice before any assignment to a Permitted Assignee; (b) give Customer the right to terminate the MSA on 30 days' notice if the assignee is a direct competitor of Customer in seed breeding; and (c) require the Permitted Assignee to assume all obligations in writing.

### 4. No Customer Termination Right on Provider Change of Control

**Where:** MSA § 14.3 (Provider termination on Customer CoC); MSA § 14.4 (Customer termination for convenience only).

**Issue.** The MSA gives Dronusk a specific right to terminate if *Tanair* undergoes a Change of Control (§ 14.3), but gives Tanair no corresponding right if *Dronusk* undergoes a Change of Control. Tanair's only exit is the general convenience termination in § 14.4 (60 days' notice), which is available but does not address the specific risk of a hostile or incompetent acquirer taking control of the entity holding Tanair's germplasm.

**Why it matters.** If Dronusk is acquired by a competitor, a financially unstable entity, or a company with a history of data-security failures, Tanair would be locked in for up to 60 days (the convenience-termination notice period) and would have no specific right to demand return of germplasm on accelerated timelines or to audit the new owner's facilities before they take possession.

**Recommended fix.** Add a provision giving Customer the right to terminate the MSA and all Order Forms on 30 days' notice if Provider undergoes a Change of Control, with accelerated germplasm return (e.g., 14 days rather than 30).

### 5. Automatic Renewal – New in Executed MSA

**Where:** MSA § 14.1; compare prior draft v2 § 14.1.

**Issue.** The executed MSA provides for a three-year initial term (March 2, 2026 – March 1, 2029) followed by automatic one-year renewals unless either Party gives notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the then-current term. The prior draft (v2 § 14.1) had no auto-renewal; it required a written agreement to extend.

**Why it matters.** The first non-renewal notice deadline is **December 1, 2028** (90 days before March 1, 2029). If Tanair misses that date, it is locked in for another year. Given the Change of Control issue (Issue 1), Tanair may want flexibility to exit or renegotiate after the Kelbrin transaction closes. An automatic renewal could complicate that.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the December 1, 2028 deadline immediately. Consider negotiating the auto-renewal out of the MSA (reverting to the draft's mutual-agreement standard) as part of any consent request under Issue 1.

### 6. No Minimum Amount for Germplasm Property Insurance

**Where:** MSA § 13(c).

**Issue.** Section 13 requires Provider to maintain: (a) CGL of at least $2M per occurrence; (b) professional liability of at least $5M per claim; and (c) "property insurance covering Customer Germplasm in Provider's custody." No minimum dollar amount is specified for (c).

**Why it matters.** Tanair's germplasm in Dronusk's custody at any given time could represent millions of dollars in breeding value. Without a stated minimum, Dronusk could carry a token policy (e.g., $100,000) and technically comply. Combined with the Order Form's $186,400 liability cap (Issue 2), Tanair's actual recovery for a total loss event could be severely limited.

**Recommended fix.** Amend MSA § 13(c) to specify a minimum coverage amount for Customer Germplasm (e.g., $10M or an amount tied to the estimated value of germplasm in custody). Require Dronusk to provide a certificate of insurance evidencing the coverage.

### 7. Turnaround Times Lack Enforceable Consequences

**Where:** MSA § 3.2; Order Form No. 1, §§ 4.1–4.2.

**Issue.** Both the MSA and the Order Form state that Provider shall use "commercially reasonable efforts" to meet turnaround times. There is no service credit, liquidated damages, or other financial remedy for missed deadlines. The only recourse is the general remedy in MSA § 10.3 (re-performance at no charge) or termination for material breach (§ 14.2(a)), which requires a 30-day cure period.

**Why it matters.** Tanair's breeding program operates on fixed seasonal windows (Order Form § 2.2). If Dronusk misses the 10-business-day turnaround for Window 2 batches (October – December), Tanair may not have genotype data in time to make selection decisions before the next planting cycle. A "commercially reasonable efforts" standard with no financial penalty gives Dronusk little incentive to prioritize Tanair's samples over others.

**Recommended fix.** Add a service-credit provision to the Order Form: e.g., for each business day a standard Batch exceeds its turnaround, Provider credits 2% of the Batch fee (capped at 20% of the Batch fee). For priority Batches (Order Form § 4.2), a higher credit rate given the compressed timeline.

### 8. Ambiguity in Sample Disposition Timeline

**Where:** Order Form No. 1, § 5.3; Quality Agreement § 6.4.

**Issue.** Order Form § 5.3 provides that within 30 days after Provider releases Deliverables for each Batch, Provider shall return or destroy remaining Samples "as Customer instructs," and if Customer gives no instruction, Provider shall "hold the Samples until Customer instructs." Quality Agreement § 6.4 provides that within 30 days after (a) completion of Services, (b) expiration/termination, or (c) Customer's written request, Provider shall return or destroy all Customer Germplasm.

Under MSA § 2.3, the Quality Agreement prevails over the MSA for "sample-handling and quality matters." Sample disposition is arguably a sample-handling matter. But the Order Form is more specific to this engagement and states that it "does not authorize Provider to keep any retention or reference portion" (Order Form § 5.3, last sentence), which is consistent with Quality Agreement § 6.6.

The practical tension: if Customer does not give a disposition instruction, Order Form § 5.3 says Provider holds the samples indefinitely, while Quality Agreement § 6.4(a) says Provider must return or destroy within 30 days of Service completion. Which controls?

**Why it matters.** Indefinite holding of Tanair's germplasm at a third-party lab increases the risk of loss, contamination, or unauthorized access over time. It also creates an operational burden on Dronusk (storage costs, monitoring) that could lead to disputes.

**Recommended fix.** Clarify in a side letter or Order Form amendment that Quality Agreement § 6.4 controls the disposition timeline, and that the "hold until Customer instructs" language in Order Form § 5.3 applies only to the *method* of disposition (return vs. destroy), not the *deadline*. Alternatively, have Tanair's Quality Contact issue a standing instruction (e.g., "destroy all Samples 30 days after Deliverable release unless I direct otherwise in writing for a specific Batch").

### 9. MSA § 8.4 – Broad Provider Rights in "Improvements"

**Where:** MSA § 8.4.

**Issue.** Section 8.4 provides: "Provider may own improvements to Provider Technology that are of general application, provided that they do not incorporate or disclose Customer Confidential Information or Customer Germplasm."

The "general application" standard is vague. The proviso only requires that the improvement not "incorporate or disclose" Customer information—it does not require that the improvement be developed *without reference to* Customer Materials. Dronusk could argue that an improvement to its bioinformatics pipeline, inspired by patterns it observed in Tanair's genotype data (which it is permitted to process), is of "general application" and does not "incorporate or disclose" Tanair's Confidential Information, even though it was developed with the benefit of Tanair's data.

**Why it matters.** Over time, Dronusk could accumulate "improvements" derived from processing multiple customers' data (including Tanair's) and claim ownership, potentially giving it a competitive advantage in the genotyping services market or allowing it to offer superior services to Tanair's competitors.

**Recommended fix.** Amend § 8.4 to require that improvements be developed "without reference to, or use of, any Customer Materials or Customer Confidential Information," not merely that they do not "incorporate or disclose" them.

### 10. NDA Survival Period Shorter Than MSA for Pre-Contractual Information

**Where:** NDA § 10.1 (two-year term, expiring October 14, 2027); NDA § 10.2 (five-year survival, i.e., until October 14, 2030); MSA § 9.5 (five years after MSA termination; indefinite for trade secrets and Customer Germplasm).

**Issue.** Information disclosed between October 14, 2025 (NDA effective date) and March 2, 2026 (MSA effective date) is governed by the NDA. The NDA's obligations survive until October 14, 2030 (five years after the NDA's own expiration). If the MSA is in effect until March 1, 2029 (or longer with auto-renewals), the MSA's confidentiality obligations for information disclosed during the MSA term run until five years after MSA termination (i.e., 2034 or later). But pre-MSA information disclosed under the NDA (e.g., Dronusk's pricing, capacity, and quality system documentation shared during the evaluation phase) loses protection in 2030—four years earlier than MSA-era information.

**Why it matters.** The practical risk is low (pre-MSA disclosures were limited to evaluation-phase information), but if Dronusk shared sensitive capacity or pricing data that remains commercially relevant beyond 2030, Tanair's protection lapses earlier than it might expect.

**Recommended fix.** Low priority. If Tanair wants alignment, it could request a NDA amendment extending the survival period to match the MSA's (five years after the later of NDA expiration or MSA termination). Alternatively, note the issue in the file and monitor.

### 11. ISO/IEC 17025 Accreditation Scope May Not Cover Line D Services

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 3.1; Order Form No. 1, § 1.1 (Line D).

**Issue.** Quality Agreement § 3.1 requires Provider to maintain ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation "for genotyping and seed purity testing." Line D (trait-introgression support, including foreground/background selection analysis and recurrent-parent recovery reports) is a bioinformatics/analytical service that may fall outside the scope of Dronusk's ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation.

**Why it matters.** If Line D services are not covered by the accreditation, they are not subject to the same external quality oversight. The acceptance criteria in Quality Agreement § 7.3 (call rate ≥ 95%, concordance ≥ 99%) are framed for genotyping plates and may not directly apply to selection reports. Tanair would have less assurance of quality for Line D Deliverables.

**Recommended fix.** Request that Dronusk confirm in writing whether its ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation encompasses the bioinformatics analyses performed under Line D. If not, negotiate additional quality controls specific to Line D (e.g., validation against known backcross progeny, peer-review of selection reports before release).

### 12. New York Governing Law and Venue – Inconvenient for Both Parties

**Where:** MSA § 17.5; NDA § 12.3.

**Issue.** Both the MSA and the NDA specify New York law and exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. Tanair is headquartered in Ames, Iowa; Dronusk is in Morrisville, North Carolina. Neither party has a New York presence.

**Why it matters.** Any dispute would require litigating in New York, imposing travel and local-counsel costs on Tanair. New York law is generally pro-contract and well-developed, so the substantive choice is not problematic, but the venue is inconvenient.

**Recommended fix.** Low priority given that the provision was presumably negotiated and accepted. If Tanair is in a position to request changes (e.g., as part of the consent negotiation under Issue 1), consider proposing Iowa or North Carolina venue, or at minimum a provision allowing either party to file in the other party's home jurisdiction for claims under $1M.
