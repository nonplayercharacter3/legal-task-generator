### 1. Change of Control Gives Dronusk a Termination Right That the Kelbrin Merger Will Trigger

**Where:** MSA §1 (definition of "Change of Control"); MSA §14.3; Tanair letter to Dronusk dated October 5, 2026, §§1–3.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §1 defines "Change of Control" as any transaction by which a person acquires "more than 50% of the voting securities of Customer **or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Customer**."
- MSA §14.3 provides: "If Customer undergoes a Change of Control without Provider's prior written consent, Provider may terminate this Agreement and all Order Forms upon 30 days' written notice to Customer."
- The October 5 letter states that Tanair Holdings Corp. (Tanair's parent) is merging into a Kelbrin subsidiary, with Tanair Holdings surviving as a wholly owned Kelbrin subsidiary. Expected closing: on or about January 15, 2027.
- The letter is framed as a courtesy notice under MSA §17.2. It does **not** request Dronusk's consent under §14.3, does **not** acknowledge §14.3, and does **not** address the termination right at all.

**Why it matters:**

The Kelbrin transaction is unambiguously a Change of Control under the MSA definition. Kelbrin will indirectly acquire 100% of the voting securities of Tanair Holdings, which directly controls Tanair. Once the transaction closes (expected ~January 15, 2027), Dronusk will have the right to terminate the MSA and all Order Forms on 30 days' notice. That would strand Tanair mid-season: Window 3 batches (Hawaii/Puerto Rico, January 5–April 30, 2027) would be in flight, and Line D selection reports would be incomplete. The prior draft v2 (February 9, 2026) contained **no** Change of Control termination provision. This was added between draft and execution, presumably at Dronusk's request.

**Recommended fix:**

- Before the Kelbrin closing, Tanair should seek Dronusk's written consent under §14.3. The October 5 letter should be supplemented with a specific request for consent, or a side letter should be negotiated.
- If Dronusk withholds consent or demands a price increase as the condition of consent, Tanair needs a contingency plan: identify backup genotyping capacity for the 2026–27 season and assess the cost of re-tendering the remaining Windows.
- Going forward, any future Tanair corporate transaction (including the Kelbrin integration itself if structured differently) should be screened against §14.3 well in advance.

### 2. Order Form No. 1 §6 Caps Provider's Liability at $186,400 and Eliminates the MSA's Critical Carve-Outs

**Where:** Order Form No. 1 §6; MSA §2.3 (order of precedence); MSA §11.1–11.3.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA §11.2 caps each party's liability at "Fees paid or payable under this Agreement in the 12 months preceding the claim."
- MSA §11.3 carves three categories **out** of both the indirect-damages exclusion (§11.1) and the cap (§11.2): (a) breach of confidentiality (§9); (b) indemnification obligations (§12); and (c) "loss, contamination or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm by Provider, its personnel or its subcontractors."
- Order Form No. 1 §6 states: "Provider's total liability arising out of or relating to this Order Form, whether in contract, tort (including negligence) or otherwise, shall not exceed the total Fees payable under this Order Form." Total Fees = $186,400 (Order Form §3.1).
- MSA §2.3 provides that "the applicable Order Form shall prevail over the body of this Agreement" (subject to the Quality Agreement prevailing on sample-handling and quality matters).

**Why it matters:**

Because the Order Form prevails over the MSA body under §2.3, Order Form §6 overrides MSA §11.2 **and** MSA §11.3. The practical effect:

- If Dronusk loses, contaminates, or misappropriates Tanair's proprietary germplasm (inbred lines, doubled haploids, backcross materials) while performing Order Form No. 1 services, Tanair's maximum recovery is $186,400. The replacement value of a single inbred line in a commercial corn or soybean breeding program can run into the millions.
- If Dronusk breaches confidentiality in connection with Order Form No. 1 work (e.g., discloses Tanair's genotype data or line identities to a third party), the same $186,400 cap applies.
- If a third party sues Tanair based on an IP infringement allegation covered by MSA §12.1 (Provider's indemnity), the Order Form cap would arguably limit recovery to $186,400.

The prior draft v2 had no Order Form-specific liability cap, and the MSA §11.3 carve-outs would have applied in full. This is a significant regression.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend Order Form No. 1 §6 to expressly preserve the MSA §11.3 carve-outs. Suggested language: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the limitations in this Section 6 shall not apply to (a) Provider's breach of Section 9 of the MSA; (b) Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 12 of the MSA; or (c) loss, contamination, or misappropriation of Customer Germplasm by Provider, its personnel, or its subcontractors."
- If Dronusk resists, at minimum carve out germplasm loss/contamination, given that MSA §7.3 places risk of loss on Provider and MSA §11.3(c) was clearly negotiated to address this.
- For any future Order Forms, ensure the template does not repeat this error.

### 3. Automatic Renewal Creates a 90-Day Notice Deadline Tanair Must Calendar

**Where:** MSA §14.1.

**What the documents say:**

The MSA has an initial three-year term (March 2, 2026 through March 1, 2029) and then "renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either Party gives notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the then-current term." The prior draft v2 had no auto-renewal; it provided only that "The Parties may extend the term by written agreement."

**Why it matters:**

If Tanair wants the relationship to end at the close of the initial term, it must deliver non-renewal notice by approximately **December 1, 2028** (90 days before March 1, 2029). Missing that deadline locks Tanair in for another year. Given the Change of Control issue (Item 1 above), Tanair may want the flexibility to exit if the Kelbrin integration creates strategic misalignment with Dronusk.

**Recommended fix:**

- Enter a calendar reminder for **October 1, 2028** (giving a two-month buffer before the 90-day deadline) to decide whether to renew.
- Consider whether the auto-renewal should be negotiated out at the next amendment opportunity, or whether Tanair is comfortable with it given the convenience-termination right in §14.4 (60 days' notice).

### 4. Permitted Assignee Provision Allows Dronusk to Assign to an Acquirer Without Tanair's Consent

**Where:** MSA §1 (definition of "Permitted Assignee"); MSA §15.2.

**What the documents say:**

MSA §15.2 permits either Party to assign the Agreement "without consent, to a Permitted Assignee by written notice." "Permitted Assignee" includes (a) any Affiliate of the assigning Party, or (b) "any person that acquires all or substantially all of the assets or business to which this Agreement relates."

The prior draft v2 §15.2 required consent for **all** assignments, subject to a "not unreasonably withheld" standard. There was no Permitted Assignee carve-out.

**Why it matters:**

If Dronusk is acquired by a competitor of Tanair (or by a company whose strategic interests conflict with Tanair's), Dronusk could assign the MSA to the acquirer on notice alone. Tanair's germplasm, genotype data, and breeding pipeline information would then be in the hands of a potentially adverse party. The MSA's confidentiality and germplasm restrictions would bind the assignee, but the commercial dynamic changes.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a deletion of the Permitted Assignee carve-out for Provider, or at minimum limit it to Affiliates (category (a)) and delete category (b).
- Alternatively, add a condition that any assignment by Provider to a non-Affiliate acquirer requires that the acquirer not be a direct competitor of Customer in seed breeding or genotyping services.
- This is lower urgency than Items 1 and 2 but should be addressed at the next amendment.

### 5. New MSA §8.4 (Improvements) Was Not in the Prior Draft and Could Allow Dronusk to Claim Ownership of Derivative IP

**Where:** MSA §8.4. Absent from prior draft v2 (which ended at §8.3).

**What the documents say:**

"Provider may own improvements to Provider Technology that are of general application, provided that they do not incorporate or disclose Customer Confidential Information or Customer Germplasm."

**Why it matters:**

This is a new provision that carves out a category of Provider-owned IP that did not exist in the prior draft. The practical risk: Dronusk develops a bioinformatics pipeline improvement while running Tanair's genotyping work. Dronusk characterizes it as "of general application" and argues it does not "incorporate or disclose" Tanair's CI. Tanair then finds that Dronusk is offering the improved pipeline to Tanair's competitors. The "incorporate or disclose" standard is a high bar for Tanair to meet in a dispute, especially with bioinformatics code where the line between a general algorithmic improvement and one trained on or tuned to Tanair's specific marker panels can be thin.

**Recommended fix:**

- Narrow §8.4 to require that improvements also "do not derive from, are not trained on, or are not calibrated using Customer Materials or Deliverables."
- Add a requirement that Provider give Customer 30 days' prior written notice before commercializing any improvement that was developed during the term of the MSA, so Customer can object.

### 6. No Integration or Supersession Clause Between the NDA and the MSA

**Where:** Mutual NDA (October 14, 2025), §§9.2, 10.1; MSA §9 (Confidentiality).

**What the documents say:**

- The NDA has a two-year term (expires October 14, 2027) with five-year survival of obligations (through approximately October 14, 2032).
- NDA §9.2 states: "A commercial relationship will arise only under a definitive written agreement signed by both Parties." It does **not** state that the NDA is superseded, terminated, or merged into any definitive agreement.
- The MSA's confidentiality section (§9) covers information "disclosed...in connection with this Agreement" (i.e., the MSA). It does not expressly incorporate or reference the NDA.
- The NDA's definition of Confidential Information (§2.1) is more specific for Tanair: it enumerates "breeding program information, pedigree records, line names and codes, trait targets, marker panel specifications, sample volume forecasts and pricing information." The MSA's definition (§9.1) is broader in scope but less specific in its enumeration.
- The NDA contains a "No Reverse Engineering" clause (§4.5) that has no direct equivalent in the MSA (MSA §7.2(b) covers reverse engineering of germplasm only, not data or other materials).

**Why it matters:**

Information Tanair shared with Dronusk between October 14, 2025 and March 2, 2026 (the pre-MSA evaluation period) is protected by the NDA, not the MSA. After October 14, 2027 (NDA expiration), the NDA's survival clause keeps obligations alive for five more years, but the standard of care and scope are those of the NDA, not the MSA. If a dispute arises over pre-MSA disclosures after October 14, 2027, Tanair would be relying on the NDA's terms, which are in some respects less protective (e.g., no express reference to Customer Germplasm, no equitable-relief-without-bond provision).

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a sentence to the MSA (or a side letter) stating: "The Mutual Nondisclosure Agreement dated October 14, 2025 between the Parties is incorporated by reference and shall continue in effect for its stated term. To the extent of any conflict between the NDA and this Agreement with respect to information disclosed before the Effective Date of this Agreement, the NDA shall control."
- Alternatively, confirm that the NDA's October 14, 2027 expiration and five-year survival are sufficient, and that no material pre-MSA disclosures are at risk.

### 7. Order of Precedence (MSA §2.3) Is a Structural Risk for Future Order Forms

**Where:** MSA §2.3.

**What the documents say:**

"the Quality Agreement shall prevail with respect to sample-handling and quality matters. Subject to that rule, the applicable Order Form shall prevail over the body of this Agreement."

This provision was not in the prior draft v2, which had no order-of-precedence clause at all.

**Why it matters:**

This is the mechanism that causes Issue 2 above (Order Form §6 overriding MSA §11.3). But it also means that **any** future Order Form can inadvertently override MSA protections. If a future Order Form contains a liability cap, a shortened confidentiality period, or a weakened warranty, it will prevail over the MSA by operation of §2.3. The only safe harbor is the Quality Agreement for "sample-handling and quality matters."

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a "savings clause" to MSA §2.3: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, no Order Form shall modify or waive Sections 7 (Customer Germplasm), 9 (Confidentiality), 11 (Limitation of Liability), or 12 (Indemnification) of this Agreement unless the Order Form expressly identifies the section being modified and is signed by Customer's General Counsel."
- In the interim, implement an internal review step so that every Order Form is checked against the MSA before signature.

### 8. Sample Disposition Requires Tanair Affirmative Instruction; Inaction Leaves Germplasm at Dronusk's Facility

**Where:** Quality Agreement §6.4; Order Form No. 1 §5.3.

**What the documents say:**

- QA §6.4: Within 30 days after completion, expiration, or Customer's written request, Provider shall either (i) return germplasm or (ii) destroy it "if Customer so instructs in writing."
- Order Form §5.3: "If Customer gives no instruction, Provider shall hold the Samples until Customer instructs."

**Why it matters:**

The combined effect is that Dronusk is not obligated to return or destroy samples unless Tanair affirmatively instructs it to do so. If Tanair's operations team (Dr. Ruehl or the project manager) is slow to issue disposition instructions, samples accumulate at Dronusk's Morrisville facility. While MSA §7.3 places risk of loss on Dronusk during that period, the longer samples sit, the greater the operational risk (power failure, contamination event, personnel turnover). Additionally, MSA §13(c) requires Dronusk to maintain property insurance covering Customer Germplasm in its custody, but the scope and limits of that policy are not specified.

**Recommended fix:**

- Establish an internal protocol: after each Batch's Deliverables are released, the project manager (Lucas Brandvold) issues a written disposition instruction (return or destroy) within 10 business days.
- At the end of each Order Form term, issue a blanket instruction to destroy all remaining Customer Germplasm and request the certificate of destruction under QA §6.5.
- Request a copy of Dronusk's property insurance policy (or at least the relevant declarations page) to confirm coverage limits for Customer Germplasm.
