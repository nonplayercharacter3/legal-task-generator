## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Change of Control Traps Logith in the Ostrander Merger

**Where:** Agreement §15.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital B; Agreement §12.5

**Issue:** Section 15.2 provides that any direct or indirect change of control of Licensee, "including any such transaction involving any entity that directly or indirectly controls Licensee," is a deemed assignment requiring Draibav's prior written consent. Without consent, it is "a material breach of this Agreement that is not capable of cure," and Draibav may terminate, in which case §12.5 accelerates all remaining Subscription Fees for the then-current term.

Amendment No. 1, Recital B confirms that Logith Holdings, Inc. (Logith's parent) has signed a merger agreement with Ostrander Remarketing Partners, L.P. (dated April 20, 2026), expected to close in Q4 2026. This is squarely within §15.2's scope.

**Why it matters:** At $78,000/month, the 60-month Initial Subscription Term (running July 1, 2025 – June 30, 2030) represents up to $4.68 million in total fees. If Ostrander closes without consent and Draibav terminates, Logith owes the full remainder of the term immediately, plus loses its platform for six auction sites with only the §12.7 transition assistance (6 months at then-current rates). The breach is expressly "not capable of cure," so Logith cannot fix the problem after the fact.

**Recommended fix:** Obtain Draibav's written consent to the Ostrander change of control before the merger closes. If Draibav resists, negotiate an amendment to §15.2 that (a) carves out changes of control of Logith's parent so long as Logith itself remains the operating entity and continues to perform, or (b) at minimum provides that a consent-withheld change of control gives Logith a cure period (e.g., 90 days to find an alternative platform) before Draibav may terminate and accelerate fees. Given that the parties are already in a disputed posture over the Termination Notice (see Issue 4), this should be raised immediately and in writing.

### 2. Exclusivity Clause Is Far Broader Than the Term Sheet

**Where:** Agreement §3.4; Term Sheet §4.1–4.2

**Issue:** The Term Sheet agreed that exclusivity would apply only "at the six Licensed Sites only" and that "[n]othing in this Section 4 restricts Logith or its affiliates in respect of any other software or any other facility" (§4.2). The executed Agreement §3.4 instead prohibits Logith **and its Affiliates** from "directly or indirectly licens[ing], us[ing], develop[ing] or procur[ing], **anywhere in the United States or Canada**, any online, simulcast or mobile vehicle auction software, bidding software or condition reporting software other than the Platform."

This is a U.S.-and-Canada-wide, all-affiliates, all-facilities prohibition that even bars Logith from developing its own auction software.

**Why it matters:** Logith operates under Logith Holdings, Inc., which may have or acquire other auction or remarketing operations. The clause as written could prohibit those entities from using or developing any competing software anywhere in North America for the duration of the Term (60 months, plus auto-renewals). It also prevents Logith from building in-house capability as a hedge against Draibav — which is particularly concerning given the availability problems already documented (see Issue 4).

**Recommended fix:** Amend §3.4 to conform to the Term Sheet: limit exclusivity to the Licensed Sites identified in the Order Form, and add an express carve-out for Affiliates and for any facility not listed as a Licensed Site. If Draibav insists on broader exclusivity as a condition of the Ostrander consent (Issue 1), trade it explicitly and price it.

### 3. Order Form Narrows the "Licensee Data" Definition, Expanding Draibav's Commercialization Rights

**Where:** Order Form §4.1; Agreement §1.9; Agreement §2.3 (Order of Precedence); Agreement §7.4

**Issue:** The Agreement defines "Licensee Data" broadly (§1.9) to include data "submitted to, collected by, entered into, stored in or generated by the Platform," including bid histories, hammer prices, condition reports, and "all derivatives, compilations and analyses." The Order Form defines it narrowly as "data and information **uploaded to the Platform by Licensee's Authorized Users**" (§4.1).

Under the Order of Precedence (§2.3), the Order Form controls over the body of the Agreement. The Term Sheet (§7.1) clearly intended the broader definition: "Logith will own all data submitted to, collected by or generated by the Platform in connection with Logith's auctions."

**Why it matters:** Because the Order Form's definition prevails, data that is (a) generated by the Platform's own processes (e.g., auto-compiled analytics, system-generated reports), (b) collected from Bidders or Consignors directly through the Platform (e.g., bidder registration data that the bidder enters, not "uploaded by" a Logith Authorized User), or (c) derived by the Platform's algorithms, may fall outside "Licensee Data." That data would then be "Platform Data" under §7.4, which Draibav may "collect, use, aggregate, analyze and commercialize … to develop and sell analytics products, market reports and market pricing benchmarks to third parties." For an auction company, hammer prices, bid patterns, and condition-report data are highly sensitive competitive information. Draibav could potentially package and sell Logith's pricing data to competitors.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Order Form §4.1 to adopt the Agreement's broader definition (or the Term Sheet's language). Alternatively, add a proviso to §7.4 stating that "Platform Data" does not include any data that identifies or can reasonably be associated with Licensee, any Licensed Site, any Bidder or any Consignor, and that Draibav will not sell, license or make available any pricing, volume or condition benchmarks derived from Licensee's auctions to any third party without Logith's prior written consent.

### 4. Chronic Failure Termination Notice Appears Four Days Late — Dispute Is Live

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Recital C; Service Level Schedule §5.1–5.2; Agreement §12.2

**Issue:** The SLS defines a "Chronic Failure" as Monthly Availability below 99.0% in any three calendar months within a rolling six-month period (§5.1). Draibav's own monthly reports recorded 98.1% (Jan 2026), 97.6% (Feb 2026), and 98.4% (Mar 2026) — all below 99.0%. A Chronic Failure therefore occurred at the end of March 2026.

Section 5.2 requires Licensee to give termination notice "within thirty (30) days after the end of the third affected month." The third affected month ended March 31, 2026, making the deadline **April 30, 2026**. Logith's Termination Notice is dated **May 4, 2026** — four days late.

Draibav disputed the notice's effectiveness by letter dated May 11, 2026. Amendment No. 1, §6.1 expressly reserves both parties' rights "as to whether a Chronic Failure occurred, whether the Termination Notice was timely and effective, and the consequences of the Termination Notice."

**Why it matters:** If the notice is held ineffective, Logith has lost its right to terminate for Chronic Failure and is locked into the 60-month term (expiring June 30, 2030) with 60-month auto-renewals (see Issue 5). Logith would have to continue paying $78,000/month for a platform that has demonstrated sub-99% availability for three consecutive months, with its only monetary remedy being Service Credits capped at 50% of monthly fees (see Issue 7). The reservation of rights in the Amendment means this is an active, unresolved dispute that could end up in Delaware litigation.

**Recommended fix:**
- **Short term:** Assess the strength of the timeliness argument. Four days is a narrow margin; arguments exist that (a) the "end of the third affected month" could be construed as the date the monthly report was delivered (SLS §2.4 requires reports within 10 days after month-end, so the March report could have arrived as late as April 10, making the 30-day window run to May 10), or (b) equitable doctrines (substantial compliance, waiver by Draibav's continued performance under the Amendment) may apply.
- **Strategic:** Use the Ostrander consent negotiation (Issue 1) as leverage to resolve the termination dispute. Offer to withdraw or re-issue the Termination Notice in exchange for (a) Draibav's consent to the change of control, (b) a revised §3.4 exclusivity clause, and (c) a corrected data definition. If the parties cannot reach a global resolution, prepare for a declaratory judgment action in Delaware.
- **Going forward:** If the termination is deemed ineffective, calendar the next non-renewal notice deadline immediately and ensure it is met.

## HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 5. Auto-Renewal Terms Are 60 Months, Not 12

**Where:** Agreement §12.2; Order Form §2.2; Term Sheet §3.2

**Issue:** The Term Sheet contemplated "successive **12-month** renewal terms." The Agreement provides that each Renewal Term is "equal in length to the Initial Subscription Term" (§12.2), which is 60 months per Order Form §2.2. Logith therefore faces 5-year auto-renewals.

**Why it matters:** Combined with the narrow non-renewal notice window (Issue 6), Logith could be locked in for five additional years if it misses a 30-day window by even one day. For a business that has already experienced chronic availability failures and is undergoing a change of control, this is an unacceptable level of lock-in.

**Recommended fix:** Amend §12.2 to provide for 12-month Renewal Terms, consistent with the Term Sheet. If Draibav resists, accept 24-month terms as a fallback, but do not accept 60-month renewals.

### 6. Non-Renewal Notice Window Is Only 30 Days and Opens 180 Days Before Term End

**Where:** Agreement §12.2; Term Sheet §3.2

**Issue:** The Agreement requires non-renewal notice to be delivered "during the thirty (30) day period ending 180 days before the expiration of the then-current Initial Subscription Term or Renewal Term." The Term Sheet simply required notice "at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term."

For the Initial Subscription Term (expiring June 30, 2030), Logith must deliver notice between approximately **January 1, 2030 and January 30, 2030**. Missing this 30-day window by a single day triggers a 60-month (or 12-month, if Issue 5 is fixed) auto-renewal.

**Why it matters:** The window is narrow, falls well in advance of term expiration (when business conditions may have changed), and creates a significant calendar risk. A single administrative oversight locks Logith in for years.

**Recommended fix:** Amend §12.2 to require notice at least 90 days before term expiration (no latest-notice cutoff), consistent with the Term Sheet. At minimum, extend the window to 60 days and move it closer to term expiration (e.g., "during the 60-day period ending 120 days before expiration"). Implement an internal calendar alert at T-minus 240 days.

### 7. Service Credits Capped at 50% of Monthly Fees

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §4.3

**Issue:** Section 4.3 provides that "Service Credits for any month may not exceed in total 50% of the Subscription Fees for that month." Even if the Platform is completely unavailable for an entire month (0% Monthly Availability), Logith's maximum credit is $39,000 (50% of $78,000).

**Why it matters:** For a business conducting live auctions six days a week across five states, a full month of platform unavailability would cause far more than $39,000 in lost revenue. The cap means Logith bears the economic loss of extended outages beyond the 50% threshold, with no additional monetary remedy short of meeting the Chronic Failure or Severity 1 termination thresholds (which have their own difficulties, as shown by Issue 4).

**Recommended fix:** Increase the cap to 100% of monthly Subscription Fees for months where Monthly Availability falls below 95.0%. Alternatively, add a provision that if Downtime exceeds 24 consecutive hours, the cap does not apply and Logith is entitled to a full refund of Subscription Fees for the affected period.

### 8. "Best Efforts" Standard for Auction Hours Undermines the Availability Commitment

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §2.1–2.2

**Issue:** Section 2.1 commits to 99.9% Monthly Availability (a firm obligation). But Section 2.2 provides that during Auction Hours (6:00 a.m. – 8:00 p.m. on sale days), Licensor need only use its "best efforts" to prevent Downtime. "Best efforts" is a lower standard than "shall" and does not create a firm obligation.

**Why it matters:** Auction Hours are when the Platform is most critical — live simulcast bidding is happening, dealers are on the phone, and downtime directly interrupts sales. The 99.9% monthly figure allows approximately 43 minutes of Downtime per month. If all 43 minutes occur outside Auction Hours, Logith gets full compliance. If a 30-minute outage hits during a Friday evening sale, Draibav can argue it used "best efforts" and the monthly figure was still met. The firm remedy (Service Credits, termination) is therefore decoupled from the time of greatest business impact.

**Recommended fix:** Replace "best efforts" in §2.2 with a firm obligation: "Licensor shall ensure that no Downtime occurs during Auction Hours, except for Scheduled Maintenance performed in accordance with Section 2.3." If Draibav will not accept zero-downtime during Auction Hours, at minimum require that any Downtime during Auction Hours counts double (or triple) toward the Monthly Availability calculation, making it mathematically harder to meet the 99.9% threshold if auction-hour outages occur.

### 9. Insurance Cancellation Notice for Nonpayment Is Only 10 Days

**Where:** Agreement §11.2; Certificate of Insurance §7.2

**Issue:** The Agreement requires Draibav to give "at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice of cancellation or material reduction of any required coverage." The Certificate of Insurance §7.2 confirms a 30-day notice endorsement, but carves out nonpayment of premium: "Notice of cancellation for nonpayment of premium will be given at least **ten (10) days** in advance."

**Why it matters:** If Draibav fails to pay its insurance premium (a financial-distress signal), Logith gets only 10 days' notice that the Technology E&O and Cyber Liability policies — the two most important coverages for a software provider — will lapse. The Agreement's 30-day requirement is not met in this scenario. Logith could be left without the insurance protection it bargained for, at the precise moment Draibav's financial condition is most concerning.

**Recommended fix:** Add to §11.2: "Notwithstanding any policy provision to the contrary, Licensor shall provide Licensee at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice of any cancellation or material reduction of coverage, including cancellation for nonpayment of premium." Alternatively, require Draibav to prepay its annual premium or to provide a letter of credit covering 12 months of premium.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 10. Downtime Is Measured Primarily by Licensor's Tools

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §1.4

**Issue:** "Downtime" is defined as unavailability "as measured by Licensor's monitoring tools and confirmed by Licensee's reasonable records." The phrase "confirmed by" is ambiguous: it could mean Licensor's measurement is primary and Logith's records merely confirm, or it could mean both must align.

**Why it matters:** In a dispute over whether an outage occurred (e.g., the platform was slow but not fully down, or Draibav's monitoring showed "up" while Logith's users could not bid), the measurement standard matters. If Draibav's tools are the primary measure, Logith bears the burden of proving Draibav's tools were wrong.

**Recommended fix:** Revise §1.4 to state: "Downtime shall be determined by reference to Licensee's reasonable records, including user-access logs, call-center records and auction-day incident reports, cross-referenced with Licensor's monitoring data. In the event of a discrepancy, the Party with the more granular and time-stamped record shall prevail, provided such record is made available to the other Party within five (5) Business Days of request."

### 11. Draibav May List Logith as a Customer Without Ongoing Consent

**Where:** Agreement §13.1

**Issue:** Section 13.1 permits Licensor to "identify Licensee as a customer in a customer list after the Subscription Start Date" without Logith's consent.

**Why it matters:** Given the exclusivity clause (Issue 2), being publicly identified as a Draibav customer signals to the market that Logith is locked into a particular platform. If the relationship sours (as the current dispute suggests it may), Logith would prefer not to be associated with Draibav in its customer lists. It also has implications for Logith's own marketing — competitors of Draibav may target Logith based on its public association.

**Recommended fix:** Delete the customer-list exception or limit it to "upon Logith's prior written consent, which may be granted or withheld in Logith's discretion."

---

### 12. Transition Assistance Limited to Six Months

**Where:** Agreement §12.7

**Issue:** On termination, Draibav is obligated to provide transition assistance for "up to six (6) months." Migrating six auction sites, re-implementing simulcast bidding, re-training staff, and re-integrating APIs with a new platform is a significant project.

**Why it matters:** If the six-month window expires before Logith has completed its migration, Logith is on its own — potentially without access to the Platform and without Draibav's cooperation. Given the data-export window is only 60 days (§7.3), the practical migration window is even shorter.

**Recommended fix:** Extend transition assistance to 12 months. Alternatively, tie it to the data-export period plus a reasonable implementation period for a replacement platform (e.g., "the later of (a) twelve (12) months after termination or (b) six (6) months after Licensee's written confirmation that its replacement platform is operational at all Licensed Sites").

### 13. No Specific Remedy for Licensor-Caused Implementation Delays

**Where:** Order Form §6.3, §7.2

**Issue:** The Implementation Fee is $96,000, with 40% ($38,400) due on the Effective Date (June 16, 2025) regardless of progress. The implementation plan is to be delivered within 15 Business Days (§7.2), but there is no liquidated damages or fee-credit mechanism if Licensor delays go-live through its own fault.

**Why it matters:** If Draibav's implementation team is slow (as the availability issues suggest its engineering may be stretched), Logith pays the full implementation fee and loses auction revenue during the delay, with no offset. Subscription Fees don't start until go-live (§2.4), which helps, but the $38,400 upfront payment is at risk.

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision to the Order Form: "If a Licensed Site's Go-Live Date is delayed more than thirty (30) days beyond the target date in the Implementation Plan due to Licensor's fault or failure to perform, the Implementation Fee shall be reduced by $[X] per day of delay for that Site, and Licensee shall be entitled to terminate the Implementation Services for that Site with a pro rata refund of unearned Implementation Fees."
