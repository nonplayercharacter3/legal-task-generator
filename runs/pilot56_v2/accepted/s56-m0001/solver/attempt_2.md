## Summary

I have reviewed all seven documents in the matter folder. I identify **fourteen issues**, several of which are critical and time-sensitive. The most urgent concern is that Logith's parent company (Logith Holdings, Inc.) is expected to merge with Ostrander Remarketing Partners, L.P. in Q4 2026, but the Agreement treats any indirect change of control as a deemed assignment requiring Draibav's consent — and no such consent appears in the file. A refusal to consent would permit Draibav to terminate and accelerate all remaining fees.

## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Change of Control – No Consent for Pending Merger

**Where:** License Agreement § 15.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital B; Term Sheet § 10.

**Issue:** Section 15.2 provides that any "direct or indirect change of control of Licensee … including any such transaction involving any entity that directly or indirectly controls Licensee" is deemed an assignment requiring Draibav's prior written consent. Without consent, it is "a material breach of this Agreement that is not capable of cure," and Draibav may terminate, triggering § 12.5 (all remaining Subscription Fees become immediately due).

Amendment No. 1, Recital B, confirms that Logith Holdings, Inc. (Logith's parent) signed a merger agreement with Ostrander Remarketing Partners, L.P. on April 20, 2026, with closing expected in Q4 2026. The Amendment (executed May 29, 2026 — *after* the merger agreement was signed) does **not** include any consent to the change of control, any waiver of § 15.2, or any modification of its terms.

The Term Sheet (§ 10) left change-of-control treatment "to be addressed in the Definitive Agreement," so Draibav will argue the final language is enforceable as written.

**Why it matters:** If the merger closes in Q4 2026 without Draibav's consent, Draibav can terminate immediately and accelerate roughly 34 months of remaining fees (~$2.65 million at $78,000/month). Because the breach is "not capable of cure," Logith cannot fix the problem after the fact. This also creates deal risk for the Ostrander merger itself — a counterparty or lender could view the potential loss of the auction platform as a material adverse effect.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain written consent from Draibav to the change of control **before** the Ostrander merger closes. This should be a condition to closing in the merger agreement if not already one.
- Negotiate a consent that (a) confirms § 15.2 is satisfied, (b) clarifies that the merger does not trigger § 12.5, and (c) addresses whether Ostrander's existing auction facilities would be subject to the exclusivity restriction (see Issue 2 below).
- If Draibav resists, consider whether the Chronic Failure termination (Issue 4) provides leverage to negotiate a global resolution.

### 2. Exclusivity Clause – Scope Far Exceeds Term Sheet

**Where:** License Agreement § 3.4; Term Sheet § 4.

**Issue:** The Term Sheet (§ 4.1–4.2) limited exclusivity to the six Licensed Sites and expressly stated: "Nothing in this Section 4 restricts Logith or its affiliates in respect of any other software or any other facility."

The final Agreement (§ 3.4) provides that during the Term, Logith "shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, directly or indirectly license, use, develop or procure, **anywhere in the United States or Canada**, any online, simulcast or mobile vehicle auction software, bidding software or condition reporting software other than the Platform, **whether provided by a third party or developed internally**."

This is a material expansion in three dimensions:
- **Geography:** From six Licensed Sites to all of the US and Canada.
- **Entities:** From Logith alone to all Affiliates (which, post-merger, would include Ostrander and its subsidiaries).
- **Activity:** From using competing software to also "develop[ing]" or "procuring" any such software, including internally.

**Why it matters:** If the Ostrander merger closes, Ostrander's auction operations (wherever located in the US or Canada) would be restricted from using any auction software other than LaneCast. Ostrander may already use a different platform or have its own technology. This could force Ostrander to migrate to LaneCast or breach the Agreement. Even pre-merger, Logith is prohibited from developing internal tools for any facility in North America.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the change-of-control consent negotiation (Issue 1), seek to narrow § 3.4 to the Licensed Sites (or to Licensed Sites plus sites owned by Logith Holdings and its subsidiaries *at the time of signing*), and delete the "develop … internally" language or limit it to the Licensed Sites.
- If Draibav will not agree to a narrowing, price the risk: confirm with the client whether Ostrander's current technology stack is compatible with LaneCast and what the migration cost would be.

### 3. "Licensee Data" Definition Conflict – Order Form Controls and Narrows Ownership

**Where:** Order Form § 4.1; License Agreement § 1.9; License Agreement § 2.3 (Order of Precedence); Term Sheet § 7.1.

**Issue:** The Agreement (§ 1.9) defines "Licensee Data" broadly to include all data "submitted to, collected by, entered into, stored in or generated by the Platform," with six enumerated categories (bidder/consignor registration data, bid histories, hammer/reserve prices, condition reports, transaction data, and derivatives).

The Order Form (§ 4.1) defines "Licensee Data" narrowly as "data and information **uploaded to the Platform by Licensee's Authorized Users** in connection with Licensee's use of the Platform."

Under § 2.3 (Order of Precedence), the Order Form governs over the body of the Agreement. The Order Form's definition therefore controls.

The Term Sheet (§ 7.1) contemplated the broader definition: "Logith will own all data submitted to, collected by or generated by the Platform in connection with Logith's auctions, including bidder and consignor registration data, bid histories, hammer and reserve prices, condition reports and transaction data."

**Why it matters:** Under the controlling (narrow) definition, data that is *generated by the Platform* or *entered by Bidders or Consignors directly* (e.g., bid timestamps, proxy bids, mobile bids, condition report photographs taken through the Platform, registration data entered by Bidders) may **not** be "Licensee Data." Consequences:
- Logith may not "own" that data under § 7.1.
- Draibav may be able to commercialize it under § 7.4 (see Issue 8).
- The data may not be subject to the return/deletion obligations in § 7.3 or the security protections in § 8A.
- In a termination scenario, Logith could lose access to its own bid histories and sale results.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend Order Form § 4.1 to conform to the Agreement's broader definition in § 1.9, or to at minimum track the Term Sheet language ("submitted to, collected by or generated by the Platform in connection with Logith's auctions").
- This is a one-line fix but should be addressed in the next amendment or as part of the change-of-control consent negotiation.

### 4. Termination Notice – Potential Untimeliness

**Where:** Service Level Schedule § 5.1–5.2; Amendment No. 1, Recitals C and § 6.

**Issue:** SLS § 5.1 defines "Chronic Failure" as Monthly Availability below 99.0% in any three calendar months within a rolling six-month period. The monthly reports show: January 2026 – 98.1%; February 2026 – 97.6%; March 2026 – 98.4%. All three are below 99.0% and fall within a rolling six-month period. A Chronic Failure appears to have occurred.

SLS § 5.2 requires termination notice "within thirty (30) days after the end of the third affected month." The third affected month is March 2026, which ended March 31, 2026. Thirty days later is **April 30, 2026**. Logith's Termination Notice was delivered **May 4, 2026** — four days late.

Draibav disputed the notice's effectiveness on May 11, 2026 (Amendment No. 1, Recital C). Amendment No. 1 § 6 expressly reserves both parties' rights on this point.

**Why it matters:** If the notice is untimely and invalid, the Agreement continues for the full 60-month term (through June 30, 2030), and Logith loses its right to exit for Chronic Failure. This also removes Logith's leverage in the change-of-control negotiation (Issue 1). Conversely, if the notice is valid, the Agreement terminates October 31, 2026, and Logith must plan for platform transition during the same period the Ostrander merger is closing.

**Arguments that the notice may still be valid:**
- SLS § 2.4 requires Draibav to deliver the monthly availability report within 10 days after month-end. The March report was therefore not due until April 10, 2026. One could argue that "the end of the third affected month" should be read as the point at which Chronic Failure is *confirmed* by the required report, giving Logith until May 10.
- The four-day delay is minor, and Draibav's own failure to timely deliver reports (if it did not deliver the March report by April 10) could estop it from raising the timeliness defense.

**Arguments against:**
- The plain language says "after the end of the third affected month," not "after receipt of the report."
- Logith had actual knowledge of the availability figures (it operates the sites and would have experienced the downtime).

**Recommended fix:**
- Determine whether Draibav actually delivered the March 2026 report by April 10, 2026. If it did not, this strengthens Logith's position.
- In the change-of-control consent negotiation, seek a global resolution that either (a) confirms the Termination Notice is valid and effective, or (b) if Draibav prevails on timeliness, provides Logith an alternative exit right (e.g., termination for convenience with a reduced fee) effective on a date certain.
- Do **not** assume the termination is valid. Plan operationally for both scenarios (termination October 31, 2026 vs. continuation through 2030).

## HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 5. Liability Cap Exclusion – Section 8A May Not Be Covered

**Where:** License Agreement § 10.4(b); Amendment No. 1 §§ 2–3 (deleting § 8.4–8.7, inserting new § 8A); Amendment No. 1 § 5.1.

**Issue:** Section 10.4(b) excludes "a Party's breach of Section 8" from the liability cap (§ 10.3) and the indirect damages waiver (§ 10.1). Originally, the data security provisions (§§ 8.4–8.7) were part of Section 8, so a data security breach was uncapped.

Amendment No. 1 deletes §§ 8.4–8.7 and inserts a new, separate "Section 8A" immediately after Section 8. Section 8 now contains only §§ 8.1–8.3 (confidentiality). Section 8A contains the data security obligations.

The question is whether "breach of Section 8" in § 10.4(b) encompasses a breach of the separate Section 8A. On a plain reading, Section 8A is a distinct section, and § 10.4(b) references only "Section 8." This means a Draibav data security breach under the new, more detailed § 8A (e.g., failure to patch within 15 days, failure to notify within 48 hours, unauthorized subprocessor access) may be **subject to the 12-month fee cap** (~$936,000) rather than uncapped.

**Why it matters:** A major data breach affecting Logith's bidder and consignor information (dealer licenses, financial data, personal information subject to GLBA) could generate liabilities (regulatory fines, class actions, credit monitoring costs) far exceeding $936,000. The cap would leave Logith bearing the excess.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend § 10.4(b) to read "a Party's breach of Section 8 **or Section 8A**." This is a simple, targeted fix that should be included in the next amendment or as part of the change-of-control consent.
- Alternatively, amend § 10.4(b) to read "a Party's breach of Section 8 (including Section 8A)."

### 6. Renewal Term Length – 60 Months vs. Term Sheet's 12 Months

**Where:** License Agreement § 12.2; Term Sheet § 3.2.

**Issue:** The Term Sheet provided for "successive **12-month** renewal terms." The Agreement provides that each Renewal Term is "equal in length to the Initial Subscription Term" — i.e., **60 months**.

**Why it matters:** If Logith does not give non-renewal notice during the narrow window described in Issue 7, it will be locked in for another five years rather than one. This dramatically reduces Logith's negotiating leverage and exit flexibility after the Initial Term.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend § 12.2 to provide for 12-month renewal terms, consistent with the Term Sheet.
- If the Chronic Failure termination (Issue 4) is valid and the Agreement ends October 31, 2026, this issue becomes moot. But if the termination is invalid, this is a significant lock-in.

### 7. Non-Renewal Notice Window – 180 Days vs. Term Sheet's 90 Days

**Where:** License Agreement § 12.2; Term Sheet § 3.2.

**Issue:** The Term Sheet required non-renewal notice "at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term." The Agreement requires notice to be delivered "during the thirty (30) day period ending 180 days before the expiration" of the then-current term.

For the Initial Term ending June 30, 2030, the notice window is approximately **December 2, 2029 through January 1, 2030** — a 30-day window opening 210 days before expiration.

**Why it matters:** Logith must make a multi-million-dollar go/no-go decision 180 days before the term ends, rather than 90 days. If Logith misses this narrow window, it is locked in for another Renewal Term (60 months under the current Agreement, per Issue 6). The window is also unusually short (30 days) for a decision of this magnitude.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend § 12.2 to require notice at least 90 days before expiration (matching the Term Sheet), or to provide a longer window (e.g., 90–180 days before expiration).
- Calendar the notice deadline immediately upon resolution of the termination dispute (Issue 4) so the client is not caught by surprise.

### 8. Draibav's Right to Commercialize Data – Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where:** License Agreement § 7.4; Term Sheet § 7.2.

**Issue:** The Term Sheet limited Draibav to "Platform operational and performance data that **does not identify Logith, its sites, bidders or consignors**."

The Agreement (§ 7.4) permits Draibav to "collect, use, aggregate, analyze and commercialize data and information relating to the operation and use of the Platform **other than Licensee Data**," including to "develop and sell analytics products, market reports and market pricing benchmarks to third parties."

Given the narrow Order Form definition of "Licensee Data" (Issue 3), a large category of data — including Platform-generated bid data, pricing outcomes, and usage statistics that identify Logith's sites — may fall outside "Licensee Data" and thus be freely commercializable by Draibav.

**Why it matters:** Draibav could package Logith's hammer prices, auction volumes, and pricing trends into "market pricing benchmarks" sold to Logith's competitors (other auction companies, dealers, or investors). This is a direct competitive harm.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend § 7.4 to add the Term Sheet's limitation: Draibav may commercialize only data that does not identify Logith, any Licensed Site, any Bidder, or any Consignor, either individually or in aggregate.
- Alternatively, expand the definition of "Licensee Data" (Issue 3) to capture all data that identifies or can be associated with Logith's operations, which would automatically exclude it from § 7.4.

### 9. Fee Acceleration on Licensor Termination (§ 12.5)

**Where:** License Agreement §§ 12.3, 12.5, 15.2.

**Issue:** If Draibav terminates for Logith's material breach (e.g., an unauthorized change of control under § 15.2), § 12.5 makes "all Subscription Fees for the remainder of the then-current Initial Subscription Term or Renewal Term … immediately due and payable." There is no early-termination fee; the entire remaining term's fees accelerate.

**Why it matters:** At $78,000/month, a termination in Q4 2026 would accelerate approximately $2.65 million. This is a severe penalty for what may be a routine corporate transaction (a merger of the parent). It also gives Draibav enormous leverage: it can threaten to accelerate fees to extract a consent fee or other concessions.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the change-of-control consent, negotiate that § 12.5 will not apply to a termination resulting from the Ostrander merger (or that consent, once given, eliminates the breach entirely).
- More broadly, consider negotiating a cap on acceleration (e.g., limited to 6 months of fees) or a buyout formula in lieu of full acceleration.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 10. Insurance Cancellation Notice – 10-Day Gap for Nonpayment

**Where:** License Agreement § 11.2; Certificate of Insurance § 7.2.

**Issue:** The Agreement requires Draibav to give "at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice of cancellation or material reduction of any required coverage." The Certificate of Insurance (§ 7.2) confirms 30 days' notice for most cancellations but provides only **10 days' notice for cancellation due to nonpayment of premium**.

**Why it matters:** If Draibav fails to pay its insurance premium, the policy could be cancelled with only 10 days' notice, leaving Logith with less protection than the Agreement requires. In a claims-made policy (TEO and Cyber), a cancellation could create a coverage gap for incidents that occurred during the policy period but are reported after cancellation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Request that Draibav's broker confirm the 30-day notice applies regardless of the reason for cancellation, or that Draibav will maintain a premium payment escrow or automatic payment arrangement to eliminate the nonpayment risk.
- Add a covenant requiring Draibav to notify Logith within 48 hours of receiving any notice of cancellation or nonpayment from its insurer, regardless of the policy's own notice provisions.

### 11. Downtime Definition – Ambiguous Confirmation Standard

**Where:** Service Level Schedule § 1.4.

**Issue:** "Downtime" is defined as unavailability "as measured by Licensor's monitoring tools **and confirmed by** Licensee's reasonable records." The conjunction "and" creates ambiguity: does it mean (a) Licensor's tools measure the event and Licensee's records confirm it (i.e., both must agree), or (b) the measurement is by Licensor's tools, with Licensee's records available to confirm or supplement?

**Why it matters:** In a dispute (e.g., the Platform was slow or partially degraded but Licensor's monitoring showed "available"), reading (a) would require Logith to independently prove the downtime, while reading (b) would place the initial burden on Licensor's tools. Given that the Chronic Failure dispute (Issue 4) turned on availability measurements, this definitional point could recur.

**Recommended fix:**
- Clarify in the next amendment that Downtime is "as measured by Licensor's monitoring tools, which Licensee may supplement with its own reasonable records," or that a material discrepancy between the parties' records triggers a joint investigation.

### 12. Implementation Fees – No Protection for Licensor Delay

**Where:** Order Form §§ 6.3, 7.2.

**Issue:** The $96,000 implementation fee is payable in milestones tied to Go-Live Dates (40% at signing, 30% at third site go-live, 30% at last site go-live). There is no provision reducing or deferring these payments if Licensor fails to meet the implementation plan timeline or if Go-Live Dates are delayed due to Licensor's fault.

**Why it matters:** If Draibav's implementation is delayed (e.g., due to the same infrastructure issues causing the Chronic Failure), Logith still owes milestone payments on the original schedule or, at minimum, has no express right to withhold.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a provision that implementation fee milestones are tied to the *actual* Go-Live Dates (as defined in Order Form § 3.2, which already requires Licensee's written acceptance) and that Logith may withhold milestone payments if the delay is attributable to Licensor.
- Confirm with the client whether all implementation milestones have been paid and all sites are live. If so, this is a lower-priority issue.

### 13. Transition Assistance – Uncapped Professional Services Rates

**Where:** License Agreement § 12.7; Order Form § 6.4.

**Issue:** On termination, Licensor must provide transition assistance "at its then-current professional services rates" (Order Form § 6.4: $195/hour). There is no cap on hours or total cost.

**Why it matters:** A six-site transition (data migration, reconfiguration, parallel running) could require substantial hours. At $195/hour, a 500-hour transition would cost $97,500 on top of any remaining subscription fees during the 6-month transition period.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a fixed-fee or capped transition assistance package in the next amendment, or at minimum a not-to-exceed amount.
- If the Chronic Failure termination is valid, ensure the transition plan is scoped and budgeted before October 31, 2026.

### 14. Operational Risk – Termination and Merger Closing in Same Period

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Recitals B and C; SLS § 5.2.

**Issue:** If the Termination Notice is valid, the Agreement ends October 31, 2026. The Ostrander merger is expected to close in Q4 2026. Logith would be migrating six auction sites off its primary bidding platform during the same period it is integrating a new parent company and potentially new subsidiaries.

**Why it matters:** Auction operations cannot simply stop. A failed or delayed transition could result in lost revenue, bidder/consignor dissatisfaction, and potential breach of Logith's own contracts with dealers. The timing also complicates the change-of-control consent negotiation (Issue 1): if the Agreement is terminating anyway, Draibav's leverage on consent is reduced, but Logith's need for a clean transition is increased.

**Recommended fix:**
- Develop a contingency transition plan now, assuming the Agreement terminates October 31, 2026. Identify alternative platforms or internal workarounds.
- Coordinate the timing of the change-of-control consent request with the transition plan: if Logith is exiting, the consent negotiation should focus on a clean wind-down (data return, transition assistance, no fee acceleration) rather than a long-term relationship.
- Brief the client's CFO and COO on the dual-track scenario (termination vs. continuation) so operational planning can proceed in parallel.
