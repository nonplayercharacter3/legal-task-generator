## HIGH PRIORITY

### 1. Hollis Ridge consent missing from Schedule 4.4

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 6 (Schedule 4.4); Disclosure Schedule § 8, item 1 (Hollis Ridge Contract, Assignment clause); APA § 4.3(c), § 4.4, § 7.2(c)

**Issue:** The Hollis Ridge Contract—the largest district contract at approximately $3.1M annually—requires "the prior written approval of the District's Board of Education" for any assignment or change of control (Disclosure Schedule § 8, item 1, describing Section 14 of the contract). Yet Schedule 4.4 lists only two consents: Dunmore and Ashgrove. Hollis Ridge is absent.

This creates two problems:
- Seller's representation in APA § 4.3(c) (no consent required "except as set forth on Schedule 4.4") is inaccurate as of signing.
- APA § 7.2(c) conditions Closing on receipt of "each Consent listed on Schedule 4.4." Because Hollis Ridge is not listed, Laivoul has no contractual hook to require it before Closing. If the assignment proceeds without Board approval, the District could treat the assignment as void, and Laivoul would lose its largest revenue contract.

**Recommendation:** Add the Hollis Ridge Board of Education consent to Schedule 4.4 before signing. Confirm that the consent process has been initiated and that a written consent (or at minimum a letter of intent to consent) is a condition to Closing under § 7.2(c). If the District's process requires a Board vote, build sufficient lead time into the outside date.

### 2. No closing condition for payoff and release of Keystone UCC lien

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 7 (Schedule 4.5, last paragraph); APA § 4.5, Article VII (no corresponding condition)

**Issue:** A UCC financing statement filed by Keystone Commercial Equipment Finance, LLC covers Units 149 through 190 (42 IC Bus CE Series, model years 2021–2022). The Disclosure Schedule states the debt "will be paid off, and a UCC-3 termination statement and lien releases delivered, at or before the Closing." However:
- This is a statement in the Disclosure Schedule, not a covenant in the APA.
- No condition in APA § 7.2 requires delivery of the UCC-3 or lien releases.
- No item in Seller's Closing deliverables (§ 3.2) includes lien releases.

If the payoff does not occur or the releases are not delivered, Laivoul would take 42 buses subject to a third-party security interest. The rep in § 4.5 (title free of Encumbrances other than Permitted Encumbrances) is expressly contradicted by the Disclosure Schedule, which states the Keystone filing "is not a Permitted Encumbrance." So at signing, the rep is technically false, and the only protection is the 12% indemnity cap (see Issue 10 below).

**Recommendation:** Add to APA § 7.2 a new condition: "Seller shall have delivered (i) evidence of payoff of the Keystone Commercial Equipment Finance indebtedness and (ii) a UCC-3 termination statement and individual lien releases for Units 149–190, in form reasonably satisfactory to Buyer." Alternatively, add the releases to § 3.2 as a Seller Closing deliverable.

### 3. TSA permits Seller to subcontract driving services without Laivoul's consent

**Where:** Transition Services Agreement § 12.3

**Issue:** "Provider may assign, delegate or subcontract this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations hereunder, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient." During the Transition Period, Seller's drivers (or whoever Seller substitutes) will be transporting schoolchildren in Laivoul's buses under Laivoul's assumed district contracts. If Seller subcontracts to an unknown operator with inferior drivers, inadequate insurance, or a poor safety record, Laivoul bears the operational and reputational risk (and, under the TSA indemnity in § 8.1, the financial risk as well—see Issue 4).

The asymmetry is stark: Laivoul may not assign without Seller's consent (second sentence of § 12.3), but Seller may assign to anyone freely.

**Recommendation:** Delete or revise the first sentence of § 12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment, delegation, or subcontracting of the Services. At minimum, require that any subcontractor meet the same qualification standards in § 3.1 (CDL, P/S endorsements, Article 19-A, drug and alcohol testing) and maintain equivalent insurance.

### 4. TSA indemnity requires Laivoul to cover Seller's ordinary negligence

**Where:** Transition Services Agreement § 8.1; APA § 2.3(b) (Operational Liabilities as Assumed Liabilities)

**Issue:** TSA § 8.1 provides that Laivoul "shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Provider" from all Losses arising out of the Services "except to the extent caused by Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct." This means Laivoul absorbs the cost of any accident caused by a Seller driver's ordinary negligence during the 150-day Transition Period.

Compounding this, APA § 2.3(b) makes Operational Liabilities (all Liabilities arising from operation of the Vehicles after Closing) an Assumed Liability. So if a Seller driver rear-ends a car on day 30 of the transition:
- The third-party claim is an Operational Liability that Laivoul has assumed under the APA; and
- Laivoul must additionally indemnify Seller under TSA § 8.1.

The net effect is that Laivoul bears 100% of the cost of Seller's drivers' ordinary negligence, even though those drivers are Seller's employees, supervised by Seller, and operating under Seller's authority.

**Recommendation:** Revise TSA § 8.1 to limit Laivoul's indemnity to Losses "to the extent caused by Recipient's negligence or willful misconduct." Alternatively, change the standard to "except to the extent caused by Provider's negligence or willful misconduct" (i.e., Seller indemnifies Laivoul for Seller's ordinary negligence, and Laivoul indemnifies Seller only for its own). This aligns the TSA with the general principle that each party bears the consequences of its own employees' conduct.

## MODERATE PRIORITY

### 5. Non-fundamental representations survive only 120 days post-Closing

**Where:** APA § 1.1 (definition of "Survival Date"); § 9.1; Seller Note § 2.2 (first Scheduled Payment Date = 120 days after Issue Date)

**Issue:** All non-fundamental reps (vehicles, contracts, billing, compliance, environmental, IP, employees, etc.) survive only until the first Scheduled Payment Date under the Seller Note—120 days after Closing. For a business with 212 buses, four district contracts worth ~$8.4M annually, complex regulatory compliance (NYSDOT inspections, Article 19-A, drug testing, transportation aid billing), and 290 employees, many material problems will not surface within 120 days. Examples:
- A district audit of transportation aid billing typically occurs on an annual or multi-year cycle; a billing error from the prior school year may not be discovered until well after 120 days.
- A latent vehicle defect (brake system, frame corrosion) may not manifest until the next inspection cycle or a failure in service.
- A driver qualification issue (lapsed medical certificate, undetected disqualifying conviction) may not be caught until a DOT audit or a district complaint.

The Fundamental Representations (organization, authority, title, taxes, brokers) survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations, which is reasonable. But the operational reps that matter most to Laivoul die in four months.

**Recommendation:** Extend the survival period for non-fundamental reps to at least 18 months post-Closing (covering a full school year plus the next inspection cycle). Alternatively, tie survival to the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations for all reps, with a backstop of 24 months. At minimum, extend survival for § 4.6 (Vehicles), § 4.8 (Material Contracts), § 4.9 (District Billing), and § 4.10 (Compliance) to 18 months.

### 6. Governing law and forum conflict between the APA and the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note §§ 10.1–10.2 (Pennsylvania law; Allegheny County, PA); APA §§ 11.8–11.9 (New York law; New York County, NY); APA § 11.12 (Note controls for "payment, enforcement and collection")

**Issue:** The Note is governed by Pennsylvania law with exclusive jurisdiction in Allegheny County. The APA is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. APA § 11.12 concedes that the Note controls for payment, enforcement, and collection. This creates a practical problem for the set-off right in APA § 9.7: if Laivoul exercises a set-off against a Note payment and Seller disputes it, Seller could file in Allegheny County under PA law, while Laivoul's underlying indemnification claim arises under NY law in New York County. The result is potentially parallel or sequential proceedings in two states under two bodies of law.

**Recommendation:** Conform Note §§ 10.1 and 10.2 to New York law and New York County jurisdiction. If Seller resists (PA is its home state), at minimum add a provision to the Note stating that any dispute arising from or relating to a set-off under APA § 9.7 shall be governed by New York law and submitted to the jurisdiction of New York County courts.

### 7. Laivoul receives routing data but no routing software; 150-day window to stand up its own platform

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (Excluded IP definition); Disclosure Schedule § 5(b); TSA § 3.3 (read-and-export access); TSA § 2.1 (150-day Transition Period)

**Issue:** Seller's routing, dispatch, and payroll software platforms are Excluded IP (IP Assignment § 1.1; Disclosure Schedule § 5(b)). Laivoul receives the data (in CSV and shapefile format per IP Assignment Exhibit A) but not the software. During the Transition Period, Laivoul has read-and-export access to Seller's platforms (TSA § 3.3) for migration purposes. After the TSA expires (day 150), Laivoul must operate on its own systems.

If Laivoul has not contracted for and implemented its own routing/dispatch platform before the Transition Period ends, it faces a gap: no software to plan routes, dispatch drivers, or generate the route sheets and mileage reports required by the District Contracts. This is not a theoretical risk—routing software implementation for a 212-bus, 180+ route operation typically takes 60–120 days from data import to go-live.

**Recommendation:** Before signing, confirm that Laivoul has a routing/dispatch platform selected, contracted, and on a timeline to be operational before day 150. If not, negotiate one of the following:
- A short-term software license from Seller (e.g., 60–90 days post-TSA) as an addendum to the TSA, or
- An extension of the TSA Transition Period (see Issue 8), or
- A specific covenant in the TSA that Seller will provide software access beyond the 150-day period if Laivoul's platform is not yet live, at a reduced fee.

### 8. 150-day Transition Period may be insufficient to obtain operating authority

**Where:** TSA § 2.1; APA § 6.6; TSA § 10.1

**Issue:** The Transition Period is a fixed 150 days. During this period, Seller operates the buses under its own USDOT number and NYSDOT authority. Laivoul must obtain its own operating authority and permits (APA § 6.6). If the application process is delayed (incomplete filing, FMCSA review, NYSDOT inspection of the depot, etc.), Laivoul could reach day 150 without authority. At that point:
- The TSA expires automatically (§ 2.2).
- Laivoul has no drivers (they are Seller's employees during the TSA).
- Laivoul has no operating authority.
- The District Contracts require continuous service.

There is no extension mechanism in the TSA.

**Recommendation:** Add an extension provision to TSA § 2.1: if Laivoul's application for operating authority is pending with a Governmental Authority as of the 150th day, the Transition Period extends automatically for up to 60 additional days (or until the application is resolved, whichever is earlier), with Service Fees continuing at the same rate. Alternatively, increase the base Transition Period to 180 days.

### 9. Taconic Falls and Verbank Ridge contracts expire June 30, 2027

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 8, items 3 and 4; APA § 4.8

**Issue:** The Taconic Falls Contract (~$2.38M/yr) runs through June 30, 2027, with a possible extension at the District's discretion under Education Law § 305(14). The Verbank Ridge Contract (~$910K/yr) runs through June 30, 2027 with no stated extension mechanism. If Closing occurs in, say, December 2026, both contracts have approximately 6–7 months remaining. Combined, they represent ~$3.3M in annual revenue (roughly 39% of total district contract value).

Seller's rep in APA § 4.8 states that "Seller has received no written notice of termination, non-renewal or intent to rebid from any counterparty." But the absence of a termination notice is not the same as a commitment to renew. If either District decides not to extend or rebids, Laivoul loses significant revenue shortly after Closing, and the 120-day survival period (Issue 5) may have already expired.

**Recommendation:** Before signing, request written confirmation from Taconic Falls and Verbank Ridge of their intent to extend or renew (or at minimum, that no rebid is planned). Add a specific rep to APA § 4.8: "As of the date hereof, neither Taconic Falls Central School District nor Verbank Ridge Union Free School District has indicated an intention to rebid, terminate or non-renew the applicable contract." Extend survival of § 4.8 to cover at least the first full school year post-Closing.

### 10. 12% indemnity cap may be inadequate

**Where:** APA § 9.5

**Issue:** The cap on Seller's indemnification for non-fundamental rep breaches is 12% of the Purchase Price = $2,208,000. Consider the following potential loss scenarios that could each approach or exceed this amount:
- Loss of the Hollis Ridge contract (if assignment is challenged): ~$3.1M in annual revenue.
- A fleet-wide compliance issue (e.g., systematic inspection failures across the 212 buses): remediation costs, out-of-service periods, and potential district penalties.
- A district billing audit revealing systematic overbilling or underbilling across multiple districts and multiple years.

The cap is not adjusted for the size or complexity of the business. For an $18.4M acquisition of an operating business with significant regulatory exposure, 12% is on the low end.

**Recommendation:** Increase the cap to 15–20% of the Purchase Price ($2.76M–$3.68M). Alternatively, carve specific high-risk reps out of the cap (e.g., § 4.6 Vehicles, § 4.8 Material Contracts, § 4.9 District Billing) and subject them to a higher cap or the Fundamental Rep cap (100% of Purchase Price).

### 11. Mechanics and office staff excluded from retention Side Letter

**Where:** Side Letter Annex A (271 Covered Employees); Disclosure Schedule § 10.1 (290 total employees); APA § 6.5

**Issue:** The Side Letter covers 224 drivers, 41 monitors, and 6 dispatchers = 271 employees. The 14 mechanics/shop personnel and 5 depot manager/office staff are not Covered Employees. APA § 6.5 requires Laivoul to offer employment only to those "identified in, and on the terms set forth in, the Driver Retention Side Letter." The non-solicitation in Side Letter § 4.4 covers only Transferred Employees.

The mechanics are critical: they maintain 212 buses, perform NYSDOT-required inspections, and manage the parts inventory (APA § 2.1(d)). If they walk, Laivoul loses institutional knowledge of the fleet and may face inspection delays. Seller is free to solicit them for its Pennsylvania operations or other ventures.

**Recommendation:** Add the 14 mechanics and 5 office staff to Annex A as Covered Employees, or at minimum add them to the non-solicitation in Side Letter § 4.4. If Laivoul does not intend to hire all of them, a targeted non-solicitation (e.g., "Seller shall not solicit any mechanic or shop employee of the Business for two years") is the minimum protection.

### 12. Tax allocation to be agreed post-Closing with no dispute mechanism

**Where:** APA § 2.7

**Issue:** The parties must agree on the § 1060 allocation "within sixty (60) days after the Closing Date." If they cannot agree, the provision is silent on what happens. There is no default allocation method, no expert determination, and no tie to the APA's dispute resolution (which is litigation in New York County under § 11.9). A dispute over allocation could delay tax filings and create inconsistent reporting.

**Recommendation:** Add a default: "If the Parties are unable to agree on the allocation within the 60-day period, the allocation shall be made in accordance with Treasury Regulation § 1.1060-1(c) (residual method) and the Parties shall file consistently therewith." Alternatively, add a binding expert determination mechanism.

## LOWER PRIORITY / MONITORING ITEMS

### 13. District revenue remittance during Transition Period

**Where:** TSA § 4.4

**Issue:** During the Transition Period, Seller collects district payments (because it is the named contractor and operates under its authority). TSA § 4.4 requires Seller to remit to Laivoul within five Business Days after receipt. For four contracts generating ~$8.4M annually (~$1.6M/month), a five-day lag means Seller is holding roughly $250K–$300K of Laivoul's revenue at any time. More importantly, if Seller becomes financially distressed during the transition, those funds are at risk.

**Recommendation:** Acceptable as drafted given the short duration, but monitor remittance timing closely. If Laivoul has concerns about Seller's financial condition, consider requiring a daily remittance or a letter of credit/escrow for district collections during the TSA.

### 14. Post-transition insurance requirements for Hollis Ridge

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 8, item 1 (Hollis Ridge insurance requirement); TSA § 7.1 (Provider insurance during transition); APA § 2.2 (insurance policies are Excluded Assets)

**Issue:** The Hollis Ridge Contract requires $10M combined auto liability with the District named as additional insured. During the Transition Period, Seller's policy covers this (TSA § 7.1). After the TSA expires, Laivoul must have its own $10M policy in place. Insurance policies are Excluded Assets (APA § 2.2), so Laivoul cannot simply assume Seller's policy. Binding a $10M auto liability policy for a 212-bus operation takes time and may require a broker placement process.

**Recommendation:** Confirm before Closing that Laivoul has engaged an insurance broker and has a plan to bind compliant coverage before the Transition Period ends. Consider adding a covenant in the TSA that Seller will maintain its insurance (with Laivoul and the districts as additional insureds) for 30 days beyond the Transition Period as a bridge, in case Laivoul's policy has a gap in effectiveness.
