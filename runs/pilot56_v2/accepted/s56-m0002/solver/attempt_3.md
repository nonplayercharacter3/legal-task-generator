## 1. Hollis Ridge Consent Missing from Schedule 4.4

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 6 (Schedule 4.4) lists only two consents (Dunmore and Ashgrove). Disclosure Schedule § 8.1 describes the Hollis Ridge Contract's assignment restriction. APA § 4.4, § 7.2(c).

**Why it matters:** The Hollis Ridge Contract is the largest District Contract (~$3.1M/year, 61 routes). Its Section 14 prohibits assignment "without the prior written approval of the District's Board of Education." That consent is not on Schedule 4.4, so it is not a Closing condition under APA § 7.2(c). If the assignment proceeds without Board approval, the Hollis Ridge Contract may be voidable at the District's election, and Laivoul would lose its single largest revenue source with no indemnification trigger (because the rep in § 4.8 that "Seller has received no written notice of termination" is not the rep that's breached—§ 4.4 is, and § 4.4 is not a Fundamental Representation).

**Recommended fix:** Add the Hollis Ridge Board of Education consent to Schedule 4.4 before signing. Confirm with Seller that the consent request has been made and is in process. If Seller resists, at minimum add a specific Closing condition in Article VII that the Hollis Ridge consent be delivered, and consider adding § 4.4 to the Fundamental Representations so a breach carries the full Purchase Price cap and longer survival.

## 2. Non-Fundamental Rep Survival Is Only 120 Days

**Where:** APA § 1.1 (definition of "Survival Date"); APA § 9.1; Seller Promissory Note § 2.2 (first Scheduled Payment Date = 120 days after Issue Date).

**Why it matters:** All Seller reps other than the Fundamental Reps (Organization, Authority, Title, Taxes, Brokers) survive only until the first Scheduled Payment Date—120 days after Closing. For a pupil transportation business, the most operationally important reps (§ 4.6 vehicle condition, § 4.8 contract status, § 4.9 billing accuracy, § 4.10 compliance, § 4.11 permits, § 4.13 employees) all fall within this short window. A school-year billing audit, a district rebid notice, or a driver qualification issue could surface after day 120, leaving Laivoul with no indemnification remedy for a rep breach (only the Excluded Liability route under § 9.2(c), which is narrower).

**Recommended fix:** Extend the Survival Date for non-Fundamental reps to at least the end of the first full school year after Closing (i.e., June 30 of the year following Closing), or alternatively to 18 months post-Closing. If Seller insists on tying survival to the Note, negotiate a longer first payment deferral or add a separate survival period for the operational reps (§§ 4.6–4.13) independent of the Note schedule.

## 3. No Maintenance or Management Staff Covered in Transition

**Where:** TSA § 1.1 (definition of "Service Personnel" = drivers, monitors, attendants, dispatchers, routers only); TSA § 3.7 (maintenance, repair, inspection, fueling, cleaning excluded from Services); Side Letter § 1.2 and Annex A (Covered Employees = 271 Service Personnel only); Disclosure Schedule § 10.1 (Business employs 14 mechanics and 5 depot manager/office staff).

**Why it matters:** At Closing, Laivoul takes possession of 212 buses, the depot, and the obligation to keep them in NYSDOT-compliant condition (APA § 6.1 requires Seller to maintain condition *until* Closing; after Closing it is Laivoul's responsibility). But Laivoul has no mechanics, no depot manager, and no office staff. The 14 mechanics and 5 management/office employees are not Service Personnel, not Covered Employees, and not subject to the retention offer obligation. Laivoul must immediately hire or contract for all maintenance and management functions on day one, with no transition support.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Expand the definition of "Service Personnel" in the TSA to include mechanics and shop personnel, so they are provided during the Transition Period; or (b) add the 14 mechanics and 5 management/office staff to Annex A of the Side Letter as Covered Employees so Laivoul is obligated to make them offers; or (c) at minimum, extend the Transition Period by 30–60 days specifically for the purpose of recruiting and onboarding maintenance and management staff, with Seller providing interim coverage. Option (a) is cleanest.

## 4. TSA Has a Hard 150-Day Expiration with No Extension

**Where:** TSA § 2.1 (Transition Period = 150 days); TSA § 2.2 (automatic expiration); APA § 6.6 (Seller provides drivers/authority "until" Buyer obtains its own).

**Why it matters:** If Laivoul has not obtained its own USDOT number, NYSDOT motor carrier registration, and operating authority within 150 days of Closing, the TSA simply expires. There is no extension mechanism, no holdover provision, and no penalty on Seller for the expiration. Laivoul would then hold 212 buses, a depot lease, and four District Contracts but no drivers, no dispatch, no operating authority, and no compliance program. The District Contracts likely have service-level requirements that would be breached immediately.

**Recommended fix:** Add an extension provision to TSA § 2.1: if Laivoul has not obtained its own authority by day 150, the Transition Period extends automatically for an additional 90 days (or until authority is obtained, whichever is earlier), with Service Fees continuing at the same rate. Alternatively, make Seller's failure to cooperate in Laivoul's authority application (e.g., providing required records, driver qualification files) a breach that tolls the 150-day period.

## 5. Seller May Subcontract TSA Services Without Buyer Consent

**Where:** TSA § 12.3 ("Provider may assign, delegate or subcontract this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations hereunder, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient.").

**Why it matters:** During the Transition Period, Seller is operating Laivoul's buses with Laivoul's students on board, under Laivoul's District Contracts. This clause permits Seller to hand off driving, dispatch, or compliance functions to any third party—without Laivoul's knowledge or approval. For a school bus operation subject to Article 19-A, 8 NYCRR § 156.3, and DOT drug/alcohol testing, the identity and qualifications of the personnel matter. A subcontractor's driver who fails a qualification check could create liability for Laivoul as the contract holder of record with the districts.

**Recommended fix:** Delete or substantially limit TSA § 12.3. At minimum, require that (a) any subcontractor of driving or dispatch services be pre-approved by Laivoul in its reasonable discretion; (b) the subcontractor meet the same qualification standards (CDL, P/S endorsements, Article 19-A, drug/alcohol testing); and (c) Seller remains fully liable for the subcontractor's performance. The one-sided assignment right (Seller free to assign, Laivoul cannot) should be made mutual.

## 6. UCC Lien on Units 149–190 Not an Express Closing Condition

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 7 (Schedule 4.5): UCC filing by Keystone Commercial Equipment Finance covers 42 buses; "will be paid off, and a UCC-3 termination statement and lien releases delivered, at or before the Closing." APA § 2.1 (assets transferred free of Encumbrances other than Permitted Encumbrances); APA Article VII (Closing conditions).

**Why it matters:** The lien is not a Permitted Encumbrance, so the assets must be delivered free of it. The Disclosure Schedule states it will be paid off at or before Closing, but this is not codified as a Closing condition in Article VII or a Seller deliverable in § 3.2. If the payoff falls through (e.g., Keystone disputes the payoff amount), Laivoul could close with 42 buses still subject to a third-party security interest, impairing title and potentially triggering a default under the Hollis Ridge or other District Contracts if the buses are out of service.

**Recommended fix:** Add to APA § 3.2 (Seller Closing Deliverables): "UCC-3 termination statements and lien releases from Keystone Commercial Equipment Finance, LLC, covering Units 149 through 190, in form satisfactory to Buyer." Add to APA § 7.2 (Conditions to Buyer's Obligations): "The UCC filing described on Schedule 4.5 shall have been terminated of record."

## 7. Governing Law and Jurisdiction Conflict Between APA and Seller Note

**Where:** APA § 11.8 (NY law), § 11.9 (NY County jurisdiction); Seller Note § 10.1 (PA law), § 10.2 (Allegheny County, PA jurisdiction); APA § 11.12 (Note controls for "payment, enforcement and collection").

**Why it matters:** Laivoul's primary self-help remedy against Seller is the set-off right in APA § 9.7 / Note § 5. A set-off directly affects "payment" of the Note. Under APA § 11.12, the Note controls for payment matters, which means PA law and PA jurisdiction would govern a set-off dispute. This creates uncertainty: Laivoul would need to litigate a set-off claim in Pennsylvania, under Pennsylvania law, even though the underlying indemnification claim arises from a New York-governed APA and involves New York operations. It also means Seller could potentially argue PA usury or contract principles to limit Laivoul's set-off.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Note § 10.1 and § 10.2 to conform to the APA (NY law, NY County jurisdiction), or add a proviso to APA § 11.12: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the rights and remedies of Buyer under Section 9.7 shall be governed by and enforced in accordance with the laws of the State of New York and the jurisdiction set forth in Section 11.9."

## 8. 12% Cap on Seller's Rep-Breach Indemnification May Be Inadequate

**Where:** APA § 9.5 (cap = 12% of Purchase Price = $2,208,000 for § 9.2(a) claims).

**Why it matters:** The cap applies to all non-Fundamental rep breaches. Given that the Disclosure Schedule reveals a pending personal injury litigation (Okafor, Schedule 4.12), three buses that were out of service at spring inspection (Schedule 2.3), and a $4.2M seller-financed component, a $2.2M cap may not cover a single significant rep breach (e.g., if multiple buses fail inspection post-Closing, or if a district billing audit reveals systemic overbilling). The cap does not apply to Fundamental Reps, covenant breaches, Excluded Liabilities, or fraud—but the most likely post-Closing disputes (vehicle condition, billing accuracy, compliance) are all non-Fundamental rep issues subject to the cap.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the cap to 20–25% of the Purchase Price ($3.7M–$4.6M), or carve out specific high-risk reps (§§ 4.6, 4.9, 4.10) from the cap. Alternatively, tie the cap to the Seller Note balance (so it declines as Laivoul pays down the Note, reflecting decreasing risk).

## 9. Estoppel Certificate Not Listed as a Closing Deliverable

**Where:** APA § 3.2 (Seller Closing Deliverables) lists the Lease Assignment but not the estoppel certificate. APA § 7.2(c) requires delivery of consents on Schedule 4.4. The estoppel (Depot Lease Estoppel Certificate § 9.1) contains the landlord's consent.

**Why it matters:** If the estoppel is not expressly required as a Closing deliverable, Seller could argue it satisfied § 7.2(c) by delivering a bare consent letter without the estoppel's representations (no defaults, rent paid current, security deposit intact, SNDA in place). Laivoul would then lack the landlord's acknowledgment of those facts, weakening its position if a dispute arises post-Closing.

**Recommended fix:** Add to APA § 3.2: "(j) the estoppel certificate from Ashgrove Depot Realty LLC in the form attached hereto as Exhibit [●]."

## 10. Hollis Ridge $10M Insurance Requirement – Post-Transition Gap

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 8.1 (Hollis Ridge requires $10M combined auto liability, District as additional insured); TSA § 7.1 (Provider maintains insurance during Transition Period); TSA § 2.2 (TSA expires at end of Transition Period).

**Why it matters:** During the Transition Period, Seller's auto liability policy covers the buses and names the Districts as additional insureds. Once the TSA expires (and Seller's USDOT markings come off the vehicles per TSA § 10.3), Laivoul must have its own $10M auto liability policy in place to comply with the Hollis Ridge Contract. If Laivoul's own authority and insurance are not in place by the TSA expiration date, there is a coverage gap during which the Hollis Ridge Contract's insurance requirement is unmet—potentially a material breach allowing the District to terminate.

**Recommended fix:** Make it a condition in the TSA (or a covenant in the APA) that Laivoul's auto liability insurance meeting the District Contracts' requirements be bound and in force no later than the TSA expiration date. Add a TSA provision requiring Seller to maintain its insurance coverage for 30 days past the Transition Period as a tail, to bridge any gap.

## 11. Side Letter § 4.5 – Buyer Reimburses Seller for WARN if Buyer Fails to Make Offer

**Where:** Side Letter § 4.5 (last sentence): "if Buyer fails to make an offer to a Covered Employee as required by Section 2, Buyer shall reimburse Seller for severance and notice Liabilities attributable to that Covered Employee."

**Why it matters:** This creates a financial penalty on Laivoul if it inadvertently fails to extend an offer to any one of the 271 Covered Employees (e.g., due to a data error in Annex A or a timing miss under § 2.1). With 271 employees, the risk of a single administrative oversight triggering a WARN/severance reimbursement is non-trivial. The penalty is uncapped and not subject to the APA's indemnification limits (the Side Letter is an Ancillary Agreement, and its obligations are "covenants" under Side Letter § 8.1, so they fall under APA § 9.3(b)—but the cap in § 9.5 applies to Buyer's § 9.3 obligations, so Laivoul's exposure here is capped at $2,208,000 in the aggregate with all other Buyer covenant breaches).

**Recommended fix:** Add a cure period: if Buyer discovers it failed to make a required offer, it may cure by making the offer within 10 Business Days of discovery, and the § 4.5 reimbursement applies only if the failure is not cured. Alternatively, limit the reimbursement to actual out-of-pocket severance and WARN penalties (not including attorneys' fees or consequential amounts).

## 12. APA § 6.8 Non-Solicitation Runs Only Against Seller, Not Against Seller's Affiliates' Existing Contracts

**Where:** APA § 6.8: "Seller shall not solicit any school district party to a District Contract to award pupil transportation services within New York to Seller or its Affiliates."

**Why it matters:** The restriction is on *solicitation*, not on *performance*. If a school district voluntarily approaches Seller (or an Affiliate) for a new RFP or rebid, Seller is not prohibited from responding. Given that Seller retains its Pennsylvania motorcoach/charter operations (Disclosure Schedule § 5(d)) and its enterprise software licenses, Seller has the infrastructure to bid on New York routes if invited. The 3-year period is reasonable, but the solicitation-only standard is weaker than a full non-compete.

**Recommended fix:** Expand § 6.8 to prohibit Seller and its Affiliates from *providing* pupil transportation services within the Hudson Valley region (or within the service areas of the District Contracts) for 3 years post-Closing, regardless of whether the district solicits. If Seller resists a full non-compete as overbroad, at minimum add: "Seller shall not respond to any RFP, invitation to bid, or request for proposals issued by any school district party to a District Contract for pupil transportation services within New York during the restricted period."
