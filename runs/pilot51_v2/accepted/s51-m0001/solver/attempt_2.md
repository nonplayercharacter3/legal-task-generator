## ISSUES REQUIRING YOUR IMMEDIATE ATTENTION

### 1. Buyer Has Likely Already Lost Indemnification Rights for the Form 483 Matter Under SPA § 5.6(c)

**Documents:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 5.6(c); Side Letter, Recital A

**Issue.** Section 5.6(c) of the SPA provides that if Buyer does not deliver a notice of termination within fifteen (15) Business Days after receipt of a Schedule Supplement, Buyer is "deemed to have irrevocably waived its right to terminate" and the supplement "shall be deemed to have cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty arising from such matter **for all purposes of this Agreement, including for purposes of Section 6.2(a) and Article 9**."

Seller delivered the September Supplement on September 8, 2026 (Side Letter, Recital A). Fifteen Business Days from that date expired on or about September 29, 2026. Buyer's October 20, 2026 response letter disputed the supplement but did not terminate. The deadline has passed.

**Why it matters.** The deeming language operates automatically by its terms. Its practical effect is:

- The inaccuracy in Seller's representation at § 4.12(c) (that the Company has not received any FDA Form 483 since January 1, 2021) is deemed cured.
- Buyer has no indemnification claim under Article 9 for Losses arising from the Form 483 Matter, even if it causes actual post-Closing losses (e.g., FDA enforcement action, recall, loss of clearances).
- The closing condition in § 6.2(a) is deemed satisfied with respect to this matter.

The Side Letter's "Reservation of Positions" clause (§ 4.1–4.2) states that neither party admits anything and both "reserve" their positions. However, a reservation of positions is not a waiver of the contractual deeming mechanism in § 5.6(c). The deeming is operative law between the parties; it does not require an admission to trigger.

**Recommendation.** Before advising the client to sign the Side Letter, we need to assess whether there is any argument that the 15-Business-Day period was tolled, extended, or that the October 20 response letter constituted a sufficient "notice of termination" (it does not on its face—it "disputed" the supplement). If the deeming has in fact operated, Buyer's leverage is significantly reduced, and the Side Letter negotiation should be approached with that reality in mind. We should also consider whether the Side Letter can be used as the vehicle to carve the Form 483 Matter back out of the § 5.6(c) deeming (e.g., by providing that the deeming does not apply to the Form 483 Matter for purposes of Article 9). **This is the single most consequential issue in the file.**

### 2. Side Letter Removes Regulatory Compliance and Capitalization from Fundamental Representations

**Documents:** Side Letter § 3.1; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations"); SPA §§ 6.2(a), 9.1, 9.4(b)

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Fundamental Representations" as §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.12. The Side Letter (a Seller draft) replaces this with only §§ 4.1 and 4.2.

**Why it matters.** Fundamental Representations enjoy three advantages for Buyer:

| Protection | Fundamental Reps | Other Reps |
|---|---|---|
| Closing standard (§ 6.2(a)) | True and correct **in all respects** | True and correct except where failure would not have a MAE |
| Survival (§ 9.1) | 60 days after expiration of applicable statute of limitations | 18 months post-Closing |
| Indemnity limits (§ 9.4(b)) | **Not** subject to Deductible or Cap; cap = full Purchase Price | Subject to $1.86M Deductible and $18.6M Cap |

Removing § 4.12 (Regulatory Compliance) means:
- The FDA compliance representation survives only 18 months (not until the statute of limitations expires plus 60 days—potentially 5–6 years for device-related claims).
- Any regulatory breach is subject to the $1.86M Deductible and $18.6M Cap.
- The closing standard drops from "true and correct in all respects" to the MAE threshold.

Removing § 4.3 (Capitalization) means a capitalization defect (e.g., undisclosed options or liens on shares) would be subject to the Deductible and Cap rather than the full-Purchase-Price ceiling.

**Recommendation.** Do not sign the Side Letter as drafted. If Buyer agrees to remove § 4.12 from Fundamental Representations (which is the Seller's obvious objective given the Form 483 dispute), Buyer should extract concessions: (a) a specific indemnity for the Form 483 Matter that is not subject to the Deductible or Cap and survives until resolution of all FDA-related matters; (b) retention of § 4.3 as a Fundamental Rep; and/or (c) a reduction in the Purchase Price or an increase in the Escrow Amount to compensate for the reduced protection.

### 3. Transition Services Agreement Will Terminate Automatically at Closing Unless Seller Consents

**Documents:** TSA § 9.3; TSA § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"); SPA § 5.7; SPA § 2.3(b)(vii)

**Issue.** TSA § 9.3 provides: "This Agreement shall terminate automatically upon a Change of Control of the Service Recipient, **unless the Change of Control has received the prior written consent of the Service Provider**." The SPA closing is unambiguously a Change of Control under TSA § 1.1(a) (Develstrand, a Person other than the Service Provider or its Affiliates, acquires more than 50% of the Service Recipient's voting securities).

SPA § 5.7 requires Seller to provide ERP hosting, regulatory affairs support, and field service dispatch "under the Transition Services Agreement in accordance with its terms through June 30, 2027." SPA § 2.3(b)(vii) contemplates that the TSA is the one Affiliate Contract that survives closing (all others must be terminated).

**Why it matters.** Without Seller's prior written consent, the TSA terminates the moment the stock changes hands at Closing. Buyer would lose:
- ERP hosting (order management, procurement, inventory, production planning, finance, **device history record** modules)—critical for FDA compliance under 21 C.F.R. § 820;
- Regulatory affairs support (510(k) submissions, MDRs, FDA correspondence); and
- Field service dispatch.

The TSA's migration assistance provision (§ 9.7) provides only 90 days of transition help at cost plus 5%, which is likely insufficient to stand up replacement systems—particularly the device history record module, which has no quick substitute.

**Recommendation.** Obtain Seller's written consent to the Change of Control under TSA § 9.3 **before** Closing. This should be a condition to Closing or a Closing deliverable. The Seller Board Resolutions do not evidence this consent. If Seller refuses consent, Buyer should either (a) negotiate a replacement services agreement with Seller on the same terms, or (b) treat the absence of consent as a failure of the § 6.2(b) condition (Seller's failure to perform covenants in all material respects) or the § 6.2(f) condition (failure to deliver § 2.3(b) items, since § 2.3(b)(vii) implies the TSA will survive).

## OTHER ISSUES

### 4. Key Employee Agreement "Business" Definition Is Materially Narrower Than the SPA's

**Documents:** Key Employee Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Business"); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Business"); SPA § 6.2(e)

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Business" as "the design, manufacture, **sale and servicing** of **MRI receive coils, CT detector modules and ultrasound transducer arrays**." The Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" as "the design and manufacture of **CT detector modules**."

The Key Employee Agreement's non-compete (§ 8.1) and the SPA's closing condition (§ 6.2(e)) both reference "the Business." Because the Key Employee Agreement is a standalone contract with its own definitions, its narrower "Business" term governs the scope of Dr. Okafor-Brandt's covenants.

**Why it matters.** Dr. Okafor-Brandt is VP of Engineering with access to trade secrets, product designs, and regulatory submissions across all three product lines (per the Key Employee Agreement's own Recital C and the "Confidential Information" definition in § 1.1, which covers all of the Company's products). Yet her non-compete bars her only from CT detector module design and manufacture. She could lawfully join a competitor working on MRI receive coils or ultrasound transducer arrays, or work in the sale or servicing of CT modules, without breach.

The SPA's closing condition (§ 6.2(e)) requires the Key Employee Agreement to include "the Key Employee's covenant not to compete with the Business for a period of 24 months." If "the Business" in the closing condition refers to the SPA's definition, the Key Employee Agreement as drafted does not satisfy the condition.

**Recommendation.** Amend the Key Employee Agreement's definition of "Business" to match the SPA's definition (or at minimum to include all three product lines and "sale and servicing"). This should be done before the agreement is executed as a Closing deliverable.

### 5. Seller Board Resolutions Do Not Authorize the Side Letter or TSA Consent

**Documents:** Seller Board Resolutions §§ 2, 3, 7; Side Letter; TSA § 9.3

**Issue.** The resolutions (July 13, 2026) approve the Purchase Agreement "substantially in the form presented to the Board" and the Escrow Agreement. They do not reference:
- The Side Letter (November 5, 2026), which amends the Purchase Agreement;
- The Key Employee Agreement; or
- Any consent to the Change of Control under the TSA.

Section 7 grants general authority to take actions the Authorized Officer determines "necessary or appropriate," but the specific approvals in §§ 2–3 are limited to the forms "presented to the Board."

**Why it matters.** (a) If the Side Letter is challenged, Seller could argue its officers lacked authority to agree to the amendment (though this cuts in Buyer's favor—the original, broader Fundamental Reps definition would apply). (b) More pressingly, no one has authorized the TSA consent required under Issue 3 above. The Authorized Officers' general authority may cover it, but it would be prudent to require a specific board action or officer certificate evidencing TSA consent as a Closing deliverable.

**Recommendation.** Require Seller to deliver, at or before Closing, either (a) supplemental board resolutions or (b) an officer's certificate specifically evidencing (i) authorization of the Side Letter and (ii) consent to the Change of Control under TSA § 9.3.

### 7. Escrow Amount Does Not Fully Backstop the Indemnity Cap

**Documents:** SPA § 1.1 ("Escrow Amount" = $14,000,000; "Indemnity Cap" = $18,600,000); SPA § 9.6; Escrow Agreement § 4.4

**Issue.** The Indemnity Cap is $18.6M (10% of Purchase Price), but the Escrow Amount is only $14M. If Buyer's aggregate indemnification claims (net of the Deductible) exceed $14M, the Escrow Agreement § 4.4 provides that the Escrow Agent pays Buyer the remaining Escrow Funds and has "no further obligation." The shortfall is "recoverable directly from Seller under Section 9.6."

**Why it matters.** Post-Closing, Seller will have received $172M in cash ($186M − $14M escrow). Recovering up to $4.6M from Seller depends on Seller's post-Closing solvency and willingness to pay. There is no security for the excess.

**Recommendation.** Consider negotiating an increase in the Escrow Amount to $18.6M (matching the Cap) or, alternatively, a reduction in the Indemnity Cap to $14M. If neither is achievable, ensure the Escrow Agreement's call-back and security procedures ( §§ 9.1–9.2) are robust to prevent Seller from diverting funds.

### 8. TSA Fee Structure Uses 2023 Base Rates

**Documents:** TSA Schedule B; TSA § 4.4

**Issue.** Schedule B labels the fees as "Monthly Fee (2023 base)." The TSA was signed March 1, 2023. Section 4.4 permits one adjustment per calendar year, capped at 5% increase over the preceding 12 months.

**Why it matters.** By Q4 2026 (expected Closing) and through June 2027, the fees may be $350,500/month ($142,000 + $96,500 + $118,000) at the 2023 base, or up to approximately $454,000/month if Seller has exercised the 5% annual adjustment in 2024, 2025, and 2026. Buyer should confirm what the current fee levels are and whether Seller has issued adjustment notices. Underfunded services risk degraded performance (particularly the device history record module and regulatory filings), while overfunding is a direct cost.

**Recommendation.** Request Seller's current fee schedule and any adjustment notices issued under § 4.4. Confirm the fees in effect at Closing and through June 30, 2027.

### 9. TSA Assignment Clause Is One-Way

**Documents:** TSA § 12.4

**Issue.** The Service Provider (Seller) may assign the TSA "in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of the Service Recipient." The Service Recipient (Kestrova) may not assign without the Service Provider's consent.

**Why it matters.** Post-Closing, Kestrova is owned by Develstrand. If Develstrand wishes to reorganize (e.g., merge Kestrova into another operating subsidiary, or transfer the TSA to a Develstrand affiliate that will assume the services), it cannot do so without Seller's consent. Meanwhile, Seller can assign its obligations to any third party (including a competitor) without Kestrova's consent.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a bilateral assignment right or, at minimum, a requirement that any assignee of the Service Provider's obligations be an entity of comparable financial and operational capability. This could be addressed in the TSA consent letter required under Issue 3.

### 10. Side Letter Effectiveness Creates Timing Ambiguity with Closing Conditions

**Documents:** Side Letter § 5.1; SPA § 6.2(a)

**Issue.** The Side Letter becomes effective "on the Closing." The closing condition in SPA § 6.2(a) requires representations to be true and correct "as of the Closing Date as though made on that date." If the Side Letter is effective only at Closing, there is an argument that the closing condition must be evaluated under the **original** Fundamental Representations definition (including §§ 4.3 and 4.12) because the amendment has not yet taken effect at the moment the condition is assessed.

**Why it matters.** This cuts in Buyer's favor: if the original definition applies at the moment of condition assessment, § 4.12 must be "true and correct in all respects" (subject to the § 5.6(c) deeming, which is the real problem per Issue 1). But it also creates uncertainty that Seller may exploit to argue the condition is satisfied under the narrower definition.

**Recommendation.** Clarify in the Side Letter (or in a side agreement) that the amended definition applies for purposes of evaluating § 6.2(a) at Closing, if that is the commercial deal. Alternatively, if Buyer wants the broader definition to apply at Closing, do not sign the Side Letter and rely on the original terms (subject to the § 5.6(c) issue in Issue 1).
