## ISSUES REQUIRING IMMEDIATE PARTNER ATTENTION

### 1. Section 5.6(c) Deemed Waiver – Form 483 Matter Likely Already Waived

**Documents:** SPA §5.6(c); Side Letter Recitals A–B

**Issue.** SPA §5.6(c) provides that if a Schedule Supplement discloses a matter that "would cause the condition in Section 6.2(a) not to be satisfied," Buyer may terminate within **fifteen (15) Business Days** of receipt. If Buyer does not terminate within that window, two things happen automatically: (i) Buyer is "deemed to have irrevocably waived its right to terminate" with respect to that matter, and (ii) the supplement "shall be deemed to have cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty arising from such matter **for all purposes of this Agreement, including for purposes of Section 6.2(a) and Article 9**."

The Side Letter recitals confirm that Seller delivered the September Supplement on **September 8, 2026**, disclosing an FDA Form 483. Buyer's response letter is dated **October 20, 2026** — approximately 27 Business Days later. Fifteen Business Days from September 8, 2026 (a Tuesday) ends on or about **September 29, 2026**. Buyer did not deliver a termination notice within that window.

Under the SPA as originally signed, §4.12 (Regulatory Compliance) is a **Fundamental Representation** (SPA §1.1), and §6.2(a) requires Fundamental Reps to be true "in all respects" at closing. A Form 483 directly contradicts §4.12(c) ("Since January 1, 2021, the Company has not received any FDA Form 483…"). The September Supplement therefore "would cause the condition in Section 6.2(a) not to be satisfied," triggering the 15-Business-Day clock.

**Why it matters.** If §5.6(c) has been triggered, Develstrand has lost:
- The right to refuse to close based on the Form 483; and
- **All indemnification rights** under Article 9 for the Form 483 matter (the inaccuracy is "deemed cured… for all purposes… including… Article 9").

The Side Letter's "reservation of positions" clause (§§4.1–4.2) does not override the operative waiver language in §5.6(c). A reservation of positions is not a cure of a contractual deemed waiver.

**Complicating factor.** The Side Letter (if executed) amends the definition of "Fundamental Representations" to exclude §4.12. But the Side Letter is not effective until Closing (Side Letter §5.1). At the time the September Supplement was delivered, the original SPA definitions governed, and §4.12 was a Fundamental Rep. The §5.6(c) analysis should be done under the SPA as it existed on September 8.

**Recommendation.** Do **not** execute the Side Letter without first resolving this issue. We need to:
- Confirm the exact date Buyer received the September Supplement (the 15-day clock runs from "receipt," not from Seller's delivery).
- Assess whether the Form 483 was issued before or after the SPA signing date (July 14, 2026). If issued before July 14, Seller's original §4.12(c) representation was false at signing, and §5.6(a) (which covers matters "first arising after the date hereof") may not apply at all — meaning the Schedule Supplement was improper and §5.6(c) was never triggered.
- If the Form 483 was issued after July 14, we are likely locked into the deemed waiver. In that case, negotiate a **specific indemnity** for the Form 483 matter in the Side Letter that carves it out of §5.6(c)'s deemed cure, or obtain a reduction in the Purchase Price.

### 2. Side Letter Amends "Fundamental Representations" to Strip Regulatory Compliance (and Capitalization) of Enhanced Protection

**Documents:** SPA §1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations"); Side Letter §3.1

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Fundamental Representations" as §§4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and **4.12 (Regulatory Compliance)**. Side Letter §3.1 deletes that definition and replaces it with only **§§4.1 and 4.2**. This removes both Capitalization (§4.3) and Regulatory Compliance (§4.12) from the Fundamental tier.

**Why it matters.** The consequences of removing §4.12 from Fundamental Reps are severe for Develstrand:

| Protection | As Original SPA (4.12 = Fundamental) | As Amended by Side Letter (4.12 = Non-Fundamental) |
|---|---|---|
| Closing bring-down (§6.2(a)) | True "in all respects" | True except where failure would not have a MAE |
| Survival (§9.1) | 60 days after applicable statute of limitations | 18 months after Closing |
| Indemnity cap (§9.4(b)) | Purchase Price ($186M) | $18.6M (10% of Purchase Price) |
| Deductible (§9.4(a)) | Does not apply | $1.86M applies |
| Escrow backstop | Full $14M available without deductible | $14M less $1.86M deductible |

A single Form 483, while serious, may not meet the MAE threshold ("materially adverse to the business, results of operations or financial condition"). If it does not, Seller can close even with the Form 483 outstanding, and Develstrand's recovery is limited to $18.6M minus a $1.86M deductible, with only 18 months to bring a claim.

Removing §4.3 (Capitalization) is also adverse: if there is a defect in the Shares (e.g., a lien not disclosed, or shares not "validly issued, fully paid and non-assessable"), Develstrand would lose the enhanced survival and uncapped-by-deductible protection.

**Recommendation.** **Do not agree to Side Letter §3.1 as drafted.** If the Form 483 issue is to be resolved, the proper mechanism is a specific indemnity or price adjustment, not a wholesale downgrade of the Regulatory Compliance representation. At minimum, negotiate to retain §4.12 as a Fundamental Rep. If Seller insists on removing it, require:
- A specific, uncapped indemnity for the Form 483 matter and any related FDA enforcement action, surviving for the full statute of limitations; and
- Retention of §4.3 (Capitalization) as a Fundamental Rep.

### 3. Key Employee Agreement "Business" Definition Is Materially Narrower Than the SPA

**Documents:** SPA §1.1 (definition of "Business"); SPA §6.2(e); KEA §1.1 (definition of "Business"); KEA §8.1

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Business" as "the design, manufacture, sale and servicing of **MRI receive coils, CT detector modules and ultrasound transducer arrays**." The Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" as "the design and manufacture of **CT detector modules**" only.

SPA §6.2(e) makes it a **closing condition** that the Key Employee Agreement be "in the agreed form attached as Exhibit C" and include "the Key Employee's covenant not to compete with the Business for a period of 24 months after the Closing Date."

The KEA's non-compete (§8.1) restricts Dr. Okafor-Brandt only from the "design and manufacture of CT detector modules." She is free to:
- Design, manufacture, **sell or service** CT detector modules (the KEA omits "sale and servicing");
- Engage in **MRI receive coils** or **ultrasound transducer arrays** in any capacity.

**Why it matters.** Dr. Okafor-Brandt is VP of Engineering, responsible for all product lines. If she leaves after closing (or even during the Restricted Period) and joins a competitor working on MRI coils or ultrasound arrays, Develstrand has no contractual restriction. The entire engineering roadmap for two of the three product lines would be at risk. This is a gap between what the SPA promises as a closing condition and what the actual KEA delivers.

**Recommendation.** Before closing, amend the KEA to:
- Replace the "Business" definition with the SPA's full definition (all three product lines, including sale and servicing); or
- Add a separate non-compete covering the full Business as defined in the SPA.

Because §6.2(e) requires the KEA to be "in the agreed form attached as Exhibit C," and the current KEA is labeled "AGREED FORM: EXHIBIT C TO STOCK PURCHASE AGREEMENT," there is an argument that the SPA's closing condition is not satisfied. Develstrand should **not waive this condition** until the definition is corrected.

### 4. Transition Services Agreement Terminates Automatically at Closing (Change of Control)

**Documents:** TSA §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"); TSA §9.3; SPA §5.7; SPA §2.3(b)(vii); SPA §1.1 (definition of "Transition Services Agreement")

**Issue.** TSA §9.3 provides: "This Agreement shall terminate automatically upon a Change of Control of the Service Recipient, **unless the Change of Control has received the prior written consent of the Service Provider**."

TSA §1.1 defines "Change of Control" to include "any Person or group of Persons, other than the Service Provider or its Affiliates, acquiring beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the outstanding voting securities of the Service Recipient." The SPA closing is precisely that event: Develstrand (not Proubeirt or its Affiliates) will acquire 100% of Kestrova.

SPA §5.7 covenants that "From and after the Closing, Seller shall provide… the ERP hosting, regulatory affairs support and field service dispatch services… under the Transition Services Agreement in accordance with its terms through June 30, 2027." SPA §2.3(b)(vii) carves the TSA out of the Affiliate Contract termination requirement, confirming the parties' intent that the TSA survive closing.

**Why it matters.** Without Seller's prior written consent, the TSA terminates the moment closing occurs. Develstrand would lose:
- **ERP hosting** (order management, procurement, inventory, production planning, finance, device history record modules) — $142,000/month;
- **Regulatory affairs support** (510(k) submissions, MDRs, FDA correspondence, Form 483 responses) — $96,500/month;
- **Field service dispatch** (call center, ticketing, technician scheduling) — $118,000/month.

Total: approximately **$356,500/month** in critical services, including the team handling the outstanding Form 483 response.

While SPA §10.9 (Order of Precedence) provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Agreements, and the TSA is defined as an Ancillary Agreement (SPA §1.1), this creates at best a breach-of-contract claim against Seller for failing to perform §5.7 — not a continuing right to the services. Practical continuity is the real concern.

**Recommendation.** Obtain Seller's **prior written consent** under TSA §9.3 before the Closing Date. This should be a condition to closing or a closing deliverable. Alternatively, have Seller execute an amendment to TSA §9.3 waiving the Change of Control termination as to this transaction.

### 5. TSA Assignment Restriction Favors Seller

**Documents:** TSA §12.4

**Issue.** TSA §12.4 permits the Service Provider (Proubeirt) to assign the TSA "in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of the Service Recipient." Conversely, the Service Recipient (Kestrova) "may not assign this Agreement, by operation of law or otherwise, without the prior written consent of the Service Provider."

**Why it matters.** After closing, if Develstrand restructures Kestrova (e.g., merges it into another subsidiary or transfers operations), the TSA cannot be assigned without Proubeirt's consent. Proubeirt, however, can assign its obligations to any third party (e.g., a cheaper or less qualified vendor) without Kestrova's consent.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a mutual assignment right or, at minimum, require that any assignment by Proubeirt be to a successor with equivalent capability. This can be addressed in the consent letter required under Issue 4.

### 6. Ambiguity: Was the Form 483 Issued Before or After SPA Signing?

**Documents:** SPA §4.12(c); SPA §5.6(a); Side Letter Recital A

**Issue.** SPA §4.12(c) represents that "Since January 1, 2021, the Company has not received any FDA Form 483…" This representation is made "as of the date hereof" (July 14, 2026) and as of the Closing Date.

The Side Letter recital states the September Supplement "discloses the inspectional observations issued to the Company on FDA Form 483 following the FDA's inspection of the Company's principal manufacturing facility." It does not state when the inspection occurred or when the Form 483 was issued.

SPA §5.6(a) permits Schedule Supplements only for matters "first arising after the date hereof, or of which Seller first obtains knowledge after the date hereof."

**Why it matters.**
- If the Form 483 was **issued before July 14, 2026**: Seller's original §4.12(c) representation was false at signing. The Schedule Supplement mechanism (§5.6) was not designed for this — it covers new matters, not pre-existing misrepresentations. The §5.6(c) deemed waiver may not apply because the inaccuracy did not "first arise after the date hereof." Develstrand would have a direct claim for breach of a Fundamental Rep, subject to the Purchase Price cap, with long survival.
- If the Form 483 was **issued after July 14, 2026**: The Schedule Supplement is proper under §5.6(a), and the §5.6(c) 15-Business-Day analysis in Issue 1 applies.

**Recommendation.** Determine the issuance date of the Form 483 immediately. This is dispositive of whether §5.6(c) was properly triggered. Request the Form 483 and all related FDA correspondence from Seller under SPA §5.2 (Access to Information) if not already in hand.

### 7. SPA §5.9 Notification Covenant – Potential Pre-Closing Breach

**Documents:** SPA §5.9

**Issue.** SPA §5.9 requires Seller to "promptly notify Buyer in writing of any FDA inspection of any facility of the Company and of any correspondence received from the FDA" from the date of the SPA until Closing.

If the FDA inspection occurred after July 14, 2026, and Seller did not notify Buyer promptly (instead waiting until September 8 to deliver a Schedule Supplement), Seller may have breached §5.9. A breach of a covenant is independently indemnifiable under §9.2(b) and is not subject to the §5.6(c) deemed cure (which applies only to "inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty").

**Why it matters.** Even if the §4.12(c) representation breach is deemed cured under §5.6(c), a separate claim for breach of the §5.9 notification covenant may survive. The delay (inspection to September 8 disclosure) could constitute a covenant breach giving rise to indemnification outside the deemed waiver.

**Recommendation.** Preserve this claim. Document the timeline of the FDA inspection, the Form 483 issuance, and Seller's notification. The §9.2(b) covenant indemnity is not subject to the §5.6(c) cure provision.

### 8. Escrow Tax Treatment – Buyer Bears Income Tax on Escrow Earnings

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement §8.1

**Issue.** Escrow Agreement §8.1 provides that "Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds until they are disbursed. Buyer shall report all income earned on the Escrow Funds, whether or not distributed." Each disbursement to Seller is "treated as a payment of Purchase Price, with imputed interest as required by Section 483 or Section 1274."

**Why it matters.** Develstrand will owe income tax on interest earned by the $14M escrow during the 18-month hold period, even though the funds are not available for its use. Additionally, the imputed interest rules under §483/§1274 could create a taxable event for Develstrand at closing (deemed receipt of interest on the "deferred" purchase price held in escrow). This is a cash tax cost that should be factored into the deal economics.

**Recommendation.** Confirm with tax counsel the specific §483/§1274 implications given the 18-month escrow period and the $14M amount. Model the tax cost. This is not a drafting fix but a deal-economics item.

### 12. SPA §5.6(c) "Deemed Cure" Language Is Unusually Broad

**Documents:** SPA §5.6(c)

**Issue.** The provision deems a Schedule Supplement to have "cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty arising from such matter **for all purposes of this Agreement, including for purposes of Section 6.2(a) and Article 9**." This is broader than market. Typical SPA provisions either (a) allow the supplement to update the Disclosure Schedule (so the rep is read as qualified by the supplement) or (b) give the buyer a termination right. They do not typically provide that the supplement **cures the breach for indemnification purposes**.

**Why it matters.** Even in situations where the 15-Business-Day window has not yet run (e.g., a future Schedule Supplement), this language means Buyer's indemnification rights are extinguished for the disclosed matter if Buyer does not terminate. This creates a powerful incentive for Seller to disclose problems via Schedule Supplement and wait out the 15-day window, knowing Buyer will lose its indemnity.

**Recommendation.** If the SPA is still being negotiated (i.e., if we have leverage before closing), seek to amend §5.6(c) to limit the deemed cure to the closing condition (§6.2(a)) and preserve Article 9 indemnification rights. If the SPA is already signed and this issue is academic for the Form 483 (see Issue 1), flag it for any future Schedule Supplements that may arrive before closing.
