### 1. Buyer May Have Deemed-Waived Termination Rights and Indemnification for the Form 483 Matter Under SPA §5.6(c)

**Documents:** SPA §5.6(c); Side Letter Recitals A–B

**Facts.** Seller delivered a Schedule Supplement on September 8, 2026 disclosing FDA Form 483 inspectional observations (the "Form 483 Matter"). SPA §5.6(c) provided Buyer 15 Business Days from receipt to deliver a "written notice of termination." Fifteen Business Days from Tuesday, September 8, 2026 expired on or about Tuesday, September 29, 2026. Buyer's response letter is dated October 20, 2026—three weeks late—and it disputes the Supplement rather than terminating the Agreement.

**Why it matters.** Section 5.6(c) provides that if Buyer does not terminate within the 15-Business-Day window, two things happen automatically: (i) Buyer "shall be deemed to have irrevocably waived its right to terminate this Agreement with respect to such matter," and (ii) the Schedule Supplement "shall be deemed to have cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty arising from such matter for all purposes of this Agreement, including for purposes of Section 6.2(a) and Article 9." The Form 483 Matter directly implicates SPA §4.12(c), which represents that since January 1, 2021 the Company "has not received any FDA Form 483." If that representation was inaccurate at signing and the Supplement disclosed the inaccuracy, §5.6(c) operates to cure the breach for indemnification purposes. Buyer would then have no post-closing recourse under Article 9 for the Form 483 Matter, and could not use it to block closing under §6.2(a).

The Side Letter's "Reservation of Positions" clause (§4.1–4.2) does not override §5.6(c). A contractual reservation of rights cannot negate a deemed waiver that the SPA itself creates by operation of time.

**Potential counterarguments to explore.**
- Whether the Form 483 was issued *before* the SPA signing date (July 14, 2026) and whether Seller had actual knowledge before signing. If so, the matter may not qualify as "first arising after the date hereof, or of which Seller first obtains knowledge after the date hereof" (SPA §5.6(a)), and §5.6(c) may not apply at all. The Supplement would then be an improper disclosure of a pre-existing matter, and the original representation remains breached.
- Whether the Form 483 constitutes Fraud (SPA §1.1 defines Fraud as "actual and intentional fraud … with respect to the making of any representation or warranty in Article 4"). Fraud is carved out of the exclusive remedy in §9.7 and is not subject to the §5.6(c) cure (which operates on "representations and warranties," not on Fraud).
- Whether the September Supplement was adequate in detail under §5.6(b) ("describe the relevant matter in reasonable detail"). An inadequate supplement might arguably restart the clock or prevent the deemed cure from attaching.

**Recommendation.** Before Buyer executes the Side Letter or takes any further action, we need to (a) confirm the date the FDA issued the Form 483 and the date Seller first received or became aware of it; (b) review the full text of the September Supplement for adequacy; and (c) assess whether Seller's failure to include the Form 483 in the original Disclosure Schedule (if it was known pre-signing) rises to Fraud. If the Form 483 was issued post-signing and Seller had no prior knowledge, §5.6(c) likely applies and Buyer's position is significantly weakened—this changes our negotiating posture on the Side Letter.

### 2. Side Letter Would Strip Regulatory Compliance (§4.12) from Fundamental Representations

**Document:** Side Letter §3.1

**Facts.** The Side Letter (marked "Seller Draft: For Execution at Closing") amends the SPA's definition of "Fundamental Representations" from Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.12 to only Sections 4.1 and 4.2. This removes both Capitalization (§4.3) and Regulatory Compliance (§4.12).

**Why it matters.** Fundamental Representations receive three enhanced protections under the SPA:
- **Bring-down standard:** §6.2(a) requires Fundamental Reps to be true and correct "in all respects" at closing, with no Material Adverse Effect qualifier. Non-Fundamental Reps need only be true and correct except where failure "would have a Material Adverse Effect."
- **Survival:** §9.1 gives Fundamental Reps survival until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations expires. Non-Fundamental Reps survive only 18 months.
- **Indemnity limits:** §9.4(b) excludes Fundamental Rep breaches from both the $1,860,000 Deductible and the $18,600,000 Indemnity Cap.

Removing §4.12 means that any future regulatory compliance failure (additional Form 483s, warning letters, recalls, QSR violations) would be subject to the deductible, the cap, the 18-month survival window, and the MAE-qualified bring-down. For a medical device company whose value is heavily dependent on regulatory standing, this is a material reduction in Buyer's protection.

The amendment is also directly responsive to the Form 483 dispute: by removing §4.12 from Fundamental Reps, Seller reduces its exposure for the very matter at issue.

**Recommendation.** Do not execute the Side Letter as drafted. If a side letter is needed to address closing logistics (payment instructions, resignation timing, escrow mechanics), those provisions (§2.1–2.4) are acceptable. But §3 (the Fundamental Reps amendment) should be deleted. If Seller insists on modifying the Fundamental Reps definition as a condition of closing, Buyer should at minimum retain §4.12 as a Fundamental Rep or negotiate a specific indemnity carve-out for the Form 483 Matter that survives the §5.6(c) deemed cure (see Issue 1).

### 3. Transition Services Agreement Terminates Automatically at Closing

**Documents:** TSA §9.3; TSA §1.1 (Change of Control definition); SPA §5.7; SPA §2.3(b)(vii)

**Facts.** TSA §9.3 provides: "This Agreement shall terminate automatically upon a Change of Control of the Service Recipient, unless the Change of Control has received the prior written consent of the Service Provider." The closing of the SPA is unambiguously a Change of Control under TSA §1.1(a) (Develstrand acquires >50% of the Company's voting securities). The Service Provider is Seller.

Meanwhile, SPA §5.7 covenants that Seller "shall provide … the ERP hosting, regulatory affairs support and field service dispatch services … under the Transition Services Agreement in accordance with its terms through June 30, 2027." SPA §2.3(b)(vii) contemplates the TSA surviving closing (it is the one Affiliate Contract expressly carved out from the termination requirement).

**Why it matters.** At the moment of closing, the TSA will terminate automatically unless Seller affirmatively consents. This creates an immediate gap in:
- **ERP hosting** (order management, procurement, inventory, production planning, finance, and the device history record module);
- **Regulatory affairs support** (510(k) submissions, establishment registrations, MDRs, responses to FDA correspondence); and
- **Field service dispatch** (customer service call center and technician scheduling).

For a medical device company, loss of regulatory affairs support is particularly acute: the Company must continue to file MDRs, respond to FDA inspections, and maintain its QSR system. A gap in these functions could itself create regulatory non-compliance.

SPA §5.7 becomes impossible to perform if the TSA has terminated. The Company would have no contractual basis to require Seller to continue providing services.

**Recommendation.** Before closing, obtain Seller's written consent under TSA §9.3 to the Change of Control, or amend the TSA to carve out this specific transaction from the automatic termination trigger. This should be added as a closing deliverable (a new item under SPA §2.3(b)) or a condition to Buyer's closing obligations under Article 6. Without this, Buyer should not close.

### 4. Key Employee Agreement "Business" Definition Is Narrower Than the SPA's, Undermining the Non-Compete and the Closing Condition

**Documents:** KEA §1.1 ("Business" = "the design and manufacture of CT detector modules"); SPA §1.1 ("Business" = "the design, manufacture, sale and servicing of MRI receive coils, CT detector modules and ultrasound transducer arrays"); SPA §6.2(e); KEA §8.1

**Facts.** The KEA defines "Business" as only CT detector modules. The non-compete in KEA §8.1 restricts the Executive from engaging in "the Business" for 24 months post-closing. SPA §6.2(e) makes it a condition to Buyer's closing obligation that the KEA include "the Key Employee's covenant not to compete with the Business for a period of 24 months after the Closing Date," using the SPA's defined term "Business" (all three product lines).

**Why it matters.** As drafted, the KEA non-compete covers only one of the Company's three product lines. Dr. Okafor-Brandt, as VP of Engineering with access to product designs, firmware, and regulatory submissions across all product lines (KEA §3.2, Recital C), could compete in MRI receive coils and ultrasound transducer arrays the day after her restricted period for CT modules expires—or immediately, since those product lines are not restricted at all. This defeats the purpose of the Key Employee condition and the retention investment ($600K retention bonus + $900K RSUs).

Additionally, there is a technical mismatch: SPA §6.2(e) references "the Business" (SPA definition), but the KEA uses its own narrower definition. A strict reading could mean the KEA as drafted does not satisfy the closing condition, giving Buyer a technical basis to refuse closing—or, conversely, giving Seller an argument that the condition is met because the KEA contains *a* 24-month non-compete.

**Recommendation.** Amend the KEA's definition of "Business" to match the SPA definition (all three product lines) before the KEA is executed as a closing deliverable. Alternatively, expand KEA §8.1 to expressly cover all product lines of the Company. This is a condition to Buyer's protection and should not be conceded.

### 5. SPA §5.7 Is Internally Inconsistent With the TSA's Assignment Restrictions

**Documents:** SPA §5.7; TSA §12.4

**Facts.** TSA §12.4 permits the Service Provider (Seller) to assign the TSA to any Person without the Service Recipient's consent, but prohibits the Service Recipient (the Company) from assigning without the Service Provider's consent.

**Why it matters.** Post-closing, the Company is Buyer's subsidiary. If Buyer wishes to migrate the Company's operations to a different service provider or integrate the ERP into Buyer's own systems before June 30, 2027, the Company cannot assign or novate the TSA without Seller's consent. Meanwhile, Seller could assign its obligations to a third party (e.g., a lower-cost affiliate) without the Company's approval, potentially degrading service quality. The TSA §9.2 right to terminate individual services on 90 days' notice provides some flexibility, but the 90-day notice period is operationally significant for ERP and regulatory functions.

**Recommendation.** In negotiating the TSA consent (see Issue 3), also seek to (a) remove or narrow the Service Provider's unilateral assignment right, and (b) confirm that the Company's right to terminate individual services under §9.2 is not subject to any additional consent. At minimum, ensure the 90-day migration assistance under TSA §9.7 is robust.

### 8. Escrow Tax Treatment Imposes Income Tax on Buyer

**Document:** Escrow Agreement §8.1

**Facts.** Buyer is treated as the tax owner of the $14 million Escrow Funds. All interest and income earned on the escrow is taxable to Buyer whether or not distributed. Each disbursement to Seller is treated as a payment of Purchase Price with imputed interest under IRC §483 or §1274.

**Why it matters.** Buyer will recognize taxable interest income on the escrow account during the 18-month hold period. While the escrow is invested in an FDIC-insured deposit or money market fund (Escrow Agreement §3.1), even modest yields on $14 million represent a real tax cost. The §483/§1274 imputed interest treatment on disbursements to Seller could create additional tax complexity.

**Recommendation.** Flag to client's tax team to model the escrow interest income and the §483/§1274 implications. Confirm the investment direction in Schedule 2 is optimized for after-tax return.

### 9. Indemnity Structure Is a True Deductible, Not a Tipping Threshold

**Document:** SPA §9.4(a)

**Facts.** The $1,860,000 Deductible operates as a true deductible: "Seller shall be liable only for Losses in excess of the Deductible." If aggregate losses are $2 million, Buyer recovers only $140,000.

**Why it matters.** This is less favorable to Buyer than a tipping threshold (where once the threshold is crossed, Seller is liable from the first dollar). Combined with the $18.6 million cap, Buyer's maximum recovery for non-Fundamental Rep breaches is $18.6 million, and it bears the first $1.86 million of all losses. For a $186 million acquisition, the deductible represents 1% of the purchase price—within market range but worth noting.

**Recommendation.** This is an agreed term in the executed SPA. Not worth reopening unless the Side Letter negotiation provides leverage. Ensure the client understands the practical effect: small-to-moderate regulatory or operational issues post-closing will be absorbed by Buyer up to $1.86 million in the aggregate.

### 11. SPA §5.9 FDA Notification Covenant May Be Inadequate Given the Form 483 History

**Document:** SPA §5.9

**Facts.** SPA §5.9 requires Seller to "promptly notify Buyer in writing of any FDA inspection of any facility of the Company and of any correspondence received from the FDA" from signing until closing.

**Why it matters.** Given that a Form 483 was already issued (and disclosed only via Schedule Supplement in September), there is a risk of additional FDA follow-up (e.g., a re-inspection, a warning letter escalation, or a request for a corrective action plan). The "promptly" standard is subjective. If Seller delays notification of further FDA correspondence until after the §5.6(c) window has run on a new Supplement, Buyer could face the same deemed-waiver trap.

**Recommendation.** Monitor for any further FDA correspondence. If Seller delivers another Schedule Supplement before closing, calendar the 15-Business-Day termination window immediately upon receipt and escalate to you the same day. Consider whether the Side Letter should include a specific covenant that Seller will not deliver additional Schedule Supplements without prior discussion.
