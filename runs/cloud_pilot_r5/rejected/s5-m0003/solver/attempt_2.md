---

## 1. Bottom Line

1. **Crestline's consent is required, not merely a notice.** The Stock Purchase with Varga Diagnostics Holdings Corp. ("Varga") is a Change of Control under the Distribution Agreement. Section 15.4 prohibits that Change of Control without Crestline's prior written consent. The Seller Disclosure Schedule classifies Crestline as "Notice only; no consent required," which conflicts with the Agreement and with the Schedule's own definitions.
2. **The 60-day consent request appears not to have been made.** Section 15.4 requires a written request at least 60 days before the anticipated closing. Nothing in the file shows that a request was sent.
3. **Closing without consent triggers a termination right and a fee.** On the Schedule A minimums, the fee is roughly **$3.24 million** at 2026 prices and roughly **$3.36 million** at the 2027 adjusted prices. Actual invoiced volumes will control the final number.
4. **There is a separate confidentiality exposure.** The Agreement's terms and pricing are Confidential Information under Section 9. The Data Room disclosure to Varga does not appear to fit any permitted recipient category, and the First Amendment removed the liability cap for Section 9 breaches.
5. **The First Amendment changed the liability framework** in ways the Disclosure Schedule does not mention.

Several items cannot be resolved from the four documents, including the SPA itself. Those are flagged as open points below.

## 3. Issues

### Issue 1: Crestline's consent is required, but the Schedule says notice only (Critical)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §1.1 ("Change of Control," clause (a)): an acquisition of more than 50% of Supplier's voting stock "by stock purchase, merger, consolidation or otherwise."
- Agreement §14.1: a Change of Control of Supplier "is governed by Section 15.4."
- Agreement §15.4: "Supplier shall not undergo a Change of Control without Distributor's prior written consent."
- Schedule §3.4: "Relevant provision: Supplier change in ownership — notice only; no consent required." Category: "Notice only." Status: notice after Closing.
- Schedule §2.2(a): "Consent required" includes any case where the counterparty's consent is needed to consummate the Transactions "without breach, default, termination right or acceleration."
- Schedule §4.2: Crestline notice to be delivered post-Closing under SPA §5.6.
- Schedule §2.1: restates the SPA §3.5 representation that no consent of any Material Contract counterparty is required except as set forth in Part 3.5.

**Why it matters:** Under Section 15.4, closing without consent is a breach. It also gives Crestline a termination right and a fee (Issue 3). The Schedule's own definitions in §2.2(a) put this in the "Consent required" category, so the "Notice only" label is internally inconsistent. The Schedule also cites no section of the Agreement for this item. As drafted, the Section 3.5 representation would be inaccurate. That exposes the Sellers and the Company to an indemnity claim and to a closing-condition dispute under SPA §6.2(d). Because Margit E. Halvorsen signed the Agreement, signed the Notice, and is the Sellers' Representative, the Buyer may argue the inaccuracy was known, which would bear on any knowledge-qualified or fraud-related provisions.

**Recommended fix:**
- Reclassify the Crestline item in Schedule §3.4 as "Consent required," cite Agreement §§1.1, 14.1 and 15.4, and describe the fee and termination right.
- Add the Crestline consent to Schedule §4.1 as a closing deliverable under SPA §6.2(d). Remove the Crestline item from Schedule §4.2.
- Alternatively, if the Company cannot obtain consent before closing, negotiate a specific indemnity or a price mechanism with Varga, and disclose the risk fully. Do not leave the item as notice-only.

**Open point:** Whether the SPA is already signed. The Schedule is dated "as of September 8, 2026," but the instruction refers to signing as still pending. If the SPA is signed, the fix is a schedule update or amendment, with a waiver from Varga.

### Issue 2: The 60-day consent request appears not to have been made (Critical)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §15.4, second sentence: the Supplier "shall request that consent in writing not less than sixty (60) days before the anticipated closing of the Change of Control, identifying the acquiring person."
- Schedule §3.1 shows that a landlord consent request was sent September 15, 2026. No comparable Crestline request is recorded anywhere in Part 3.5.

**Why it matters:** A late or missing request is a separate breach of the first sentence of Section 15.4, apart from the closing itself. Crestline could assert it under Section 15.1 after a 30-day cure period. Because the request must be made at least 60 days before closing, the earliest permitted closing depends on when the request is sent:

| Date Request Sent | Earliest Compliant Closing |
|---|---|
| October 18, 2026 (today) | December 17, 2026 |
| Later date | Request date plus 60 days |

**Recommended fix:**
- Send a written consent request to Crestline now, identifying Varga Diagnostics Holdings Corp. as the acquiring person, as Section 15.4 requires.
- Deliver it under Agreement §17.3 (hand, nationally recognized overnight courier, or certified mail), addressed to the President, with a copy to the Director of Contracts per Amendment §4.1. Note that the copy does not constitute notice.
- Before sending, confirm that Varga consents to disclosure of its identity and the transaction to Crestline. See Issue 5.
- Calendar the 60-day date against the target closing date.

**Open points:**
- The anticipated closing date (not stated in the Schedule or the SPA extract).
- Whether any request or informal communication has already been sent to Crestline.

### Issue 3: Termination right and fee if closing occurs without consent (High)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §15.4, final sentence: Crestline may terminate by written notice given within 180 days after it "learns of the closing." The Supplier must then pay, within 30 days after termination, a fee equal to 12 times the average monthly amount invoiced to Crestline for Products in the 12 full calendar months preceding closing.
- Agreement §15.6: §15.4 (as to the fee) survives termination.
- Agreement §3.2 (Minimum Purchase Commitment) and Schedule A (minimum annual units). Schedule B (prices).

**Why it matters:** The fee is formula-based and tied to actual invoices, not the minimums. The 180-day window runs from when Crestline learns of the closing, so the post-closing notice in Schedule §4.2 would likely start the clock. The Agreement also says nothing about whether the fee is an exclusive remedy, so Crestline may argue for damages in addition to the fee.

**Illustrative exposure (minimum volumes only, excluding HS-SPK "as ordered" spare parts and any volume above the minimums):**

| Product | Min. Units | 2026 Price | Annual at 2026 Price | 2027 Adjusted Price | Annual at 2027 Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HB-2200 | 24 | $38,500.00 | $924,000 | $39,847.50 | $956,340 |
| HB-3400 | 18 | $46,200.00 | $831,600 | $47,586.00 | $856,548 |
| HR-2200-RK | 1,200 | $612.00 | $734,400 | $636.48 | $763,776 |
| HR-3400-RK | 900 | $734.00 | $660,600 | $763.36 | $687,024 |
| HC-QC10 | 600 | $148.00 | $88,800 | $155.40 | $93,240 |
| **Total** | | | **$3,239,400** | | **$3,356,928** |

Because the fee is 12 times the average monthly invoices, the illustrative fee approximates the 12-month invoiced total. Actual exposure will be higher if Crestline's purchases exceed the minimums, and lower if they fall short.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Crestline's invoice history for the 12 full months before the anticipated closing, and model the fee on that basis.
- Negotiate with Crestline a written consent that expressly waives the §15.4 termination right and fee for this transaction. Pair it with an estoppel confirming no defaults, no pending claims, and current purchase levels.
- Ask Varga whether it will bear, share, or be indemnified for the fee. Address this in the SPA.
- Research whether the fee is enforceable under Delaware law or is an unenforceable penalty. This is a legal question the documents do not answer.

**Open points:** actual invoice history; whether Crestline would treat the fee as exclusive of damages; and enforceability.

### Issue 4: No consent standard, and possible leverage from the price increase (High)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §15.4: the clause does not say consent may not be unreasonably withheld, delayed, or conditioned.
- Notice §§2.1–3.2: a 3.0% to 5.0% price increase effective March 3, 2027, sent October 5, 2026, while the transaction is pending.

**Why it matters:** Absent a reasonableness standard, Crestline may withhold consent for any reason, including to extract concessions. Crestline can also see the price increase and the consent request together. Whether any implied standard applies under Delaware law is an open research point.

**Recommended fix:**
- Plan the consent approach with the commercial team before the request goes out.
- Avoid any statement or term linking the price increase to the consent. If Crestline raises the link, respond through counsel.
- Research the implied covenant and consent standard under Delaware law.

**Open point:** Whether Crestline has already raised the increase or the transaction in any discussion.

### Issue 5: Confidentiality. Disclosure of the Agreement to Varga and the consent request (High)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §9.1: "Confidential Information" expressly includes "pricing" and "the terms of this Agreement."
- Agreement §9.2: disclosure is permitted only to "its and its Affiliates' employees, advisors and contractors who need to know" and who are bound by confidentiality obligations.
- Agreement §9.3: the exceptions do not include disclosure to a prospective acquirer. The only other exception is disclosure required by law.
- Agreement §1.1 ("Affiliate"): Varga is not an Affiliate of Halvorsen before closing.
- Amendment §3.1 (restated §12.3): the liability cap and consequential-damages exclusion "shall not apply to a party's breach of Section 9." Section 9 breaches are therefore uncapped.
- Schedule §1.5: complete copies of the Crestline agreements, including amendments, were made available to Buyer in the Data Room (Data Room 3.1.1–3.1.2).

**Why it matters:** Making the full Agreement, including Schedule B pricing and the Notice, available to Varga in the Data Room appears not to fall within any permitted disclosure category under §9.2. A Section 9 breach is uncapped under the amended §12.3, and Crestline could claim lost profits and consequential damages. The consent request under §15.4 also requires identifying Varga, which discloses the transaction to Crestline. Varga's NDA and the SPA's confidentiality provisions must allow that disclosure.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm whether Crestline consented to disclosure of the Agreement to Varga, or whether any other basis exists. If not, consider a written waiver or consent from Crestline.
- Confirm that Varga's NDA covers the Crestline materials and restricts their use.
- Going forward, redact Schedule B pricing and the Notice from Data Room copies, or restrict access to clean-team advisors only.
- Confirm that the consent request is sent within the confidentiality terms agreed with Varga.

**Open points:** whether Varga is bound by an NDA that covers these materials; whether Crestline has given any consent to disclosure.

### Issue 6: The First Amendment changed the liability framework (High)

**Where it appears:**
- Amendment §3.1 restates Agreement §12.3 as follows: "The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 shall not apply to a party's breach of Section 9."
- Original Agreement §12.3 excluded from the caps: breaches of Sections 9 and 10, and indemnification obligations under Section 11.
- Agreement §12.1 excludes indirect, consequential, and lost-profit damages. Agreement §12.2 caps each party's aggregate liability at amounts paid or payable to Supplier in the preceding 12 months.
- Schedule §3.4 describes the Amendment only as extending the Initial Term to March 2, 2029. It does not mention the §12.3 change.

**Why it matters:** The restatement removed the carve-outs for Section 10 (trademarks and IP) and Section 11 (indemnities). As a result:
- Halvorsen's indemnity obligations to Crestline under §11.1 (product defects and IP infringement) are now capped and subject to the consequential-damages exclusion. This favors Halvorsen.
- Crestline's indemnity obligations to Halvorsen under §11.2 are also capped, and Halvorsen's recovery for Crestline's trademark misuse under §10 is now capped and limited to direct damages. This disfavors Halvorsen.
- Varga may treat the Marks and IP as valuable, and will want to know the recovery position.

The Schedule's incomplete description is a disclosure gap. Under Schedule §1.5, the amendment is in the Data Room, but the summary omits a substantive change.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether the §12.3 restatement was intended, and whether Crestline negotiated it.
- Revise the Schedule §3.4 description to disclose the §12.3 change, and cross-reference it in the Material Contracts representation.

**Open point:** The Amendment has no recital or explanation for the change. Intent cannot be determined from the face of the documents.

### Issue 7: Other Agreement terms that constrain post-closing integration and are not in the Schedule (Medium)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §2.2: Supplier may not appoint another distributor, agent, or reseller for Products in the Territory (Ohio, Indiana, Michigan) or sell Products directly to Customers there, except for Schedule D national accounts and sales Crestline declines in writing.
- Agreement §14.1: Supplier may not assign the Agreement, "whether voluntarily or by operation of law," without Crestline's consent. Unlike §14.2 (Distributor), there is no Affiliate exception for Supplier.
- Agreement §14.2: Crestline may assign to an Affiliate or a Qualified Transferee on notice, without Halvorsen's consent.
- Agreement §16.1 and Amendment §2.1: exclusivity runs to March 2, 2029, with automatic one-year renewals under §16.2.
- Agreement Schedule D: Meridian Health Purchasing Alliance (MHPA-2021-0447) and Lakeshore Reference Laboratories are excluded national accounts.

**Why it matters:** If Varga plans to sell Halvorsen Products in the Territory through its own channels or affiliates, or to reorganize Halvorsen and move the Agreement to an affiliate, those steps would need Crestline's consent or would breach the Agreement. Crestline's free assignment right under §14.2 could also introduce a competitor of Varga into the Territory. The Schedule's §3.4 summary does not mention these terms.

**Recommended fix:**
- Brief Varga on the §2.2 and §14.1 constraints before signing, and reflect the agreed post-closing plan in the SPA.
- Add the Schedule D carve-outs and the §2.2 and §14.1 restrictions to the Schedule §3.4 summary.
- Consider whether the consent under Issue 1 should also address §14.1 for any planned post-closing reorganization.

**Open point:** Varga's post-closing commercial and legal structure, which the documents do not describe.

### Issue 8: The 2026 price increase (Notice) appears compliant, but raises timing and factual questions (Medium)

**Where it appears:**
- Notice §2.1: adjusted prices effective March 3, 2027, the first day of the next Contract Year.
- Notice §2.2: delivered more than 60 days before the adjustment date. Under Schedule B ¶2, 60 days before March 3, 2027 is January 2, 2027. The October 5, 2026 delivery is compliant.
- Notice §3.2–3.3: each increase is at or below the 5% cap in Schedule B ¶2. I recomputed each adjusted price, and they are arithmetically correct: HB-2200 +3.5%, HB-3400 +3.0%, HR-2200-RK +4.0%, HR-3400-RK +4.0%, HC-QC10 +5.0%.
- Notice §3.1: Halvorsen "has made no adjustment … since the Effective Date."
- Notice §3.3 and Schedule B ¶2: the cap applies per Contract Year, so there is no cumulative issue.
- Notice §1.1 and §5.2: the Notice is correctly addressed and delivered by overnight courier.
- Agreement §9.1: pricing is Confidential Information (see Issue 5).

**Why it matters:**
- **Interim covenants.** If the SPA restricts price changes or Material Contract actions between signing and closing, this Notice may require Varga's consent. The SPA was not provided.
- **Fee base.** If closing occurs after March 3, 2027, invoices at the higher prices will increase the Issue 3 fee.
- **Factual accuracy.** The statement in §3.1 that no prior adjustment was made must be verified against the price file, because an inaccuracy would be a Halvorsen representation to Crestline.
- **Leverage.** See Issue 4.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm the SPA's interim operating covenants and, if needed, obtain Varga's written consent to the Notice.
- Verify the §3.1 statement against Halvorsen's historical price file.
- Do not withdraw or modify the Notice without analysis. Any change should be documented and sent in compliance with §17.3.

**Open points:** SPA interim covenants; the accuracy of §3.1; whether Varga knew of the Notice before it was sent.

### Issue 9: Closing deliverables and post-closing notice list are inconsistent with the Agreement (Medium)

**Where it appears:**
- Schedule §4.1: the only third-party consent that is a closing deliverable under SPA §6.2(d) is Brandywine Commerce Partners (lease).
- Schedule §4.2: Crestline notice is to be delivered post-closing under SPA §5.6.

**Why it matters:** Because the Crestline consent is missing from §4.1, Varga may have no contractual right to require it as a closing deliverable. The Company then faces either a closing without consent (Issue 3) or a dispute about the closing condition. Listing Crestline as a post-closing notice also reinforces the mischaracterization in Issue 1.

**Recommended fix:** Add the Crestline consent to Schedule §4.1, remove the Crestline item from §4.2, and align the SPA's closing conditions and §5.6 with the corrected classification.

### Issue 10: Confirm the Change of Control trigger and transaction structure (Medium)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §1.1 ("Change of Control"): more than 50% of outstanding voting stock acquired by any person or group "acting together," including through "a series of related transactions," and by "stock purchase, merger, consolidation or otherwise."
- Schedule §3.4 and the SPA recitals refer to "the sale of the Shares." The percentage and any rollover or staged purchase are not stated in the Schedule.

**Why it matters:** The trigger is a bright-line 50% test, and it covers beneficial ownership and related transactions. A structure that involves a merger, a staged purchase, or a rollover could be captured in a way the Schedule does not reflect. A merger would also raise the §14.1 "operation of law" issue.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm the percentage of voting stock to be acquired, whether any step, option, or rollover is part of the same series, and whether the structure will change. Treat the transaction as a Change of Control under §15.4 in all cases.

**Open point:** The SPA share count and structure.
