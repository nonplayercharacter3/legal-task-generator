## 2. Bottom Line

1. **Crestline's consent is required, and the Disclosure Schedule says it is not.** The Varga stock purchase is a Change of Control under the Agreement. Section 15.4 prohibits it without Crestline's prior written consent. The Disclosure Schedule classifies the Crestline item as "Notice only; no consent required." That classification is wrong on the face of the Agreement and should be corrected before signing.
2. **The consent process has a 60-day lead time that may already be unachievable for an early closing.** The folder shows no request sent to Crestline.
3. **Closing without consent exposes Halvorsen to termination of its exclusive U.S. Midwest distribution arrangement and to a termination fee.** On the Agreement's minimum purchase figures, the fee is likely in the range of roughly $3.2 million or more. The actual figure depends on invoicing data that is not in the folder.
4. **Two confidentiality and disclosure points create additional exposure.** Placing the Agreement and its pricing in the Data Room may breach Section 9. Breaches of Section 9 are uncapped under the First Amendment.
5. **The First Amendment changed the liability carve-outs,** and the Disclosure Schedule's summary of the amendment omits that change.

## 3. Issues

### Issue 1 (Critical): Crestline consent is required, but the Disclosure Schedule classifies the item as notice only

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §1.1, definition of "Change of Control," clause (a): acquisition of beneficial ownership of more than 50% of Supplier's voting stock "by stock purchase, merger, consolidation or otherwise."
- Agreement §14.1, last sentence: "A Change of Control of Supplier is governed by Section 15.4."
- Agreement §15.4, first sentence: "Supplier shall not undergo a Change of Control without Distributor's prior written consent."
- Disclosure Schedule, item 3.4: "Relevant provision: Supplier change in ownership — notice only; no consent required. Category: Notice only."
- Disclosure Schedule §2.2(a) and (b): "Consent required" means consent is needed to consummate without "breach, default, termination right or acceleration." "Notice only" means no consent is required.
- Disclosure Schedule §4.1: the only consent that is a closing deliverable is the Brandywine landlord consent (item 3.1). Crestline is not listed.
- Disclosure Schedule §2.1: Part 3.5 responds to SPA §3.5, which says that except as set out in Part 3.5, no consent or notice to a counterparty to a Material Contract is required for the sale of the Shares.

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- The Varga purchase of 100% of the stock is a Change of Control under §1.1(a). Section 15.4 therefore requires Crestline's prior written consent. The Agreement has no "notice only" provision for a stock sale. Section 17.3 governs only the mechanics of notices.
- Under the Disclosure Schedule's own definitions (§2.2(a)), closing without consent would be a breach and would give Crestline a termination right. That makes the item "Consent required" by definition.
- Because the item is misclassified, §4.1 leaves it out of closing deliverables. The Buyer therefore has no closing-condition protection for it, but Halvorsen would still be in breach of §15.4 at closing. The inaccurate disclosure also exposes the Sellers and the Company to a claim that the §3.5 representation was breached. Under Disclosure Schedule §1.3, a disclosure qualifies a representation only to the extent it is accurate. A misclassified disclosure is a weak defense.
- After closing, Halvorsen is owned by Varga. The Company, not Varga, is the party that would be in breach and would pay any fee.

**Recommended fix**
- Reclassify item 3.4 as "Consent required," and cite §§1.1, 14.1 and 15.4 as the relevant provisions.
- Add the Crestline consent to the §4.1 list of closing deliverables, or, if we can obtain it before signing, describe it as obtained in the schedule.
- Obtain a written consent that expressly (a) consents to the Varga Change of Control, (b) waives the §15.4 termination right and termination fee for this transaction, and (c) addresses the points in Issue 8 (Affiliate integration) and Issue 5 (confidentiality).
- If Crestline does not consent, negotiate special treatment in the SPA (for example, a specific indemnity, escrow, or price adjustment) before signing. Do not sign a §3.5 representation we know to be inaccurate.

**Open points**
- Whether the SPA has been signed. If it has, the Disclosure Schedule may need to be amended and the representation may need a bring-down analysis.
- The SPA definition of "Material Contract" and whether the Agreement qualifies (we assume it does, given its size and exclusivity).

### Issue 2 (High): The 60-day advance request has not been shown to have been made, and the timing may not work

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §15.4, second sentence: Supplier "shall request that consent in writing not less than sixty (60) days before the anticipated closing of the Change of Control, identifying the acquiring person."
- Agreement §17.3: notices must be delivered by hand, nationally recognized overnight courier or certified mail, to "Attention: President," and are effective on receipt.
- First Amendment §4.1: notices to Distributor must include a copy, which does not constitute notice, to "Attention: Director of Contracts," at the same address.
- Disclosure Schedule, item 3.4 (status: notice to be delivered after Closing) and §4.2 (post-closing notices under SPA §5.6). The schedule shows the Brandywine consent request was sent September 15, 2026. It shows no request to Crestline.

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- The 60-day requirement is an independent obligation. Halvorsen breaches §15.4 if it fails to request consent 60 days before closing, even if Crestline later consents.
- Today is October 18, 2026. A request sent today can support a closing no earlier than December 17, 2026. A closing on December 31, 2026, for example, would require a request on or before November 1, 2026.
- The schedule's post-closing treatment of the Crestline item (§4.2) is inconsistent with the Agreement's pre-closing requirement.

**Recommended fix**
- Send the written consent request now. Deliver it by overnight courier to Crestline, Attention: President, with a copy to Attention: Director of Contracts (First Amendment §4.1). Identify Varga Diagnostics Holdings Corp. as the acquirer, as §15.4 requires.
- Ask Crestline in the request for a written waiver of the 60-day period if the closing date cannot move.
- Move the Crestline item from the post-closing notice list in §4.2 to the pre-closing consent list.
- Confirm the anticipated closing date with the deal team and calendar the §15.4 deadline.
- Retain proof of delivery.

**Open points**
- The anticipated closing date. It is not in the folder.
- Whether the deal team has communicated with Crestline about the transaction, and what has been said.
- Whether the SPA's confidentiality or announcement covenants restrict the content of the request. The request must identify the acquirer.

### Issue 3 (High): Termination right and termination fee if closing occurs without consent

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §15.4, third sentence onward: Distributor may terminate by notice within 180 days after it "learns of the closing." Supplier must then pay, within 30 days after termination, "a termination fee equal to twelve months of average Product purchases," calculated as 12 times the average monthly amount invoiced during the 12 full calendar months before the closing.
- Agreement §15.6: §15.4 (as to the termination fee) survives termination.
- Agreement §15.5: on termination, Distributor may sell through remaining inventory for 90 days, or Supplier may elect to repurchase that inventory at Distributor's landed cost.
- Agreement §2.1 and First Amendment §2.1: the exclusive Territory is Ohio, Indiana and Michigan, and the Initial Term now runs through March 2, 2029.
- Agreement §3.2 and Schedule A: minimum annual units.

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- The termination fee is the most quantifiable exposure. The fee equals total invoiced Product purchases in the 12 months before closing. Schedule A sets minimum annual units, and §3.2 requires Crestline to meet them. If Crestline has met its minimums, the 12-month total would be at least roughly the amount below, at current prices:

| Product | Schedule A minimum units | Current price (Schedule B ¶1; no adjustment yet per Price Notice §3.1) | Minimum annual value |
|---|---|---|---|
| HB-2200 | 24 | $38,500.00 | $924,000 |
| HB-3400 | 18 | $46,200.00 | $831,600 |
| HR-2200-RK | 1,200 | $612.00 | $734,400 |
| HR-3400-RK | 900 | $734.00 | $660,600 |
| HC-QC10 | 600 | $148.00 | $88,800 |
| HS-SPK | As ordered | Excluded | Not calculated |
| **Total (excluding HS-SPK)** | | | **$3,239,400** |

- This is an illustration only. Actual invoices control, the 12-month look-back may span two Contract Years, and HS-SPK purchases are excluded.
- The termination trigger is "learns of the closing," with no fixed start date. Without a waiver, the 180-day window may remain open for an uncertain period after closing.
- Loss of the exclusive distributor for three states is a commercial exposure beyond the fee. The §15.5 inventory repurchase obligation is a further cash exposure.

**Recommended fix**
- Obtain Crestline's written waiver of the §15.4 termination right and the termination fee for this transaction. Do not rely on Crestline not learning of the closing.
- Obtain Crestline's invoice history for the most recent 12 full calendar months and model the fee for alternative closing dates.
- If a waiver is not obtained, address the fee in the SPA: a specific indemnity, escrow, or purchase price reduction, with the cost allocated to the Sellers if that is the negotiated outcome.

**Open points**
- Actual 12-month invoice totals and Crestline's purchase performance against Schedule A minimums. Neither is in the folder.
- Whether the fee would be enforced as stated. Delaware law governs (Agreement §17.1), and the fee is a fixed amount tied to purchases. I have not analyzed whether it would be treated as a penalty, and I do not assume the answer.

### Issue 5 (High): Confidentiality. Disclosing the Agreement to Varga may breach Section 9, and that breach is uncapped

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §9.1: Confidential Information includes "pricing" and "the terms of this Agreement."
- Agreement §9.2: disclosure is permitted only to the receiving party's and its Affiliates' employees, advisors and contractors who need to know. Varga is not an Affiliate of Halvorsen before closing, and it is not an advisor.
- Agreement §9.3: the exceptions do not obviously cover disclosure to a prospective buyer. The folder shows no Crestline consent to disclosure.
- First Amendment §3.1 (restated §12.3): "The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 shall not apply to a party's breach of Section 9."
- Disclosure Schedule §1.5: "True and complete copies of each listed Contract, including all amendments, have been made available to Buyer in the [Data Room]." Item 3.4 states the Crestline exclusivity terms and cites Data Room 3.1.1–3.1.2.

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- If the full Agreement, including Schedule B prices and Schedule A minimums, is in the Data Room, Halvorsen may already be in breach of §9.2.
- Because breaches of §9 are excluded from both the §12.1 consequential-damages waiver and the §12.2 cap, Crestline's claim would be uncapped, and lost profits and other consequential damages could be claimed.
- The Disclosure Schedule's own description of the Crestline terms (item 3.4) may also be a disclosure of Confidential Information.

**Recommended fix**
- Confirm immediately what has been posted to the Data Room, who has access, and whether a confidentiality agreement with Varga covers the Crestline materials.
- If no adequate protection is in place, restrict access, redact Schedule A and Schedule B pricing and minimums, or move the materials to a clean-team arrangement.
- Request Crestline's written consent to disclosure as part of the consent request (Issue 2) or the waiver (Issue 3).
- Consider whether the Disclosure Schedule should describe the Crestline terms in less detail.

**Open points**
- Whether Crestline consented to disclosure, and whether Varga has signed an NDA that addresses the Crestline materials. The folder does not show either.

### Issue 6 (High): The First Amendment changed the liability carve-outs, and the Disclosure Schedule does not describe the change

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §12.3 (original): the limitations in §§12.1 and 12.2 do not apply to breaches of §9 or §10, or to indemnification obligations under §11.
- First Amendment §3.1: §12.3 is restated to cover only "a party's breach of Section 9." Sections 10 and 11 are removed from the carve-outs.
- First Amendment Recital B: the parties agreed to "make certain other changes to the Agreement." The recital does not describe them.
- Disclosure Schedule, item 3.4: the First Amendment is described only as having "extended the Initial Term through March 2, 2029."

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- The change cuts both ways. As Supplier, Halvorsen benefits because its product-defect and IP indemnities under §11.1 are now subject to the §12.2 cap (amounts paid or payable to Supplier in the preceding 12 months) and the §12.1 exclusion of consequential damages.
- The change also limits Halvorsen's recourse against Crestline. Crestline's indemnities under §11.2, and its trademark misuse and reverse-engineering breaches under §10.3, are now subject to the same cap and exclusion.
- The Disclosure Schedule's summary of the amendment is incomplete. Under §1.5, Buyer has the full document, and it may view the summary as misleading.

**Recommended fix**
- Confirm the business reason for the change and the negotiating history, and whether any other changes were made. Ask the client directly.
- Revise item 3.4 to describe the change in §12.3, and to describe the other amendments.
- Confirm that the SPA material-contract representation, which we have not seen, is not undermined by the incomplete summary.

**Open points**
- Whether the change was intentional and negotiated, and what "certain other changes" in Recital B refers to.

### Issue 7 (Medium): Whether the termination fee is subject to the liability cap is unclear

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §12.1 (exclusion of consequential damages and lost profits), §12.2 (cap: amounts paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim), §12.3 as restated (carve-out for §9 only).
- Agreement §15.4 (fee) and §15.6 (survival of the fee).

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- Section 15.4 is not listed in §12.3. The fee is therefore arguably subject to the §12.2 cap and the §12.1 exclusion. Crestline may argue the fee is a contractual payment obligation that falls outside the limitations, and that the limitations do not apply to it.
- The answer changes the maximum exposure. I do not assume an answer.

**Recommended fix**
- Do not model the exposure as capped. Assume the full fee, plus any additional damages Crestline may claim, until the point is resolved.
- Address the fee expressly in any consent or waiver.

**Open points**
- The characterization of the §15.4 fee under §§12.1 and 12.2.

### Issue 8 (Medium): Exclusivity and post-closing integration with Varga

**Where it appears**
- Agreement §2.2: "Supplier shall not appoint any other distributor, agent or reseller for the Products in the Territory and shall not sell Products directly to Customers in the Territory," except for Schedule D national accounts and sales Distributor declines in writing.

- Agreement §9.2 (Affiliates may receive Confidential Information).

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**

- Integration of Varga's group purchasing or national account relationships could also affect the Schedule D carve-outs.

**Recommended fix**
- Map Varga's current and planned sales channels in the Territory before closing.
- Design any post-closing integration to comply with §2.2 or obtain Crestline's clarification in the consent.

**Open points**
- Varga's existing business and channels in the Territory, and its post-closing plans. Neither is in the folder.

### Issue 9 (Medium): The Price Notice is compliant on its face, but its timing and context need attention

**Where it appears**
- Price Notice §1.3, §2.1–2.3 (Adjustment Date March 3, 2027; delivered October 5, 2026), §3.2–3.3 (adjusted prices and 5% limit), §4.1–4.2 (price protection), §5.1 (price file by February 1, 2027).
- Agreement §5.1 and Schedule B ¶2 (once per Contract Year, at least 60 days' notice, 5% cap) and ¶3 (price protection).

**Why it matters for Halvorsen**
- Compliance check. Each increase is within the 5% cap: HB-2200 +3.5% ($39,847.50); HB-3400 +3.0% ($47,586.00); HR-2200-RK +4.0% ($636.48); HR-3400-RK +4.0% ($763.36); HC-QC10 +5.0% ($155.40). The notice was delivered more than 60 days before March 3, 2027. The 60-day cut-off is January 2, 2027. The adjustment date is the first day of a Contract Year, as ¶2 requires.
- The Price Notice is dated October 5, 2026, which is after the SPA date of September 8, 2026. If the SPA restricts pricing changes or changes to material contracts between signing and closing, this notice may need Buyer consent or disclosure. The folder does not show either.
- The notice arrives days before the Crestline consent request is due (Issue 2), and it may affect Crestline's posture in the consent negotiation.
- If closing occurs after March 3, 2027, part of the 12-month fee look-back (Issue 3) will include adjusted prices.

**Recommended fix**
- Confirm SPA interim operating covenants and whether Buyer consent was obtained or Buyer was informed before the notice was sent.
- Coordinate the consent request and the Price Notice so Crestline does not receive them as unrelated communications.
- Retain proof of delivery for the Price Notice and confirm the receipt date.
- Confirm the Price Notice's statement in §3.1 that no price adjustment has been made since the Effective Date.

**Open points**
- The SPA interim covenants, and whether the notice was sent with Buyer knowledge.
