## 1. Summary

The Crestline agreement is a material contract with a change-of-control consent requirement. The Seller Disclosure Schedule classifies it as "notice only," and that classification is wrong. Under Section 15.4 of the Exclusive Distribution Agreement, Crestline's prior written consent is required before the Varga closing. If Halvorsen closes without it, Crestline may terminate and Halvorsen must pay a termination fee. The Disclosure Schedule also omits Crestline's consent from the closing deliverables.

Three further issues need attention before signing or closing:

- The Crestline agreement and its pricing may already have been placed in the data room. Section 9 of that agreement restricts disclosure to Varga, and a Section 9 breach is uncapped under the First Amendment.
- The 60-day advance consent request requirement may already make a closing before December 17, 2026 impossible to satisfy.
- Halvorsen's October 5, 2026 price increase should be checked against any interim operating covenants in the SPA, which is not in the folder.

## 2. Issues

### Issue 1: Crestline's consent is required, but the Disclosure Schedule says notice only (HIGH)

**Where it appears:**
- Disclosure Schedule §3.4 ("Relevant provision: Supplier change in ownership — notice only; no consent required"; "Category: Notice only"; "Status: Notice to be delivered… following the Closing").
- Agreement §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), §14.1 (cross-referring Change of Control to §15.4), §15.4 (Supplier Change of Control).

**Analysis:** Under §1.1, a Change of Control includes any transaction in which a person acquires beneficial ownership of more than 50% of Halvorsen's outstanding voting stock "by stock purchase." The Varga transaction is a stock purchase of the Shares. Section 15.4 provides that Supplier "shall not undergo a Change of Control without Distributor's prior written consent." The Agreement contains no post-closing notice requirement for a Change of Control. The Disclosure Schedule's "notice only" category has no basis in the Agreement.

**Why it matters:**
- The Disclosure Schedule's §3.5 rep (as described in Disclosure Schedule §2.1) says no counterparty consent is required except as listed in Part 3.5. Halvorsen is a party to the SPA as the Company, so it bears the risk of an inaccurate disclosure. An inaccuracy could lead to indemnity claims, or to a fraud allegation if the error is known and uncorrected.
- Crestline's consent has no reasonableness standard. Section 15.4 says only "prior written consent." Crestline therefore has hold-up leverage.

**Recommended fix:**
- Revise Disclosure Schedule §3.4 to "Consent required," citing Agreement §§1.1, 14.1 and 15.4. Describe the termination right and fee accurately.
- Before signing, confirm the corrected classification with Varga and the sellers' counsel. If the SPA is already signed, evaluate whether an amendment or updated schedule is needed.

### Issue 2: The 60-day consent request requirement may already constrain the closing date (HIGH)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §15.4, second sentence: Supplier "shall request that consent in writing not less than sixty (60) days before the anticipated closing of the Change of Control, identifying the acquiring person."
- Agreement §17.3 (notices are effective on receipt).
- Disclosure Schedule §4.1 (closing deliverables) and §3.4 (status).

**Analysis:** The folder contains no evidence that a consent request has been sent to Crestline. If the request is delivered today (October 18, 2026), the earliest compliant anticipated closing date is December 17, 2026. Any earlier closing would breach §15.4's advance-request requirement, regardless of whether Crestline consents.

**Why it matters:** A late request is an independent breach of §15.4 by Halvorsen. It could support a Crestline claim, and it would weaken Halvorsen's position in any consent negotiation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm immediately whether a request has been sent. If not, send a written request to Crestline (Attention: President, with a copy to Director of Contracts, per Agreement §17.3 and First Amendment §4.1). The request should identify Varga Diagnostics Holdings Corp. as the acquiring person.
- Build the closing timetable around the 60-day period.

### Issue 3: Crestline's consent is missing from the closing deliverables, and the post-closing notice treatment is inconsistent with the Agreement (HIGH)

**Where it appears:**
- Disclosure Schedule §4.1 (consents to be delivered at or before Closing "are limited to the consent described in item 3.1" (Brandywine)).
- Disclosure Schedule §4.2 (notices in items 3.2, 3.4, 3.5 and 3.7 to be delivered post-closing under SPA §5.6).
- Disclosure Schedule §3.4 (status: notice after closing).

**Analysis:** Section 6.2(d) of the SPA (as referenced in Disclosure Schedule §4.1) would not require Crestline's consent as a closing deliverable. A post-closing "notice" cannot satisfy §15.4, which requires prior written consent. The Disclosure Schedule therefore treats a pre-closing consent as a post-closing notice.

**Why it matters:** If the SPA closing conditions track the Disclosure Schedule, Varga could be required to close without Crestline's consent. Halvorsen would then carry the termination exposure in Issue 4.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add Crestline's written consent (with no adverse modification of the Agreement, and ideally with an estoppel) to the §6.2(d) closing deliverables or closing conditions.
- Remove Crestline from the §4.2 post-closing notice list. If a post-closing notice is still given, it should be an addition, not a substitute.

### Issue 4: Termination right and termination fee if Halvorsen closes without consent (HIGH; quantum open)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §15.4, final sentence (termination right within 180 days after Distributor learns of the closing; termination fee payable within 30 days after termination, equal to 12 × the average monthly amount invoiced in the 12 full calendar months before closing).
- Agreement §15.6 (§15.4 survives "as to the termination fee").
- Agreement §12.1 and §12.2 (exclusions and cap, which by their terms apply to §15.4 claims because §12.3 carves out only §§9, 10 and 11 as amended).

**Analysis:**
- The fee is measured by actual invoices, not by Schedule A minimums. The folder contains no invoice history, so the amount cannot be calculated from the documents.
- As an order-of-magnitude check only: at Schedule A minimum volumes and Schedule B prices (which, per Price Notice §3.1, still apply for the Contract Year running March 3, 2026 to March 2, 2027), annual product value excluding HS-SPK would be approximately $3.24 million (HB-2200 24 × $38,500 = $924,000; HB-3400 18 × $46,200 = $831,600; HR-2200-RK 1,200 × $612 = $734,400; HR-3400-RK 900 × $734 = $660,600; HC-QC10 600 × $148 = $88,800). The fee would be roughly 12 months of that figure, subject to actual purchases.
- It is unclear whether the fee is Crestline's exclusive remedy for a §15.4 breach. Section 15.4 does not say so. Crestline could argue for damages beyond the fee, subject to the §12 limits.
- The Company, not Varga, would be the obligor after a stock sale. The exposure therefore stays with the target unless the SPA allocates it.

**Why it matters:** Crestline's termination would remove Halvorsen's exclusive channel for three states, which is an operational loss in addition to the fee.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Crestline's 12-month invoice data and calculate the fee.
- Seek Crestline's consent with a written waiver of the §15.4 termination right and fee for this transaction.
- If consent is not obtained, the parties should negotiate a specific indemnity, escrow or purchase price adjustment for the fee, with the allocation set out in the SPA.

### Issue 5: Data-room disclosure of the Agreement and pricing may breach the confidentiality covenant (HIGH)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §9.1 (Confidential Information includes "pricing" and "the terms of this Agreement"); §9.2 (disclosure only to the receiving party's "Affiliates' employees, advisors and contractors who need to know").
- Agreement §12.3, as restated by First Amendment §3.1: the §12.1 and §12.2 limits "shall not apply to a party's breach of Section 9."
- Disclosure Schedule §1.5 (true and complete copies of each listed Contract, including amendments, "have been made available to Buyer in the electronic data room").

**Analysis:** Varga is a prospective buyer and is neither an affiliate of Halvorsen nor an advisor or contractor of it. Section 9.2 does not clearly permit disclosure to a bidder. The Agreement's terms and Schedule B pricing are Confidential Information. Section 9.3 offers no obvious exception, and nothing in the folder shows Crestline's consent. The Price Notice may also have been placed in the data room.

**Why it matters:** Under the First Amendment, a Section 9 breach is outside both the consequential-damages exclusion and the cap. Halvorsen's exposure to Crestline for a confidentiality breach is therefore uncapped.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm what has been uploaded to the data room and when, and whether Varga is under a confidentiality agreement.
- Obtain Crestline's written consent to the disclosure, or remove or redact Schedule B pricing and the Agreement terms from the data room until consent is obtained.
- Confirm that the Varga NDA covers the information and that Varga's use is restricted as §9.2 requires.

### Issue 6: First Amendment §3.1 changed the liability carve-outs; the change is not described in the Disclosure Schedule (MEDIUM)

**Where it appears:**
- First Amendment §3.1 (restatement of §12.3, excluding only a breach of §9).
- Agreement §12.3 (original version, excluding §§9, 10 and 11).
- Disclosure Schedule §3.4 (describes the First Amendment as extending the term only).

**Analysis:** The original §12.3 excluded from the damages exclusion and cap the breaches of §§9 and 10 and the indemnities in §11. The First Amendment removed §§10 and 11 from that list. Two consequences follow:
- Halvorsen's product-defect and IP indemnities under Agreement §11.1 are now capped under §12.2. This benefits Halvorsen as indemnitor.
- Confidentiality breaches under §9 are now uncapped, as noted in Issue 5. This is a detriment.

The recitals to the First Amendment do not explain the change. The Disclosure Schedule does not mention it.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether the change was intended and whether anyone considered the Section 9 trade-off.
- Consider disclosing the §12.3 change in Disclosure Schedule §3.4 for completeness, so that the buyer's diligence record is accurate.

### Issue 8: Exclusivity scope, the Schedule D exception, and post-closing affiliate reach (MEDIUM)

**Where it appears:**
- Agreement §2.2 (no other distributor and no direct sales in the Territory, except (a) "sales to national accounts listed in Schedule D under Supplier's existing group purchasing contracts" and (b) sales Distributor declines in writing).
- Agreement Schedule D, item 1 (Meridian group purchasing contract MHPA-2021-0447) and item 2 (Lakeshore Reference Laboratories, "national supply agreement dated October 1, 2021").
- Agreement §1.1 ("Affiliate"); §2.2 (the restriction binds Supplier only).
- Disclosure Schedule §3.5 and §3.6 (confirming the Meridian and Lakeshore agreements).

**Analysis:**
- Exception (a) covers "group purchasing contracts." Meridian is a group purchasing contract, but Lakeshore is described as a direct "national supply agreement." Schedule D lists Lakeshore as an excluded national account, but the text of §2.2(a) does not clearly reach it. This is an open point.
- Section 2.2 binds only Halvorsen. After closing, however, Varga and its affiliates will be Halvorsen's affiliates. If they sell competing or Product-equivalent instruments into Ohio, Indiana or Michigan, or cross-sell through their own channels, Halvorsen could be in breach. The Agreement does not extend §2.2 to affiliates, but Crestline could argue that Halvorsen's conduct through affiliates is covered.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether the Lakeshore agreement is in fact a group purchasing arrangement, and whether Lakeshore sales have been treated as excluded.
- Map Varga's product lines and sales channels in the Territory before closing. Consider whether post-closing integration plans need Crestline's consent or carve-out.

### Issue 12: Transaction structure (LOW; open)

**Where it appears:** Agreement §14.1 (assignment "whether voluntarily or by operation of law" requires Crestline's consent, and Change of Control is governed by §15.4); Agreement §1.1 (Change of Control includes a merger).
