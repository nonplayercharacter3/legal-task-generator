1. **Most urgent: the Vantrell supply position.** Corbel's supply contract for its resin products is at risk on two independent grounds. First, the sale is a change of control that requires Vantrell's prior written consent, failing which Vantrell may terminate on 30 days' notice (SA §14.2). - SL preamble describes the Side Letter as "a condition to [the parties'] execution of the Purchase Agreement." This looks like a Seller ask, which the client should evaluate.

**Recommended fix:** Delete SL §2 and restore the SPA §1.1 definition expressly. Consider adding a title rep and §4.16 (Brokers) to the Fundamental Representations.

### Issue 6: Escrow release and claim mechanics let the Seller stall recovery

**Where:** ESC §4.2 (disbursement on joint written instruction), §4.3 (disbursement on Final Order), §4.4 (release on joint instruction), §4.1 and §1.1 ("Claim Notice" and "Pending Claim Amount" count only notices copied to the Escrow Agent), §8.4 (notices to agent effective only on actual receipt), §6.1 (agent not bound by SPA). SL §3.3 (notices to Seller). SL §4.1 (joint instructions within five Business Days after an amount "becomes payable"). SL §4.3 (no unreasonable withholding). SL §6 (Delaware court forum).

**Why it matters:**
- Halvorsen has no unilateral right to release escrow funds. Without the Seller's signature, the only route is a Final Order, which requires litigation. A Seller that disputes a claim can keep the escrow frozen while the dispute is litigated.
- "Becomes payable" (SL §4.1) is undefined and assumes agreement or a final order. There is no deemed-acceptance mechanism for claims the Seller fails to answer.
- The Pending Claim Amount trap: under ESC §§1.1 and 4.1, only Claim Notices actually copied to the Escrow Agent hold back funds. If the copy is omitted or not actually received (ESC §8.4), funds may be released on the Release Date. The Escrow Agent has no duty to check the SPA (ESC §6.1).

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a deemed-acceptance mechanism for claims the Seller does not dispute in writing within 30 days.
- Clarify that a failure to copy the Escrow Agent does not limit Buyer's rights against Seller and can be cured before the Release Date.
- Add a mechanism for expedited resolution of amount disputes, if the client wants it.
- Conform the Escrow Agreement.

**Proposed language:**

> **Side Letter §4.4 [new].** If Seller does not deliver a written objection, stating in reasonable detail its basis, within thirty (30) days after receipt of a claim notice under Section 3, Seller shall be deemed to have agreed the Losses claimed in it. If Seller does not execute a Joint Written Instruction within five (5) Business Days after that deemed agreement, Buyer may deliver to the Escrow Agent a certificate of deemed agreement, and the Escrow Agent shall disburse in accordance with it.

> **ESC §4.1 (added sentence).** Buyer's failure to deliver a copy of any Claim Notice to the Escrow Agent shall not limit any Buyer Indemnitee's rights against Seller, and Buyer may deliver that copy at any time before the Release Date.

### Issue 12: Supply Agreement terms that the SPA does not address

Each item below is a risk the buyer inherits through the Company. Several need changes in the SA itself through an estoppel or amendment, not just SPA language.

**(a) Company's indemnity to Vantrell is broad and appears uncapped.** SA §11.2(a) requires the Company to indemnify Vantrell for third-party claims "based on Customer's products," except to the extent caused by Nonconforming Product. The Company bears these claims without a fault requirement. Vantrell's indemnity (SA §11.1) is narrower and covers only Nonconforming Product, IP infringement, and Vantrell's negligence or violation of law. §12.1 excludes indemnity obligations from the consequential-damages bar, and §12.2's cap applies "except for the matters excluded in Section 12.1." On that reading, the Company's §11.2 indemnity is uncapped. *Fix:* Negotiate a fault-based indemnity with a cap in any SA amendment. Obtain a Seller special indemnity for pre-Closing product claims.

**(b) Corbel cannot recover lost profits from Vantrell for supply failure.** SA §12.1 bars lost profits except for the listed matters. Corbel's remedies are limited to §7.3 (expedited shipping or cover costs) and §15.2 (pro rata allocation and cover during force majeure). *Fix:* Negotiate a lost-profit remedy or a supply-failure cap in any amendment.

**(c) 100% requirements and take-or-pay exposure.** SA §2.2 (100% requirements), §2.4 (1,800,000 kg minimum; 8% shortfall payment), §4.1 (first three months of forecast binding at 80%), and §4.3 (Purchase Orders deemed accepted unless above 120% of forecast). If Corbel's volumes fall, it pays the shortfall. *Fix:* Obtain purchase history and confirm compliance with the minimum. Include compliance in the Vantrell estoppel.

**(d) Index-linked pricing with no cap.** SA §5.2 and Schedule B, Part 1 permit quarterly adjustments up or down on 30 days' notice, with a 40% pass-through of index changes and no cap. *Fix:* Negotiate a cap or collar, or a fixed-price period, in any amendment.

**(e) Vantrell may assign freely.** SA §16.1 lets Vantrell assign without consent, including on a sale of its resin business. The Company may not assign without consent. *Fix:* Require consent for assignment to a competitor, or a change-of-control notice.

**(f) Confidentiality may restrict diligence sharing.** SA §§10.1–10.2 treat Vantrell's pricing, Specifications and formulations as Vantrell's Confidential Information. Disclosure is permitted only to the disclosing Company's employees, advisors and contractors with a need to know, and to its Affiliates. Halvorsen is not an Affiliate before Closing. If the Company shared Vantrell pricing or specifications with Halvorsen, that may have breached §10.2, which bears on the Seller's no-breach rep (SPA §4.10(b)). *Fix:* Confirm what has been shared and how. Seek Vantrell's written consent, or move to a clean-team arrangement.

**(g) Insurance requirement with no rep.** SA §13 requires the Company to maintain $5,000,000 CGL with products coverage from A- rated insurers. The SPA has no insurance rep, so compliance is unverified. *Fix:* Add an insurance rep (Issue 13) and request certificates.

**(h) Force majeure.** SA §15.1 excuses performance for labor disputes not involving the affected party's own workforce. Vantrell's labor disruption is therefore excused, and the Company bears the supply risk. *Fix:* Note for the client; address in any amendment.

**(i) Incorporated technical materials are missing.** SA Schedule A incorporates Supplier technical data sheets, Revision C, and plaque CF-BK-01. Neither is in the folder.

### Issue 14: Narrow Fraud definition and no pro-sandbagging clause

**Where:** SPA §1.1 ("Fraud" means "actual and intentional fraud under the Laws of the State of New York with respect to the making of the representations and warranties in Article IV or Article V"). SPA §9.5(b)(i) and §9.8 (carve-outs depend on Fraud). SPA §9.2(a) (covers certificates). SPA §6.4 (notices do not cure breaches). SPA §8.2 (post-termination liability only for "willful breach").

**Why it matters:**
- "Fraud" does not cover fraud in a certificate, which §9.2(a) indemnifies, and it does not cover reckless misrepresentation.
- The SPA has no express pro-sandbagging clause. §6.4 helps, but it covers only notices. We now know about the Vantrell issue from the documents we reviewed. If Halvorsen closes without a reservation, Seller may argue waiver or knowledge. The governing New York law on this point must be researched (Part G).
- §8.2 preserves only willful breach after termination, not fraud.

**Recommended fix:** Broaden the Fraud definition and add a pro-sandbagging clause. Preserve fraud after termination. Reserve rights in the closing letter.

**Proposed language:**

> "**Fraud**" means actual fraud or intentional misrepresentation, including reckless indifference to the truth, in the making of any representation or warranty in Article IV or Article V or in any certificate delivered under this Agreement.

> **9.7A [new] Reliance.** The rights of the Buyer Indemnitees to indemnification, payment or any other remedy under this Agreement are not affected by any investigation conducted by or on behalf of Buyer, or any knowledge acquired (or capable of being acquired) by Buyer at any time, whether before or after the execution of this Agreement or the Closing, with respect to the accuracy or inaccuracy of any representation or warranty.

### Issue 15: Tax covenants

**Where:** SPA §10.1 (Seller prepares pre-Closing period returns due after Closing; Buyer may review and comment; no consent right). SPA §9.2(c) (pre-Closing taxes "attributable to any taxable period... ending on or before the Closing Date"). No straddle-period allocation in the SPA. SPA §10.2 (transfer taxes split 50/50). SPA §4.12 (limited tax rep).

**Why it matters:** The SPA does not allocate taxes for a period that begins before and ends after the Closing Date. §9.2(c) is limited to periods ending on or before Closing, leaving straddle periods unaddressed. Buyer also has no consent right over Seller's preparation of returns that affect post-Closing periods.

**Recommended fix:** Add straddle-period allocation, a Buyer consent right over returns affecting post-Closing periods, and expanded tax reps.

**Proposed language:**

> **9.2(c) (addition).** For any taxable period beginning on or before and ending after the Closing Date (a "Straddle Period"), (i) property Taxes shall be apportioned on a daily basis, and (ii) all other Taxes shall be determined as if the taxable period ended at the end of the Closing Date.

> **10.1 (addition).** Seller shall not cause the Company to file any Tax return for a Straddle Period or any pre-Closing period that affects a post-Closing period without Buyer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld.

### Issue 16: Interim covenants and MAE are too thin for the Vantrell scenario

**Where:** SPA §6.1 (conduct of business; covers amendments, terminations, waivers and equity issuance). SPA §6.3 (consents limited to Schedule 4.4). SPA §1.1 ("Material Adverse Effect," including carve-out (d) for the announcement of the transactions). SPA §7.2(e).

**Why it matters:**
- §6.1 does not restrict new Material Contracts, pricing changes, capital expenditures, dividends, indebtedness, compensation or bonus changes, asset sales, settlements, tax elections or accounting changes. §6.1(c) does not prohibit failing to exercise an option, which is relevant to the Vantrell extension (Issue 2).
- §6.3 is empty as written (Issue 1).
- Whether a Vantrell termination would be a Material Adverse Effect is uncertain. Carve-out (d) could be argued to apply because the termination right is triggered by the transaction. Relying on the MAE condition is not a sound basis for a walk right.

**Recommended fix:** Expand §6.1. Make Vantrell an express condition (Issue 1) rather than relying on the MAE condition.

**Proposed language (additional §6.1 items):**

> (e) not enter into, amend, terminate or waive any material right under any Contract with a customer or supplier involving more than $[●] per year; (f) not make any capital expenditure in excess of $[●] in aggregate; (g) not declare or pay any dividend or distribution, or incur indebtedness other than under the existing Revolving Credit Agreement in the ordinary course; (h) not increase compensation or bonus opportunities, or adopt or amend any benefit plan, other than in the ordinary course consistent with past practice; and (i) not make any Tax election, settle any Tax claim, or change any accounting method, practice or policy.

## 5. Escrow Agreement Operational Points

### Issue 17: Escrow Agreement operational and drafting points

- **Call-back verification (ESC §4.6).** Verification by call-back applies only to disbursements to a new account. Require call-back verification for every disbursement and every change in wire instructions, using Exhibit B representatives.
- **Joint and several liability to the agent (ESC §6.4).** Buyer and Seller are jointly and severally liable to the Escrow Agent, with a 50/50 internal split. The agreement should make each party liable for only its share, as between the two of them and to the agent.
- **Default investment and FDIC coverage (ESC §3.1).** Absent instructions, funds sit uninvested in a non-interest-bearing account insured "to the extent available." An $8.45 million balance likely exceeds standard deposit insurance. The closing instructions should designate a Treasury money market fund.
- **Tax ownership of earnings (ESC §5.1).** Buyer is treated as owner and reports earnings even though escrow funds may be paid to Seller. The tax allocation should be aligned with SPA §9.7 and reviewed by tax counsel.
- **Blanks.** The Effective Date ([●]), Exhibit A amounts and payee details, and Exhibit B names and telephone numbers are blank. Seller's authorized representatives are not identified.
- **Shortfall and agent obligations.** ESC §4.5 places any shortfall on Seller under SPA §9.6. The agent has no further obligation. This is consistent with Issue 5 but should be cross-referenced in the SPA.
