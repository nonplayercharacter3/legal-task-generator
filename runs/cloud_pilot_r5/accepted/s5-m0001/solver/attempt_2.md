### Issue 1: Side Letter removes Capitalization and Taxes from Fundamental Representations (Critical)

**Where:** Side Letter §2 ("solely Sections 4.1 and 4.2"); Side Letter §2 expressly applies to SPA §§7.2(a), 9.1, and 9.5(b). The SPA definition is at SPA §1.1 ("Fundamental Representations" includes §§4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.12). SPA §11.11 makes the Side Letter prevail over the SPA.

**Why it matters:** If Side Letter §2 stands, the following changes apply to §4.3 (Capitalization) and §4.12 (Taxes):

- Their bring-down at closing drops from "true in all respects" to "true in all material respects" (SPA §7.2(a)).

The Tax rep is partly mitigated because the pre-closing Tax indemnity in SPA §9.2(c) is uncapped under SPA §9.5(b), but the Capitalization loss is not mitigated anywhere.

**Recommended fix:**
- Delete Side Letter §2 in its entirety.
- Revise SPA §11.11 so the Agreement controls over the Side Letter unless the Side Letter expressly identifies a specific SPA section it amends.

> **Proposed §11.11 (revised):** "In the event of any conflict among the Transaction Documents, this Agreement shall control, except that the Side Letter shall control over this Agreement solely to the extent the Side Letter expressly identifies the specific Section of this Agreement it amends and states that it so amends it."

### Issue 5: Escrow release mechanics could release funds despite pending claims (High)

**Where:**
- Escrow §1.1: "Pending Claim Amount" counts only Claim Notices delivered to the Escrow Agent.
- Escrow §4.1: Buyer must "concurrently" copy the Escrow Agent. Escrow §8.4: notices to the Agent are effective only on actual receipt.
- Escrow §§4.2, 4.4: every disbursement requires a Joint Written Instruction. The release at 18 months is reduced only by the Pending Claim Amount.
- SPA §2.5: release is reduced by "the aggregate amount of any then-pending claims."
- SPA §9.1: a claim asserted in writing to the indemnitor before the survival period expires survives.
- Side Letter §§3.2, 4.1: Joint Written Instruction within five Business Days after an amount "becomes payable." No mechanism applies if Seller refuses to sign.

**Why it matters:**
- A claim validly asserted to Seller under SPA §9.4 may survive under SPA §9.1 but still not be held back if Buyer missed copying the Agent. Funds could then go to Seller at the release date.
- If Seller refuses to sign a Joint Written Instruction, the only path is a Final Order (Escrow §4.3), which means litigation in New York or Delaware.
- "Becomes payable" is undefined, so Seller can argue no obligation to sign has arisen.

**Recommended fix:**
- Measure the Pending Claim Amount by claims delivered to Seller, whether or not copied to the Agent.
- Add a deemed-acceptance mechanism if Seller does not object within 30 days.
- Define "payable" as an amount agreed in writing or finally determined.
- Add release of undisputed amounts.

> **Proposed Escrow §1.1 (revised definition):** "'Pending Claim Amount' means, at any time, the aggregate amount of Losses claimed in all claim notices delivered to Seller under Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement before that time (whether or not a copy has been delivered to the Escrow Agent) that have not been resolved and paid or released under Section 4."

> **Proposed new Escrow §4.1A:** "If Seller does not deliver to Buyer and the Escrow Agent a written objection, stating in reasonable detail the basis for objection, within thirty (30) days after receipt of a Claim Notice, Seller shall be deemed to have agreed to the claim. Buyer may then deliver to the Escrow Agent a certificate to that effect, on which the Escrow Agent may rely, and the Escrow Agent shall disburse the claimed amount to Buyer within two (2) Business Days."

### Issue 8: Damages architecture undercuts recovery (High)

**Where:**
- SPA §9.5(a): Deductible of $633,750 (0.75% of price) is a true deductible. Cap is $8.45 million (10%).
- SPA §9.5(c): materiality scrape applies to Losses only, not to determining breach.
- Article IX and elsewhere: no pro-sandbagging clause.
- SPA §6.4: Seller's notices do not cure breaches, but this does not address Buyer's knowledge.

**Why it matters:**
- Under the true deductible, a $700,000 loss yields only $66,250 of recovery.
- Materiality qualifiers in reps such as §4.10(b) ("material breach") and §4.14 would not be disregarded in deciding whether a breach occurred.
- Under New York law, a buyer that closes with knowledge of a breach may be found to have waived it unless it expressly preserves its rights. See *CBS Inc. v. Ziff-Davis Publishing Co.*, 75 N.Y.2d 496 (1990), and *Galli v. Metz*, 973 F.2d 145 (2d Cir. 1992). These authorities must be cite-checked before we rely on them externally.
- Halvorsen now knows of Issues 2 and 3. Closing without a reservation could waive those claims.

**Recommended fix:** Adopt a double materiality scrape, a tipping basket, and an express pro-sandbagging clause. Send a reservation-of-rights letter before any signing or closing.

> **Proposed §9.5(a) (revised, last sentence):** "Once aggregate Losses under Section 9.2(a) exceed the Deductible, Seller shall be liable for all such Losses from the first dollar, including the amount of the Deductible."

> **Proposed §9.5(c) (replacement):** "For purposes of determining whether any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty has occurred and for calculating Losses under this Article IX, each materiality, Material Adverse Effect or similar qualification shall be disregarded (other than the defined term 'Material Contracts')."

> **Proposed new §9.10:** "The rights of the Buyer Indemnitees to indemnification, payment, reimbursement or any other remedy shall not be affected or limited by any investigation conducted by or on behalf of Buyer, or by any knowledge acquired (or capable of being acquired) at any time by Buyer, whether before or after the execution of this Agreement or the Closing, with respect to the accuracy or inaccuracy of any representation, warranty, covenant or agreement. The representations, warranties, covenants and agreements are bargained-for allocations of risk."

### Issue 9: Fraud definition is narrow (High)

**Where:** SPA §1.1 ("Fraud" means "actual and intentional fraud under the Laws of the State of New York with respect to the making of the representations and warranties in Article IV or Article V"). Fraud is the basis for the carve-outs in SPA §§9.5(b)(i) and 9.8.

**Why it matters:** The definition excludes fraud in the certificates under §§3.2(d) and 7.2(d), the Disclosure Schedule, and the Side Letter. It also excludes reckless misrepresentation. Because the Fraud carve-outs from the cap and exclusive remedy depend on this definition, a narrow definition shrinks Halvorsen's protection in the cases that matter most.

**Recommended fix:** Broaden the definition to cover all representations in the Agreement, the Disclosure Schedule, certificates, and the Side Letter, and include recklessness. Seller will likely resist recklessness, so consider it a trading point.

> **Proposed §1.1 "Fraud":** "'Fraud' means fraud under the Laws of the State of New York (including fraud based on reckless indifference to the truth) in the making of any representation or warranty in this Agreement, the Disclosure Schedule, any certificate delivered under this Agreement, or the Side Letter."

### Issue 11: Conflicting governing-law and forum clauses (High)

**Where:**
- SPA §§11.8–11.10: New York law; exclusive New York County courts; jury waiver.
- Side Letter §6.1: Delaware law for indemnification disputes. §6.2: exclusive Court of Chancery jurisdiction, with Superior Court or D. Del. only if Chancery lacks subject-matter jurisdiction. §6.3: jury waiver.
- Escrow §§8.1–8.3: New York law; exclusive New York County courts; jury waiver.

Because the Side Letter prevails under SPA §11.11, its Delaware provisions could control even though the SPA is governed by New York law.

**Recommended fix:** Conform the Side Letter to New York law and New York County courts, consistent with the SPA, Escrow, and Supply Agreement. If the client prefers Delaware, move all four documents.

> **Proposed Side Letter §6.1 and §6.2 (replacement):** "6.1 This Letter Agreement, and all claims arising out of or relating to it, shall be governed by the internal laws of the State of New York, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law provision that would cause the application of any other jurisdiction's laws. 6.2 Section 11.9 of the Purchase Agreement applies to this Letter Agreement."

### Issue 12: Interim covenants are thin; no leakage protection (Medium-High)

**Where:** SPA §6.1 (ordinary course; "commercially reasonable efforts"; limited to amending, terminating, or waiving Material Contract rights and issuing equity); SPA §6.2 (access); SPA §6.3 (commercially reasonable efforts for consents); SPA §4.7 (ordinary course since December 31, 2025); SPA §2.4 (working capital adjustment only).

**Why it matters:** The covenants do not restrict dividends or distributions, new debt, capital expenditures, changes to pricing or minimum volume under the Supply Agreement, bonuses, settlements, Seller affiliate transactions, or payment of Seller's expenses by the Company.
