## 1. Scope, Sources, and Short Answer

**Documents reviewed (all four in the matter folder):**

- Stock Purchase Agreement dated as of September 21, 2026, with Disclosure Schedule ("**SPA**")
- Master Supply Agreement between Vantrell Polymer Corp. ("Vantrell") and Corbel Fluidics, Inc. ("Corbel" or the "Company"), dated December 15, 2021 ("**Supply Agreement**")
- Letter Agreement regarding indemnification dated September 21, 2026 ("**Side Letter**")
- Escrow Agreement among Halvorsen, Tarrant Peak Partners LP ("**Seller**") and Granite Ledger Trust Company, in draft with blanks ("**Escrow Agreement**")

Section references are to the document named in each entry. I have not reviewed anything outside the folder. The Parkway lease, the First Meridian revolver, the Ostrander distribution agreement, the financial statements, any Supply Agreement amendments, and the Vantrell technical data sheets are referenced but not in the folder (see Part 5).

**Short answer.** As drafted, the package leaves Halvorsen exposed in six critical areas:

1. **No Vantrell consent.** The stock sale triggers a Supply Agreement consent and termination right, but the Disclosure Schedule says "None" and the SPA has no covenant or closing condition for it.
2. **Supply contract may have lapsed.** The Supply Agreement expires December 31, 2026, unless Corbel gave an extension notice by about September 2, 2026. The folder does not show that notice was given.
3. **The Side Letter gutted the fundamental representations.** It removes Capitalization and Taxes from "Fundamental Representations," and it overrides the SPA.
4. **Conflicting law and forums.** The Side Letter selects Delaware law and Chancery; the SPA and Escrow Agreement select New York.
5. **Weak post-closing credit.** The Seller is a limited partnership with no guaranty, no net-worth covenant, and an 18-month escrow.
6. **Price adjustments rest on undefined terms.** Working capital and indebtedness are not defined, and no accounting principles are attached.

**Timing.** The outside date (SPA §8.1(d)) is December 15, 2026, 58 days from today. The Supply Agreement's Initial Term ends December 31, 2026, 16 days after the outside date.

## 3. Issues, in Priority Order

### A. Critical

**Issue 1. Vantrell change-of-control consent is missing from the deal documents.**

- **Where:** Supply Agreement §14.2; SPA Schedule 4.4 ("None"); SPA §4.4(c); SPA §6.3; SPA §7.2(c); SPA §3.2(g).
- **Why it matters:** Section 14.2 provides that any change of more than 50% of Corbel's voting stock "shall be deemed an assignment" that requires Vantrell's prior written consent. Without consent, Vantrell may terminate on 30 days' notice. This stock sale transfers 100%. Vantrell is the sole source for the Company's resin grades and is entitled to 100% of Corbel's U.S. requirements (Supply Agt §2.2; SPA Sch. 4.10, item 1). Schedule 4.4 says "None," so the §4.4(c) no-conflicts representation is inaccurate on its face. Because §6.3 and §7.2(c) only reach parties listed on Schedule 4.4, the SPA gives Halvorsen no covenant to seek consent and no condition requiring it. Vantrell will also have leverage to reprice, and the consent process could run past the outside date.
- **Fix:**
  - Add the Vantrell consent to Schedule 4.4 and correct §4.4(c).
  - Add a closing condition in §7.2(c) and a deliverable in §3.2.
  - Add a covenant in §6.3 that sets a timetable, gives Buyer participation rights, prohibits concessions without Buyer's consent, and allocates consent costs to Seller.
  - Evaluate whether consent should be a signing deliverable.

> **Proposed §7.2(c):** "Buyer shall have received the written consent of Vantrell Polymer Corp. under Section 14.2 of the Supply Agreement, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer, which (i) irrevocably waives any right of termination, repricing or modification arising from the transactions contemplated hereby; (ii) confirms that the Supply Agreement is in full force and effect and that the Term has been extended under Section 3.2 thereof; (iii) confirms that no default exists and no amount is past due thereunder; and (iv) confirms the Product prices then in effect under Schedule B."
>
> **Proposed §6.3 addition:** "Seller shall cause the Company, within five (5) Business Days after the date hereof, to request the consent described in Section 7.2(c) in a form approved by Buyer, shall keep Buyer informed of all communications with Vantrell, and shall not agree to any amendment, price change or other concession as a condition to such consent without Buyer's prior written consent. Seller shall bear any consent fee or other cost."

**Issue 2. The Supply Agreement may expire at the end of 2026, and the SPA does not address this.**

- **Why it matters:** Because the Side Letter outranks the SPA, Section 2 controls. Its effect:

Survival falls from statute of limitations plus 60 days (§9.1) to 18 months. The representation loses its uncapped treatment (§9.5(b)) and becomes subject to the Deductible and Cap.

  - Tax indemnity under §9.2(c) remains outside the limitations under §9.5(b), but its survival is unstated (Issue 8).
- **Fix:**
  - Delete Side Letter §2 and restore the four-section definition.
  - Better still, fold the Side Letter's substantive terms into SPA Article IX so there is one operative document.
  - Reverse the precedence in §11.11.

> **Proposed SPA §1.1:** "'Fundamental Representations' means the representations and warranties set forth in Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 (Capitalization) and 4.12 (Taxes)."

**Issue 4. Conflicting governing law and forums across the package.**

- **Where:** SPA §11.8 (New York law) and §11.9 (exclusive New York County courts, covering "the other Transaction Documents"); Side Letter §6.1 (Delaware law for "indemnification under the Purchase Agreement") and §6.2 (exclusive Court of Chancery, with a fallback to Delaware Superior Court or the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware "if, and only if," Chancery lacks subject-matter jurisdiction); Escrow Agreement §§8.1–8.2 (New York).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Indemnity claims, which is what Article IX is about, would be governed by Delaware law and heard in Chancery under the Side Letter. The same claim, or an escrow release dispute, could also be litigated in New York.
  - Chancery's jurisdiction over purely monetary claims is limited, so the fallback may apply. That fallback depends on the federal diversity analysis for the LP and LLC members, which the folder does not contain.
  - The SPA has no express pro-sandbagging clause. New York case law can limit recovery for breaches the buyer knew about before closing unless the buyer preserved its rights. Delaware law is generally more favorable to buyers on this point. This needs research before we respond.
- **Fix:** Conform the Side Letter to New York law and New York County courts. Add an express pro-sandbagging clause. Confirm that §11.9 governs all Transaction Documents.

> **Proposed Side Letter §6.1–6.2:** "This Letter Agreement shall be governed by the internal Laws of the State of New York. Any suit arising out of or relating to this Letter Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the courts specified in Section 11.9 of the Purchase Agreement."
>
> **Proposed SPA addition (§9.1):** "The rights of the Buyer Indemnitees under this Article IX shall not be affected by any investigation conducted by or on behalf of Buyer, or by any knowledge acquired (or capable of being acquired) at any time, whether before or after the execution of this Agreement or the Closing."

**Issue 5. Seller credit risk after escrow release.**

- **Where:** SPA §2.5 (escrow of $8,450,000 released at 18 months); SPA §9.1 (general survival 18 months; fundamentals to statute of limitations plus 60 days); SPA §9.6(b) (Seller pays directly beyond the escrow); SPA §11.4 (Seller cannot assign, but nothing restricts distributions); no Seller guaranty or net-worth covenant anywhere in the package; Escrow Agreement §4.4 and Side Letter §4.2 (release to Seller).

The escrow is the entire general cap (§9.5(a) cap = $8,450,000). Seller can distribute the proceeds and dissolve without restriction.

### B. High

**Issue 7. Escrow claim mechanics leave claims unprotected.**

- **Where:** Escrow §1.1 ("Pending Claim Amount" counts only Claim Notices "delivered to the Escrow Agent"); Escrow §4.1 (Buyer must concurrently send a copy to the Escrow Agent); Escrow §§4.2–4.4 (disbursement only on a Joint Written Instruction or a Final Order); Escrow §8.4 (notice effective only on actual receipt); SPA §2.5 ("then-pending claims," undefined); SPA §9.1 (claim survives if asserted in writing to the indemnifying party); Side Letter §§3.2, 4.1, 4.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - A claim properly noticed to Seller under §9.1 is not held back if Halvorsen's copy to the Escrow Agent is late or missed. The escrow would then release at 18 months.
  - The holdback is the Buyer's good-faith estimate, but there is no clear right to increase it after notice.
  - Seller has no deadline to object. If Seller simply does not sign, Halvorsen must litigate to obtain a Final Order before the funds move.
  - The SPA and the Escrow Agreement use different definitions of pending claims.
- **Fix:**
  - Include claims noticed to Seller in the Pending Claim Amount.
  - Permit Buyer to supplement the amount.
  - Add a deemed-acceptance mechanism with a 30-day objection window and a unilateral release right for undisputed claims.

> **Proposed Escrow §4.7 (new):** "If Seller does not deliver a written objection to a Claim Notice within thirty (30) days after receipt, Seller shall be deemed to have agreed to the amount claimed in it. Buyer may then deliver to the Escrow Agent a certificate to that effect, with a copy to Seller, and the Escrow Agent shall disburse that amount under Section 4.2 unless Seller delivers a written objection to the Escrow Agent within five (5) Business Days after receiving Buyer's certificate."
>
> **Proposed Escrow §1.1, "Pending Claim Amount":** "means, at any time, the aggregate amount of Losses (including Buyer's good-faith estimate of Losses not yet determinable) claimed in all claim notices delivered to Seller under Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement, or to the Escrow Agent under Section 4.1, before that time and not yet resolved and paid or released. Failure to deliver a copy to the Escrow Agent shall not affect the retention of the amount claimed if the copy is delivered within three (3) Business Days after the notice to Seller."

**Issue 8. Tax indemnity gaps.**

- **Where:** SPA §9.2(c) (covers Taxes "attributable to any taxable period ... ending on or before the Closing Date"); SPA §9.1 (survival covers representations and covenants only; §9.2(c) is neither); SPA §9.5(b) (§9.2(c) excluded from limits); SPA §10.1 (Seller prepares pre-closing returns due after closing); SPA §10.3 (cooperation only); SPA §4.12 (representation that there are no pending audits).

**Issue 9. Buyer's indemnity is uncapped and not subject to the Deductible.**

**Issue 10. Deductible, cap, and survival structure.**

- **Where:** SPA §9.5(a) (Deductible $633,750 = 0.75% of the Purchase Price; Seller liable "only for Losses in excess of the Deductible," i.e., a true deductible; Cap = 10% of the Purchase Price = $8,450,000); SPA §2.5 (escrow = $8,450,000); SPA §9.1 (18-month survival for general representations, including §4.15 Environmental).
- **Why it matters:**
  - A true deductible means the first $633,750 of Losses are Halvorsen's, even if the breach is large.
  - The cap equals the escrow, so the escrow fully secures the general cap. But general representations survive only 18 months, which may be short for environmental exposure at a manufacturing site.
- **Fix:** Propose a tipping basket. Client to set the appetite for a lower Deductible (for example, 0.5% of the Purchase Price, or $422,500, as an opening position). Extend survival of §§4.15 and product-related representations to at least 36 months, or add special indemnities for known issues. Confirm whether the cap should exceed the escrow if Seller's credit is not improved (Issue 5).

> **Proposed §9.5(a) (replacement):** "Seller shall be liable under Section 9.2(a) from the first dollar of Losses once the aggregate Losses in respect thereof exceed the Deductible of $[422,500] (the 'Deductible'), and Seller's aggregate liability under Section 9.2(a) shall not exceed the Cap."

**Issue 11. The "Fraud" carve-out is too narrow.**

- **Where:** SPA §1.1 ("Fraud" means "actual and intentional fraud under the Laws of the State of New York with respect to the making of the representations and warranties in Article IV or Article V"); SPA §9.5(b)(i); SPA §9.8.
- **Why it matters:** The definition excludes fraud in certificates delivered under §§3.2(d) and 7.2(d), fraud in the Disclosure Schedule (which is arguably not "Article IV"), and reckless misstatements. Because "Fraud" is the carve-out from both the cap and the exclusive-remedy clause, it matters.
- **Fix:** Expand to cover the Agreement, the Disclosure Schedule, the Transaction Documents, and certificates, and include recklessness (expect resistance on recklessness).

> **Proposed §1.1, "Fraud":** "means actual and intentional fraud, or fraud based on reckless indifference to the truth, under the Laws of the State of New York, in the making of any representation or warranty in this Agreement (including the Disclosure Schedule), any Transaction Document or any certificate delivered under this Agreement."

**Issue 13. Supply Agreement volume commitments, binding forecasts, and pricing.**

- **Where:** Supply Agreement §2.4 (1,800,000 kg annual minimum; shortfall payment of 8% of "the then-applicable average price per kilogram" times shortfall); §4.1 (first three months of each forecast binding at 80%); §5.2 (quarterly index adjustment, with a 40% factor under Sch.   - Post-closing integration plans (moving volume, dual-sourcing or reducing purchases) could trigger the shortfall payment.
  - Binding forecasts create commitments that are not clearly reflected in the balance sheet.
- **Fix:** Obtain 2026 actual volumes, forecasts, and shortfall history. Add a representation that no shortfall is accrued or unpaid. Include accrued shortfall in Indebtedness (Issue 6). Clarify the average-price definition. Seek an amendment that measures the minimum annually with carryforward, or reduces it.

**Issue 14. Supplier-failure remedies are thin.**

- **Where:** Supply Agreement §12.1 (excludes lost profits and consequential damages except for indemnities, breach of §10, gross negligence or willful misconduct); §12.2 (aggregate cap equals amounts paid and payable in the prior 12 months); §7.3 (late delivery remedy limited to expedited shipping or incremental cover cost); §2.3 (capacity commitment at 120% of forecast; 180 days' notice of discontinuation or process changes); §15.2 (allocation under force majeure); §13 (CGL insurance of $5,000,000 per occurrence and aggregate).
- **Why it matters:** If Vantrell stops supplying, Corbel's recovery is limited to cover costs and direct damages capped at 12 months' payments. The lost margin from a production stoppage, which is the real loss, is excluded. For a sole-source input, the cap could be far below the loss. The $5 million insurance limit should be checked against the products-liability exposure described in §11.2 with Halvorsen's risk team.

### C. Medium

**Issue 19. Losses definition and exclusive remedy.** The definition of "Losses" (SPA §1.1) is an inclusive list that does not expressly cover diminution in value, lost profits or consequential damages, and §9.8 makes Article IX exclusive. Seller will argue these are not recoverable. *Fix:* "Losses shall include diminution in value and lost profits, and consequential damages to the extent reasonably foreseeable, but shall exclude punitive damages except to the extent paid to a third party."

**Issue 20. Seller can control third-party defense too broadly.** SPA §9.4 lets the indemnifying party assume the defense of any third-party claim. A claim involving a key customer, supplier, governmental authority or tax matter should not be controlled by Seller. *Fix:*

> **Proposed §9.4 addition:** "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Buyer Indemnitees shall control the defense of any third-party claim that (i) is asserted by or against a customer, supplier or other counterparty under a Material Contract; (ii) seeks injunctive relief or involves a Governmental Authority or a criminal allegation; (iii) relates to Taxes, which shall be governed by Article X; or (iv) is reasonably expected to result in Losses exceeding the amount then recoverable from Seller."

**Issue 21. Escrow is first source for all claims.** SPA §9.6 requires that claims be satisfied "first, from the Escrow Amount," before direct recovery from Seller. For fundamental and tax claims that fall outside the escrow's purpose, this delays recovery. *Fix:* Escrow is first source only for §9.2(a) claims. Buyer may elect direct recovery for others.

**Issue 22. Interim covenants and schedule-update protection.**

- SPA §6.1(c) prohibits amending, terminating or waiving rights under Material Contracts, but it does not require Seller to cause Corbel to exercise or preserve the Supply Agreement extension option, to avoid binding forecast commitments, or to give Buyer notice of Vantrell communications.
- SPA §6.4 says notices do not cure breaches, but this applies only to MAE and §7.2 matters. The package does not say that Disclosure Schedule updates do not cure breaches.

*Fix:*

> **Proposed §6.1(e)–(f):** "(e) timely exercise, and not waive or permit to lapse, any option or right under the Supply Agreement (including Section 3.2 thereof), and not issue any Forecast, Purchase Order or pricing commitment outside the ordinary course without Buyer's consent; and (f) promptly deliver to Buyer copies of all written communications with Vantrell."
>
> **Proposed §6.4 addition:** "No update or supplement to the Disclosure Schedule after the date hereof, and no notice under this Section 6.4, shall cure any breach of any representation or warranty or affect any right of Buyer under this Agreement."

**Issue 23. No Seller restrictive covenants or release.** SPA Art. VI has only post-closing confidentiality (§6.6). There is no non-compete, non-solicit (customers, suppliers including Vantrell, or employees), Seller release of claims against the Company, or termination of affiliate arrangements. *Fix:* Add a three-year non-compete and non-solicit (scope to be set with the client, and subject to New York enforceability review in the sale-of-business context), plus a Seller release and termination of affiliate arrangements without liability to the Company.

**Issue 24. Working capital mechanics.** Beyond Issue 6: §2.3 gives Seller's estimate no Buyer review or revision right; §2.4 does not name the independent accounting firm; there is no interest on late payments. *Fix:* Buyer review and comment right on the §2.3 estimate; name a firm or selection mechanism in §2.4; add interest on late amounts.

**Issue 25. Outside date and regulatory approvals.** SPA §8.1(d) sets December 15, 2026, with no extension. The SPA does not address antitrust filings. The $84.5 million price should be checked against the current HSR size-of-transaction thresholds. The folder contains no antitrust analysis. *Fix:* Add an HSR covenant and condition if applicable, and an automatic extension of the outside date if only regulatory or consent conditions remain unsatisfied.

**Issue 26. Buyer financing risk.** SPA §5.4 has Halvorsen represent that it has sufficient funds, and there is no financing condition. If Halvorsen relies on third-party debt, the risk is Halvorsen's. *Fix:* Client call (Part 4).

**Issue 27. Materiality scrape is limited.** SPA §9.5(c) disregards materiality only "for purposes of calculating Losses ... (but not for determining whether any breach has occurred)." *Fix:* Apply a double materiality scrape for both breach and Losses.

**Issue 28. Escrow earnings, tax treatment, and an unfinished escrow form.**
