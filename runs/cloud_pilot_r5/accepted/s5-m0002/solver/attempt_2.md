### Issue 1: The 14% price increase is in effect and appears binding

**Where:** SA §5.3 (notice content, once-per-year limit, 60-day minimum); SA §5.4 (30-day objection, deemed acceptance); Amend. Recital C (July 30 notice, August 3 receipt, no objection delivered); SA Exh. B.

**Why it matters:**
- Receipt on August 3, 2026 started the 30-day objection period under SA §5.4, which ended **September 2, 2026**.
- Amend. Recital C states that no objection has been delivered. Under SA §5.4, the adjustment is therefore "deemed accepted" and takes effect on the date stated in the notice, **October 2, 2026**.
- The October 2 effective date is exactly 60 days after receipt. It satisfies the SA §5.3 floor, but with no margin. If receipt occurred later than August 3, the effective date would be non-compliant.
- If the full 14% applies, illustrative per-gross prices are approximately: A-1 $43.78 (from $38.40); A-2 $69.77 (from $61.20); A-3 $41.15 (from $36.10). Rounding must be checked against the notice.
- Under SA §5.3, any further adjustment is limited to once in any twelve-month period. There is no cap on the size of an adjustment (see Issue 10).

**Recommended fix:**
1. Obtain the July 30 notice and confirm three things: (a) whether it contains the "reasonable supporting detail of the cost changes" that SA §5.3 requires; (b) whether it was delivered by hand, nationally recognised overnight courier or certified mail, as SA §15.4 requires for "all notices"; and (c) the actual date and manner of receipt.
2. If the notice lacks cost detail or was not delivered in a permitted manner, there is a documented argument that it is not a valid Price Adjustment Notice, so the §5.4 clock never started. This is the only route I can identify to reverse the increase. Its strength is reduced by Amend. Recital C and by the September 21 execution of the Amendment (Issue 7). It should be assessed on the facts before we assert it.
3. Quantify exposure: 14% of Harrowgate's spend with Veltmar since October 2, 2026, plus forecast spend.
4. Do not rely on a late objection. The window closed September 2 (subject to confirming that no objection was sent by any permitted method).
5. Use the increase as leverage in the negotiation package described in Issue 10 (rebate, phase-in or cap).

**Open points:** Price Adjustment Notice; invoices since October 2; whether any communication about the price was sent to Veltmar (Client Q1, Q2, Q6).

### Issue 2: Amendment §2.1 narrows the liability carve-outs; recall costs and most indemnities are now capped

**Where:** Amend. §2.1 (replacement SA §11.2); Amend. §2.2 (applies to claims "arising on or after" September 21, 2026). Compare original SA §11.2(a)–(d), SA §11.1 (cap), SA §11.3 (consequential-loss and lost-profits exclusion), SA §10.2 (recall costs) and SA §9.1 (Supplier indemnity).

**Why it matters:** Original SA §11.2 removed four categories from both the cap in §11.1 and the exclusion in §11.3:
- (a) all Supplier indemnities under §9.1;
- (b) recall costs under §10;
- (c) breach of §12 (confidentiality);
- (d) gross negligence, fraud and wilful misconduct.

The amended §11.2 changes this in three ways:

- **Recall costs are removed entirely.** Under §10.2, Veltmar must reimburse notification, retrieval, transport, storage, destruction, replacement product, "lost beverage contents," and "customer credits and refunds." These now fall under the §11.1 cap (fees paid in the six months before the claim) and are exposed to the §11.3 exclusion of consequential damages and lost profits. "Lost beverage contents" and "customer credits and refunds" are the heads most likely to be characterised as consequential. This creates a direct conflict between §10.2 and §11.3, which Veltmar can exploit.
- **The §9.1 indemnity carve-out is limited "solely" to IP claims under §9.1(c).** Indemnities for product defects (§9.1(a)), personal injury and property damage (§9.1(b)) and Supplier breach or negligence (§9.1(d)) are now capped. The third-party claim most likely to be large, a bodily injury claim linked to a glass defect, is now capped at six months' fees.
- **The drafting is defective.** The phrase "which are excluded solely to third-party intellectual property claims" does not read cleanly. The most natural reading is that the indemnity is excluded from the carve-out except for IP claims. Harrowgate should not rely on an ambiguity argument.

Veltmar's own term sheet points the other way: TS §7.2 (non-binding) states that the cap "will not apply to Supplier's indemnity obligations, recall costs," and the original SA §11.2 matched that. The Amendment's recital D describes the changes only as "clarify and amend certain provisions," and does not flag this change.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Negotiate reinstatement of the original §11.2 carve-outs. At a minimum, recall costs and §9.1(a), (b) and (d) should be restored.
2. Proposed replacement §11.2:
   > "11.2 **Exclusions.** The limitation in Section 11.1 and the exclusion in Section 11.3 do not apply to (a) Supplier's indemnity obligations under Section 9.1; (b) Recall costs and other amounts payable by Supplier under Section 10, which the Parties agree are direct damages; (c) either Party's breach of Section 12; or (d) a Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct."
3. Add a clarifying sentence to §11.3:
   > "For clarity, amounts payable under Section 9.1 or Section 10.2, including the cost of lost beverage contents, replacement Products and customer credits and refunds, are direct damages and are not excluded by this Section 11.3."
4. Address the transition (Issue 9) in the same negotiation.
5. Whether the Amendment can be set aside or reformed on the facts (for example, mistake, misrepresentation or lack of authority) is a legal question for Delaware law. It is listed in Section 6 and is not analysed here.

**Open points:** Who requested the §11.2 change and whether Harrowgate was told it was a substantive change (Client Q3). Recent or pending claims and recall events (Client Q7).

### Issue 3: Supply failure: no cover right, lost profits excluded, and exclusivity blocks replacement purchasing

**Where:** SA §3.1 (exclusive source; no failure-to-supply exception); SA §3.3 (capacity at 115% of forecast; notice of shortfall only); SA §4.1 (five business days to accept or reject; no deemed acceptance); SA §4.5 (cancellation of late portion only); SA §11.3 (lost profits excluded); SA §15.7 (third-party purchasing only after 30 days of Supplier force majeure).

**Why it matters:**
- If Veltmar fails to deliver for reasons other than force majeure, Harrowgate's only express remedy is cancellation of the late portion (§4.5). Its main loss, which is lost production and sales from a stopped line, is a lost-profit or consequential loss excluded by §11.3.
- Harrowgate has no express right to buy replacement product. Buying elsewhere to keep its lines running would breach §3.1 and could support a material-breach termination under §13.1.
- Section 15.7 only permits third-party purchasing after a Supplier force majeure event lasts more than 30 days. A 29-day shortfall gives no relief.
- Section 3.3 requires only "prompt" notice of an anticipated shortfall. There is no remedy and no cure plan.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Add a new SA §3.4 (Supply Failure):
   > "3.4 **Supply Failure.** If Supplier fails to deliver any Products in full by the agreed delivery date, other than as excused under Section 15.7, Customer may purchase replacement containers from third parties for the shortfall without breaching Section 3.1. Supplier shall pay Customer, as direct damages, the amount by which the replacement price exceeds the price in Exhibit B, together with reasonable expediting, transport and line-change costs."
2. Propose that cover costs and lost profits from a Supplier supply failure be expressly carved out of §11.3, as a negotiating ask.
3. Add deemed acceptance to §4.1: "If Supplier does not accept or reject a Purchase Order within five (5) business days, the Purchase Order is deemed accepted."
4. Require a written recovery plan within five business days of any shortfall notice under §3.3.
5. Link to the termination right for chronic supply failure in Issue 22.

**Open points:** Supply performance history since March 2024 (late, short or rejected orders) (Client Q8).

### Issue 4: Supplier may assign to affiliates and successors without consent, with no conditions or exit right

**Where:** Amend. §4.1 (new SA §14.4, "notwithstanding Section 14.3"); SA §14.3 (original mutual consent requirement); TS §8.1 (mutual consent, binding on the definitive agreement); SA §1.1 (Affiliate defined by more than 50% voting equity).

**Why it matters:**
- Veltmar may assign "in whole or in part" to any Affiliate, or to a successor by merger, change of control or sale of the relevant business, without Harrowgate's consent. Notice is due only within 30 days after the assignment takes effect.
- Nothing requires the assignee to assume the obligations in writing, meet the §14.1 insurance requirement, or hold the QA §3.1 certifications.
- A partial assignment could split supply among entities with different quality systems and credit profiles.
- Harrowgate's exclusivity under SA §3.1 runs in full against any assignee.
- Harrowgate has no right to terminate on a change of control of Veltmar, or on an assignment to an unsuitable assignee.
- The clause is one-way. Section 14.3 still bars Harrowgate from making the same transfers without consent (see Issue 23).
- The mutual consent requirement in TS §8.1 was dropped without explanation.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Replace Amend. §4.1 with the following language, or an equivalent that keeps the same effect:
   > "14.4 **Assignment by Supplier.** Notwithstanding Section 14.3, Supplier may assign this Agreement in its entirety, without Customer's consent, only to (a) an Affiliate of Supplier or (b) a successor to Supplier by merger or by sale of all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, provided that: (i) Supplier gives Customer written notice at least sixty (60) days before the assignment takes effect (or, if prior notice is legally prohibited, as soon as it is permitted); (ii) the assignee agrees in writing with Customer to be bound by this Agreement and the Quality Agreement; (iii) the assignee maintains the insurance required by Section 14.1 and the certifications required by Section 3.1 of the Quality Agreement; and (iv) Supplier remains jointly and severally liable for all obligations under this Agreement. Supplier may not assign any part of this Agreement. If Supplier undergoes a change of control in favour of a [competitor of Customer], Customer may terminate this Agreement on [ninety (90)] days' written notice given within [one hundred eighty (180)] days after Customer receives notice of the change."
2. Define "competitor" (open point for the business team).
3. Propose the mutual revision in Issue 23 at the same time.

**Open points:** Whether any Veltmar reorganisation or sale is in progress (Client Q11).

### Issue 5: Exclusivity is far broader than the term sheet, and Harrowgate may already be in breach

**Where:** SA §3.1 (all glass and PET containers for any beverage product "manufactured, filled, bottled or packaged by or for Customer or its Affiliates, worldwide"; Customer "shall cause its Affiliates"); SA §1.1 (Affiliate); SA §1.3 and Exh. A (Products are three amber glass beer bottles only; no PET); TS §2.1 (amber glass bottles for beer brands at U.S. and Canadian facilities); TS §2.2 (all other containers free); SA §15.1 (supersedes term sheets); TS preamble (non-binding).

**Why it matters:**
- SA §3.1 requires Harrowgate to buy all glass and PET containers from Veltmar for any beverage product, worldwide. It covers products made "by or for" Harrowgate, which would include co-packers producing for Harrowgate, and products of any newly acquired Affiliate.
- The term sheet limited exclusivity to amber glass beer bottles at U.S. and Canadian facilities, and expressly left other containers free. SA §15.1 supersedes that. Harrowgate cannot rely on the term sheet to narrow §3.1.
- **Mismatch:** Veltmar's Products under SA §1.3 and Exh. A are three amber beer bottles. Veltmar has no stated obligation to supply PET or non-beer glass. Harrowgate could therefore be restricted from buying containers Veltmar is not obliged to offer.
- Any existing purchase of PET, non-amber or non-beer containers, or containers for non-US/Canada facilities, by Harrowgate, its Affiliates or its co-packers, may already be a breach. Section 3.1 has no carve-out for existing arrangements. A material breach not cured within 30 days could support termination under §13.1.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Restate §3.1 to match the term sheet's scope:
   > "3.1 **Exclusive Purchase.** During the Term, Customer shall purchase from Supplier all of its requirements for the Products listed in Exhibit A for beer beverage products filled or packaged by or for Customer at facilities located in the United States and Canada. Nothing in this Agreement restricts Customer or its Affiliates from purchasing any other container, including PET containers, containers of other materials or colours, containers for non-beer products, or containers for facilities outside the United States and Canada."
2. Add a Veltmar acknowledgement covering existing arrangements:
   > "Supplier acknowledges that Customer's and its Affiliates' purchases of containers from third parties as at the Amendment Date are permitted and do not breach Section 3.1."
3. Add a Supply Failure exception (Issue 3).
4. Confirm the current sourcing position with the client before raising the point with Veltmar.

**Open points:** Current container sourcing; list of Affiliates; co-packer arrangements; facility locations (Client Q5, Q12).

### Issue 10: Price adjustment mechanism has no cap, no cost audit and no downward adjustment, and an objection does not suspend the increase

**Where:** SA §5.3 (once per twelve months; "reasonable supporting detail"; 60-day minimum); SA §5.4 (30-day objection; 30-day negotiation with existing prices; referral to §15.6; deemed acceptance); SA §5.1. TS §§5.2–5.3 contain the same structure, which SA carried over.

**Why it matters:**
- Veltmar may raise prices by any amount, once a year, with only "reasonable" supporting detail. There is no index, no cap, no audit right and no obligation to reduce prices when costs fall.
- The objection period is 30 days, which is short for a glass producer's cost data to be reviewed.
- Existing prices apply during the 30-day negotiation only if Harrowgate objects in time. If the parties do not agree, a dispute goes to Wilmington courts under §15.6 while Harrowgate continues to pay the disputed price, or would have to withhold payment (see Issue 24).

**Recommended fix:**
1. Replace §5.3 with the following (bracketed terms for negotiation):
   > "5.3 **Price Adjustments.** Supplier may adjust the prices in Exhibit B not more than once in any twelve (12) month period, and no adjustment may exceed [●]% or the percentage change in [index] over the preceding twelve months, whichever is less. A Price Adjustment Notice must be delivered under Section 15.4 and must include a cost-by-cost breakdown, supporting invoices or index data, and the proposed effective date. No adjustment is effective unless it complies with this Section 5.3. If the costs on which an adjustment was based later decrease, Supplier shall reduce prices accordingly."
2. Add an audit right: "Customer may, once in any calendar year and on fifteen (15) business days' notice, audit Supplier's records supporting any adjustment."
3. Amend §5.4 so that an objection suspends the adjustment: "If Customer objects in time, the adjustment shall not take effect pending resolution. If the adjustment is later resolved in Customer's favour, Supplier shall refund any excess, with interest at [●]%."
4. Extend the objection period to sixty (60) days.

### Issue 11: Notice formalities for rejection, objection and claims

**Where:** SA §7.1 (rejection "by notice to Supplier"); SA §5.4 (objection by "written notice"); SA §15.4 (notices by hand, overnight courier or certified mail only; effective on receipt); QA §6.2 (nonconformance notice to Supplier's quality representative); QA §11.3 (routine quality communications may be exchanged by email); Amend. §3.1 (copy requirement).

**Why it matters:**
- A rejection under SA §7.1 is a "notice," so SA §15.4 applies. A rejection sent only by email to the quality team could be challenged as ineffective. If it is, Harrowgate's right to reject may be lost.
- QA §11.3 does not define "routine," and it is unclear whether a nonconformance notice under QA §6.2 is "routine."
- SA §7.1 does not say when a rejection must be given. The clause allows inspection "within thirty (30) days after delivery" and rejection "by notice," but does not say the notice must be given within that period. This matters for latent defects, such as glass inclusions found on the filling line later.
- Amend. §3.1 requires copies to Veltmar's General Counsel. Failure to copy does not affect effectiveness, so the copy requirement adds little protection either way.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Amend §7.1:
   > "Customer may reject any Products that do not conform to the Specifications by notice to Supplier given (a) within thirty (30) days after delivery or (b) for a nonconformity that could not reasonably have been detected within that period, within thirty (30) days after Customer becomes aware of it, and in any event within the period in Section 8.1. A notice under Section 6.2 of the Quality Agreement, sent by email to Supplier's quality representative, is effective as a notice under this Section 7.1 if it is acknowledged under Section 6.3 of the Quality Agreement."
2. Operationally, Harrowgate should send every rejection, objection, claim and default notice by courier with an email copy to Veltmar's General Counsel, and should maintain a compliance calendar for deadlines.

### Issue 12: Customer indemnity in §9.2 is not limited to Customer's fault

**Where:** SA §9.2 (Customer indemnity for claims "arising out of the beverage contents filled into the Products by Customer, or Customer's negligence or willful misconduct"); SA §9.1 (Supplier indemnity, which is limited "to the extent" caused by Supplier).

**Why it matters:** The Customer indemnity has no "to the extent" qualifier and no carve-out for claims caused by a Product defect. A consumer injury caused by a glass inclusion in beer could be argued to "arise out of the beverage contents," which would shift liability to Harrowgate. The Customer indemnity is currently subject to the §11.1 cap, because §11.2 carves out only Supplier indemnities. That benefits Harrowgate. Any restoration of the Supplier carve-outs should not extend to §9.2.

**Recommended fix:** Revise §9.2:
> "9.2 **By Customer.** Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Supplier ... from and against all losses ... arising from any third-party claim to the extent arising out of (a) the beverage contents filled into the Products by Customer, except to the extent the claim arises from a defect in or nonconformity of the Products or from any act or omission of Supplier; or (b) Customer's negligence or willful misconduct."

### Issue 13: Harrowgate's own defect costs are not clearly recoverable

**Where:** SA §7.2 (replacement or credit for rejected Products only); SA §9.1 (third-party claims only); SA §10.2 (recall costs only); SA §11.3 (consequential and lost-profits exclusion).

**Why it matters:** Harrowgate's own costs from defective Product (line stoppages, sorting and inspection, rework, write-off of filled product, spoiled inventory) are not expressly recoverable outside a formal recall. They may be argued to be consequential and excluded by §11.3.

**Recommended fix:** Add a new SA §7.3:
> "7.3 **Defect Costs.** Supplier shall reimburse Customer for its reasonable costs of sorting, inspecting, reworking or disposing of Products or beverage contents affected by a nonconformity, and for line stoppage caused by a nonconformity, whether or not a Recall occurs. These amounts are direct damages and are not excluded by Section 11.3."

This is a negotiating ask and should be pursued together with Issue 2.

### Issue 14: Liability cap base and amount

**Where:** SA §11.1 (cap: "fees paid by Customer in the six months before the claim arose").

**Why it matters:**
- "Paid" rather than "paid or payable" means that a claim arising early in the term, or during a period when Harrowgate is withholding amounts, produces a very small cap.
- The clause does not say whether the cap is aggregate for the life of the agreement or applies per claim.
- Six months of fees may be far below the exposure for a serious product claim or recall. With the Amendment, Veltmar's exposure is also capped, which limits recovery to insurance (see Issue 15) plus whatever the cap allows.

**Recommended fix:** Revise §11.1:
> "11.1 **Cap.** Except as provided in Section 11.2, each Party's total liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort or otherwise, shall not exceed the greater of (a) US$[●] and (b) the fees paid and payable by Customer in the twelve (12) months before the claim arose."

Clarify whether the cap is per claim or aggregate. Obtain spend data to set the figure (Client Q6).

### Issue 15: Insurance is not verified, is not clearly structured, and has no tail for claims-made policies

**Where:** SA §14.1 ($10,000,000 per occurrence, "products liability and product recall coverage," additional insured, for the Term and three years after); TS §6.3; QA §3.1 (certification of quality systems only).

**Why it matters:**
- It is not clear whether the $10,000,000 limit is shared between liability and recall coverage. A major recall could exhaust it, or leave Harrowgate with no recall cover.
- There is no obligation to deliver certificates or endorsements, no aggregate limit, no notice of cancellation, no primary or non-contributory wording, and no waiver of subrogation.
- If the policies are claims-made, a three-year tail after termination may not be enough for claims that surface later.
- Given the narrowed cap (Issue 2), Harrowgate's recovery for large losses depends heavily on this insurance.

**Recommended fix:** Add to SA §14.1:
> "Supplier shall deliver certificates of insurance and endorsements evidencing the coverage required by this Section 14.1 before first delivery and on each renewal. The coverage shall include (a) product recall coverage of not less than $[●] per occurrence and in the aggregate, separate from and in addition to the liability limit; (b) an aggregate limit of not less than $[●]; (c) primary and non-contributory cover, with a waiver of subrogation in favour of Customer; (d) at least thirty (30) days' notice to Customer of cancellation or material reduction; and (e) for any claims-made policy, a retroactive date no later than the Supply Agreement Effective Date and an extended reporting period of not less than three (3) years."

Ask the client what its own insurers require (Client Q9).

### Issue 16: Specification updates and change control

**Where:** QA §2.3 (Specifications may be updated by a written document "signed by an authorized quality representative of each Party"); QA §11.4 (QA amended only by both parties' signed instrument, "except that Specifications may be updated as provided in Section 2.3"); QA §§7.1–7.3 (change consent, 90-day notice, trial quantities); SA §6.3; SA §1.7 (Specifications defined by reference to Exh. A and the QA); SA §15.3.

**Why it matters:**
- QA §2.3 allows Specifications to be changed by quality representative sign-off, which is not defined as "authorised." That route bypasses the signed-amendment formalities of SA §15.3 and QA §11.4 and could change product obligations without senior sign-off.
- The change-control regime in QA §7 does not allocate the cost of requalifying Harrowgate's lines, does not say what happens if Harrowgate is silent, and does not say what happens if a change is implemented without consent.
- QA §7.3 allows Harrowgate to require trial quantities, but the cost is not allocated.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Revise QA §2.3:
   > "2.3 **Updates.** Specifications may be updated only by a written document signed by a [vice-president or equivalent officer] of each Party that expressly refers to this Section 2.3. No update that changes a dimension, capacity, finish, performance parameter or any filling-line compatibility characteristic is effective without Customer's written consent under Section 7 of this Quality Agreement."
2. Add to QA §7:
   > "Supplier shall bear all costs of any change, including validation, trial quantities, line trials and requalification of Customer's lines. Customer's silence is not consent. If Customer does not consent, Supplier shall continue to supply to the existing Specifications. A change implemented without Customer's written consent is a Nonconformance."

### Issue 17: Quality standards: undefined "Minor Defect" and AQL without a sampling plan

**Where:** QA §1.2 (defines Critical Defect, Major Defect, Nonconformance; does not define "Minor Defect"); QA §4.3 (AQL table includes "Minor Defect" at 2.5; refers to ANSI/ASQ Z1.4, General Inspection Level II, with "0.0 (zero tolerance)" for Critical Defects); QA §4.2; QA §6.1.

**Why it matters:**
- "Minor Defect" appears in the AQL table but is not defined, which invites dispute over what is a minor, major or critical defect.
- ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 is a sampling-plan standard. The QA does not specify lot sizes, sample sizes or accept and reject numbers. A "zero tolerance" AQL is not workable without a stated accept number of zero for a stated sample size.
- QA §4.3 governs Veltmar's release of Lots. It does not state the standard by which Harrowgate accepts or rejects Lots on incoming inspection.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Add to QA §1.2: "(f) 'Minor Defect' means a defect, other than a Critical Defect or Major Defect, that does not affect the function or appearance of the Product in a way that would materially impair its use." (Confirm with Harrowgate's quality team.)
2. Specify sample sizes and accept and reject numbers for each Specification, by reference to the Z1.4 table, in a new Schedule 2.
3. State that Supplier's release testing under §4.2 and §4.3 does not limit Customer's rights under §6.1 (this is largely present already and should be kept).
4. Obtain Harrowgate's quality team's view on classification and sampling (Client Q14).

### Issue 18: Critical defect response does not extend to delivered lots or field retrieval

**Where:** QA §6.4 (24-hour notice; hold of affected Lots "in its possession"); QA §9.2 (recall information within 24 hours of request); QA §5.3 (traceability); SA §10.1 (recall triggered by governmental requirement or Customer's reasonable determination); SA §10.2 (recall cost recovery).

**Why it matters:** QA §6.4 requires Veltmar to notify Harrowgate of a Critical Defect within 24 hours and to hold affected Lots in its own possession. It does not require Veltmar to identify affected Lots by number and delivery, to retrieve or quarantine Lots already delivered to Harrowgate, or to pay for retrieval unless a formal Recall is triggered.

**Recommended fix:** Add to QA §6.4:
> "Within twenty-four (24) hours after such notice, Supplier shall identify by Lot number and production line each affected Lot and each delivery containing it (in accordance with Section 5.3), and shall cooperate in retrieving or quarantining any affected Lot delivered to Customer. Retrieval, quarantine and related costs are recoverable under Section 10.2 of the Supply Agreement whether or not a Recall is declared."

### Issue 19: FCA delivery puts transit breakage on Harrowgate; title and risk language is unclear

**Where:** SA §4.3 (FCA Supplier's facility; delivery to a carrier designated by Customer); SA §4.4 (title and risk pass "on delivery in accordance with Section 4.3"); SA §8.1(b) (warranty against defects in materials and workmanship); SA Exh. B (prices FCA).

**Why it matters:** Harrowgate selects the carrier and bears transit risk on glass products from the point of handover. It is not clear whether breakage in transit is a "defect" covered by the warranty or a risk that has already passed to Harrowgate. Freight is also outside the quoted price.

**Recommended fix:** This is partly commercial. Options include DAP delivery to Harrowgate's facilities, or retaining FCA with express wording:
> "Title and risk of loss pass to Customer when the Products are loaded onto the carrier designated by Customer at Supplier's facility, provided the Products were packed in accordance with Section 5.1 of the Quality Agreement. Breakage or damage occurring before loading is Supplier's responsibility, whether or not it is a defect under Section 8.1."

Ask the client for its freight arrangements and breakage history (Client Q15).

### Issue 20: Force majeure scope and duration

**Where:** SA §15.7 (any event "beyond its reasonable control," other than payment obligations; third-party purchasing only after 30 days of Supplier force majeure).

**Why it matters:** Supplier-side events, such as raw material or energy shortages, failure of Supplier's own subcontractors, or labour disputes at Veltmar, may be argued to be force majeure. There is no termination right if a force majeure event continues for a long period.

**Recommended fix:** Add to §15.7:
> "Events beyond a Party's reasonable control do not include shortages of raw materials, energy or components, failure of that Party's subcontractors or suppliers, or labour disputes involving that Party's own workforce, unless the event affects the relevant industry generally. If a force majeure event affecting Supplier continues for more than [ninety (90)] days, Customer may terminate the affected Products without liability."

Consider reducing the 30-day period to [15] days (business decision).

### Issue 21: Dispute resolution: Delaware forum; no interim relief; 30-day pre-suit negotiation

**Where:** SA §15.5 (Delaware law); SA §15.6 (30-day senior executive negotiation; exclusive jurisdiction of state or federal courts in Wilmington, Delaware); Amend. §6.2; QA §11.5.

**Why it matters:**
- Harrowgate is based in Pennsylvania. Disputes must be brought in Wilmington, Veltmar's home jurisdiction.
- Urgent relief (for example, to compel supply or to stop misuse of confidential information) must wait 30 days.
- No carve-out is made for injunctive or other interim relief.

**Recommended fix:** Add to §15.6:
> "Notwithstanding the first sentence of this Section 15.6, either Party may at any time seek immediate injunctive or other interim relief, including to compel the supply of Products or to prevent the misuse of Confidential Information, in any court of competent jurisdiction."

Discuss with the partner whether to propose a Pennsylvania or neutral forum, or arbitration, and whether Delaware counsel is needed (Section 6).

### Issue 22: Termination rights are thin

**Where:** SA §13.1 (material breach with 30-day cure); SA §13.2 (insolvency); SA §13.3 (effect of termination); SA §15.7.

**Why it matters:** Harrowgate has no right to terminate for:
- repeated Critical Defects or quality failures;
- chronic late or short deliveries;
- a recall attributable to the Products;
- an assignment in breach of §14.4 (as revised);
- prolonged force majeure (Issue 20); or
- Veltmar's change of control in favour of a competitor (Issue 4).

"Material breach" is undefined. The 30-day cure applies even to breaches that cannot be cured, such as a Critical Defect that has caused injury.

**Recommended fix:** Add a new SA §13.4:
> "13.4 **Additional Termination Rights.** Customer may terminate this Agreement on written notice, without any cure period, if (a) Supplier delivers three (3) or more Lots containing Critical Defects in any twelve (12) month period; (b) Supplier fails to deliver [●]% or more of firm Purchase Order quantities on time in any two (2) consecutive months; (c) a Recall results from a defect in or nonconformity of the Products; or (d) Supplier assigns or purports to assign this Agreement in breach of Section 14.4."

### Issue 23: Harrowgate's own assignment flexibility, including by operation of law

**Where:** SA §14.3 (no assignment "whether by operation of law or otherwise" without consent; attempted assignment void).

**Why it matters:**
- Harrowgate cannot assign to an Affiliate or to a successor without Veltmar's consent.
- Because §14.3 covers assignments by operation of law, a merger or other corporate change could be argued to need consent. Whether a change of control is an assignment under Delaware law is a question for Section 6.
- The asymmetry with the revised §14.4 (Issue 4) increases the imbalance.

**Recommended fix:** Make §14.3 mutual, with the same carve-outs as Veltmar's revised §14.4:
> "Either Party may assign this Agreement without the other Party's consent to an Affiliate or to a successor by merger, consolidation or sale of all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, provided that the assignee assumes this Agreement in writing and the assigning Party gives written notice within thirty (30) days. A change of control of a Party is not an assignment for purposes of this Section 14.3."

Ask whether any corporate transaction is planned (Client Q10).
