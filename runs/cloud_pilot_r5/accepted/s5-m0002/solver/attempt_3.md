## 1. Bottom Line

Harrowgate's position is weaker than the original Supply Agreement suggests, mainly because of Amendment No. 1 (dated September 21, 2026). Four matters need attention now:

1. **Amendment No. 1 removes Harrowgate's protection for recall costs and for most of Veltmar's indemnity from the liability cap and the consequential-damages waiver** (Issues 5–6). This is the largest exposure in the file.
2. **Veltmar's 14% price increase took effect October 2, 2026.** On the documents we have, Harrowgate did not object within the 30-day window, which closed September 2, 2026. Harrowgate's own signature on the Amendment recites that fact (Issues 2–3).
3. **Harrowgate missed the non-renewal window for the Initial Term.** Unless a notice was sent in the window, the Agreement will renew automatically for five years, to approximately March 1, 2032 (Issue 4).
4. **Exclusivity is far broader than the term sheet**, reaching all glass and PET containers for any beverage, worldwide, by Harrowgate and its Affiliates. It comes with no supply guarantee (Issues 14–15). Harrowgate may already be in breach. We need facts from the client to confirm this.

We also need to verify execution. Every document in the folder has blank signature blocks (Issue 1).

**Scope and limits.** This memo is based only on the four documents in the matter folder: the Supply Agreement ("SA"), the Term Sheet ("TS"), Amendment No. 1 ("Amend.") and the Quality and Specifications Agreement ("QA"). We have not seen the Price Adjustment Notice, any Purchase Orders, invoices, insurance certificates, correspondence or purchase data. Where a conclusion depends on those, it is flagged. Legal points under Delaware law and the UCC should be confirmed before the advice goes to the client.

**Issue 2: The 14% price increase is effective and, on the record, deemed accepted (High)**

- **Where:** Amend. Recital C (notice dated July 30, 2026; received August 3, 2026; 14% increase effective October 2, 2026; no objection delivered); SA §5.3 (60-day minimum and supporting-detail requirement); SA §5.4 (30-day objection window; deemed acceptance); SA Exh. If the notice lacked adequate support, the argument is that it was not a valid Price Adjustment Notice and the objection clock never started. This argument is not strong on the present record and must be tested against the actual notice.

- **Recommended fix:**

  - Confirm that there were no other price adjustments in the preceding 12 months (SA §5.3 "once in any twelve month period").

  - Negotiate prospectively (see Issue 12). If the increase stands, seek a retroactive or staged arrangement as a commercial matter.
- **Open questions:** Was the 14% increase applied to all three SKUs? Does Veltmar's cost support tie to the increase? Did Harrowgate receive the notice through the correct channel?

**Issue 3: Recitals and ratification may be treated as admissions (High)**

- **Where:** Amend. Recital C; Amend. §5.1 (Agreement "ratified and confirmed"); Amend. §5.3 ("No Other Waiver").
- **Why it matters:**
  - Recital C states as fact that the notice was received on August 3, that no objection was delivered, and that the notice is a Price Adjustment Notice under SA §5.3. If Harrowgate signed the Amendment, Veltmar will argue these are binding admissions.
  - Amend. §5.1 ratifies the whole Agreement, including the exclusivity in SA §3.1, the auto-renewal in SA §2.2 and the price mechanism in SA §5. Veltmar may argue that Harrowgate has waived any earlier objections, including any argument that exclusivity was breached or that a prior notice was defective.
  - Amend. §5.3 helps. It says nothing in the Amendment operates as a waiver "except as expressly set out in this Amendment." Recital C is arguably not an express waiver, but the point is contestable.
  - SA §15.3 provides that no waiver is effective unless in writing and signed by the waiving Party. The Amendment is signed (if executed) and is in writing, so §15.3 does not protect Harrowgate here.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Before further correspondence, decide whether Harrowgate will challenge the price increase or the earlier-breach position. Those decisions should inform whether to rely on the Amendment at all.
  - Going forward, any further amendment should delete Recital C and §5.1's general ratification. Replace with: "Except as expressly amended, the Agreement remains in effect. Nothing in this Amendment waives or affects any claim, defence or right of either Party arising before the Amendment Date, including with respect to any Price Adjustment Notice."
  - Proposed language:
    > "5.1 Ratification. Except as expressly amended by this Amendment, the Agreement remains in effect. Nothing in this Amendment or in the Recitals constitutes an admission, acknowledgement or waiver by Customer in respect of any Price Adjustment Notice, any breach of the Agreement, or any other claim, defence or right arising before the Amendment Date, all of which are reserved."

### Part B: Liability, Indemnity, Recall and Insurance

**Issue 5: Amendment No. 1 removes recall and general indemnity protection from the cap and consequential-damages waiver (Critical)**

- **Where:**
  - Original SA §11.2 excluded from the cap (§11.1) and the consequential waiver (§11.3): (a) Supplier's indemnity under §9.1; (b) Recall costs under §10; (c) breach of §12; (d) gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct.
  - Amend. §2.1 deletes §11.2 and replaces it. The new list keeps only (a) §9.1 indemnity, "excluded solely to third-party intellectual property claims under Section 9.1(c)"; (b) breach of §12; and (c) gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct. Recall costs (former (b)) are removed.
  - Amend. §2.2 applies the change to "all claims arising on or after the Amendment Date."
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Recall costs.** SA §10.2 gives Harrowgate reimbursement of recall costs, including notification, retrieval, replacement product, "lost beverage contents," and customer credits and refunds. Those amounts are now subject to the cap in SA §11.1 (fees paid in the six months before the claim) and the consequential waiver in SA §11.3. Recall costs are likely to be argued as consequential, especially lost beverage contents and customer credits. Reimbursement under §10.2 could be largely illusory.
  - **Product-injury and defect indemnity.** Under SA §9.1(a), (b) and (d), Veltmar indemnifies third-party claims for defects, personal injury, property damage and breach. Those indemnities are now capped at six months' fees, and the amended §11.2 also brings them within the consequential waiver. Only IP indemnity (§9.1(c)) remains outside the cap.
  - **Size of the gap.** The cap equals fees paid in the prior six months, which depends on Harrowgate's volumes (data not in the folder). Veltmar's insurance is required at $10,000,000 per occurrence (SA §14.1). A serious consumer-injury or multi-line recall could greatly exceed the cap, leaving Harrowgate to absorb the excess.
  - **Harrowgate's own exposure.** As the brand owner, Harrowgate faces consumer claims and recall costs even where the root cause is a glass defect. The indemnity and recall provisions exist to shift that risk. The amendment shifts it back.
  - **Asymmetry.** The cap and waiver remain mutual, so Harrowgate's own exposure to Veltmar is also capped. The asymmetry is in Harrowgate's largest risk, not in Veltmar's.
- **Recommended fix:** Reject the Amendment's §2.1 or require it to be renegotiated. At minimum, restore the original §11.2 carve-outs for §9.1 indemnity (all paragraphs) and recall costs under §10, and add insurance breach as a carve-out. Proposed language:
  > "11.2 Exclusions. The limitation in Section 11.1 and the exclusion in Section 11.3 do not apply to (a) Supplier's indemnity obligations under Section 9.1; (b) Recall costs under Section 10, which the Parties agree are direct damages; (c) either Party's breach of Section 12; (d) Supplier's breach of Section 14.1, in the amount of insurance that should have been maintained; or (e) a Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct."
- **Negotiation note:** Veltmar drafted this change alongside the price increase. Harrowgate should consider whether it gave anything in return and whether it had counsel's review at the time. See Issue 1 and the information requests in Part 4.

**Issue 6: The "solely to IP" wording is ambiguous, and the amendment applies to claims arising after the Amendment Date (High)**

- **Where:** Amend. §2.1 (new §11.2(a): "Supplier's indemnity obligations under Section 9.1, which are excluded solely to third-party intellectual property claims under Section 9.1(c)"); Amend. §2.2.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The most natural reading is that only IP indemnity claims are outside the cap. Veltmar could argue that the sentence is a drafting error with some other meaning. Harrowgate should not assume either reading will prevail; an ambiguous limitation clause is a litigation risk.
  - Under SA §15.5 the contract is governed by Delaware law. Ambiguity may be resolved with extrinsic evidence, including the negotiation history, so the drafting history matters (see Issue 1).
  - Amend. §2.2 applies the amended cap to "all claims arising on or after the Amendment Date." Products delivered in 2024–2026 are still within the 18-month warranty period (SA §8.1), and product in the field may cause claims after September 21, 2026. Those claims would fall under the reduced protection even though the product was sold under the original terms. "Arising" is undefined: it could mean when the defect occurred, when the product was delivered, when the loss occurred or when a claim was made.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Clarify the IP-only language, or delete it and restore the §9.1 carve-out.
  - Limit any change to Products delivered after the Amendment Date. Proposed language:
    > "2.2 The amended Section 11.2 applies only to Products delivered on or after the Amendment Date. Claims relating to Products delivered before the Amendment Date are governed by Section 11.2 as in effect immediately before the Amendment Date."
  - Confirm with the client who drafted the Amendment, so that the contra proferentem position is known.

**Issue 7: Consequential-damages waiver blocks recovery for supply failure and cover costs (High)**

- **Where:** SA §11.3 (no liability for indirect, incidental, special, consequential or punitive damages or lost profits); SA §4.5 (late delivery: cancellation only, "in addition to its other remedies"); SA §3.3 (capacity, with no remedy for shortfall).
- **Why it matters:** Harrowgate's losses from a supply failure, such as line stoppages, lost sales, and the higher cost of emergency sourcing, are likely to be characterized as consequential or lost profits. Harrowgate is also bound by exclusivity (SA §3.1), so it cannot easily buy elsewhere to cover. The result is that Harrowgate can be forced to rely on a supplier that is not contractually obliged to supply it adequately, with no meaningful damages if the supplier fails.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a direct-damages clause and a cover right. Proposed language:
  > "11.4 Direct Damages. For clarity, the following are direct damages recoverable by Customer: (a) amounts payable under Section 9.1 or 9.2; (b) Recall costs under Section 10.2; (c) if Supplier fails to deliver Products as required under this Agreement, the reasonable cost of replacement Products purchased from a third party in excess of the price under Exhibit B, plus reasonable expedited freight; and (d) reasonable costs of inspecting, sorting, reworking or disposing of nonconforming Products."

**Issue 8: The cap is measured by "fees paid" and carve-outs are too narrow (Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §11.1 (cap is "fees paid by Customer in the six months before the claim arose"); SA §11.2 (carve-outs).
- **Why it matters:**
  - "Fees" is undefined. It is unclear whether it means Product prices only, or includes other charges. "Paid" differs from "payable" and could produce a very low cap early in the relationship, or after a period of disputed invoices (SA §5.2 allows withholding disputed amounts).
  - The cap applies to "total liability ... whether in contract, tort or otherwise." It does not carve out Veltmar's insurance obligations (SA §14.1), its records obligations (SA §10.3) or its cooperation obligations in recall (QA §9.2). Harrowgate's remedy for breach of those may be capped.
  - The cap does not limit injunctive relief. Veltmar could seek an injunction to enforce exclusivity, so Harrowgate's exposure to Veltmar is not fully capped.
- **Recommended fix:** Define "fees" as "amounts payable for Products under Exhibit B, excluding amounts withheld in good faith under Section 5.2." Replace "paid" with "paid or payable." Add carve-outs for breach of §14.1 and §10.3. Add a sentence stating that the cap does not limit equitable relief.

**Issue 9: Customer indemnity for "beverage contents" is overbroad (Medium–High)**

- **Where:** SA §9.2 (Customer indemnifies Supplier for third-party claims "arising out of the beverage contents filled into the Products by Customer, or Customer's negligence or willful misconduct"); SA §9.1(b) (Supplier indemnifies personal injury caused by Products).
- **Why it matters:** Read literally, §9.2 may require Harrowgate to indemnify Veltmar for a consumer injury that was caused by a glass defect, because the injury arose "out of" the beverage contents. §9.2 has no carve-out for Supplier fault, unlike §9.1 (which uses "to the extent"). The two indemnities could conflict. The §9.2 indemnity is also in the cap (§11.1), which is favourable to Harrowgate, but the cap may fall away if the client later negotiates it.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a "to the extent" qualifier and a carve-out for Supplier fault. Proposed language:
  > "9.2 By Customer. Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Supplier ... from and against ... claims to the extent arising out of the beverage contents filled into the Products by Customer, or Customer's negligence or willful misconduct, but excluding any claim to the extent arising out of a defect in or nonconformity of the Products, Supplier's negligence or willful misconduct, or any matter for which Supplier is responsible under Section 9.1."

**Issue 10: Recall procedures are underdeveloped (Medium–High)**

- **Where:** SA §10.1 (Recall triggered by government requirement or Customer's reasonable determination; "Customer shall notify Supplier," with no timing); SA §10.2 (reimbursement "to the extent caused by" a defect); SA §9.3 (indemnifying party controls defence and settlement); QA §9.2 (Supplier to provide records within 24 hours).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Under SA §9.3, the indemnifying party (Veltmar) may control defence and settlement of any third-party claim, which could include a regulatory or consumer claim arising from a recall. Harrowgate, as brand owner, needs control over consumer-facing communications and regulatory dealings.
  - SA §10.1 has no notice timing. Delay in notice could be argued to increase costs, and Veltmar could assert a failure to mitigate.
  - "To the extent caused by" will be contested. Nothing sets out a procedure to determine causation, a standard for allocating recall costs, or a process for disputes.
  - SA §10.3 requires traceability records for five years but does not require them to be available within a time limit (QA §9.2 supplies 24 hours, which helps).
- **Recommended fix:** Add a recall clause. Proposed language:
  > "10.4 Recall Control. Customer shall determine the content, timing and manner of any Recall communication and regulatory correspondence, after consulting Supplier where reasonably practicable. Section 9.3 does not apply to Recalls. Customer shall notify Supplier of a Recall as soon as reasonably practicable. A delay in notice reduces Supplier's obligations only to the extent Supplier shows actual prejudice. Where Supplier disputes causation, the Parties shall first attempt to resolve the dispute under Section 15.6, and Supplier shall pay undisputed recall costs within thirty (30) days of invoice."

**Issue 11: Insurance terms are ambiguous and unverified (Medium–High)**

- **Where:** SA §14.1 ($10,000,000 per occurrence; CGL "including products liability and product recall coverage"; additional insured; three-year tail); TS §6.3 (product liability and product recall insurance of not less than $10,000,000 per occurrence; additional insured).
- **Why it matters:**
  - §14.1 describes recall coverage as part of CGL. Recall coverage is usually a separate policy with different terms. We cannot tell from the text whether recall coverage is included, and at what limit.
  - "Per occurrence" with no aggregate may leave Harrowgate exposed to multiple occurrences in one year.
  - There is no requirement for primary and non-contributory cover, no notice of cancellation, no certificate requirement and no waiver of subrogation.
  - The $10,000,000 figure is in the term sheet and the SA, but there is no evidence in the file that Veltmar holds the cover.
- **Recommended fix:** Require separate products-liability and recall coverage, each with stated per-occurrence and aggregate limits. Proposed language:
  > "14.1 Insurance. During the Term and for three (3) years after its end, Supplier shall maintain, with insurers rated at least A- (AM Best) or equivalent: (a) products liability insurance with limits of not less than $10,000,000 per occurrence and $[●] in the aggregate; and (b) product recall insurance with limits of not less than $[●] per occurrence and in the aggregate. Each policy shall name Customer and its Affiliates as additional insureds, be primary and non-contributory, and provide at least thirty (30) days' notice to Customer of cancellation or material reduction. Supplier shall deliver certificates and endorsements before the first delivery under this Agreement, on each renewal, and on request."
- **Open questions:** Harrowgate's own insurance requirements; whether recall insurance is in place; the size of Harrowgate's exposure, which determines the aggregate amount.

**Issue 12: Price adjustment mechanics lack a cap, cost standards and fair deemed-acceptance rules (High)**

- **Where:** SA §5.3 (once per 12 months; written notice with "reasonable supporting detail"; 60-day lead time); SA §5.4 (30-day objection; negotiation for 30 days; deemed acceptance); SA §15.6 (dispute resolution); SA Exh. B (prices "subject to adjustment under Section 5").
- **Why it matters:**
  - There is no percentage cap, no index, no definition of "reasonable supporting detail" and no audit right over Veltmar's cost data. Veltmar can raise prices once a year by any amount.
  - The 30-day objection window is short and starts on receipt. The objection must be given under SA §15.4 (hand, courier or certified mail), so an email objection is not valid.
  - Deemed acceptance (SA §5.4) means Harrowgate loses the right to contest a price increase if it misses the window.
  - After a failed negotiation, the dispute goes to the courts in Wilmington (SA §15.6). Harrowgate would be litigating a price dispute with its supplier.
  - Exhibit B is not updated to reflect the 14% increase. The invoiced prices and Exhibit B may not match.
- **Recommended fix:** Replace §5.3 and §5.4. Proposed language:
  > "5.3 Price Adjustments. Supplier may adjust prices not more than once in any twelve (12) month period, and no adjustment may exceed [●]% in aggregate in any twelve (12) month period. Each Price Adjustment Notice shall include itemized cost detail for each cost component relied on, with reasonable supporting documentation, and shall be effective only on the later of the date stated and sixty (60) days after receipt. A Price Adjustment Notice that does not comply with this Section 5.3 is of no effect.
  > 5.4 Objection. Customer may object to a Price Adjustment Notice within sixty (60) days after receipt, by notice given under Section 15.4 or by email to Supplier's designated commercial contact. If Customer objects, existing prices continue and the Parties shall negotiate in good faith for thirty (30) days. If no agreement is reached, the matter may be referred to an independent accountant for determination of the cost change, and any adjustment found to be justified shall apply from the effective date with a true-up of any amounts paid. No adjustment takes effect by silence."
- **Also:** Exhibit B should be updated in writing to reflect the adjusted prices, to avoid invoicing disputes. This can be done in the same amendment.

### Part C: Exclusivity, Supply and Term

**Issue 13: Exclusivity exceeds the term sheet and reaches Affiliates and PET (Critical)**

- **Where:**
  - SA §3.1: "all glass and PET containers for any beverage product manufactured, filled, bottled or packaged by or for Customer or its Affiliates, worldwide," with Harrowgate required to "cause its Affiliates not to" purchase third-party containers.
  - TS §2.1: exclusivity limited to "amber glass bottles for Customer's beer brands at its U.S. and Canadian facilities."
  - TS §2.2: Harrowgate "remains free to source ... all other containers, including containers for non-beer products, containers made from materials other than amber glass, and containers for facilities outside the United States and Canada."
  - SA §15.1: the SA supersedes the term sheet.
  - SA Exh. A and §1.3: "Products" are only three amber glass bottles.
- **Why it matters:**
  - The SA is far broader than the term sheet. Harrowgate negotiated a narrow exclusivity (amber beer glass, US and Canada) and the SA imposes a worldwide exclusivity covering all glass and PET for any beverage.
  - Under SA §15.1, the term sheet cannot be used to narrow the SA's exclusivity. Harrowgate's reliance on the term sheet is misplaced.
  - "By or for Customer or its Affiliates" captures co-packers producing for Harrowgate, and Affiliates Harrowgate may acquire after signing. Affiliates are defined as entities under common control or more than 50% ownership (SA §1.1). An acquisition by Harrowgate's parent or a sister company would bring its containers under the restriction.
  - PET containers are covered by §3.1 but are not Products. Veltmar has no PET product, specification or price under the SA. Harrowgate cannot buy PET from Veltmar on defined terms, but is barred from buying it elsewhere.
  - **Potential existing breach.** If Harrowgate buys PET, closures, non-amber glass, non-beer containers, or containers for facilities outside the US and Canada, or uses a co-packer that sources its own glass, Harrowgate may already be in breach of SA §3.1 from March 1, 2024. Breach gives Veltmar a termination right after a 30-day cure period (SA §13.1), and a damages claim capped by SA §11.1 but not limited for injunctive relief.
  - The exclusivity also raises a possible antitrust question (see Issue 26).
- **Recommended fix:**
  - First, confirm current purchasing (Part 4). Do not make any statement to Veltmar about compliance until this is done.
  - Narrow §3.1 to the term sheet scope or a negotiated scope. Proposed language:
    > "3.1 Exclusive Purchase. During the Term, Customer shall purchase from Supplier all of its requirements of the Products listed in Exhibit A for beverage products filled at Customer's facilities in the United States and Canada. Nothing in this Agreement restricts Customer or any of its Affiliates from purchasing any other containers, including PET containers, closures, containers for non-beer beverages, containers of materials other than amber glass, or containers for facilities outside the United States and Canada. Customer shall not cause any entity that it directly controls to breach this Section 3.1. Entities that become Affiliates of Customer after the Effective Date are excluded from this Section 3.1 for twelve (12) months after they become Affiliates."
  - Consider whether the "by or for" language should be replaced with "filled by Customer" to exclude co-packers. This is a commercial point for the client.

**Issue 14: Exclusivity has no supply assurance or remedy (High)**

- **Where:** SA §3.3 (Supplier "shall maintain" capacity of 115% of forecast; "shall notify" of shortfalls; no remedy); SA §4.1 (Supplier may accept or reject POs within five business days); SA §4.5 (cancellation only after delivery is five business days late); SA §15.7 (force majeure lets Customer buy elsewhere only after 30 days).
- **Why it matters:** Harrowgate is bound to buy exclusively from Veltmar but has no remedy if Veltmar cannot supply, other than cancellation for late delivery. Veltmar's capacity obligation is in form only. Harrowgate could have a production stoppage while still being unable to buy elsewhere until a force majeure event has run for 30 days.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a supply-performance clause and an exclusivity-release right. Proposed language:
  > "3.4 Supply Performance. Supplier shall deliver at least [98]% of the quantity of Products in each accepted Purchase Order on or before the agreed delivery date, measured monthly by line item. If Supplier fails to meet this standard in any two consecutive months or in any three months in a rolling twelve-month period, Customer may, without breach of Section 3.1, purchase affected Products from third parties until Supplier has met the standard for three consecutive months. Customer's rights under this Section 3.4 are in addition to its rights under Section 4.5 and Section 11.4."
- **Note:** The 98% figure and monthly measurement are placeholders for a commercial decision.

**Issue 15: Firm forecast and PO acceptance mechanics favour Supplier (Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §3.2 (first three months of forecast "firm and binding"); SA §4.1 (acceptance within five business days; no deemed acceptance; Supplier may not reject a PO consistent with the firm portion of the forecast); SA §4.3 (delivery date in accepted PO).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Harrowgate's three firm months are binding with no tolerance band, no mechanism to change them, and no cancellation terms. If Harrowgate's volumes fall, it is liable for the firm portion.
  - Veltmar has no deadline to accept a PO outside the firm forecast, and there is no deemed acceptance if it is silent.
  - Veltmar has no binding volume commitment.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Add a tolerance band to firm forecasts, for example, Customer may vary firm quantities by up to [10]% without liability.
  - Add deemed acceptance: "If Supplier does not accept or reject a Purchase Order within five (5) business days, it is deemed accepted."
  - Add a cancellation fee schedule for firm quantities cancelled beyond the tolerance band, with the fee limited to documented costs.

**Issue 16: Force majeure is broad and the exclusivity release is slow (Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §15.7 (excuses delay or failure "caused by events beyond its reasonable control"; Customer's exclusivity release only if a Supplier force majeure event lasts over 30 days).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Glass plant outages, furnace failures, utility disruption and failures of Veltmar's own sub-suppliers may be argued to be beyond Veltmar's reasonable control. Harrowgate would bear that risk while bound by exclusivity.
  - There is no duty to allocate available production fairly among customers.
  - The 30-day trigger is long for a bottling operation.
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language:
  > "15.7 ... Force majeure does not include (a) a failure of Supplier's own equipment or facilities, except to the extent caused by an event described above and not reasonably preventable by Supplier's maintenance and back-up arrangements; or (b) a failure of a subcontractor or supplier, unless that failure is itself caused by a force majeure event. If Supplier is affected by a force majeure event, Supplier shall allocate its available production among its customers no less favourably than it allocates to itself and its Affiliates. If a force majeure event affecting Supplier continues for more than ten (10) days, Customer may purchase affected Products from third parties without breaching Section 3.1."

**Issue 17: No exit rights and no transition or tooling provisions (Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §13 (termination only for breach, insolvency and expiry); SA §13.3 (effect of termination: accepted POs fulfilled, but no transition duty); SA §6.3 (refers to "mold" changes but does not address ownership); SA §15.7.
- **Why it matters:**
  - Harrowgate has no right to terminate for convenience, for Supplier change of control, for chronic quality failure or for insurance lapse.
  - On expiry, Veltmar has no obligation to continue supply during a transition to a new supplier. Harrowgate's production could stop.
  - Harrowgate may have funded molds or tooling that are held by Veltmar. The file does not say who owns them. SA §6.3 refers to molds but is silent on ownership, return or use for third parties.
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language:
  > "13.4 Termination Rights. Customer may terminate this Agreement by written notice on [twelve (12)] months' notice at any time after [date]. Customer may also terminate immediately by written notice if (a) Supplier undergoes a change of control in favour of a Customer competitor; (b) Supplier's insurance under Section 14.1 lapses and is not reinstated within ten (10) business days; or (c) Supplier fails to meet Section 3.4 in two consecutive quarters.
  > 13.5 Transition. On expiry, or termination other than for Customer's breach, Supplier shall continue to supply Products on the terms of this Agreement for up to [twelve (12)] months at Customer's request, and shall accept a final Purchase Order for a last-time buy quantity specified by Customer.
  > 6.6 Tooling. Molds, tooling, dies and designs paid for by Customer, or made to Customer's specifications, are Customer's property. Supplier shall hold them for Customer's use, maintain them in good condition, not use them for any third party, identify them in its records, and return them to Customer on request or on expiry or termination."
- **Open question:** Who paid for and holds molds, and where are they located? Is there any Harrowgate-specific design or artwork?

### Part D: Assignment

**Issue 18: Veltmar may assign to Affiliates and successors without consent (High)**

- **Where:** Amend. §4.1 (new SA §14.4: Supplier may assign "without the consent of Customer" to any Affiliate, or to any successor by merger, consolidation, change of control or sale of all or substantially all of the relevant business; notice within 30 days after assignment takes effect); SA §14.3 (mutual consent required; "whether by operation of law or otherwise"); TS §8.1 (mutual consent).
- **Why it matters:**
  - The Amendment makes assignment by Veltmar possible without Harrowgate's consent, while Harrowgate remains bound by exclusivity (SA §3.1). Veltmar could assign to an Affiliate or successor with weaker capacity, credit or insurance, or to a company that competes with Harrowgate.
  - The notice is given after the assignment takes effect, so Harrowgate cannot object before it happens.
  - The new section does not require the assignee to assume obligations in writing, to maintain the insurance in SA §14.1, or to meet the capacity commitment in SA §3.3. Those obligations may not follow the contract.
  - Harrowgate has no reciprocal right. SA §14.3 still requires Veltmar's consent for Harrowgate's assignment, including to a successor. This is a one-sided change from the mutual regime in the TS and the original SA.
  - A change of control of Veltmar is not a trigger for any Harrowgate right.
- **Recommended fix:** Restore mutual consent, or add conditions. Proposed language replacing Amend. §4.1:
  > "14.4 Assignment by Supplier. Supplier may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, to (a) an Affiliate of Supplier, or (b) a successor to all or substantially all of the business of Supplier to which this Agreement relates, only with Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, and only if: (i) the assignee assumes all of Supplier's obligations in writing for Customer's benefit; (ii) the assignee has capacity, quality certifications and insurance at least equal to Supplier's under Sections 3.3, 3.1 and 14.1; (iii) Supplier gives Customer at least sixty (60) days' prior written notice; and (iv) if the assignee is an Affiliate, Supplier remains jointly liable with the assignee. Customer may terminate this Agreement by notice within ninety (90) days after any assignment or change of control of Supplier to a Customer competitor."
  > "14.5 Assignment by Customer. Customer may assign this Agreement without Supplier's consent to an Affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice to Supplier."
- **Negotiation note:** Mutuality is a strong argument for the 14.5 language.

### Part E: Quality and Specifications

**Issue 19: Specifications may be changed by quality representatives, with no revision control (Medium)**

- **Where:** QA §2.3 (Specifications may be updated by written document signed by "an authorized quality representative of each Party"); QA §11.4 (Specifications may be updated under §2.3 notwithstanding the amendment formality); QA Sch. 1 (specifications named "QA Spec. VG-LN12-AM" etc. with no revision number or date); SA §15.3 (formal amendment requirement); SA §6.3 (no change without written consent).
- **Why it matters:** A specification change under QA §2.3 is not an SA amendment, so it bypasses SA §15.3 and the signing formality. Authority is set by reference to "an authorized quality representative," who is not named. Schedule 1 has no revision level, so Harrowgate may not be able to prove which specification applied to a given lot. This matters for nonconformance claims and for recall traceability.
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language replacing QA §2.3:
  > "2.3 Updates. A Specification may be updated only by a written instrument signed by [named officer] of each Party, after completion of the change control process in Section 7. Each Specification shall bear a revision number and date, and the revision in effect for each Lot shall be recorded in the CoA. Section 11.4 applies to any update made under this Section 2.3."

**Issue 20: AQL table and defect definitions contain errors (Medium)**

- **Where:** QA §4.3 (table: Critical Defect "0.0 (zero tolerance)"; Major 0.65; Minor 2.5; reference to ANSI/ASQ Z1.4, General Inspection Level II); QA §1.2 (defines Critical and Major Defects; no definition of "Minor Defect").
- **Why it matters:**
  - ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 does not use an AQL of 0.0. Zero tolerance is normally achieved by an acceptance number of zero for the sample size selected. As drafted, the table may be read as unworkable or may not be applied consistently.
  - "Minor Defect" is used in the table but not defined. Veltmar could argue a defect is minor, and Harrowgate cannot easily classify it.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Replace the Critical Defect row with: "Critical Defect: acceptance number zero (Ac = 0) for the sample size determined under ANSI/ASQ Z1.4, General Inspection Level II; no Critical Defect is acceptable in any Lot."
  - Add a definition in §1.2: "(f) 'Minor Defect' means a defect other than a Critical Defect or Major Defect."
  - Confirm with the client's quality team that the sampling plan is workable for the 100% automated inspection in QA §4.1.

**Issue 21: Nonconformance and rejection notices, latent defects and corrective action timing (Medium)**

- **Where:**
  - SA §7.1 (rejection "by notice to Supplier describing the nonconformity," within 30 days of delivery for inspection).
  - SA §7.2 (acceptance does not waive warranty).
  - QA §6.2 (Nonconformance notice to "Supplier's quality representative," no method stated).
  - QA §11.3 (routine quality communications may be by email).
  - SA §15.4 (formal notices by hand, courier or certified mail).
  - QA §6.3 (acknowledgment in two business days, root-cause analysis in ten business days, implementation "within the time agreed").
- **Why it matters:**
  - It is unclear whether a rejection under SA §7.1 must be given under SA §15.4 or may be given by email to the quality representative. If the wrong method is used, Veltmar could argue the rejection was ineffective.
  - SA §7.1 gives a 30-day inspection window. Latent defects found later may be argued to be outside the rejection right, though SA §7.2 preserves warranty claims.
  - QA §6.3 has no deadline for implementing the corrective action. Harrowgate could be left with recurring defects while the corrective plan is "agreed."
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language added to QA §6.2 and SA §7.1:
  > "A Nonconformance notice under QA Section 6.2 or a rejection under SA Section 7.1 is validly given if sent by email to Supplier's quality representative and by a method permitted under SA Section 15.4 within five (5) business days after the email."
  > "SA 7.1 (added). Products may be rejected for a latent nonconformity discovered after the inspection period, by notice given within a reasonable time after discovery and within the warranty period in Section 8.1."
  > "QA 6.3 (revised). ... Supplier shall implement the plan within thirty (30) days after the root-cause analysis is delivered, or within any other period agreed in writing."

**Issue 22: Food-contact compliance covers only the US and Canada (Low–Medium)**

- **Where:** QA §2.2 (21 C.F.R. Parts 174–186 and Canada's Food and Drugs Act); SA §3.1 (exclusivity worldwide); SA §6.1 and SA §8.1(d) (general compliance with applicable laws).
- **Why it matters:** The exclusivity in §3.1 is worldwide, and the forecasts are by delivery location (SA §3.2). If Harrowgate ships to any other country, the QA's express food-contact compliance obligation does not apply. The general law reference in SA §8.1(d) may be enough, but the QA's specific standards would not.
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language:
  > "2.2 ... and, for Products delivered to any other jurisdiction, all laws applicable in that jurisdiction to materials in contact with food and beverages, as notified by Customer in writing at least sixty (60) days before the first delivery to that jurisdiction."
- **Open question:** Where does Harrowgate deliver? Does it plan to?

### Part F: Other Matters

**Issue 23: Term sheet status and divergences (Low–Medium)**

- **Where:** TS preamble (non-binding except §§10 and 11); TS §10.1 (confidentiality survives two years from January 22, 2024); TS §11.1 (costs); SA §15.1 (entire agreement; supersedes term sheets).
- **Why it matters:**
  - The term sheet is superseded by SA §15.1 on the subject matter. Its binding confidentiality clause (TS §10) expired on January 22, 2026 by its own terms. The SA's confidentiality clause (SA §12) now governs information exchanged under the SA. Information exchanged before March 1, 2024 may fall outside SA §12, so it is worth confirming whether any pre-SA information is still in use.
  - The term sheet differs from the SA in exclusivity (Issue 13), assignment (Issue 18) and renewal (Issue 4). Harrowgate should not rely on any term sheet position in negotiating the Amendment or future terms.
  - TS §6.3 describes "product liability and product recall insurance" as a separate requirement; SA §14.1 is less precise (Issue 11).
- **Recommended fix:** Advise the client that the term sheet is not a source of rights. Confirm that the client's internal records do not treat the term sheet as the operative deal.

**Issue 24: Dispute resolution has no carve-out for urgent relief (Low–Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §15.6 (30 days of executive negotiation, then exclusive jurisdiction of state or federal courts in Wilmington, Delaware); Amend. §6.2 (applies to the Amendment).
- **Why it matters:** The negotiation step has no exception for urgent relief. Harrowgate may need an emergency order to secure supply or to stop a wrongful termination or assignment. Veltmar may seek an injunction to enforce exclusivity. The exclusive Delaware forum is a commercial point but is reasonable under SA §15.5.
- **Recommended fix:** Proposed language added to SA §15.6:
  > "Nothing in this Section 15.6 prevents either Party from seeking injunctive, specific performance or other urgent relief in any court of competent jurisdiction, without first complying with the negotiation period."

**Issue 25: Notice mechanics and routing (Low–Medium)**

- **Where:** SA §15.4 (hand, courier or certified mail; effective on receipt); Amend. §3.1 (copies to Veltmar General Counsel and Harrowgate Legal Department; failure to send a copy does not affect effectiveness); QA §11.3 (routine communications by email).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Notices from Veltmar are effective on receipt at Harrowgate's Harrisburg address, even if the copy to the Legal Department is missed. Harrowgate's internal routing must catch them. The copy requirement does not protect Harrowgate.
  - Harrowgate's own notices, including objections, non-renewal and rejection, must follow the formal methods even if sent by email. Email alone is not sufficient.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Set up internal routing so that all Veltmar notices reach the Legal Department and the commercial lead on the day of receipt.
  - Propose that SA §15.4 be amended to allow email to designated contacts, provided a formal copy is sent within two business days. Proposed language:
    > "15.4 (added). Notices may also be given by email to the designated contacts listed in Schedule [●], provided a copy is sent by a method in this Section 15.4 within two (2) business days. Email notice is effective when sent if no delivery failure notice is received."

**Issue 26: Possible antitrust exposure from exclusive dealing (Flag only)**

- **Where:** SA §3.1 (worldwide, all-container exclusivity); SA §2.2 (five-year automatic renewals).
- **Why it matters:** Long, broad exclusive dealing arrangements can attract scrutiny under US antitrust law, depending on market shares and foreclosure. We have no market data in the folder, so we cannot assess this. The issue may matter for Harrowgate's own position if it wants to challenge or renegotiate the exclusivity.
- **Recommended action:** Flag for antitrust counsel if the client wants to push back on exclusivity. Obtain market share data before advising on the point.

**Issue 27: Implied-warranty disclaimer may be ineffective (Low — do not rely)**

- **Where:** SA §8.4 ("NEITHER PARTY MAKES ANY OTHER WARRANTY, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED").
- **Why it matters:** Under UCC §2-316 as adopted in Delaware, an exclusion of the implied warranty of merchantability must mention merchantability and be conspicuous. §8.4 does not mention merchantability. It is therefore likely not effective to exclude that warranty, which helps Harrowgate. We should not rely on this point, which should be confirmed, and Harrowgate should not agree to a clarified disclaimer in any future amendment without review.
- **Recommended action:** No change recommended. Flag if Veltmar proposes to revise §8.4.
