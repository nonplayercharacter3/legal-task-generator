### Issue 1: 14% Price Increase Appears to Be Binding

**Where:** SA §5.3 (notice requirements; once per 12 months; 60-day lead time), SA §5.4 (30-day objection; deemed acceptance), SA §15.4 (notice formalities); Am. Recital C; SA §5.1 and Ex. B (Exhibit B still shows old prices).

**Facts:** The notice is dated July 30, 2026. Harrowgate received it August 3, 2026. The increase is 14% and takes effect October 2, 2026. No objection was delivered (Am. Recital C).

**Analysis:**

- **Timing on its face.** The effective date is exactly 60 days after receipt (August 3 + 60 = October 2), which complies with SA §5.3.
- **Objection window.** Thirty days after receipt was September 2, 2026. Under SA §5.4, any adjustment not objected to within that period is "deemed accepted" and takes effect on the stated date. That period has passed. The increase has been in effect for about 16 days.
- **Recital C.** Harrowgate signed an instrument reciting that the notice was given under §5.3, that no objection was delivered, and that the notice "remains subject to Section 5.4." Veltmar will rely on this as acknowledgment.

**Possible challenges to evaluate (not yet verified):**

1. **Insufficient supporting detail.** SA §5.3 requires "reasonable supporting detail of the cost changes." If the notice lacked it, Veltmar may not have issued a valid Price Adjustment Notice, and arguably the §5.4 period never started. Recital C weakens this argument.
2. **Improper delivery.** SA §15.4 requires hand delivery, overnight courier, or certified mail. Email is not an authorized method for notices. QA §11.3 allows email only for routine quality communications. If the notice arrived only by email, its effectiveness is open to question. We need the transmittal and proof of delivery.
3. **Frequency.** SA §5.3 allows one adjustment per twelve months. The folder contains no price history.
4. **Non-waiver.** Am. §5.3 provides that nothing in the amendment waives rights "except as expressly set out." Harrowgate could argue that Recital C's words "remains subject to Section 5.4" do not express an acceptance. This is a weak argument but worth testing.

**Why it matters:** The increase applies to all three SKUs. If applied uniformly, the per-gross prices would be approximately:

| SKU | Exhibit B | Implied (+14%) |
|---|---|---|
| A-1 | $38.40 | ~$43.78 |
| A-2 | $61.20 | ~$69.77 |
| A-3 | $36.10 | ~$41.15 |

Actual exposure depends on volumes, which we do not have.

**Risk:** Withholding payment of the increased portion is not a safe option. SA §5.2 permits withholding only of amounts disputed in good faith with notice before the due date. Because the price is deemed accepted, withholding could be treated as a breach.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Obtain the July 30 notice, its transmittal and delivery proof, all cost detail, the 12-month price history, and any Harrowgate response between August 3 and September 2.
2. If any ground above is supportable, send a reservation-of-rights letter now and request supporting detail under §5.3.
3. Decide with the client whether to pay the adjusted invoices under protest. We should not recommend withholding.
4. Seek a commercial concession in exchange for other terms (for example, a term commitment or a reduced exclusivity scope), since the legal position is weak.
5. Calendar all future Price Adjustment Notice deadlines (see Issue 12).

**Proposed reservation-of-rights language (draft for partner review):**

> Harrowgate has received the Price Adjustment Notice dated July 30, 2026, which Veltmar states was received by Harrowgate on August 3, 2026. Harrowgate reserves all rights in respect of that notice, including whether it satisfies Section 5.3 of the Supply Agreement (including the requirement of reasonable supporting detail) and whether it was delivered in accordance with Section 15.4. Payment of invoices at the adjusted prices is made under protest and under full reservation of rights and does not waive any such right. Nothing in Amendment No. 1, including Recital C, constitutes Harrowgate's acceptance of the notice. Please provide within [10] business days the cost detail on which the adjustment is based.

### Issue 2: Amendment No. 1 Removes the Recall Carve-Out and Narrows the Indemnity Carve-Out

**Where:** Am. §2.1 (replacing SA §11.2); Am. §2.2 (application to claims arising on or after September 21, 2026); compare original SA §§11.1–11.3 and TS §7.2.

**Original SA §11.2 (as in the folder):** The cap (§11.1) and consequential-damages exclusion (§11.3) do not apply to:
(a) Supplier's indemnity under §9.1;
(b) Recall costs under §10;
(c) breach of §12 (confidentiality); or
(d) gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct.

**Amended §11.2 (Am. §2.1):** The exclusions now apply to:
(a) Supplier's indemnity under §9.1, "which are excluded solely to third-party intellectual property claims under Section 9.1(c)";
(b) breach of §12; or
(c) gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct.

**Changes:**

1. **Recall costs (§10.2) were removed entirely.** They are now subject to the cap in §11.1 (fees paid in the six months before the claim) and arguably to the exclusion in §11.3. Under §10.2, recall costs include "lost beverage contents" and "customer credits and refunds," which Veltmar may characterize as consequential damages.
2. **The §9.1 indemnity carve-out is narrowed.** The amended wording can be read in two ways:
   - *Reading A:* Only IP claims under §9.1(c) escape the cap. Product-defect, personal-injury, property-damage, and breach/negligence indemnities under §9.1(a), (b), and (d) fall under the cap and possibly §11.3.
   - *Reading B:* The language is a drafting error. Veltmar may argue Reading A and Harrowgate may argue Reading B. Either way, the provision is ambiguous, and that ambiguity is a risk.

**Why it matters:**

- Recall is among Harrowgate's largest potential exposures as a bottler.
- The cap is six months of fees paid. We have no purchase data, so we cannot size it. The insurance required by SA §14.1 is $10 million per occurrence. If six-month payments are below that level, Harrowgate's recovery may be limited to the cap even where insurance is available.
- TS §7.2 (non-binding) excluded recall costs and indemnities from the cap. The amendment departs from that commercial understanding.
- Am. §2.2 applies the change to claims "arising on or after" September 21, 2026. The word "arising" is undefined. A defect in product delivered before that date but discovered later may fall under either version.

**Enforceability:** If Harrowgate's representative signed the amendment (Sowell signature block), SA §15.3 is satisfied. Under UCC §2-209(1), a modification of a goods contract needs no consideration, so a consideration argument is unlikely to succeed (to verify). Other avoidance theories depend on facts we do not have, including how the amendment was negotiated and whether Ms. Sowell had authority. Recital D describes the amendment as "clarif[ying]," which may matter if the language is disputed.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Confirm execution and signing authority (Issue 28). Collect the negotiation file on the amendment.
2. Negotiate Amendment No. 2 restoring original §11.2, with a clarified trigger for §2.2.
3. If Veltmar will not restore the full carve-out, propose a super-cap for §9.1 and §10.2 tied to insurance.

**Proposed language (restoration):**

> **11.2 Exclusions.** The limitation in Section 11.1 and the exclusion in Section 11.3 do not apply to (a) Supplier's indemnity obligations under Section 9.1; (b) Supplier's obligation to reimburse Recall costs under Section 10.2; (c) either Party's breach of Section 12; or (d) a Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct.

**Proposed language (fallback super-cap):**

> Supplier's aggregate liability under Sections 9.1 and 10.2 shall not exceed the greater of (a) $[●] and (b) the limits of insurance required under Section 14.1. Except as stated in this Section, Section 11.1 does not apply to Supplier's liability under Sections 9.1 and 10.2.

**Proposed language (trigger clarification for §2.2):**

> For the avoidance of doubt, amended Section 11.2 applies only to claims based on a Product delivered, or an act, omission or event occurring, on or after the Amendment Date. Claims based on Products delivered before the Amendment Date remain subject to Section 11.2 as in effect before the Amendment Date.

### Issue 3: Veltmar May Assign Without Consent

**Where:** Am. §4.1 (new SA §14.4); SA §14.3 (mutual consent); SA §1.1 (Affiliate: more than 50% voting equity); TS §8.1 (mutual consent).

**Terms:** Veltmar may assign "in whole or in part" to (a) any Affiliate or (b) a successor by merger, consolidation, change of control, or sale of substantially all relevant assets. It need only give notice within 30 days after the assignment takes effect. Harrowgate has no corresponding right.

**Why it matters:**

- **No protections on the assignee.** There is no requirement for assumption in writing, no financial test, no parent guarantee, no insurance continuity, and no quality or recall capability test. The cap, insurance, and indemnity protections are only as good as the assignee.
- **Partial assignment.** Veltmar could split the relationship among entities, complicating recall and warranty claims.
- **Change of control is not a Harrowgate trigger.** A change of control of Veltmar can occur without an assignment. Section 14.4 approves transfers in that situation but gives Harrowgate no termination right.
- **Harrowgate's own flexibility.** Under SA §14.3, Harrowgate cannot assign to its own affiliates or to an acquirer of its beverage business, even though exclusivity extends to affiliates (SA §3.1).

**Recommended fix:** Replace new §14.4 with conditional assignment rights, add a reciprocal right for Harrowgate, and add a change-of-control termination right.

**Proposed language:**

> **14.4 Assignment by Supplier.** Notwithstanding Section 14.3, Supplier may assign this Agreement in whole (and not in part) to an Affiliate of Supplier, or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, without Customer's consent, only if: (a) Supplier gives Customer at least sixty (60) days' prior written notice; (b) the assignee agrees in writing, for Customer's benefit, to perform all of Supplier's obligations, and [Supplier's ultimate parent guarantees those obligations]; (c) the assignee has, at the time of assignment, financial standing and product-recall and quality capabilities no less than Supplier's, and maintains the insurance required by Section 14.1; and (d) the assignment does not reduce Supplier's obligations under Section 3.3. Customer may terminate this Agreement by written notice given within ninety (90) days after receiving Supplier's notice, without liability.
>
> **14.5 Assignment by Customer.** Customer may assign this Agreement, on written notice to Supplier, to an Affiliate of Customer or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business of Customer to which this Agreement relates, provided that the assignee assumes Customer's obligations in writing.
>
> **14.6 Change of Control.** A change of control of Supplier shall be treated as an assignment for purposes of Section 14.4. Customer may terminate this Agreement by written notice given within [ninety (90)] days after it learns of the change of control, without liability. "Change of control" means [●].

### Issue 5: Exclusivity Is Overbroad and May Already Be Breached

**Where:** SA §3.1 (all glass and PET containers for any beverage, worldwide, by or for Customer or its Affiliates); SA §1.3 and Ex. A (Products are three amber beer-bottle SKUs); SA §1.2 ("Containers"); SA §15.7 (force majeure carve-out only); TS §2.1–2.3.

**Comparison:**

| Feature | Term sheet (non-binding) | Supply Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Amber glass bottles for beer brands only (TS §2.1) | All glass and PET containers for any beverage (SA §3.1) |
| Geography | U.S. and Canadian facilities (TS §2.1) | Worldwide (SA §3.1) |
| Non-beer, non-amber, PET | Expressly excluded (TS §2.2) | Included (SA §3.1) |
| Affiliates | Not addressed | Included, plus "for" Customer (co-packers) (SA §3.1) |
| Supplier obligation to supply the covered items | Products defined (TS §1.1) | Only three SKUs (SA Ex. A) |

**Why it matters:**

1. **No supply obligation matches the exclusivity.** Veltmar is a glassworks. Nothing in the folder shows it manufactures PET, non-beer glass, or product for other regions. Harrowgate may be barred from buying those containers elsewhere with no supply source from Veltmar.
2. **Possible current breach.** If Harrowgate or any affiliate or co-packer currently buys glass or PET containers from third parties for non-beer, non-U.S./Canada, or other covered uses, it may already be in breach of §3.1. Consequences could include termination for material breach (SA §13.1, after a 30-day cure), a damages claim (subject to the cap, with lost profits excluded under §11.3), and a request for injunctive relief. SA §15.6 contains no carve-out for equitable relief.
3. **Future acquisitions.** Affiliates acquired later would be captured automatically.
4. **Not superseded by the term sheet.** SA §15.1 supersedes the term sheet, so Harrowgate cannot rely on TS §2 as contractual protection.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Immediately determine what third-party container purchases Harrowgate and its affiliates and co-packers make (Section 5 requests).
2. Narrow §3.1 to the term-sheet scope or to the Products Veltmar actually supplies.
3. Add a supply-failure carve-out (Issue 6).
4. If existing purchases conflict with §3.1, seek a written waiver or standstill from Veltmar before any dispute arises.

**Proposed language (replacement §3.1):**

> **3.1 Exclusive Purchase.** During the Term, Customer shall purchase from Supplier all of its requirements of the amber glass beer bottles listed in Exhibit A that are used for beer brands sold by Customer at its facilities located in the United States and Canada (the "Exclusive Products"). Section 3.1 does not restrict Customer from purchasing (a) any container not listed in Exhibit A; (b) any PET or other non-glass container; (c) any container for a non-beer beverage product; (d) any container used at a facility outside the United States and Canada; or (e) any container during a period described in Section 3.4. [Optional: Section 3.1 applies to an Affiliate of Customer only if the Affiliate is a party to this Agreement or Customer elects in writing to include it.]

### Issue 6: No Remedy for Supply Shortfall

**Where:** SA §3.3 (capacity at 115% of forecast; notice of shortfall, but no consequence); SA §4.1 (Supplier may reject non-firm Purchase Orders within five business days; no deemed acceptance); SA §4.5 (late-delivery remedy limited to cancellation after five business days); SA §15.7 (force majeure relief for Supplier only after 30 days, and only for "affected Products").

**Why it matters:** Harrowgate can be left without supply while remaining bound by exclusivity. Its only route to alternative supply is force majeure lasting more than 30 days. There is no allocation obligation, no damages for delay (beyond the §11.3 limits), and no right to buy substitute product and recover the cost difference.

**Recommended fix:** Add a supply-failure carve-out and a substitute-purchase right, and make the cost difference recoverable as direct damages (see Issue 7).

**Proposed language (new §3.4):**

> **3.4 Supply Failure.** If Supplier (a) fails to deliver in full any accepted Purchase Order within [ten (10)] business days after the agreed delivery date on [two (2)] or more occasions in any [six (6)]-month period; (b) notifies Customer that it cannot meet a forecast or Purchase Order under Section 3.3; or (c) fails to accept a Purchase Order in accordance with Section 4.1, then Section 3.1 does not restrict Customer from purchasing the affected quantities from third parties until Supplier demonstrates to Customer's reasonable satisfaction that it can meet Customer's requirements. Customer shall notify Supplier in writing of any such purchase.

### Issue 7: Consequential-Damages Exclusion May Capture Core Losses

**Where:** SA §11.3 (excludes indirect, incidental, consequential, punitive damages, and lost profits, except as in §11.2); SA §10.2 (recall costs include lost beverage contents and customer credits); SA §7.2 (replacement or credit only); SA §4.5.

**Why it matters:** Harrowgate's most likely losses from nonconforming bottles include:

- sorting and rework costs;
- replacement or disposal of filled product;
- line downtime;
- lost sales and brand harm.

Veltmar may characterize some or all of these as consequential or lost profits. TS §7.2 contained no consequential-damages exclusion, so §11.3 was an addition not reflected in the term sheet. Under the amendment, even the recall-cost carve-out has been removed (Issue 2).

**Recommended fix:** Add an express list of direct damages excluded from §11.3.

**Proposed language:**

> **11.3A Direct Damages.** For clarity, the following are direct damages recoverable by Customer and are not excluded by Section 11.3: (a) costs of inspecting, sorting, reworking or disposing of nonconforming Products; (b) the cost of replacement Products and the cost difference of substitute Products purchased under Section 3.4; (c) Recall costs under Section 10.2; (d) the cost of beverage contents and packaging spoiled or lost because of a nonconformity; and (e) reasonable costs of production downtime caused by a nonconformity, subject to a cap of $[●].

### Issue 8: Cap Measure Is Narrow and Uncertain

**Where:** SA §11.1 (each Party's liability capped at "fees paid by Customer in the six months before the claim arose"); SA §5.2 (45-day payment terms); SA §14.1 (insurance of $10 million); TS §7.2.

**Problems:**

1. **"Paid," not "paid or payable."** Because payment is due 45 days after receipt (SA §5.2), fees paid in the six months before a claim may exclude recent deliveries. The cap could be significantly smaller than six months of purchases.
2. **Timing of "the claim arose."** The date a claim "arose" is undefined. A latent defect may arise when the product is delivered, when it fails, or when a claim is first asserted.
3. **Cap is small relative to insurance.** The insurance is $10 million per occurrence (SA §14.1). Unless six-month payments exceed that amount, the cap will be less than the insurance Veltmar is required to carry.

**Recommended fix:** Use a twelve-month "paid or payable" measure with a fixed floor, tied to an event-based trigger.

**Proposed language:**

> **11.1 Cap.** Except as provided in Section 11.2, each Party's total liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement, whether in contract, tort or otherwise, shall not exceed the greater of (a) the fees paid and payable by Customer in the twelve (12) months before the event giving rise to the claim, and (b) $[●].

### Issue 9: Insurance Not Verified and Not Structured to Protect Harrowgate

**Where:** SA §14.1 (CGL with products liability and product recall coverage; $10 million per occurrence; additional insured; three years post-term); TS §6.3.

**Problems:**

1. **Product recall coverage.** Standard CGL policies typically exclude product recall. Section 14.1 can be read as requiring a CGL policy that includes recall coverage, which may not exist. A separate recall policy should be specified.
2. **No evidence requirement.** There is no obligation to deliver certificates or endorsements.
3. **No protective terms.** There are no requirements for primary and non-contributory coverage, notice of cancellation or reduction, or a statement that insurance does not limit liability.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm Veltmar's current coverage by obtaining certificates and endorsements, and add the terms below.

**Proposed language:**

> **14.1A Evidence of Insurance.** Supplier shall deliver certificates of insurance and additional-insured endorsements before the first delivery under this Agreement and at each renewal. Product recall coverage may be provided under a separate policy with limits of not less than $[10,000,000] per occurrence. Each policy shall be primary and non-contributory to any insurance maintained by Customer. Supplier shall give Customer at least thirty (30) days' written notice of cancellation, non-renewal or material reduction of coverage. Supplier's insurance does not limit its liability under this Agreement.

### Issue 13: Recall Causation and Payment

### Issue 14: Warranty Scope, Disclaimer, and Rejection Window

**Where:** SA §8.1 (18-month warranty of conformity, defect-freedom, fitness for holding and distributing, and compliance with law); SA §8.4 (disclaimer of all other warranties, express or implied); SA §7.1 (30-day inspection window; rejection "by notice"); SA §7.2; QA §1.2(b) (Critical Defect includes products "unusable for filling").

**Problems:**

1. **No fitness for filling.** Section 8.1(c) warrants fitness for "holding and distributing" beverage products but not fitness for filling on Harrowgate's lines. QA §1.2(b) treats filling failures as critical defects, so the gap is inconsistent.
2. **Implied warranties disclaimed.** SA §8.4 disclaims implied warranties. Under UCC §§2-314 and 2-316 (to verify), the disclaimer is likely effective, which removes merchantability as a backstop.
3. **Rejection timing unclear.** SA §7.1 allows inspection within 30 days, and rejection "by notice." It does not say whether rejection must occur within 30 days or whether latent defects discovered later can still be rejected. UCC §2-607(3)(a) requires notice of breach within a reasonable time, so Harrowgate should treat the 30-day period as a firm deadline.

**Recommended fix:** Expand the warranty, preserve merchantability for the warranty period, and clarify the rejection window.

**Proposed language (§8.1 addition):**

> **8.1(e)** be fit for filling, closing and packaging on Customer's filling and packaging lines in accordance with the Specifications, and free from Critical Defects and Major Defects (as defined in the Quality Agreement).

**Proposed language (§8.4 addition):**

> Notwithstanding the foregoing, the warranties in Section 8.1 are given in addition to, and do not exclude, any implied warranty of merchantability for the period stated in Section 8.1.

**Proposed language (§7.1 addition):**

> Customer may reject Products that do not conform to the Specifications (a) within thirty (30) days after delivery, or (b) if the nonconformity is latent and not reasonably discoverable within that period, within thirty (30) days after Customer discovers it, and in either case within the warranty period in Section 8.1. Notice of rejection shall be given in accordance with Section 15.4 and Section 6.2 of the Quality Agreement.

### Issue 17: Indemnity Scope and Defense Control

**Where:** SA §9.1 (Supplier indemnity for third-party claims: defects, personal injury or property damage, IP, breach or negligence); SA §9.2 (Customer indemnity for claims "arising out of the beverage contents filled into the Products by Customer," or Customer's negligence or willful misconduct); SA §9.3 (procedure; indemnifying party controls defense and settlement).

**Proposed language (§9.3 addition):**

> **9.3A Participation.** The indemnified Party may participate in the defense of any claim with counsel of its own choosing at its own expense (or at the indemnifying Party's expense if there is a conflict of interest). The indemnifying Party shall not, without the indemnified Party's prior written consent, settle any claim that involves a Recall, a public statement about the indemnified Party's brands, or any payment by the indemnified Party.
