## 1. Price Rejection Appears Time-Barred

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §7.3; Stareisk letter to Fevound, June 26, 2026, §§2.1, 3.1.

**Issue.** Section 7.3 provides that Distributor "may reject a price adjustment by written notice to Supplier delivered within twenty (20) days after the date of the Price Adjustment Notice." Fevound's Price Adjustment Notice is dated **June 1, 2026**. Stareisk's rejection letter is dated **June 26, 2026** — 25 days later. The 20-day window closed on **June 21, 2026**. Under §19.1, email notice is effective when sent, so the rejection was delivered outside the contractual window. Section 7.3 states: "A price adjustment that Distributor does not reject in accordance with this Section 7.3 is deemed accepted."

**Why it matters.** If the rejection is untimely, the 9.5% across-the-board price increase is deemed accepted and takes effect March 2, 2027. Stareisk has already quoted Airline Customers on Exhibit B pricing for commitments extending into H1 2027 (letter §4.4), so an unchallenged 9.5% increase would erode margin on those locked-in sales and could put menu listings at risk.

**Recommended fix.**
- Immediately send a supplemental letter to Fevound arguing that (a) the June 26 letter constitutes a rejection "in accordance with" §7.3 because it identifies the Notice, states the rejection in writing, and was delivered by the contractually specified method; and (b) in the alternative, Fevound is estopped from relying on the 20-day deadline because it has not yet invoiced at the increased prices and Stareisk has expressly reserved the right to dispute any such invoice (letter §7.3).
- Preserve the argument that Fevound's failure to provide the cost breakdown requested in letter §5.1 before the negotiation meeting constitutes a failure to engage in the good-faith process contemplated by §7.4, which supports a reading that the rejection period should be tolled or extended.
- In parallel, open the §7.4 negotiation on the merits. Do not allow the timing issue to become the sole point of dispute.

## 2. Non-Compete Scope Is Broader Than Agreed in the Term Sheet

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §4.3 and §§1.7, 1.18; SOW §§2.1–2.3; Term Sheet §5.3.

**Issue.** Term Sheet §5.3 was explicit: "The non-compete will cover competing shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed meal kits only. Stareisk's distribution of all other onboard food and beverage items, and its warehousing, catering logistics and galley-loading business generally, will not be restricted."

In the final Distribution Agreement, §4.3 prohibits Distributor from marketing, selling or distributing "any Competing Products" to "any Airline Customer **or other person** in the Territory." "Competing Products" (§1.7) means "any products, other than the Products, that fall within the Product Category." "Product Category" (§1.18) means "the category of products described in the then-current Statement of Work."

The SOW describes the Product Category in two places that pull in different directions:
- §2.1 ("Distributor's Operations") states that Stareisk "receives, stores, handles and delivers to Galley-Loading Points **all onboard food and beverage provisions, including fresh and frozen meals, snacks, beverages and bar items**."
- §2.2 ("Services") limits the contractual Services to those performed "for the Products" (i.e., the SkyCrate SKUs).

A reasonable reader — particularly a court construing the non-compete against the drafter (Fevound's counsel prepared the first drafts, per Term Sheet §2.4) — could conclude that "the category of products described in the SOW" encompasses all onboard food and beverage provisions, not just shelf-stable snack boxes and retort meal kits. The added phrase "or other person" in §4.3 (absent from the term sheet) extends the restriction beyond Airline Customers to any buyer in the Territory.

**Why it matters.** Stareisk's core business is distributing all categories of onboard provisions (fresh, frozen, ambient, beverages, bar items) to regional airlines. If the non-compete is read broadly, Stareisk could be in breach every time it sells a competing fresh meal kit or beverage product to an airline in the six-state Territory. This is an existential restriction on the business.

**Recommended fix.**
- Negotiate a clarifying amendment (or side letter) that redefines "Product Category" in §1.18 to mean "shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed retort meal kits of the type described in Exhibit A to the Distribution Agreement," tracking the term sheet language.
- Delete "or other person" from §4.3 so the non-compete runs only to Airline Customers, consistent with the exclusivity structure.
- If Fevound resists, at minimum add an express carve-out in §4.3 for "fresh and frozen meals, beverages, bar items, and all other onboard provisions that are not shelf-stable snack boxes or sealed meal kits."

## 3. Renewal Term Is Five Years Instead of One; Notice Period Is 30 Days Instead of 120

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §3.2; Term Sheet §6.2.

**Issue.** Term Sheet §6.2 provided for automatic renewal "for renewal terms of **one (1) year** per renewal" with a non-renewal notice period of "**one hundred twenty (120) days**." The final Distribution Agreement §3.2 provides for renewal "for successive renewal terms **equal in length to the Initial Term**" (i.e., five years) with a non-renewal notice period of only "**thirty (30) days**."

**Why it matters.** Stareisk is locked into five-year renewal cycles with a 30-day exit window. If the relationship underperforms in year one or two, Stareisk cannot simply let the agreement lapse after one year; it must give notice 30 days before the end of the full five-year Initial Term (i.e., by approximately February 2031) or it rolls into another five years. The short notice period also creates operational risk: a missed deadline means another five-year commitment.

**Recommended fix.**
- Raise this in the next amendment negotiation (e.g., when addressing the non-compete definition). Propose conforming §3.2 to the term sheet: one-year renewal terms with 120 days' non-renewal notice.
- If Fevound insists on longer renewal terms as a condition of other concessions, accept no more than two-year renewal terms with 90 days' notice.
- Calendar the 30-day non-renewal deadline (30 days before March 2, 2031) immediately, with internal escalation, so the issue is not missed regardless of whether the term is shortened.

## 4. Quality Agreement Governed by New York Law and Forum

**Where:** Quality Agreement §14.1; Distribution Agreement §18.1–18.2; Term Sheet §17.1–17.2.

**Issue.** The Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County (§18.1–18.2), consistent with the term sheet. The Quality Agreement, however, is governed by **New York law** with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County** (Quality Agreement §14.1). No term sheet provision contemplated a different governing law for the Quality Agreement.

**Why it matters.**
- Food-safety disputes (contamination, recall, complaint handling) will be litigated under New York law in New York courts, while the underlying distribution relationship (indemnity, liability cap, termination) is governed by Delaware law in Delaware courts. A single incident could generate parallel proceedings in two jurisdictions under two bodies of law.
- New York's product liability and food-safety jurisprudence differs from Delaware's in material respects (e.g., New York's stricter implied warranty analysis, different treatment of economic loss in food cases).
- The order of precedence in Distribution Agreement §2.3 gives the Quality Agreement priority "for food-safety and quality matters," meaning the New York-law document controls the most sensitive disputes.

**Recommended fix.**
- Negotiate a conforming amendment to Quality Agreement §14.1 to substitute Delaware law and New Castle County forum, matching the Distribution Agreement.
- If Fevound resists (perhaps because its quality team is New York-based), at minimum add a provision that the Quality Agreement's governing-law clause does not apply to disputes that are also governed by the Distribution Agreement's indemnification or limitation-of-liability sections, and that the parties consent to joinder or consolidation of related proceedings in Delaware.

## 5. Insurance Coverage Gap (March 2 – May 4, 2026)

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Recitals B–D and §2.4; Term Sheet §13.4; Order Form SC-OF-001 (dated March 9, 2026).

**Issue.** Term Sheet §13.4 contemplated that Fevound would name Stareisk as an additional insured on its product liability coverage, with specific limits "to be agreed in the Definitive Agreements." The original Distribution Agreement (executed March 2, 2026) contains **no insurance section**. The insurance requirements were added only by Amendment No. 1, effective **May 4, 2026**. Meanwhile, deliveries under Order Form SC-OF-001 began in late March/early April 2026, and Airline Customers were already requesting insurance confirmations (Amendment No. 1, Recital C).

Amendment No. 1 §2.4 confirms that Fevound maintains the required coverage "as of the Amendment Effective Date" (May 4) but does not confirm that the additional insured endorsements were in place during the March–April period.

**Why it matters.** If a product liability claim arises from Products delivered between March 26 and May 4, 2026, Stareisk may not have been an additional insured on Fevound's product liability policy during that window. Stareisk could be exposed to direct liability from passengers or Airline Customers without the benefit of Fevound's insurance tower.

**Recommended fix.**
- Request from Fevound (via its Chief Commercial Officer, the designated insurance contact in Exhibit D Part 3.5) written confirmation that the additional insured endorsements covering Stareisk were in effect on Fevound's product liability and CGL policies from March 2, 2026 forward, not merely from May 4, 2026.
- If Fevound cannot confirm, require it to obtain retroactive endorsement or confirm that its occurrence-based policies (as required by Amendment §11.1(e)) cover occurrences during the gap period.
- Going forward, calendar the 30-day advance notice requirement in Amendment §11.1(g) so that any cancellation or reduction is flagged to Stareisk before it becomes effective.

## 6. "Supply Shortfall" Carve-Out Missing from Non-Compete Exceptions

**Where:** Term Sheet §5.4; Distribution Agreement §§6.8, 16.4.

**Issue.** Term Sheet §5.4 provided that the non-compete "will not apply to substitute products that Stareisk procures to cover a late delivery, **a supply shortfall** or a force majeure event affecting Fevound." The final Distribution Agreement carves out substitute procurement only for (a) deliveries more than five Business Days late (§6.8) and (b) force majeure events (§16.4). A partial supply shortfall that does not constitute a "late delivery" (e.g., Fevound ships only 60% of the ordered quantity and delivers on time) and does not rise to the level of force majeure is not expressly covered.

**Why it matters.** If Fevound experiences a capacity constraint and can only supply a portion of an accepted Order Form on time, Stareisk may need to source the balance from a competitor to meet Airline Customer commitments. Without the "supply shortfall" language, doing so could technically breach §4.3.

**Recommended fix.**
- Add to §6.8 (or §4.3) a sentence providing that Distributor's procurement of substitute products to cover a partial supply shortfall — where Supplier delivers less than the accepted Order Form quantity for reasons other than Distributor's act or omission — is not a breach of Section 4.3.
- This can be bundled with the non-compete definitional fix in Issue 2 above.

## 7. Marketing Allowance Side Letter Is Not an "Agreement Document"

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §1.3, §2.3; Side Letter §10.1, §10.5.

**Issue.** "Agreement Documents" is defined in §1.3 as "this Agreement, the Quality Agreement, the SOW, each Order Form, and each Amendment." The Marketing Allowance Side Letter (dated March 2, 2026) is not an Amendment (it does not amend the Distribution Agreement; §10.1 states it "does not change any of its terms") and is not otherwise listed. The order of precedence in §2.3 therefore does not address the Side Letter.

The Side Letter incorporates the Distribution Agreement's dispute-resolution provisions (§10.5) and confidentiality (§10.4), but if a conflict arises between the Side Letter and, say, the SOW (e.g., on whether marketing-related logistics costs are "Eligible Costs" or "costs of the Services under the SOW" excluded by Side Letter §3.3(c)), the precedence rule is silent.

**Why it matters.** In practice, the Side Letter is unlikely to conflict with other Agreement Documents because it is self-contained and expressly subordinate. However, the gap creates uncertainty if Fevound later argues that a SOW fee covers activity that Stareisk is also claiming as an Eligible Cost under the Side Letter.

**Recommended fix.**
- Low urgency. If the non-compete or renewal-term amendment is negotiated, add the Side Letter to the definition of "Agreement Documents" in §1.3 and place it below Amendments but above the SOW in the §2.3 precedence order.
- Alternatively, add a sentence to Side Letter §10.1: "In the event of a conflict between this letter and any other Agreement Document, this letter controls with respect to the marketing allowance and Eligible Costs."

## 8. SOW Service-Level Credit Is One-Way and Modest

**Where:** SOW §6.3.

**Issue.** If Stareisk fails to meet the same Service Level in three consecutive Service Months, it must credit Fevound 5% of the pick-and-pack and galley-loading Fees for the third month. There is no reciprocal remedy for Fevound's failures (e.g., late deliveries, short shipments, failure to provide product information under SOW §5). Stareisk's late-delivery and short-shipment remedies are in the Distribution Agreement (§§6.8, 6.9) and are adequate, but the SOW itself contains no service-level credit running from Fevound to Stareisk.

**Why it matters.** The 5% credit is modest (on current volumes, roughly a few hundred dollars per month), so the financial exposure is limited. The larger concern is the absence of any SOW-level remedy if Fevound repeatedly fails to provide advance shipping notices (§5.2), product information (§5.1), or disposition decisions (§5.3) on time, which would disrupt Stareisk's operations and could cause Stareisk to miss its own Service Levels (triggering the credit to Fevound).

**Recommended fix.**
- Add to SOW §6.3 (or a new §6.5): "If Supplier fails to perform its responsibilities under Section 5 in a manner that causes Distributor to fail to meet a Service Level, that failure is excused under Section 6.2(e) and shall not count toward the three-consecutive-month threshold in Section 6.3."
- This is a clarification rather than a new remedy and should be easy to accept.
