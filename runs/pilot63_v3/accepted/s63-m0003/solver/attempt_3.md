## 1. CRITICAL: Price Adjustment Rejection Delivered Outside the Contractual Window

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 7.3; Stareisk Letter to Fevound (June 26, 2026) § 3.1

**Issue.** Section 7.3 permits Distributor to reject a Price Adjustment Notice only by "written notice to Supplier delivered within twenty (20) days after the date of the Price Adjustment Notice." Fevound's Notice is dated **June 1, 2026**. Twenty calendar days after June 1 is **June 21, 2026** (per § 1.10, "days" means calendar days). Stareisk's rejection letter is dated **June 26, 2026**—five days late. The final sentence of § 7.3 provides: "A price adjustment that Distributor does not reject in accordance with this Section 7.3 is **deemed accepted** and takes effect on the date stated in the Price Adjustment Notice."

**Why it matters.** On its face, the 9.5% across-the-board increase is now contractually effective for the second Contract Year (March 2, 2027). Stareisk's carefully reasoned rejection letter and its request for cost data under § 5.1 of the letter may be procedurally ineffective. This undermines Stareisk's negotiating position and its ability to honor existing airline customer price quotations (as noted in the letter at § 4.4).

**Recommendation.**

- Immediately assess whether Fevound has treated the rejection as valid (e.g., by engaging in the § 7.4 negotiation process the letter proposes). If Fevound has responded substantively to the rejection or confirmed a meeting date, there is an argument for waiver or course of dealing.
- Prepare a fallback position: if the increase is deemed accepted, negotiate a retroactive offset (e.g., a one-time credit, a volume rebate, or an earlier next price-adjustment date) as the cost of curing the procedural defect.
- Going forward, calendar all contractual notice deadlines with internal cutoffs at least 3 business days before the expiry date.

## 2. Renewal Term Length and Notice Period Deviate Materially from the Term Sheet

**Documents:** Term Sheet § 6.2; Distribution Agreement § 3.2

**Issue.** The Term Sheet provided for automatic renewals of **one (1) year** each, with a non-renewal notice period of **120 days**. The executed Distribution Agreement provides for renewals "equal in length to the Initial Term" (i.e., **five (5) years**), with a non-renewal notice period of only **30 days**.

**Why it matters.** Stareisk is now locked into successive five-year terms unless it gives notice a full five years before the current term ends—and it has only 30 days to do so. If the relationship underperforms or market conditions change, Stareisk faces a much longer commitment with far less planning runway than it bargained for. The 30-day window is also operationally tight: Stareisk would need to identify and qualify a replacement supplier, transition warehouse and galley-loading operations, and re-paper airline customer contracts within 30 days of deciding not to renew.

**Recommendation.**

- Raise this at the next quarterly meeting or in connection with the price negotiation. Seek an amendment reducing the Renewal Term to one year (or at most three years) and extending the non-renewal notice period to 120 days, as originally agreed.
- If Fevound resists, consider a compromise: a three-year Renewal Term with 90 days' notice.
- If no amendment is achievable, mark the internal calendar for the non-renewal notice deadline (30 days before the end of the Initial Term, i.e., by **February 1, 2031**) well in advance and build the decision process into the 2030 business planning cycle.

## 3. Non-Compete Scope Is Broader Than Agreed in the Term Sheet

**Documents:** Term Sheet § 5.3; Distribution Agreement § 4.3, §§ 1.7, 1.18; SOW § 2.1

**Issue.** The Term Sheet was explicit: "The non-compete will cover competing **shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed meal kits only**. Stareisk's distribution of all other onboard food and beverage items, and its warehousing, catering logistics and galley-loading business generally, will not be restricted."

The executed Distribution Agreement § 4.3 prohibits Distributor from marketing, selling or distributing "any **Competing Products**" to any Airline Customer or other person in the Territory. "Competing Products" (§ 1.7) means "any products, other than the Products, that fall within the **Product Category**." "Product Category" (§ 1.18) means "the category of products described in the then-current **Statement of Work**."

The SOW § 2.1 describes Distributor's operations as covering "all onboard food and beverage provisions, including fresh and frozen meals, snacks, beverages and bar items." A reader could argue the "category of products described in" the SOW is not limited to shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed meal kits but extends to the broader onboard provisions category.

**Why it matters.** Stareisk distributes a wide range of onboard food and beverage to regional airlines. If the non-compete is read broadly, Stareisk could be in breach simply by continuing its existing business of distributing competing snack boxes or meal kits from other manufacturers to the same airlines. This is an existential restriction on Stareisk's core revenue.

**Recommendation.**

- Amend § 1.18 to define "Product Category" as "shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed retort or ambient meal kits intended for in-flight service," tracking the Term Sheet language. Alternatively, amend § 1.7 to define "Competing Products" by express reference to the product formats in Exhibit A.
- Add an express carve-out: "For the avoidance of doubt, Section 4.3 does not restrict Distributor's distribution of fresh or frozen meals, beverages, bar items, or any other onboard provisions that are not shelf-stable snack boxes or sealed meal kits."
- Until amended, ensure that no Stareisk affiliate markets or sells any product that could be characterized as a "shelf-stable snack box" or "sealed meal kit" to a Territory airline, even if it is not a SkyCrate product.

## 4. Quality Agreement Governs Itself Under New York Law and Forum

**Documents:** Quality Agreement § 14.1; Distribution Agreement § 18.1–18.2; Term Sheet § 17.1–17.2

**Issue.** The Distribution Agreement selects **Delaware** law and exclusive jurisdiction in **New Castle County, Delaware** (§ 18.1–18.2). The Quality Agreement selects **New York** law and exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County** (§ 14.1). The Term Sheet addressed only the Distribution Agreement's governing law and forum; it did not contemplate a split.

**Why it matters.** A single food-safety incident (e.g., a listeria contamination in a snack box) will implicate both agreements simultaneously. Stareisk could face: (a) a product liability claim by an airline customer or passenger governed by the Distribution Agreement's indemnity (§ 11.2 as renumbered) in Delaware; and (b) a quality-agreement dispute about lot release, recall costs or specification compliance in New York under New York law. The two proceedings could reach inconsistent results on overlapping factual questions. New York's product liability and food-safety jurisprudence differs from Delaware's in material respects (e.g., New York recognizes a broader implied warranty of fitness in certain contexts). Stareisk also loses the tactical advantage of a single, home-state forum.

**Recommendation.**

- Amend Quality Agreement § 14.1 to conform to the Distribution Agreement: Delaware law, New Castle County forum. The § 14.2 technical-review escalation step can remain.
- If Fevound resists (it is a New Jersey company with New York ties), accept New Jersey law and forum as a compromise, but do not accept New York.
- In the interim, ensure that any quality-related dispute is framed, where possible, as arising under the Distribution Agreement (which has the broader indemnity and the Delaware forum) rather than the Quality Agreement.

## 5. Marketing Allowance Side Letter Is Not an "Agreement Document"

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 1.3, § 2.3, § 14.6; Side Letter § 10.1, § 10.5

**Issue.** "Agreement Documents" (§ 1.3) means "this Agreement, the Quality Agreement, the SOW, each Order Form, and each Amendment." The Marketing Allowance Side Letter is not listed. Consequences:

- **Order of precedence** (§ 2.3) does not apply to the Side Letter. If a term in the Side Letter conflicts with the Distribution Agreement (e.g., on confidentiality, notices, or set-off), the precedence rules cannot resolve it.
- **Survival** (§ 14.6) enumerates specific surviving sections of the Distribution Agreement but does not reference the Side Letter. The Side Letter has its own survival clause (§ 9.4), which helps, but the interaction is unclear.
- **"Fees" definition** (§ 1.12) covers "all amounts paid or payable under the Agreement Documents." Because the Side Letter is not an Agreement Document, marketing allowance payments may fall outside "Fees," which could affect the audit right in § 7.12 and the set-off right in § 7.11. The Side Letter partially bridges this gap (§ 5.4 expressly deems approved Claims "undisputed amounts… for the purposes of Section 7.11"; § 8.2 deems its records "records relating to the Fees for the purposes of Section 7.12"), but the fix is piecemeal.

**Why it matters.** If a dispute arises over the Allowance (e.g., Fevound refuses to pay a Claim), Stareisk may find that the Side Letter sits outside the contractual framework that governs all other aspects of the relationship. The set-off and audit protections are less certain than they appear.

**Recommendation.**

- Amend § 1.3 to add "the Side Letter dated March 2, 2026 (Marketing Allowance)" to the definition of "Agreement Documents."
- Alternatively, execute a short amendment that (a) confirms the Side Letter is governed by the Distribution Agreement's dispute-resolution, set-off, audit, and survival provisions, and (b) clarifies that amounts payable under the Side Letter are "Fees" for all purposes.

## 6. Liability Cap Will Be Nominal in the Early Months

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 12.2, § 1.12

**Issue.** The aggregate liability cap (§ 12.2) is "the total Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim." In the first few months of the relationship, total Fees will be modest (Order Form SC-OF-001 represents only ~$83,000 in product value plus SOW service fees). If a significant claim arises in, say, month four, the cap could be as low as $100,000–$150,000.

**Why it matters.** The cap does not apply to Supplier's indemnification obligations (§ 12.3(a)), Recall costs (§ 12.3(e)), or gross negligence/fraud (§ 12.3(c)), so the most serious food-safety claims are uncapped. However, claims for breach of the non-compete, failure to promote (§ 5.1), or service-level failures that fall short of gross negligence would be subject to the low early-period cap. For Stareisk, the more practical risk is that *Fevound's* liability to Stareisk for, e.g., a chronic pattern of late deliveries or specification nonconformities that don't rise to gross negligence will be capped at a small number.

**Recommendation.**

- This is a lower-priority issue given the § 12.3 carve-outs. Monitor whether the cap becomes problematic as the relationship matures.
- If the price negotiation (Issue 1) breaks down and the relationship sours, consider whether a supercap (e.g., 2× or 3× trailing 12-month Fees) for Supplier's obligations under §§ 6 and 9 would be a reasonable ask in an amendment.

## 7. Minor: Stareisk's Stated "Principal Place of Business" Is Inconsistent Across Documents

**Documents:** Office Lease (preamble); Distribution Agreement (preamble); SOW (preamble); Quality Agreement (preamble)

**Issue.** The Office Lease identifies Stareisk's principal place of business as **8600 Essington Avenue, Building C** (the Philadelphia warehouse). The Distribution Agreement, SOW, Quality Agreement, and Side Letter all identify it as **2401 Columbus Boulevard, Suite 1400** (the office).

**Why it matters.** Low risk in practice because all notice provisions use specific addresses and the Delaware registered-agent address controls for service of process. However, if a court or counterparty looks to the "principal place of business" for personal jurisdiction or convenience purposes, the inconsistency could create a minor argument.

**Recommendation.** No action required now. If Stareisk executes any future agreement, use a single, consistent address. The Columbus Boulevard address (headquarters) is the appropriate one for contract purposes.
