## 1. Renewal Term Length and Non-Renewal Notice Period

**Documents:** Term Sheet §6.2; Distribution Agreement §3.2

**Issue:** The Term Sheet provides for automatic renewal in **one-year** terms, with either Party able to prevent renewal by giving **120 days'** prior written notice. The signed Distribution Agreement §3.2 provides for automatic renewal in terms **"equal in length to the Initial Term"** (i.e., **five years**), with only **30 days'** notice to prevent renewal.

**Why it matters:** Stareisk is locked into five-year renewal cycles rather than the one-year cycles it negotiated. The shorter notice window (30 days vs. 120 days) materially increases the risk of an inadvertent lock-in. If Stareisk's operations team misses the 30-day window at the end of the Initial Term (March 2, 2031), it is committed for another five years through March 2, 2036.

**Recommendation:** Amend §3.2 to restore the one-year renewal term and 120-day non-renewal notice period as set out in the Term Sheet. If Fevound resists, a compromise of a two-year renewal term with 90-day notice would still be a significant improvement.

## 2. Quality Agreement Governed by New York Law in New York Forum

**Documents:** Quality Agreement §14.1; Term Sheet §§17.1–17.2; Distribution Agreement §§18.1–18.2

**Issue:** The Term Sheet and the Distribution Agreement both specify Delaware governing law and exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. The Quality Agreement §14.1, however, provides that it "shall be governed by the laws of the State of **New York**, with exclusive jurisdiction in the state and federal courts located in **New York County**."

**Why it matters:** Under Distribution Agreement §2.3, the Quality Agreement has **priority** over the Distribution Agreement for "food-safety and quality matters." This means the most sensitive disputes—contamination, recalls, foodborne illness, specification nonconformity—will be litigated in New York under New York law, not Delaware. New York law differs from Delaware on issues such as the enforceability of consequential damages waivers, indemnification scope, and the application of the economic loss doctrine. Stareisk also loses the convenience and predictability of a single forum.

**Recommendation:** Amend Quality Agreement §14.1 to conform to Delaware law and New Castle County jurisdiction, consistent with the Term Sheet and the Distribution Agreement. If Fevound insists on a technical review step (which §14.2 already provides), that is acceptable, but the governing law and forum should be unified.

## 3. Non-Compete Scope Far Broader Than Negotiated

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§1.7, 1.18, 4.3; SOW §2.1; Term Sheet §5.3

**Issue:** The Term Sheet §5.3 limits the non-compete to "competing **shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed meal kits only**" and expressly states that "Stareisk's distribution of all other onboard food and beverage items, and its warehousing, catering logistics and galley-loading business generally, will not be restricted."

The signed Distribution Agreement, however, prohibits Stareisk from distributing any "Competing Products" (§4.3). "Competing Products" is defined as products "that fall within the **Product Category**" (§1.7). "Product Category" is defined as "the category of products described in the **then-current Statement of Work**" (§1.18). The SOW §2.1 describes Stareisk's operations as covering "**all onboard food and beverage provisions, including fresh and frozen meals, snacks, beverages and bar items**."

Read together, the non-compete potentially restricts Stareisk from distributing **any** competing onboard food or beverage product—not just shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed meal kits—to any Airline Customer in the Territory.

**Why it matters:** This is Stareisk's core business. If a regional airline asks Stareisk to distribute a competitor's fresh meal program or beverage line, the current drafting could be read to prohibit it. This is a material restriction that was not agreed in the Term Sheet.

**Recommendation:** Amend §1.18 to define "Product Category" as "shelf-stable snack boxes and sealed retort or ambient meal kits for in-flight service," consistent with the Term Sheet. Alternatively, amend §4.3 to expressly exclude fresh, frozen, chilled, beverage and bar items from the non-compete. The SOW §2.1 description of Stareisk's broader operations should not be used to define the scope of the non-compete.

## 4. Non-Compete Extends to "Any Airline Customer or Other Person"

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §4.3; Term Sheet §5.3

**Issue:** The Term Sheet §5.3 restricts Stareisk only with respect to "Airline Customers." The signed Distribution Agreement §4.3 prohibits Stareisk from marketing, selling or distributing Competing Products "to any Airline Customer **or other person** in the Territory."

**Why it matters:** The "or other person" language could be read to prevent Stareisk from selling competing products to any customer in the six-state Territory, including non-airline institutional customers, retail accounts, or corporate clients. This goes well beyond the negotiated scope.

**Recommendation:** Delete "or other person" from §4.3 to conform to the Term Sheet. If Fevound's concern is that Stareisk might use airline customer relationships to cross-sell competing products to those airlines' non-flight operations (e.g., lounge catering), a narrower carve-back could be negotiated, but the current language is overbroad.

## 5. Cross-Reference Error in Liability Cap Exclusion After Amendment No. 1

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §12.3(a); Amendment No. 1 §§2.1–2.3

**Issue:** Amendment No. 1 inserted a new §11.1 (Supplier Insurance) and renumbered the former §11.1 (Indemnification by Supplier) as §11.2. However, §12.3(a) of the Distribution Agreement still reads: "Supplier's indemnification obligations under **Section 11.1**." After the renumbering, the indemnification obligation is in **Section 11.2**. Section 11.1 is now the insurance covenant.

**Why it matters:** If a court reads §12.3(a) literally, the exclusion from the liability cap for "Supplier's indemnification obligations under Section 11.1" could be argued to refer to the insurance provision (which imposes no indemnification obligation) rather than the actual indemnification in §11.2. This creates uncertainty about whether Fevound's indemnification for foodborne illness, product defects, and recalls is subject to the 12-month-fees cap in §12.2.

**Recommendation:** Execute a correcting amendment (or a scrivener's-error letter if the parties agree) to change the reference in §12.3(a) from "Section 11.1" to "Section 11.2." This should be done promptly, before any dispute arises.

## 6. Gap in Additional Insured Coverage During Initial Delivery Period

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1 §§2.1(c), 2.4; Order Form SC-OF-001 §§4.1–4.2; Term Sheet §13.4

**Issue:** The Term Sheet §13.4 contemplated that Fevound would name Stareisk as an additional insured on its product liability coverage in the Definitive Agreements. The original Distribution Agreement (effective March 2, 2026) contained **no** insurance section. Amendment No. 1 (effective May 4, 2026) added the insurance requirements, but §2.4 permitted Fevound up to **10 Business Days** after the Amendment Effective Date to obtain the additional insured and waiver of subrogation endorsements—i.e., by approximately **May 18, 2026**.

Initial deliveries under Order Form SC-OF-001 occurred on **March 26, 2026** (Shipment 1, FCA Secaucus) and **April 9, 2026** (Shipment 2, DAP Baltimore). During the period from March 2 through approximately May 18, 2026, Stareisk was **not** named as an additional insured on Fevound's product liability policy.

**Why it matters:** If a foodborne illness or product defect claim had arisen from Products delivered during that roughly 11-week window, Stareisk would have had to rely solely on its own insurance and on Fevound's contractual indemnity (§11.2 as renumbered), without the direct additional-insured protection on Fevound's policy. Airline Customers have already begun requesting evidence of manufacturer insurance (per Amendment No. 1, Recital C), so this gap is also a reputational and vendor-qualification concern.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm in writing that Fevound's additional insured endorsements were in fact placed by May 18, 2026, and obtain copies of the endorsements and certificates.
- Request that Fevound confirm, for the period March 2 – May 18, 2026, that no claims, occurrences or circumstances giving rise to a claim arose involving the Products. If any did, assess whether Stareisk's own policies responded adequately.
- Going forward, calendar the 30-day prior notice requirement in §11.1(g) (as amended) so that any lapse is detected immediately.

## 7. Marketing Allowance Side Letter Not Captured in Agreement Documents or Order of Precedence

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§1.3, 2.3; Fevound Side Letter (Marketing Allowance) §10.1

**Issue:** The "Agreement Documents" are defined in §1.3 as "this Agreement, the Quality Agreement, the SOW, each Order Form, and each Amendment." The Marketing Allowance Side Letter is none of these. It is entered into under §15.4 as a separate written agreement. The order of precedence in §2.3 (Amendments → Quality Agreement → Distribution Agreement → SOW → Order Forms) does not include the Side Letter.

**Why it matters:** If a conflict arises between the Side Letter and another Agreement Document (e.g., if the Side Letter's treatment of Airline Customer information in §7.4 conflicts with the Distribution Agreement's confidentiality provisions in §13, or if a Claim under the Side Letter is disputed and the question arises whether the Side Letter's dispute mechanism in §5.5 or the Distribution Agreement's escalation in §18.3 controls), there is no clear answer. The Side Letter §10.5 incorporates the Distribution Agreement's dispute resolution, which helps, but the precedence gap remains.

**Recommendation:** Add the Marketing Allowance Side Letter to the definition of "Agreement Documents" in §1.3 and insert it in the order of precedence in §2.3 (logically, it should rank below Amendments but above the Distribution Agreement, or alternatively be treated as a supplement to §15.4 with the Distribution Agreement controlling in the event of conflict). A short correcting amendment or a side letter between the Parties confirming the precedence would suffice.

## 8. No Service Levels or Credit Mechanism for Supplier Performance

**Documents:** SOW §6; Distribution Agreement §6.8

**Issue:** The SOW establishes detailed Service Levels for **Distributor's** performance (§6.1), with a 5% fee credit if the same level is missed three consecutive months (§6.3). There is no equivalent structure for **Supplier's** performance. The only remedy for late delivery is in Distribution Agreement §6.8 (cancellation of the affected portion and substitute procurement after 5 Business Days' delay).

**Why it matters:** If Fevound consistently delivers late (but by fewer than 5 Business Days), ships short, or fails to provide advance shipping notices or CoAs on time, Stareisk has no structured remedy short of arguing material breach. Given that Stareisk's galley-loading operations are scheduled around airline delivery windows, even a 2- or 3-day delay can cause a missed flight loading and a service failure to an Airline Customer for which Stareisk bears the relationship risk.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate a Supplier Service Level schedule (e.g., on-time delivery ≥ 97%, complete documentation ≥ 99%, CoA accuracy ≥ 99%) with a corresponding credit or fee-reduction mechanism. At minimum, add a provision that three or more late deliveries (≥ 2 Business Days) in a Contract Year constitute a material breach giving Stareisk termination rights under §14.1 without the need to prove the breach is "material" as a threshold matter.

## 9. Principal Place of Business Inconsistency

**Documents:** Stareisk Philadelphia Office Lease §1 (preamble); all Fevound Agreement Documents (preamble)

**Issue:** The Office Lease (signed September 16, 2024) identifies Stareisk's "principal place of business" as **8600 Essington Avenue, Building C, Philadelphia, PA 19153** (the warehouse). Every Fevound document identifies it as **2401 Columbus Boulevard, Suite 1400, Philadelphia, PA 19148** (the office).

**Why it matters:** This is likely a factual error in the Lease (the warehouse was the principal operating address at the time the Lease was signed, before the office lease commenced on January 1, 2025). It creates a minor inconsistency that could matter if service of process, jurisdictional analysis, or a "principal place of business" test (e.g., for LLC registration or tax nexus) is at issue. It is not a commercial risk in the Fevound relationship but should be cleaned up.

**Recommendation:** No action required in the Fevound documents (they are consistent with each other). If the Lease is ever amended or renewed, correct the principal place of business reference.

## 10. Marketing Allowance Claim Deadlines Are Tight

**Documents:** Fevound Side Letter §5.1

**Issue:** Claims for reimbursement must be submitted no later than **60 days after the end of the Approved Activity** and in any event no later than **60 days after the end of the Contract Year** to which the costs are charged.

**Why it matters:** Stareisk's operations team (Everett Quaidlin's group) is managing airline relationships, galley-loading, and the distribution business. If a menu presentation in, say, February 2027 generates eligible costs, the claim must be in by April 2, 2027. Missing the deadline forfeits the reimbursement with no cure mechanism.

**Recommendation:** Implement an internal tracking system (a simple shared calendar or project management tool) that flags the 60-day deadline for each Approved Activity the moment it is completed. Assign ownership to the Marketing Contact (Quaidlin) with a backup. Consider asking Fevound to extend the deadline to 90 days in a future amendment to the Side Letter, though this is low-priority given the small dollar amounts involved.
