## Issue 1: Objection to Rate Adjustment Notice Appears Late

**Where:** MSA §5.4; Vaitix Letter §1.1, §2.1; Rate Adjustment Notice (referenced in Letter §1.1, dated June 26, 2026, received June 29, 2026).

**Analysis.** Section 5.4 of the MSA provides that Customer "may object to a Rate Adjustment Notice by written notice to Provider given within thirty (30) days after receipt of the Rate Adjustment Notice." Vaitix received the notice on June 29, 2026. Thirty days from that date is July 29, 2026. The objection letter is dated August 14, 2026—approximately 46 days after receipt, or 16 days past the deadline.

Section 5.4 further provides: "If Customer does not give a timely objection, the adjusted Fees set out in the Rate Adjustment Notice will be deemed accepted by Customer and will apply from the effective date stated in the Rate Adjustment Notice." The stated effective date is September 1, 2026.

**Why it matters.** If the objection is deemed untimely, the 11.5% across-the-board increase (approximately $205,747 per year based on current volumes, per Letter Annex A, Part 2) is contractually deemed accepted as of September 1, 2026. Vaitix would lose its principal leverage in the rate negotiation and the right to terminate the Order Form without the Early Termination Fee under §5.4(c).

**Recommended fix.**

- Immediately assess whether any argument exists that the 30-day period has not run. Potential (though weak) angles: (a) whether the notice was "received" for §5.4 purposes on a date later than June 29 given the §19.1 notice mechanics (email + overnight courier; the letter was sent "by email and overnight courier" per its header, but the Rate Adjustment Notice itself was a letter—confirm the delivery method and date on the courier record); (b) whether Drenind's failure to provide the supporting data that §5.4 contemplates (the notice includes only a "one-page summary" per Letter §1.2) constitutes a defective notice that restarts or tolls the objection period. Neither argument is strong, but they should be evaluated before any further communication.
- If the objection is indeed late, Vaitix should not concede the point. Send a supplemental letter that (i) reaffirms the objection, (ii) argues the notice was deficient in content (no cost breakdown, no allocation methodology), and (iii) makes clear that Vaitix will treat any invoice at the increased rates as a disputed amount under §6.3. This preserves the dispute mechanism even if the deemed-acceptance clause applies.
- Strategically, the chronic service level failure (Issue 4 below) and the Change of Control issue (Issue 2 below) give Vaitix significant leverage. A global settlement of the rate dispute, the CoC consent, and the operational items should be pursued together.

## Issue 2: Pending Change of Control – Consent Not Sought

**Where:** MSA §16.2; Vaitix Letter §3.1–3.4; Order Form §9.1 (ETF calculation).

**Analysis.** On July 30, 2026, Vaitix Holdings, LLC (sole member of Vaitix) signed a definitive agreement to sell 100% of its equity to Ridgeholt Capital Partners, L.P. Closing is expected in Q4 2026 (Letter §3.1).

Section 16.2 of the MSA defines "Change of Control" to include "the acquisition by any person or group of persons acting together, directly or indirectly, of beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity of that party **or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls such party**" (§16.2(a)). Ridgeholt's acquisition of Vaitix Holdings is squarely within this definition.

Section 16.2 further provides: "Any Change of Control of a party is deemed an assignment of this Agreement by that party and requires the prior written consent of the other party." And: "Any Change of Control effected without the other party's prior written consent is a material breach of this Agreement that entitles the other party to terminate this Agreement under Section 12.4, **without any cure period**, and, where the party undergoing the Change of Control is Customer, to recover the Early Termination Fee."

The August 14 letter discloses the transaction as a "courtesy" (§3.1) and states that "Vaitix will remain the contracting party" (§3.2), but it does not:
- Identify the transaction as a Change of Control under §16.2;
- Request Drenind's prior written consent; or
- Acknowledge that consent is a condition to closing.

**Why it matters.** When the Ridgeholt transaction closes without Drenind's prior written consent, Vaitix will be in material breach. Drenind will have the right to terminate immediately (no cure period) and claim the Early Termination Fee. Based on Order Form §9.1:

- ETF component (a): 50% × $95,000 × 12 months (capped) = **$570,000**
- ETF component (b): Unamortized implementation costs. Total $125,000 (Order Form Schedule 2) amortized over 36 months from Go-Live (May 12, 2025). At Q4 2026 closing (~18 months elapsed), remaining = $125,000 × (18/36) = **$62,500**
- **Total potential ETF exposure: approximately $632,500**

In addition, Drenind could use the breach as leverage in the rate adjustment dispute or refuse to provide the 2026 Peak Season capacity plan.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Before closing**, send a formal request for consent under §16.2. Frame it as a condition to closing. Provide Drenind with: (i) the identity of Ridgeholt; (ii) confirmation that Vaitix's legal entity, notice addresses, and payment accounts will not change; (iii) confirmation that current management (COO, VP Supply Chain) will remain; and (iv) a representation that Ridgeholt supports the DTC growth strategy and volume is expected to increase (as stated in Letter §3.3).
- Offer Drenind a commercial concession in exchange for consent (e.g., agreement to a moderated rate increase, a volume commitment, or an extension of the Service Commitment Period).
- If Drenind unreasonably withholds consent, Vaitix should document the request and the business justification. While §16.2 does not contain a "not unreasonably withheld" standard (unlike the subcontracting consent in §16.3), a court may be sympathetic to a customer whose parent is being acquired by a financial sponsor that is increasing, not decreasing, business.
- **Do not allow the transaction to close without either consent or a written waiver from Drenind.**

## Issue 3: Exclusivity Scope – MSA Dramatically Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where:** Term Sheet §§4.1–4.2; MSA §4.1.

**Analysis.** The Term Sheet, which reflected the parties' negotiated commercial understanding, limited exclusivity to:

> "direct-to-consumer e-commerce fulfillment in the continental United States only" (Term Sheet §4.1)

and expressly carved out:
- Wholesale distribution to retail and specialty accounts (§4.2(a));
- Replenishment of Vaitix-owned or partner retail stores (§4.2(b));
- Orders shipped to Alaska, Hawaii, U.S. territories, or outside the United States (§4.2(c));
- Vaitix's existing logistics arrangements in Canada and the European Union (§4.2(d)); and
- Marketplace orders fulfilled by the marketplace operator's own network (§4.2(e)).

The MSA as executed provides:

> "Customer shall use Provider exclusively for **all warehousing, fulfillment, distribution and returns processing** of Customer Products in **North America and the European Union**, through **all sales channels, including wholesale and retail replenishment, direct-to-consumer e-commerce and marketplace channels**. Customer shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, engage any third party, or perform itself, any such services for Customer Products in those territories during the Term." (MSA §4.1)

The MSA eliminates every carve-out in the Term Sheet and expands the geography from the continental U.S. to all of North America and the EU, and the scope from DTC e-commerce fulfillment to all warehousing, distribution, and returns processing across all channels.

**Why it matters.** If Vaitix currently uses any other provider (or performs in-house) for:
- Wholesale distribution in North America or the EU;
- Retail store replenishment;
- Returns processing outside the DTC channel; or
- Any logistics activity in Canada or the EU;

it may be **already in breach** of §4.1. This is a live, ongoing breach that accrues with each day of non-compliant activity. It also means Vaitix cannot pivot its supply chain for any channel or territory without Drenind's consent, severely limiting operational flexibility for the remainder of the Term (at least through May 2029, plus any renewal).

**Recommended fix.**

- Conduct an immediate internal audit of all current logistics arrangements to identify any activity that falls within the scope of MSA §4.1 but outside Drenind's services. This includes any Canadian or EU fulfillment, any wholesale distribution through a third party, and any in-house distribution activity.
- If any such activity exists, either (a) bring it within Drenind's scope by negotiating an Order Form, or (b) seek an amendment to §4.1 to restore the Term Sheet carve-outs.
- In the negotiation with Drenind (tied to the CoC consent and rate dispute), propose an amendment to §4.1 that aligns it with the Term Sheet. The argument is that the MSA as drafted does not reflect the parties' actual agreement, as evidenced by the Term Sheet. While the MSA is the executed document and the Term Sheet is non-binding (Term Sheet §1.3), the discrepancy is so substantial that it suggests a drafting error. If Drenind resists, Vaitix should consider whether to invoke the chronic service level breach (Issue 4) as leverage to negotiate a correction.
- If Vaitix cannot obtain an amendment, it should at minimum document its position that the MSA §4.1 does not reflect the bargained-for deal, to preserve any equitable arguments (reformation, mutual mistake) should a dispute arise.

## Issue 4: Chronic Service Level Failure – Potential Termination Right

**Where:** MSA §8.3; Order Form §7.1 (SL-2 designated Critical); Vaitix Letter Annex B.

**Analysis.** Section 8.3 of the MSA provides:

> "If Provider fails to meet the same critical Service Level (as designated in the Order Form) in any three (3) months within any rolling six (6) month period, or fails to meet three (3) or more critical Service Levels in any single month, the failure is a material breach of this Agreement for the purposes of Section 12.4, and **no cure period will apply**."

From Annex B to the August 14 letter, SL-2 (Same-day ship, target 99.5%, designated Critical in Order Form §7.1) was missed in:
- October 2025: 99.1%
- November 2025: 98.7%
- December 2025: 99.2%

These are three consecutive months of failure on the same critical Service Level, well within any rolling six-month period. The Order Form §7.5 Stabilization Period covers only the first two full calendar months after Go-Live (June and July 2025), so these failures count toward §8.3.

This constitutes a material breach under §8.3, and under §12.4, Vaitix may terminate the Agreement or the affected Order Form by written notice. Because §8.3 states "no cure period will apply," Drenind cannot cure.

**Why it matters.** This is Vaitix's strongest contractual lever. It gives Vaitix the right to:
- Terminate the Order Form (and potentially the MSA) without paying the Early Termination Fee (termination for Provider's breach under §12.4 does not trigger the ETF, which applies only "If Provider terminates this Agreement or an Order Form under this Section 12.4 because of Customer's material breach" per MSA §12.4, second sentence);
- Demand Transition Services at no charge for items (b) through (e) under MSA §13.2; and
- Use the threat of termination as negotiating leverage on the rate adjustment, the exclusivity scope, and the CoC consent.

However, Vaitix has not yet exercised this right. The letter references the failures only as context for the rate objection (§2.2(e)) and does not put Drenind on notice of a §8.3 breach or reserve termination rights explicitly.

**Recommended fix.**

- Send a supplemental notice (or include in the CoC consent request letter) that expressly identifies the SL-2 failures in October–December 2025 as a chronic failure under §8.3 constituting a material breach, and that Vaitix reserves all rights and remedies including termination under §12.4.
- Do not terminate at this time unless the strategy calls for it. The right to terminate is more valuable as leverage than as an exercised remedy, given that Vaitix needs Drenind's cooperation for the 2026 Peak Season and the CoC consent.
- Track SL-4 (Inventory accuracy) failures: March 2026 (99.4%) and May 2026 (99.5%). If a third failure occurs within a rolling six-month period, that would be a second independent chronic failure, strengthening Vaitix's position further.
- Ensure the Corrective Action Plan for SL-4 (due August 31, 2026 per Letter §5.1) is delivered and is meaningful. If it is not, that is an additional breach of MSA §8.2.

## Issue 5: Amendment No. 1 Expands "Affiliate" to Include Network Partners – Assignment and Confidentiality Risk

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §2.1; MSA §§10.3, 16.1, 16.2.

**Analysis.** Amendment No. 1 §2.1 amends and restates the definition of "Affiliate" in MSA §1.1 "for all purposes of the Agreement" to add:

> "(b) in the case of Provider, any Network Partner. 'Network Partner' means any third-party logistics provider with which Provider has a written capacity-sharing or overflow agreement."

This means any 3PL with which Drenind has a capacity-sharing or overflow agreement is now Drenind's "Affiliate" for all purposes of the MSA. The consequences:

1. **Assignment (MSA §16.1):** "Either party may assign this Agreement to an Affiliate without the consent of the other party upon written notice." Drenind could potentially assign the entire MSA to a Network Partner with only written notice to Vaitix. While this seems extreme, the language is unambiguous.

2. **Confidential Information (MSA §10.3):** The Receiving Party may disclose to "its and its Affiliates' employees, officers, directors, professional advisers, financing sources, auditors and subcontractors who need to know it." Drenind could share Vaitix's Confidential Information (including Customer Data, product plans, sales volumes, pricing) with any Network Partner.

3. **Change of Control (MSA §16.2):** If a Network Partner acquires a controlling interest in Drenind, the expanded Affiliate definition could complicate the CoC analysis (though §16.2 focuses on the party itself or its controller, not its Affiliates).

**Why it matters.** Vaitix's competitive intelligence—volume data, pricing, product launch calendars, margin information—is Confidential Information under MSA §10.1. Allowing Drenind to share it with an undefined class of "Network Partners" (any 3PL with a capacity-sharing agreement) is a significant data protection and competitive risk. The assignment risk is more theoretical but still concerning.

**Recommended fix.**

- Seek an amendment to Amendment No. 1 §2.1 that either (a) removes the Network Partner expansion from the "Affiliate" definition, or (b) limits it to specific purposes (e.g., "for purposes of Section 4.3 and Schedule B only") rather than "for all purposes of the Agreement."
- In the interim, send a notice to Drenind identifying the Network Partners currently in Schedule B (the Obetz Annex and Plainfield facilities are operated by Drenind itself, so no third-party Network Partners are currently listed). Request confirmation that no other Network Partners exist and that no assignment or confidentiality disclosure has been or will be made to any Network Partner.
- Add a requirement that any future Network Partner be identified to Vaitix in writing before being treated as an Affiliate, and that Vaitix's prior written approval is required for any assignment to a Network Partner.

## Issue 8: Order of Precedence Favors MSA Over Order Form

**Where:** MSA §2.3.

**Analysis.** Section 2.3 establishes the following order of precedence: (a) Amendment No. 1; (b) the MSA; (c) the Quality Agreement; and (d) the Order Form. The Order Form has the **lowest** precedence.

This means that where the Order Form contains specific protections for Vaitix (e.g., the Minimum Monthly Fee reductions in §5.3, the service credit percentages in §7.2, the stabilization period in §7.5, the ETF mitigation in §9.2), a general provision in the MSA could override them if interpreted as in conflict.

**Why it matters.** For example:
- MSA §5.6 states: "If an Order Form sets out a minimum monthly fee or minimum volume commitment, Customer shall pay the amount specified in the Order Form in accordance with its terms." This is consistent with the Order Form, but the precedence rule means that if any other MSA provision were read to modify the minimum fee mechanism, the MSA would control.
- MSA §6.5 provides that service credits "are not Customer's sole and exclusive remedy, except to the extent an Order Form expressly provides otherwise." The Order Form §7.3 confirms they are not the exclusive remedy. This is consistent, but the structure creates risk if future amendments to the MSA are not carefully checked against Order Form provisions.

**Recommended fix.**

- This is a structural issue that is difficult to fix after the fact without amending the MSA. In any future amendment (including the one needed to address the CoC consent or exclusivity scope), propose reordering the precedence to: (a) Amendment No. 1; (b) the Order Form; (c) the MSA; (d) the Quality Agreement. The rationale is that the more specific document should control over the more general one.
- In the interim, ensure that all future Order Forms and amendments explicitly state that they are intended to supplement (not be overridden by) the MSA, and that any apparent conflict is resolved in favor of the Order Form.
- Flag this issue for the client's operations team: when reviewing any Drenind communication or invoice, check it against the Order Form first, and if there's a tension with the MSA, escalate to legal.

## Issue 9: Parent Guaranty – Governing Law and Jurisdiction Mismatch

**Where:** MSA §18.1–18.2 (Delaware law, New Castle County forum); Parent Guaranty §9.1–9.2 (New York law, New York County forum).

**Analysis.** The MSA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. The Parent Guaranty is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York. If Vaitix needs to enforce both the MSA (against Drenind Fulfillment Services, LLC) and the Guaranty (against Drenind Logistics Group, Inc.) in connection with the same dispute, it would need to file in two different jurisdictions under two different bodies of law.

**Why it matters.** This creates:
- Litigation complexity and cost (two forums, two sets of procedural rules);
- Risk of inconsistent outcomes;
- Strategic difficulty in coordinating a demand on the Guarantor with a claim against the Provider;
- Potential for Drenind Logistics Group to raise New York-specific defenses or procedural arguments unavailable under Delaware law.

Additionally, Guaranty §2.6 provides that "Any limitation or exclusion of liability under Section 15 of the Agreement applies to Guarantor's liability for the corresponding Guaranteed Obligations." This means the $2M cap (MSA §15.2) and consequential damages exclusion (MSA §15.1) apply to the Guarantor. The guaranty is therefore not a full-uncapped guarantee of all of Drenind's obligations—it mirrors Drenind's own liability limits.

**Recommended fix.**

- In any renegotiation (tied to the CoC consent or exclusivity amendment), request that the Guaranty be amended to conform its governing law and jurisdiction to Delaware/New Castle County, consistent with the MSA.
- If Drenind resists, at minimum seek a provision allowing Vaitix to join the Guarantor in any Delaware action under the MSA (i.e., a non-exclusive jurisdiction clause in the Guaranty for actions brought in the MSA forum).
- Be aware that the liability cap in the Guaranty limits its practical value. For claims within the §15.3 exceptions (indemnities, data breaches, gross negligence/fraud, product loss, wrongful refusal to release, payment obligations), the Guarantor is fully exposed. For other claims, the Guarantor's exposure is capped at the greater of trailing 12-month fees or $2M. Given that Vaitix's annual fees are approximately $1.79M (Letter Annex A), the cap is likely the $2M floor, which provides meaningful but not unlimited protection.

## Issue 10: Automatic Renewal and Long-Term Lock-In

**Where:** MSA §§12.1–12.2; Order Form §3.1.

**Analysis.** The Initial Term runs until the end of the Service Commitment Period under the first Order Form: 48 months from the Go-Live Date of May 12, 2025, i.e., until May 12, 2029 (MSA §12.1; Order Form §3.1). Upon expiry, the MSA automatically renews for successive 48-month Renewal Terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before expiry (MSA §12.2). The first non-renewal notice deadline is therefore **February 11, 2029**.

**Why it matters.** Vaitix is committed to Drenind for a minimum of 48 months (through May 2029) and, if it misses the February 2029 deadline, for another 48 months (through May 2033). Combined with:
- The broad exclusivity in MSA §4.1 (Issue 3);
- The ETF of approximately $632,500 if Vaitix terminates for its own breach;
- The Change of Control restriction in §16.2;

Vaitix has very limited exit flexibility. If the Ridgeholt transaction changes Vaitix's strategic direction (e.g., consolidation of logistics, in-sourcing, or a different 3PL for certain channels), Vaitix would face significant costs.

**Recommended fix.**

- Calendar the February 11, 2029 non-renewal notice deadline immediately. Set a reminder for no later than December 2028 to begin the decision process.
- In the current negotiation with Drenind (CoC consent, rate dispute, exclusivity amendment), seek to insert a provision allowing Vaitix to terminate without ETF upon a Change of Control of Vaitix, provided Vaitix gives 180 days' notice and pays Fees through the termination date. This is a common provision in commercial agreements and would protect Vaitix's flexibility in the event of a future sale or restructuring.
- Consider whether the 48-month Service Commitment Period is appropriate given Vaitix's growth trajectory. If volumes are growing (as the Ridgeholt transaction suggests), the Minimum Monthly Fee becomes less of a constraint, but the exclusivity and ETF remain significant.

## Issue 14: Mutual NDA – Ongoing Obligations and Additional Protection

**Where:** NDA §§3.1, 9.1–9.2.

**Analysis.** The NDA's disclosure period runs until the earlier of November 4, 2026 (second anniversary) or written notice ending discussions (§9.1). Since the MSA was signed in March 2025, the NDA's purpose has been substantially fulfilled, but the disclosure period has not yet ended. The confidentiality obligations continue for three years after the end of the disclosure period (§9.2), i.e., until approximately November 2029.

NDA §3.1 contains a useful restriction: "Drenind shall not use Vaitix's Confidential Information to solicit, price or plan services for any other customer." This provides an additional layer of protection beyond MSA §10 during the NDA's remaining term.

**Why it matters.** The NDA's non-solicitation-of-business provision (§3.1) and the MSA's confidentiality provisions (§10) operate in parallel. If Drenind uses Vaitix's volume data, pricing, or operational information to bid on or service another customer in a competitive segment, Vaitix has a claim under both the NDA and the MSA. The NDA also provides for attorneys' fees to the prevailing party (§11.2), which the MSA does not.

**Recommended fix.**

- If the exclusivity issue (Issue 3) leads to a dispute or termination, the NDA's continued confidentiality obligations (through ~November 2029) will protect Vaitix's information during any transition period.

## Issue 15: MSA §15.3(d) – Product Loss Not Subject to Per-Pound Limitation

**Where:** MSA §§7.5, 7.6, 15.3(d).

**Analysis.** Section 15.3(d) provides that the liability cap and consequential damages exclusion do not apply to "Provider's liability for loss of or damage to Customer Products under Section 7, which is governed by Section 7.6 and is not subject to any per-pound, per-package or similar limitation." Combined with §7.5 (Provider bears risk of loss from delivery to Facility until tender to carrier) and §7.6 (reimbursement at Customer's landed cost for shrinkage above the 0.10% allowance), Vaitix has a strong position on product loss.

**Why it matters.** This is a favorable provision that should be preserved. It means that if Drenind loses or damages Vaitix's inventory, Vaitix can recover at full landed cost without being subject to the general $2M cap or any per-unit limitation. This is particularly important for high-value outdoor equipment.

**Recommended fix.** No action needed. Ensure that any amendment to the MSA does not inadvertently modify §15.3(d) or §7.6.
