## I. CRITICAL ISSUES REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ACTION

### 1. Change of Control – Ridgeholt Transaction (MSA §16.2; Letter ¶3)

**Where:** MSA §16.2 (Change of Control); Vaitix Letter to Drenind dated August 14, 2026, ¶¶3.1–3.4.

**Issue:** The letter discloses that Vaitix Holdings, LLC (sole member of Vaitix) signed a definitive agreement on July 30, 2026 to sell 100% of its equity to Ridgeholt Capital Partners, L.P., with closing expected in Q4 2026. Under MSA §16.2(a), this is a "Change of Control" of Vaitix (acquisition of >50% of voting equity of an entity that directly or indirectly controls Vaitix). Section 16.2 provides that a Change of Control "is deemed an assignment of this Agreement" and "requires the prior written consent of the other party." A Change of Control effected without consent "is a material breach of this Agreement that entitles the other party to terminate this Agreement under Section 12.4, without any cure period."

The letter frames the disclosure as "a courtesy" (¶3.1) and does not request Drenind's consent. As drafted, it puts Vaitix at risk of being in material breach the moment the Ridgeholt transaction closes if Drenind has not given written consent.

**Why it matters:** If Drenind treats the closing as an unconsented Change of Control, it can terminate the MSA immediately without a cure period. Given the 48-month Service Commitment Period (Order Form §3.1) and the fact that Vaitix is mid-negotiation on rates, Drenind would have significant leverage. Conversely, if Vaitix wants to preserve the relationship under new ownership, it needs Drenind's buy-in now.

**Recommended fix:**
- Send a supplemental letter (or add to the next commercial meeting agenda) that expressly requests Drenind's written consent to the Change of Control under §16.2, providing the information Drenind would reasonably need (identity of Ridgeholt, expected closing date, confirmation that Vaitix remains the contracting entity, management continuity).
- Do not allow the Ridgeholt closing to occur before receiving Drenind's written consent. If Drenind unreasonably withholds consent, document the request and seek a determination that consent was unreasonably withheld (though the MSA does not contain a "not unreasonably withheld" standard for §16.2 consent, which cuts against Vaitix).
- Consider whether to negotiate a side letter or amendment confirming that the Change of Control will not trigger the Early Termination Fee (which §16.2 provides for where Customer undergoes the Change of Control).

### 2. Exclusivity Scope – Material Discrepancy Between Term Sheet and MSA (Term Sheet §4; MSA §4.1)

**Where:** Term Sheet §§4.1–4.2; MSA §4.1.

**Issue:** The Term Sheet (Jan. 17, 2025) scoped exclusivity narrowly: "Vaitix will use Drenind on an exclusive basis for direct-to-consumer e-commerce fulfillment in the continental United States only" (§4.1). Section 4.2 then carved out wholesale distribution, retail store replenishment, Alaska/Hawaii/territories/international, existing Canada and EU arrangements, and marketplace-fulfilled orders.

The executed MSA (Mar. 1, 2025) §4.1 is dramatically broader: "Customer shall use Provider exclusively for **all** warehousing, fulfillment, distribution and returns processing of Customer Products in **North America and the European Union**, through **all sales channels**, including **wholesale and retail replenishment**, direct-to-consumer e-commerce and **marketplace channels**."

This expansion was not flagged in the Term Sheet's binding provisions (§15.1), and the Term Sheet is non-binding as to commercial terms (§1.3). The MSA controls.

**Why it matters:** If Vaitix has any existing or planned wholesale distribution, retail replenishment, or EU fulfillment arrangements with other providers (or performed in-house), it is potentially in breach of MSA §4.1 as of the Effective Date. The Term Sheet expressly contemplated that Vaitix would retain its "existing logistics arrangements in Canada and the European Union" (§4.2(d)) and would remain free to handle wholesale and retail replenishment itself (§4.2(a)–(b)). The MSA eliminated all of those carve-outs.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with Vaitix's supply chain team whether any current operations (wholesale, retail replenishment, EU, Canada) fall within the MSA's exclusivity scope. If so, Vaitix is in ongoing breach.
- Negotiate an amendment to MSA §4.1 restoring the Term Sheet's exclusivity scope (DTC e-commerce, continental US only, with the §4.2 carve-outs). Frame it as a correction of a drafting error that departed from the agreed commercial deal.
- If Drenind resists, at minimum seek a written acknowledgment of which channels and territories are covered, and a cure period for any existing non-compliant arrangements.
- If Vaitix cannot get the exclusivity narrowed, assess the cost of bringing all North American and EU fulfillment in-house or through Drenind, versus the risk of a material breach claim.

### 3. Chronic Service Level Failure – SL-2 Same-Day Ship (MSA §8.3; Order Form §7.1; Letter Annex B)

**Where:** MSA §8.3 (Chronic Failure); Order Form §7.1 (SL-2 designated "Critical"); Letter Annex B (performance data).

**Issue:** SL-2 (same-day ship, 99.5% target, designated Critical) was missed in three consecutive months: October 2025 (99.1%), November 2025 (98.7%), and December 2025 (99.2%). MSA §8.3 provides: "If Provider fails to meet the same critical Service Level (as designated in the Order Form) in any three (3) months within any rolling six (6) month period … the failure is a material breach of this Agreement for the purposes of Section 12.4, and **no cure period will apply**."

The Order Form §7.5 stabilization period (first two full calendar months after Go-Live) ended in July 2025 and does not protect Drenind for the October–December 2025 failures.

**Why it matters:** Vaitix has a present, unexercised right to terminate the MSA (or the affected Order Form) for material breach without a cure period under §12.4. This is powerful leverage in the current rate negotiation. However, if Vaitix has continued to perform and accept services without asserting this right, Drenind could argue waiver (though MSA §19.5 provides that no failure or delay operates as a waiver).

**Recommended fix:**
- Preserve the right by expressly reserving it in writing (the August 14 letter ¶2.2(e) references the SL-2 failures in the context of opposing the rate increase, but does not expressly invoke §8.3 or reserve the termination right). Send a short supplemental notice stating that Vaitix acknowledges the chronic failure under §8.3, reserves all rights and remedies including termination under §12.4, and will not be deemed to have waived that right.
- Use the chronic failure as a negotiating lever in the rate adjustment discussion: Vaitix is entitled to push back on an 11.5% increase while Drenind is in material breach.
- If Vaitix wants to terminate, it should do so promptly and in accordance with §12.4 (written notice describing the breach). Note that termination for Provider's breach entitles Vaitix to Transition Services at no charge under MSA §13.2 (second sentence).

### 4. Rate Adjustment Notice – Active Dispute (MSA §5.4; Order Form Schedule 1; Letter ¶¶1–2)

**Where:** MSA §5.4 (Rate Adjustments); Letter ¶¶1.1–2.5 and Annex A.

**Issue:** Drenind issued a Rate Adjustment Notice on June 26, 2026 (received June 29), proposing an across-the-board 11.5% increase effective September 1, 2026. Vaitix objected in writing on August 14, 2026, within the 30-day objection window under §5.4(c). The objection is timely and valid.

Under §5.4, because Vaitix gave a timely objection: (a) pre-adjustment Fees continue to apply pending negotiation; (b) the parties must negotiate in good faith for 45 days after Drenind's receipt of the objection (i.e., through approximately mid-September 2026); and (c) if no agreement is reached, Vaitix may terminate the affected Order Form on ≥90 days' notice without paying the Early Termination Fee.

**Why it matters:** Vaitix's estimated annual cost increase is $205,747 (Letter Annex A, Part 2), and would be higher at forecast 2027 volumes. The termination right in §5.4(c) is a walk-away that does not trigger the ETF – this is Vaitix's strongest structural protection against an unreasonable rate increase.

**Recommended fix:**
- Continue paying at current rates through September 1, 2026 (as the letter states in ¶2.3). If Drenind invoices at the increased rates, treat the delta as a disputed amount under §6.3 and withhold.
- Press for the documentation requested in Letter ¶2.4 (cost breakdown, labor rate data, occupancy allocation, packaging invoices). Without it, Drenind's "basis for the adjustment" under §5.4 is conclusory.
- Calendar the 45-day negotiation deadline (approximately mid-September 2026). If no agreement is reached by then, Vaitix should be prepared to issue a termination notice under §5.4(c) if the commercial terms are not acceptable. Before doing so, assess whether a successor 3PL is available (the exclusivity issue in ¶2 above would need to be resolved first).
- Leverage the chronic SL-2 failure (Issue 3) and the service level performance record in Annex B to argue that a rate increase is inappropriate while Drenind is underperforming.

### 5. Peak Season 2026 – Capacity Plan Not Delivered (Amendment No. 1 §5.2; Letter ¶4.2)

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §5.2 (Capacity Plan); Letter ¶4.2.

**Issue:** Vaitix delivered its Peak Forecast on June 30, 2026 (Letter ¶4.1). Amendment No. 1 §5.2 requires Provider to deliver a written capacity plan "within fifteen (15) Business Days after receipt of the Peak Forecast." That deadline fell approximately July 21, 2026. As of the August 14 letter, the capacity plan has not been delivered.

**Why it matters:** Without the capacity plan, Vaitix cannot make an informed decision about whether to exercise a Capacity Reservation under Amendment No. 1 §5.3 (deadline: September 30, 2026). The capacity plan is supposed to confirm Base Daily Capacity, identify Overflow Facilities and their daily overflow capacity, describe the labor plan, and identify constraints. If Vaitix misses the September 30 deadline for a Capacity Reservation because Drenind hasn't provided the information needed to decide, Vaitix could face a capacity shortfall during the 2026 Peak Season with no contractual protection.

Additionally, Amendment No. 1 §5.4 provides that if Provider fails to make available reserved capacity, Customer is not required to pay the reservation fee and §17.3 (Force Majeure alternative arrangements) applies. But Vaitix can't invoke §5.4 if it never made a reservation because it didn't have the information to do so.

**Recommended fix:**
- Escalate the request for the capacity plan at the August monthly business review (Letter ¶6.1(a)). Put it in writing again with a firm deadline (e.g., August 22, 2026) and state that if it is not received, Vaitix will make a Capacity Reservation based on its own assessment of needs and will hold Drenind to §5.4 for any shortfall.
- If the capacity plan is not delivered by early September, consider making a Capacity Reservation for the maximum anticipated overflow to lock in capacity, then negotiate down. The reservation fee is $0.40 per reserved order per Business Day (Schedule A), which is modest relative to the cost of a capacity failure during peak.
- Document the breach of §5.2. It supports Vaitix's position in the rate negotiation and, if the Peak Season goes poorly, a claim for damages.

### 6. Shrinkage Reconciliation Overdue – Monetary Claim (Order Form §§6.2–6.3; Letter ¶5.2)

**Where:** Order Form §6.2 (Shrinkage Allowance: 0.10%); Order Form §6.3 (Annual Reconciliation: due within 30 days after end of Contract Year); Letter ¶5.2.

**Issue:** The first Contract Year ended February 28, 2026. The shrinkage reconciliation was due by March 30, 2026. Drenind delivered a draft on April 14, 2026 (15 days late). Vaitix sent comments on May 1, 2026. As of August 14, 2026, no final reconciliation has been delivered. The draft shows shrinkage of approximately 0.14% of units received, exceeding the 0.10% allowance by 0.04%.

**Why it matters:** Under Order Form §6.2, Provider shall reimburse Customer at landed cost for all shrinkage above the allowance. The reimbursement is made "by credit on the invoice issued after the annual reconciliation." Drenind is in breach of its §6.3 obligation to deliver the final reconciliation, and Vaitix has a quantifiable monetary claim for the excess shrinkage.

**Recommended fix:**
- Demand the final reconciliation in writing with a specific deadline (e.g., 10 Business Days). State that if not received, Vaitix will calculate the excess shrinkage itself from the data in the draft and the monthly activity reports, and will debit the amount from the next invoice under §6.3 (disputed amounts) or seek a direct payment.
- Quantify the claim: 0.04% of units received in the Contract Year ended February 28, 2026, at Vaitix's landed cost per unit. Based on ~44,600 orders/month × 2.1 units × 12 months ≈ 1.12 million units received, 0.04% ≈ 448 units. At an average landed cost (to be confirmed with Vaitix), this is a specific dollar amount.
- Reserve the right to audit under MSA §6.6 if the final reconciliation is not satisfactory.

## III. MODERATE ISSUES

### 8. Expanded "Affiliate" Definition – Network Partner Exposure (Amendment No. 1 §2.1; MSA §§9.1, 10.3, 16.1)

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §2.1 (amends MSA §1.1 definition of "Affiliate" to include "Network Partners" for Provider); MSA §9.1 (Customer Data), §10.3 (Confidential Information disclosure to Affiliates), §16.1 (assignment to Affiliates without consent).

**Issue:** Amendment No. 1 §2.1 adds to the definition of "Affiliate" (for Provider) "any Network Partner," defined as "any third-party logistics provider with which Provider has a written capacity-sharing or overflow agreement." This is a broad expansion. A Network Partner is not a corporate Affiliate in the traditional sense – it is an independent third-party 3PL.

Under MSA §10.3, the Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to its "Affiliates' employees, officers, directors … who need to know it." Under MSA §9.1, Customer Data may be shared with "approved subcontractors who need access to perform the Services and are bound by obligations at least as protective as those in this Agreement." The Network Partner expansion could be read to allow broader data and information sharing with third-party 3PLs than the original MSA contemplated.

Additionally, MSA §16.1 permits assignment to an "Affiliate" without consent. While it would be a stretch to argue that a Network Partner can receive an assignment of the MSA, the expanded definition creates ambiguity.

**Why it matters:** Vaitix's Customer Data includes end-customer Personal Information, pricing, sales volumes, and product plans. Its Confidential Information includes the terms of the Agreement itself. If Drenind can share these with Network Partners (who are not corporate Affiliates but independent businesses) under the "Affiliate" umbrella, Vaitix's data protection and confidentiality posture is weakened.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek clarification or an amendment confirming that the Network Partner addition to "Affiliate" does not expand the scope of §10.3 (Confidential Information disclosure) or §9.1 (Customer Data sharing) beyond what is already permitted for "approved subcontractors" under §16.3.
- Require that before any Customer Data or Customer Products are made available to a Network Partner (other than the two Overflow Facility operators already identified in Schedule B to Amendment No. 1), Drenind provides written notice and the Network Partner executes data protection and confidentiality undertakings in a form acceptable to Vaitix.
- Note that Amendment No. 1 §4.3 already requires that operators of Overflow Facilities be bound by written obligations consistent with MSA §§7.1, 7.2, 9 and 10 before Customer Products or Data are made available. Confirm this is being enforced for the Obetz Annex and Plainfield facilities.

### 9. Parent Guaranty – Governing Law and Jurisdiction Mismatch (Parent Guaranty §9; MSA §18)

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §§9.1–9.2 (New York law; New York County courts); MSA §§18.1–18.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County, Delaware courts).

**Issue:** The MSA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. The Parent Guaranty is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York. If Vaitix needs to enforce the Guaranty (e.g., if Drenind becomes insolvent or fails to release products), it must do so in New York, while any underlying MSA claim would be in Delaware.

**Why it matters:** This creates practical complications: (a) Vaitix may need to litigate in two forums; (b) the New York forum may not have personal jurisdiction over Drenind (the Provider) for the underlying MSA claims, so Vaitix might need to join Drenind in Delaware separately; (c) the different governing laws could produce different outcomes on issues like the scope of the guaranty or available defenses.

That said, the Guaranty is strongly drafted in Vaitix's favor (primary obligor, not surety – §2.1; guaranty of payment not collection – §2.4; broad waivers – §4; reinstatement – §5; subordination of Provider's debts to Guarantor – §6.2). The mismatch is more of a logistical inconvenience than a substantive risk.

**Recommended fix:**
- Note for enforcement planning: if a Guaranty claim arises, it will be brought in New York. Ensure Vaitix has NY counsel identified or on standby.
- At the next amendment opportunity (e.g., if negotiating the exclusivity fix or rate adjustment), consider requesting that the Guaranty's governing law and forum be conformed to Delaware. Low priority given the Guaranty's strong terms, but cleaner if achieved.

### 11. Automatic Renewal – Calendar Risk (MSA §12.2; Order Form §3.1)

**Where:** MSA §12.2 (Renewal); Order Form §3.1 (Service Commitment Period: 48 months from Go-Live).

**Issue:** The MSA auto-renews for successive 48-month Renewal Terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before expiry. If Go-Live was May 12, 2025, the Initial Term ends May 12, 2029. Vaitix must give non-renewal notice by approximately February 11, 2029 (90 days prior) to avoid a further 48-month lock-in.

**Why it matters:** Combined with the rate adjustment dispute and the Change of Control, Vaitix needs to be strategic about whether it wants to renew. If the Ridgeholt transaction changes Vaitix's growth trajectory or logistics strategy, the auto-renewal could lock Vaitix in on unfavorable terms.

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (February 11, 2029) immediately. Set internal reminders at 180 days and 120 days before the deadline.
- Factor the renewal decision into the current rate negotiation: if Vaitix is going to renew, it should lock in favorable multi-year rate terms now. If it may not renew, it should preserve its exit options (including the §5.4(c) termination right if the rate negotiation fails).

### 13. Order of Precedence – Order Form Has Lowest Priority (MSA §2.3)

**Where:** MSA §2.3: Precedence is (a) Amendment No. 1; (b) MSA; (c) Quality Agreement; (d) Order Form.

**Issue:** The Order Form contains the most specific commercial terms (fees, service levels, minimum monthly fee, shrinkage allowance, ETF calculation). Yet it has the lowest precedence. If there is any conflict between the Order Form and the MSA, the MSA controls.

**Why it matters:** In practice, conflicts are unlikely to arise because the Order Form is drafted to be consistent with the MSA. But if a dispute arises over, e.g., the scope of service credits or the calculation of the Minimum Monthly Fee, Drenind could argue that a general MSA provision overrides the specific Order Form term.

**Recommended fix:** No immediate action. Be aware of this hierarchy when interpreting any ambiguous cross-reference. If a future amendment is negotiated, consider whether the precedence should be adjusted (e.g., Order Form should control over MSA for the specific commercial terms it addresses).
