## Issue 1: Rate Adjustment Objection Likely Untimely

**Where:** MSA § 5.4; Vaitix Letter § 1.1, § 2.1; Order Form § 5.1

**Facts.** Drenind's Rate Adjustment Notice was received by Vaitix on June 29, 2026 (Letter § 1.1). MSA § 5.4 provides that "Customer may object to a Rate Adjustment Notice by written notice to Provider given within thirty (30) days after receipt." Thirty days after June 29, 2026 is July 29, 2026. Vaitix's objection letter is dated August 14, 2026—16 days after the deadline.

**Why it matters.** MSA § 5.4 states: "If Customer does not give a timely objection, the adjusted Fees set out in the Rate Adjustment Notice will be deemed accepted by Customer and will apply from the effective date stated in the Rate Adjustment Notice." The effective date is September 1, 2026. If the objection is deemed untimely, the 11.5% increase (approximately $205,747 per year at current volumes, per Letter Annex A) is contractually locked in, and Vaitix loses the right to terminate the Order Form without the Early Termination Fee under § 5.4(c).

**Recommended fix.**

- Immediately assess whether Drenind has acknowledged or disputed the timeliness of the objection. If Drenind has not raised the issue, Vaitix should not draw attention to it, but should be prepared for Drenind to assert deemed acceptance.
- If Drenind asserts the objection was late, Vaitix should argue that (a) the Rate Adjustment Notice itself was deficient in content (no supporting data, no cost breakdown, no attribution analysis—Letter § 1.2), and that a notice lacking the "basis for the adjustment" required by § 5.4 does not start the 30-day clock; and (b) in any event, Drenind's continued acceptance of payments at the old rates and its engagement in the negotiation process constitute a waiver or estoppel.
- As a fallback, Vaitix should preserve its right to treat the increased amounts as disputed under MSA § 6.3 (withhold in good faith, pay undisputed portion) and should not pay the increased rates without a reservation of rights.
- Going forward, implement an internal calendar system that tracks all contractual notice deadlines from the date of receipt, not the date of drafting.

## Issue 2: Exclusivity Scope – MSA Materially Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where:** MSA § 4.1; Term Sheet §§ 4.1–4.2; Order Form § 2.1(c)

**Facts.** The Term Sheet (the commercial baseline) agreed to exclusivity for "direct-to-consumer e-commerce fulfillment in the continental United States only" (Term Sheet § 4.1), with express carve-outs for: (a) wholesale distribution; (b) retail store replenishment; (c) Alaska, Hawaii, U.S. territories and international; (d) Vaitix's existing Canada and EU logistics; and (e) marketplace-fulfilled orders (Term Sheet § 4.2).

The executed MSA § 4.1 provides: "Customer shall use Provider exclusively for all warehousing, fulfillment, distribution and returns processing of Customer Products in North America and the European Union, through all sales channels, including wholesale and retail replenishment, direct-to-consumer e-commerce and marketplace channels."

**Why it matters.** The MSA eliminates every carve-out in the Term Sheet. Vaitix is now contractually prohibited from:
- Using any other 3PL or performing its own wholesale distribution in North America or the EU;
- Replenishing its own or partner retail stores through any channel other than Drenind;
- Shipping to Alaska, Hawaii, or internationally through any other provider;
- Using marketplace fulfillment (e.g., Amazon FBA) for marketplace orders.

Critically, the Order Form (§ 2.1(c)) only obligates Drenind to perform D2C e-commerce and connected marketplace storefront services. There is no Order Form covering wholesale, retail replenishment, or international. MSA § 4.2 requires the parties to "negotiate in good faith" a new Order Form for new channels, but it does not obligate Drenind to accept those channels. Vaitix is thus locked out of using any other provider for wholesale and retail, while Drenind has no corresponding obligation to perform those services.

**Recommended fix.**

- Raise this at the September commercial meeting (Letter § 6.1(b)) as a primary negotiation item. Seek an amendment to MSA § 4.1 restoring the Term Sheet carve-outs, or at minimum adding a proviso that the exclusivity obligation applies only to Services for which an executed Order Form is in effect.
- If Drenind resists, consider whether the deviation from the Term Sheet supports a claim of mutual mistake or non-conformity to the agreed commercial terms. The Term Sheet is non-binding (Term Sheet § 1.3), but it is admissible as evidence of the parties' intent during contract formation.
- In the interim, if Vaitix is currently performing any excluded activity (e.g., wholesale shipping through a different carrier or 3PL), it should cease or document a good-faith reliance on the Term Sheet scope to mitigate breach exposure.

## Issue 3: Change of Control – Ridgeholt Transaction Requires Drenind's Consent

**Where:** MSA § 16.2; Letter § 3.1–3.4; Order Form § 9.1

**Facts.** MSA § 16.2 provides that "Any Change of Control of a party is deemed an assignment of this Agreement by that party and requires the prior written consent of the other party." "Change of Control" includes "the acquisition by any person or group of persons acting together, directly or indirectly, of beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity of that party or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls such party" (§ 16.2(a)).

The Ridgeholt transaction involves the sale of 100% of Vaitix Holdings, LLC (Vaitix's sole member) to Ridgeholt Capital Partners, L.P. (Letter § 3.1). This is squarely a Change of Control of Vaitix under § 16.2(a).

Vaitix disclosed the transaction in its August 14, 2026 letter as a "courtesy" (§ 3.1) but did not request Drenind's consent. The letter states that "Vaitix will remain the contracting party" and that management will continue (§ 3.2), but it does not address the consent requirement.

**Why it matters.** MSA § 16.2 provides: "Any Change of Control effected without the other party's prior written consent is a material breach of this Agreement that entitles the other party to terminate this Agreement under Section 12.4, without any cure period, and, where the party undergoing the Change of Control is Customer, to recover the Early Termination Fee."

If Ridgeholt closes (expected Q4 2026) without Drenind's prior written consent, Drenind can:
- Terminate the MSA and all Order Forms immediately, without a cure period; and
- Recover the Early Termination Fee.

The ETF under Order Form § 9.1 is: (a) 50% × $95,000 × up to 12 months = up to $570,000; plus (b) unamortized implementation costs from Schedule 2 ($125,000 total, amortized over 36 months from Go-Live). If Go-Live occurred May 2025, by Q4 2026 approximately 17–19 months of amortization would have elapsed, leaving roughly $57,000–$65,000 unamortized. Total ETF exposure: approximately $627,000–$635,000.

Beyond the ETF, loss of the Drenind relationship mid-contract would disrupt Vaitix's entire North American and EU fulfillment operation (given the broad exclusivity in Issue 2), with 120 days of transition assistance at best (MSA § 13.1).

**Recommended fix.**

- Before Ridgeholt closes, Vaitix must obtain Drenind's prior written consent under § 16.2. This should be a condition to closing in the Ridgeholt definitive agreement, or at minimum a condition that Vaitix's deal team tracks.
- Approach Drenind proactively. Frame the consent request around the points Vaitix already made in Letter § 3.2–3.3: same contracting entity, same management, same operational contacts, expected volume growth. Offer to execute a side letter confirming these points.
- If Drenind conditions consent on commercial concessions (e.g., accepting the rate increase, extending the term, or waiving the exclusivity overreach), Vaitix should evaluate those demands in the context of the overall negotiation.
- Do NOT allow the transaction to close without consent. The consequence is automatic material breach with no cure period.
- Note: The Portland office lease is NOT triggered. Lease § 13.3 expressly states: "A transfer of equity interests in Tenant, or in any entity that owns Tenant, is not an assignment of this Lease." The Ridgeholt deal is an equity transfer at the Vaitix Holdings level.

## Issue 4: Chronic Service Level Failure – Unexercised Termination Right

**Where:** MSA §§ 8.3, 12.4; Order Form §§ 7.1, 7.5; Letter Annex B

**Facts.** Order Form § 7.1 designates SL-2 (same-day ship, 99.5%) as a Critical Service Level. Letter Annex B shows SL-2 was missed in:
- October 2025: 99.1%
- November 2025: 98.7%
- December 2025: 99.2%

MSA § 8.3: "If Provider fails to meet the same critical Service Level in any three (3) months within any rolling six (6) month period... the failure is a material breach of this Agreement for the purposes of Section 12.4, and no cure period will apply."

Three failures of SL-2 within the rolling six-month period of July–December 2025 constitute a chronic failure and a material breach as of December 2025 (or January 2026, when the monthly report confirmed the third failure).

The Order Form § 7.5 Stabilization Period (first two months after Go-Live) does not apply—Go-Live was targeted for May 12, 2025, so the stabilization period ended approximately June 30, 2025.

**Why it matters.** Vaitix holds the right to terminate the MSA or the affected Order Form for material breach without any cure period (MSA § 12.4 read with § 8.3). This right has been outstanding since approximately January 2026. While MSA § 19.5 provides that "No failure or delay by a party in exercising any right or remedy operates as a waiver of it," the longer Vaitix waits, the weaker its practical position becomes—Drenind will argue course of performance, and Vaitix will have accepted service credits and continued the relationship.

This right is also Vaitix's strongest negotiating lever in the rate adjustment dispute (Issue 1) and the exclusivity renegotiation (Issue 2).

**Recommended fix.**

- Do not terminate at this time (the commercial relationship is otherwise functioning and Vaitix is growing volumes). But preserve the right expressly.
- In the September commercial meeting, reference the chronic failure as context for why the 11.5% rate increase is inappropriate (Letter § 2.2(e) already does this). Make clear that Vaitix is aware of its termination right under § 8.3 and has not waived it.
- If Drenind's performance does not improve (particularly SL-2 and SL-4, which remain on open Corrective Action Plans per Letter § 5.1), reassess whether to exercise the termination right or use it as leverage for a more favorable settlement of the rate dispute.
- Document the SL-2 failures in the monthly business review minutes to create a contemporaneous record.

## Issue 5: Auto-Renewal Creates Extended Lock-In

**Where:** MSA §§ 12.1, 12.2; Order Form § 3.1

**Facts.** The Service Commitment Period is 48 months from Go-Live (Order Form § 3.1). Go-Live was targeted for May 12, 2025, so the Initial Term runs to approximately May 2029. MSA § 12.2 provides for automatic renewal for successive Renewal Terms "each Renewal Term being equal in length to the Service Commitment Period set out in the then-current Order Form"—i.e., another 48 months—unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before expiry.

**Why it matters.** Absent action, Vaitix is locked in through approximately May 2033 (48 + 48 months). The non-renewal notice deadline for the first renewal is approximately February 2029. Combined with the broad exclusivity (Issue 2), this means Vaitix cannot use any other 3PL in North America or the EU for nearly eight years from Go-Live unless it renegotiates or terminates for cause.

**Recommended fix.**

- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (approximately February 1, 2029) immediately. Assign ownership to the VP, Supply Chain or General Counsel.
- Use the current rate adjustment negotiation and the Ridgeholt consent process as opportunities to seek a shorter renewal term or a mutual right to reopen commercial terms at the end of the Initial Term.
- If the exclusivity issue (Issue 2) is not resolved, the auto-renewal makes it more acute—Vaitix would be renewing into another 48 months of over-broad exclusivity.

## Issue 7: Shrinkage Reconciliation Overdue and Excess Shrinkage

**Where:** Order Form §§ 6.2, 6.3; MSA § 7.6; Letter § 5.2

**Facts.** The Contract Year ended February 28, 2026. Order Form § 6.3 required the shrinkage reconciliation within 30 days (by March 30, 2026). The amount is not enormous, but the four-month delay in finalizing the reconciliation is a pattern of non-performance that undermines trust and complicates Vaitix's audit rights.

**Recommended fix.**

- Demand the final reconciliation immediately, with a specific deadline (e.g., 10 Business Days). If not delivered, exercise audit rights under MSA § 6.6 (15 Business Days' notice) to reconstruct the numbers from Drenind's records.
- Apply the credit to the next invoice as Order Form § 6.2 requires.

## Issue 8: Parent Guaranty – Different Governing Law and Forum

**Where:** MSA §§ 18.1–18.2; Parent Guaranty §§ 9.1–9.2

**Facts.** The MSA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware (MSA §§ 18.1–18.2). The Parent Guaranty is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York (Guaranty §§ 9.1–9.2).

**Why it matters.** If Vaitix needs to enforce the Guaranty in connection with an MSA dispute (e.g., Drenind becomes insolvent and Vaitix needs the parent to perform Transition Services or release inventory), Vaitix must litigate in New York under New York law, while the underlying MSA dispute is in Delaware under Delaware law. This creates:
- Practical complexity and increased cost (two forums, two sets of counsel);
- Risk of inconsistent interpretations (e.g., what constitutes a "material breach" under Delaware law vs. New York law);
- Potential for Drenind Logistics Group to assert defenses under New York suretyship law that would not be available under Delaware law (though Guaranty § 4.2 waives most defenses).

**Recommended fix.**

- In any renegotiation of the MSA (triggered by the rate dispute, the CoC consent, or the exclusivity issue), seek to conform the Guaranty's governing law and forum to Delaware. The Guaranty § 12.2 requires a written amendment signed by both Beneficiary and Guarantor, so Drenind Logistics Group's agreement is needed.
- If Drenind resists, at minimum seek a provision allowing Vaitix to join a Guarantor in the Delaware MSA action (or vice versa) rather than requiring separate proceedings.
- This is a lower-priority item but should be on the list for the next amendment cycle.

## Issue 9: Amendment No. 1 Expands "Affiliate" to Include Network Partners

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 2.1; MSA §§ 10.3, 16.1

**Facts.** Amendment No. 1 § 2.1 restates the definition of "Affiliate" to add, for Provider: "any Network Partner," defined as "any third-party logistics provider with which Provider has a written capacity-sharing or overflow agreement."

**Why it matters.** Under MSA § 10.3, Provider may disclose Confidential Information to "its and its Affiliates' employees, officers, directors, professional advisers, financing sources, auditors and subcontractors who need to know it." By making Network Partners "Affiliates" of Provider, the amendment potentially expands the universe of persons who can access Vaitix's Confidential Information (which includes Customer Data, product plans, sales volumes, forecasts, pricing—MSA § 10.1) without Vaitix's specific consent.

Additionally, MSA § 16.1 permits assignment to an "Affiliate" without consent. If a Network Partner is an "Affiliate" of Provider, there is an argument that Provider could assign the Agreement to a Network Partner without Vaitix's consent, though this reading is aggressive and likely not intended.

**Mitigating factor.** Amendment No. 1 § 4.3 requires that before any Customer Products or Data are made available to an Overflow Facility operator, the operator must be bound by written obligations consistent with MSA §§ 7.1, 7.2, 9 and 10. So there is a contractual backstop.

**Recommended fix.**

- Seek to narrow the Affiliate amendment in the next round of negotiations. At minimum, add a proviso that Network Partners included as "Affiliates" for purposes of the definition are not treated as Affiliates for purposes of MSA § 16.1 (assignment).
- Confirm that the written obligations required by Amendment No. 1 § 4.3 have been executed for both Overflow Facility operators (Drenind Obetz Annex and Drenind Plainfield). Request copies.
- For any future Overflow Facility added under Amendment No. 1 § 4.2, make execution of the data protection and confidentiality undertakings a condition precedent to Vaitix's approval.

## Issue 10: Service Credits May Undercompensate for SL-5 Returns Failures

**Where:** Order Form § 7.2; Letter Annex B, Note 2; Quality Agreement § 9.6

**Facts.** SL-5 (returns processed and data transmitted within 72 hours for 97% of units) was missed in January 2026 (91.4%) and February 2026 (94.2%). The service credit for SL-5 failure is only 1.0% of Monthly Core Fees (Order Form § 7.2). Letter Annex B, Note 2 states: "The credits applied for SL-5 in January and February 2026 are subject to Vaitix's comments of March 18, 2026 on the measurement of returns received during the post-Peak Season period."

**Why it matters.** Returns data delays directly impact Vaitix's ability to issue refunds to end customers, which drives customer satisfaction, chargeback rates, and marketplace performance metrics. A 1.0% credit on ~$95,000–$150,000 of Monthly Core Fees is only $950–$1,500 per month—far less than the actual cost to Vaitix of delayed refunds (customer service labor, chargebacks, marketplace penalties). The post-Peak Season measurement issue (Note 2) suggests the failures may be even worse than reported if returns received in the 30 days after Peak Season end (through February 28) are properly included.

**Recommended fix.**

- Resolve the measurement dispute (Letter Annex B, Note 2) at the August monthly business review. If the post-Peak Season returns are properly included, the SL-5 failure rates may be lower, strengthening Vaitix's position.
- In the rate negotiation, argue that the service credit structure for SL-5 is inadequate and should be increased, or that Vaitix should be entitled to recover actual damages from delayed refunds in excess of the credit. MSA § 6.5 provides that service credits "are not Customer's sole and exclusive remedy, except to the extent an Order Form expressly provides otherwise for a particular Service Level." The Order Form does not expressly make the SL-5 credit the exclusive remedy, so Vaitix retains the right to claim additional damages.
- Require that the Corrective Action Plan for the returns backlog (if one was delivered) include specific milestones and that failure to meet them triggers the chronic failure analysis.

**Where:** NDA § 3.1; NDA § 9.1–9.2

**Facts.** NDA § 3.1 provides: "Drenind shall not use Vaitix's Confidential Information to solicit, price or plan services for any other customer." - Monitor for any signs that Drenind is using Vaitix's volume or pricing data in proposals to other outdoor/apparel customers. The NDA provides for injunctive relief without bond (NDA § 11.1) and attorneys' fees to the prevailing party (NDA § 11.2).

## Issue 15: Order of Precedence – Potential Conflicts

**Where:** MSA § 2.3; Amendment No. 1 § 7.1; Order Form § 7.1

**Facts.** MSA § 2.3 establishes the order of precedence: (a) Amendment No. 1; (b) MSA; (c) Quality Agreement; (d) Order Form.

Amendment No. 1 § 7.1 relaxes the same-day ship Service Level for overflow orders: "the same-day ship Service Level is measured as shipment by the end of the next Business Day after release of the order." The Order Form § 7.1 sets SL-2 at "99.5%" for same-day ship (orders received by 2:00 p.m. Eastern).

**Why it matters.** Because Amendment No. 1 has the highest precedence, the relaxed next-day standard for overflow orders controls. This means that during peak season, when overflow is triggered, Vaitix's same-day ship commitment to its end customers is effectively degraded to next-day for all overflow orders. Given that peak season is when Vaitix's customers are most sensitive to delivery speed (and when marketplace performance metrics are most closely watched), this is a meaningful service degradation that is baked into the contract.

Additionally, Amendment No. 1 § 7.2 provides that service credits are calculated "on whichever basis results in the higher credit for the affected month" (combined vs. separate measurement). This is protective of Vaitix and should be monitored during peak season.

**Recommended fix.**

- Ensure that Vaitix's customer-facing delivery commitments (on its e-commerce site and marketplace listings) are calibrated to the next-day standard during peak season overflow periods, to avoid setting expectations that Drenind is not contractually required to meet.
- In the post-season review (Amendment No. 1 § 7.4, due within 30 days after January 31), specifically analyze the impact of the relaxed SL-2 on customer satisfaction metrics and chargeback rates. If the impact is material, negotiate a tighter standard for the following year.

## Additional Observations

**A. Implementation Fee Discrepancy.** The Term Sheet Annex A shows an implementation fee of $45,000. The executed Order Form Schedule 1 shows $42,500. This is a minor variance in Vaitix's favor and requires no action, but it confirms that the final negotiated terms differed from the indicative Term Sheet in places other than exclusivity.

**B. Minimum Monthly Fee.** The Term Sheet Annex A indicated a range of $85,000–$105,000. The Order Form set it at $95,000 (mid-range). If the 11.5% rate increase is deemed accepted (Issue 1), the MMF rises to $105,925—above the top of the originally indicated range.

**C. Storage Fee Calculation.** Order Form Schedule 1, Note 1: "Storage Fees are calculated on the peak number of pallets and bins occupied by Customer Products on any day during the calendar month." This means a single day of high inventory (e.g., pre-peak positioning) sets the storage charge for the entire month. Vaitix should monitor its peak daily pallet/bin counts and ensure that pre-positioned inventory for peak season does not artificially inflate the monthly peak.

**D. Facility Operating Hours.** Order Form Schedule 3: Standard hours are 6:00 a.m.–11:00 p.m. Eastern, Monday–Friday; 7:00 a.m.–3:30 p.m. Eastern, Saturday. No Sunday operations. The 2:00 p.m. Eastern daily cut-off for same-day ship means Saturday orders received after 2:00 p.m. will not ship until Monday (a 3-day gap). Vaitix should confirm this aligns with its customer-facing delivery promises.
