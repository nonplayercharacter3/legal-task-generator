## Summary

I have reviewed the four documents in the matter folder: the MSA (effective March 1, 2025), Schedule B (Service Level Schedule), the unsigned Draft Amendment No. 1 (proposed effective September 1, 2026), and the Q2 2026 business review meeting notes (July 16, 2026). The single most urgent item is **Issue 1**: the draft amendment contains a provision that would expand Peigirt's exclusivity obligation from a narrow, channel- and geography-limited commitment to a worldwide, all-channel lock-in. This appears inconsistent with the stated purpose of the amendment and should not be accepted as drafted.

## Issue 1 – Draft Amendment No. 1, Section 2: Drastic Expansion of Exclusivity

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, § 2 ("Replacement of MSA Section 2.4").

**What it says:** The draft deletes and replaces MSA § 2.4. The current MSA § 2.4 limits exclusivity to "the fulfillment of E-Commerce Orders shipped to addresses in the Territory" (defined as the United States and Canada) and expressly preserves Peigirt's right to use other providers for "any other channel, product or geography." The replacement provision reads:

> "During the Term, Peigirt shall use Giseind as its exclusive provider of **all warehousing, fulfillment and distribution services for Products in any channel worldwide**."

**Why it matters:** This is not a marginal change. It would obligate Peigirt to route *every* product, in *every* channel (wholesale, retail, B2B, direct-to-consumer, international), through Giseind, *worldwide*, for the duration of the Term (which, under the auto-renewal provision, could extend to 10+ years—see Issue 4). The stated purpose of the amendment (Recital C) is solely to add Canadian returns processing at Mississauga. The exclusivity expansion is unrelated to that purpose and appears to be either a drafting error or a deliberate attempt by Giseind to extract a far broader commercial commitment under the cover of a narrow operational amendment. If Peigirt signs this, it loses the ability to use any other 3PL, to self-fulfill, or to serve wholesale or international channels independently.

**Recommended fix:** Delete Draft Amendment § 2 in its entirety. Do not amend MSA § 2.4. If Giseind raises the exclusivity point separately, treat it as a new commercial negotiation with its own business case, not as a rider to a returns-processing amendment. Flag this to the client immediately before the amendment is circulated for signature.

## Issue 2 – Schedule B, Section 3.3: No Service Levels or Credits During Peak Season

**Where:** Schedule B, § 3.3 ("Peak Season").

**What it says:**

> "During Peak Season, the Dispatch Service Level does not apply and **no Service Credits accrue**. Giseind shall continue to use commercially reasonable efforts to Dispatch Orders within the Dispatch Service Level and shall continue to report its performance under Section 5."

**Why it matters:** The phrase "no Service Credits accrue" is not limited to the Dispatch SL. Read in context, it eliminates *all* service credit remedies during Peak Season. Schedule B defines Peak Season as **September 1 through January 31**—five months, encompassing the holiday rush and the post-holiday return surge. During this period, Peigirt has no financial remedy if Giseind misses *any* service level (dispatch, accuracy, inventory, receiving, returns, or integration). The only obligation is "commercially reasonable efforts," which is not a measurable standard and carries no consequence for failure. For an outdoor apparel brand whose revenue is heavily concentrated in Q4, this is a significant gap in protection.

Compounding the problem: the Draft Amendment is effective **September 1, 2026**—the first day of Peak Season. Canadian returns would launch into a period where no service credits are available if performance degrades under the added volume.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Schedule B § 3.3 to provide that (a) the Dispatch SL continues to apply during Peak Season, or at minimum (b) service credits continue to accrue for all SLs other than Dispatch, or (c) if Giseind resists, narrow the Peak Season definition for SL purposes to the MSA's shorter window (November 15 – December 31; see Issue 5). Alternatively, add a "Peak Season super-credit" that increases the credit percentage for any SL missed during the holiday window to compensate for the higher stakes.

## Issue 3 – MSA, Section 16.3: Giseind's Unilateral Assignment Right

**Where:** MSA, § 16.3 ("Permitted Assignment").

**What it says:** Giseind may assign the Agreement, in whole or in part, **without notice to or consent of Peigirt**, to: (a) any Affiliate; (b) any successor by merger, consolidation, reorganization or change of control; or (c) any acquirer of all or substantially all of its fulfillment business or assets.

**Why it matters:** Peigirt has no veto, no notice, and no exit right if Giseind is sold or restructured. In a change-of-control scenario, Peigirt could find itself locked into an exclusivity arrangement (especially if Issue 1 is not fixed) with an unknown entity that may have different capabilities, different pricing incentives, or different strategic priorities. The MSA's termination rights (§ 13) do not include a change-of-control trigger for Peigirt. Combined with the 5-year auto-renewal (Issue 4), Peigirt could be bound to an unvetted counterparty for an extended period.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 16.3 to require Giseind to give Peigirt at least 60 days' prior written notice of any permitted assignment and to grant Peigirt a termination right (effective on the assignment date) if the assignee does not meet minimum financial or operational criteria to be specified in the amendment. At minimum, require notice so Peigirt can assess the situation before it is done.

## Issue 4 – MSA, Section 12.2: Five-Year Auto-Renewal with Narrow Opt-Out Window

**Where:** MSA, § 12.2 ("Renewal").

**What it says:** At the end of the Initial Term (March 1, 2028), the Agreement automatically renews for successive **five (5) year** terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal **no earlier than 300 days and no later than 270 days** before expiration.

**Why it matters:** The opt-out window is a 30-day band (roughly May 15 – June 15, 2028 for the first renewal). If Peigirt misses that window by even one day, it is locked in for another five years. Five years is an extremely long commitment for a fulfillment relationship, particularly if the exclusivity is expanded (Issue 1) or if Giseind's ownership changes (Issue 3). The asymmetry is notable: the Initial Term is 3 years, but each renewal is 5 years, meaning the longer Peigirt stays, the longer each subsequent lock-in becomes.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Shorten the renewal term from 5 years to 1 or 2 years; (b) widen the notice window (e.g., 180–90 days before expiration) to reduce the risk of missing the deadline; and (c) add a right for Peigirt to terminate for convenience on 12 months' notice at the end of any renewal term. Calendar the notice deadline now and assign ownership internally.

## Issue 5 – Conflicting Definitions of "Peak Season"

**Where:** MSA, § 1.1 (defines "Peak Season" as **November 15 through December 31**); Schedule B, § 1 (defines "Peak Season" as **September 1 through January 31**).

**Why it matters:** The two definitions differ by over three months. MSA § 17.3 provides that the Service Level Schedule prevails over the body of the Agreement "with respect to service levels and service credits." So for SL purposes, the broader Schedule B definition (Sept 1 – Jan 31) likely controls. But the MSA definition may still apply to other provisions that reference "Peak Season" without specifying which definition governs—for example, MSA § 4.3 requires the annual physical inventory to be conducted "outside Peak Season." If the MSA definition applies there, the inventory can be scheduled from January 1 to November 14; if the Schedule B definition applies, the window is only January 31 to November 14. This ambiguity could create a dispute over whether a chosen inventory date is compliant.

**Recommended fix:** Add a clarifying provision (in the next amendment or as a side letter) stating which definition of "Peak Season" applies to which sections, or conform the two definitions to a single period. Given the commercial reality (outdoor apparel peaks in Q4), the MSA's shorter definition is more appropriate for operational provisions like inventory timing, while the broader Schedule B definition may be justified for SL carve-outs. Make the allocation explicit.

## Issue 6 – Draft Amendment No. 1, Section 1.4: Combined Returns SL Measurement

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, § 1.4.

**What it says:** The Returns Processing SL (Schedule B § 2.5: 97% within 3 Business Days) applies to Mississauga returns "measured with returns received at the Reno, Nevada Facility."

**Why it matters:** Pooling the two facilities means a volume spike or operational issue at Reno can drag the combined percentage below target, triggering a service credit that Peigirt might not otherwise receive for a Canadian-specific problem—or, conversely, strong Reno performance could mask a Canadian underperformance. More practically, if Canadian return volumes are initially low (as they will be at launch), they will have minimal statistical impact on the combined number, meaning Peigirt gets little effective SL protection for the new Canadian operation in its early months.

**Recommended fix:** Require separate measurement by facility for the first 12 months after the Amendment Effective Date, then allow pooling if both facilities are consistently meeting target. Alternatively, set a minimum Canadian return volume threshold below which the SL is not measured (to avoid the small-sample problem) but above which it is measured separately.

## Issue 7 – Integration Dependency: Amendment Requires Capability Not Yet Built

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, § 1.3(b) (requires Giseind to "transmit the inspection grade and disposition status to Peigirt through the Integration within one (1) Business Day after grading"); Q2 2026 Meeting Notes, item 6.2 and Action Item 1 (Giseind to scope adding return grading and disposition status to the Integration feed by August 14, 2026).

**Why it matters:** The Amendment obligates Giseind to push return grading data through the Integration, but the Integration does not currently support that data flow. Giseind has only agreed to *scope* the enhancement, with a due date of August 14, 2026. The Amendment Effective Date is September 1, 2026, and § 1.6 requires Giseind to begin accepting Canadian returns within 30 days of that date (i.e., by October 1, 2026). If the Integration build is not complete and tested by then, Giseind will be in breach of § 1.3(b) from day one of the Canadian returns operation, or Peigirt will be operating blind on Canadian return status.

**Recommended fix:** Add a condition precedent to the Amendment: the Integration enhancement must be live and tested no later than the Amendment Effective Date (or the 30-day transition deadline in § 1.6, whichever is earlier). If Giseind cannot meet that date, the Canadian returns obligation should be deferred until the Integration is ready. Track Action Item 1 (due August 14) closely.

## Issue 8 – MSA, Section 11.5: Liability Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** MSA, § 11.5 ("Limitation of Liability").

**What it says:** Except for indemnity obligations, confidentiality breaches, and gross negligence/willful misconduct, each Party's aggregate liability is capped at **Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim**.

**Why it matters:** For a fulfillment provider holding Peigirt's inventory and processing customer data, a single significant event (e.g., a warehouse fire destroying a season's stock, a major data breach affecting customer PII, or a prolonged system outage during Peak Season) could generate losses well in excess of 12 months of fees. The insurance requirements in MSA § 10 (CGL $2M, warehouse legal liability at full replacement value, cyber $5M) provide some backstop, but the liability cap limits Peigirt's direct contractual recovery against Giseind. The cap is also a "contract year" cap, not an aggregate cap, so repeated smaller claims in different years each get their own 12-month fee lookback—but a single catastrophic event in one year is still capped.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a super-cap for specific high-risk events (e.g., inventory loss, data breach) at 2–3x the standard cap, or tie the cap to the insurance limits in § 10. At minimum, confirm that the warehouse legal liability insurance covers the full replacement value of Inventory as actually held (not a stated maximum) and that Peigirt is named as a loss payee or additional insured.

## Issue 9 – Schedule B, Section 4.2: 15% Cap on Aggregate Service Credits

**Where:** Schedule B, § 4.2.

**What it says:** Total service credits in any Measurement Period shall not exceed 15% of Monthly Fulfillment Fees.

**Why it matters:** Monthly Fulfillment Fees are defined (Schedule B § 1) as only the order fulfillment fees (first unit + additional unit), excluding storage, receiving, returns, carrier, and packaging. If Peigirt's fulfillment fees are, say, $100,000/month, the maximum credit is $15,000. In a month where multiple SLs are missed (e.g., Dispatch at 95%, Order Accuracy at 99%, Returns at 94%), the individual credits would be 10% + 3% + 2% = 15%, hitting the cap exactly. If one more SL is missed, Peigirt receives no additional credit. The cap is reasonable in isolation but becomes more significant when combined with the Peak Season carve-out (Issue 2), which already eliminates all credits for five months.

**Recommended fix:** Raise the cap to 20–25% of Monthly Fulfillment Fees, or define the cap as a percentage of total monthly Fees (including storage, receiving, returns) to increase the dollar value. Alternatively, exclude the cap during any month in which Peigirt's termination right under § 13.2(c) is triggered (chronic dispatch failure), as a backstop.

## Issue 10 – Draft Amendment Effective Date Falls on First Day of Peak Season

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, preamble (Amendment Effective Date: September 1, 2026); Schedule B, § 1 (Peak Season: September 1 – January 31).

**Why it matters:** Launching a new returns operation at a second facility on the first day of the five-month Peak Season window—when (per Issue 2) no service credits are available and when return volumes are historically elevated—is operationally risky. If the Canadian returns process has teething problems, Peigirt has no SL remedy during exactly the period when the problems would be most costly.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) delay the Amendment Effective Date to February 1, 2027 (after Peak Season), or (b) if the business need requires a September launch, negotiate a "ramp-up" period (e.g., the first 60 days) during which the Returns Processing SL applies at a reduced target (e.g., 90% instead of 97%) and service credits are available despite the Peak Season carve-out.

## Issue 11 – MSA, Section 14.1: 120-Day Transition May Be Insufficient

**Where:** MSA, § 14.1 ("Transition Assistance").

**What it says:** On expiry or termination, Giseind shall continue performing Services for up to 120 days and provide reasonable cooperation to transfer to a replacement provider.

**Why it matters:** If the exclusivity is expanded as drafted in the Amendment (Issue 1), Peigirt would be relying on Giseind for all channels worldwide. Transitioning a global, multi-channel fulfillment operation in 120 days is ambitious. The provision also does not specify what "reasonable cooperation" entails (e.g., data migration format, staff training for the successor, carrier account transfers). MSA § 14.2 requires Giseind to release Inventory within 10 Business Days of request, which is helpful, but the broader transition logistics are under-specified.

**Recommended fix:** Extend the transition period to 180 days (or 120 days plus one 60-day extension at Peigirt's option). Add specific transition deliverables: data export in agreed formats, carrier account portability, packing material inventory transfer, and a named transition manager on Giseind's side.

**Where:** Q2 2026 Meeting Notes, items 4.1, 4.2, 4.4.

**What it says:** April Order Accuracy was 99.6% against a 99.7% target (a 0.1pp miss), triggering a 3% service credit (confirmed paid, item 7.1). June Returns Processing was 97.4% against a 97% target—above target but only by 0.4pp. Giseind attributed the June near-miss to post-Father's Day volume and added a weekend shift through August.

The June returns figure is more concerning as a leading indicator: if Canadian returns add volume at Mississauga during Peak Season (when no credits are available per Issue 2), and Reno is already running near the line in a normal month, the combined returns SL (Issue 6) is at risk. The weekend shift through August is a short-term fix with no contractual commitment beyond that period.

**Recommended fix:** At the Q3 business review (October 2026), request that Giseind confirm in writing whether the weekend shift will continue into Peak Season and what additional staffing or capacity is planned for Mississauga returns. If Giseind cannot commit, consider whether the Canadian returns launch should be deferred (see Issue 10).

## Items Flagged as Unclear or Incomplete

1. **Draft Amendment is unsigned.** It is a draft only. It should not be treated as an agreed term. Confirm with the client whether Giseind has circulated it for signature or whether it remains in negotiation. The meeting notes (item 9.3) indicate Giseind was to "prepare a draft amendment and send it to Peigirt for review," consistent with it being in draft stage.

2. **MSA § 2.6 is cut across a page break** (page 1 ends mid-sentence at "responsibl" and page 2 continues with "e for the acts and omissions..."). The full text reads: "Giseind remains responsible for the acts and omissions of its subcontractors as if they were its own." This appears complete but I flag it in case the pagination obscured any additional language.

3. **No SLA for the Integration itself.** MSA § 3.4 requires Giseind to maintain the Integration for "near real time" order acknowledgment and 1-hour shipment confirmation, and Schedule B § 2.6 sets a 99% SL for confirmations. But there is no SL for *order acknowledgment* latency (the "near real time" standard is undefined) and no SL for Integration *uptime* or *availability*. The May 14 incident (40 minutes of queued orders, Meeting Notes item 6.1) did not trigger any service credit because the confirmations were eventually delivered within the 1-hour window. If a longer outage occurred, Peigirt's only remedy would be the general warranty in MSA § 11.2. Consider adding an Integration uptime SL (e.g., 99.5% monthly availability) and a defined maximum for order acknowledgment latency.

4. **No dispute resolution mechanism.** MSA § 17.1 provides for exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware state and federal courts, but there is no arbitration clause, no mediation step, and no expert determination mechanism for SL measurement disputes. If Peigirt and Giseind disagree about whether an SL was met (e.g., the validity of exclusions under Schedule B § 3.2), the only path is litigation in Delaware. Consider adding a tiered dispute resolution clause (good-faith negotiation → expert determination on measurement disputes → arbitration or litigation).
