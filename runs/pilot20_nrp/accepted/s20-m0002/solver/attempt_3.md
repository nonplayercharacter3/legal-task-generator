## Issue 1 — Draft Amendment No. 1, Section 2: Drastic Expansion of Exclusivity

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, Section 2 ("Replacement of MSA Section 2.4"). Compare MSA § 2.4 and § 1.1 (definition of "Territory").

**What it says:** The original MSA § 2.4 limits exclusivity to "the fulfillment of E-Commerce Orders shipped to addresses in the Territory" (Territory = United States and Canada), and expressly carves out Peigirt's right to use other providers or perform services itself "for any other channel, product or geography." The Draft Amendment deletes all of that and replaces it with:

> "During the Term, Peigirt shall use Giseind as its exclusive provider of all warehousing, fulfillment and distribution services for Products in any channel worldwide."

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- **Scope explosion.** The amendment expands exclusivity from (i) e-commerce order fulfillment only, to (ii) all warehousing, fulfillment *and distribution* services; from (iii) US and Canada, to (iv) worldwide; and from (v) one channel (e-commerce DTC), to (vi) any channel (wholesale, retail, B2B, marketplace, etc.).
- **No carve-out.** The original safety valve allowing Peigirt to self-perform or use other providers for other channels, products or geographies is gone.
- **Bundled with an unrelated ask.** The stated purpose of the amendment (Recital C) is to add Canadian returns at Mississauga. The exclusivity expansion is buried in Section 2 with no recital or explanation. This is not a negotiated trade; it appears to be a drive-by amendment.
- **Compounds the auto-renewal risk (Issue 4).** If Peigirt misses the non-renewal window, it could be locked into worldwide exclusivity for five additional years.
- **Interacts with the assignment clause (Issue 5).** If Giseind is acquired by a competitor or a less capable provider, Peigirt would be contractually barred from using any alternative provider anywhere in the world.

**Recommended fix:**

- Delete Section 2 of the Draft Amendment in its entirety. The Canadian returns addition does not require any change to the exclusivity clause.
- If Giseind raises exclusivity as a separate commercial ask, negotiate it on its own terms with appropriate carve-outs (e.g., exclude wholesale/B2B channels, exclude geographies outside North America, retain the right to self-perform, add a termination right if Giseind is assigned to a competitor).
- Make clear in any discussion with Giseind that the Canadian returns amendment and any exclusivity change are separate matters.

## Issue 2 — Conflicting "Peak Season" Definitions

**Where:** MSA § 1.1 defines "Peak Season" as **November 15 through December 31**. Schedule B § 1 defines "Peak Season" as **September 1 through January 31**.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

The two definitions differ by roughly four months. Peak Season is used in multiple provisions with different consequences:

| Provision | Effect of Peak Season definition |
|---|---|
| Schedule B § 3.3 | Dispatch SLA suspended; no service credits accrue |
| MSA § 4.3 | Annual physical inventory must be scheduled outside Peak Season |
| MSA § 2.5 | Separate Peak Season forecast due by September 1 |
| MSA § 13.2(c) / Schedule B § 6 | Chronic-failure termination trigger (3 months in rolling 6) — fewer months available if Peak Season is longer |

Under MSA § 17.3 (Order of Precedence), Schedule B controls "with respect to service levels and service credits." So for SLA purposes, the broader Schedule B definition (Sep 1 – Jan 31) likely governs. But for the physical inventory scheduling (MSA § 4.3) and the forecast obligation (MSA § 2.5), the MSA body definition (Nov 15 – Dec 31) arguably applies. This creates genuine ambiguity about when the annual count can be done and what forecast obligations attach.

The Q2 2026 meeting notes (§ 8.3) show the parties are already trying to schedule the annual physical inventory "outside Peak Season" without having resolved which definition applies.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend MSA § 1.1 to conform the Peak Season definition to a single, agreed period. If the business reality is that Giseind needs the broader window (Sep 1 – Jan 31) for SLA relief, Peigirt should extract value in return (e.g., higher SLA targets outside Peak Season, or a reduced suspension window).
- Alternatively, use two defined terms: "Peak Season" (narrow, for inventory and forecasting) and "Extended Peak Period" (broad, for SLA suspension), so each provision references the correct one.
- Until resolved, flag to the client that the annual physical inventory window may be narrower than Peigirt assumes.

## Issue 3 — No Dispatch SLA or Service Credits During Peak Season

**Where:** Schedule B § 3.3.

**What it says:** During Peak Season, the Dispatch Service Level "does not apply and no Service Credits accrue." Giseind only must use "commercially reasonable efforts."

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- If the broader Schedule B definition governs (Sep 1 – Jan 31, per Issue 2), Peigirt has **no contractual dispatch remedy for five months of the year**, including the entire Q4 holiday peak — the period when fulfillment failures are most costly to the brand (lost sales, damaged customer experience, marketplace penalties).
- The chronic-failure termination right (MSA § 13.2(c); Schedule B § 6) is also weakened because fewer months count toward the "3 in 6" trigger.
- "Commercially reasonable efforts" is not a measurable standard and provides no basis for a service credit or termination.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a reduced (but not eliminated) Dispatch SLA during Peak Season — e.g., 95% instead of 98% — with corresponding (reduced) service credits.
- If Giseind will not agree to any SLA during Peak Season, at minimum: (a) narrow the Peak Season window for SLA purposes to the MSA definition (Nov 15 – Dec 31); (b) add a requirement that Giseind provide a Peak Season readiness plan (staffing, capacity, carrier contracts) by a fixed date; and (c) add a termination right if Giseind's Peak Season performance falls below an absolute floor (e.g., 90% dispatch within 24 hours) in any two consecutive Peak Seasons.

## Issue 4 — Five-Year Auto-Renewal with a 30-Day Opt-Out Window

**Where:** MSA § 12.2.

**What it says:** After the Initial Term (3 years, ending February 28, 2028), the Agreement auto-renews for successive **five-year** terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal in a window of **300 to 270 days** before expiration (i.e., a 30-day window roughly between June 15 and July 15, 2027 for the first renewal).

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- Five-year renewal terms are unusually long for a 3PL relationship where technology, carrier networks and competitive dynamics shift rapidly.
- The 30-day notice window is narrow and easy to miss. A single administrative oversight locks Peigirt in for five more years.
- Combined with Issue 1 (if the expanded exclusivity is signed), a missed renewal window would mean worldwide, all-channel exclusivity for five additional years with no exit.
- There is no right to terminate for convenience at the end of the Initial Term; the only out is the non-renewal notice.

**Recommended fix:**

- Calendar the non-renewal notice window immediately (300 days before February 28, 2028 = approximately June 15, 2027; 270 days before = approximately July 15, 2027). Set internal reminders at 360, 330 and 310 days out.
- In any renegotiation or amendment, seek to: (a) shorten renewal terms to one or two years; (b) widen the notice window (e.g., 180 to 90 days before expiration); and (c) add a termination-for-convenience right at the end of the Initial Term on 12 months' notice.

## Issue 5 — Giseind's Unilateral Assignment Right

**Where:** MSA § 16.3.

**What it says:** Giseind may assign the Agreement **without notice to or consent of Peigirt** to (a) any Affiliate, (b) any successor by merger, consolidation, reorganization or change of control, or (c) any acquirer of all or substantially all of its fulfillment business or assets.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- Peigirt has no say in who inherits the contract. Giseind could be acquired by a direct competitor of Peigirt, a financially weaker company, or a firm with inferior infrastructure.
- There is no notice requirement, so Peigirt may not even learn of the assignment until it affects operations.
- There is no termination right triggered by assignment.
- Under the original MSA, this is manageable because exclusivity is limited. Under the Draft Amendment's expanded exclusivity (Issue 1), it becomes a serious strategic risk.
- The "Affiliate" prong is particularly broad: Giseind could shift performance to a newly formed, undercapitalized affiliate.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend § 16.3 to require: (a) prior written notice to Peigirt at least 60 days before any assignment; (b) Peigirt's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for assignments to a direct competitor of Peigirt or to an entity that does not maintain insurance levels comparable to Giseind's; and (c) a termination right for Peigirt (without penalty) if the assignee fails to meet the service levels for two consecutive months post-assignment.
- At minimum, add a notice requirement even if consent is not achievable.

## Issue 6 — Forecast-Based SLA Exclusion Creates a Trap

**Where:** Schedule B § 3.2(d); MSA § 2.5.

**What it says:** Orders are excluded from Service Level calculations to the extent volume in a Measurement Period exceeds **130% of the most recent forecast** for that period. Peigirt's forecasts are "non-binding" (MSA § 2.5).

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- Because forecasts are non-binding, Peigirt has no contractual penalty for under-forecasting. But if Peigirt under-forecasts (even inadvertently), and actual volume exceeds 130% of that low number, Giseind gets SLA relief for the excess. Peigirt loses its service credit protection during volume spikes — precisely when it needs Giseind to perform.
- The Q2 2026 meeting notes (§ 8.2) flag that the fall outerwear launch will bring inbound receipts forward by two weeks. If the launch drives a volume spike above 130% of forecast, Peigirt could lose SLA protection during a critical product launch window.
- The exclusion applies only to volume "above that threshold," so the first 130% still counts. But in a high-growth month, the excess could be material.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a higher threshold (e.g., 150% or 175%) before the exclusion kicks in.
- Add a requirement that Giseind provide 10 business days' notice before relying on the exclusion, so Peigirt can verify the forecast baseline.
- Alternatively, make the forecast "binding" in the sense that Peigirt warrants it is made in good faith and is within a reasonable range, in exchange for a higher exclusion threshold.

## Issue 7 — Liability Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** MSA § 11.5.

**What it says:** Each Party's aggregate liability is capped at "Fees paid or payable in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim," subject to carve-outs for indemnity obligations (§§ 11.3, 11.4), confidentiality breach (§ 9), and gross negligence or willful misconduct.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- The cap is tied to fees, not to the value of inventory or the magnitude of potential loss. If Giseind's negligence (short of gross negligence) results in the destruction of a high-value inventory lot (e.g., a full container of premium outerwear), Peigirt's recovery is limited to roughly one year of fees, which may be well below the actual loss.
- The shrinkage provision (MSA § 4.4) only covers loss above 0.25% of average inventory value, and that claim would likely be subject to the § 11.5 cap.
- The carve-out for "gross negligence or willful misconduct" is helpful but sets a high bar; ordinary negligence in a warehouse fire or a systemic packing error would fall under the cap.

**Recommended fix:**

- Seek a super-cap for inventory loss: e.g., the general cap applies, but liability for loss or damage to Inventory is capped at the full replacement value of the affected Inventory (which is already covered by Giseind's warehouse legal liability insurance under MSA § 10(b)).
- Alternatively, increase the general cap to the greater of (a) 12 months' fees or (b) USD [X] (a fixed dollar floor).
- Confirm that the warehouse legal liability insurance (MSA § 10(b)) is primary and that Peigirt can look to the insurance proceeds directly in the event of a loss.

## Issue 8 — Shrinkage Threshold and Measure

**Where:** MSA § 4.4.

**What it says:** Giseind is responsible only for shrinkage **exceeding 0.25%** of the average value of Inventory held during the contract year, measured at **replacement cost**.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- The first 0.25% of annual shrinkage is effectively Peigirt's economic loss. Depending on inventory value, this could be a meaningful sum (e.g., if average inventory value is $5M, the first $12,500 of shrink per year is on Peigirt).
- "Replacement cost" may undervalue the loss if the item is a limited-edition or seasonal product that cannot be replaced at the same cost or at all.
- There is no requirement that Giseind investigate or report shrinkage events in real time; the obligation appears to be an annual true-up.

**Recommended fix:**

- Reduce the threshold to 0.10% or eliminate it (make Giseind responsible for all shrinkage, as is market-standard for a 3PL that holds title-free inventory).
- Add a requirement for Giseind to notify Peigirt of any single shrinkage event exceeding a de minimis threshold (e.g., $500) within 5 business days.
- Clarify that "replacement cost" means the cost to Peigirt to replace the unit (including COGS), not Giseind's cost to source a comparable item.

## Issue 9 — Combined Measurement of Canadian and US Returns

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1, § 1.4.

**What it says:** The Returns Processing Service Level (97% within 3 Business Days) applies to Mississauga returns "measured with returns received at the Reno, Nevada Facility."

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- In the early months, Canadian returns volume will be small relative to US volume. A handful of late Canadian returns could be masked by strong US performance, giving Peigirt no visibility into Canadian-specific issues.
- Conversely, if US returns volume spikes (e.g., post-holiday) and drags the combined number below 97%, Peigirt gets a service credit even if Canadian returns were performed perfectly.
- The Q2 2026 meeting notes (§ 9.1) identify Canadian refund turnaround as "the most frequent complaint in post-purchase surveys." Blended measurement makes it harder to pinpoint and fix Canadian-specific bottlenecks.

**Recommended fix:**

- Require separate reporting by facility in the monthly SLA report (the report format in Schedule B § 5.1 already calls for facility-level data, but the service credit calculation should also be facility-specific for returns).
- Alternatively, apply the combined SLA only once Canadian returns volume exceeds a minimum threshold (e.g., 10% of total returns volume); below that, measure separately.

## Issue 10 — Integration Readiness Gap for Canadian Returns

**Where:** Q2 2026 meeting notes § 6.2 and Action Item 1; Draft Amendment No. 1 § 1.3(b).

**What it says:** The Draft Amendment requires Giseind to "transmit the inspection grade and disposition status to Peigirt through the Integration within one (1) Business Day after grading" for Canadian returns. However, the meeting notes show that this Integration capability does not yet exist — Giseind only agreed to "scope" it, with a due date of August 14, 2026. The Canadian returns go live 30 days after the Amendment Effective Date (i.e., by October 1, 2026).

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- If the amendment is signed with a September 1 effective date and the Integration is not live by October 1, Giseind will be in immediate breach of § 1.3(b) on day one of Canadian returns.
- More practically, without the Integration feed, Peigirt cannot track Canadian return status in real time, defeating the business purpose (faster refund turnaround).

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a condition precedent to the Draft Amendment: the Canadian Returns Services (and the § 1.3(b) Integration obligation) become effective only when the Integration capability is confirmed live and tested. Alternatively, extend the 30-day transition period in § 1.6 until Integration is confirmed.
- Require Giseind to deliver a written Integration readiness confirmation at least 10 business days before the go-live date.
- Until Integration is live, require Giseind to provide the grade and disposition data via a fallback method (e.g., daily CSV export or email).

## Issue 11 — Transition Assistance Period May Be Inadequate

**Where:** MSA § 14.1.

**What it says:** On expiry or termination, Giseind shall provide transition assistance for "up to one hundred twenty (120) days" at then-current fees.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- 120 days may be insufficient to: (a) transfer inventory across three facilities (and potentially more under the Draft Amendment's worldwide scope); (b) re-point the Integration to a new 3PL; (c) migrate carrier accounts; and (d) transition returns processing.
- "Up to" 120 days gives Giseind discretion to provide less. There is no minimum.
- There is no penalty for Giseind's failure to cooperate within the period, and no obligation to assist after it ends.
- If Peigirt is locked in by the auto-renewal (Issue 4) or the expanded exclusivity (Issue 1), the transition clause is the only exit mechanism, and it needs to be robust.

**Recommended fix:**

- Change "up to 120 days" to "a minimum of 180 days" (or 120 days plus an extension if Peigirt's replacement provider requires more, at Giseind's then-current fees).
- Add specific transition deliverables: data migration in agreed formats, inventory count and handover protocol, carrier account transfer assistance, and Integration decommissioning support.
- Add a liquidated damages or fee-credit mechanism if Giseind fails to meet transition milestones.

## Issue 12 — Service Credit Cap and Structure

**Where:** Schedule B § 4.1–4.2.

**What it says:** Service credits are a percentage of Monthly Fulfillment Fees (which exclude storage, receiving, returns, carrier and packaging charges). The aggregate cap is 15% of Monthly Fulfillment Fees per month.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- Because "Monthly Fulfillment Fees" exclude storage, receiving, returns processing, carrier pass-through and packaging, the credit base is relatively narrow. If Peigirt's cost base is heavily weighted toward storage (e.g., slow-moving inventory), the effective value of a service credit is low relative to total spend.
- The 15% cap means that even if Giseind misses every single service level in a month, the maximum credit is 15% of fulfillment fees. Given the per-level credits range from 1% to 10%, it is possible (though unlikely) to hit the cap if multiple levels are missed simultaneously.
- Service credits are the **exclusive** remedy for SLA failures (per Schedule B § 4.4 and MSA § 6.2, which states credits are "without prejudice to Peigirt's rights under Section 13.2(c)" — i.e., the chronic-failure termination right). There is no right to terminate for a single month's SLA failure.

**Recommended fix:**

- Widen the credit base to include all Fees (storage, receiving, returns, etc.) or at least add returns processing fees to the base, given that returns are a growing component of the relationship.
- Consider adding a "clawback" or make-whole provision: if Giseind misses the same SLA in three consecutive months (short of the chronic-failure termination trigger), the credit percentage doubles for the third month.
- Clarify that service credits are without prejudice to Peigirt's right to claim damages for losses exceeding the credit (e.g., if a dispatch failure causes Peigirt to miss a marketplace deadline and incur a penalty).

## Issue 13 — MSA § 13.2(a): Termination for Giseind's Failure to Pay Peigirt

**Where:** MSA § 13.2(a).

**What it says:** Peigirt may terminate if "Giseind fails to pay any amount due to Peigirt and does not cure within thirty (30) days of notice."

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

This clause is oddly drafted. In a typical 3PL arrangement, the service provider (Giseind) does not pay the customer (Peigirt) — it is the other way around. The only scenario in which Giseind would "pay an amount due to Peigirt" is a service credit that exceeds the next invoice (a net refund). As drafted, this termination right is almost inoperable because there is rarely a scenario where Giseind owes Peigirt a standalone payment.

This may be a drafting error. The intended provision might have been a mutual payment-default clause, or it might be a leftover from a template.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm with the client whether this was negotiated intentionally. If it is a drafting error, either delete it or replace it with a more useful trigger (e.g., termination if Giseind fails to pay a net service credit refund within 30 days of becoming due under Schedule B § 4.3).

## Issue 14 — Amendment Not Yet Executed; Timing Risk

**Where:** Draft Amendment No. 1 (signature blocks blank); Q2 2026 meeting notes § 9.3 and Action Item 5.

**What it says:** The amendment is in draft form. The meeting notes confirm Giseind will "circulate draft amendment" by August 2026. The proposed effective date is September 1, 2026, with Canadian returns go-live by October 1, 2026.

**Why it matters for Peigirt:**

- The Peak Season forecast is due September 1, 2026 (MSA § 2.5; meeting notes Action Item 3). If the amendment is not signed by then, Peigirt is delivering its Peak Season forecast under the old agreement while the new agreement (with potentially different scope) is in limbo.
- If Peigirt pushes back on the exclusivity expansion (Issue 1) and negotiations extend past September 1, the amendment's effective date needs to be adjusted, and the 30-day transition clock in § 1.6 resets.

**Recommended fix:**

- Do not allow the September 1 effective date to drive the negotiation timeline. If Peigirt is not ready to sign by mid-August, propose moving the effective date to October 1 or November 1 to allow adequate review time.
- Ensure the Peak Season forecast (due September 1) is delivered under the existing MSA regardless of the amendment status, to avoid any technical breach.
