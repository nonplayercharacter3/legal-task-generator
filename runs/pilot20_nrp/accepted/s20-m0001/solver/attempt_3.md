## Issue 1 (Critical): Expansion of Assumed Product Liability Beyond the LOI

**Where:** APA § 1.3(c) vs. LOI ¶ 4(a)(iii) and ¶ 4(b); APA § 9.5(c) and § 9.3(b).

**Explanation:**

The LOI drew a clear line: Buyer would assume product liability only for "products manufactured and sold by Buyer **after the Closing**" (LOI ¶ 4(a)(iii)), and "Pre-closing product liability will be retained by Seller as an Excluded Liability" (LOI ¶ 4(b)).

The APA deletes that limitation. Section 1.3(c) provides that Buyer assumes "all liabilities for product liability, personal injury, property damage, product warranty and field corrective action claims relating to the Products, **whether manufactured or sold before or after the Closing**." The defined term "Products" (§ 1.1(f)) sweeps in the entire cervical and lumbar interbody fusion implant line as it existed in the Business.

The practical consequence is compounded by § 9.5(c): "No cap, basket or other limitation in this Article IX applies to claims under Section 9.3(b)." Section 9.3(b) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller for any Assumed Liability. Because pre-Closing product liability is now an Assumed Liability, Muvux's indemnification exposure to Seller for those claims is **uncapped**. The $8.16M Seller Indemnity Cap that protects Muvux against Seller's breaches does not reciprocally protect Muvux against its own assumed-liability indemnity.

Additionally, APA § 11.9 expressly states that the APA "supersedes the non-binding letter of intent," so the LOI's allocation cannot be used as a basis for reformation or parol-evidence argument.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** Muvux has taken on unknown, potentially catastrophic pre-Closing product liability exposure (implant failures, spinal complications) with no cap on its indemnification obligation to Seller. The Disclosure Schedule (§ 4.8) lists pending and recently resolved claims, but the full tail of latent product liability for implanted devices can extend many years.

**Recommended fix:**

- Before Closing, negotiate a conforming amendment to § 1.3(c) to restore the LOI's allocation: limit Assumed product liability to Products manufactured or sold **after** the Closing Date, and move pre-Closing product liability into § 1.4 as an Excluded Liability.
- If Pisen resists, at minimum negotiate a separate cap (e.g., 2× the Seller Indemnity Cap) on Buyer's § 9.3(b) indemnification for pre-Closing product liability, or a specific indemnity from Seller for pre-Closing product claims that is not subject to the general cap.
- Request the complete § 4.8 Disclosure Schedule and any product liability reserve data from Pisen's actuaries to quantify the exposure before agreeing to any compromise.

## Issue 2 (Critical): Parent Merger Triggers Change-of-Control Default Under the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note §§ 1(b)(ii), 6(e), 7(a); APA § 5.5.

**Explanation:**

APA § 5.5 represents that Parent (Muvux Orthopedics Holdings, Inc.) has entered into a merger agreement with Ardentis Surgical Partners, L.P. under which "an affiliate of Ardentis Surgical Partners, L.P. will merge with and into Parent, with Parent surviving."

Seller Note § 6(e) makes it an Event of Default if "Maker, or any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker, undergoes a Change of Control without the prior written consent of Holder." Parent is the sole member of Maker (APA § 5.1), so Parent directly controls Maker.

The Change of Control definition (§ 1(b)(ii)(B)) captures "any merger, consolidation or other business combination involving that Person, whether or not that Person is the surviving entity, after which the holders of that Person's voting equity interests immediately before the transaction hold less than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity interests of the surviving or resulting entity or its ultimate parent." In a typical reverse-merger structure where Ardentis's affiliate merges into Parent and Ardentis shareholders receive the majority of the surviving entity, the pre-merger Parent holders will hold less than 50%, triggering the definition.

Consequence: Pisen may accelerate the entire $14,000,000 principal plus accrued interest under § 7(a), and default interest at 10% (6% + 4% per § 7(c)) begins to run.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** Muvux is contractually committed to the Ardentis transaction (represented in § 5.5). If the Parent merger closes before obtaining Pisen's consent, the Seller Note accelerates immediately. Muvux would face a $14M+ cash call on short notice, potentially at the worst possible time (integrating a new business while its parent is undergoing its own merger).

**Recommended fix:**

- Obtain Pisen's prior written consent under § 6(e) **before** the Parent merger closes. This should be a condition to the Ardentis closing or addressed in a side letter.
- If Pisen's consent is conditioned on fee or other consideration, negotiate that now while the relationship is cooperative.
- Alternatively, seek an amendment to § 6(e) to carve out the specific Ardentis transaction by name, so that only other Change of Control events trigger the default.
- Coordinate timing: if possible, close the Pisen asset purchase before the Ardentis merger to avoid the trigger entirely (though the Seller Note would still be outstanding and the Change of Control would occur post-Closing, so consent is still needed).

## Issue 3 (High): Seller Note Caps Set-Off Recovery for Excluded Liabilities, Contradicting the APA

**Where:** Seller Note § 8(b) vs. APA §§ 9.5(b) and 11.9.

**Explanation:**

APA § 9.5(b) provides: "The Seller Indemnity Cap does not apply to Losses arising from Fundamental Representations, Fraud, and **claims under Section 9.2(c) for Excluded Liabilities**." In other words, the APA makes Excluded Liability claims uncapped.

Seller Note § 8(b) provides: "Holder's aggregate liability for all claims under Section 9.2 of the Purchase Agreement, **including claims under Section 9.2(c) for Excluded Liabilities**, whether recovered by set-off under this Section 8 or otherwise, shall not exceed the Seller Indemnity Cap, except for Losses arising from Fraud."

These provisions directly conflict on whether the $8.16M cap applies to Excluded Liability claims recovered via set-off against the Note. APA § 11.9 resolves the conflict: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and the Seller Note, **the Seller Note shall control**."

The practical effect: Muvux can set off against the $14M Note no more than $8.16M for Excluded Liabilities (e.g., Warning Letter remediation costs, pre-Closing environmental liabilities, pre-Closing employee claims, Seller's tax liabilities). Any excess must be pursued as a standalone indemnification claim against Pisen—which may be of limited value if Pisen distributes its cash at or shortly after Closing and is left as a shell.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** The Warning Letter alone could generate significant remediation and CAPA costs (APA § 6.3 obligates Seller to continue CAPA at its own cost, but if Seller defaults, Muvux's set-off recovery is capped). Pre-Closing environmental or employment liabilities are similarly capped at $8.16M in set-off.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a conforming amendment to Seller Note § 8(b) to delete the phrase "including claims under Section 9.2(c) for Excluded Liabilities," aligning it with APA § 9.5(b).
- If Pisen will not agree, consider whether the $14M Note principal is sufficient to cover the realistic Excluded Liability exposure. If the total Excluded Liability risk (Warning Letter, environmental, employment, tax) could exceed $8.16M, Muvux should either (a) require a larger Note or (b) seek a specific escrow or holdback for identified Excluded Liabilities.
- At minimum, document the conflict and preserve the argument that § 11.9's precedence rule was not intended to override the substantive allocation in § 9.5(b) (though this is a weak position given the plain text).

## Issue 4 (Medium): Subordination to Any Future Credit Facility Is Overly Broad

**Where:** Seller Note §§ 1(b)(v), 5(a)–(b).

**Explanation:**

"Senior Indebtedness" is defined as "all indebtedness for borrowed money of Maker owed to banks, insurance companies or other institutional lenders under **any credit facility in effect on or after the Closing Date**" (§ 1(b)(v)). Subordination under § 5(a) is to the "prior payment in full in cash of all Senior Indebtedness."

This means that if Muvux enters into a new revolving credit facility, term loan, or even a delayed draw commitment after Closing, the Seller Note is automatically subordinated to it. Muvux cannot make payments on the Seller Note (other than scheduled interest and the maturity principal, per § 5(b)) if there is a payment default under any such facility.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** Post-Closing, Muvux will likely need working capital or acquisition financing (particularly given the Parent merger). Any such facility will automatically subordinate the Pisen Note. While this is presumably by design (Pisen agreed to subordination in the LOI, ¶ 2(b)), the breadth ("any credit facility") gives Muvux no ability to take on debt without triggering subordination, and gives Pisen no visibility into what that debt will be.

**Recommended fix:**

- This is likely acceptable as negotiated, but Muvux should be aware that any future financing will trigger the subordination. If Muvux plans to raise debt in the near term (e.g., to fund the Parent merger integration), it should notify Pisen and confirm that the subordination mechanics in § 5(b) (Permitted Payments) will allow continued interest payments absent a default under the new facility.
- If Muvux wants flexibility, consider negotiating a "Permitted Senior Indebtedness" carve-out for a specified amount or type of facility that would not trigger the turnover provision in § 5(c).

## Issue 5 (Medium): Outside Date Leaves Minimal Buffer

**Where:** APA § 8.1(b); email thread (proposed Closing Date of December 4, 2026).

**Explanation:**

The APA permits either party to terminate if Closing has not occurred by **December 31, 2026** (§ 8.1(b)). The deal teams are targeting a Closing Date of Friday, December 4, 2026 (email from D. Okafor, Nov. 2). That leaves approximately four weeks of buffer.

Risk factors that could consume the buffer:
- The inventory count (Nov. 30 – Dec. 1) may produce disputed items requiring reconciliation before funds flow is finalized.
- FDA transfer of Regulatory Authorizations (APA § 3.2(b), § 6.3) may not be complete by Dec. 4.
- The Change of Control consent issue (Issue 2) may require negotiation with Pisen that delays Closing.
- Third-party consents (APA § 7.2(d)) may not all be in hand.

If the Closing slips past December 31 and Muvux is not in breach, Pisen could terminate. If Muvux is in breach (e.g., failure to obtain the § 5.4 funding representation due to Parent merger timing), Pisen could terminate and potentially claim damages.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** Loss of the deal would waste transaction costs and leave Muvux without the Pisen product line it presumably needs for its business plan (and for the Ardentis merger narrative).

**Recommended fix:**

- If any of the above risks materialize, negotiate a short extension of the outside date (e.g., to January 31, 2027) by written amendment (§ 11.3). Do this proactively rather than at the eleventh hour.
- Confirm the status of FDA transfer and third-party consents well before December 4.
- Ensure the § 5.4 funding representation can be satisfied independent of the Parent merger closing (i.e., Muvux has standalone access to the $54M cash).

## Issue 6 (Medium): Warning Letter CAPA Obligation and Limited Remedies

**Where:** APA §§ 6.3, 1.4(c), 9.2(c); Seller Note § 8(b).

**Explanation:**

APA § 6.3 requires Seller to "continue, at its own cost, all corrective and preventive actions required under the Warning Letter until the FDA has issued a close-out letter." This is a post-Closing covenant. If Seller fails to perform, Muvux has a claim under § 9.2(b) (breach of covenant) and § 9.2(c) (Excluded Liability, since all Warning Letter liabilities are excluded per § 1.4(c)).

However, per Issue 3, Muvux's set-off recovery for Excluded Liabilities is capped at $8.16M under the Seller Note. If the Warning Letter remediation costs exceed that amount (not unreasonable for a quality system warning letter involving a medical device manufacturer), Muvux's effective recovery via the Note is limited.

Furthermore, § 9.8 (Exclusive Remedy) limits post-Closing claims to the indemnification provisions, except for Fraud and specific performance. Muvux could seek specific performance of § 6.3 (compel Pisen to perform CAPA), but that is an impractical remedy for ongoing regulatory work.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** The Warning Letter is a known, identified risk. If Pisen walks away post-Closing (having received $54M cash) and fails to complete CAPA, FDA could escalate to an injunction or seizure, which would directly impair Muvux's ability to sell the Products. Muvux's monetary remedy is capped.

**Recommended fix:**

- Before Closing, require Pisen to provide a detailed CAPA plan with milestones and a budget estimate.
- Negotiate a specific indemnity or escrow for Warning Letter remediation costs that is not subject to the § 8(b) cap (tie this to the fix in Issue 3).
- Consider requiring Pisen to maintain a regulatory compliance officer or consultant through the close-out, at Pisen's cost, as a covenant with specific performance available.
- Confirm whether the FDA has indicated a timeline for close-out; if it is likely to extend well beyond the 18-month survival period (§ 9.1), note that covenant claims survive until "fully performed" (§ 9.1, last sentence), so the claim is not time-barred—but collection risk remains.

## Issue 7 (Low): Inventory Count – Practical Gaps and the October Baseline

**Where:** Email thread (messages 2, 3, 6); APA § 2.3(a).

**Explanation:**

The count is scheduled for November 30 – December 1, 2026, but the only inventory data exchanged to date is the **October month-end** listing (Renata's email, Nov. 4, attaching *Pisen_Inventory_by_Location_Oct2026.xlsx*). That is approximately four weeks before the count. In a medical device business with active surgical cases and consignment turnover, inventory levels can shift materially in that window.

Operational points that are agreed but not yet documented in a binding instrument:
- Cutoff procedures (last receiving/shipping document numbers) – agreed in principle (Marcus, Nov. 3) but no formal cutoff protocol has been signed.
- Consigned inventory verification at the ten largest accounts by Muvux personnel – agreed, but the identities of those accounts are only in the October spreadsheet (column H), which may not reflect the largest accounts as of the count date.
- Treatment of QA-hold, MRB, expired, and near-expiry items – Marcus proposed segregation and tagging; the "value assigned to those items" is to be "worked through in the reconciliation." This is unresolved.
- The count memorandum that Priya offered to prepare (email 5, Nov. 4) has not yet been circulated or signed.

None of these change the APA's terms (the count memorandum "will refer back to Section 2.3 and will not change it," per Priya), but ambiguities in execution could feed the disputed-items log and ultimately the § 2.3(b) independent accountant process, which delays Closing.

**Why it matters to Muvux:** A disputed inventory count could delay the Closing Date, compress the buffer to the outside date (Issue 5), and create friction with Pisen at the worst time. If quarantined or expired inventory is included in the count at full standard cost, the Target Inventory Value comparison is distorted in Pisen's favor (less shortfall, higher cash payment by Muvux).

**Recommended fix:**

- Ensure the count memorandum is finalized and signed by both controllers before November 30. It should specify: (a) the cutoff protocol; (b) that QA-hold, MRB, expired, and near-expiry items are excluded from the count or valued at net realizable value (not standard cost); (c) the methodology for consigned sets that are partially used (component-level count per Marcus's email); and (d) that the ten largest consignment accounts are re-identified as of the count date, not the October date.
- Request a November month-end preliminary listing from Pisen before the count to reduce the data gap.
- Pre-agree with Pisen on the treatment of "urgent surgical case orders" shipped during the warehouse freeze window (Renata's email, Nov. 2) – these are to be "logged separately and netted out," but the netting methodology should be specified.
