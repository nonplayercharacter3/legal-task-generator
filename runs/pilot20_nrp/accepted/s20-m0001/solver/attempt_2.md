## Issue 1 (Critical): Product Liability Assumption Deviates Materially from the LOI

**Where it appears:**
- APA § 1.3(c) (Assumed Liabilities)
- LOI ¶ 4(a)(iii) and ¶ 4(b) (Assumed and Excluded Liabilities)

**Explanation:**

The LOI draws a clear line: Buyer assumes product liability only for products "manufactured and sold by Buyer **after the Closing**" (LOI ¶ 4(a)(iii)), and Seller retains "all liabilities for product liability, personal injury, property damage, product warranty, recall and field corrective action claims relating to products manufactured or sold by Seller **before the Closing**" as an Excluded Liability (LOI ¶ 4(b)).

The APA, however, provides in § 1.3(c) that Buyer assumes "all liabilities for product liability, personal injury, property damage, product warranty and field corrective action claims relating to the Products, **whether manufactured or sold before or after the Closing**." This eliminates the pre/post-Closing distinction entirely and also adds "property damage" to the assumed category (the LOI's assumed-liability provision in ¶ 4(a)(iii) does not include property damage; it appears only on the excluded side in ¶ 4(b)).

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

Medical device product liability exposure can be substantial and long-tail. By assuming pre-Closing product liability, Muvux takes on claims arising from products that were designed, manufactured, and sold under Pisen's quality system—including the system that was the subject of the FDA Warning Letter (APA § 1.1(i)). This is a fundamental shift in risk allocation from what the LOI contemplated. Combined with Issue 2 below (the cap on set-off for Excluded Liabilities), Muvux could be left bearing significant pre-Closing product liability costs with limited recourse against Pisen.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend APA § 1.3(c) to track the LOI: limit the assumed product liability to claims "relating to the Products manufactured or sold by Buyer after the Closing Date." Correspondingly, add pre-Closing product liability to § 1.4 (Excluded Liabilities) with language mirroring LOI ¶ 4(b). If Pisen resists, consider a compromise such as a shorter look-back period (e.g., products sold within 12 months before Closing) or a separate escrow or R&W insurance policy to cover pre-Closing product liability.

## Issue 2 (High): Seller Note Caps Set-Off for Excluded Liabilities, Contradicting the APA

**Where it appears:**
- Seller Note § 8(b) (Limitation on Holder Liability)
- APA § 9.5(b) (Caps – exclusion for Excluded Liabilities)
- APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence)

**Explanation:**

APA § 9.5(b) expressly states: "The Seller Indemnity Cap does not apply to Losses arising from Fundamental Representations, Fraud, **and claims under Section 9.2(c) for Excluded Liabilities**." In other words, under the APA, Seller's indemnification obligation for Excluded Liabilities is uncapped.

Seller Note § 8(b), however, provides: "Holder's aggregate liability for all claims under Section 9.2 of the Purchase Agreement, **including claims under Section 9.2(c) for Excluded Liabilities**, whether recovered by set-off under this Section 8 or otherwise, shall not exceed the Seller Indemnity Cap, except for Losses arising from Fraud."

The Seller Indemnity Cap is $8,160,000 (12% of the $68M Purchase Price) per APA § 9.5(a).

APA § 11.9 resolves the conflict: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and the Seller Note, **the Seller Note shall control**."

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

Muvux's primary practical remedy for Excluded Liabilities (Warning Letter costs, pre-Closing taxes, pre-Closing employment claims, etc.) is set-off against the $14M Seller Note. Under the Seller Note's controlling language, Muvux can set off at most $8,160,000 of Excluded Liability claims against the note (less any amounts already set off for other claims). If Excluded Liability exposure exceeds that amount—and in the medical device context, Warning Letter remediation plus potential FDA enforcement could easily do—Muvux would need to pursue Pisen in a separate action to recover the excess, against a counterparty that has just sold substantially all of its assets and may have limited remaining resources.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend Seller Note § 8(b) to carve out claims under APA § 9.2(c) (Excluded Liabilities) from the cap, consistent with APA § 9.5(b). Alternatively, if Pisen insists on the cap, negotiate an increase in the Seller Note principal or a separate holdback/escrow to cover anticipated Excluded Liability exposure (particularly Warning Letter remediation costs). At minimum, ensure the APA's order-of-precedence clause (§ 11.9) is amended so that the APA controls over the Seller Note on indemnification matters.

## Issue 3 (High): Ardentis Merger Will Trigger Change of Control Default Under the Seller Note

**Where it appears:**
- Seller Note § 6(e) (Events of Default – Change of Control)
- Seller Note § 1(b)(ii) (Definition of "Change of Control")
- APA § 5.5 (Parent Transaction representation)

**Explanation:**

APA § 5.5 discloses that Muvux Orthopedics Holdings, Inc. ("Parent"), the sole member of Buyer, has entered into a merger agreement dated August 27, 2026 with Ardentis Surgical Partners, L.P., under which "an affiliate of Ardentis Surgical Partners, L.P. will merge with and into Parent, with Parent surviving."

Seller Note § 6(e) makes it an Event of Default if "Maker, or any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker, undergoes a Change of Control without the prior written consent of Holder." The definition of "Change of Control" in § 1(b)(ii)(B) captures "any merger, consolidation or other business combination involving that Person, whether or not that Person is the surviving entity, after which the holders of that Person's voting equity interests immediately before the transaction hold less than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity interests of the surviving or resulting entity or its ultimate parent."

If the Ardentis merger closes and Ardentis (or its affiliate) ends up holding more than 50% of Parent's voting equity, Parent has undergone a Change of Control. Because Parent is the sole member of Maker, Parent "directly controls Maker." The trigger in § 6(e) is met.

Upon an Event of Default under § 6(e), Pisen may accelerate the entire $14M note under § 7(a), and default interest at 10% (6% + 4% per § 7(c)) begins to run.

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

The Ardentis merger is presumably a core strategic transaction for Muvux's parent. If closing that merger triggers an immediate acceleration of the Seller Note, Muvux faces a $14M+ cash outflow it may not be prepared for, potentially jeopardizing both the Ardentis merger and the Pisen asset purchase. The APA § 5.5 representation only addresses whether the Parent Merger Agreement *prohibits* the asset purchase—it does not address the Seller Note's Change of Control consent requirement.

**Recommended fix:**

Before Closing, obtain Pisen's written consent to the Ardentis Change of Control, either as a condition to Closing or via a side letter. The consent should be specific: identify the Ardentis merger by reference to the Parent Merger Agreement dated August 27, 2026, and confirm that its consummation will not constitute an Event of Default under Seller Note § 6(e). If Pisen requires consideration for the consent (e.g., a fee or prepayment of a portion of the note), negotiate that now. Alternatively, amend Seller Note § 6(e) to carve out the Ardentis transaction by name.

## Issue 4 (Moderate): "Finally Determined" Standard for Set-Off Is Restrictive

**Where it appears:**
- Seller Note § 8(a) (Right of Set-Off)
- APA § 9.7 (Set-Off)

**Explanation:**

Seller Note § 8(a) defines "finally determined" as an amount "agreed in writing by Maker and Holder or determined by a final, non-appealable order of a court of competent jurisdiction." This means Muvux can only actually *set off* (i.e., permanently reduce the note balance) an indemnification claim once Pisen agrees in writing or a court has issued a final, non-appealable judgment.

In practice, Pisen is unlikely to agree in writing to disputed claims, and litigation to a final non-appealable judgment in Delaware could take two to three years or more. During that time, Muvux's only protection is the withholding mechanism in Seller Note § 8(c), which allows Muvux to withhold a "good faith estimate" from payments then due. But withholding is not set-off: if the claim is ultimately determined not to be owed, Muvux must pay the withheld amount back with interest at 6%.

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

If Muvux has a legitimate indemnification claim (e.g., a breach of Seller's regulatory representations or an Excluded Liability), it cannot reduce the note principal until the claim is resolved through agreement or final litigation. This gives Pisen significant leverage: Muvux continues to make interest payments (and eventually the full principal) while its claim is pending, and Pisen can argue the withholding was not a "good faith estimate" if Muvux withholds too aggressively.

**Recommended fix:**

Negotiate an amendment to Seller Note § 8(a) to lower the threshold. Options include: (a) allowing set-off upon a determination by the independent accounting firm referenced in APA § 2.3(b) (if that mechanism is extended to indemnification disputes); (b) allowing set-off upon a final determination by an arbitrator; or (c) permitting set-off upon a good faith written assertion by Muvux that is later found to be reasonable, with the burden on Pisen to prove the claim was made in bad faith. At minimum, clarify in § 8(c) that a good faith estimate need not be precisely calibrated and that Pisen bears the risk of over-withholding only if Muvux acted in objectively unreasonable bad faith.

## Issue 5 (Moderate): Order of Precedence Favors the Seller Note

**Where it appears:**
- APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence)

**Explanation:**

APA § 11.9 provides: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and the Seller Note, the Seller Note shall control." This is the reverse of the more common approach, where the master agreement (the APA) controls over ancillary documents.

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

This clause means that any ambiguity or tension between the APA's indemnification, set-off, or limitation-of-liability provisions and the Seller Note will be resolved in favor of the Seller Note—which was drafted with Pisen's interests in mind as the noteholder. This clause is what elevates Issue 2 from a drafting discrepancy to a binding limitation on Muvux's remedies. It also means that if future disputes arise over the interaction between the two documents, Muvux starts from a structural disadvantage.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend APA § 11.9 to provide that the APA controls over the Seller Note with respect to indemnification, set-off, and limitation-of-liability matters, while the Seller Note continues to control on matters specific to the note (payment mechanics, subordination, events of default, acceleration). Alternatively, if Pisen resists changing the precedence clause, ensure that the specific conflicts identified in Issues 2 and 4 are resolved by direct amendment of the Seller Note so that no ambiguity remains.

## Issue 6 (Moderate): Warning Letter Remediation – Ongoing Operational and Regulatory Risk

**Where it appears:**
- APA § 1.1(i) (Definition of "Warning Letter")
- APA § 1.4(c) (Excluded Liabilities – Warning Letter costs)
- APA § 4.7 (Regulatory Matters representation)
- APA § 6.3 (Regulatory Transfer covenant)

**Explanation:**

The APA acknowledges an FDA Warning Letter relating to Pisen's quality system for the Products (defined in § 1.1(i) and disclosed in the Disclosure Schedule). Seller bears the cost of remediation (§ 1.4(c)) and must continue corrective and preventive actions "until the FDA has issued a close-out letter" (§ 6.3). However, the Regulatory Authorizations (510(k) clearances, establishment registration, etc.) transfer to Buyer at Closing (§ 1.2(a)(ii), § 3.2(b)).

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

Post-Closing, Muvux operates the facility and quality system that is the subject of the Warning Letter. If FDA close-out is delayed (which is common—Warning Letter remediation can take 12–24 months or longer), Muvux is running a business with a known, publicly documented quality system deficiency. This creates: (a) commercial risk—hospitals and GPOs may be reluctant to purchase from a company under an open FDA Warning Letter; (b) regulatory risk—if the deficiency is not corrected, FDA could escalate to an injunction or seizure, which would directly impair Muvux's ability to sell the Products; and (c) indemnification risk—while Seller bears the *cost* of remediation, if the Warning Letter leads to a product recall or market withdrawal, the resulting losses may be characterized as "Excluded Liabilities" (recoverable, but subject to the cap issue in Issue 2) or as "Assumed Liabilities" under § 1.3(c) (if the recall relates to a product sold after Closing, which Muvux would bear in full).

**Recommended fix:**

(1) Negotiate a specific covenant requiring Seller to achieve FDA close-out within a defined period (e.g., 12 months post-Closing), with a liquidated damages or indemnity enhancement if close-out is not achieved by that date. (2) Clarify in the APA that any FDA escalation (injunction, seizure, mandatory recall) arising from the Warning Letter is an Excluded Liability regardless of when the resulting product claims arise. (3) Require Seller to provide a detailed remediation plan and timeline as a condition to Closing, and consider making Closing conditional on the FDA acknowledging receipt of the plan.

## Issue 7 (Lower): Consigned Inventory Count Methodology Creates Accuracy Risk

**Where it appears:**
- Email Thread, items 2–3 (Renata Halvorsen's and Marcus Whitfield's proposals)
- APA § 2.3(a) (Inventory Adjustment)

**Explanation:**

The agreed count methodology for consigned inventory (held at hospitals and independent distributors) relies primarily on Pisen's field reps carrying out physical verification using signed count sheets, with independent distributors providing signed attestations. Muvux personnel will accompany reps only at the ten largest consignment accounts by value. For all other accounts, Muvux will rely on the rep count sheets, distributor attestations, and the consignment ledger.

**Why it matters for Muvux:**

The inventory count determines whether the cash purchase price is reduced under APA § 2.3(a). If consigned inventory is overstated (e.g., damaged sets counted as good, expired lots not properly segregated per Marcus Whitfield's point 3 in the Email Thread), Muvux pays full price for inventory that is not saleable. Because Muvux is not physically present at most consignment accounts, it has limited ability to verify accuracy in real time. The October month-end book value was "roughly $6.5 million" (Renata's email, item 2), which is only $300,000 above the $6.2M target—meaning even a modest count discrepancy could trigger a price adjustment or, conversely, a missed adjustment.

**Recommended fix:**

(1) In the count memorandum (to be prepared by Priya per Email Thread item 5), specify that expired, near-expiry, QA-hold, and MRB items are excluded from the count value unless clearly identified and agreed by both parties. (2) Require that the consignment ledger be made available to Muvux for reconciliation *before* the count, not just after, so Muvux can identify discrepancies in advance. (3) Consider expanding Muvux's on-site presence beyond the top ten accounts if the consignment inventory represents a material portion of the total (we would need the October listing to assess this). (4) Confirm that the "disputed-items log" process (Email Thread item 4) includes a mechanism for Muvux to challenge distributor attestations that appear inconsistent with the consignment ledger.
