## Issues Requiring Immediate Attention

### 1. Seller Note Caps Recovery for Fundamental Representations and Pre-Closing Taxes at $4.85M and Eliminates Direct Recourse Against Seller

**Documents:** Seller Note § 5.2; SPA §§ 9.5(c), 9.6, 12.10(b)

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.5(c) provides that the Cap does not apply to Fundamental Representation breaches or Pre-Closing Taxes, and that Seller's aggregate liability for such claims "shall not exceed the Purchase Price" ($48.5M). SPA § 9.6 establishes a three-tier recovery: (a) Escrow Funds, (b) set-off against the Seller Note, (c) directly from Seller.

However, Seller Note § 5.2 states that set-off is "the sole and exclusive means by which Maker and the other Buyer Indemnitees may recover from Holder any Losses in excess of the Escrow Funds," and that the aggregate of set-off plus escrow disbursements "shall not exceed the Cap, **including claims for breach of Fundamental Representations and claims for Taxes**."

Because SPA § 12.10(b) provides that "with respect to payment, set-off and recourse under the Seller Note, the Seller Note shall control," the Seller Note's limitations almost certainly override the SPA's broader indemnification rights. The practical effect: Saitosk's maximum recovery for a Fundamental Rep breach (e.g., Seller did not own the Shares free and clear) or for Pre-Closing Taxes is $4,850,000 total—not the $48.5M the SPA purports to allow—and Saitosk has no right to sue Seller directly for amounts above the escrow.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Seller Note § 5.2 to (a) carve out Fundamental Representation claims and Pre-Closing Tax claims from the Cap, consistent with SPA § 9.5(c), and (b) delete or revise the "sole and exclusive means" language to preserve the third tier of direct recourse against Seller under SPA § 9.6(c). Alternatively, if the business deal is that the Note is the exclusive recourse, the SPA should be amended to conform, and the client should be advised of the materially reduced protection.

### 2. Escrow Agreement Excludes Fines, Penalties, and Governmental Payments from "Losses," Undermining the Food Safety Indemnity

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Losses"), § 4.5; SPA §§ 1.1 (definition of "Losses"), 9.4

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.4 provides a specific indemnity for the Pre-Closing Food Safety Matters (the January 2026 listeria finding and FDA warning letter), covering "fines, penalties, recall and remediation costs and amounts paid to any Governmental Authority." SPA § 9.4 further states these claims "shall be payable first from the Escrow Funds."

The SPA's definition of "Losses" (§ 1.1) expressly includes "fines, penalties." But the Escrow Agreement defines "Losses" (§ 1.1) as "actually incurred by a Buyer Indemnitee, **excluding fines, penalties and other amounts paid to any Governmental Authority**." Escrow Agreement § 4.5 then provides that the Escrow Agent "shall disburse Escrow Funds to Buyer under this Article 4 **only in respect of Losses**" (i.e., as defined in the Escrow Agreement).

The result: If the FDA imposes a fine or penalty related to the listeria finding, the Escrow Agent is contractually obligated to refuse disbursement from the escrow for that amount, even though the SPA says food safety claims are "payable first from the Escrow Funds." Combined with Issue 1 above (which caps set-off at $4.85M and eliminates direct recourse), Saitosk could be left with no practical remedy for the most likely food safety exposure.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Escrow Agreement § 1.1 to either (a) conform the definition of "Losses" to the SPA's definition (including fines and penalties), or (b) add a carve-out providing that, for purposes of Article 4, "Losses" includes all amounts covered by SPA § 9.4 (the Pre-Closing Food Safety Matters). The Escrow Agent's consent to the amendment will be required under Escrow Agreement § 11.3.

## Other Issues

### 3. Missing Ashgrove Airport Properties LLC Consent

**Documents:** SPA § 7.1(c); Office Lease § 11.2

**Explanation:** SPA § 7.1(c) makes it a condition to Buyer's closing obligation that "the consent of Ashgrove Airport Properties LLC under the Company's administrative office lease" be obtained. Office Lease § 11.2 provides that a transfer of more than 50% of the voting stock of Tenant (the Company) "is an assignment for purposes of this Article 11 and requires Landlord's consent." Saitosk is acquiring 100% of the Company's stock, so this consent is mandatory.

The Corriedale consent letter is in the folder, but no Ashgrove consent is. Without it, the closing condition in SPA § 7.1(c) cannot be satisfied, and Saitosk would have no obligation to close.

**Recommended Fix:** Confirm with the deal team whether the Ashgrove consent has been requested and, if so, its status. If not yet requested, initiate immediately—the landlord has 15 Business Days to respond under Office Lease § 11.1, and the consent must be in hand before the April 30, 2026 target closing date.

### 4. Conflicting Governing Law and Jurisdiction Between SPA and Escrow Agreement

**Documents:** SPA § 12.8; Escrow Agreement § 11.2

**Explanation:** The SPA is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in Manhattan state or federal courts. The Escrow Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (or Superior Court if Chancery lacks subject-matter jurisdiction).

This creates a risk of inconsistent interpretation of shared concepts. The most acute example is the "Losses" definition conflict (Issue 2 above): a New York court interpreting the SPA and a Delaware court interpreting the Escrow Agreement could reach different conclusions about what amounts are recoverable from the escrow. Additionally, the Delaware Court of Chancery has limited jurisdiction over pure contract disputes (it is primarily a court of equity for corporate and fiduciary matters), which could create threshold jurisdictional disputes before the merits are reached.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Escrow Agreement § 11.2 to conform to the SPA's New York governing law and Manhattan jurisdiction. If the Escrow Agent resists (national banks sometimes prefer Delaware for escrow arrangements), at minimum add a provision that, in the event of a conflict between the SPA and the Escrow Agreement regarding the scope of indemnifiable amounts, the SPA's definitions control.

### 5. Seller Note Cross-Default Triggered by Senior Debt Acceleration

**Documents:** Seller Note § 8.1(f), § 8.2

**Explanation:** Seller Note § 8.1(f) provides that an Event of Default occurs if "Senior Debt in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $5,000,000 is accelerated, and the acceleration is not rescinded within 30 days." Upon an Event of Default under § 8.1(d) or (e) (bankruptcy), acceleration is automatic. For other Events of Default (including § 8.1(f)), the Holder (Noumisk) may accelerate by written notice (§ 8.2).

"Senior Debt" is broadly defined (§ 1.1) to include all indebtedness under "any credit agreement, loan agreement, note purchase agreement or similar financing arrangement with one or more banks, institutional lenders or other financial institutions." If Saitosk's acquisition financing (or any other senior facility over $5M) is accelerated—whether due to a technical default, a covenant breach, or a lender's discretionary action—Noumisk could accelerate the $6M Seller Note, creating an immediate liquidity demand on Saitosk.

**Recommended Fix:** Consider negotiating (a) a higher threshold (e.g., $10M) or (b) a longer cure period (e.g., 60 days) for the § 8.1(f) trigger, or (c) a carve-out for accelerations that are subsequently rescinded or settled within the cure period. At minimum, Saitosk's finance team should be aware of this cross-default when structuring the acquisition facility.

### 6. Burdensome Monthly Financial Reporting Obligation

**Documents:** Seller Note § 7.1(a)

**Explanation:** Saitosk must deliver to Noumisk, within 30 days after each month-end, the unaudited balance sheet and income/cash flow statements of the Company, "certified by the chief financial officer of Maker [Saitosk] as fairly presenting in all material respects the financial position and results of operations of the Company."

Two concerns: (a) the 30-day deadline is aggressive for monthly financials, particularly for a company that may not have a robust month-end close process; and (b) the certification is by Saitosk's CFO (not the Company's CFO), which creates personal exposure for Saitosk's CFO and is unusual for a subsidiary's financials. A breach of this covenant is an Event of Default (subject to a 30-day cure period under § 8.1(c)).

**Recommended Fix:** Negotiate to (a) extend the deadline to 45 days, (b) change the certifying officer to the Company's CFO (or "an officer of Maker or the Company"), and (c) add a materiality qualifier to the certification ("fairly presenting in all material respects, without audit qualifications").

### 7. Escrow Tax Treatment May Leave Saitosk Short on Tax Distributions

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement §§ 6.1, 6.3

**Explanation:** Escrow Agreement § 6.1 provides that "Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds until they are disbursed" and "Buyer shall report all income earned on the Escrow Funds." Section 6.3 allows the Escrow Agent to release to Buyer "an amount equal to 40% of the earnings allocable to Buyer for the preceding calendar year" upon written request.

If Saitosk's combined federal and state tax rate exceeds 40% (e.g., 21% federal + 8.85% New York + any local tax), the 40% distribution may be insufficient to cover the tax on the escrow earnings, leaving Saitosk out of pocket. Additionally, because the distribution is based on the "preceding calendar year," there is a timing mismatch: Saitosk pays tax in year X on earnings accrued in year X, but the distribution is calculated from year X-1 earnings.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Escrow Agreement § 6.3 to (a) increase the distribution percentage to 50% (or the applicable combined tax rate plus a cushion), and (b) allow distributions based on the current year's earnings (with an estimated true-up) rather than solely the preceding year. Alternatively, negotiate a provision under which Seller reimburses Buyer for any tax in excess of the distributed amount.

### 8. Bill of Sale Representations Run to the Company, Not to Buyer

**Documents:** Bill of Sale § 3.1; SPA § 12.5

**Explanation:** Bill of Sale § 3.1 provides that "Seller represents **to the Company**" that it held good and marketable title to the Catering Trucks free and clear of all liens. The Bill of Sale is a bilateral agreement between Seller and the Company only; Saitosk (Buyer) is not a party. SPA § 12.5 confirms that the SPA is for the sole benefit of the parties and their permitted assigns, with the exception of the Indemnitees under Article IX.

In practice, because Saitosk will own 100% of the Company, it can direct the Company to enforce the Bill of Sale representations. However, if a title defect on the Catering Trucks emerges after the 18-month survival period (SPA § 9.1) or if the Company's claim is subject to the basket or cap, Saitosk may have a gap in protection. The SPA's indemnification (§ 9.2(a)) covers breaches of Seller's representations "in this Agreement or in any certificate delivered under it," but the Bill of Sale is a separate Ancillary Agreement, not a certificate delivered under the SPA.

**Recommended Fix:** Add Saitosk as a third-party beneficiary of the Bill of Sale's title representations, or add a specific representation in the SPA (e.g., in Article IV) that the Catering Trucks are free and clear of all liens, so that any breach is covered by the SPA's indemnification framework.

### 9. Corriedale Consent Lapses on the Same Date as the SPA Outside Date (No Buffer)

**Documents:** Corriedale Consent Letter § 4.2; SPA § 8.1(b)

**Explanation:** The Corriedale consent lapses if the Closing has not occurred by June 30, 2026 (§ 4.2). The SPA's outside date for termination is also June 30, 2026 (§ 8.1(b)). There is no buffer: if the closing is delayed to June 29 or 30, any further slip causes the consent to lapse simultaneously with the SPA's termination right. If the consent lapses, the closing condition in SPA § 7.1(c) fails, and Saitosk would need to re-negotiate with Corriedale (a key airline customer) under time pressure.

**Recommended Fix:** Request that Corriedale extend the lapse date to 30–60 days beyond the SPA outside date (e.g., July 31 or August 30, 2026). Alternatively, negotiate a short extension of the SPA outside date to provide a cushion.

### 10. SPA § 6.5 Imposes an Affirmative Post-Closing Obligation on Buyer to Release Seller from Guarantees

**Documents:** SPA § 6.5; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Seller Guarantees")

**Explanation:** SPA § 6.5 requires that "Following the Closing, Buyer shall use commercially reasonable efforts to cause Seller to be released from the Seller Guarantees, including by offering substitute credit support reasonably acceptable to the relevant counterparties." The Seller Guarantees cover the Company's obligations under the Flight Kitchen lease and food and beverage supply contracts.

This is an ongoing post-closing covenant. If the Flight Kitchen landlord or supply contract counterparties refuse to accept Saitosk's substitute credit support (e.g., because Saitosk's credit profile is weaker than Noumisk's), Seller remains on the guarantee indefinitely. While this protects Seller, it creates an ongoing entanglement between Saitosk and Noumisk and a potential source of friction. More importantly for Saitosk, if Saitosk fails to use "commercially reasonable efforts," it would be in breach of a surviving covenant (SPA § 9.1: "Covenants shall survive in accordance with their terms"), exposing Saitosk to indemnification liability under SPA § 9.3(b).

**Recommended Fix:** Add a time limit (e.g., 12 months post-closing) after which the obligation terminates, and clarify that "commercially reasonable efforts" does not require Saitosk to provide a parent guarantee or letter of credit if the counterparty's requirements are unreasonable. Also confirm whether the Flight Kitchen lease itself requires landlord consent to substitute the guarantor (the Flight Kitchen lease is not in the folder—request it).
