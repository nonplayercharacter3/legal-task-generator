## Priority 1 – Critical

### Issue 1: Change of Control Triggers Termination Right and Fee Acceleration

**Where:** License Agreement § 14.4; Amendment No. 1, Recital B; Parent Guaranty §§ 3.1(f), 5.6

**What:** Section 14.4 provides that Tilil may terminate the Agreement upon 30 days' notice if Kunoux undergoes a Change of Control without Tilil's prior written consent, and upon such termination *all Subscription Fees for the remainder of the Initial Term become immediately due and payable*. The defined term "Change of Control" (§ 1) captures the sale of more than 50% of the voting equity of any entity controlling Licensee.

Amendment No. 1, Recital B, acknowledges that Kunoux Holdings signed an equity purchase agreement on September 18, 2026 to sell its general partner and operating subsidiaries to Ostrander Dairy Partners III, L.P., with closing expected in Q1 2027. Yet the Amendment does not (a) obtain Tilil's consent to the Change of Control, (b) waive or amend Section 14.4, or (c) address the fee acceleration consequence.

**Why it matters:** At closing, Tilil will have an unqualified right to terminate the Agreement and demand approximately $1.9 million in accelerated fees (four remaining Subscription Years × $486,000, less any amounts already invoiced). Kunoux would lose its traceability platform at the worst possible moment—mid-implementation of Phase 2—and the parent guaranty (which survives the Change of Control by its terms, see Parent Guaranty § 3.1(f)) would backstop the accelerated obligation. This is a material financial and operational risk that appears to have been overlooked in the Amendment.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Ostrander closing, negotiate an amendment or side letter with Tilil that either (a) obtains Tilil's written consent to the Change of Control under § 14.4 (which would negate the termination right), or (b) amends § 14.4 to provide that the Change of Control to Ostrander does not trigger termination or fee acceleration, or (c) at minimum, caps the acceleration at a reasonable amount and provides a transition period. Given that Tilil has already signed the Amendment acknowledging the transaction, it is in a difficult position to refuse consent without appearing to act in bad faith. If Tilil resists, consider whether the commercial relationship supports a fee-for-consent negotiation.

### Issue 2: Exclusivity Scope Is Far Broader Than Negotiated

**Where:** Term Sheet § 4.1; Order Form § 3.1; License Agreement § 2.6

**What:** The Term Sheet states: "Kunoux will agree that the exclusivity commitment in the Definitive Agreements will cover milk traceability software at the **Eau Claire and Sheboygan, Wisconsin plants only**." (Term Sheet § 4.1, emphasis added.)

The License Agreement § 2.6 prohibits Kunoux from using any third-party or internally developed software performing Traceability Functions "at any **Covered Facility**." The Order Form § 3.1 defines "Covered Facilities" as "all facilities owned, leased or operated by Licensee or any of its Affiliates, **wherever located**, including any processing plant, receiving station, cold storage warehouse, distribution center or laboratory, and including any such facility that Licensee or any of its Affiliates **acquires, leases or begins to operate after the Order Form Date**."

**Why it matters:** Kunoux is locked into DairyLedger for traceability functions at every facility it owns or operates anywhere in the world, including facilities it has not yet acquired, for the entire five-year Initial Term (and any Renewal Term). If Kunoux acquires a new plant or opens a distribution center, it cannot deploy a different traceability solution there without breaching § 2.6. This is a significant operational constraint that goes well beyond the deal as negotiated.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Order Form § 3.1 to limit "Covered Facilities" for purposes of the § 2.6 exclusivity obligation to the Eau Claire and Sheboygan plants (or, if Kunoux wants some flexibility, to facilities where the Subscription Services are actually deployed and in production use). Alternatively, add a carve-out in § 2.6 for facilities where the Subscription Services have not been implemented. This should be raised in connection with the Change of Control amendment (Issue 1) as part of a package negotiation with Tilil.

### Issue 3: Parent Guaranty Survives Change of Control and Is Extremely Broad

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §§ 1.1, 2.1, 3.1(a), 3.1(f), 4.3, 5.6; Amendment No. 1 § 6.2

**What:** The Guaranty is an absolute, unconditional, continuing guaranty of payment *and performance* (not merely collection). It covers all obligations under the Agreement "whether now existing or arising later" (§ 1.1). It is expressly not released by any amendment that increases fees, extends the term, or adds facilities (§ 3.1(a)), and not affected by "any change in the name, organization, structure, ownership or business of Licensee or the Guarantor" (§ 3.1(f)). Amendment No. 1 § 6.2 has the Guarantor confirming the guaranty "remains in full force and effect" and "extends to Licensee's obligations under the Agreement as amended."

Critically, § 5.6 contains a representation that the Guarantor "directly or indirectly owns all of the outstanding equity interests of Licensee." After the Ostrander transaction, this will no longer be true. Yet § 3.1(f) provides that the guaranty is not affected by changes in ownership.

**Why it matters:** (a) If the Ostrander transaction closes, Kunoux Holdings may no longer be the parent of Licensee, but the guaranty continues to bind it (or its successor) for the full five-year term. This creates an unexpected contingent liability for the selling entity (or its buyers) that was not part of the equity purchase agreement's risk allocation. (b) The guaranty covers *performance* obligations, not just payment—meaning Kunoux Holdings is on the hook if Kunoux Creamery fails to, e.g., provide timely access to plant networks or cleanse data. (c) The subordination clause (§ 4.3) means any intercompany debt from Kunoux Creamery to Kunoux Holdings is subordinated to Tilil's claims while a demand is outstanding or an insolvency proceeding is pending.

**Recommended fix:** In connection with the Ostrander transaction, negotiate with Tilil to either (a) release the guaranty upon closing of the Change of Control (or upon the acquirer providing a replacement guaranty), or (b) at minimum, limit the guaranty to payment obligations only and cap it at a stated dollar amount. Kunoux Holdings should also confirm with its own M&A counsel that the equity purchase agreement allocates this contingent liability appropriately. If the guaranty cannot be released, ensure the Ostrander buyers understand and price this exposure.

## Priority 2 – High

### Issue 4: DPA Indemnity Is Uncapped

**Where:** DPA §§ 8.1–8.2; License Agreement § 12.2

**What:** DPA § 8.1 requires Licensee to indemnify Licensor for all losses arising from Licensee Data or Licensee's processing instructions, including claims by Data Subjects, regulators or third parties. DPA § 8.2 states: "The obligations of Licensee under Section 8.1 shall **not** be subject to any limitation of liability in the Agreement, including Section 12 of the License Agreement."

**Why it matters:** The general liability cap in License Agreement § 12.2 is fees paid or payable in the preceding 12 months (approximately $486,000). But the DPA indemnity is uncapped. If a driver or farm supplier brings a privacy claim (e.g., regarding GPS location data collected under the Farm Pickup module), or a regulator imposes a fine, Kunoux's exposure is unlimited. Given that the platform processes personal data of up to 900 farm suppliers, 400 drivers, and consumers, this is a meaningful risk.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a cap on the DPA indemnity, or at minimum carve it into the general cap in § 12.2 with a super-cap (e.g., 2× or 3× annual fees). If Tilil resists, ensure Kunoux's cyber/privacy insurance covers this exposure and that the DPA indemnity is limited to claims arising from Licensee's actual breach of its instructions or failure to obtain required consents (as opposed to claims arising from Licensor's own processing).

### Issue 5: Sole Remedy for Availability Failures May Be Inadequate in a Recall Scenario

**Where:** License Agreement § 5.6; Exhibit A §§ A.3–A.4

**What:** Section 5.6 provides that service credits are Licensee's "sole monetary remedy" for failure to meet the 99.9% availability commitment, except for the chronic failure termination right (three months below 99.0% in a rolling 12-month period) and termination for material breach under § 14.3.

**Why it matters:** If the platform is down during an active recall or regulatory trace request, Kunoux's only monetary remedy is a service credit of up to 30% of the monthly fee (approximately $12,150 per month at the highest tier). The actual cost of a delayed recall response—product destruction, regulatory sanctions, customer claims, organic certification jeopardy—could be orders of magnitude higher. The chronic failure termination right is of limited help in an acute recall scenario because it requires three bad months in a rolling 12-month period.

**Recommended fix:** Add a carve-out to § 5.6 for availability failures that occur during a Recall Event or Regulatory Trace Request (as defined in the Quality Agreement). In that scenario, service credits should not be the sole remedy; Kunoux should be able to claim actual damages up to the general cap (or a super-cap). Alternatively, negotiate a higher service credit tier (e.g., 50% or 100% of the monthly fee) for outages that coincide with a Severity 1 incident involving trace or recall functions.

### Issue 6: Escalation Structure Has No Independent Kunoux Voice Above the Project Manager

**Where:** SOW § 7.5; Implementation Kickoff Minutes § 4.4

**What:** The escalation table in SOW § 7.5 designates Kunoux's Level 1 contact as the Project Manager (Marcus Lindqvist) and Level 2 as the Director of Plant Systems (also Marcus Lindqvist). The kickoff minutes confirm: "This means Mr. Lindqvist holds both Level 1 and Level 2 for Kunoux." The minutes note that Kunoux "will consider whether to nominate a different Level 1 contact after Go-Live at the Eau Claire Plant."

**Why it matters:** If a support dispute arises that Mr. Lindqvist cannot resolve at the project manager level, there is no independent Kunoux escalation path before jumping to the VP of Operations (Level 3). This creates a structural weakness in dispute resolution during the implementation period, when issues are most likely to arise.

**Recommended fix:** Designate a different Level 2 contact (e.g., the IT integration lead or a senior receiving supervisor) and update the escalation table by written notice under SOW § 7.5. This is a low-cost, low-friction fix that should be done now, not deferred to post-Go-Live.

## Priority 3 – Moderate

### Issue 7: Press Release Timeline Is Now Inconsistent with the Amended Schedule

**Where:** Press Release § 4 (Rollout Plan); Amendment No. 1 § 3.1

**What:** The press release (dated March 10, 2026) states that Sheboygan go-live is "targeted for later in 2026." Amendment No. 1 moved Milestone M6 (Sheboygan Go-Live) to January 18, 2027. The press release is a public document that may be cited by regulators, customers or organic certifiers as evidence of Kunoux's implementation timeline.

**Why it matters:** Low direct legal risk, but if a regulator or certifier asks about implementation status and references the press release, the discrepancy could create confusion or suggest poor project management. More practically, if the January 2027 date slips further, Kunoux will have two public statements to reconcile.

**Recommended fix:** No immediate action required, but Kunoux should be prepared to provide an updated timeline if asked by a regulator or certifier. If a second press release or customer communication is planned, ensure it reflects the amended dates.

### Issue 8: 60-Day Post-Termination Data Export Window May Be Insufficient for Regulatory Records

**Where:** License Agreement § 8.8; Quality Agreement § 4.6; DPA § 9.2

**What:** Upon termination or expiration, Licensor must make Licensee Data available for export for 60 days, after which it will be deleted. Quality Agreement § 4.6 confirms that on termination, § 8.8 applies and "Licensee is responsible for keeping exported Quality Records for any remaining period required by Applicable Food Laws."

**Why it matters:** If the Agreement terminates unexpectedly (e.g., under § 14.4 following the Change of Control), Kunoux has only 60 days to export all trace and cold-chain records that it must retain for 3–5 years under the Quality Agreement Schedule 2. Given the volume of data (up to 1.6 billion pounds of milk, 60,000 tanker loads, 900 suppliers per year), and the need to preserve lot genealogy linkages, 60 days may be operationally challenging, particularly if the termination is contested or if Kunoux is simultaneously managing the Change of Control integration.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an extension of the export window to 90 or 120 days, or add a provision that the export window does not begin to run until any dispute regarding the termination is resolved. At minimum, ensure Kunoux's IT team has a tested export procedure in place before the Change of Control closes.

### Issue 9: Volume Entitlements May Be Tight for Growth Scenarios

**Where:** Order Form §§ 5.2, 6.1; Term Sheet § 6.5

**What:** The Order Form caps raw milk received at 1.6 billion pounds per Subscription Year, tanker loads at 60,000, and monitored temperature points at 400. The Term Sheet stated these were sized to 2025 volumes "with headroom for approximately 20% growth." Overage rates include $0.00025 per excess pound of raw milk and $1.50 per excess tanker load.

**Why it matters:** If Kunoux's volumes grow beyond the 20% headroom (e.g., due to the Ostrander transaction bringing in additional supply or production capacity), overage charges will apply. At 1.6 billion pounds, even a 100-million-pound excess would cost $25,000 annually in overages. More importantly, the monitored temperature point entitlement (400) is a hard constraint: if Kunoux adds silos or tanker assignments, it will need to purchase additional hardware at $640–$1,850 per unit plus $180 per point per year in overage fees.

**Recommended fix:** Monitor actual usage against entitlements through the monthly reports (Exhibit A § A.8, as amended by Amendment No. 1 § 2.4). If usage is trending toward 80% of any entitlement in the first year, initiate a good-faith discussion under License Agreement § 3.2 to amend the Order Form before overages trigger. In connection with the Change of Control negotiation, consider requesting a one-time upward adjustment to volume entitlements to reflect post-transaction scale.

### Issue 10: Driver GPS Location Data Requires Specific Notices

**Where:** DPA § 2.4; DPA Schedule 1 ¶ 5; Implementation Kickoff Minutes § 11.2

**What:** DPA § 2.4 makes Licensee responsible for "providing any notices to Data Subjects, and obtaining any consents, required under Data Protection Laws for the Processing described in this DPA, including the collection of location data from drivers and haulers through the mobile applications." DPA Schedule 1 ¶ 5 identifies "precise geolocation of drivers collected at the time of recording a farm pickup" as sensitive personal data. The kickoff minutes confirm Kunoux will "prepare a short driver notice for inclusion in the onboarding kit."

**Why it matters:** Several states (including potentially Wisconsin, depending on its data security laws) and the CCPA/CPRA (to the extent any California-based haulers are involved) require notice before collecting precise location data. If Kunoux fails to provide adequate notice and obtain any required consent, it exposes itself to the uncapped DPA indemnity (Issue 4) and potential regulatory enforcement.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm that the driver notice has been drafted, reviewed by counsel, and included in the onboarding kit before Phase 2 go-live at Sheboygan (January 2027). Document that the notice was provided to all drivers. If any drivers are employed by independent haulers (not Kunoux), confirm that Kunoux's agreements with those haulers require the haulers to provide the notice to their own employees.

## Priority 4 – Lower

### Issue 11: Fee Acceleration on Licensor's Termination for Cause

**Where:** License Agreement § 14.5(d)

**What:** If Tilil terminates for Kunoux's material breach under § 14.3(a), "all Subscription Fees for the remainder of the then-current Initial Term or Renewal Term shall become due and payable."

**Why it matters:** This means a single uncured material breach (e.g., a failure to pay an invoice, even if disputed in good faith but not properly noticed under § 7.4) could trigger acceleration of up to ~$2 million in remaining fees. The interaction with the parent guaranty (Issue 3) amplifies this risk.

**Recommended fix:** This is harder to negotiate post-signing, but Kunoux should ensure its accounts payable process is robust and that any disputed invoice is handled strictly in accordance with § 7.4 (written notice before the due date, payment of undisputed portion). Consider adding an internal compliance checklist for invoice disputes.

### Issue 12: Licensor's Right to Use Feedback Without Restriction

**Where:** License Agreement § 9.4

**What:** "If Licensee or any Authorized User provides suggestions or feedback regarding the Subscription Services, Licensor may use that feedback without restriction or obligation."

**Why it matters:** Kunoux's quality assurance and operations teams will inevitably suggest workflow improvements, alert threshold logic, or report formats during implementation and hypercare. If those suggestions incorporate Kunoux's proprietary processes (e.g., its specific lot genealogy model for cheese, or its override logic for load release), Tilil could use them for other customers without compensation. The only protection is that Tilil cannot identify Kunoux as the source or disclose Kunoux Confidential Information.

**Recommended fix:** Low priority, but Kunoux should train its implementation team to mark any truly proprietary process suggestions as Confidential Information before sharing them with Tilil, so that the § 9.4 carve-out for Confidential Information applies. Alternatively, in the next amendment, narrow § 9.4 to exclude feedback that constitutes or incorporates a party's Confidential Information.

### Issue 13: Order of Precedence May Create Ambiguity

**Where:** License Agreement § 16.4

**What:** The order of precedence is: DPA (as to personal data) → License Agreement → Order Form → Quality Agreement → SOW.

**Why it matters:** The Quality Agreement ranks *below* the Order Form. If the Order Form and Quality Agreement are inconsistent on a point relevant to food safety functions (e.g., retention periods, recall support obligations), the Order Form controls. This is counterintuitive given that the Quality Agreement is the more specific document for those subject matters. In practice, I have not identified a direct conflict, but the hierarchy creates a latent risk if the documents are amended independently in the future.

**Recommended fix:** No immediate action. Flag for the team to be aware when negotiating any future amendments that touch both the Order Form and the Quality Agreement, to ensure consistency.
