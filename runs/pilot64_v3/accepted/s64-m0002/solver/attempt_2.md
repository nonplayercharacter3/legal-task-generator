## Executive Summary

Review of all ten documents in the matter folder reveals two critical issues requiring immediate attention, several high-priority financial and operational risks, and a number of moderate items that should be addressed in the next amendment cycle or at renewal. The most urgent matter is the interaction between the pending Change of Control (Kunoux Holdings' sale to Ostrander Dairy Partners III) and Section 14.4 of the License Agreement, which gives Tilil the right to terminate and accelerate all remaining Subscription Fees upon closing. The second critical issue is that the executed Order Form defines "Covered Facilities" far more broadly than the Term Sheet contemplated, which in turn expands the exclusivity covenant in Section 2.6 of the License Agreement to all of Kunoux's current and future facilities, not just the two Wisconsin plants.

## Priority 1 – Critical

### Issue 1: Change of Control Triggers Termination Right and Fee Acceleration

**Where:** License Agreement § 14.4; Amendment No. 1, Recital B; Parent Guaranty § 1.1 (Guaranteed Obligations, clause (b))

**The problem.** Section 14.4 provides that Licensor may terminate the Agreement upon 30 days' written notice if Licensee undergoes a Change of Control without Licensor's prior written consent. Upon such termination, "all Subscription Fees for the remainder of the Initial Term shall become immediately due and payable." The Initial Term runs to February 28, 2031. At the Annual Subscription Fee of $486,000, the remaining fees after a Q1 2027 closing would be approximately $1.9 million, all accelerated.

Amendment No. 1, Recital B, acknowledges that Kunoux Holdings signed an equity purchase agreement on September 18, 2026 to sell to Ostrander Dairy Partners III, L.P., with closing expected in Q1 2027. However, the Amendment does not obtain Tilil's consent to the Change of Control, does not waive Section 14.4, and does not modify the fee-acceleration consequence. The Guarantor's acknowledgment in Section 6 of the Amendment confirms the guaranty "extends to Licensee's obligations under the Agreement as amended," which includes any accelerated fees under § 14.4 (expressly captured by Parent Guaranty § 1.1(b)).

**Why it matters.** If the Ostrander transaction closes without a negotiated solution, Tilil can terminate the entire Agreement and demand approximately $1.9 million in accelerated fees, all backstopped by the Parent Guaranty. This would also trigger the transition provisions (§ 14.6), but Kunoux would lose the benefit of the fixed-price Initial Term and the exclusivity protection (which cuts both ways—Kunoux would be free to use alternative software, but would also lose the DairyLedger platform mid-contract).

**Recommended fix.** Before the Ostrander closing, negotiate with Tilil one of the following:

- **Preferred:** An amendment or side letter in which Tilil consents to the Change of Control in exchange for a modest fee (e.g., a one-time change-of-control fee of 10–15% of remaining Subscription Fees, or a right to renew at then-market pricing). This preserves the fixed-price benefit and the platform continuity.
- **Alternative:** If Tilil insists on the termination right, negotiate that any termination under § 14.4 will be treated as a mutual termination with a pro rata refund of prepaid fees (rather than full acceleration), plus an extended transition period (e.g., 180 days instead of 90) given the regulatory record-keeping obligations under the Quality Agreement.
- **Floor:** Ensure the Ostrander deal documents include a condition that Kunoux Holdings will not close unless this issue is resolved, and that the Parent Guaranty is released or capped in connection with the Change of Control.

### Issue 2: Exclusivity Scope Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where:** Term Sheet § 4.1; Order Form § 3.1; License Agreement § 2.6

**The problem.** The Term Sheet stated: "the exclusivity commitment in the Definitive Agreements will cover milk traceability software at the Eau Claire and Sheboygan, Wisconsin plants only" (Term Sheet § 4.1). The executed License Agreement § 2.6, however, prohibits use of competing traceability software "at any Covered Facility." The Order Form § 3.1 defines "Covered Facilities" as "all facilities owned, leased or operated by Licensee or any of its Affiliates, wherever located, including any processing plant, receiving station, cold storage warehouse, distribution center or laboratory, and including any such facility that Licensee or any of its Affiliates acquires, leases or begins to operate after the Order Form Date."

This means Kunoux is contractually barred from using any third-party (or internally developed) software performing Traceability Functions at *any* facility it currently operates or may acquire in the future, worldwide, for the duration of the Term (five years plus renewals). The Term Sheet's intent was clearly limited to the two Wisconsin plants.

**Why it matters.** If Kunoux acquires or opens a new facility (e.g., a cheese aging warehouse, a distribution center, or a plant in another state), it cannot deploy a different traceability system there without breaching § 2.6. The breach would give Tilil the right to terminate under § 14.3(a) and, under § 14.5(d), accelerate all remaining Subscription Fees. The Parent Guaranty backstops that acceleration. This is a significant strategic constraint that Kunoux's management may not be aware of.

**Recommended fix.** In the next amendment (ideally bundled with the Change of Control negotiation in Issue 1), amend Order Form § 3.1 or License Agreement § 2.6 to limit the exclusivity covenant to the Eau Claire and Sheboygan plants (i.e., the "Implementation Sites" as defined in the SOW), consistent with the Term Sheet. Alternatively, if Tilil insists on a broader definition for billing purposes, carve the exclusivity obligation out and tie it specifically to the Implementation Sites.

## Priority 2 – High

### Issue 3: Fee Acceleration on Licensor Termination for Breach

**Where:** License Agreement § 14.5(d)

**The problem.** If Tilil terminates for Kunoux's material breach (other than non-payment), "all Subscription Fees for the remainder of the then-current Initial Term or Renewal Term shall become due and payable." This means a single material breach—potentially even an inadvertent one (e.g., a supplier using a competing app at a new facility under the broad Covered Facilities definition in Issue 2)—could trigger acceleration of up to ~$2.4 million.

**Why it matters.** The 30-day cure period in § 14.3(a) provides some protection, but if the breach is not curable (e.g., a structural exclusivity violation) or if Kunoux fails to cure within 30 days, the financial consequence is severe and disproportionate.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate an amendment to § 14.5(d) providing that fee acceleration applies only to breaches that are (a) willful or repeated after notice, and (b) not curable. For curable breaches, the remedy should be limited to actual damages plus a pro rata refund of prepaid fees for the post-termination period (as already provided in § 14.5(c) for Licensee-initiated termination). At minimum, ensure the Change of Control consent (Issue 1) includes a waiver of § 14.5(d) for any breach arising from the broadened Covered Facilities definition.

### Issue 4: Service Credits as Sole Remedy for Availability Failures During Recalls

**Where:** License Agreement § 5.6; Exhibit A § A.3 (service credit table); Quality Agreement § 8.2, § 8.4

**The problem.** Section 5.6 states that service credits are "Licensee's sole monetary remedy for Licensor's failure to meet the availability commitment," except for § 14.3 (termination for cause) and the chronic failure right (Exhibit A § A.4). The maximum service credit is 30% of the monthly Subscription Fee (~$12,150 per month). Meanwhile, the Quality Agreement (§ 8.2) correctly classifies a recall event as Severity 1, and § 8.4 obligates Tilil to provide extensive recall support. But if the platform is unavailable during a recall, Kunoux's monetary recovery is capped at a service credit.

The actual cost of a failed or delayed recall response—product destruction, regulatory penalties, organic certification revocation, consumer harm, brand damage—could be orders of magnitude greater than $12,150.

**Why it matters.** For an organic dairy processor, a recall failure is an existential risk. The current allocation means Kunoux bears virtually all the financial risk of platform unavailability during a recall, with Tilil's exposure limited to a modest service credit and the general liability cap (§ 12.2, which is 12 months of fees = $486,000).

**Recommended fix.** In the next amendment, add a carve-out from § 5.6 for availability failures that occur during a Recall Event or Regulatory Trace Request. For those events, service credits should not be the sole remedy, and the liability cap in § 12.2 should be raised (e.g., to 2x or 3x annual fees) for Tilil's failure to make the recall functions available. Alternatively, require Tilil to maintain recall-specific business continuity protections (e.g., a dedicated failover for the Trace and Recall module) and make failure to maintain those protections a separate, uncapped obligation.

### Issue 5: Uncapped Indemnity for Licensee Data in the DPA

**Where:** DPA § 8.1, § 8.2

**The problem.** Section 8.1 requires Licensee to indemnify Licensor against all losses arising from Licensee Data or Licensee's processing instructions, including claims by Data Subjects, regulators or third parties. Section 8.2 expressly states this indemnity "shall not be subject to any limitation of liability in the Agreement, including Section 12 of the License Agreement." This is uncapped.

The Licensee Data includes farm supplier records, driver GPS location data, consumer complaint records (potentially including health information), and pricing/premium data. If a data breach occurs and Kunoux is found to have provided inadequate instructions or unlawful data, its indemnity exposure to Tilil is unlimited.

**Why it matters.** The asymmetry is notable: Tilil's liability under the DPA is subject to the general cap (§ 8.3: "Licensor's liability arising out of or relating to this DPA is subject to Section 12 of the License Agreement"), while Kunoux's is not. Given the sensitivity of the data (organic certification records, driver location, consumer health information), a single serious incident could generate regulatory fines and class-action exposure well beyond the $486,000 cap.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate a mutual cap on the DPA indemnities. At minimum, cap Licensee's indemnity under DPA § 8.1 at the greater of (a) the general liability cap in § 12.2, or (b) the limit of Kunoux's cyber insurance coverage. If Tilil resists, seek to narrow the scope of § 8.1 to exclude claims arising from Licensor's own security failures or processing errors.

### Issue 6: Parent Guaranty Is Overbroad and Auto-Extends

**Where:** Parent Guaranty § 1.1 (Guaranteed Obligations), § 2.1, § 3.1(a), § 4.3; Amendment No. 1 § 6.2

**The problem.** Several features of the Parent Guaranty create significant risk for Kunoux Holdings, L.P.:

- **Scope:** "Guaranteed Obligations" includes "the performance of all other covenants and obligations of Licensee under the Agreement" (§ 1.1(f)). This means the parent guarantees not just payment but *performance*—including compliance with the exclusivity covenant, data protection obligations, and any other covenant. A breach of exclusivity (see Issue 2) would make the parent liable for damages.
- **Auto-extension:** § 3.1(a) provides the Guaranty is not affected by "any amendment, supplement, restatement, replacement, extension, renewal or waiver of or under the Agreement. This includes any additional Order Form, statement of work, change order or amendment that increases the Fees, extends the Term or adds facilities, modules, entitlements or services." Kunoux Holdings did not consent to any of this; it is automatic.
- **Subordination:** § 4.3 subordinates all intercompany indebtedness of Kunoux Creamery to Kunoux Holdings in favor of Tilil. This could restrict the parent's ability to manage cash flows within the group.
- **Amendment confirmation:** Amendment No. 1 § 6.2 has the Guarantor confirming the guaranty "extends to Licensee's obligations under the Agreement as amended by this Amendment," including the increased Implementation Fees ($334,500 vs. $312,000) and the revised expense cap ($44,000 vs. $38,000).

**Why it matters.** Kunoux Holdings is the entity being sold to Ostrander. The new owners will inherit this guaranty with its auto-extension feature. If they are not aware of the breadth (performance guaranty, not just payment), this could be a deal issue or a post-closing surprise.

**Recommended fix.** In connection with the Change of Control negotiation (Issue 1), seek to:
- Limit the Guaranty to payment obligations only (delete § 1.1(f) and the performance guaranty in § 2.1);
- Delete or narrow § 3.1(a) so that amendments increasing fees require Guarantor consent;
- Release the Guaranty upon consummation of the Change of Control (or replace it with a guaranty from the Ostrander entity, if Tilil requires one);
- Remove the subordination in § 4.3 or limit it to payment obligations.

If the Ostrander deal proceeds and Tilil requires a new credit support, negotiate a cap (e.g., 12 months of Subscription Fees) and a sunset (e.g., upon the first Renewal Term).

## Priority 3 – Moderate

### Issue 7: Order of Precedence Places Quality Agreement Below Order Form

**Where:** License Agreement § 16.4

**The problem.** The order of precedence is: DPA → License Agreement → Order Form → Quality Agreement → SOW. The Quality Agreement, which allocates food safety responsibilities, sets retention periods, and defines recall support obligations, has *lower* precedence than the Order Form. If a provision in the Order Form (e.g., a support tier description or a data retention reference) conflicts with the Quality Agreement, the Order Form controls.

**Why it matters.** The Quality Agreement is the document that protects Kunoux's regulatory and organic certification interests. Having it subordinate to the Order Form (which is primarily a commercial/pricing document) creates an unnecessary risk of conflict in a food-safety-critical context.

**Recommended fix.** Amend § 16.4 to place the Quality Agreement above the Order Form in the precedence order, at least as to matters of food safety, record retention, and recall support. A reasonable revised order: DPA → License Agreement → Quality Agreement → Order Form → SOW.

### Issue 8: No Protection Against Module Withdrawal

**Where:** Term Sheet § 4.3; License Agreement § 5.4, § 13.4(c)

**The problem.** The Term Sheet provided that exclusivity "will cease to apply to any module that Tilil withdraws or is unable to continue to provide" (§ 4.3). The License Agreement addresses this only in the IP-infringement context: § 13.4(c) releases § 2.6 if Tilil terminates affected services due to an infringement claim. There is no general provision for what happens if Tilil simply decides to discontinue a module for business reasons (e.g., sunsets the Organic Certification module or the Consumer Complaints module).

Section 5.4 prohibits updates that "materially reduce the core functionality" but does not address complete withdrawal of a subscribed module.

**Why it matters.** If Tilil withdraws a module that Kunoux depends for regulatory compliance (e.g., Organic Certification tracking), Kunoux would be locked into the exclusivity covenant for that function while having no platform to perform it. This is a particular risk for an organic processor.

**Recommended fix.** Add a provision (in the next amendment) stating that if Tilil withdraws or discontinues any subscribed module during the Term, (a) the exclusivity covenant in § 2.6 ceases to apply to the Traceability Functions performed by that module, (b) Tilil shall refund prepaid fees attributable to that module, and (c) Tilil shall provide a migration path and data export for the affected module.

### Issue 9: Deemed Acceptance Window Is Short

**Where:** SOW § 6.5; License Agreement § 4.5

**The problem.** Under SOW § 6.5, if Licensee neither signs the Acceptance Certificate nor gives notice of nonconformity within 10 Business Days, the deliverable is "deemed accepted." The same 10-Business-Day window applies under License Agreement § 4.5 for deliverables without a specific acceptance procedure.

**Why it matters.** During Go-Live and Hypercare periods, Kunoux's plant teams are under operational pressure. A 10-Business-Day window to review complex deliverables (e.g., the Solution Design Document, the Test Plan, or a cutover plan) is tight, especially if the review requires input from multiple departments (quality assurance, receiving, IT, laboratory). Missing the window means the deliverable is accepted and Kunoux loses leverage to require corrections.

**Recommended fix.** In the next SOW amendment or change order, extend the deemed acceptance period to 15 Business Days for major deliverables (Solution Design Document, Test Plan, cutover plans) and clarify that the period runs from delivery of a complete and final version (not a draft). Alternatively, add a provision that production use does not constitute acceptance of any deliverable other than the specific Go-Live milestone (the current § 6.5 last sentence partially addresses this but could be clearer).

### Issue 10: Escalation Contact Gap at Kunoux

**Where:** SOW § 7.5; Kickoff Minutes § 4.4

**The problem.** The Kickoff Minutes record that "Mr. Lindqvist holds both Level 1 and Level 2 for Kunoux." The minutes note that Kunoux "will consider whether to nominate a different Level 1 contact after Go-Live at the Eau Claire Plant," but no change has been documented in the folder. If a dispute arises with the project manager during implementation, there is no independent internal escalation path—the same person is both the party in dispute and the escalation contact.

**Why it matters.** In a Severity 1 support situation or a project dispute, the escalation process (SOW § 7.5) is designed to move the issue up the chain. If Level 1 and Level 2 are the same person, the escalation is circular and delays resolution.

**Recommended fix.** Kunoux should formally designate a different Level 1 contact (e.g., the receiving lead or IT integration lead for the relevant site) and provide written notice to Tilil's Project Manager under SOW § 7.5. This is a low-cost, immediate action that Kunoux can take unilaterally.

### Issue 11: Schedule Float Agreed at Kickoff Is Not Contractually Binding

**Where:** Kickoff Minutes § 5.3; SOW § 5.3

**The problem.** At the kickoff, Tilil's project manager "agreed to build two weeks of schedule float into Phase 1 between Milestone M3 and the start of user acceptance testing" to protect the August 17 Go-Live date. However, the minutes expressly state they "do not amend the SOW" (§ 2.3), and SOW § 5.3 provides that target dates "may be adjusted only through the change control procedure in Section 10." The float is therefore an internal project management commitment, not a contractual right.

**Why it matters.** If Phase 1 slips and the float is consumed, Kunoux has no contractual mechanism to require Tilil to protect the Go-Live date. The only remedy is the general material breach provision (§ 14.3(a)), which requires a 30-day cure period and is a high bar to invoke for a schedule delay.

**Recommended fix.** This is now moot for Phase 1 (Go-Live occurred August 17, 2026, per Amendment § 3.2(b)). For Phase 2, ensure the updated project plan (DEL-1, to be revised under Amendment § 7.4) explicitly documents any schedule float and the conditions under which it may be used. If Kunoux wants contractual protection for the January 18, 2027 Go-Live date, consider a change order that makes the date a hard milestone with specific consequences for delay (e.g., liquidated damages or a right to terminate the SOW).

### Issue 12: Order Form Schedule A Not Updated for Amendment Fees

**Where:** Order Form, Schedule A; Amendment No. 1 § 3.6

**The problem.** Order Form Schedule A states "Implementation Fees (Section 8.2): Fixed fee under Section 9.1 of the SOW, invoiced on Milestones – $312,000." Amendment No. 1 § 3.6 increased the total to $334,500 and added two new billing events (INT-6 deployment: $15,000; additional onboarding: $7,500). The Order Form was not amended to reflect this.

**Why it matters.** While the Amendment is effective as a matter of contract (it amends the SOW directly), the Order Form's Schedule A is now inaccurate. This could cause confusion in invoicing, audit, or if the Order Form is referenced in a dispute. It also means the Order Form's statement in § 8.2 ("As of the Order Form Date, the Implementation Fees are a fixed fee of $312,000") is outdated.

**Recommended fix.** In the next amendment (or as a housekeeping item), update Order Form § 8.2 and Schedule A to reflect the $334,500 total and the additional billing events. This is low-risk but should be done to maintain document consistency.

## Priority 4 – Monitoring / Lower

### Issue 13: Volume Entitlements May Be Tight

**Where:** Order Form § 5.2; Term Sheet § 6.5

**The problem.** The Term Sheet stated entitlements were "sized to Kunoux's 2025 volumes at the two plants, with headroom for approximately 20% growth." The raw milk entitlement is 1.6 billion pounds per Subscription Year. If Kunoux's volumes grow faster than 20% (e.g., due to new supplier onboarding, market growth, or the Ostrander investment enabling expansion), overage charges will apply at $0.00025 per pound. At 200 million pounds of overage, that's $50,000 per year.

**Why it matters.** The Ostrander transaction (Recital B to Amendment No. 1) suggests growth capital is coming. If Kunoux plans to expand volumes, it should proactively negotiate an entitlement increase before hitting overage, rather than reacting to an invoice.

**Recommended fix.** Monitor monthly usage reports (Exhibit A § A.8, as supplemented by Amendment § 2.4). If usage exceeds 80% of any volume entitlement for two consecutive months, initiate a good-faith discussion under License Agreement § 3.2 to amend the Order Form before overage is triggered.

### Issue 14: Press Release Timeline Is Now Outdated

**Where:** Press Release § 4, § 10 (Fact Sheet)

**The problem.** The press release (dated March 10, 2026) states Phase 2 Go-Live is "targeted for later in 2026." Amendment No. 1 (October 2, 2026) moved this to January 18, 2027. The Fact Sheet in the press release also states "Phase 2: Sheboygan plant; go-live targeted for later in 2026."

**Why it matters.** The press release was issued with Kunoux's approval (it states "This press release has been approved for release by Kunoux Creamery, Inc. and Tilil Systems, Inc."). If Kunoux's suppliers, customers, or regulators rely on the stated timeline, the delay could erode confidence. More practically, if the press release is referenced in a regulatory or organic certification context, the outdated timeline could create confusion.

**Recommended fix.** No contractual action required, but Kunoux's communications team should be aware that the public timeline is now inaccurate. If a correction is warranted, issue a brief update or ensure that any future public statements reference the amended timeline.

### Issue 15: Quality Agreement Retention Periods Are Minimums Only

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Schedule 2; § 4.3

**The problem.** Schedule 2 sets minimum retention periods (3 years for most records, 5 years for recall reports and organic certification records). Section 4.3 permits Licensee to "set longer retention periods for any category of Quality Record through the configuration of the Subscription Services." However, there is no obligation on Tilil to automatically extend retention if Applicable Food Laws change.

**Why it matters.** If FDA or the USDA National Organic Program extends record-keeping requirements (e.g., from 3 to 5 years for receiving records), Kunoux would need to reconfigure the system and confirm the change. If it fails to do so, records could be deleted at the end of the current retention period, creating a regulatory gap.

**Recommended fix.** Kunoux's Quality Representative should confirm at each annual review (Quality Agreement § 13.4) that the configured retention periods still meet current regulatory requirements. Document any configuration changes in the Solution Design Document. Consider adding a provision to the Quality Agreement requiring Tilil to notify Licensee 90 days before any Quality Record reaches the end of its configured retention period, so Kunoux has time to decide whether to extend.
