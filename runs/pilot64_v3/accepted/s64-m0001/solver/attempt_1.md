## Executive Summary

Review of all seven documents in the matter folder reveals several material issues, two of which are critical and require immediate attention. The most urgent is that Dribas's August 14, 2026 termination notice appears to have been delivered **15 days after** the contractual deadline, giving Brikon a colorable argument that the Termination Right was lost. The second critical issue is that the exclusivity covenant in the executed Agreement is **dramatically broader** than what was agreed in the Term Sheet, restricting Dribas's use of inventory, point-of-sale, and distribution software worldwide and binding its Affiliates—none of which was in the negotiated deal. Additionally, Amendment No. 1 was executed without the Board approval required by Dribas's own governance documents, and it imposes a new one-way indemnity on Dribas that was not contemplated in the Term Sheet.

Issues are listed below in order of priority.

## Issue 1 (CRITICAL): Termination Notice Likely Late – Termination Right May Be Lost

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital C and § 6.1

**Facts.** Side Letter § 2.1 sets the Acceptance Deadline for the Brewhouse Integration Module (BIM) at **June 30, 2026**. Side Letter § 2.2 provides that if the BIM has not been Accepted by that date, Dribas may terminate "by written notice to Licensor (a 'Termination Notice') delivered **no later than thirty (30) days after the Acceptance Deadline**." Thirty days after June 30, 2026 is **July 30, 2026**.

Amendment No. 1, Recital C, states that Dribas delivered its August Notice on **August 14, 2026**—**15 days after** the July 30 deadline. Brikon disputed the notice's effectiveness by letter dated August 28, 2026. Amendment § 6.1 records that Brikon "disputes that the August Notice validly exercised the Termination Right" and that "nothing in this Amendment resolves that dispute."

**Why it matters.** If Brikon's challenge succeeds, Dribas loses its right to walk away from the Agreement. Dribas would be locked in for the balance of the Initial Term (through **January 14, 2029**) and subject to auto-renewal for successive one-year terms (§ 12.1 of the Agreement). Critically, Dribas would remain bound by the overbroad exclusivity covenant (see Issue 2) and the new indemnity obligation in Amendment No. 1 (see Issue 4). The entire strategic rationale for the Side Letter—giving Dribas an exit if the BIM failed—would be defeated.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Immediately** assess whether any equitable argument exists (e.g., Brikon's course of performance, estoppel, or the argument that Brikon's entry into Amendment No. 1—negotiating a remediation plan rather than simply demanding performance—constitutes an acknowledgment that the termination was effective). Note, however, that Amendment § 6.2 expressly disclaims any waiver by Brikon, which weakens this argument.
- Prepare for the possibility that the Termination Right is lost. If so, Dribas's leverage shifts to: (a) the BIM Remediation Plan (Amendment § 5), which obligates Brikon to fix the BIM at its own cost; (b) the chronic failure termination right (Agreement § 5.5), if availability issues persist; and (c) the non-renewal right at the end of the Initial Term (90 days' notice by **October 16, 2028**).
- Refer the dispute to the escalation procedure under Agreement § 14.1 (permitted by Amendment § 6.4) and preserve all arguments. Do **not** take any action that could be construed as affirming the Agreement (e.g., do not voluntarily pay the LFC Module implementation milestone without reserving rights in writing).
- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (October 16, 2028) as a fallback exit point.

## Issue 2 (CRITICAL): Exclusivity Covenant Massively Expanded Beyond Term Sheet

**Where:** Agreement § 2.5; Schedule A.7; Term Sheet §§ 5.1–5.4

**Facts.** The Term Sheet agreed the following exclusivity scope:

| Element | Term Sheet (§§ 5.1–5.3) | Executed Agreement (§ 2.5) |
|---|---|---|
| Software types covered | "production-scheduling software" only | "brewery management, inventory, point-of-sale or distribution software" |
| Geographic scope | "Wilmington and Dover breweries only" | "anywhere in the world" |
| Affiliate binding | "The covenant will bind Dribas only, and not its Affiliates" (§ 5.2) | "Licensee shall…cause its Affiliates not to" |
| Excluded functions | "taproom point-of-sale, distributor and sales management, accounting, and raw material and finished goods inventory systems" (§ 5.2) | None expressly excluded |
| System of record | Not mentioned | "Licensee shall use the Licensed Software as its system of record for the functions it supports" |
| Transition period | "until the Acceptance of the Production Scheduling Module at each brewery" (§ 5.3) | None in § 2.5 |

Schedule A.7 confirms the 20% pricing discount was given "in consideration of the covenant in Section 2.5," tying the commercial deal to this expanded restriction.

**Why it matters.** This is the single most commercially significant deviation from the negotiated terms. As drafted, § 2.5 would prohibit Dribas from:

- Using any other **inventory management** software (e.g., for raw materials such as hops, grain, or yeast, or for finished goods in warehouses not at the two licensed Sites);
- Using any other **point-of-sale** system (the Term Sheet explicitly carved out "taproom point-of-sale");
- Using any other **distribution** software (the Term Sheet explicitly carved out "distributor and sales management");
- Using any **brewery management** software at any location worldwide (not just the two licensed Sites);
- Permitting any **Affiliate** to use alternative software (the Term Sheet excluded Affiliates).

For a brewing company that may expand, open new taprooms, or acquire other breweries, this covenant is a severe operational constraint. The "system of record" language further complicates any fallback to legacy or alternative systems during outages.

**Recommended fix.**

- If the termination dispute is resolved in Dribas's favor, this issue becomes moot.
- If Dribas remains in the Agreement, **negotiate a restatement of § 2.5** to conform to the Term Sheet scope: limited to production-scheduling software at the two licensed Sites, not binding Affiliates, with express carve-outs for taproom POS, distributor/sales management, accounting, and raw material/finished goods inventory systems. Add the transition period from Term Sheet § 5.3.
- In the alternative, if Brikon will not narrow the covenant, seek a corresponding price reduction (the 20% discount was justified by the narrower covenant per the Term Sheet).
- Document this discrepancy now as evidence of Brikon's departure from the agreed commercial terms, which supports any argument that Brikon should not be permitted to enforce the expanded covenant (unconscionability, failure of consideration, or rectification).

## Issue 3 (HIGH): Amendment No. 1 Not Approved by the Board

**Where:** Dribas Board Consent §§ 9.3(c), 2.1–4.2; Amendment No. 1 (entire document)

**Facts.** The Board Consent (January 12, 2026) approves only the three original Transaction Documents: the Software License Agreement, the Side Letter, and the DPA (Exhibit A). Section 9.3(c) provides that "any amendment of the License Agreement, the Side Letter or the DPA that **increases the Company's financial commitment**, extends the Term or **limits the Company's rights**" requires **prior approval of the Board**.

Amendment No. 1 (September 15, 2026):
- **Increases financial commitment**: adds $18,000/year in LFC Module subscription fees and $12,000 in implementation fees (Amendment §§ 4.1, 4.3);
- **Limits the Company's rights**: replaces the entire indemnification section with a new one-way Licensee indemnity (Amendment § 3, restated § 10.1);
- Could be argued to **extend the Term** by keeping the Agreement alive during the disputed termination period.

There is no evidence in the folder of a subsequent Board consent or resolution approving Amendment No. 1.

**Why it matters.** If the Amendment was not properly authorized under Dribas's LLC Agreement and the Board Consent, it may be voidable at Dribas's election. This could be used strategically: Dribas could disaffirm the Amendment to (a) remove the LFC Module commitment, (b) remove the new Licensee indemnity, and (c) potentially argue that Brikon's reliance on the Amendment (e.g., its BIM Remediation Plan obligations) is undermined. However, disaffirming the Amendment also removes Dribas's benefit of the BIM Remediation Plan (Amendment § 5), which is valuable if Dribas intends to stay in the Agreement.

**Recommended fix.**

- Determine whether the CEO had authority under the "carve-out" in Board Consent § 9.3 (last sentence): "where the Chief Executive Officer determines that a notice must be given before the Board can reasonably act, the Chief Executive Officer may give the notice after consulting the Company's counsel and at least one other member of the Board." If Elena Dribas consulted counsel and a Board member before executing the Amendment, the authorization issue is cured. Obtain written confirmation of that consultation.
- If no such consultation occurred, consider whether to (a) ratify the Amendment by Board resolution (if Dribas wants to keep the BIM Remediation Plan benefits) or (b) disaffirm it (if Dribas wants to shed the LFC Module and new indemnity). The decision should be made in light of the outcome of the termination dispute (Issue 1).
- In any event, ensure all future amendments are submitted to the Board before execution, per § 9.3(c).

## Issue 4 (HIGH): New One-Way Licensee Indemnity in Amendment No. 1

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 3 (restated Agreement § 10.1); Term Sheet § 10 (no corresponding provision)

**Facts.** The Term Sheet (§ 10) contemplated only a **Licensor-to-Licensee** IP infringement indemnity. The executed Agreement (§ 10.1) contained only that indemnity. Amendment No. 1 § 3 **deletes and replaces** the entire indemnification section, adding a new **Licensee-to-Licensor** indemnity (restated § 10.1) under which Dribas must defend and indemnify Brikon against any "Licensee-Data Claim" to the extent it arises from:

(a) any label, artwork, formula, statement of composition or other Regulatory Submission Data submitted by Dribas;
(b) any filing Dribas makes to a regulatory authority using output of the Licensed Software; or
(c) **Dribas's failure to comply with laws governing the production, labeling, advertising, distribution or sale of alcoholic beverages.**

**Why it matters.** Sub-clause (c) is particularly broad. It requires Dribas to indemnify Brikon if Brikon is sued or fined by a third party or regulator because of Dribas's own regulatory non-compliance. For example, if Dribas ships a product with a non-compliant label and the TTB fines Brikon (as the software provider that generated the label), Dribas would owe Brikon that fine plus Brikon's defense costs. While restated § 10.3 excludes claims arising from "an Error in the Licensed Software" or "Licensor's breach of this Agreement," the practical burden would be on Dribas to prove the software error caused the regulatory issue.

This indemnity was not in the Term Sheet, was not flagged in the Board Consent, and represents a significant new financial exposure.

**Recommended fix.**

- If Dribas disaffirms the Amendment (see Issue 3), this indemnity falls away.
- If Dribas ratifies or retains the Amendment, **negotiate to narrow restated § 10.1(c)** to cover only claims arising from Dribas's intentional or grossly negligent regulatory violations, not mere compliance failures. Alternatively, cap Dribas's indemnity obligation at a stated amount.
- Ensure Dribas's regulatory compliance team is aware of this indemnity and that all label and formula submissions are reviewed before being entered into the LFC Module.

## Issue 5 (HIGH): DPA Governed by New York Law – Inconsistent with All Other Documents

**Where:** DPA §§ 12.1–12.2; Agreement § 15.1; Side Letter § 9.5; Amendment § 9.4; Term Sheet § 13.1

**Facts.** The Term Sheet (§ 13.1) provided that "the definitive documentation will be governed by the laws of the State of Delaware" with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County. The Agreement (§ 15.1), Side Letter (§ 9.5), and Amendment (§ 9.4) all use Delaware law and New Castle County forum.

The DPA, however, provides for **New York** governing law (§ 12.1) and exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County** courts (§ 12.2). There is no explanation for this deviation in any of the documents.

**Why it matters.** If a data protection dispute arises (e.g., a Personal Data Breach, a subprocessor objection, or a data subject request issue), Dribas would need to litigate in New York under New York law, while any related contractual dispute (e.g., whether Brikon breached its security obligations under Agreement § 7.4) would be in Delaware. This creates:

- Practical complexity and increased cost (two forums, two bodies of law);
- Risk of inconsistent outcomes (e.g., a New York court finds no DPA breach while a Delaware court finds a breach of Agreement § 7.4);
- Potential strategic disadvantage if Brikon is more comfortable litigating in New York.

**Recommended fix.**

- Raise this with Brikon as a drafting error or oversight. Request an amendment to conform the DPA's governing law and forum to Delaware / New Castle County, consistent with the rest of the transaction documents and the Term Sheet.
- If Brikon resists (e.g., because the DPA is a form document used across its customer base), at minimum ensure that the DPA's escalation provision (§ 12.4, which incorporates Agreement § 14.1) is used to resolve disputes before litigation, reducing the practical impact of the split forum.

## Issue 6 (MEDIUM): No Transition Period for Exclusivity in Final Agreement

**Where:** Agreement § 2.5; Term Sheet § 5.3; Side Letter § 4.6

**Facts.** Term Sheet § 5.3 provided: "Dribas will have until the Acceptance of the Production Scheduling Module at each brewery to retire its existing production-scheduling spreadsheets at that brewery. Parallel use during implementation and acceptance testing will not breach the exclusivity covenant."

The final Agreement § 2.5 contains no such transition language. It simply requires Dribas not to use other covered software and to use BrewLedger as its "system of record."

Side Letter § 4.6 provides partial protection: Brikon "shall not require Licensee to decommission its legacy production database or spreadsheet systems at that Site **as a condition of performing any Implementation Services**" and "shall support Licensee's parallel operation." However, this protects parallel operation only in the context of Implementation Services—it does not expressly state that parallel use will not **breach** the exclusivity covenant.

The Board Consent (§ 7.1) directs management to keep legacy systems operational until BIM Acceptance, creating an internal governance expectation that may conflict with Agreement § 2.5 if Brikon takes a strict view.

**Why it matters.** If Brikon argues that Dribas's continued use of legacy spreadsheets (as directed by the Board) violates § 2.5, Dribas could be in material breach, giving Brikon a termination right under § 12.2 or a defense against any claim Dribas brings.

**Recommended fix.**

- Seek a clarifying amendment or side letter provision stating that parallel operation of legacy systems during the implementation and remediation period does not constitute a breach of § 2.5.
- In the interim, ensure that any continued use of legacy systems is documented as being for "regulatory recordkeeping continuity" (which Side Letter § 4.6 expressly contemplates) rather than as an alternative production-scheduling system.
- If the exclusivity covenant is narrowed per Issue 2, this issue resolves itself.

## Issue 7 (MEDIUM): Liability Cap May Be Inadequate for Operational Risk

**Where:** Agreement §§ 11.1–11.2; Schedule A.4

**Facts.** The liability cap is "the Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim" (§ 11.2). With the base subscription of $96,000 (now $114,000 including the LFC Module), the cap is approximately **$96,000–$114,000**. Consequential damages (lost profits, business opportunity) are excluded (§ 11.1).

The exceptions (§ 11.3) carve out: IP indemnity, confidentiality/data security breaches, gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud, payment obligations, and IP infringement. A simple failure of the software to perform (e.g., a bug that corrupts production schedules, causing a brewing halt) would be subject to the cap and the consequential damages exclusion.

**Why it matters.** A brewing operation that loses production scheduling or tank management data could face significant operational disruption (wasted ingredients, missed shipping windows, regulatory filing delays). The cap may not cover Dribas's actual losses. The consequential damages exclusion means Dribas cannot recover lost profits from a production halt caused by a software Error (short of gross negligence).

**Recommended fix.**

- This is a standard SaaS limitation and difficult to change at this stage. However, if Dribas negotiates any amendment (e.g., to fix the exclusivity covenant), consider requesting:
  - A super-cap (e.g., 2x or 3x annual fees) for breaches of the availability SLA or data integrity obligations;
  - An express carve-out from the consequential damages exclusion for losses arising from corruption or loss of Customer Data (as distinct from a Security Incident).
- Ensure Dribas's own cyber/operational risk insurance covers the gap (Board Consent § 8.5 directs the Controller to review this).

## Issue 8 (MEDIUM): Deemed Acceptance Risk Under § 4.3(c)

**Where:** Agreement § 4.3(c); Board Consent § 6.3

**Facts.** A Module is deemed "Accepted" if "Licensee uses the Module in live production at any Site for a continuous period of thirty (30) days other than for testing purposes." This is automatic—no notice or election is required.

The Board Consent (§ 6.3) requires that "no notice confirming that a Module meets the Acceptance Criteria shall be given on behalf of the Company unless it has been approved in writing by the project manager and by the Chief Executive Officer." However, this internal control addresses only **affirmative** acceptance notices. It does not prevent **deemed** acceptance under § 4.3(c), which occurs by operation of use.

**Why it matters.** If Dribas's brewers start using the Production Scheduling Module or Tank Management Module in live production for 30 continuous days (perhaps out of operational necessity while the BIM is still in remediation), those modules become Accepted. Once Accepted, Dribas loses its right to terminate for failure to meet Acceptance Criteria under § 4.4. This could lock Dribas into modules that are not fully functional.

**Recommended fix.**

- Brief all site leads and brewers that **live production use** of any module starts the 30-day deemed acceptance clock. Use of modules should be limited to the test environment (Side Letter § 4.5) until Dribas is ready to accept.
- If live production use is operationally necessary before formal Acceptance, document it as "testing" or "pilot" use and ensure it does not constitute continuous production use for 30 days without a deliberate decision to accept.
- Add this to the calendar of key dates maintained by the project manager (Board Consent § 6.5).

## Issue 11 (LOW): DPA "Customer Data" Definition Narrower Than Agreement

**Where:** DPA § 1.1; Agreement § 1.4

**Facts.** In the DPA, "Customer Data" means only "Personal Data that Licensor Processes on behalf of Licensee" (i.e., data relating to identified or identifiable natural persons). In the Agreement, "Customer Data" means "all data Licensee submits, including recipes, formulations, production records and distributor sales data" (much broader).

**Why it matters.** The DPA's specific protections (data subject rights, Personal Data Breach notification, subprocessor restrictions, audit rights) apply only to the Personal Data subset. Dribas's recipes, production records, and compliance data are protected by the Agreement's general confidentiality (§ 8) and data security (§ 7.4) provisions, but not by the DPA's data-protection-law-specific obligations. This is not necessarily a problem (the Agreement protections are robust), but Dribas should be aware that a corruption of production records that does not involve Personal Data would be a "Security Incident" under Agreement § 7.5 but **not** a "Personal Data Breach" under the DPA, and would not trigger DPA § 9.5 (Brikon's obligation to reimburse notification and credit monitoring costs).

**Recommended fix.**

- No immediate action required. Be aware of the definitional distinction when assessing any data incident. If an incident involves both Personal Data and non-Personal Customer Data, ensure the response addresses both the DPA and Agreement § 7.5 obligations.

## Issue 12 (LOW): Brikon's Publicity Right

**Where:** Agreement § 15.12

**Facts.** Brikon "may identify Licensee as a customer in a non-public list provided to prospective investors or acquirers under confidentiality obligations."

**Why it matters.** This permits Brikon to disclose Dribas's name (and by implication, the existence of the relationship and possibly the scope of the license) to potential buyers of Brikon. While limited to "non-public" lists under confidentiality, this could be relevant if Dribas is concerned about competitive intelligence (Brikon's acquirer might learn about Dribas's systems, volumes, and compliance posture from the relationship).

**Recommended fix.**

- Low risk given the confidentiality condition. No action needed unless Dribas has a specific competitive concern. If so, request that § 15.12 be amended to require Dribas's prior written consent for any such disclosure.
