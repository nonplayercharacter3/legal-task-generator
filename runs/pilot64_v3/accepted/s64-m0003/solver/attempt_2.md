### 1. Praitir Has Likely Missed the Deadline to Dispute the Closing Inventory Value

**Documents:** APA §2.6(b)–(c); Board Consent Recital C, Resolution 2

The Closing Statement was delivered by Glailirt on **March 30, 2026** (Board Consent, Recital C). Section 2.6(b) of the APA gives Buyer **thirty (30) days** after delivery to serve a Notice of Disagreement. That deadline was **April 29, 2026**.

The Board Consent authorizing the Notice of Disagreement is dated **May 12, 2026** — thirteen days after the deadline expired. Resolution 2.2 directs the Authorized Officers to "prepare, execute and deliver" the notice, which implies it has not yet been sent.

Section 2.6(c) provides that if Buyer does not deliver a timely Notice of Disagreement, "the Closing Statement and the Closing Inventory Value set forth in it shall be **final and binding** on the Parties."

**Why it matters:** Glailirt's Closing Statement values inventory at $1,326,450, yielding a $111,650 payment *from* Praitir *to* Glailirt under §2.6(e). Praitir's own analysis (Board Consent, Recital D; Exhibit B) supports a value of $1,048,900, which would mean Glailirt owes Praitir approximately $165,900. The swing is **$277,550**. If the Review Period has lapsed, Praitir is contractually locked into the higher number.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm immediately whether a Notice of Disagreement was in fact delivered on or before April 29, 2026 (check the file for any earlier draft, email, or courier receipt). If it was, the Board Consent is a ratification and there is no problem.
- If it was not delivered, assess whether Glailirt's own conduct (e.g., continued negotiation, acceptance of Praitir's count data, or course of performance) could support a waiver or estoppel argument under §2.6(b). This is a weak position but worth evaluating before we communicate with the other side.
- In any event, do **not** make the $111,650 payment under §2.6(e) until the issue is resolved. Resolution 2.4 already directs the officers not to pay, which is correct.
- If the deadline was missed, consider whether the dispute can be reframed as a claim for indemnification under §9.2(a) (inaccuracy in Seller's Closing Statement preparation) or §4.10 (Inventory representation), though the survival and cap limitations (see Issue 4 below) would apply.

### 2. The Copperline Sale Will Trigger the TSA Change-of-Control Termination Right

**Documents:** TSA §12.2; Board Consent Recital E, Resolution 4, Exhibit A

TSA §12.2 provides that "any change of control of Buyer shall be deemed an assignment" and that "if any assignment or deemed assignment occurs without Seller's prior written consent, **Seller may terminate this Agreement upon ten (10) days' notice** to Buyer."

The Copperline Sale is expected to close **on or about July 15, 2026** (Board Consent, Recital E). The Transition Period runs **180 days from March 2, 2026**, i.e., through **August 29, 2026** (TSA §1.1). The change of control will therefore occur roughly six weeks before the TSA would otherwise expire.

Exhibit A to the Board Consent lists only two Required Consents: (1) Hudson Commerce Bank / Required Lenders under the Credit Agreement, and (2) Tidewater under the Equipment Lease. **Glailirt's consent under TSA §12.2 is not listed.**

**Why it matters:** If Glailirt terminates the TSA upon the Copperline closing, Praitir immediately loses:
- Production planning and scheduling (Service A-1)
- Quality assurance and food safety support (Service A-2)
- Procurement under unassigned supply contracts (Service A-3)
- Logistics and distribution (Service A-4)
- **Access to the ERP, order management, warehouse management, and route accounting systems** (Service A-5)
- Payroll processing (Service A-6, though this is only 90 days and would have ended June 1)
- Finance and accounting support (Service A-7)

Service A-5 is the linchpin: Schedule A to the TSA states that A-5 "supports Services A-1, A-3, A-4 and A-7 and may not be terminated under Section 5.2 while any of those Services remains in effect." Praitir does not own these systems (Bill of Sale, Schedule 1, Part 5: "Seller's enterprise resource planning, order management, warehouse management, route accounting and payroll systems…are **not** Conveyed Property"). Loss of access would disrupt order intake, production scheduling, and customer deliveries.

Additionally, the NY State Department of Agriculture and Markets letter (Email Thread, Message 13) permits Praitir to operate under Seller's existing food processing license only "provided the licensed operation **continues uninterrupted**." A TSA termination that disrupts production could jeopardize this arrangement.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add Glailirt's written consent under TSA §12.2 to the Required Consents in Exhibit A to the Board Consent, or obtain a separate board authorization.
- Request Glailirt's consent now, well before the July 15 target. Frame it as a condition to the Copperline Sale that Praitir needs to satisfy.
- In the alternative, negotiate a TSA amendment or side letter before the Copperline closing that (a) waives the change-of-control termination right, (b) extends the Transition Period to a date after Praitir has replaced all services, or (c) provides a wind-down period with reduced fees.
- Accelerate the replacement of Service A-5 (ERP/IT) and Services A-1, A-3, A-4, A-7. Resolution 5.3 authorizes agreements with third-party providers up to $350,000 first-year cost. Given the July 15 closing date, any replacement must be substantially in place by then.
- Confirm with the Ag & Markets Department whether the "uninterrupted operation" condition is satisfied if there is a brief transition gap in QA support.

## OTHER ISSUES

### 3. TSA §4.3 Occupancy Costs Reference "Facilities" (Including the Excluded Secaucus Depot)

**Documents:** TSA §4.3; APA §1.1 ("Facilities"); APA §2.2(d) (Secaucus Depot as Excluded Asset)

TSA §4.3 requires Buyer to reimburse Seller for "all rent, utilities…real estate taxes, insurance premiums allocable to the premises, and repair and maintenance costs of the **Facilities**." The APA defines "Facilities" as "the Bronx Plant **and the Secaucus Depot**" (APA §1.1). The Secaucus Depot is an Excluded Asset (APA §2.2(d)); Buyer does not own or use it.

For the Bronx Plant, Buyer acquired fee simple title at Closing (APA §2.1(a); Bill of Sale, Recital D). Seller is not paying rent or real estate taxes on a property it no longer owns, so the "to the extent paid or incurred by Seller" qualifier should limit practical exposure. But for the Secaucus Depot, Seller is the tenant and would be incurring rent and operating costs. The question is whether Buyer should be reimbursing Seller for the full occupancy of the Secaucus Depot or only the portion attributable to Seller's use of that facility in providing Services to Buyer (e.g., logistics staging under Service A-4).

**Why it matters:** Ambiguity in the occupancy cost reimbursement could lead to disputed invoices during the Transition Period, particularly if Seller bills for 100% of Secaucus Depot costs rather than an allocable portion.

**Recommended fix:** In the next invoice review or as part of the Glailirt consent negotiation (Issue 2), clarify that §4.3 occupancy costs are limited to (a) the Bronx Plant only to the extent Seller actually incurs and pays costs (e.g., if utilities remain in Seller's name), and (b) the Secaucus Depot only to the extent allocable to the provision of Services. Consider a written side letter or invoice dispute under TSA §4.5 if Seller bills for the full Secaucus Depot.

### 4. Survival Period for Non-Fundamental Representations Is Short

**Documents:** APA §9.1; TSA §1.1 ("Transition Period")

Section 9.1 provides that all representations and warranties survive until "sixty (60) days after the expiration of the Transition Period." The Transition Period is 180 days from March 2, 2026, expiring **August 29, 2026**. Add 60 days and the survival period ends approximately **October 28, 2026** — roughly eight months after Closing.

**Why it matters:** For a food manufacturing plant with ammonia refrigeration, combustible dust handling, industrial wastewater discharge, and FDA-regulated production, latent problems (equipment defects, environmental contamination, food safety non-compliance) may not manifest within eight months. Any claim must be "asserted in good faith with reasonable specificity and in writing" before the survival period expires (APA §9.1, second sentence).

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the October 28, 2026 deadline (and the earlier August 29, 2026 Transition Period end date) immediately.
- Conduct a thorough post-closing inspection of the ammonia refrigeration system, dust collection system, grease interceptor, and wastewater pretreatment equipment before the survival period ends, and document any deficiencies.
- If any issues are found, assert claims in writing before the deadline to preserve them under the "claim asserted before expiration" proviso in §9.1.
- If the Glailirt consent negotiation (Issue 2) is ongoing, consider requesting an extension of the survival period for environmental and food safety representations as part of the package.

### 5. Indemnification Cap Is Low and Environmental/Food Safety Reps Are Not Fundamental

**Documents:** APA §9.5(a)–(c); APA §1.1 ("Fundamental Representations"); APA §4.16, §4.19

The indemnification cap is **$1,840,000** (10% of the Base Purchase Price) (APA §9.5(b)). The deductible is **$184,000** (1%) (APA §9.5(a)). Only four representations are "Fundamental" (exempt from both the deductible and the cap): §4.1 (Organization), §4.2 (Authority), §4.7 (Title), and §4.20 (Brokers) (APA §1.1).

This means claims based on:
- Environmental compliance (§4.16)
- Food safety and regulatory compliance (§4.19)
- Financial statements (§4.4)
- Undisclosed liabilities (§4.5)
- Condition of assets (§4.8)

are all subject to the $184,000 deductible and the $1,840,000 cap.

**Why it matters:** A single significant environmental remediation (e.g., ammonia leak, contaminated groundwater) or a major food safety recall could easily exceed $1.84 million. The cap leaves Praitir bearing the excess.

**Recommended fix:**
- This is a negotiated term that is now fixed. The practical mitigation is to (a) ensure the insurance program in place post-closing covers environmental and product liability risks (confirm with the broker that the policies are bound and adequate), and (b) preserve indemnification claims for any pre-closing condition that surfaces within the survival period.
- If the Glailirt consent negotiation (Issue 2) opens a dialogue, consider requesting that the cap be increased for environmental and food safety claims, or that §4.16 and §4.19 be added to the Fundamental Representations. This is an uphill ask post-closing but worth exploring if Glailirt needs Praitir's cooperation for the consent.

### 6. Capital Expenditures Suggest Possible Inaccuracy in Seller's Environmental Representations

**Documents:** APA §4.16(c)–(d); Board Consent Resolution 6; Bill of Sale Schedule 1, Part 2

APA §4.16(c) represents that there are no "grease interceptors **in disrepair**" at the Facilities. APA §4.16(d) represents that Seller "has **implemented the recommendations**" of its NFPA 652/61 dust hazard analysis and has not received any OSHA or fire code citation relating to combustible flour dust.

Board Consent Resolution 6 approves capital expenditures for:
- (a) "the upgrade of the dust collection system serving the flour silos and mixing room, in accordance with the recommendations of the Company's combustible dust consultant"; and
- (b) "the replacement of the grease interceptor serving the laminating and sanitation areas."

The Bill of Sale Schedule 1, Part 2 lists both the "Dust collection system" (GAB-0216) and the "Grease interceptor and pretreatment equipment" (GAB-0230) as Conveyed Property.

**Why it matters:** If the grease interceptor was "in disrepair" at Closing, §4.16(c) was inaccurate. If the dust collection system did not reflect implemented recommendations, §4.16(d) was inaccurate. These would give rise to indemnification claims under §9.2(a). However, such claims are subject to the $184,000 deductible and $1,840,000 cap (see Issue 5), and must be asserted within the survival period (see Issue 4).

**Recommended fix:**
- Determine from the combustible dust consultant's report and the grease interceptor inspection whether the deficiencies existed **before** the Closing Date or arose after. If pre-closing, this is a potential indemnification claim.
- If the deficiencies are pre-closing, document them now and assert a claim under §9.2(a) before the survival period expires. The cost of the capital expenditures (up to $920,000 per Resolution 6.1) could support the damages calculation.
- Note that the §9.5(d) materiality scrape means the claim is evaluated without regard to the "material" qualifier in §4.16, which helps Praitir.

### 7. TSA Key Personnel: No Remedy for Reassignment

**Documents:** TSA §6.2; TSA Schedule A ("Key Personnel")

TSA §6.2 requires Seller to use "commercially reasonable efforts to make available the individuals identified for each Service in Schedule A" and provides that Seller "shall not reassign any of those individuals away from the Services without **reasonable prior notice** to Buyer." The Key Personnel are: plant operations director (A-1), quality assurance manager (A-2), and director of logistics (A-4).

There is no fee reduction, termination right, or other remedy if Seller reassigns a key person after giving notice. The only recourse is the general material breach termination in §5.3, which requires a 30-day cure period.

**Why it matters:** The quality assurance manager and plant operations director are critical for food safety compliance during the transition. If they are reassigned to Seller's remaining retail bakery-café business (which is carved out of the non-compete in APA §6.7(a)), Praitir could lose institutional knowledge at a vulnerable time.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the Service Coordinator meetings (TSA §6.1, biweekly), confirm the continued assignment of Key Personnel.
- If any Key Person is reassigned, document the impact on service quality and reserve the right to treat it as a material breach under §5.3 if performance degrades.
- As part of the Glailirt consent negotiation (Issue 2), request a contractual commitment that Key Personnel will remain assigned through the end of the Transition Period (or any extension).

### 8. Praitir Does Not Own Its Core IT Systems

**Documents:** Bill of Sale Schedule 1, Part 5; TSA Schedule A, Service A-5; Board Consent Resolution 5.3

The Bill of Sale expressly excludes Seller's ERP, order management, warehouse management, route accounting, and payroll systems from the Conveyed Property. Praitir's access to these systems is solely through Service A-5 under the TSA. When the TSA ends (August 29, 2026, or earlier if terminated under Issue 2), Praitir loses access.

Resolution 5.3 authorizes the officers to negotiate replacement agreements with a $350,000 first-year cap. However, the TSA dependency structure (A-5 supports A-1, A-3, A-4, A-7) means that a full migration is complex and likely cannot be completed in the six weeks between the expected Copperline closing (July 15) and the TSA expiration (August 29) — or sooner if the TSA is terminated on 10 days' notice.

**Why it matters:** Operational continuity risk. Without the ERP and route accounting systems, Praitir cannot process customer orders, schedule production, manage inventory, or run delivery routes.

**Recommended fix:**
- Accelerate the IT migration plan. The board has already authorized replacement agreements. Identify the target systems and confirm implementation timelines.
- As part of the Glailirt consent negotiation, request that the TSA be extended (or that a post-termination data access and transition assistance period be added) to bridge the gap until Praitir's replacement systems are live.
- Ensure that the TSA §2.5 migration obligations (knowledge transfer, documentation, data extracts in industry-standard format) are being fulfilled now, not at the end of the Transition Period.

The NY State Department of Agriculture and Markets issued a letter confirming that Praitir "may continue operations at the Bronx Plant under Seller's existing license pending issuance of Praitir's license, **provided the licensed operation continues uninterrupted**."

**Why it matters:** If the TSA is terminated (Issue 2) or if a food safety incident occurs during the transition, and either event causes a production interruption, Praitir could lose the authority to operate the plant. This would be an existential business risk.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the Ag & Markets Department (or through counsel) what constitutes an "interruption" — is a one-day shutdown for equipment repair a violation? What is the maximum permissible downtime?
- Ensure the food safety plan and environmental monitoring program (which Praitir now controls) are robust enough to prevent any inspection finding that could trigger a shutdown order.
- Factor this risk into the Glailirt consent negotiation: Praitir has a strong practical need for the TSA to continue, which is leverage to obtain consent.

### 10. Buyer Assumes All WARN Act Liabilities, Including Pre-Closing

**Documents:** APA §6.8(b)

Section 6.8(b) provides that "Buyer shall be responsible for, and shall pay, perform and discharge when due, all Liabilities…under the WARN Act, in each case relating to the Business Employees, **whether arising before, on or after the Closing Date**."

**Why it matters:** If Seller failed to provide adequate WARN notices for any pre-closing layoff or plant closure (e.g., if any Business Employees were terminated between the signing and closing without proper notice), Praitir would bear the back-pay and benefits exposure. The APA §4.17(c) representation states Seller has not implemented any plant closing or mass layoff within 90 days before the Agreement date, but this is subject to the survival period and cap.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with HR that no Business Employees were terminated or had hours reduced between February 9, 2026 (signing) and March 2, 2026 (closing) in a manner that could trigger WARN.
- If any such event occurred, assess the exposure and preserve a claim under §9.2(a) (breach of §4.17(c)) within the survival period.

### 12. TSA §10.2 Liability Cap Is Limited to Service Fees

**Documents:** TSA §10.2

"The aggregate liability of Seller under this Agreement shall not exceed the **total Service Fees paid and payable** under this Agreement." Total Service Fees = $142,000/month × 6 months = $852,000, plus Pass-Through Costs and §4.3 Occupancy Costs (amount unknown but potentially significant given the Bronx Plant utilities and the Secaucus Depot rent).

The cap does not apply to breaches of §7 (Systems Access and Data), §8 (Confidentiality), §11 (Indemnification), or fraud/gross negligence/willful misconduct.

**Why it matters:** If Seller's personnel cause a food safety incident during the transition (e.g., a sanitation failure leading to a pathogen contamination), Praitir's recovery under the TSA is capped at the Service Fees. The APA indemnification (§9.2) might also apply, but it is subject to its own cap ($1.84M) and survival period.

**Recommended fix:**
- Ensure Praitir's own food safety plan and environmental monitoring program (now Praitir's responsibility post-closing per APA §2.1(k)) are independently robust and do not depend solely on Seller's QA personnel.
- Confirm that Praitir's product liability and food recall insurance (which should be in place post-closing per APA §6.1(f) and the Board Consent Resolution 5.1) covers incidents during the transition period.
