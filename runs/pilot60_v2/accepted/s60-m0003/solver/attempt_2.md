## Executive Summary

Napais has given notice of non-renewal of its Software License Agreement (the "SLA") with Baivusk, effective at the end of the Initial Term on February 28, 2026. However, three interlocking problems threaten to derail the exit and complicate Kelderhorn's post-closing technology integration:

1. The non-renewal notice appears to be **14 days late**, which likely triggers an automatic 24-month renewal.
2. The acquisition itself is a **change of control** that requires Baivusk's prior written consent under the SLA; without it, Baivusk can terminate immediately.
3. The Side Letter imposes a **12-month post-termination exclusivity tail** (potentially 36 months if the renewal is triggered) that will block Kelderhorn's planned system alignment.

These issues are addressed in detail below, followed by secondary matters.

---

## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Likely Late

**Where it appears:** SLA §12.2 (Renewal); Napais Letter to Baivusk, Nov. 14, 2025, §2.1–2.2; SLA §18.4 (Notices).

**The problem.** Section 12.2 provides that the Agreement will not renew only if a party gives written notice of non-renewal "at least 120 days before the end of the then-current term." The Initial Term is 36 months from March 1, 2023, ending February 28, 2026. Counting back 120 days from February 28, 2026 lands on **October 31, 2025**. Napais's notice is dated November 14, 2025 and was sent via overnight courier and certified mail. Under §18.4, a notice is "effective on receipt," so the operative date is no earlier than November 15, 2025 — approximately **105 days** before the term end. The notice is 14–15 days short of the contractual deadline.

**Why it matters.** If the notice is ineffective, the SLA renews automatically for a 24-month Renewal Term (through approximately February 28, 2028). Consequences:

- Napais (and post-closing, Kelderhorn) is locked into $684,000/year in subscription fees (Order Form No. 001, Part C.1) plus any fee increase of up to 5% under SLA §5.5.
- The Side Letter exclusivity obligation (§2.1) runs for the full Term *plus* 12 months after, meaning a potential exclusivity restriction through **February 2029**.
- The 90-day transition assistance under SLA §13.4 becomes unavailable until the renewed term ends (or Napais terminates for cause).

**Recommended fix.**

- **Immediately** send a supplemental or clarifying notice to Baivusk acknowledging the timing issue and requesting that Baivusk confirm in writing that it accepts the November 14 notice as effective, or that it waives the 120-day requirement. Frame it as a course-of-dealing or waiver argument: Napais has acted consistently with its intent not to renew, and Baivusk has had actual notice since mid-November.
- If Baivusk refuses, evaluate whether the late notice constitutes a material breach by Napais (it likely does not — the notice was given in good faith and within a reasonable time, and Baivusk has suffered no prejudice if it was already on notice of the Kelderhorn deal). Under New York law (which governs the Side Letter) and Delaware law (which governs the SLA), a party may be estopped from enforcing a notice deadline where it has received actual notice and has not acted in reliance on the strict deadline. This is a facts-and-circumstances argument, not a sure thing.
- **In the Kelderhorn acquisition agreement**, include a specific condition or covenant that Napais's members will use reasonable best efforts to obtain Baivusk's written confirmation that the non-renewal is effective, and allocate the risk of a 24-month renewal (including the associated fees and exclusivity) in the purchase price or indemnity.

## Issue 2: Change of Control Requires Baivusk's Consent

**Where it appears:** SLA §15.1 (Assignment by Licensee); SLA §13.2(c) (Termination by Licensor); Napais Letter, §1.1 (disclosing the Kelderhorn Equity Purchase Agreement dated October 30, 2025); Side Letter §6.3 (Successors).

**The problem.** Section 15.1 states:

> "Any change of control of Licensee, whether by sale of equity, merger, consolidation or otherwise, is deemed an assignment requiring Licensor's prior written consent."

Kelderhorn's acquisition of 100% of Napais's membership interests is unambiguously a change of control. The consent requirement applies to the *transaction itself*, not just to a post-closing assignment. Section 15.1 further provides that "[a]ny purported assignment in breach of this Section 15.1 is void," and §13.2(c) gives Baivusk the right to terminate **immediately** if Napais "assigns or transfers, or purports to assign or transfer, this Agreement in breach of Section 15.1."

The Side Letter is also implicated: §6.3 binds "permitted successors and assigns under Section 15 of the Agreement." If the change of control is not consented to, the Side Letter's exclusivity and pricing commitments may not extend to the post-closing entity, creating uncertainty about whether the exclusivity tail still applies (arguably it does, because Napais remains the same legal entity, but the consent issue clouds the entire relationship).

**Why it matters.**

- If Kelderhorn closes without Baivusk's consent, Baivusk has an immediate termination right under §13.2(c). That would cut off the 90-day transition assistance (though §13.4 excludes only termination under §13.2(a) for non-payment, so a §13.2(c) termination would still allow transition — but only if Napais requests it *before* termination, per the first sentence of §13.4).
- More practically, Baivusk holds leverage: it can condition consent on commercial terms (e.g., accepting the renewal, paying a consent fee, or agreeing to extended exclusivity).
- The November 14 letter discloses the Kelderhorn deal to Baivusk but does not explicitly request consent under §15.1. This is a gap.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Before closing**, send a formal request for consent under §15.1, identifying the Kelderhorn transaction and the expected closing date. The November 14 letter's disclosure of the Equity Purchase Agreement (§1.1) provides actual notice but is not a consent request.
- Negotiate the consent as part of the non-renewal discussion (Issue 1). A practical package: Baivusk consents to the change of control *and* confirms the non-renewal is effective, in exchange for (a) payment of all accrued fees through expiration, (b) the 90-day transition at current rates, and (c) a mutual release of claims.
- **In the Kelderhorn acquisition agreement**, make Baivusk's consent (or at least the absence of a termination notice) a condition to closing, or include a specific indemnity for losses arising from a §13.2(c) termination.
- Note that the Ostrander Reefer Maintenance Agreement (§12.2) and the NDA (§10.3) do **not** have change-of-control provisions. Napais remains the same legal entity post-closing, so no consent is needed under those agreements.

## Issue 3: Exclusivity Obligation Blocks Kelderhorn Technology Integration

**Where it appears:** Side Letter §2.1 (Exclusive Provider); Side Letter §2.2 (Existing Systems); Term Sheet §4.1 (Agreed Scope) and §4.3 (Outside Scope); Napais Letter, §1.2 (stating intent to "align its technology systems with those used across the Kelderhorn group").

**The problem.** Side Letter §2.1 provides:

> "During the Term, and for 12 months after the expiration or termination of the Agreement, Licensee shall not license, develop or use any temperature monitoring, warehouse management, yard management or transportation management software from any provider other than Licensor, in any jurisdiction worldwide."

Key features:

- **Scope is far broader than the Term Sheet.** The Term Sheet (§4.1) limited exclusivity to "temperature telemetry software for Napais's United States refrigerated trailer fleet during the term." The Side Letter expanded this to four software categories (temperature monitoring, warehouse management, yard management, transportation management), worldwide, with a 12-month post-termination tail. The Term Sheet was non-binding (§11.2), so it cannot override the Side Letter, but it is useful context if Napais argues for a narrower construction.
- **"Develop" is defined broadly.** Side Letter §2.1 states that "develop" includes development "by or for Licensee or any of its Affiliates, whether internally or through a third-party developer." If Kelderhorn has an existing TMS or WMS that it wants Napais to adopt, that is "using" Kelderhorn's software from "a provider other than Licensor." Even if Kelderhorn's in-house team builds a custom module for Napais, that could be "development for Licensee."
- **Affiliate restriction.** The Side Letter requires Napais to "cause each of its Affiliates to comply." Post-closing, Kelderhorn and its other operating companies will be Napais's parent and siblings. If any Kelderhorn affiliate uses a competing TMS, Napais is in breach unless it can "cause" compliance (which it likely cannot after the change of control).
- **Duration.** If the non-renewal is effective, the exclusivity tail runs 12 months from February 28, 2026 — i.e., through **February 28, 2027**. If the notice is late and the term renews for 24 months, the tail runs through **February 28, 2029**.

**Why it matters.** Napais's stated post-closing plan is to "align its technology systems with those used across the Kelderhorn group" (Letter §1.2). If Kelderhorn uses a TMS, WMS, or yard management platform from another vendor, onboarding Napais onto that platform would violate Side Letter §2.1 during the exclusivity tail. The penalty for breach is not stated in the Side Letter, but under the SLA's general framework, Baivusk could seek injunctive relief (SLA §17.4 covers breaches of §2.4 and §9, not §2.1 of the Side Letter, but a court could grant equitable relief for a standalone contractual breach) and damages (subject to the SLA's liability cap in §14.2, unless the breach is an "Excluded Claim" under §14.3 — a Side Letter breach is not listed as excluded, so the cap would apply).

**Recommended fix.**

- **Negotiate a release or amendment of the exclusivity obligation as part of the consent-to-change-of-control discussion (Issue 2).** Offer Baivusk a transition period (e.g., 6 months post-expiration) during which Napais may implement Kelderhorn's systems, in exchange for the 90-day transition assistance and payment of accrued fees.
- If a full release is not achievable, seek a **carve-out** for software that is (a) already in use by Kelderhorn or its Affiliates as of the closing date, or (b) deployed by Kelderhorn as part of a group-wide technology standardization. The "Existing Systems" provision in Side Letter §2.2 shows the parties were willing to grant transition carve-outs at the outset; a similar approach should be available now.
- **In the Kelderhorn acquisition agreement**, include a specific covenant that Kelderhorn will not require Napais to migrate to a competing platform before the exclusivity tail expires, or that Kelderhorn will indemnify Napais for any breach of the Side Letter arising from post-closing system integration.
- If the non-renewal notice is confirmed effective (Issue 1), calendar the exclusivity tail expiration (February 28, 2027) and plan the Kelderhorn migration to begin on or after that date.

## Issue 4: Split Governing Law and Forum

**Where it appears:** Side Letter §5.1–5.2 (New York law; New York County courts); SLA §17.1–17.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County courts); SLA §18.3 (Order of Precedence: Side Letter controls first).

**The problem.** The Side Letter — which contains the exclusivity obligation, the most-favored-pricing commitment, and the implementation credit — is governed by New York law and must be litigated in New York. The SLA — which contains the license grant, termination rights, assignment consent, data obligations, and limitation of liability — is governed by Delaware law and must be litigated in Delaware. Section 18.3 gives the Side Letter priority in case of conflict, but it does not harmonize the governing law.

**Why it matters.**

- If Napais seeks to enforce the non-renewal or challenge the exclusivity, it may face parallel proceedings in two jurisdictions under two bodies of law.
- New York law is generally more favorable to enforcing contractual restrictions (e.g., non-competes and exclusivity clauses) than Delaware law. Baivusk may prefer to litigate the exclusivity issue in New York.
- The most-favored-pricing commitment (Side Letter §3.1) applies only "during the Initial Term." If the term renews (Issue 1), the MFN protection lapses, and Baivusk can raise fees by up to 5% under SLA §5.5.

**Recommended fix.**

- In any negotiation with Baivusk (consent, non-renewal confirmation, exclusivity release), seek a **global resolution** documented in a single instrument governed by one law, to avoid the split-forum problem.
- If litigation becomes necessary, file the SLA-related claims (assignment consent, termination, data) in Delaware and the Side Letter claims (exclusivity, MFN) in New York, but coordinate strategy to avoid inconsistent rulings. Consider whether a single Delaware action can reach the Side Letter issues under a theory that the Side Letter is integrated into the SLA for purposes of dispute resolution (weak argument, given the express NY forum clause, but worth evaluating).

## Issue 5: Customer Data Migration and the Exclusivity Constraint

**Where it appears:** SLA §6.4 (Return and Deletion); SLA §1.5 (Customer Data definition); Napais Letter, §4.1–4.3 (data export requests); Side Letter §2.1 (exclusivity).

**The problem.** Section 6.4 requires Baivusk to make all Customer Data available for export in a "standard machine-readable format" within 30 days after expiration, and to delete it within 60 days after the export window closes. Napais's letter requests shipment records, temperature/humidity readings, location/route data, equipment status data, and alarm history for the full Term.

The tension: during the 12-month exclusivity tail, Napais cannot "use" another provider's temperature monitoring or transportation management software. If Kelderhorn's platform is a TMS or temperature monitoring system, importing Napais's historical data into that system could constitute "using" the software in violation of Side Letter §2.1. Napais can presumably retain and review the data internally, but operationalizing it in a competing platform is restricted.

**Why it matters.** Cold chain data (temperature excursions, alarm history, route performance) has regulatory and commercial value. If Napais cannot load the data into Kelderhorn's systems for 12 months, it loses the ability to (a) demonstrate compliance to customers and regulators, (b) benchmark performance against the Kelderhorn group, and (c) plan capacity and routing.

**Recommended fix.**

- In the exclusivity negotiation (Issue 3), seek an express carve-out permitting Napais to **import and analyze historical Customer Data** in Kelderhorn's systems for internal business purposes, even if those systems would otherwise be restricted. Distinguish between "using" a platform for ongoing operations (restricted) and "analyzing historical data" (should be permitted).
- Confirm with Baivusk the format and completeness of the data export. The SLA requires "standard machine-readable format" but does not specify which format. Napais should propose CSV or a documented API export in the transition planning (Letter §3.2 requests a migration schedule).
- Calendar the deletion deadline: 30 days post-expiration for export + 60 days for deletion = data is gone approximately **90 days after February 28, 2026** (i.e., by ~May 29, 2026). Ensure all data is exported and verified before that date.

## Issue 6: Transition Assistance and Its Limits

**Where it appears:** SLA §13.4 (Transition Assistance); Napais Letter, §3.1–3.2; SLA §13.2(a) (exception for non-payment termination).

**The problem.** Section 13.4 provides 90 days of continued Platform access at "fee rates then in effect" upon Licensee's request made "before expiration or termination." Napais's letter makes this request. The section is unavailable only if Baivusk terminates for non-payment under §13.2(a).

However, two practical concerns:

- The 90-day window (March 1 – May 29, 2026) overlaps with the exclusivity tail. Napais will be paying Baivusk for 90 more days while simultaneously being barred from using Kelderhorn's systems. This is a cost with no operational benefit if the data cannot be migrated.
- Section 13.4 does not address the **telematics gateways** (1,800 TG-4 units purchased by Napais, Order Form C.4). Title to the gateways passed to Napais on delivery (SLA §3.2), so Napais owns the hardware. But the gateway *firmware* "remains licensed and is not sold" (§3.2). After the SLA expires, the firmware license ends (§13.3(a)). Napais needs to confirm whether the gateways will function without the licensed firmware, or whether Baivusk will provide a firmware export or standalone mode.

**Recommended fix.**

- In the transition planning (Letter §3.2), specifically address the firmware question. Request that Baivusk confirm in writing whether the TG-4 gateways will continue to operate in a standalone or data-logging mode after the license expires, or whether a final firmware update will be provided.
- If the gateways become bricked, factor the cost of 1,800 replacement units (at $310/unit = $558,000, per Order Form C.4) into the transition budget, or negotiate with Baivusk for a discounted replacement or a limited post-termination firmware license.
- The 90-day transition cost is approximately $684,000 × (90/365) ≈ **$168,466**. Confirm this with Baivusk in writing.

## Issue 7: Ostrander Reefer Maintenance Agreement — Low Risk

**Where it appears:** Ostrander Agreement §12.2 (Assignment); §11.1 (Term); §11.2 (Termination for Convenience).

**Why it is lower risk.** The Ostrander Agreement has no change-of-control provision. Napais remains the same legal entity post-closing, so the agreement continues unaffected. Section 12.2 permits assignment to "a successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets" on written notice, which would cover a post-closing reorganization if Kelderhorn later merges Napais into another entity.

The term is 24 months from July 1, 2024, ending **June 30, 2026**, with automatic 12-month renewals unless 60 days' notice is given (by **May 1, 2026**). Either party can also terminate for convenience on 90 days' notice (§11.2).

**Recommended fix.**

- If Kelderhorn has its own reefer maintenance provider and wants to consolidate, Napais can terminate the Ostrander Agreement for convenience on 90 days' notice at any time. No consent is needed.
- If Napais wants the Ostrander agreement to continue post-closing (e.g., for service continuity during the transition), no action is needed. Calendar the May 1, 2026 non-renewal deadline in case the decision changes.

## Issue 8: NDA — Expired but Obligations Survive

**Where it appears:** NDA §8.1 (Term: 2 years from January 9, 2023 → expired January 9, 2025); §8.2 (Obligations survive 3 years → through January 9, 2028); §10.3 (Assignment to successor permitted).

**Why it matters.** The NDA has expired, but the confidentiality obligations survive through January 9, 2028. This means that information Baivusk disclosed during the diligence and negotiation phase (product architecture, security documentation, pricing — NDA §1.2(a)) remains confidential. Post-closing, Kelderhorn's deal team and advisors who received this information are bound. The NDA permits assignment to a successor (§10.3), so if the agreement is technically "assigned" in connection with the deal structure, no consent is needed.

**Recommended fix.** No action required beyond ensuring that Kelderhorn's post-closing team understands the surviving confidentiality obligations and does not inadvertently use Baivusk's pre-deal information (e.g., pricing data) in a way that could be characterized as a breach.
