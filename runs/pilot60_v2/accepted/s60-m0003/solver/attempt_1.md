# MEMORANDUM

## Executive Summary

Napais's acquisition by Kelderhorn Cold Freight Holdings, Inc. (100% equity purchase, signed October 30, 2025) creates two immediate and serious problems under the Baivusk Software License Agreement ("SLA"): (1) the change of control is a deemed assignment requiring Baivusk's prior written consent, and (2) Napais's non-renewal notice of November 14, 2025 was sent approximately two weeks after the contractual deadline, meaning the SLA will likely auto-renew for 24 months. Compounding both issues, the Side Letter imposes an exclusivity obligation far broader than what the parties negotiated in the Term Sheet, and that obligation extends to all of Napais's Affiliates—including Kelderhorn and its other subsidiaries post-closing.

---

## Issue 1: Change of Control Requires Baivusk's Consent

**Where:** SLA § 15.1; SLA § 13.2(c); Napais Letter to Baivusk, Nov. 14, 2025, ¶ 1.1.

**The problem.** Section 15.1 provides that "Any change of control of Licensee, whether by sale of equity, merger, consolidation or otherwise, is deemed an assignment requiring Licensor's prior written consent." Kelderhorn's acquisition of 100% of Napais's membership interests is squarely a change of control by sale of equity. The same section states that "Any purported assignment in breach of this Section 15.1 is void," and § 13.2(c) gives Baivusk the right to terminate immediately if Napais "assigns or transfers, or purports to assign or transfer, this Agreement in breach of Section 15.1."

**Why it matters.** The Equity Purchase Agreement was signed October 30, 2025. If closing has occurred or is imminent without Baivusk's consent, Napais is in breach. Baivusk could (a) terminate the SLA immediately under § 13.2(c), cutting off Platform access mid-migration, or (b) use the breach as leverage in any consent negotiation to extract concessions (higher fees, longer term, removal of the non-renewal position). Note that § 15.1 says consent "shall not be unreasonably withheld," but that standard gives Baivusk significant room to argue reasonableness in the context of a competitor's acquisition.

**Recommendation.**

- If closing has not yet occurred: make Baivusk's written consent a condition to closing under the Equity Purchase Agreement, or obtain consent before closing.
- If closing has already occurred: contact Baivusk immediately to seek retroactive consent. Frame it as a routine change-of-control notice. Offer to confirm that Kelderhorn will honor the existing terms. Do not wait for Baivusk to raise it.
- In either case, be prepared for Baivusk to link consent to the non-renewal issue (below). A package resolution is likely the most efficient path.

## Issue 2: Non-Renewal Notice Was Late—Agreement Will Auto-Renew

**Where:** SLA § 12.2; Napais Letter to Baivusk, Nov. 14, 2025, ¶¶ 2.1–2.2; SLA § 18.4 (notices effective on receipt).

**The problem.** Section 12.2 requires written notice of non-renewal "at least 120 days before the end of the then-current term." The Initial Term is 36 months from March 1, 2023, ending February 28, 2026. Counting back 120 days from February 28, 2026 yields **October 31, 2025**. The non-renewal letter is dated November 14, 2025 and was sent by overnight courier and certified mail. Under § 18.4, a notice is effective on receipt—so even on the most favorable reading, it was received no earlier than November 15, 2025, approximately **15 days after the deadline**.

**Why it matters.** Because the notice was untimely, the SLA will renew automatically for a 24-month Renewal Term beginning March 1, 2026 and ending February 28, 2028. This has cascading consequences:

- **Exclusivity continues** (see Issue 3) for the full Renewal Term plus 12 months.
- **Fee increases** become available: under § 5.5, Baivusk may raise subscription fees by up to 5% with 150 days' notice before the Renewal Term starts (i.e., by approximately November 1, 2025—already past).
- **Transition assistance** under § 13.4 is only available "on Licensee's request made before expiration or termination"—Napais's November 14 letter requesting 90 days of transition assistance is premature if the term renews, and Baivusk may refuse it on that basis.
- **The non-renewal position in the letter is likely ineffective**, undermining Napais's (and Kelderhorn's) stated plan to migrate off ColdTrace.

**Recommendation.**

- Accept that the auto-renewal has likely triggered. The 15-day miss is difficult to cure absent a waiver or a finding of excusable delay (e.g., if the internal approval process was delayed by the acquisition itself, there may be an argument, but § 18.2 requires amendments in writing and there is no force-majeure or equitable tolling provision in the SLA).
- Pivot strategy: rather than fighting the renewal, negotiate a **mutual early termination or a short-term extension with a firm end date** that aligns with Kelderhorn's migration timeline. Baivusk has an interest in avoiding a dispute over the change-of-control consent (Issue 1) and may be amenable to a clean exit in exchange for:
  - Payment of fees through a negotiated date (e.g., 6–9 months into the Renewal Term);
  - A release of the exclusivity obligation (see Issue 3); and
  - Confirmation of the change-of-control consent.
- If Baivusk resists, the fallback is to serve a **new** non-renewal notice 120 days before the end of the Renewal Term (i.e., by approximately October 31, 2027), but this locks Napais in for up to 20 months of additional fees and exclusivity. This should be the last resort.

## Issue 3: Exclusivity Obligation Is Far Broader Than Negotiated and Binds the Kelderhorn Group

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.1; Term Sheet §§ 4.1, 4.3; SLA § 1.2 (definition of "Affiliate"); SLA § 18.3 (order of precedence).

**The problem.** The Term Sheet—the document that captured the parties' negotiated deal—agreed to a narrow exclusivity: "Baivusk as its exclusive provider of **temperature telemetry software** for Napais's **United States refrigerated trailer fleet**" (Term Sheet § 4.1), with an express carve-out: "Napais and its affiliates are not restricted from licensing, developing or using other software outside the agreed scope" (Term Sheet § 4.3).

The executed Side Letter is dramatically broader. Section 2.1 restricts Napais from licensing, developing, or using "**temperature monitoring, warehouse management, yard management or transportation management software from any provider other than Licensor, in any jurisdiction worldwide**." It also captures development "by or for Licensee or any of its Affiliates, whether internally or through a third-party developer," and requires Napais to "cause each of its Affiliates to comply with the same restriction."

Under SLA § 18.3, the Side Letter controls over the SLA in case of conflict. There is no conflict here—the SLA is silent on exclusivity—but the Side Letter's scope is the operative restriction.

**Why it matters.**

- Post-acquisition, Kelderhorn controls Napais and is therefore an "Affiliate" under SLA § 1.2. The Side Letter's requirement that Napais "cause each of its Affiliates to comply" means **Kelderhorn and all of its other subsidiaries** are bound by the exclusivity.
- The restriction covers not just temperature monitoring but also warehouse management, yard management, and transportation management software. If Kelderhorn operates a TMS, WMS, or YMS across its group, using or even developing those systems could constitute a breach.
- The restriction is worldwide, not limited to the U.S.
- The "develop" language means Kelderhorn cannot even commission a custom solution from a third-party vendor for any of the covered categories.
- The exclusivity runs for the Term **plus 12 months** after expiration or termination (Side Letter § 2.1). If the SLA auto-renews through February 28, 2028, exclusivity runs through **February 28, 2029**.
- Side Letter § 2.3 requires Napais to notify Baivusk promptly if it or any Affiliate "receives a proposal from or enters into discussions with another provider for software of a type described in Section 2.1." Kelderhorn's internal technology standardization discussions likely trigger this notice obligation.

**Recommendation.**

- This is the highest-stakes commercial issue. In any negotiation with Baivusk (whether for change-of-control consent, early termination, or fee resolution), **make release of the exclusivity obligation a central ask**.
- Argue that the Side Letter as executed departs materially from the Term Sheet and that Baivusk's consent to the change of control should be conditioned on a narrowing of the exclusivity to match the Term Sheet scope (U.S. temperature telemetry for Napais's refrigerated trailer fleet only).
- If Baivusk will not agree to a full release, negotiate at minimum: (a) removal of Kelderhorn and its other subsidiaries from the Affiliate restriction; (b) limitation to temperature monitoring for Napais's U.S. trailer fleet; and (c) a defined sunset (e.g., 6 months post-termination rather than 12).
- Document Kelderhorn's existing technology stack (TMS, WMS, YMS) now, before closing or as part of integration planning, to identify any potential existing-use issues under Side Letter § 2.2 (which allows continued use of systems in place as of March 1, 2023, but only until the earlier of go-live or 12 months after the Side Letter date—i.e., that transition right expired by March 1, 2024 at the latest).

## Issue 4: Split Governing Law and Forum

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 5.1–5.2 (New York law; New York County courts); SLA § 17.1–17.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County courts); SLA § 18.3 (Side Letter controls in case of conflict).

**The problem.** The Side Letter is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, while the SLA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. Because the Side Letter controls under § 18.3, any dispute that implicates both documents (e.g., a challenge to the exclusivity scope, or the interaction between the change-of-control consent and the non-renewal) could be litigated in New York under New York law, even though the underlying SLA is a Delaware contract.

**Why it matters.** This creates procedural complexity and potential for forum disputes. If Baivusk asserts that the non-renewal notice was ineffective (a question under the SLA's Delaware law) but Napais counters that the Side Letter's exclusivity should be narrowed (a question under New York law), the parties may end up in two forums or face a motion to dismiss for improper venue.

**Recommendation.**

- In any settlement or amendment negotiated with Baivusk, conform the governing law and forum provisions across all documents (SLA, Side Letter, Order Form) to a single jurisdiction. Delaware is the natural choice given the SLA and the parties' entity types.
- If no amendment is reached, be aware that Baivusk has the advantage of choosing to file in New York for Side Letter-related claims.

## Issue 5: Transition Assistance Is Conditionally Available

**Where:** SLA § 13.4; Napais Letter to Baivusk, Nov. 14, 2025, ¶¶ 3.1–3.2.

**The problem.** Section 13.4 provides 90 days of continued Platform access at then-current fee rates, but only if (a) requested "before expiration or termination" and (b) not triggered by a Baivusk termination under § 13.2(a) (non-payment). Napais's November 14 letter requests transition assistance, but if the SLA auto-renews (Issue 2), there is no expiration or termination occurring in the near term, making the request premature. Additionally, if Baivusk terminates for the change-of-control breach (§ 13.2(c)), transition assistance is still available (the exclusion applies only to § 13.2(a)), but if any fee dispute arises, Baivusk could argue § 13.2(a) applies.

**Why it matters.** Napais needs the Platform during the migration window. If Baivusk refuses transition assistance on the ground that no termination is occurring (because of auto-renewal), Napais is locked into paying full subscription fees while running parallel systems.

**Recommendation.**

- Include transition assistance (or its equivalent—continued access at a reduced rate) in any negotiated early-termination or consent package.
- Ensure that all fees are paid current and undisputed to avoid giving Baivusk a § 13.2(a) termination right that would strip transition assistance.
- The 15-day request for a transition lead in Napais's letter (¶ 3.2) is reasonable; hold Baivusk to it if the relationship continues.

## Issue 6: Customer Data Export—Scope and Timing

**Where:** SLA § 6.4; Napais Letter to Baivusk, Nov. 14, 2025, ¶¶ 4.1–4.3.

**The problem.** Section 6.4 requires Baivusk to make Customer Data available for export in "a standard machine-readable format" within 30 days after expiration or termination, then delete within 60 days after the export window closes. Napais's letter requests five specific data categories (shipment records, temperature/humidity readings, location/route data, equipment status data, alarm history for the full Term). The SLA does not define "standard machine-readable format," which could lead to disputes over whether CSV, JSON, or API-based export satisfies the obligation.

**Why it matters.** If the SLA auto-renews, § 6.4 is not yet triggered. Napais cannot demand export until the agreement actually terminates. If termination occurs in 2028, the 30-day export window and 60-day deletion timeline will apply from that date. In the interim, all data remains subject to the SLA's confidentiality and data-use provisions.

**Recommendation.**

- In any negotiated exit, specify the export format (e.g., CSV or Parquet for tabular data; raw JSON for API-based data) and confirm that "alarm history for the full Term" includes all historical readings, not just those within a rolling window.
- If the agreement renews and Napais plans to exit at the end of the Renewal Term, begin planning the data migration 6–9 months in advance to ensure the 30-day export window is sufficient for 1,800 trailers and 6 facilities of telemetry data.
- Confirm that the telematics gateways (which Napais purchased under Order Form § C.4) can be reprogrammed or replaced without Baivusk's involvement. Title passed to Napais on delivery (SLA § 3.2), but the firmware remains licensed (SLA § 3.2), so post-termination the gateways may be bricked if the firmware license ends.

## Issue 7: Ostrander Reefer Maintenance Agreement—No Immediate Issue

**Where:** Ostrander Agreement § 12.2 (assignment); § 11.1 (term and renewal).

**The problem (or lack thereof).** The Ostrander agreement requires consent for assignment but permits assignment without consent to "a successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets." In an equity purchase, Napais, LLC remains the same legal entity—no assignment of the contract occurs. The agreement simply continues with Napais as the client, now owned by Kelderhorn. There is no change-of-control provision in the Ostrander agreement.

The term is 24 months from July 1, 2024 (ending June 30, 2026), with automatic 12-month renewals unless 60 days' notice of non-renewal is given (i.e., by May 1, 2026).

**Why it matters.** This is not a problem for the acquisition, but Kelderhorn should decide whether to continue or replace Ostrander as part of its technology and vendor standardization. If it wants to exit, the non-renewal notice deadline is **May 1, 2026**.

**Recommendation.**

- No action required for the acquisition itself.
- Calendar the May 1, 2026 non-renewal deadline if Kelderhorn intends to consolidate reefer maintenance under a different provider.
- Note that the Ostrander agreement is governed by Ohio law (Franklin County)—consistent with Napais's principal place of business.

## Issue 8: Mutual NDA—Still in Effect but Not a Barrier

**Where:** NDA § 8.1 (two-year term from January 9, 2023, expired January 9, 2025); § 8.2 (obligations continue three years post-expiration, i.e., through January 9, 2028); § 10.3 (assignment).

**The problem.** The NDA term expired in January 2025, but the confidentiality obligations survive until January 9, 2028. The NDA permits assignment without consent to "a successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets that agrees in writing to be bound." As with the Ostrander agreement, no assignment occurs in an equity purchase—Napais remains the same entity.

**Why it matters.** Kelderhorn's deal team and integration personnel will have access to Napais's confidential information (fleet data, routes, customer identities, pricing) under the NDA's definition of "Representatives" (§ 1.3), which includes affiliates. Post-closing, Kelderhorn is an affiliate. The NDA obligations protect both parties' information, so Kelderhorn should ensure its personnel handling Napais data understand the confidentiality duties that run through January 2028.

**Recommendation.**

- No consent or amendment needed for the acquisition.
- Include a reminder about NDA obligations in Kelderhorn's post-closing integration playbook, particularly regarding Napais's fleet composition, routes, and customer data, which remain confidential until January 2028.
