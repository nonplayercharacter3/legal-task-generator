## Material Issues

### 1. Product Indemnity Now Subject to Liability Cap (Drafting Error in Amendment No. 1)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §11.2(a); Amendment No. 1 §§2.1, 2.4.

**Issue.** Supply Agreement §11.2(a) provides that the liability cap in §11.1 does not apply to "Supplier's obligations under Section 10.1." Before the Amendment, §10.1 was the product indemnity (bodily injury, property damage, defects). Amendment §2.1 deleted and replaced §10.1 with a new IP indemnity. The former product indemnity was reinserted as new §10.3 (Amendment §2.4). The cross-reference in §11.2(a) was not updated. As a result, the product indemnity is no longer carved out of the cap and is now subject to the 12-month fee limitation in §11.1.

**Why it matters.** A baghouse fire, explosion, or emissions event caused by defective filter felt could generate claims well in excess of 12 months of fees (minimum ~$6.2M under Order Form §3.1). Before the Amendment, those claims were uncapped. Now they are not. This also affects the Parent Guaranty: §2.4 of the Guaranty limits Guarantor's liability to "no greater than the liability of the Obligor for that obligation under the Supply Agreement," so the cap flows through to the guaranty as well.

**Recommended fix.** Execute a corrective amendment updating §11.2(a) to read "Supplier's obligations under Section 10" (covering both the IP and product indemnities), or alternatively to read "Supplier's obligations under Sections 10.1 and 10.3." Confirm that the Parent Guaranty acknowledgment required by Amendment §5.3 reflects the corrected language.

### 2. Change of Control Consent Required Before Larchfield Closing

**Where:** Supply Agreement §15.3; Amendment No. 1, Recital C.

**Issue.** Section 15.3 deems a Change of Control of Buyer to be an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent. "Change of Control" is defined as acquisition of more than 50% of Buyer's voting equity. Amendment Recital C confirms that Moseth Group Holdings, LLC will sell all outstanding equity of Buyer to Larchfield Capital Partners, L.P., with closing expected in Q1 2027. The Amendment acknowledges the transaction but does not obtain Supplier's consent or waive the §15.3 requirement.

**Why it matters.** If the Larchfield transaction closes without Supplier's consent, Supplier may terminate the Agreement on 30 days' written notice (§15.3, last sentence). Given the $6.2M annual minimum purchase commitment and the specialized nature of the Products, loss of this supply relationship would be operationally disruptive. The transition supply right in §13.5 would not apply because the termination would be by Supplier under §15.3 (which is not §13.1 or §13.2, but §13.5's carve-out only excludes terminations "by Supplier under Section 13.1 or Section 13.2"—a termination under §15.3 is arguably not excluded, but the safer reading is that §13.5 applies only to the terminations it references, and §15.3 is a separate termination right).

**Recommended fix.** Obtain Supplier's written consent to the Change of Control as a condition to closing the Larchfield transaction. If Supplier withholds consent, negotiate a termination fee or extended transition period. Note that §15.1 (assignment by Buyer) requires Supplier's consent that "shall not be unreasonably withheld," but §15.3 does not contain that qualifier—Supplier has unfettered discretion over Change of Control consent.

### 3. Conflicting "Delivery Date" Definitions Undermine Service Level Protection

**Where:** Supply Agreement §1.4; Service Level Schedule §1.3; Supply Agreement §17.4(b).

**Issue.** The Supply Agreement defines "Delivery Date" as the date Products *arrive* at Buyer's Delivery Location (§1.4). The Service Level Schedule defines "Delivery Date" (for SLS purposes) as the date Supplier *tenders* Products to the carrier at Supplier's facility, as shown on the bill of lading (§1.3). Under the order of precedence in §17.4(b), the SLS prevails "as to service levels." All service level calculations—On-Time Delivery Rate (§1.6, §1.7), Late Shipment (§1.4), and Critical Delivery Failure (§4.1)—use the SLS definition.

**Why it matters.** A shipment that leaves Supplier's dock on the Required Date but arrives at Buyer's facility three days later is "on time" under the SLS and generates no service credit, even though it breaches the delivery obligation in Supply Agreement §4.1 ("Supplier shall deliver Products to the Delivery Location on or before the requested Delivery Date"). Given that Supplier selects and engages the carrier (§4.1) and the carrier "acts on Supplier's behalf," Supplier controls transit time, yet Buyer bears the risk of carrier delay without any service level remedy.

**Recommended fix.** Amend SLS §1.3 to conform to the Supply Agreement definition (arrival at Buyer's dock), or add a provision stating that if the carrier delivers after the Required Date, the Shipment is a Late Shipment regardless of the bill of lading date. Alternatively, add a separate "Arrival Service Level" that measures on-time arrival at Buyer's facility.

### 4. Chronic Failure Termination Right Likely Time-Barred

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §§4.1, 4.2, 5.1(a), 5.3; Supply Agreement §16.4; Amendment No. 1, Recital B.

**Issue.** Amendment Recital B states that the third Critical Delivery Failure occurred on June 12, 2026. Under SLS §4.2, Buyer may terminate for chronic failure by written notice "within sixty (60) days after the third Critical Delivery Failure in that rolling ninety (90) day period." Sixty days after June 12, 2026 is August 11, 2026. The Amendment was executed on September 15, 2026—after the 60-day window closed. No termination notice appears in the folder.

SLS §5.3 provides that while Buyer disputes Supplier's force majeure exclusion, the shipments still count toward CDFs. So the CDFs were valid for counting purposes. But the 60-day exercise window is a condition on the termination right itself, and it appears to have lapsed.

**Why it matters.** Buyer has likely lost its cleanest termination right (no liability, pro rata reduction of minimum purchase commitment under SLS §4.3(b)–(c), and transition supply under §4.3(d)). Buyer's remaining options are:
- **Extended Force Majeure termination (§16.4):** Available if the FM event continued more than 45 days. No deadline is stated. But this requires the FM event to have actually continued that long.
- **General termination for material breach (§13.1):** Requires 30-day cure period. Supplier could argue the failures were excused FM, making them not a "breach" at all.
- **Service credits:** Accrued and payable regardless of the termination question (SLS §5.3).

**Recommended fix.** (a) Confirm whether the June 2026 flooding continued for more than 45 days. If so, §16.4 provides a termination right that has not time-barred. (b) In any event, ensure all accrued service credits for the June 2026 Measurement Month are collected. (c) If Buyer wishes to preserve leverage in the Change of Control negotiation (Issue 2), it may be strategically preferable to forgo termination and instead negotiate a consent package that includes enhanced service level commitments.

**Cannot resolve from documents alone:** Whether Buyer gave a §16.4 termination notice or whether the flooding continued >45 days. The folder contains no termination notice and no evidence of when Supplier's finishing line was restored.

### 5. Guarantor's Performance Obligation Subject to 90-Day Location Change Notice

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §2.2; Supply Agreement §6.3.

**Issue.** Parent Guaranty §2.2 provides that if Supplier fails to supply Products, Guarantor "may perform by causing Products conforming to the Specifications to be supplied from another facility owned by Guarantor or its subsidiaries, subject to Section 6.3 of the Supply Agreement." Section 6.3 requires at least 90 days' prior written notice and Buyer's written approval before any change in manufacturing location.

**Why it matters.** The Guaranty is intended to provide a backstop if Supplier fails. But if the only way Guarantor can perform is from a different facility, and it must give 90 days' notice and obtain Buyer's approval before doing so, the guaranty is effectively useless in an acute supply failure. Buyer could be without supply for 90+ days while waiting for Guarantor to satisfy the §6.3 condition.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Guaranty §2.2 to delete "subject to Section 6.3 of the Supply Agreement" or replace it with a shortened emergency provision (e.g., 10 business days' notice, with Buyer's approval not to be unreasonably withheld). Alternatively, add a provision that Guarantor may supply from any facility that meets the Specifications without regard to §6.3, provided the Products conform.

## Moderate Issues

### 6. PPS Price Index Fallback Is Inappropriate

**Where:** Order Form §5.2(b).

**Issue.** For PPS Products, the price adjustment index is "the published contract price for PPS staple fiber agreed by the Parties in writing at the start of the Contract Year, or, absent agreement, the polyester index in Section 5.2(a)." PPS fiber and polyester fiber have very different cost structures and market dynamics. If the Parties fail to agree on a PPS-specific index each year, PPS prices (already at $21.30–$23.75/lm vs. $7.85–$9.65/lm for polyester) would be adjusted based on polyester prices, which could produce significant over- or under-adjustment.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate a specific, objective PPS fiber index (e.g., a published price from a named supplier or a commodity index) and incorporate it into the Order Form by amendment or side letter before the next Contract Year adjustment date.

### 7. Receiving Dock "Availability" Ambiguity (Forklift Dependency)

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §5.1(c); Supply Agreement §1.4; Forklift Maintenance Agreement (Pellingham) §§2.2, 2.5.

**Issue.** SLS §5.1(c) excludes a Shipment from service level calculations if there is "Buyer's failure to make its receiving dock available during its normal receiving hours." If a forklift breakdown (despite the Pellingham maintenance agreement) prevents Buyer from unloading, Supplier could argue the dock was not "available." The Supply Agreement's definition of Delivery Date (§1.4) focuses on the carrier making Products "available for unloading," which is arguably a physical-access test rather than an equipment-readiness test. But the SLS exclusion language is broader.

**Recommended fix.** Add a clarifying sentence to SLS §5.1(c) (or a side letter) stating that "available" means the dock is physically accessible and Buyer has not affirmatively prevented the carrier from tendering the shipment, and does not include Buyer's internal equipment readiness. Operationally, ensure forklift redundancy at the Newark facility.

### 8. Outbound Freight Liability Limit May Be Inadequate

**Where:** Rusk Valley Rate Confirmation §6.1.

**Issue.** Carrier liability is limited to $100,000 per truckload shipment (or $25/lb for LTL) unless Shipper declares a higher value on the bill of lading. Finished filter bags, cages, and cartridges for industrial customers could easily exceed $100,000 per load, particularly for PPS-based products.

**Recommended fix.** Implement a shipping procedure requiring declaration of actual value on the bill of lading for any load exceeding $80,000. Alternatively, negotiate a higher per-load limit with Rusk Valley at the next rate confirmation renewal (current Rate Period ends October 31, 2027).

### 9. Non-Renewal Notice Window Is Narrow

**Where:** Order Form §2.2.

**Issue.** Non-renewal notice must be given no more than 210 days and no less than 180 days before expiration. For the Initial Term (expiring January 5, 2028), the window is approximately July 9, 2027 through August 8, 2027—a 30-day window. If Moseth misses it, the Agreement auto-renews for another three years.

**Why it matters in context.** Given the Change of Control (Issue 2), Moseth may want the option to exit or renegotiate after the Larchfield transaction closes (expected Q1 2027). The non-renewal window opens only about 6 months after expected closing, leaving limited time to evaluate the relationship under new ownership.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the July 9, 2027 deadline immediately. Consider negotiating an extension of the non-renewal notice window (e.g., 270 to 180 days) as part of the Change of Control consent package.
