## Issue 1 – Laseisk May Have Lost Its Right to Convert Konais to Non-Exclusive for Contract Year 1

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 9.3; Amendment No. 1, Recital B and § 3.1.

**The problem.** Section 9.3 gives Laseisk the right to convert Konais from exclusive to non-exclusive if Konais misses the Minimum Annual Volume. The right must be exercised by written notice "within 45 days after the end of the relevant Contract Year, failing which such right is waived for that Contract Year."

Contract Year 1 ended February 28, 2026. The 45-day deadline fell on or about **April 14, 2026**. Amendment No. 1 is dated **May 20, 2026**—five weeks after the deadline. Section 3.1 of the Amendment states that "Supplier reserves all rights under Section 9.3 of the Agreement in respect of the first Contract Year."

A "reservation" in a subsequent instrument does not clearly revive a right that the contract itself says is automatically waived by the passage of time. Konais will argue the right is gone. Laseisk's position is weaker than it may realise.

**Why it matters.** If the conversion right is lost, Laseisk has **no contractual remedy** for Konais's Year 1 shortfall (940 of 1,200). Section 9.4 makes conversion the "sole remedy" for a volume failure. Laseisk cannot terminate for underperformance (there is no termination right tied to the Minimum Annual Volume), and it cannot appoint another distributor while the exclusive appointment is in force (§ 3.2).

**Recommended action.**

- **Confirm with Laseisk:** Did it send any written notice of conversion before April 14, 2026? If yes, this issue is moot. If no, we need to assess the strength of the "reservation" argument.
- If the notice was not given, Laseisk should not rely on the Amendment's reservation language as a standalone fix. Instead, in the next negotiation with Konais (see Issue 3 below), Laseisk should seek an express amendment to Section 9.3 that either (a) confirms the right was preserved by mutual agreement, or (b) extends the deadline for Year 1 to a date after the Amendment, or (c) provides a different remedy for the Year 1 shortfall (e.g., a volume buy-back or a reduction in Year 2's Minimum Annual Volume calibrated to the outage period).
- **Negotiating position:** Laseisk should make clear that it signed the Amendment on the understanding that the conversion right remained available, and that the price concession in the Amendment (Issue 4) was given in part on that basis.

## Issue 2 – The Side Letter's Narrower Scope Is Subordinate to the Agreement's Broad Exclusivity, Locking Laseisk Out of Most of the Market

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 3.1–3.2, 1.15, 17.3; Side Letter §§ 2.1–2.3.

**The problem.** The Agreement grants Konais an **exclusive** appointment for the entire Territory (United States and Canada) through **all** wholesale remarketing channels (§ 3.1). Section 3.2 prohibits Laseisk from selling directly or appointing any other distributor anywhere in the Territory.

The Side Letter (§ 2.1) "confirms that the exclusive scope agreed between them for Distributor's appointment is **franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut**." This was clearly the commercial understanding: Konais gets a three-state, franchised-dealer-only book of business.

But Section 17.3 sets the order of precedence as: Amendment No. 1 → Distribution Agreement → Service Level Schedule → **Side Letter** (last). Where the two documents conflict, the Agreement wins. The Agreement says all of US and Canada; the Side Letter says NY/NJ/CT franchised dealers. Under the precedence rule, Konais has legal exclusivity over the entire Territory.

**Why it matters.** Laseisk cannot:
- Sell subscriptions directly to independent dealers in NY/NJ/CT;
- Sell to franchised dealers in any other state or province;
- Appoint a second distributor for any segment of the market.

Yet Konais is only contractually focused on three states of franchised dealers (Side Letter § 2.3). If Konais underperforms in that narrow segment, Laseisk cannot fill the gap elsewhere because the broader exclusivity blocks it.

**Recommended action.**

- Laseisk should propose an amendment to § 3.1 and § 3.2 that aligns the Agreement's exclusivity with the Side Letter's commercial scope. For example: "Supplier appoints Distributor as its exclusive distributor of Auction Access Subscriptions to franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut. With respect to all other Dealers and all other areas of the Territory, the appointment is non-exclusive."
- **Alternative:** If Laseisk wants to preserve the option of broader exclusivity (e.g., if Konais proves it can handle more), the fix could be to make the Side Letter's scope the *initial* exclusive territory, with a mechanism to expand it by mutual agreement as Konais demonstrates capacity.
- **Confirm with Laseisk:** Has it been selling directly to any dealers in the Territory during the Term? If so, it is in breach of § 3.2 as currently drafted, and this issue becomes more urgent.

## Issue 3 – No Effective Remedy for Chronic Underperformance

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 5.1(a), 9.1–9.4, 14.2.

**The problem.** Konais's sales obligation is "commercially reasonable efforts" (§ 5.1(a)), a low standard. The Minimum Annual Volume (§ 9.1) is the only quantitative target, and its sole remedy is conversion to non-exclusive (§ 9.4)—which Laseisk may have lost (Issue 1). There is no termination right for failure to meet the volume target. There is no "best efforts" or "reasonable sales target" short of the MAE that would give Laseisk a breach claim.

If Konais continues to sell at ~940 units per year (78% of target), Laseisk's options are:
- Wait for the initial three-year term to end (February 28, 2028) and give non-renewal notice 90 days prior (§ 14.1); or
- Argue that chronic underperformance constitutes a "material breach" of § 5.1(a) (commercially reasonable efforts) under § 14.2. This is a fact-intensive argument and Konais will resist it.

**Why it matters.** Laseisk is potentially locked in with an underperforming exclusive distributor for up to two more years (through the end of the initial term), with no practical lever to improve performance or exit early.

**Recommended action.**

- In the next amendment or renewal negotiation, Laseisk should seek:
  - A **tiered remedy**: if Konais misses the MAE by more than 20% in any Contract Year, Laseisk may (a) convert to non-exclusive, (b) reduce the Territory, or (c) terminate on 60 days' notice.
  - A **clawback or price adjustment** tied to volume: if Konais sells below a threshold (e.g., 80% of MAE), the Wholesale Price reverts to the pre-Amendment levels or increases by a specified percentage.
  - A **minimum quarterly run-rate** with a cure period, giving Laseisk an earlier warning than the year-end statement.
- **Confirm with Laseisk:** What is Konais's Year 2 pipeline and forecast? Has the meeting under Amendment § 3.3 taken place, and if so, what was discussed?

## Issue 4 – Amendment No. 1 Gave a ~5% Price Reduction Without a Clear Quid Pro Quo and Deferred Laseisk's Adjustment Right

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1 §§ 2.1–2.4; Distribution Agreement, Schedule A.

**The problem.** The Amendment reduces all Wholesale Prices by approximately 5%:

| Type | Before | After | Change |
|------|--------|-------|--------|
| Buyer | $1,150 | $1,095 | −4.8% |
| Seller | $1,350 | $1,285 | −4.8% |
| Combined | $2,100 | $1,995 | −5.0% |

The Amendment does not state what Laseisk receives in exchange. The recitals reference the volume dispute (Recital B), and § 3.1 "reserves" the conversion right—but as noted in Issue 1, that right may already be waived. So the practical consideration Laseisk received is uncertain.

Additionally, § 2.4 defers Laseisk's annual price-adjustment right until the Contract Year beginning **March 1, 2027**. The new prices took effect June 1, 2026. Laseisk is therefore locked into reduced pricing for approximately **nine months** with no ability to adjust.

**Why it matters.** If the Platform outage did not in fact cause the volume shortfall (Laseisk's position), Laseisk has given a price concession based on a disputed premise. And the deferred adjustment right means Laseisk cannot recover the margin even if market conditions warrant it.

**Recommended action.**

- **Confirm with Laseisk:** Was the price reduction negotiated as a concession for the outage, or as a separate commercial point? Understanding the negotiation history will inform whether Laseisk can argue the Amendment is voidable or subject to rectification if the outage claim fails.
- In the next negotiation, Laseisk should seek to **restore its annual adjustment right** for the current Contract Year (i.e., allow one adjustment on 60 days' notice before March 1, 2027), or at minimum shorten the lock-in period.
- If Laseisk ultimately prevails on the outage dispute (i.e., the outage did not materially affect sales), it should seek to **revert the prices** to the pre-Amendment levels, arguing the concession was given on the basis of a false premise.

## Issue 5 – The Platform Outage Dispute Is Unresolved and the Amendment's Process Steps May Have Lapsed

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, Recital B and §§ 3.2–3.3; Service Level Schedule §§ 2, 4.

**The problem.** The Amendment does not resolve whether the Year 1 Platform outage excuses Konais's volume shortfall. It sets up a process:
- § 3.2: Konais to deliver a written summary of the outage and affected applications within **15 business days** of May 20, 2026 (≈ June 10, 2026).
- § 3.3: Senior commercial reps to meet within **30 days** (≈ June 19, 2026).

**Confirm with Laseisk:** Have these steps been completed? If Konais has not delivered the summary, it is in breach of the Amendment. If the meeting has not occurred, Laseisk should put Konais on notice.

**On the merits:** The Agreement contains no force majeure or excuse provision in Section 9 (Minimum Annual Volume). Section 15 (Force Majeure) relieves a party from "failure or delay in performance," but the MAE is Konais's obligation to *sell* subscriptions, not Laseisk's obligation to keep the Platform running. A Platform outage might affect dealer renewals (dealers who can't use their subscription may not renew), but it is harder to see how it prevents Konais from *selling new* subscriptions. Laseisk's position—that the outage does not excuse the shortfall—is textually strong.

**Recommended action.**

- Press Konais for the § 3.2 summary if not yet received.
- In the summary, Laseisk should require Konais to quantify: (a) the duration of the outage, (b) the number of Dealer applications actually lost or delayed, and (c) the causal link between the outage and the 260-unit shortfall.
- Laseisk should prepare its own monitoring data (which it is obligated to share under § 3.2 and SLS § 6.3) to demonstrate the actual scope of any outage.
- **Do not concede** that the outage excuses the volume. The contractual position is that Section 9 has no carve-out, and the Service Level Schedule's remedy for outages is service credits (SLS § 4), not volume relief.

## Issue 6 – Konais Could Argue a Prolonged Outage Is a Material Breach Giving It Termination Rights

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 4.1(b), 11.2, 14.2; Service Level Schedule § 4.5.

**The problem.** Section 11.2 limits Konais's remedy for SLA failures to service credits. But SLS § 4.5 expressly preserves "either Party's rights under Section 14.2" (termination for material breach). If the Year 1 outage was prolonged or severe, Konais could argue that Laseisk's failure to maintain the Platform in accordance with the SLS constitutes a **material breach** of § 4.1(b), entitling Konais to terminate under § 14.2 (30-day cure period).

This is a risk Laseisk should anticipate, particularly if the outage dispute escalates.

**Why it matters.** If Konais terminates, Laseisk loses its distribution channel for the Territory. While § 14.4(c) allows Laseisk to contract directly with existing dealers after their current terms expire, there is a transition gap, and Laseisk would need to build a direct sales capability or find a new distributor.

**Recommended action.**

- **Confirm with Laseisk:** What was the actual duration and severity of the Year 1 outage? Was it a single event or recurring? Did it cross the Severity 1 threshold (SLS § 1.2(g))?
- If the outage was brief and within the SLS parameters, Laseisk's position is strong that no material breach occurred. Document this.
- Ensure the SLS § 6.1 monthly reports for the affected period are complete and show that any service credits were calculated and paid. If Laseisk paid service credits, that undercuts any later argument by Konais that the failure was "material."
- In any negotiation with Konais, Laseisk should make clear that the SLS remedy (service credits) was the agreed exclusive remedy for the outage, and that Konais accepted that framework when it agreed to the SLS.

## Issue 7 – The Minimum Annual Volume May Be Miscalibrated to the Actual Scope

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 9.1; Side Letter § 2.1.

**The problem.** The MAE is 1,200 Dealer Subscriptions per Contract Year. If the true commercial scope is franchised dealers in NY, NJ and CT only (per the Side Letter), the question is whether 1,200 is achievable in that market. Konais sold 940 in Year 1.

**Confirm with Laseisk:**
- What is the total number of active franchised dealer groups in NY, NJ and CT?
- What is the realistic penetration rate (i.e., what percentage of those dealers would buy a subscription)?
- Was the 1,200 figure calibrated to the three-state scope or to the broader Territory?

If 1,200 was set for the broader Territory but Konais is only covering three states, the target is unrealistic and Konais will use that to justify underperformance. If it was set for the three-state scope, then 940 is a genuine shortfall and Laseisk has a stronger position.

**Recommended action.** Use the data to inform the negotiation on Issue 3 (remedies for underperformance). If the target is miscalibrated, Laseisk may need to accept a lower MAE in exchange for stronger remedies (e.g., termination right, territory reduction).

## Issue 8 – Laseisk's Annual Price-Adjustment Right Is Constrained

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement, Schedule A (paragraph after table); Amendment No. 1 § 2.4.

**The problem.** Under Schedule A, Laseisk may adjust Wholesale Prices once per Contract Year on 60 days' notice, but no single adjustment may exceed **5%**. Given that the Amendment already reduced prices by ~5%, and Laseisk's next adjustment right does not arise until the Contract Year beginning March 1, 2027, Laseisk is effectively capped at recovering its margin over a very long period.

If inflation, platform development costs, or market conditions warrant a larger increase, Laseisk is limited to 5% per year absent mutual agreement (§ 17.4).

**Recommended action.**

- In the next amendment, seek to either (a) increase the cap to 7–8%, (b) allow a "catch-up" adjustment for the first year after the Amendment (i.e., permit one adjustment before March 1, 2027), or (c) tie the adjustment to a cost index (e.g., CPI) so that the 5% cap is a floor rather than a ceiling in high-inflation periods.

## Issue 9 – The NDA Non-Solicitation Has Expired; Confidentiality Overlap Creates Minor Ambiguity

**Documents:** Mutual NDA §§ 8.1, 9.1–9.2; Distribution Agreement § 12.4.

**The problem (minor).** The NDA's 12-month non-solicitation (§ 8.1) expired on November 4, 2025. If there are any employee mobility concerns, they are no longer protected by the NDA.

On confidentiality: the NDA covers information disclosed during the two-year period from November 4, 2024 (i.e., through November 4, 2026), with obligations lasting three years after that (through November 4, 2029). The Agreement's § 12.4 provides three-year survival post-termination, with Dealer Data and trade secrets surviving indefinitely. These overlap but are not identical. In practice, the Agreement's provisions will govern the ongoing commercial relationship, and the NDA is a backstop for pre-Agreement disclosures.

**Recommended action.** No immediate action needed, but if the relationship terminates, ensure that any pre-Agreement confidential information (e.g., business plans shared during the NDA period) is covered by the NDA's longer tail for trade secrets.

## Issue 10 – Konais Can Use Sub-Agents with Only "Not Unreasonably Withheld" Consent

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 16.3.

**The problem.** Konais may use sub-agents or subcontractors in the Territory with Laseisk's prior written consent, which "shall not unreasonably be withheld." This means Laseisk cannot block Konais from delegating its sales function to a third party unless the objection is reasonable. Given the scope issues (Issue 2), Laseisk may want tighter control over who is actually selling its subscriptions.

**Recommended action.** In the next amendment, consider adding: (a) a requirement that sub-agents be bound by confidentiality and data protection obligations no less protective than the Agreement; (b) a right of Laseisk to approve the identity of any sub-agent (not merely consent to the concept); and (c) a prohibition on sub-agents that are competitors of Laseisk or its Affiliates.

## Suggested Negotiating Priorities (in order)
