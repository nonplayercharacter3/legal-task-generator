## Summary

The document set contains three material problems for Laseisk: (1) the order of precedence undermines the Side Letter's narrower exclusivity scope, leaving Laseisk locked out of the entire US and Canada; (2) Laseisk has likely already waived its right to convert Konais to non-exclusive for Contract Year 1; and (3) Konais's claim that a Platform outage excuses its volume shortfall has no support in the documents. Additional issues around pricing, data disclosure, and structural remedies are noted below.

---

## Issue 1 – Exclusivity Scope: Side Letter Defeated by Order of Precedence

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 1.15, 3.1–3.2; Side Letter § 2.1; Distribution Agreement § 17.3

**The problem.** Section 3.1 of the Distribution Agreement appoints Konais as exclusive distributor "in the Territory" (defined as the United States and Canada, § 1.15) "through all wholesale remarketing channels." Section 3.2 prohibits Laseisk from selling directly or appointing any other distributor anywhere in the Territory.

The Side Letter (§ 2.1) confirms that "the exclusive scope agreed between them for Distributor's appointment is franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut." This was the commercial deal.

But § 17.3 sets the order of precedence as: Amendment No. 1 → Distribution Agreement → Service Level Schedule → Side Letter. The Side Letter is last. Where it conflicts with the Distribution Agreement, the Distribution Agreement wins. The result is that, as the documents currently read, Laseisk is excluded from selling Auction Access Subscriptions directly or through any other channel to **any dealer, anywhere in the US and Canada**, for the duration of the Term.

**Why it matters.** This is potentially the most consequential issue. Laseisk's own auction platform serves dealers across the country. If the broad exclusivity in § 3.1–3.2 controls, Laseisk cannot onboard a single non-franchised dealer in, say, Texas or Michigan without Konais's involvement. That is almost certainly not what was commercially agreed, and it could strangle Laseisk's direct business.

**Recommendation.**

- **Immediate:** Confirm with Laseisk whether it has, since the Effective Date, sold or attempted to sell any subscriptions directly or through other channels outside NY/NJ/CT. If so, Laseisk may already be in breach of § 3.2, which would weaken its negotiating position.
- **Drafting fix:** Amend § 3.1 and § 3.2 of the Distribution Agreement (or add a further amendment) to conform the exclusivity to the Side Letter's scope: "franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut." Alternatively, elevate the Side Letter in the order of precedence in § 17.3 so it prevails over the Distribution Agreement on scope.
- **Negotiating position:** Laseisk should tell Konais that the Side Letter records the parties' agreed commercial scope and that the broad language in § 3.1 was a drafting error. If Konais resists, Laseisk's leverage is that the current text also prevents Konais from focusing exclusively on the three-state franchised segment it was hired to serve.

## Issue 2 – Contract Year 1 Conversion Right Likely Waived

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 9.1, 9.3, 9.4; Amendment No. 1, Recital B and § 3.1

**The problem.** Konais sold 940 subscriptions in Contract Year 1 against a minimum of 1,200. Under § 9.3, Laseisk could convert Konais to non-exclusive by giving written notice "within 45 days after the end of the relevant Contract Year, failing which such right is waived for that Contract Year."

Contract Year 1 ended February 28, 2026. The 45-day deadline fell on approximately April 14, 2026. The Amendment is dated May 20, 2026—more than five weeks after the deadline.

Amendment § 3.1 states that "Supplier reserves all rights under Section 9.3 of the Agreement in respect of the first Contract Year." But a reservation in a bilateral amendment signed after the deadline does not revive a right that § 9.3 expressly provides is "waived" for failure to act in time. The section uses mandatory language ("failing which such right is waived"), not discretionary language.

**Why it matters.** § 9.4 makes conversion Laseisk's *sole* remedy for a volume shortfall. If the conversion right is waived, Laseisk has **no remedy at all** for the 260-subscription shortfall in Contract Year 1. No damages, no termination, no conversion. Konais underperformed by 22% and faces no consequence.

**Flag – facts to confirm with Laseisk:**

- Did Laseisk send any written notice of conversion (or intent to convert) before April 14, 2026? Even an informal email could be argued to satisfy the "written notice" requirement.
- Was there any side communication or course of dealing that could support an argument that the deadline was extended by agreement before it lapsed?
- What was the actual date Konais delivered its § 9.2 shortfall statement? If it was late, that might support a separate argument (though § 9.3's deadline runs from the end of the Contract Year, not from receipt of the statement).

**Recommendation.**

- If no pre-deadline notice was sent, Laseisk should proceed on the assumption that the Contract Year 1 conversion right is lost. Do not build the negotiating strategy on recovering it.
- For Contract Year 2 (ending February 28, 2027), calendar the 45-day deadline immediately (approximately April 14, 2027) and set an internal reminder well in advance.
- **Drafting fix for the future:** Amend § 9.3 to (a) extend the notice period to 90 days, and (b) provide that the deadline runs from the later of the end of the Contract Year or Laseisk's receipt of the § 9.2 statement. Also consider adding a liquidated shortfall payment as an alternative or additional remedy in § 9.4, so Laseisk is not dependent on a single time-limited election.

## Issue 3 – Konais's Outage Excuse Has No Textual Support

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 4.1(b), 9.1–9.4; Service Level Schedule §§ 4.1–4.5, 11.2 (cross-ref); Amendment No. 1, Recital B

**The problem.** Konais argues that a Platform outage during Contract Year 1 excuses its failure to meet the Minimum Annual Volume. The documents do not support this.

- The Minimum Annual Volume (§ 9.1) is Konais's own sales target. It is not conditioned on Platform availability.
- The remedy for a service level failure is expressly limited to Service Credits (Service Level Schedule § 4.5; Distribution Agreement § 11.2). There is no provision that a service level failure relieves the Distributor of its volume commitment.
- Force majeure (§ 15) excuses a party's own performance obligations caused by an external event. A Platform outage is Laseisk's failure to perform § 4.1(b), not a force majeure event affecting Konais.
- Even if Konais could argue that Laseisk's breach of § 4.1(b) prevented Konais from performing § 9.1, the documents provide a specific remedy for § 4.1(b) failures (Service Credits) and a specific (sole) remedy for § 9.1 failures (conversion). The structure indicates the parties allocated these risks separately.

**Why it matters.** If Laseisk accepts the outage excuse, it sets a precedent that any Platform disruption reduces Konais's volume target. Given that Laseisk operates the Platform, this gives Konais a recurring, self-inflicted excuse for underperformance.

**Recommendation.**

- **Negotiating position:** Laseisk should reject the excuse outright. The correct framework is: if the outage warranted Service Credits, Laseisk pays the credits; Konais still owes the full Minimum Annual Volume. The two obligations are independent.
- **Drafting fix:** Add a sentence to § 9.1 or § 9.4 making explicit that the Minimum Annual Volume is not reduced or excused by any failure of the Platform to meet service levels, and that Service Credits are the sole adjustment mechanism for such failures.
- In the Amendment § 3.3 meeting (senior commercial representatives, within 30 days of May 20, 2026), Laseisk should put this position on the table clearly and in writing.

## Issue 4 – 5% Price Reduction Without Clear Quid Pro Quo

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, §§ 2.1–2.4; Distribution Agreement, Schedule A

**The problem.** Amendment No. 1 reduces all Wholesale Prices by approximately 5% effective June 1, 2026. The Amendment's recitals do not state the consideration for this reduction. It appears to have been traded against Laseisk's agreement to "reserve" (rather than exercise) the conversion right in § 3.1 and to engage in further discussion in § 3.3.

If the conversion right was already waived (see Issue 2), then Laseisk gave up 5% of its revenue per subscription in exchange for reserving a right it no longer had.

**Why it matters.** At the revised prices, each Combined Subscription generates $105 less per 12-month term. Across 1,200 subscriptions (the minimum), that is $126,000 per year in lost revenue, before volume shortfalls are factored in.

**Recommendation.**

- Confirm with Laseisk's commercial team what was actually agreed as the trade-off for the price reduction. If it was meant to be "Laseisk drops the conversion right in exchange for the price cut," then Laseisk has already paid for the Contract Year 1 shortfall and should not be expected to give further concessions.
- If the price reduction was meant to be temporary or conditional on Konais meeting Contract Year 2 volume, that condition is not in the Amendment. **Drafting fix:** add a clawback or reversion clause—if Konais misses the Contract Year 2 minimum, the Wholesale Prices revert to the pre-Amendment levels for Contract Year 3.
- Note that § 2.4 preserves Laseisk's right to make one further 5% adjustment in the Contract Year beginning March 1, 2027. Laseisk should be prepared to use that if the relationship continues, but should not rely on it as a remedy for the current dispute.

## Issue 5 – Monitoring Data Disclosure Obligation

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, § 3.2; Service Level Schedule, § 6.3

**The problem.** Amendment § 3.2 requires Laseisk to "make available to Distributor the relevant extracts of its monitoring data under Section 6.3 of the Service Level Schedule" in connection with the volume dispute. Section 6.3 was designed for verifying Service Credit claims, not for litigating volume shortfalls. The Amendment repurposes it.

**Why it matters.** The monitoring data will show the duration and severity of the outage. If the outage was brief (e.g., a few hours) and did not overlap with major auction events, the data will undermine Konais's excuse. But if the outage was prolonged or coincided with high-volume sale days, the data could be used to argue that dealer activity was materially disrupted. Laseisk should control the narrative.

**Recommendation.**

- Before producing data, have Laseisk's operations team prepare a summary that contextualizes the outage: duration, which services were affected, whether any live auctions were impacted, and what Service Credits (if any) were already paid.
- Produce the data in response to Konais's written summary (required by Amendment § 3.2 to be delivered within 15 business days of May 20, 2026, i.e., by approximately June 4, 2026). Do not produce data proactively before seeing Konais's specific claims.
- **Flag:** Confirm with Laseisk whether Service Credits were actually claimed and paid for the outage in question. If they were, that undercuts any further argument by Konais that the outage was severe enough to excuse 260 subscriptions.

## Issue 6 – No Termination Right for Persistent Volume Failure

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 9.3–9.4, 14.2

**The problem.** Laseisk's only remedy for a volume shortfall is conversion to non-exclusive (§ 9.3), and that is the *sole* remedy (§ 9.4). Laseisk cannot terminate for a volume miss. Termination for material breach (§ 14.2) is theoretically available, but a single year's shortfall where the parties have agreed on a specific sole remedy is unlikely to be found "material" in a way that supports termination.

**Why it matters.** If Konais chronically underperforms (e.g., 940 in Year 1, and again in Year 2), Laseisk's options are limited to converting to non-exclusive (if within the deadline) or waiting for the Term to expire. It cannot walk away.

**Recommendation.**

- **Drafting fix:** Amend § 9.4 to provide that if Konais fails to meet the Minimum Annual Volume in two consecutive Contract Years, Laseisk may terminate the Agreement on 60 days' written notice, in addition to (or in lieu of) conversion.
- In the interim, Laseisk should track Contract Year 2 performance closely and be ready to exercise the conversion right (or a new termination right, if negotiated) well before the 45-day deadline.

## Issue 7 – Auto-Renewal and Exit Timing

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 14.1

**The problem.** The initial term is three Contract Years (March 1, 2025 – February 28, 2028). It then renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either party gives 90 days' notice of non-renewal. The first non-renewal notice deadline is **December 1, 2027**.

**Why it matters.** If Laseisk wants the option to exit after the initial term, it must decide by December 1, 2027. Given the current disputes, Laseisk may want that option. But if Laseisk is in the middle of negotiating a resolution with Konais in 2027, it needs to be aware of the deadline.

**Recommendation.** Calendar the December 1, 2027 date now. No action required unless Laseisk decides it does not want to renew.

## Issue 8 – Side Letter Launch Plan: No Consequence for Non-Delivery

**Documents:** Side Letter § 3.1

**The problem.** Konais was required to deliver a written launch plan within 30 days of the Effective Date (by approximately March 31, 2025). The Side Letter specifies no consequence for failure to deliver.

**Flag – facts to confirm:** Did Konais deliver the launch plan? If not, while this is not a standalone termination trigger, it is relevant context for the volume dispute (i.e., Konais may not have had a credible sales plan from the outset) and could support an argument that Konais's "commercially reasonable efforts" obligation (§ 5.1(a)) was not met.

**Recommendation.** Low priority. Use as supporting context in the § 3.3 meeting if the plan was not delivered.

## Issue 9 – NDA Non-Solicitation Has Expired; No Ongoing Restriction on Laseisk Hiring from Konais

**Documents:** Mutual NDA § 8.1

**The problem.** The 12-month non-solicitation ran from November 4, 2024 to November 4, 2025. It has lapsed. Neither party is currently restricted from soliciting the other's employees.

**Why it matters.** Minor, but if the relationship deteriorates and Laseisk wants to hire Konais staff who have dealer relationships in the three-state scope, there is no contractual barrier. Conversely, Konais could poach Laseisk's platform or sales staff.

**Recommendation.** No action needed unless Laseisk is actively considering hiring from Konais or is concerned about key staff retention. If so, consider adding a non-solicitation to the Distribution Agreement itself (which would survive for the Term plus a tail period).
