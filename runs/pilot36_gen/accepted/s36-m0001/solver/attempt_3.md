## Issue 1 – Exclusivity Scope: Side Letter Is Neutralised by the Order of Precedence

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §3.1–3.2, §17.3; Side Letter §2.1

**The problem.** Section 3.1 of the Distribution Agreement appoints Konais as the *exclusive* distributor of Auction Access Subscriptions "in the Territory" (defined as the United States and Canada, §1.15) "through all wholesale remarketing channels." Section 3.2 prohibits Laseisk from selling directly to any Dealer in the Territory. The Side Letter (§2.1) was clearly intended to narrow this to "franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut." But Section 17.3 sets the order of precedence as: Amendment No. 1 → Distribution Agreement → Service Level Schedule → Side Letter. The Side Letter sits at the bottom. As a matter of plain construction, the broader exclusivity in the main Agreement controls, and the Side Letter's narrower scope is overridden.

**Why it matters.** Laseisk is contractually barred from selling subscriptions directly to *any* dealer anywhere in the US or Canada for the duration of the Term (at least three Contract Years, i.e., through February 28, 2028, and potentially longer via auto-renewal). This almost certainly goes well beyond the commercial deal the parties struck. If Laseisk has an existing direct dealer base or plans to grow one, it is currently in breach of §3.2.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Immediate:** Confirm with Laseisk whether it has sold or attempted to sell any subscriptions directly since March 1, 2025. If so, assess exposure and consider a voluntary standstill or cure.
- **Negotiating position:** Propose an amendment (or Amendment No. 2) that either (a) revises §3.1 to limit the exclusive appointment to "franchised dealers in New York, New Jersey and Connecticut" (tracking the Side Letter), or (b) carves out of §3.2 any dealers with whom Laseisk had an existing direct relationship before the Effective Date, plus Laseisk's own online self-serve channel. Use the Year 2 planning meeting (Amendment No. 1, §3.3) as the forum.
- **Fallback:** If Konais resists narrowing, seek a most-favoured-nation or market-expansion trigger: if Konais fails to meet a revised minimum in Year 2, Laseisk can appoint additional distributors or resume direct sales in specified sub-territories.

## Issue 2 – Laseisk Has Likely Waived Its Right to Convert to Non-Exclusive for Contract Year 1

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §9.3; Amendment No. 1, Recital B, §3.1

**The problem.** Section 9.3 gives Laseisk the right to convert Konais to a non-exclusive appointment if Konais misses the Minimum Annual Volume (1,200 subscriptions). The right must be exercised by written notice "within 45 days after the end of the relevant Contract Year, **failing which such right is waived** for that Contract Year." Contract Year 1 ran March 1, 2025 – February 28, 2026. The 45-day deadline fell on **April 14, 2026**. Amendment No. 1 is dated **May 20, 2026** – 36 days after the deadline. Its §3.1 states that "Supplier reserves all rights under Section 9.3 in respect of the first Contract Year," but this language appears in a document executed *after* the express waiver took effect.

**Why it matters.** If the waiver is effective (as the plain language provides), Laseisk cannot convert Konais to non-exclusive for Year 1. Konais retains full exclusivity for the remainder of the initial three-year term regardless of its 940-subscription performance. The "reservation" in the Amendment is, at best, an argument that the parties implicitly extended the window by agreeing to the Amendment; at worst, it is ineffective because §17.5 provides that no failure or delay operates as a waiver "except as expressly provided in this Agreement" – and §9.3 is precisely such an express provision.

**Facts to confirm with Laseisk:**

- Did Laseisk send *any* written notice to Konais regarding the Year 1 shortfall before April 14, 2026? (Even an informal email could support an argument that the right was exercised or that the deadline was tolled.)
- Was the Amendment negotiated with the understanding that the 45-day window was still open, or did Laseisk's team believe it had already lapsed?

**Recommended fix.**

- If no pre-deadline notice was sent, Laseisk should not assume the conversion right is available for Year 1. Plan on the basis that Konais remains exclusive.
- For **Year 2** (ending February 28, 2027), calendar the 45-day deadline immediately (April 14, 2027) and set an internal trigger to send notice well in advance if the minimum is missed.
- In the next amendment, propose adding a mechanism that links the Minimum Annual Volume to the actual scope of the appointment (see Issue 1). If the scope is narrowed to franchised dealers in three states, the 1,200 minimum should be recalibrated, and the conversion trigger should be made automatic (no notice required) or the window extended.

## Issue 3 – Konais's "Outage Excuse" Has No Textual Basis in the Agreement

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, Recital B, §3.2; Distribution Agreement §9, §15; Service Level Schedule §4

**The problem.** Konais has argued that a Platform outage during Contract Year 1 "excuses" its failure to meet the 1,200 minimum. No provision of the Distribution Agreement conditions the Minimum Annual Volume on Platform availability. Section 15 (Force Majeure) excuses a party's "failure or delay in performance" caused by listed external events; a failure in Laseisk's own Platform is not a force majeure event. The Service Level Schedule provides Konais a remedy for SLA failures – service credits capped at 15% of monthly wholesale prices (§4.3) – but does not suspend or reduce the Minimum Annual Volume.

**Why it matters.** If Laseisk concedes the outage excuses the shortfall, it sets a precedent that Konais can invoke in future years, and it undermines Laseisk's leverage in the Year 2 planning discussion. The Amendment's §3.2 (requiring Konais to produce a written outage summary and affected-application list within 15 business days of May 20, 2026 – i.e., by approximately June 10, 2026) is the right procedural step; Laseisk should use it to test Konais's claim.

**Recommended position.**

- Maintain that the outage does not excuse the minimum. The correct remedy for an SLA failure is a service credit, which Konais could have claimed under SLS §4.4 (30-day claim window). Confirm whether Konais actually claimed a service credit for the outage month. If it did not, that further weakens its position.
- When Konais delivers its §3.2 summary, cross-reference against Laseisk's monitoring data (which Laseisk is obliged to make available under SLS §6.3). Quantify the actual downtime and assess whether it plausibly caused the 260-subscription gap.
- If the outage was genuinely severe (e.g., a multi-day Severity 1 event), Laseisk may wish to offer a *prospective* concession (e.g., a one-time reduction in the Year 2 minimum by a negotiated number) in exchange for Konais dropping the Year 1 argument and agreeing to the scope narrowing in Issue 1. Do not concede retroactively without getting something in return.

## Issue 4 – Three-Year Lock-In with Auto-Renewal

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §14.1

**The problem.** The initial term is three Contract Years (through February 28, 2028). It then renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either party gives 90 days' written notice of non-renewal. The first non-renewal notice deadline is **December 1, 2027**.

**Why it matters.** Combined with the broad exclusivity (Issue 1), Laseisk is locked in with Konais as its sole distribution channel for the entire US and Canada for at least three years, with the option to extend indefinitely. If the relationship underperforms, Laseisk's only levers are the conversion right (now likely waived for Year 1, see Issue 2) and termination for material breach (§14.2), which requires a 30-day cure period and a showing of materiality.

**Recommended fix.**

- Calendar the December 1, 2027 non-renewal deadline now.
- In the next amendment, negotiate either (a) a mutual right to terminate on 180 days' notice after Contract Year 2 if the Minimum Annual Volume is missed in two consecutive years, or (b) a "sunset" on the exclusivity: after Year 2, the appointment automatically becomes non-exclusive unless Konais meets a rising minimum.
- Ensure that any termination or non-renewal does not trigger the §14.4(c) trap (see Issue 7).

## Issue 5 – No Non-Solicitation Protection in the Distribution Agreement

**Documents:** Mutual NDA §8.1; Distribution Agreement (no equivalent provision)

**The problem.** The NDA's mutual non-solicitation clause (§8.1) ran for 12 months from November 4, 2024 and **expired on November 4, 2025**. The Distribution Agreement contains no non-solicitation or non-compete provision. Konais has been working closely with Laseisk's team since March 2025 and has visibility into Laseisk's operations, dealer relationships, and Platform roadmap (all Confidential Information under §12.1).

**Why it matters.** Konais (or its acquirer) can freely solicit Laseisk's employees starting now. Given that Konais is in the dealer-services space, poached Laseisk staff could take institutional knowledge to a competitor or to Konais's own direct-sales efforts.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add a mutual non-solicitation clause to the next amendment: 12–18 months post-termination, covering employees and contractors with whom the other party had material contact in connection with the Agreement.
- As an interim measure, ensure that sensitive operational information (roadmap, pricing models, dealer pipeline) is shared only on a need-to-know basis and is clearly marked as Confidential Information to preserve the §12 protections (which survive three years post-termination under §12.4).

## Issue 6 – Assignment to a Permitted Assignee Requires No Consent

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §16.2, §1.10

**The problem.** Either party may assign to a "Permitted Assignee" – defined as an Affiliate or any acquirer of "all or substantially all of its assets or of the business to which this Agreement relates" – without the other party's consent. Only 10 business days' post-assignment notice is required.

**Why it matters.** If Konais is acquired by a strategic competitor of Laseisk (e.g., another auction platform or a large dealer group building its own), the acquirer steps into Konais's shoes with full exclusive distribution rights. Laseisk has no approval right and no termination trigger. Conversely, if Laseisk is acquired, the new owner inherits the obligation to deal exclusively with Konais.

**Recommended fix.**

- Negotiate a "change of control" provision: if Konais (or its Permitted Assignee) comes under the control of a direct competitor of Laseisk's Platform business, Laseisk may terminate on 90 days' notice without penalty.
- Alternatively, require prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for assignment to any entity that operates a competing auction or dealer-subscription platform.

## Issue 7 – Post-Termination: Laseisk Cannot Immediately Go Direct to Existing Dealers

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §14.4(c)

**The problem.** On expiry or termination, "existing Dealer Subscriptions continue for the remainder of their current paid term, after which Supplier may contract directly with the Dealers." Since subscriptions are 12-month terms (Schedule A), a dealer who subscribed in March 2026 would not be available for direct contracting until March 2027 – even if Laseisk terminates in April 2026.

**Why it matters.** If Laseisk exits the relationship (by non-renewal, termination for breach, or mutual agreement), it cannot immediately re-engage its dealer base. There is a transition gap of up to 12 months during which Konais's dealers are still "locked in" to the Konais-distributed subscription and Laseisk cannot offer them a direct relationship.

**Recommended fix.**

- Negotiate a transition-assistance clause: on termination, Konais shall (a) provide Laseisk with a list of all active Dealer Subscriptions and their expiry dates within 10 business days; (b) not renew or extend any subscription without Laseisk's consent during a 90-day transition window; and (c) assign to Laseisk any unearned revenue from prepaid subscriptions.
- Alternatively, shorten the tail: existing subscriptions continue only for 90 days post-termination, after which Laseisk may contract directly.

## Issue 8 – Wholesale Prices Were Reduced by ~5% in the Amendment

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement, Schedule A; Amendment No. 1, §2.1

**The problem.** The Amendment reduced all three subscription tiers by approximately 5% (Buyer: $1,150 → $1,095; Seller: $1,350 → $1,285; Combined: $2,100 → $1,995), effective June 1, 2026. Section 2.4 confirms this was a negotiated revision under §17.4, not a Schedule A adjustment, so Laseisk retains its right to make one further 5% adjustment in the Contract Year beginning March 1, 2027.

**Why it matters.** Laseisk's revenue per subscription is now ~5% lower than at signing. If the minimum volume is not met (as in Year 1), the revenue impact is compounded. The 5% reduction was presumably the price Laseisk paid for Konais's agreement to the Amendment (which reserved the Year 1 dispute). Laseisk should factor this into its Year 2 financial model.

**Recommended position.**

- When exercising the 2027 adjustment right (notice due by January 1, 2027), consider whether to (a) restore prices to original levels (a ~4.8% increase), (b) hold flat, or (c) increase by the full 5%. Use the Year 2 volume performance and the outcome of the scope negotiation (Issue 1) as leverage.
- If the scope is narrowed (Issue 1), the lower price may be acceptable because Laseisk regains the ability to sell direct in the rest of the market at full retail.

## Issue 9 – 30-Day Notice for "Material" Changes to Subscription Features

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §4.1(d)

**The problem.** Laseisk must give Konais at least 30 days' prior written notice of "any material change to the Auction Access Subscription features or dealer terms of use." The term "material" is not defined.

**Why it matters.** Laseisk is a Platform business that iterates quickly. An undefined "materiality" threshold gives Konais an argument that any feature change it dislikes requires 30 days' notice and potentially opens a dispute. In the worst case, Konais could characterise a routine update as "material" and claim Laseisk is in breach for not giving notice.

**Recommended fix.**

- Propose a clarification: "material change" means a change that (a) reduces the scope of access or functionality available to a Dealer under its subscription, or (b) increases fees charged to the Dealer. Routine bug fixes, performance improvements, and additive features are excluded.
- Alternatively, shorten the notice period to 14 days for non-pricing changes.
