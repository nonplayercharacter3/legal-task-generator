## 1. Norrbeck Biologics Contract: Consent Not Obtained, No Price Adjustment, and Not a Closing Condition

**Where:** APA § 2.7; Schedule 4.7, item 1; Schedule 7.2(c); Integration Notes § 10.2

**Issue.** Schedule 4.7, item 1 identifies the Norrbeck Biologics Master Temperature-Controlled Storage Agreement (dated September 1, 2022) as an Assigned Contract that requires counterparty consent to assignment and states: "Consent has not been obtained as of the date of this Agreement." Section 2.7 provides that if consent has not been obtained by Closing, the contract "shall be deemed an Excluded Asset for all purposes of this Agreement, **without adjustment to the Purchase Price**."

Critically, the Norrbeck consent is **not** listed on Schedule 7.2(c) (Required Consents). Only the two landlord consents (Linden, NJ and Allentown, PA) appear there. Because § 7.2(c) makes each Required Consent a condition to Buyer's closing obligation, Teivav has **no right to walk** if the Norrbeck consent is not obtained. Teivav must close and pay the full $42 million even if it loses this customer.

The integration notes (§ 10.2) discuss post-closing notice obligations for the Refrigerated Transportation Services Agreement (Schedule 4.7, item 2) but do not mention the Norrbeck consent at all, suggesting the deal team is not actively tracking it.

**Why it matters.** The Norrbeck agreement is a "Master Temperature-Controlled Storage Agreement"—the core service line of the acquired Business. Losing it without a price reduction means Teivav pays for revenue it will not receive. The contract's anti-assignment clause also gives Norrbeck the right to terminate if assignment occurs without consent, compounding the risk.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add the Norrbeck consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent, making it a condition to Teivav's closing obligation. Alternatively, negotiate a specific price reduction (or escrow holdback) tied to the Norrbeck contract's trailing 12-month revenue if consent is not obtained.
- If Seller resists making it a closing condition, at minimum amend § 2.7 to provide: *"If any Assigned Contract identified on Schedule 4.7 as requiring counterparty consent has not been assigned as of the Closing, the Purchase Price shall be reduced by the amount of revenue generated under such contract in the twelve (12) months preceding the Closing Date, as set forth on Schedule 4.7."*
- Direct the deal team to confirm current status of the Norrbeck consent and add it to the integration action-item tracker.

## 2. Escrow Agreement Cap Silently Applies to Excluded Liabilities, Contradicting the APA

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 6.3; APA § 9.5(b); APA § 11.9

**Issue.** APA § 9.5(b) states: "The Cap shall not apply to Losses arising from Fraud, breach of Fundamental Representations, **and Excluded Liabilities**." This means Teivav's recovery for Excluded Liabilities (pre-closing environmental claims, cargo claims, tax liabilities, etc.) is theoretically uncapped.

However, Escrow Agreement § 6.3 states: "The Cap shall apply to **all claims under Section 9.2** of the Purchase Agreement other than claims for Fraud or breach of Fundamental Representations." Section 9.2(c) covers Excluded Liabilities. The Escrow Agreement's Cap provision does **not** carve out Excluded Liabilities.

APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence) resolves the conflict: "the Escrow Agreement shall control on indemnification claims and limits." Therefore, the more restrictive Escrow Agreement language governs, and Excluded Liabilities are subject to the $4.2 million Cap.

**Why it matters.** Excluded Liabilities include all pre-closing operational liabilities: cargo claims, temperature excursion claims, motor carrier violations, pre-closing environmental liabilities, and pre-closing taxes (APA § 2.4(a)–(f)). In a cold-chain business with ammonia refrigeration systems and a reefer fleet, a single significant cargo loss or environmental event could exceed $4.2 million. Under the Escrow Agreement as drafted, Teivav's maximum recovery for all such claims combined is $4.2 million—the same amount as the escrow itself. Once the escrow is released at 90 days, Teivav has no further secured recovery for Excluded Liabilities.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Escrow Agreement § 6.3 to conform to APA § 9.5(b):

> "The Cap shall apply to all claims under Section 9.2 of the Purchase Agreement other than claims for Fraud, breach of Fundamental Representations, **or Excluded Liabilities**."

If Seller resists, consider increasing the Escrow Amount or extending the Escrow Period to provide additional security for Excluded Liabilities claims.

## 3. Ninety-Day Escrow Period Is Inadequate for Environmental Representations

**Where:** APA § 9.1(b); Escrow Agreement § 1.1 ("Escrow Period"); APA § 4.12

**Issue.** APA § 9.1(b) provides that the Environmental Representations (APA § 4.12) survive "until the expiration of the Escrow Period." The Escrow Agreement defines the Escrow Period as **90 days** after the Closing Date (Escrow § 1.1). The escrow is released to Seller within five Business Days after that 90-day window (Escrow § 7.1).

Section 4.12 covers anhydrous ammonia refrigeration systems (RMP and PSM compliance), refrigerant leak records under 40 C.F.R. Part 82 Subpart F, and absence of reported releases. In a business operating multiple refrigerated facilities with ammonia systems, environmental issues (small leaks, record-keeping gaps, RMP deficiencies) often surface only after extended operation or upon regulatory inspection. Ninety days is insufficient to discover latent environmental conditions.

**Why it matters.** After 90 days, the escrow is gone and the Environmental Representations have expired. Teivav would have no contractual remedy for environmental non-compliance discovered in month four or later, even though the underlying liability (remediation, fines, third-party claims) may not manifest for years. The Excluded Liabilities provision (§ 2.4(f)) shifts pre-closing environmental liability to Seller, but Teivav's indemnity claim for that Excluded Liability is (i) subject to the Cap under Issue 2 above, and (ii) unsecured once the escrow is released.

**Recommended fix.**

- Extend the survival period for Environmental Representations. Proposed language for APA § 9.1(b): *"The Environmental Representations shall survive the Closing until the earlier of (i) the date that is three (3) years after the Closing Date, or (ii) the final resolution of any claim asserted in writing before such date."*
- If a longer survival period is agreed, either extend the Escrow Period correspondingly or negotiate a separate environmental escrow (e.g., $1 million held for 18 months) to secure the extended tail.
- As an alternative, require Seller to deliver a pre-closing environmental compliance certificate (RMP/PSM audit results, refrigerant leak log summary) as a Closing deliverable under § 3.5, giving Teivav a baseline to measure against.

## 4. Buyer Indemnifies Seller for Transferred Customer Contracts "Whether Arising Before or After the Closing"

**Where:** APA § 9.3(c); APA § 2.4(b); APA § 9.2(c)

**Issue.** APA § 9.3(c) provides that Buyer indemnifies Seller for Losses arising from "the Transferred Customer Contracts, **whether arising before or after the Closing**." Meanwhile, APA § 2.4(b) designates pre-closing customer contract claims (temperature excursions, spoiled product, service-level credits, chargebacks) as Excluded Liabilities, and § 9.2(c) requires Seller to indemnify Buyer for Excluded Liabilities.

This creates a circular or overlapping indemnity structure. If a customer asserts a claim for a pre-closing temperature excursion:

- The customer may sue Teivav (as the current contract holder). Teivav pays the customer.
- Teivav seeks indemnity from Seller under § 9.2(c) (Excluded Liability).
- But Seller can argue that § 9.3(c) requires Teivav to indemnify **Seller** for the same claim (because it arises under a Transferred Customer Contract and "whether arising before or after the Closing").
- Alternatively, if the customer sues Seller directly, § 9.3(c) requires Teivav to indemnify Seller.

**Why it matters.** In the worst case, Teivav is on the hook for pre-closing customer contract claims regardless of which party the customer sues, and Seller's Excluded Liability indemnity to Teivav is subject to the Cap (see Issue 2). The practical effect is that Teivav bears the economic risk of pre-closing service failures under customer contracts.

**Recommended fix.** Amend APA § 9.3(c) to exclude pre-closing matters:

> "(c) the Transferred Customer Contracts, **to the extent arising from performance or non-performance after the Closing Date**."

This aligns § 9.3(c) with the Assumed Liabilities / Excluded Liabilities split in §§ 2.3–2.4 and eliminates the circularity.

## 5. Insurance Gap: No Coverage for Pre-Closing Acts Between Seller's Excluded Policies and Teivav's New Program

**Where:** APA § 2.2(c); APA § 2.4(a)–(e); Integration Notes § 9.1

**Issue.** Seller's insurance policies are Excluded Assets (APA § 2.2(c)). Teivav will bind its own program effective 12:01 a.m. on the Closing Date (Integration Notes § 9.1). Standard commercial policies (auto liability, motor truck cargo, warehouse legal liability) contain exclusions for acts or omissions occurring before the policy inception date.

This creates a gap: if a pre-closing incident (e.g., a cargo spoilage event, a fleet accident, a warehouse fire) gives rise to a claim that is filed or discovered after Closing, (i) Seller's policies are not available to Teivav (they are Excluded Assets and remain with Seller), and (ii) Teivav's new policies will not cover the pre-closing act. Teivav must pay the claim out of pocket and then seek indemnity from Seller under § 9.2(c) (Excluded Liability)—subject to the Cap (Issue 2) and the practical difficulty of collecting from Seller post-closing.

**Why it matters.** In a cold-chain business, cargo claims and temperature excursion claims are frequent and can be significant. The gap means Teivav is self-insuring for pre-closing events with no policy backing, relying solely on Seller's unsecured (post-escrow) indemnity.

**Recommended fix.**

- Require Seller to maintain its existing insurance policies (or obtain tail coverage) for a period of at least 12 months post-Closing, with Teivav named as an additional insured or loss payee for pre-closing acts. Add as a new APA § 3.5 deliverable: *"Certificates of insurance evidencing Seller's maintenance of [specified coverages] for a period of twelve (12) months following the Closing Date, with Buyer named as additional insured for acts or omissions occurring prior to the Closing Date."*
- Alternatively, negotiate a specific insurance escrow or holdback from the Purchase Price to cover the gap period.
- Confirm with Teivav's broker whether the new program can be structured to include a "prior acts" endorsement covering pre-closing operations for the first policy period.

## 6. Key Employee Agreement Defines "Business" More Narrowly Than the APA, Weakening the Non-Compete

**Where:** Key Employee Agreement § 1.1; APA § 1.1; Key Employee Agreement § 6.1(a)–(b)

**Issue.** The APA defines "Business" as "temperature-controlled warehousing, refrigerated and frozen freight transportation, and cross-dock services" (APA § 1.1). The Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" as only "the operation of temperature-controlled warehouse facilities" (Key Employee Agt. § 1.1).

The non-compete in § 6.1(a) prohibits the Executive from engaging in "the Business" within the Restricted Territory. The customer non-solicit in § 6.1(b) covers "any customer of the Business." Under the Key Employee Agreement's narrower definition, the Executive could arguably:

- Compete in refrigerated transportation or cross-dock services (not "temperature-controlled warehouse facilities"); and
- Solicit customers who use only the transportation or cross-dock services (not "customers of the Business" as narrowly defined).

**Why it matters.** Teivav is paying $42 million for a three-line business (warehousing, transportation, cross-dock). The Key Employee (former COO of Seller) presumably has relationships and knowledge across all three lines. If the non-compete does not protect the transportation and cross-dock lines, the Executive could join a competitor in those segments and take customer relationships.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Key Employee Agreement § 1.1 to conform to the APA:

> "**Business**" means temperature-controlled warehousing, refrigerated and frozen freight transportation, and cross-dock services, as conducted by Seller as of the date of the Purchase Agreement.

## 7. Software License Transferability Unconfirmed; Potential Gap in Purchased Assets

**Where:** APA § 2.1(f); APA § 4.8; Integration Notes § 6.1–6.2

**Issue.** APA § 2.1(f) includes "all intellectual property, software licenses and warehouse management system data used in the Business" as Purchased Assets. Section 4.8 represents that the Purchased Assets "constitute all of the assets necessary to conduct the Business as currently conducted." However, the integration team has not yet confirmed whether the WMS and routing software licenses are actually transferable (Integration Notes § 6.2, action item due April 10, 2026).

If a material software license (e.g., the WMS) is non-transferable, Teivav acquires the data but not the software. It would need to procure a replacement, incurring unbudgeted cost and potential operational disruption during the 90-day transition period described in Integration Notes § 6.1(a).

**Why it matters.** The WMS is operationally critical for a warehousing business. If the license is tied to Seller's entity (e.g., a per-company subscription that does not permit assignment), Teivav may face a service gap or must pay a premium for an emergency replacement license.

**Recommended fix.**

- Before Closing, require Seller to deliver written confirmation from each software vendor that the license is assignable to Buyer, or to obtain vendor consent as a condition to Closing.
- If a key license is non-transferable, negotiate a specific price reduction or require Seller to fund a replacement license as a Closing deliverable.
- Add to APA § 3.5: *"Written evidence of assignability or vendor consent for each material software license included in the Purchased Assets."*

**Open question:** What is the identity and materiality of the WMS and routing software licenses? The integration notes do not specify vendor names or contract values. Need client confirmation.

## 8. Schedule 4.7 Identifies Customer Contracts by Data Room Reference Rather Than by Name

**Where:** Schedule 4.7, items 2–5

**Issue.** Schedule 4.7 lists Material Contracts, but items 2 through 5 identify the counterparty only as "Customer listed in data room folder 4.7.X" or "Equipment lessor listed in data room folder 4.7.X." Only item 1 (Norrbeck Biologics) names the counterparty.

**Why it matters.** In a dispute over whether a particular contract is an Assigned Contract (and thus subject to the § 2.7 non-assignment rule or the § 9.3(c) indemnity), the lack of a named counterparty creates ambiguity. A data room folder reference is not a fixed identifier—it depends on the state of the data room at a given time and is not incorporated into the agreement by reference.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Schedule 4.7 to identify each contract by the legal name of the counterparty and, where applicable, the specific contract number or effective date. If the parties intend to keep certain customer names confidential, use a defined code (e.g., "Customer A") and attach a separate confidential schedule accessible only to the parties and their counsel.

## 9. No Remedy if Key Employee Does Not Report for Employment at Closing

**Where:** APA § 10.2; Key Employee Agreement § 2.1; APA § 7.2 (no condition tied to Key Employee)

**Issue.** The Key Employee Agreement becomes effective on the Closing Date (Key Employee Agt. § 2.1). Between signing (March 3, 2026) and Closing (targeted late May 2026), the Executive remains employed by Seller. Nothing in the APA or the Key Employee Agreement obligates the Executive to actually report to Teivav on the Closing Date, nor does the APA make the Executive's commencement of employment a condition to Closing.

If the Executive resigns from Seller before Closing and simply does not show up for Teivav, or if Seller terminates the Executive pre-Closing, Teivav has no contractual remedy against either party. The Key Employee Agreement is void if the APA is terminated (Key Employee Agt. § 2.1), but there is no analogous provision if the Executive simply fails to commence employment after Closing.

**Why it matters.** The Executive is the former COO of Seller with direct knowledge of operations, customers, and the fleet. The $250,000 retention bonus (Key Employee Agt. § 3.3) is structured to keep him for two years post-Closing, but provides no protection if he never starts.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add a condition to Buyer's closing obligation in APA § 7.2: *"The Key Employee shall have executed the Key Employee Agreement and shall commence employment with Buyer effective as of the Closing Date."*
- Alternatively, add a specific indemnity or liquidated damages provision in the Key Employee Agreement for failure to commence employment: *"If the Executive fails to commence employment on the Effective Date without good cause, the Executive shall pay to the Company $[●] as liquidated damages, which the parties agree is a reasonable estimate of the Company's harm and not a penalty."*

**Open question:** How material is the Key Employee to the value Teivav is paying for? If his relationships and institutional knowledge represent a significant portion of the $42 million price, the absence of a commencement condition is more acute.

## 10. Permits Transferred Only "To the Extent Transferable" With No Fallback

**Where:** APA § 2.1(d); APA § 4.10

**Issue.** Section 2.1(d) includes "all permits, licenses and registrations held for the Business, **to the extent transferable**" in the Purchased Assets. Section 4.10 represents that Seller "holds all permits required to conduct the Business" but does not represent that all such permits are transferable.

If a required permit (e.g., a state environmental permit for the ammonia systems, a local operating permit, or a health department permit) is non-transferable, Teivav must apply for a new one. There is no representation that the new permit will be granted, no covenant that Seller will cooperate in the transfer application, and no price adjustment if the permit is not obtained.

**Why it matters.** Operating temperature-controlled facilities with ammonia refrigeration systems typically requires multiple environmental and operational permits. If a key permit cannot be transferred and the replacement application is pending, Teivav could face an operational shutdown or regulatory exposure on Day One.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add to APA § 6.5 (Further Assurances): *"Seller shall cooperate in good faith with Buyer's applications for any permit, license or registration that is not transferable by assignment, including executing such documents and providing such information as reasonably requested."*
- Require Seller to deliver a permit inventory at Closing identifying which permits are transferable and which require re-application, with estimated timelines.
- Consider making the transfer (or re-application) of specified critical permits a condition to Closing.

**Open question:** Which specific permits are required for the Linden and Allentown facilities, and which are entity-specific (non-transferable)? Need client/EHS confirmation.

## 11. General Representations Survive 12 Months But Escrow Security Lasts Only 90 Days

**Where:** APA § 9.1(a); Escrow Agreement § 1.1, § 7.1

**Issue.** General representations and warranties survive for 12 months post-Closing (APA § 9.1(a)). The Escrow Period is 90 days (Escrow § 1.1), after which the escrow is released to Seller (Escrow § 7.1). For the remaining nine months of the survival period, Teivav's indemnity claim against Seller is unsecured.

**Why it matters.** If Seller distributes its remaining assets to shareholders post-Closing (a common occurrence after a major asset sale), Teivav may find itself with a valid indemnity claim but no ability to collect. The 90-day escrow provides security only for claims identified and asserted within that window.

**Recommended fix.**

- Extend the Escrow Period to at least 12 months to align with the general survival period. If Seller resists a full 12-month escrow, consider a tiered release: 50% at 90 days, 50% at 12 months.
- Alternatively, require Seller to maintain a minimum net worth or liquidity covenant post-Closing for the duration of the survival period.
- At minimum, confirm that Seller will not distribute proceeds of the sale to shareholders for 12 months post-Closing (a "no-distribution" covenant).

## 12. Officer Certificate in § 7.2(d) Omits Conditions (c) and (f)

**Where:** APA § 7.2(d)

**Issue.** Section 7.2(d) requires delivery of "a certificate of an officer of Seller certifying satisfaction of the conditions in Sections 7.2(a), (b) and (e)." It omits § 7.2(c) (Required Consents) and § 7.2(f) (delivery of Closing items).

While the omission may be intentional (consents and deliverables are self-evident from the documents themselves), it creates a technical gap: if a Required Consent is defective or a Closing deliverable is missing, there is no officer certification addressing it, and the condition may be harder to enforce.

**Recommended fix.** Amend § 7.2(d) to read: *"Buyer shall have received a certificate of an officer of Seller certifying satisfaction of the conditions in Sections 7.2(a), (b), (c), (e) and (f)."*
