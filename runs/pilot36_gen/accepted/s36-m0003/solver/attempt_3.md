## Issues Ranked by Importance

### 1. NON-RENEWAL NOTICE APPEARS UNTIMELY — DRULUN MAY BE LOCKED INTO A TWO-YEAR RENEWAL

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 2.2; Order Form No. 3, Recital C

**What the documents say:**

Section 2.2 requires a non-renewal notice to be given "at least 180 days before expiry of the then-current term." The Initial Term runs three years from February 1, 2024, expiring **January 31, 2027**. Counting back 180 days from January 31, 2027 yields a deadline of **August 4, 2026**.

Order Form No. 3, Recital C states: "Drulun delivered to Pruvax a written notice of non-renewal of the Supply Agreement under Section 2.2 of the Supply Agreement, **dated August 14, 2026**." That is ten days after the deadline.

Recital C further provides: "Each Party reserves all rights regarding the effectiveness of the Non-Renewal Notice and the expiry or continuation of the Term."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement auto-renews for a two-year Renewal Term (through January 31, 2029). Drulun would remain bound to the 60,000-vial Annual Minimum (Exhibit B, ¶ 2) for two additional Contract Years, with a 35% shortfall charge (Exhibit B, ¶ 3) on any year it orders less. At the current $412/vial price, the shortfall exposure on a single unmet year is approximately **$861,600** (60,000 × $412 × 35%). Over two renewal years, that is over $1.7 million in potential shortfall liability on top of the cost of product Drulun may not want.

Section 17.6 provides that a notice "is effective on receipt." If Drulun can show the notice was physically received by Pruvax on or before August 4, 2026 (e.g., sent by overnight courier on August 3), it may still be timely. The date on the face of the notice (August 14) is strong evidence against Drulun, but receipt controls.

**Recommendation / Action needed:**

- **Drulun must confirm immediately** how the notice was sent and when Pruvax actually received it. Pull the courier tracking record or email timestamp. If it was received by August 4, the notice is likely effective and this issue dissolves.
- If it was received after August 4, Drulun should open a discussion with Pruvax. The "reservation of rights" language in Recital C suggests Pruvax is aware of the problem and has not yet asserted the notice is void. Drulun should seek a written confirmation from Pruvax that the non-renewal is effective, or negotiate a mutual termination of the Renewal Term.
- If Pruvax asserts the renewal is in effect, Drulun's contractual exit options are limited: termination for material breach (§ 16.1, 60-day cure), regulatory termination (§ 16.3, only if FDA action or permanent discontinuation), or insolvency (§ 16.2). None are clean. A negotiated resolution is strongly preferred.
- **This needs a decision from Drulun this week.**

### 2. ORDER FORM NO. 3 REDEFINES "DELIVERY" AS FCA PRUVAX FACILITY — SHIFTS ALL TRANSIT RISK TO DRULUN

**Documents:** Supply Agreement §§ 1.7, 5.3; Order Form No. 3, § 4.1; Supply Agreement § 17.3

**What the documents say:**

Supply Agreement § 1.7 defines "Delivery" as "receipt of Product at Drulun's designated distribution center in Edison, New Jersey." Section 5.3 then provides that "Title to Product and risk of loss of or damage to Product pass from Pruvax to Drulun **on Delivery**," and that "[u]ntil Delivery, Pruvax bears all risk of loss of, damage to, or temperature excursion affecting the Product, **including during transit**."

Order Form No. 3, § 4.1 overrides this: "In this Order Form, 'Delivery' means **tender to the first carrier at Pruvax's facility** at 2100 Ellis Road, Durham, North Carolina 27703, **FCA Pruvax Facility (Incoterms 2020)**."

Under § 17.3 (Order of Precedence), the Order Form controls over the Supply Agreement on "delivery terms." The FCA definition therefore governs.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

The Product is a refrigerated oncology injectable that must be maintained at 2°C to 8°C (Exhibit A, ¶ 2). Under the FCA definition:

- Title and risk of loss pass to Drulun the moment the carrier picks up the shipment in Durham, NC.
- If the product freezes, is exposed to heat, or is otherwise damaged in transit between Durham and Edison, NJ, **Drulun bears the loss**.
- Drulun's rejection right under § 6.2 (temperature excursion outside the § 5.1 range) still exists, but Drulun would be rejecting product it already owns and for which it has already assumed the risk. The practical remedy is a replacement or credit under § 6.4, but Drulun has lost the cleaner position of "this never became my problem."
- The § 5.4 late-delivery cancellation right (cancel if >30 days late "for reasons within Pruvax's control") becomes harder to invoke, because once the carrier has the product, delays may be characterized as outside Pruvax's control.

This appears to be a significant departure from the negotiated baseline. The November 2023 draft (which matches the executed Supply Agreement) used the Edison receipt definition throughout. There is no indication in the draft that FCA was ever discussed.

**Recommendation / Action needed:**

- **Drulun should not sign any further Order Forms with the FCA delivery definition without pushing back.**
- For Order Form No. 3 specifically (already signed), Drulun should raise this with Pruvax and seek either (a) a side letter restoring the Supply Agreement delivery definition for OF3, or (b) at minimum, a Pruvax commitment to bear transit risk for cold-chain excursions regardless of the FCA title passage.
- Going forward, Drulun should insist that any Order Form conform to the Supply Agreement's delivery definition unless there is a specific, documented business reason for FCA. If Pruvax insists on FCA for commercial reasons (e.g., carrier booking), the risk allocation in § 5.3 should be expressly preserved: "Notwithstanding the FCA delivery definition, Pruvax shall remain responsible for temperature excursions occurring in transit."
- **This needs a decision from Drulun before the next Order Form is negotiated.**

### 3. PRICE ADJUSTMENT NOTICE APPEARS TO VIOLATE THE 90-DAY ADVANCE NOTICE REQUIREMENT

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 4.2; Order Form No. 3, § 3.1

**What the documents say:**

Section 4.2: "Once per Contract Year, **beginning with the second Contract Year**, Pruvax may adjust prices by giving Drulun **at least 90 days' written notice**."

Order Form No. 3, § 3.1: "The price for each finished vial is US$412.00. This price reflects the adjustment notified by Pruvax under Section 4.2 of the Supply Agreement **on May 15, 2026**, effective for the **Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026**."

The third Contract Year began February 1, 2026. The notice was given May 15, 2026 — **104 days after** the Contract Year it was supposed to govern had already started. The 90-day advance notice would have required the notice by approximately **November 3, 2025**.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

Drulun signed an Order Form at $412/vial incorporating a price adjustment that Pruvax was not contractually entitled to impose on that timeline. The total Order Form value is $8,240,000. If the prior-year price was, say, $380/vial (a typical single-digit PPI adjustment), the overcharge on OF3 alone could be in the range of $500,000–$600,000.

Complicating factors:
- By signing OF3 at $412, Drulun may have arguably accepted the price, weakening a later challenge.
- However, § 4.2 is a condition on Pruvax's right to adjust. If the condition wasn't met, the adjustment may be void, and the prior price continues to apply.
- The "reservation of rights" language in Recital C of OF3 (though directed at the non-renewal notice) signals that the parties are in a tense negotiating posture.

**Recommendation / Action needed:**

- Confirm the prior Contract Year unit price (from Order Forms 1 and 2 or the applicable Exhibit B pricing) to quantify the delta.
- Raise the issue with Pruvax in the same conversation as the non-renewal and delivery-definition issues. Frame it as: "The § 4.2 notice was not given 90 days before the effective date; we believe the prior price continues to apply to OF3."
- If Pruvax resists, assess whether this constitutes a material breach supporting termination under § 16.1 (unlikely on its own) or whether it strengthens Drulun's negotiating position on the non-renewal issue.
- **Decision needed:** whether to assert the price adjustment was ineffective or to accept $412 as part of a broader settlement of the non-renewal issue.

### 4. ANNUAL MINIMUM COUNTING SHIFTS WITH THE REDEFINED "DELIVERY"

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 3.4; Order Form No. 3, §§ 2.4, 4.1

**What the documents say:**

Section 3.4: Drulun must "order and accept" at least the Annual Minimum "in each Contract Year." Order Form No. 3, § 2.4: "Quantities ordered under this Order Form count toward the Annual Minimum for the Contract Year **in which the applicable Batch is Delivered**."

Because OF3 redefines "Delivery" as FCA tender (Issue #2), the Contract Year in which a batch counts toward the minimum is determined by when the carrier picks it up in Durham, not when Drulun receives it in Edison.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

The OF3 delivery dates are January 11, February 8, March 8, and April 12, 2027. Under the Supply Agreement definition, the January batch would fall in Contract Year 3 (ending January 31, 2027) and the remaining three in Contract Year 4. Under the FCA definition with the § 4.5 early-delivery window (5 business days before the requested date), the January batch could be "Delivered" as early as January 4, 2027 — still CY3. The practical difference is narrow for OF3 specifically, but the principle matters for future Order Forms near Contract Year boundaries.

More importantly, if the non-renewal is ineffective and the Agreement renews, Drulun faces 60,000-vial minimums in CY4 (Feb 2027–Jan 2028) and CY5 (Feb 2028–Jan 2029). OF3's 20,000 vials would count toward CY4 (or split between CY3 and CY4 depending on exact delivery timing), leaving a 40,000-vial gap to fill or a shortfall charge to pay.

**Recommendation:**

Address as part of the broader non-renewal negotiation. If Drulun is exiting, the Annual Minimum issue becomes moot. If it is locked in, Drulun needs a supply plan for the remaining 40,000 vials per year or a negotiated reduction of the minimum.

### 5. SPLIT GOVERNING LAW AND FORUM: NEW YORK (SUPPLY AGREEMENT) vs. DELAWARE (DPA)

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 15.1; DPA §§ 12.1–12.2

**What the documents say:**

Supply Agreement § 15.1: New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, NY.
DPA § 12.1: Delaware law, expressly "notwithstanding Section 15.1 of the Supply Agreement."
DPA § 12.2: Exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware state and federal courts.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

If a dispute involves both supply-chain issues and data-protection issues (e.g., a Security Incident that also involves a product complaint handling failure), the parties could end up in parallel proceedings in different states under different laws. This increases cost and creates the risk of inconsistent outcomes.

That said, Delaware is Drulun's state of incorporation, and the DPA's Delaware forum is not unfavorable to Drulun. The practical risk is moderate.

**Recommendation:**

Not worth reopening the DPA now. If a data-protection dispute arises, Drulun is comfortable in Delaware. Note the issue for any future renegotiation: consider conforming the DPA to New York law and forum to avoid split proceedings.

### 6. DPA SECURITY INCIDENT NOTIFICATION (48 HOURS) IS LONGER THAN DRULUN MAY NEED FOR REGULATORY RESPONSES

**Documents:** DPA § 6.1; Supply Agreement § 8.2

**What the documents say:**

DPA § 6.1: Pruvax must notify Drulun of a Security Incident "without undue delay, and in any event **within 48 hours**."
Supply Agreement § 8.2: Pruvax must forward adverse event reports "within **one business day** of receipt, or within **24 hours** for serious adverse events."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

If a Security Incident involves exposure of patient data in adverse event reports, Drulun may have its own notification clocks running under state breach laws (many require notification within 30–60 days of discovery, but the clock starts when Drulun "knows or should know"). A 48-hour outer limit from Pruvax is workable but leaves little margin. The 24-hour standard for serious adverse events in § 8.2 is tighter and more appropriate for the health-data context.

**Recommendation:**

In any DPA amendment (e.g., triggered by a new subprocessor or regulatory change), seek to tighten the Security Incident notification to 24 hours, matching the serious-adverse-event standard. Low urgency; address in the next amendment cycle.

### 7. DPA SCHEDULE 3 LISTED NO SUBPROCESSORS AT INCEPTION — MONITOR FOR ADDITIONS

**Documents:** DPA § 5.1, Schedule 3

**What the documents say:**

Schedule 3: "None as of the DPA Effective Date."
Section 5.1: Pruvax must give 30 days' prior written notice before engaging any new subprocessor. Drulun may object on reasonable data protection grounds.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

A biologics manufacturer almost certainly uses third-party IT providers, cloud hosting, or serialization platform vendors. If Pruvax has already engaged subprocessors without notice, that would be a DPA breach. If it has given notice and Drulun did not object within the 30-day window, the subprocessor is authorized.

**Recommendation:**

- Ask Pruvax to confirm in writing whether any subprocessors have been engaged since the DPA Effective Date and, if so, provide the § 5.1 notices that were given.
- Calendar any 30-day objection windows if new subprocessor notices arrive.
- Low urgency, but a quick diligence item.

### 8. NO MANDATORY PRICE DECREASE IF PPI IS NEGATIVE

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 4.2

**What the documents say:**

Section 4.2: "Pruvax **may** adjust prices by giving Drulun at least 90 days' written notice. No adjustment may exceed the percentage change in the [PPI] over the preceding twelve months."

The word "may" makes the adjustment permissive. If the PPI declines, Pruvax is not obligated to reduce prices; it simply cannot increase them by more than the (negative) PPI change, which effectively means no increase.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

In a deflationary period for pharmaceutical manufacturing inputs, Drulun would pay the same price while Pruvax's costs fall. This is a modest economic point, not a legal defect.

**Recommendation:**

If renegotiating (e.g., as part of resolving Issues 1–3), consider adding: "If the PPI change is negative, Pruvax shall reduce prices by the same percentage." Low priority.
