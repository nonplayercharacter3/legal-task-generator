## Issue 1 – Non-Renewal Notice Appears Nine to Ten Days Late

**Documents:** Supply Agreement §2.2; Order Form No. 3, Recital C

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §2.2 provides that the Initial Term (three years from the February 1, 2024 Effective Date) renews automatically for successive two-year periods unless a Party gives written non-renewal notice "at least 180 days before expiry of the then-current term."
- The Initial Term expires on February 1, 2027 (the three-year anniversary). Counting back 180 days from February 1, 2027 yields **August 5, 2026** as the deadline.
- Order Form No. 3, Recital C states: "Drulun delivered to Pruvax a written notice of non-renewal of the Supply Agreement under Section 2.2 of the Supply Agreement, dated **August 14, 2026**."
- Recital C further provides: "Each Party reserves all rights regarding the effectiveness of the Non-Renewal Notice and the expiry or continuation of the Term. Signing this Order Form waives no such right."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

The August 14 notice is **9 days late** (or 10 days late if the term is read as expiring January 31, 2027). If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement auto-renews for a two-year Renewal Term through **January 31, 2029**, locking Drulun into the Annual Minimum (60,000 vials/year), the 35% shortfall charge, and all other commercial terms for two additional years. The "reserves all rights" language in Recital C confirms both parties are aware of the dispute but have not resolved it.

**Recommended fix:**

Drulun should decide promptly among the following options:

1. **Seek Pruvax's written confirmation** that the non-renewal notice is effective despite the 9-day gap (e.g., by estoppel, course of dealing, or mutual assent). This is the cleanest outcome if Pruvax is amenable.
2. **Argue the term expires January 31, 2027** (end of the third contract year rather than the anniversary date), which would make the deadline August 4, 2026 — this makes the problem worse, so this argument should not be advanced.
3. **Accept the renewal** and plan to give timely non-renewal notice before the next deadline (180 days before January 31, 2029 = approximately August 4, 2028).
4. **Negotiate a mutual amendment** that confirms the term will expire January 31, 2027 regardless of the notice timing.

> **⚠️ NEEDS QUICK DECISION FROM DRULUN.** The parties are already in a position of performing under the Order Form (Recital C acknowledges the dispute). The longer this remains unresolved, the stronger Pruvax's argument for renewal by acquiescence. Recommend Drulun instruct within the next two weeks.

## Issue 2 – Order Form No. 3 Redefines "Delivery" as FCA at Pruvax's Facility, Shifting Transit Risk to Drulun

**Documents:** Order Form No. 3 §4.1; Supply Agreement §§1.7, 5.1, 5.3

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §1.7 defines "Delivery" as "receipt of Product at Drulun's designated distribution center in Edison, New Jersey."
- Supply Agreement §5.3 provides that "Title to Product and risk of loss of or damage to Product pass from Pruvax to Drulun on Delivery." Under the Supply Agreement definition, risk passes when the product arrives at Drulun's DC.
- Order Form No. 3 §4.1 states: "In this Order Form, 'Delivery' means **tender to the first carrier at Pruvax's facility** at 2100 Ellis Road, Durham, North Carolina 27703, **FCA Pruvax Facility (Incoterms 2020)**. 'Delivered' has a corresponding meaning."
- Supply Agreement §17.3(b) gives the Order Form precedence over the Supply Agreement "on Products, quantities, pricing and **delivery terms**."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

This is a fundamental shift in risk allocation for a **refrigerated oncology injectable** that must be maintained at 2°C–8°C. Under Order Form No. 3:

- Title and risk of loss pass to Drulun the moment the product is loaded onto the carrier at Pruvax's dock in Durham, NC — not when it arrives in Edison, NJ.
- If a temperature excursion occurs in transit, Drulun bears the loss. Pruvax's obligation under §5.1 to use a qualified cold-chain carrier remains, but the *risk* of failure has moved to Drulun.
- The 30-day rejection window in Supply Agreement §6.2 runs from "Delivery" — meaning the clock starts at Pruvax's dock, potentially before the product has even arrived. Drulun could lose the right to reject a temperature-excursed shipment if it takes more than 30 days from tender to discover the problem.
- The late-delivery remedy in §5.4 (cancellation if more than 30 days late) is measured from Pruvax's facility, not Drulun's receiving dock.

**Recommended fix:**

1. **Primary:** Seek to amend Order Form No. 3 §4.1 to restore the Supply Agreement definition of Delivery (receipt at Drulun's Edison DC), or at minimum to provide that risk of loss for temperature excursions in transit remains with Pruvax.
2. **Alternative (if Pruvax resists):** Add a specific provision that Pruvax remains liable for temperature excursions occurring in transit despite the FCA risk shift, and extend the §6.2 rejection period to run from actual receipt at Drulun's DC rather than FCA tender.
3. **Insurance check:** Confirm Drulun's cargo/in-transit insurance covers refrigerated pharmaceutical product for the Durham-to-Edison lane and that the policy limits are adequate for an $8.24M order.

> **⚠️ NEEDS QUICK DECISION FROM DRULUN.** Order Form No. 3 is already signed. If Drulun wants to correct this, it should raise the issue with Pruvax before the first batch (OF3-01, due January 11, 2027) is shipped. The longer Drulun accepts performance under the FCA term, the harder it will be to argue the term was a drafting error.

## Issue 3 – Price Adjustment Notice Timing and Potential Retroactivity

**Documents:** Order Form No. 3 §3.1; Supply Agreement §4.2

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §4.2: "Once per Contract Year, beginning with the second Contract Year, Pruvax may adjust prices by giving Drulun **at least 90 days' written notice**."
- Order Form No. 3 §3.1: "The price for each finished vial is US$412.00. This price reflects the adjustment notified by Pruvax under Section 4.2 of the Supply Agreement on **May 15, 2026**, effective for the Contract Year beginning **February 1, 2026**."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

Contract Year 3 began February 1, 2026. The 90-day notice given on May 15, 2026 would make the new price effective no earlier than approximately **August 13, 2026**. Yet the Order Form characterizes the price as "effective for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026" — language that suggests the $412.00 rate was meant to apply from the start of CY3, i.e., retroactively for nearly four months. If Drulun paid a lower rate on any orders placed between February 1 and August 13, 2026, Pruvax may attempt to bill the difference. Alternatively, if the $412.00 rate is simply the "CY3 rate" going forward (applicable to orders placed after the 90-day period), the language is imprecise but not substantively problematic.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Confirm with Drulun's commercial team whether any Order Forms were signed between February 1, 2026 and August 13, 2026 at a pre-adjustment price. If so, assess Pruvax's exposure to a retroactive true-up claim.
2. In the next discussion with Pruvax, seek clarification that the $412.00 rate applies only to Order Forms signed on or after the date the 90-day notice period expired (i.e., on or after August 13, 2026), and that no retroactive adjustment applies to previously signed Order Forms.

## Issue 4 – Batches Delivering After Initial Term Expiry Create Annual Minimum Ambiguity

**Documents:** Order Form No. 3 §§2.2, 2.4; Supply Agreement §§2.2, 2.3, 3.4; Exhibit B ¶2

**What the documents say:**

- Order Form No. 3 schedules four batches for delivery between January 11, 2027 and April 12, 2027.
- Only OF3-01 (January 11, 2027) falls within the Initial Term (which expires January 31 or February 1, 2027).
- OF3-02, OF3-03, and OF3-04 (February–April 2027) deliver after the Initial Term expires.
- Order Form No. 3 §2.4: "Quantities ordered under this Order Form count toward the Annual Minimum for the Contract Year in which the applicable Batch is Delivered."
- Supply Agreement §3.4 and Exhibit B ¶2: Annual Minimum is 60,000 vials per Contract Year.
- Supply Agreement §2.3: Order Forms signed before expiry remain binding after expiry.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

If the non-renewal is effective (see Issue 1), the Agreement expires in early 2027 and there is no Contract Year 4. The 15,000 vials delivering in February–April 2027 have no Contract Year in which to count toward the Annual Minimum. This creates ambiguity about:

- Whether the Annual Minimum shortfall calculation for CY3 (60,000 vials) is affected by the fact that 15,000 of the 20,000 ordered under OF3 deliver outside CY3.
- Whether the shortfall rate (35% of unit price, Exhibit B ¶3) applies to the CY3 shortfall if Drulun's total CY3 deliveries (counting only OF3-01's 5,000 vials from this Order Form, plus any other CY3 orders) fall below 60,000.
- The legal framework governing post-expiry performance (Supply Agreement §2.3 preserves the Order Form but the Annual Minimum mechanism in §3.4 is tied to "each Contract Year during the Term").

**Recommended fix:**

1. In the non-renewal discussion (Issue 1), seek a side letter or amendment confirming that:
   - The 20,000 vials under Order Form No. 3 count toward the CY3 Annual Minimum regardless of actual delivery date, **or**
   - The Annual Minimum for CY3 is reduced by the 15,000 vials that will deliver post-expiry.
2. Confirm Drulun's total CY3 orders (all Order Forms) against the 60,000-vial minimum to quantify any potential shortfall exposure (35% × $412 × shortfall vials).

## Issue 5 – Erroneous PPI Index Number in Price Adjustment Cap

**Documents:** Supply Agreement §4.2

**What the documents say:**

- §4.2 caps any price adjustment at "the percentage change in the Producer Price Index for Pharmaceutical Preparation Manufacturing (**PCU325412325412**) over the preceding twelve months."
- The actual BLS index for Pharmaceutical Preparation Manufacturing is **PCU325412** (a 6-digit code). The 12-digit number in the Agreement appears to be a drafting error (possibly a duplicated string).

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

If Pruvax invokes a price adjustment and cites the (correct) PCU325412 index, Drulun could argue the contractual cap references a non-existent index and is therefore inoperable — potentially voiding the cap entirely or, conversely, giving Pruvax an argument that the cap is meaningless and no limit applies. This cuts both ways but creates unnecessary uncertainty.

**Recommended fix:**

Raise this as a cleanup item in the next amendment or side letter with Pruvax. Propose correcting the index number to PCU325412. Low urgency unless a price adjustment dispute arises, but easy to fix now while the parties are already in negotiation on other points.

## Issue 6 – DPA and Supply Agreement Governed by Different States' Law

**Documents:** DPA §§12.1–12.2; Supply Agreement §15.1; Supply Agreement §17.3(a)

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §15.1: New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in New York County courts.
- DPA §12.1: "This DPA … is governed by the laws of the State of **Delaware** … This Section 12.1 applies **notwithstanding Section 15.1** of the Supply Agreement."
- DPA §12.2: Exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware state and federal courts.
- Supply Agreement §17.3(a): The DPA takes precedence "on Personal Data matters."

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

Any dispute involving both data protection and commercial/quality issues (e.g., a Security Incident that also involves a supply failure) could require parallel proceedings in two different jurisdictions under two different bodies of law. This increases cost and creates strategic complexity. The split appears deliberate (the DPA expressly overrides §15.1), so it is unlikely to be re-opened, but Drulun should be aware of it for litigation planning.

**Recommended fix:**

No immediate action required. Flag for the client's awareness. If a multi-issue dispute arises, engage local Delaware counsel early.

## Issue 7 – DPA Schedule 3 Lists No Authorized Subprocessors

**Documents:** DPA Schedule 3; DPA §5.1

**What the documents say:**

- Schedule 3 states: "None as of the DPA Effective Date."
- DPA §5.1 requires Pruvax to give Drulun at least 30 days' prior written notice before engaging any new Subprocessor, and Drulun may object on reasonable data protection grounds.

**Why it matters to Drulun:**

The DPA has been in effect since February 1, 2024. If Pruvax has engaged any subprocessors in the interim (e.g., cloud hosting for serialization data, IT service providers with access to complaint records), those engagements may not have been authorized under the DPA. This could constitute a breach of DPA §5.1 and potentially trigger the DPA indemnity in §11.1.

**Recommended fix:**

Send a routine information request to Pruvax asking for a current list of all subprocessors that have accessed or processed Personal Data under the Supply Agreement since February 1, 2024, and confirmation that each was authorized under DPA §5.1. This can be bundled with the other items raised in the non-renewal / Order Form discussion.
