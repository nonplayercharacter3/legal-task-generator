## Issue 1 – Non-Renewal Notice Was 10 Days Late; Agreement Will Likely Auto-Renew

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement §2.2; Order Form No. 3, Recital C.

**What happened:** The Initial Term is three years from February 1, 2024, expiring January 31, 2027. Section 2.2 requires non-renewal notice "at least 180 days before expiry of the then-current term." Counting back 180 days from January 31, 2027 gives a deadline of **August 4, 2026**. Drulun's Non-Renewal Notice is dated **August 14, 2026** — ten days late. Section 17.6 provides that a notice is "effective on receipt," and a notice dated August 14 cannot have been received before August 4.

**Why it matters:** If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement auto-renews for a two-year Renewal Term (February 1, 2027 – January 31, 2029). Drulun is then locked in for another two years, subject to the Annual Minimum (60,000 vials/Contract Year) and the 35% shortfall charge (Exhibit B, §3). Given that Drulun clearly intended to exit, this is the single most consequential issue.

**Complicating factor:** Order Form No. 3, Recital C states: *"Each Party reserves all rights regarding the effectiveness of the Non-Renewal Notice and the expiry or continuation of the Term. Signing this Order Form waives no such right."* This preserves Drulun's argument but does not cure the lateness. It also signals that Pruvax is aware of the timing problem and is not conceding effectiveness.

**Recommendation:**
- **Decide now** whether to (a) accept the renewal and negotiate a mid-term exit or early-termination fee with Pruvax, or (b) assert the notice was effective (a weak position given the 10-day gap) and be prepared for Pruvax to dispute.
- If Drulun wants out, the practical path is to negotiate a mutual termination or an early-exit amendment before February 1, 2027. Leverage: Pruvax has already accepted Order Form No. 3 with deliveries extending into April 2027, so it has a commercial interest in keeping the relationship stable.
- Document the internal timeline for the late notice (who approved it, when it was sent, why it missed the deadline) in case this becomes a dispute.

**⚠️ NEEDS QUICK DECISION FROM DRULUN.**

## Issue 2 – Order Form No. 3 Redefines "Delivery" to FCA at Pruvax's Facility, Shifting All Transit Risk to Drulun

**Documents and Sections:** Order Form No. 3 §4.1; Supply Agreement §§1.7, 5.1, 5.3, 6.2; Supply Agreement §17.3 (Order of Precedence).

**The conflict:**

| Provision | Definition of "Delivery" |
|---|---|
| Supply Agreement §1.7 | "Receipt of Product at Drulun's designated distribution center in Edison, New Jersey." |
| Order Form No. 3 §4.1 | "Tender to the first carrier at Pruvax's facility at 2100 Ellis Road, Durham, North Carolina 27703, FCA Pruvax Facility (Incoterms 2020)." |

Section 17.3(b) gives the Order Form precedence over the Supply Agreement "on Products, quantities, pricing and **delivery terms**." A redefined delivery point is squarely a delivery term, so the Order Form controls.

**Why it matters:**

1. **Risk of loss.** Supply Agreement §5.3 provides that "risk of loss of or damage to Product" passes "on Delivery." Under the Order Form, risk passes when Pruvax tenders the product to the carrier in Durham. If the product is damaged, lost, or suffers a temperature excursion in transit to Edison, **Drulun bears the loss** even though Pruvax selected and booked the carrier (Order Form §4.2: "Pruvax shall book carriage on Drulun's behalf").

2. **Rejection window.** Supply Agreement §6.2 gives Drulun 30 days "after Delivery" to reject nonconforming product. If Delivery occurs at Pruvax's facility, the 30-day clock starts running before the product arrives at Edison. For a refrigerated product in transit, this could consume a meaningful portion of the inspection window.

3. **Late delivery remedy.** Supply Agreement §5.4 measures lateness from the "date in the applicable Order Form." If Delivery is tender at Durham, a batch that leaves Durham on time but arrives late in Edison (e.g., carrier delay) is not "late" under the contract.

4. **No direct carrier relationship.** Drulun does not contract with the carrier. Pruvax books it "on Drulun's behalf," but the FCA term means Drulun bears the risk without the corresponding right to select or direct the carrier.

**Recommendation:**
- Raise with Pruvax immediately. Request that Order Form No. 3 be amended to conform to the Supply Agreement's delivery definition (receipt at Edison), or at minimum to provide that risk of loss and temperature excursion remain with Pruvax until product is received and accepted at Drulun's DC.
- If Pruvax resists, negotiate a middle ground: retain FCA for title passage but add a specific provision that Pruvax remains liable for temperature excursions and transit damage (i.e., decouple risk of loss from the FCA point for cold-chain product).
- For future Order Forms, ensure the delivery point is not redefined without Drulun's commercial team and legal sign-off.

**⚠️ NEEDS QUICK DECISION FROM DRULUN** (especially given that the first batch is scheduled for delivery January 11, 2027 — less than four months away).

## Issue 3 – Price Adjustment Appears to Violate the 90-Day Advance Notice Requirement

**Documents and Sections:** Order Form No. 3 §3.1; Supply Agreement §4.2.

**What happened:** Order Form No. 3 §3.1 sets the unit price at US$412.00 and states this "reflects the adjustment notified by Pruvax under Section 4.2 of the Supply Agreement on **May 15, 2026**, effective for the Contract Year beginning **February 1, 2026**."

Section 4.2 requires Pruvax to give "at least 90 days' written notice" before a price adjustment takes effect. An adjustment effective February 1, 2026 required notice by approximately **November 3, 2025**. The notice was given **May 15, 2026** — more than three months *after* the stated effective date.

**Why it matters:** The price increase appears retroactive and non-compliant with the contractual notice requirement. If the adjustment is invalid, the proper price for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026 is whatever was in effect before the (defective) adjustment. Drulun would be overpaying on 20,000 vials under Order Form No. 3.

At US$412.00 per vial × 20,000 vials = US$8,240,000 total. If the prior price was, say, US$380 (hypothetical), the overpayment would be ~US$640,000. The actual delta depends on the prior price, which is not stated in the documents I reviewed.

**Recommendation:**
- Request from Pruvax: (a) a copy of the May 15, 2026 price adjustment notice; (b) confirmation of the prior unit price; and (c) Pruvax's basis for applying the adjustment retroactively to February 1, 2026.
- If Pruvax cannot demonstrate compliance with §4.2, reserve Drulun's right to pay at the prior price or to credit the difference against future invoices.
- Do not concede the price in any correspondence with Pruvax until this is resolved.

## Issue 4 – Split Governing Law and Jurisdiction Between Supply Agreement and DPA

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement §15.1 (New York law; New York County courts); DPA §§12.1–12.2 (Delaware law; Delaware courts); Supply Agreement §17.3(a) (DPA controls on Personal Data matters).

**Why it matters:** A dispute involving both supply-chain issues and data protection issues (e.g., a Security Incident that also involves nonconforming product records) could trigger parallel proceedings in two different states under two different bodies of law. This increases cost and creates strategic complications.

**Practical assessment:** This is a moderate concern. Delaware is Drulun's state of incorporation, so the DPA's choice of law is not adverse to Drulun. The main risk is procedural complexity in a multi-faceted dispute.

**Recommendation:** Note for the file. If Drulun and Pruvax renegotiate (see Issue 1), consider aligning the DPA's governing law and forum with the Supply Agreement to avoid split proceedings. Low urgency.

## Issue 5 – DPA Schedule 3 Lists No Subprocessors; Confirm Pruvax's Actual Data Processing Architecture

**Documents and Sections:** DPA Schedule 3; DPA §5.1.

**What the document says:** Schedule 3 states "None as of the DPA Effective Date" (February 1, 2024). Section 5.1 requires 30 days' prior written notice before engaging any new Subprocessor.

**Why it matters:** Any organization handling complaint and adverse event data almost certainly uses third-party IT vendors (cloud hosting, email systems, serialization platform integrations). If Pruvax has been using subprocessors without notifying Drulun, it is in breach of the DPA. Conversely, if Pruvax has been compliant and truly uses no subprocessors, that is reassuring but should be confirmed.

**Recommendation:**
- Send Pruvax a written request (under DPA §8.1, which entitles Drulun to "all information necessary to demonstrate its compliance with this DPA") asking Pruvax to confirm whether it has engaged any Subprocessors since February 1, 2024 and, if so, to identify them and the basis for authorization.
- If subprocessors exist that were not authorized, this is a DPA breach that triggers the indemnity in DPA §11.1 and could support a for-cause audit under DPA §8.2.

## Issue 6 – Order Form No. 3 Batches Span Two Contract Years; Annual Minimum Allocation Ambiguous if Term Ends

**Documents and Sections:** Order Form No. 3 §§2.2, 2.4; Supply Agreement §§2.3, 3.4; Exhibit B §2.

**The problem:** The Annual Minimum is 60,000 vials per Contract Year. Contract Year 3 runs February 1, 2026 – January 31, 2027. Contract Year 4 (if the Agreement renews) runs February 1, 2027 – January 31, 2028.

Order Form No. 3 delivery dates:
- OF3-01: January 11, 2027 → Contract Year 3
- OF3-02: February 8, 2027 → Contract Year 4
- OF3-03: March 8, 2027 → Contract Year 4
- OF3-04: April 12, 2027 → Contract Year 4

Order Form §2.4 provides that quantities "count toward the Annual Minimum for the Contract Year in which the applicable Batch is Delivered." If the Agreement does **not** renew (i.e., if the non-renewal notice is ultimately held effective or the parties agree to end the relationship), there is no Contract Year 4. The 15,000 vials in OF3-02 through OF3-04 would be delivered under an Order Form that remains binding per Supply Agreement §2.3, but there would be no Contract Year against which to measure the Annual Minimum.

**Why it matters:** This is a secondary issue that becomes relevant only if Drulun successfully exits at the end of the Initial Term. In that scenario, Drulun should confirm in writing that the Annual Minimum obligation does not apply to any period after January 31, 2027, and that the shortfall charge (Exhibit B §3) is not triggered by the absence of a Contract Year 4.

**Recommendation:** If pursuing non-renewal or negotiated exit, include a provision in the termination or transition agreement confirming that the Annual Minimum applies only to complete Contract Years within the Term and that no shortfall invoice is due for any partial or post-Term period.
