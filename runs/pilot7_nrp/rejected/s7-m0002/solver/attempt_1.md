## Summary

The current APA, as compared to our August 21, 2026 draft, contains several material changes that significantly increase Roumoux's post-closing risk. The most consequential are: (1) the Key Employee Agreement is structurally incapable of valid assignment without Dana Okafor-Lindqvist's consent, which she has not given and which the Disclosure Schedule affirmatively represents is not required; (2) pre-closing warranty obligations covering approximately 4,100 residential systems and the *Halvorsen* putative class action have been recharacterized from Excluded Liabilities (in our draft) to Assumed Liabilities (in the current paper), making Roumoux directly and uncappedly liable for both; and (3) Seller's free assignment right combined with expanded earnout information rights exposes Roumoux's most sensitive commercial data to third parties. Details and recommended fixes follow.

## Issue 1: Key Employee Agreement Cannot Be Validly Assigned; Okafor-Lindqvist's Consent Is Missing

### Where it appears

- **APA § 2.1(c)(iii):** Identifies the Key Employee Agreement as an Assigned Contract to be transferred at Closing.
- **Key Employee Agreement § 9.2:** Provides that "The Company may not assign this Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations under this Agreement, without the prior written consent of Employee. This restriction applies to any assignment by operation of law or otherwise, including an assignment in connection with a merger, consolidation, or sale of all or substantially all of the assets of the Company. **Any purported assignment without that consent is void.**"
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.6, § 6.1:** States "Assigned Contracts. **None.**" as the Required Consents.
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.8:** Lists all four contractor licenses (ROC 318842, 318843, 324105, 324106) with Dana Okafor-Lindqvist as the sole qualifying party.
- **APA § 6.4:** Requires Seller to cooperate with Buyer's license application and to make the qualifying party available for 180 days post-Closing.

### Why it matters for Roumoux

This is the single most dangerous structural defect in the current paper. The APA purports to transfer the Key Employee Agreement as a Purchased Asset, but the agreement itself renders any such transfer **void** absent Okafor-Lindqvist's written consent. The Disclosure Schedule compounds the problem by affirmatively representing that no consents are required for the Assigned Contracts (§ 6.1: "None"), which is directly contradicted by § 9.2 of the Key Employee Agreement.

The practical consequence is existential: Okafor-Lindqvist is the qualifying party on all four Arizona contractor licenses. Without a validly assigned Key Employee Agreement, Roumoux has no contractual mechanism to (a) require her to continue serving as qualifying party, (b) compel her cooperation in transitioning to a successor qualifying party (Key Employee Agreement § 4.5), or (c) enforce the restrictive covenants in Key Employee Agreement Article 6 (non-compete, non-solicitation) against her. APA § 6.4 obligates *Seller* to cooperate, but if the underlying employment agreement is voidly assigned, Seller's ability to direct Okafor-Lindqvist's cooperation is undermined, and Seller's own non-compete (APA § 6.7) does not bind her personally.

The licenses expire June 30, 2027 (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.8). Roumoux must have a compliant qualifying party in place before then or it cannot legally perform solar installations in Arizona.

### Recommended fix

1. **Obtain Okafor-Lindqvist's written consent to the assignment** before Closing. This should be a tripartite joinder or a standalone consent and assumption agreement in which she (a) consents to the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement to Roumoux, (b) agrees to continue serving as qualifying party through a transition period (no fewer than 180 days post-Closing, matching APA § 6.4), and (c) acknowledges that the restrictive covenants in Key Employee Agreement Article 6 run in favor of Roumoux as assignee.

2. **Correct Disclosure Schedule § 6.1.** The statement "None" is factually wrong. Schedule 4.6 should list the consent of Dana Okafor-Lindqvist under the Key Employee Agreement § 9.2 as a Required Consent. This also protects Roumoux under the APA § 7.2(a) closing condition (all Required Consents obtained) and the APA § 4.3(c) / § 4.6 representations.

3. **Add a closing condition in APA § 7.2** requiring delivery of Okafor-Lindqvist's signed consent as a Seller Closing Deliverable under § 3.2.

**Proposed language for a new APA § 3.2(h):**

> (h) a written consent and assumption agreement, duly executed by Dana Okafor-Lindqvist, (i) consenting to the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement to Buyer pursuant to Section 9.2 thereof, (ii) agreeing to continue to serve as qualifying party on the licenses listed on Schedule 4.8 for a period of not less than one hundred eighty (180) days following the Closing Date, and (iii) acknowledging that Buyer and its Affiliates are intended third-party beneficiaries of the covenants in Article 6 of the Key Employee Agreement.

**Proposed correction to Disclosure Schedule § 6.1:**

> **6.1 Assigned Contracts.** The consent of Dana Okafor-Lindqvist to the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement dated March 1, 2024, as required by Section 9.2 thereof.

## Issue 2: *Halvorsen* Class Action Recharacterized as an Assumed Liability

### Where it appears

- **Prior Draft APA § 2.4(f):** Listed as an Excluded Liability: "any Liability arising out of any action, suit, claim or proceeding that was pending or threatened against Seller as of the Closing, including each matter set forth on Schedule 4.9."
- **Current APA § 2.4:** Contains no such exclusion. The Excluded Liabilities are limited to (a) taxes, (b) pre-closing employment, (c) borrowed money, (d) Excluded Assets, and (e) Seller's obligations under the Agreement.
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.3(c), § 4.2:** Affirmatively lists the *Halvorsen* matter as an Assumed Liability: "All Liabilities arising out of or relating to the matter described in Section 7.1 of Schedule 4.9 (*Halvorsen, et al. v. Tredeil Sunworks, Inc.*), a putative class action by homeowners alleging roof leaks and related property damage from roof penetrations made during installations by Seller."
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9, § 8.1:** Describes the litigation: filed November 7, 2025; putative class of Arizona homeowners with systems installed between January 1, 2019 and present; claims for breach of 10-year roof-penetration warranty, negligence, and Arizona Consumer Fraud Act violations; class certification pending.

### Why it matters for Roumoux

In our draft, Roumoux would have been protected: the class action would have been an Excluded Liability, meaning (a) Seller retained it, (b) Seller indemnified Roumoux under APA § 8.2(c) for any Excluded Liability, and (c) that indemnification was **not subject to the cap** (Prior Draft § 8.5(b): "Claims Outside the Cap" expressly excluded the cap for Excluded Liability claims under § 8.2(c)).

In the current paper, Roumoux **directly assumes** the class action. It is an Assumed Liability under § 2.3(c). This means:

- Roumoux is the named defendant (or will be substituted as such) and is directly liable to the putative class.
- Seller does **not** indemnify Roumoux for this under § 8.2(c) because § 8.2(c) covers only Excluded Liabilities, and this is no longer one.
- The only potential recourse against Seller is a claim that Seller breached its litigation representation (§ 4.9), but such a claim is subject to the $2,760,000 cap and the $138,000 deductible (Current APA § 8.5(a)).
- The putative class encompasses all residential installations from January 2019 forward (~4,100 systems per Disclosure Schedule § 4.1). If class certification is granted and the Consumer Fraud Act claims succeed, exposure could be substantial—potentially well in excess of the $2.76M cap.
- The case is in active litigation with class certification pending. Roumoux would be stepping into an uncertain and potentially large liability with no meaningful indemnity backstop.

### Recommended fix

**Revert to the prior draft structure.** The *Halvorsen* litigation should be an Excluded Liability, not an Assumed Liability.

1. **Delete Disclosure Schedule § 4.2** (the paragraph listing *Halvorsen* as an Assumed Liability).
2. **Add to Current APA § 2.4** a new subsection:

> (f) any Liability arising out of any action, suit, claim or proceeding that was pending or threatened against Seller as of the Closing Date, including each matter set forth on Schedule 4.9;

3. **Confirm** that § 8.5(b) preserves the uncapped indemnity for Excluded Liabilities. The current § 8.5(b) already states: "Losses arising out of any Excluded Liability under Section 8.2(c) … shall not be subject to the Cap or the deductible in Section 8.5(a)." This is sufficient if the *Halvorsen* matter is restored to Excluded status.

**Judgment call for partner:** If the business rationale for Roumoux assuming the class action was price (i.e., Seller reduced the Base Purchase Price in exchange), we need to confirm with the client whether the $18.4M price was negotiated on the assumption that Roumoux would take this liability. If so, the question becomes whether the price adequately compensates for the exposure, and whether we should instead negotiate a specific indemnity from Seller for the *Halvorsen* matter with its own (higher) cap or no cap. If the price was *not* adjusted for this assumption, we should demand the revert without concession.

## Issue 3: Pre-Closing Warranty Obligations (~4,100 Systems) Recharacterized as Assumed Liabilities

### Where it appears

- **Prior Draft APA § 2.4(e):** Listed as an Excluded Liability: "any Liability arising out of any solar photovoltaic system that Seller installed, or any service Seller performed, before the Closing, including all workmanship, roof-penetration and other warranty obligations for those systems."
- **Current APA § 2.4:** No such exclusion.
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.3(c), § 4.1:** Affirmatively lists as an Assumed Liability: "All workmanship and roof-penetration warranty obligations of Seller for solar photovoltaic systems installed by Seller before the Closing. These obligations cover approximately 4,100 residential systems installed in the State of Arizona, each covered by Seller's standard 10-year workmanship warranty and 10-year roof-penetration warranty in the forms listed on Schedule 4.10, together with the small-commercial systems recorded in the Warranty Book."
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.10:** Describes the warranty forms: 10-year workmanship (Form TS-W1), 10-year roof-penetration (Form TS-W2), 5-year commercial workmanship (Form TS-W3), all running from permission to operate (residential) or commissioning (commercial).
- **APA § 2.2(f):** Seller's insurance policies are Excluded Assets—Roumoux does not receive them.

### Why it matters for Roumoux

Roumoux is assuming direct, uncapped liability for 10-year warranties on approximately 4,100 residential systems (and the small-commercial systems in the Warranty Book). The oldest systems were installed in January 2019, meaning their warranties run to approximately 2029. The newest pre-closing systems could have warranties running to approximately 2036. This is a multi-year tail liability with:

- **No cap:** Because it is an Assumed Liability, not an Excluded Liability, Seller's indemnity under § 8.2(c) does not apply.
- **No insurance:** Seller's insurance policies are Excluded Assets (§ 2.2(f)). Roumoux must obtain its own coverage, and insurers may charge a premium (or decline) for assumed pre-closing liabilities.
- **No meaningful recourse against Seller:** The only path is a breach-of-rep claim under § 4.10 (Warranty Book accuracy) or § 4.5 (Condition of Assets), both subject to the $2.76M cap and $138K deductible.
- **Compounded by the *Halvorsen* action:** The class action (Issue 2) alleges the same roof-penetration failures that the 10-year warranty covers. Roumoux is effectively assuming both the warranty obligation and the litigation challenging it.

### Recommended fix

**Revert to the prior draft structure.** Pre-closing warranty obligations should be Excluded Liabilities.

1. **Delete Disclosure Schedule § 4.1** (the paragraph listing warranty obligations as Assumed Liabilities).
2. **Add to Current APA § 2.4** a new subsection:

> (e) any Liability arising out of any solar photovoltaic system that Seller installed, or any service Seller performed, before the Closing Date, including all workmanship, roof-penetration and other warranty obligations for those systems;

3. **Alternatively**, if the commercial deal requires Roumoux to assume these warranties (e.g., to preserve customer relationships and the value of the Warranty Book), negotiate:
   - A **specific, uncapped indemnity** from Seller for pre-closing warranty claims, separate from the general indemnity in Article VIII;
   - A **warranty reserve or escrow** funded from the Base Purchase Price (e.g., $2–3M held in escrow for 12–18 months post-closing to address early-emanating claims);
   - Seller's cooperation in obtaining **run-off insurance** or a **warranty assumption policy** at Seller's expense (or as a shared cost) before Closing.

**Judgment call for partner:** The assumption of the Warranty Book (APA § 2.1(e)) makes commercial sense—Roumoux wants the customer relationships. But assuming the *liability* under those warranties is a different question. We need to understand the client's appetite. If Roumoux wants the Warranty Book for customer-relationship value but does not want the 10-year tail liability, the structure should be: Roumoux receives the Warranty Book as a Purchased Asset, but the warranty obligations remain Excluded Liabilities of Seller, with Seller providing a service-level commitment (or funding a reserve) to honor them. This preserves the customer goodwill without the balance-sheet risk.

## Issue 4: Seller's Unrestricted Assignment Right Combined with Expanded Earnout Information Rights

### Where it appears

- **Current APA § 10.6:** "Seller may assign any of its rights under this Agreement, including the right to receive Earnout Payments and to make claims under Article VIII, to any Person without the consent of Buyer."
- **Prior Draft APA § 10.6:** "Neither Party may assign its rights or obligations under this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party, and any purported assignment without that consent is void. Buyer may, however, assign its rights under this Agreement without Seller's consent to one or more of its Affiliates, or to any lender as collateral security." (No comparable free assignment right for Seller.)
- **Current APA § 2.6(d):** "Buyer shall provide Seller **or its permitted assignee**, within twenty (20) days after the end of each calendar quarter, reports of installation volumes by system and permission-to-operate date, and shall afford Seller **or its permitted assignee** reasonable access during normal business hours to **Buyer's customer pipeline reports, pricing and proposal data**, and books and records relating to Installed Megawatts."
- **Prior Draft APA § 2.6(d):** "Buyer shall provide Seller with a report of installation volumes by system and permission-to-operate date. Seller may use these reports only to verify Installed Megawatts and shall treat them as confidential information of Buyer under Section 6.6. … **Buyer is not required to disclose customer pipeline, pricing or proposal data.**"

### Why it matters for Roumoux

The combination of these two provisions creates a serious competitive and confidentiality risk:

1. Seller can assign its earnout rights (and the associated information rights under § 2.6(d)) to **any Person** without Roumoux's consent.
2. That assignee is entitled to receive Roumoux's **customer pipeline reports, pricing, and proposal data**—the most commercially sensitive information a solar installer possesses.
3. There is no restriction on *who* Seller can assign to. A direct competitor, a private equity firm, or a data broker could become the recipient.
4. The confidentiality covenant in APA § 6.6 binds only "Seller," not Seller's assignees. The prior draft at least referenced § 2.6(d) information explicitly in the confidentiality provision; the current draft does not.

### Recommended fix

1. **Delete the free assignment right for Seller.** Restore the prior draft language in § 10.6:

> Neither Party may assign its rights or obligations under this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party, and any purported assignment without that consent is void. Buyer may assign its rights under this Agreement to one or more of its Affiliates or to any lender as collateral security without Seller's consent. Seller may assign its right to receive Earnout Payments to a financial institution solely as security for a loan, provided that such assignee agrees in writing to be bound by the confidentiality obligations of this Agreement and Section 2.6(d).

2. **Narrow § 2.6(d) information rights.** Restore the prior draft limitation:

> During each Earnout Period and until the final determination of the Earnout Payment for the Second Earnout Period, Buyer shall provide Seller, within twenty (20) days after the end of each calendar quarter, a report of installation volumes by system and permission-to-operate date, solely to permit verification of Installed Megawatts. Following delivery of an Earnout Statement, Buyer shall give the accounting firm referred to in Section 2.6(c), or Seller's independent accountants subject to a customary confidentiality undertaking, reasonable access to books and records relating to Installed Megawatts, solely to the extent necessary to verify the Earnout Statement. **Buyer is not required to disclose customer pipeline reports, pricing, or proposal data.**

3. **Extend the confidentiality covenant to assignees.** Amend § 6.6:

> From and after the Closing, Seller and its permitted assignees shall hold in confidence all confidential information concerning the Business, the Purchased Assets, and all information received under Section 2.6(d). This obligation does not apply to information that becomes publicly available other than through a breach of this Section 6.6.

## Issue 5: Confidentiality Provision Narrowed; No Binding Effect on Seller's Assignees

### Where it appears

- **Current APA § 6.6:** "From and after the Closing, Seller shall hold in confidence all confidential information concerning the Business and the Purchased Assets, except to the extent such information becomes publicly available other than through Seller's breach of this Section 6.6."
- **Prior Draft APA § 6.6:** "From and after the Closing, Seller shall hold in confidence all confidential information concerning the Business and the Purchased Assets, **and all information Seller receives under Section 2.6(d).** This obligation does not apply to information that becomes publicly available other than through Seller's breach of this Section 6.6."

### Why it matters for Roumoux

The current provision omits the explicit reference to § 2.6(d) information. While one could argue that earnout reports are "confidential information concerning the Business," the explicit reference in our draft removed any ambiguity. More critically, neither version binds Seller's assignees, which is a significant gap given the free assignment right in current § 10.6 (see Issue 4). If Seller assigns its earnout rights to a third party, that third party receives the § 2.6(d) data but is not bound by § 6.6.

### Recommended fix

Addressed in Issue 4, item 3 above (extend § 6.6 to "Seller and its permitted assignees" and restore the § 2.6(d) reference).

## Issue 6: No Insurance Coverage for Assumed Pre-Closing Liabilities

### Where it appears

- **APA § 2.2(f):** "all insurance policies of Seller and rights thereunder" are Excluded Assets.
- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.3(c), §§ 4.1–4.2:** Roumoux assumes the warranty obligations and the *Halvorsen* class action.
- **APA § 8.5(c):** "Losses shall be calculated net of any insurance proceeds actually received by the Indemnified Party in respect thereof."

### Why it matters for Roumoux

Roumoux is assuming significant pre-closing liabilities (warranty tail, class action) but is not receiving Seller's insurance policies that may have been responding to or covering those liabilities. If Seller had a general liability policy or a professional liability/contractor's pollution policy that was already on notice of the *Halvorsen* claims or the roof-penetration warranty claims, Roumoux cannot access those policies. Roumoux must either (a) negotiate for the insurance policies to be included as Purchased Assets (or at least for the rights to make claims under them), or (b) purchase its own run-off or assumed-liability coverage, at potentially significant cost.

### Recommended fix

1. **If Issues 2 and 3 are resolved by reverting to Excluded Liability status**, this issue is largely moot (Seller retains the liabilities and its insurance).
2. **If Roumoux agrees to assume the liabilities**, add to APA § 2.1 a new subsection:

> (i) all of Seller's insurance policies, and all of Seller's rights to make claims under any such policy, to the extent such policies or claims relate to the Assumed Liabilities;

And add a corresponding deletion from § 2.2(f), or carve out policies relating to Assumed Liabilities from the exclusion.

3. **Require as a closing condition** that Seller deliver evidence of all insurance policies in force as of the Closing Date that could respond to the Assumed Liabilities, along with any claims-made or notice-of-circumstances letters.

## Issue 7: Indemnification Cap Inadequate for Scale of Assumed Liabilities

### Where it appears

- **Current APA § 8.5(a):** Cap of 15% of Base Purchase Price = $2,760,000; deductible of $138,000.
- **Current APA § 8.5(b):** Excludes from the cap: Fundamental Rep breaches, fraud, Excluded Liability claims under § 8.2(c), and Assumed Liability claims under § 8.3(c).
- **Current APA § 8.2(c):** Seller indemnifies Buyer for "any Excluded Liability or Excluded Asset."

### Why it matters for Roumoux

Because the *Halvorsen* class action and the warranty obligations are currently Assumed Liabilities (not Excluded Liabilities), they fall outside § 8.2(c). Seller does not indemnify Roumoux for them. Roumoux's only recourse against Seller for these items would be a breach-of-representation claim (e.g., § 4.9 litigation rep, § 4.10 warranty rep), which is capped at $2.76M.

A putative class action involving 4,100+ homeowners with property damage claims, plus a Consumer Fraud Act theory (which can carry enhanced remedies in Arizona), could easily produce a judgment or settlement well in excess of $2.76M. The cap provides a false sense of security.

### Recommended fix

This is addressed by the structural fix in Issues 2 and 3 (reverting to Excluded Liability status, which triggers the uncapped § 8.2(c) indemnity). If for commercial reasons Roumoux must assume these liabilities, we need:

- A **specific indemnity** from Seller for the *Halvorsen* matter and pre-closing warranty claims, with its own cap (e.g., 100% of the Base Purchase Price) or no cap, and expressly excluded from the general cap in § 8.5(a).
- **Proposed language** (new § 8.2(d)):

> (d) any Liability assumed by Buyer under Section 2.3(c) that arises from facts, circumstances or conditions existing before the Closing Date (a "Pre-Closing Assumed Liability"). Seller's indemnification obligation under this Section 8.2(d) shall not be subject to the Cap or the deductible in Section 8.5(a) but shall be subject to a separate cap equal to [●] (the "Specific Cap").

## Issue 8: Bill of Sale Governing Law and Jurisdiction Inconsistent with APA

### Where it appears

- **APA § 10.10:** Delaware governing law.
- **APA § 10.11:** Exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware Court of Chancery (or Superior Court).
- **Bill of Sale § 6.1:** Arizona governing law.
- **Bill of Sale § 6.2:** Exclusive jurisdiction in Maricopa County, Arizona state courts.
- **APA § 10.13:** "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and the Disclosure Schedule or the Bill of Sale, this Agreement prevails, except that the Bill of Sale controls on matters concerning the transfer of title to the Purchased Assets."

### Why it matters for Roumoux

This creates a bifurcated dispute-resolution framework. A title dispute (e.g., whether a particular asset was properly conveyed, whether an encumbrance was properly released) is governed by Arizona law in Arizona courts. A contractual dispute (e.g., whether a representation was breached, whether an indemnity obligation is triggered) is governed by Delaware law in Delaware courts. In practice, a single factual dispute (e.g., "Did Seller have good title to the Fleet and Equipment?") could implicate both instruments and both forums, leading to inconsistent rulings, duplicative litigation, or forum-fighting.

This was present in the prior draft as well, so it is not a regression, but it remains a structural weakness.

### Recommended fix

Conform the Bill of Sale to the APA:

- **Bill of Sale § 6.1:** Change to "the laws of the State of Delaware."
- **Bill of Sale § 6.2:** Change to "the exclusive jurisdiction of the Delaware Court of Chancery (or, solely if such court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware)."

Alternatively, if Arizona law is preferable for UCC/title matters (and it likely is, given the assets are in Arizona and the UCC-1s are filed in Arizona), consider a split: Arizona law for matters of title and perfection, Delaware law for all other matters. But the cleaner approach is to conform to the APA and rely on the APA's Delaware choice of law for the contractual framework.

**Note:** This is lower priority than Issues 1–4 and may be acceptable as-is if the partner prefers not to reopen a point that was in the prior draft. Flagging for completeness.

## Issue 9: Good Reason Trigger Risk in Key Employee Agreement

### Where it appears

- **Key Employee Agreement § 4.4:** "Good Reason" includes "(b) a material diminution in title, authority or duties."
- **Key Employee Agreement § 1.1:** Employee reports to "the President of the Company."
- **Key Employee Agreement § 5.2:** Good Reason resignation triggers 12 months' salary continuation + COBRA.

### Why it matters for Roumoux

If the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement is void (Issue 1), Roumoux will presumably want to hire Okafor-Lindqvist directly. In doing so, it must replicate or improve the terms. If Roumoux's organizational structure does not have a "President" to whom she reports, or if her title or duties are materially different, she could argue a Good Reason trigger under the *new* agreement (if drafted carelessly) or simply refuse to join.

Even if the assignment is made valid with her consent (per the fix in Issue 1), the change in employer from an Arizona corporation to a Delaware LLC could be characterized as a material change in the employment relationship, particularly if Roumoux's management structure differs.

### Recommended fix

In the consent and assumption agreement (see Issue 1, proposed fix), include an express provision:

> Employee acknowledges that the assignment of this Agreement to Buyer constitutes a change of employer only and does not, in and of itself, constitute a material diminution in title, authority or duties within the meaning of Section 4.4. Employee's title, reporting structure, duties, base salary, bonus eligibility, and benefits shall remain unchanged as a result of the assignment, except as expressly set forth in this consent.

If Roumoux intends to change any terms (title, reporting line, compensation), those changes should be made only with her express written agreement and should not be characterized as occurring "without Employee's written consent" (which is the Good Reason trigger).

## Issue 10: Contractor License Expiration Creates Urgency

### Where it appears

- **Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.8:** All four licenses expire **June 30, 2027**.
- **APA § 6.4:** Seller cooperation obligation runs for 180 days post-Closing (i.e., through approximately March 30, 2027).
- **Key Employee Agreement § 1.3:** Okafor-Lindqvist must "maintain all personal qualifications required to serve in that capacity."

### Why it matters for Roumoux

The 180-day cooperation period in APA § 6.4 ends approximately March 30, 2027. The licenses expire June 30, 2027. That leaves a roughly three-month gap in which Roumoux must have its own qualifying party fully approved by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, but Seller's contractual cooperation obligation has lapsed. If Okafor-Lindqvist is not validly bound to Roumoux (Issue 1), she has no obligation to cooperate after Seller's § 6.4 period ends.

### Recommended fix

1. **Extend the cooperation period in APA § 6.4** to at least 270 days post-Closing (through approximately June 30, 2027), or alternatively tie it to the later of (a) 180 days post-Closing or (b) the date on which Buyer obtains all contractor licenses in its own name with its own qualifying party.

**Proposed language:**

> Seller shall cooperate with Buyer, before and for a period continuing until the later of (i) one hundred eighty (180) days after the Closing Date or (ii) the date on which Buyer (or its Affiliate) holds all contractor licenses listed on Schedule 4.8 in good standing with a qualifying party other than Dana Okafor-Lindqvist, in Buyer's application for contractor licenses from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors, including by making available the qualifying party listed on Schedule 4.8.

2. **Require Okafor-Lindqvist's consent** (per Issue 1) to include a commitment to serve as qualifying party until Roumoux has a fully approved successor, with no outside date earlier than June 30, 2027.
