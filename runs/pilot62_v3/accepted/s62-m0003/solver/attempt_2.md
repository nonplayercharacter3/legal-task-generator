## 1. Pellam Ground Lease Change-of-Control Consent Missing from Required Consents

**Where:** SPA § 4.4; Disclosure Schedule § 6 (Schedule 4.4); Disclosure Schedule § 9 (Schedule 4.9, item 3); Disclosure Schedule § 15 (Schedule 7.2(c)).

**Issue.** Schedule 4.9, item 3 discloses that Section 14.2 of the Pellam Ground Lease deems any transfer of more than 50% of the stock of Tenant (the Company) "an assignment" requiring Landlord's prior written consent under Section 14.1. Yet Schedule 4.4 (No Conflicts; Consents) states "None," and Schedule 7.2(c) (Required Consents) lists only liquor license transfers. The ground lease consent is absent from both.

**Why it matters.** Hotel Pellam is the portfolio's flagship (142 rooms, Baltimore). If the closing occurs without Landlord's consent, the transfer of 100% of the Company's stock to Draibeir is an unauthorized assignment under the ground lease, exposing the Company to a default that could support termination or acceleration. Because the consent is not a "Required Consent" under Schedule 7.2(c), it is not a closing condition under SPA § 7.2(c), meaning Draibeir could close without it and discover the problem post-closing. The SPA § 4.4 representation (no Material Contract requires consent except as set forth on Schedule 4.4) is directly contradicted by the disclosed ground lease terms, giving Draibeir a rep-breach claim—but under § 9.8, indemnification is the exclusive post-closing remedy, and the claim would be subject to the Deductible and Cap.

**Recommendation.** (1) Add the Pellam Ground Lease consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent and to Schedule 4.4. (2) Confirm with Seller's counsel whether the Landlord has been notified and whether consent has been or will be granted. (3) Request a current estoppel certificate from Ashcombe Quay Land Trust—the one in the data room is dated June 2, 2017, nearly nine years old. **This needs a call with the client before we advise on closing readiness.**

## 2. Bill of Sale Fails to Convey Software Licenses, Guest Data, and Domain Names

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Hotel Operating Assets"); Bill of Sale § 1.2 (definition of "Hotel Operating Assets"); Bill of Sale, Annex A.

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Hotel Operating Assets" to include five categories: (a) FF&E, (b) inventories, (c) "the licenses and subscriptions for the property-management system and the central reservation system software used at the Hotels, together with any related hardware and service agreements," (d) "the Guest Data," and (e) "the Hotel Domain Names." The Bill of Sale, however, defines "Hotel Operating Assets" as only "all furniture, fixtures, equipment and inventory owned by Transferor that are located at the Hotels and listed on Annex A." Annex A lists FF&E and inventory only. The software licenses, Guest Data, and domain names are not in Annex A and are not captured by the Bill of Sale's operative definition.

**Why it matters.** The domain names (sablewick.com, hotelpellam.com, etc.) are registered in the name of the Service Affiliate (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 1.1(b)). The Guest Data is held by the Service Affiliate as part of the centralized services it provides. The PMS/CRS software licenses are similarly held by the Service Affiliate. Without an effective conveyance instrument, these assets may not transfer at Closing, leaving Draibeir without the domain names, guest records, and reservation system it needs to operate the hotels. SPA § 11.10 provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Agreements in case of conflict, "except that each Ancillary Agreement shall govern the conveyance of the assets transferred under it"—which cuts the other way: the Bill of Sale governs what is actually conveyed under it, and it conveys less than the SPA contemplates.

**Recommendation.** Amend the Bill of Sale before Closing to (1) expand the definition of "Hotel Operating Assets" to match the SPA definition (including software licenses/subscriptions, Guest Data, and Hotel Domain Names), and (2) add a new Annex (or expand Annex A) listing those items with sufficient specificity (e.g., domain names by registrant and expiration, software by vendor and subscription ID, Guest Data by category). Alternatively, add a separate assignment schedule for intangible assets. **Flag for client call: confirm which PMS/CRS platform the hotels use and whether the licenses are assignable or require vendor consent.**

## 3. Escrow Releases at 6 Months but General Representations Survive 18 Months

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(b) (Release Date = 6 months); SPA § 9.1 (General Reps survive 18 months); SPA § 9.4(a) (Cap = 10% of Purchase Price = $14,850,000 = Escrow Amount).

**Issue.** The $14.85M escrow is Draibeir's primary security for Seller's indemnification obligations. It releases in full (less reserved amounts for pending claims) six months after Closing. But the General Representations (all reps other than Fundamental Reps) survive for 18 months. The Cap equals the Escrow Amount exactly. So for months 7 through 18, Draibeir has no escrow security and its maximum recoverable loss ($14.85M) exceeds any remaining escrow (zero). Draibeir would have to pursue Seller directly for any claim arising in that window.

**Why it matters.** Many of the most likely post-closing claims—tax issues, environmental matters, employment disputes, contract breaches—tend to surface after the initial 6-month period. Without the escrow as a self-help remedy, Draibeir must litigate or arbitrate against Seller to recover, bearing the cost and risk of collection.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate to extend the Release Date to 18 months (matching the General Rep survival period), or at minimum 12 months. If Seller resists, consider a tiered release (e.g., 50% at 6 months, 50% at 18 months). Alternatively, if the release date cannot be moved, seek a reduction in the Cap to reflect the shorter security period, or require Seller to maintain a letter of credit or other credit support for the tail period. **Call with client to discuss risk appetite on this point before the next negotiation session.**

## 4. Seller May Assign Its Obligations and Be Released (SPA § 11.6)

**Where:** SPA § 11.6 (Successors and Assigns).

**Issue.** The last sentence of § 11.6 provides: "Seller may assign this Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations under it, to any Person without the consent of Buyer, and upon any such assignment Seller shall thereupon be released from the obligations so assigned." This means Seller can transfer its indemnification obligations (Article IX) to any third party—and be released—without Draibeir's consent.

**Why it matters.** If Seller assigns its indemnity obligations to an affiliate, a successor entity, or a third party with lesser creditworthiness, Draibeir's recovery is impaired. The escrow secures "the indemnification obligations of Seller" (SPA § 2.5(a)), but if Seller is released, it's unclear whether the escrow continues to secure the assignee's obligations or reverts to Seller (now released). This creates a structural gap in Draibeir's security.

**Recommendation.** Delete or revise the last sentence of § 11.6 to require Buyer's consent for any assignment of Seller's indemnification or other post-closing obligations, or to provide that no assignment releases Seller from its obligations under Article IX. At minimum, add language confirming that the Escrow Fund continues to secure the obligations of any assignee of Seller.

## 5. Hotel Services Agreement Terminates at Closing with No Transition Mechanism

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 9 (Schedule 4.9, item 5); Press Release § 2.3; SPA § 5.8.

**Issue.** The Hotel Services Agreement (centralized reservations, revenue management, sales and marketing, accounting, IT, and procurement for all five hotels, at 2.75% of gross operating revenue) is "terminable by either party on thirty (30) days' notice" and "Seller will cause it to be terminated effective as of the Closing, without cost to the Company." There is no transition services agreement (TSA) in the document set. The press release states that "Draibeir and Bruneis will work together on an orderly transition" (§ 2.3), but this is a public statement, not a contractual obligation.

**Why it matters.** On day one after Closing, the Company loses its reservation system, revenue management, accounting, IT, and procurement infrastructure. If Draibeir has not contracted for replacements or built internal capability, the hotels face immediate operational disruption—missed reservations, inability to process payments, loss of revenue management optimization. The 30-day termination notice means Seller could theoretically give notice before Closing and have the agreement end before the Closing Date, creating a gap even pre-closing.

**Recommendation.** (1) Negotiate a Transition Services Agreement under which the Service Affiliate provides the centralized services for 6–12 months post-closing at the existing fee (2.75% of gor), with a phase-out schedule. (2) If a TSA is not feasible, confirm that Draibeir has replacement vendors contracted and data migration plans in place before Closing. (3) Add the TSA (if agreed) to the definition of "Ancillary Agreements" in SPA § 1.1. **Call with client: confirm Draibeir's operational readiness to absorb these functions on day one.**

## 6. Lisset Hotel Liquor License Expires December 31, 2026

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 10 (Schedule 4.10, Part 1, item 3); Press Release § 7.2.

**Issue.** The Lisset Hotel's liquor license (CHT-ALC-20-0988, City of Savannah / Georgia DOR) expires December 31, 2026. The expected closing is "fourth quarter of 2026" (Press Release § 1.5). If closing occurs in late November or December, the license may expire before or around the Closing Date, and the transfer approval may not be in place by then.

**Why it matters.** If the license lapses before the transfer is approved, the Lisset Hotel may be unable to serve alcohol—a material revenue stream for a Savannah hotel whose rooftop bar is a key differentiator. The SPA makes liquor license approvals a Required Consent (Schedule 7.2(c), item 1), so closing is conditioned on them. But if the license expires before approval is granted, the condition may be impossible to satisfy as drafted (you can't transfer a lapsed license).

**Recommendation.** (1) Confirm with Seller's counsel the status of the Lisset renewal/transfer application and the expected timeline from the Savannah Revenue Department and Georgia DOR. (2) If there is any risk of expiration before approval, negotiate an amendment to the Required Consent provision to accept a pending renewal application (with the license in good standing through the renewal) as satisfaction of the condition. (3) Consider whether the Outside Date (January 29, 2027) provides sufficient buffer.

## 7. Draibeir's Guarantee in the IP Assignment Agreement

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 6.4; SPA § 3.3(c).

**Issue.** Draibeir, as Guarantor, "irrevocably and unconditionally guarantees to the Assignor Indemnitees, as primary obligor and not merely as surety, the due and punctual payment and performance of all obligations of Assignee under Section 6.2." Section 6.2 requires Sablewick (Assignee) to indemnify Bruneis Brand Holdings (Assignor) for, among other things, "any claim by a third party that any such use or exploitation of the Assigned Marks infringes, dilutes or otherwise violates the trademark or other Intellectual Property rights of that third party" and "the operation of the Hotels under the Assigned Marks from and after the Assignment Date."

**Why it matters.** This is Draibeir's parent-level guarantee of the Company's post-closing IP indemnity to Seller's brand affiliate. If a third party asserts that the Sablewick marks (or the operation of the hotels under them) infringe that third party's rights, Sablewick must indemnify Bruneis Brand Holdings—and Draibeir is on the hook as primary obligor. The guarantee is not subject to the Cap or Deductible by its own terms (though § 6.2 references "the limitations set forth in Section 9.4 of the Purchase Agreement," which should flow through). Draibeir should confirm that the § 9.4 limitations (Cap, Deductible, materiality scrape) apply to the guaranteed obligations.

**Recommendation.** (1) Confirm that § 6.4(a) is expressly subject to the § 9.4 limitations by adding a reference in § 6.4 or in § 6.2. (2) Assess the likelihood of third-party IP challenges to the Sablewick marks (the SPA § 4.8(b) rep says no claims in 3 years, but that's a Seller rep, not a guarantee of future non-infringement). (3) Ensure Draibeir's board/management has approved this guarantee as a material off-balance-sheet obligation.

## 8. Press Release Omits MEREWEATHER INN Mark and Unregistered Marks

**Where:** Press Release § 1.3; IP Assignment Agreement, Schedule A; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 1.1(c).

**Issue.** The press release states that the marks being assigned "include SABLEWICK, HOTEL PELLAM, THE CORVEN HOUSE, LISSET HOTEL, HALDEN ROW and THE PELLAM BAR." It omits MEREWEATHER INN (a pending U.S. application, Serial No. 98/442,107), SABLEWICK STAYS (a pending intent-to-use application, Serial No. 99/016,884), and the unregistered marks (the wick-and-flame logo, the "Small House, Long Stay" tagline, and "Corven Table").

**Why it matters.** While a press release is not a contract, it is a public statement made by Draibeir. If the MEREWEATHER INN mark were later subject to a dispute (e.g., a third party claims it was not part of the assignment), the press release could be cited as evidence that the parties' public understanding excluded it. More practically, the omission of the tagline and logo is inconsistent with the press release's own description of the portfolio's brand identity (Press Release § 11 references the "Small House, Long Stay" tagline).

**Recommendation.** Amend the press release to add "MEREWEATHER INN" to the list of marks and to reference the Sablewick family of marks "as listed on Schedule 1.1(c) to the Stock Purchase Agreement" (or similar broad reference) to avoid the implication that the list is exhaustive. Coordinate with Seller's communications team before the release is finalized.

The Escrow Agent requires completed schedules to (a) verify the identity of individuals giving instructions, (b) perform call-back verification under § 6.7, and (c) make wire disbursements under § 5.4.

## 10. Stale Estoppel and Ground Lease Risk (Supplement to Issue 1)

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 8 (Schedule 4.7(b), item 1); Disclosure Schedule § 9 (Schedule 4.9, item 3).

**Issue.** The only estoppel certificate for the Pellam Ground Lease is dated June 2, 2017—nearly nine years before the expected Closing. During that time, the lease was amended (Second Amendment, January 8, 2023), rent has escalated 2.5% annually since 2017, and the percentage rent breakpoint ($16.5M) may no longer reflect current revenue. The ground lease also contains a leasehold mortgage provision (§ 18) that is relevant to the Stellmark Bank payoff (the loan is secured by a "leasehold mortgage on the Pellam Ground Lease" per Schedule 4.9, item 13).

**Why it matters.** Without a current estoppel, Draibeir cannot confirm: (a) that the lease is in full force and effect; (b) that no default exists; (c) the current rent and percentage rent breakpoint; (d) that no amendments exist beyond the two disclosed; or (e) the status of the leasehold mortgage and its interaction with the Stellmark payoff.

**Recommendation.** As a condition to Closing (or as a pre-closing deliverable), require Seller to deliver a current estoppel certificate from Ashcombe Quay Land Trust, dated within 30 days of the Closing Date, confirming the lease terms, absence of default, and the status of the leasehold mortgage. Add this to the Seller Closing Deliverables in SPA § 3.2.
