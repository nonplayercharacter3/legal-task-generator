## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Pellam Ground Lease – Landlord Consent Required but Not Disclosed

**Where:** SPA § 4.4(c); Disclosure Schedule § 6 (Schedule 4.4); Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 3 (Schedule 4.9(a)(i)); Disclosure Schedule § 15 (Schedule 7.2(c)).

**Issue:** Section 14.2 of the Pellam Ground Lease (summarized in Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 3) provides that any transfer of more than 50% of the stock of Tenant "or of any Person controlling Tenant" is deemed an assignment requiring Landlord's prior written consent under Section 14.1. The sale of 100% of the Company's shares to Draibeir triggers this provision. Yet Schedule 4.4 states "None," and Schedule 7.2(c) (Required Consents) lists only liquor license transfers. Landlord consent is neither disclosed nor included as a closing condition.

**Why it matters:** Hotel Pellam is the portfolio's flagship (142 rooms; ground lease through 2067 with two 15-year renewals). If the Landlord's consent is not obtained, the stock sale constitutes an unauthorized assignment, giving the Landlord potential termination or default rights. This is also a direct breach of Seller's representation in § 4.4(c), which is a Fundamental Representation (SPA § 1.1, "Fundamental Representations").

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately request that Seller add the Pellam Ground Lease Landlord consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent and to Schedule 4.4.
- Require delivery of Landlord's written consent (or a waiver of the consent requirement) as a condition to Closing under § 7.2(c).
- Obtain an updated estoppel certificate from Ashcombe Quay Land Trust confirming no existing defaults and acknowledging the pending transfer.
- **Flag for client call:** This is a material misrepresentation by Seller. The client should decide whether to (a) require correction before proceeding, (b) negotiate a specific indemnity for this item outside the cap, or (c) treat it as leverage in the overall negotiation.

### 2. Bill of Sale Omits Software Licenses, Guest Data, and Domain Names

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Hotel Operating Assets"); SPA § 5.8; Bill of Sale § 1.2 (definition of "Hotel Operating Assets"); Bill of Sale Annex A; Disclosure Schedule § 3 (Schedule 1.1(b)).

**Issue:** The SPA defines "Hotel Operating Assets" to include five categories: (a) FF&E; (b) inventories; (c) PMS/CRS software licenses and related hardware and service agreements; (d) Guest Data; and (e) Hotel Domain Names. SPA § 5.8 requires Seller to cause the Service Affiliate to transfer *all* Hotel Operating Assets "by executing and delivering to the Company the Bill of Sale." However, the Bill of Sale redefines "Hotel Operating Assets" as only "all furniture, fixtures, equipment and inventory owned by Transferor that are located at the Hotels and listed on Annex A." Annex A contains only FF&E and inventory. Software licenses, Guest Data, and domain names are entirely absent.

The Disclosure Schedule confirms the domain names are registered in the Service Affiliate's name with Tallmast Registrar Services (Disclosure Schedule § 3, Part 1). The Guest Data and PMS/CRS licenses are similarly held by the Service Affiliate per the SPA's own definitions.

**Why it matters:** Without the domain names, the Company cannot control its own web properties (sablewick.com, hotelpellam.com, etc.) post-closing. Without the Guest Data, the Company loses its guest profiles, loyalty records, and reservation history—core operational assets for a hospitality business. Without the PMS/CRS software licenses, the Company may lose its property management and central reservation systems on day one.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend the Bill of Sale to conform its definition of "Hotel Operating Assets" to the SPA definition, or add separate assignment schedules for (i) software licenses and service agreements, (ii) Guest Data, and (iii) Hotel Domain Names.
- For domain names: require the Service Affiliate to execute registrar transfer authorizations (or provide power of attorney to Tallmast) as a Closing deliverable.
- For Guest Data: require delivery of all data in a usable format (not merely a representation of compliance) and confirm no contractual restrictions on transfer.
- For PMS/CRS: identify the vendors and confirm whether the licenses are assignable or require vendor consent; add any required consents to Schedule 7.2(c).
- **Flag for client call:** The client's operations team should confirm which specific PMS/CRS platforms are in use and whether vendor consent will be needed. This may require additional lead time.

### 3. General Representation Survival Effectively Truncated to Six Months

**Where:** SPA § 9.1 (first sentence); SPA § 2.5(b) (Release Date = 6 months post-Closing).

**Issue:** Section 9.1 provides that General Representations survive "until the earlier of (a) the date that is eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date and (b) the date on which the Escrow Amount is released pursuant to Section 2.5." The Escrow Amount is released six months after Closing (SPA § 2.5(b)). The "earlier of" language means General Representations expire at the 6-month escrow release, not at 18 months. The 18-month period is illusory.

**Why it matters:** The Hotel Pellam renovation (floors 4–7, GMP $4.75M, $1.21M paid as of August 31, 2026) has substantial completion scheduled for February 15, 2027 (Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 11). If Closing occurs in, say, November 2026, the 6-month survival period expires in May 2027—after renovation completion. Construction defects, latent issues, or disputes with the general contractor (Torrance & Vail Builders) that surface post-completion would fall outside the indemnification period. Similarly, the Master Linen and Laundry Agreement expires February 28, 2027 (Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 7), and any dispute arising from that transition would be uncovered.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend § 9.1 to decouple survival from the escrow release: General Representations should survive for the full 18 months regardless of when the escrow is released. The escrow is security; it should not define the limitations period.
- Alternatively, extend the escrow Release Date to 18 months post-Closing (matching the intended survival period).
- If Seller resists, negotiate a specific survival extension (to at least 24 months) for representations relating to the Hotel Pellam construction contract and the Real Property Leases.
- **Flag for client call:** The client should confirm the expected Closing date so we can map the survival period against known future events (renovation completion, contract expirations, liquor license renewals).

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 4. Draibeir's Unconditional Guarantee of Company's IP Indemnification

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 6.2 (Assignee indemnification); § 6.4 (Guarantee); SPA § 3.3(c) (Buyer delivers IP Assignment Agreement "duly executed by Buyer as guarantor").

**Issue:** Under IP Assignment Agreement § 6.2, the Company (Assignee) indemnifies the Brand Affiliate (Assignor) for post-closing third-party claims that the Company's use of the Hotel Marks infringes any IP rights, for the operation of the Hotels under the marks, and for covenant breaches. Draibeir, as Guarantor, unconditionally guarantees the Company's obligations under § 6.2 as "primary obligor and not merely as surety" (§ 6.4(a)), with broad waivers (§ 6.4(c)) and no release upon the Company's insolvency (§ 6.4(d)(iii)).

The guarantee is subject to the SPA § 9.4 limitations by incorporation (§ 6.2 opening language), so Draibeir's maximum exposure is the Cap ($14,850,000) less the Deductible ($742,500). However, the guarantee is not subject to the survival period in § 9.1—there is no time limit on the Guaranteed Obligations other than the underlying claim.

**Why it matters:** The Brand Affiliate owned and used the Hotel Marks for years pre-closing. If a third party asserts a prior-rights claim (e.g., a common-law user in one of the five markets), the claim will likely be brought against the current owner (the Company post-closing). The Company would then owe the Brand Affiliate under § 6.2(a), and Draibeir would be on the hook as guarantor—even though the risk was created by the Brand Affiliate's pre-closing conduct. This is an asymmetric allocation: the party that created the IP risk (Brand Affiliate) is indemnified by the party that acquired the marks (Company/Draibeir).

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a corresponding indemnity from the Brand Affiliate (or Seller) to the Company for pre-closing IP infringement claims, so the risk is borne by the party that created it.
- Limit Draibeir's guarantee to the same survival period as the SPA indemnification (i.e., 18 months for General Reps).
- Alternatively, make the guarantee subject to a separate cap lower than the full $14.85M (e.g., 5% of Purchase Price).
- **Flag for client call:** The client should understand that this guarantee is a direct, uncapped-in-time liability of Draibeir (the parent LLC), not just the Company. If the Company is later sold or restructured, the guarantee survives (§ 6.4(d)(ii)).

### 5. Seller's Unrestricted Assignment and Release Right

**Where:** SPA § 11.6 (second to last sentence).

**Issue:** "Seller may assign this Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations under it, to any Person without the consent of Buyer, and upon any such assignment Seller shall thereupon be released from the obligations so assigned." This permits Seller to assign its indemnification obligations to any entity—potentially an empty shell or an insolvent affiliate—and walk away.

**Why it matters:** Combined with the 6-month effective survival period (Issue 3 above), Draibeir's primary recourse for post-closing indemnification claims is the escrow. Once the escrow is released at 6 months, Draibeir's only remedy is a direct claim against Seller. If Seller has assigned its indemnification obligations to a creditworthy entity, Draibeir is protected. If Seller assigns them to a shell, Draibeir's post-escrow recovery is illusory.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend § 11.6 to require Buyer's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment of Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX.
- Alternatively, require that any assignee of Seller's indemnification obligations have net worth of at least the Cap amount ($14,850,000) and that the assignment not relieve Seller of liability unless the assignee's obligations are guaranteed by an entity with equivalent creditworthiness.

### 6. No Transition Services Agreement for Centralized Operations

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 5 (Hotel Services Agreement); Press Release § 2.3; SPA (no TSA provision).

**Issue:** The Hotel Services Agreement (between Service Affiliate and Company, dated January 1, 2018) provides centralized reservations, revenue management, sales and marketing, accounting, IT, and procurement services for a fee of 2.75% of gross operating revenue plus costs. It is terminable on 30 days' notice, and "Seller will cause it to be terminated effective as of the Closing, without cost to the Company" (Disclosure Schedule § 9, item 5). The press release publicly states that "Draibeir and Bruneis will work together on an orderly transition of the centralized services" (Press Release § 2.3). No Transition Services Agreement (TSA) is included in the deal documents, and no covenant in the SPA requires Seller or the Service Affiliate to provide post-closing transition services.

**Why it matters:** On day one post-closing, the Company loses all centralized back-office functions. For a five-property portfolio, this means Draibeir must immediately stand up (or contract for) reservations, revenue management, marketing, accounting, IT, and procurement. The public commitment to an "orderly transition" creates expectations with guests, vendors, and employees that are not contractually supported. If the Service Affiliate pulls services abruptly, there is no remedy.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a TSA covering the core services (reservations/CRS, revenue management, accounting, IT) for a period of 6–12 months post-closing, at the existing 2.75% fee or a mutually agreed transition fee.
- Include service-level commitments and a wind-down schedule.
- Make the TSA an Ancillary Agreement under the SPA so that the SPA's dispute-resolution and governing-law provisions apply.
- **Flag for client call:** The client's operations team should identify which services they can absorb immediately versus which require a transition period, and what the cost of stand-up would be if no TSA is agreed.

### 7. Lisset Hotel Liquor License Expiring December 31, 2026

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule § 10, Part 1, item 3; Disclosure Schedule § 15 (Schedule 7.2(c)).

**Issue:** The Lisset Hotel's liquor license (CHT-ALC-20-0988, issued by the City of Savannah Revenue Department / Georgia DOR) expires December 31, 2026. The press release states the parties "expect the transaction to close in the fourth quarter of 2026" (Press Release § 1.5, § 7.1). If Closing occurs in late November or December 2026, the license may expire before or immediately after Closing. The Required Consents include "issuance of any amended or reissued license, or any interim or temporary operating authority" (Disclosure Schedule § 15), but there is no specific timeline or long-stop date tied to this particular license.

**Why it matters:** A gap in the Lisset Hotel's liquor license would interrupt revenue from its restaurant, rooftop bar, and in-room minibars. In Savannah's competitive hospitality market, even a short interruption could damage the property's standing.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm the current status of the Lisset license renewal application. If not yet filed, require Seller to file immediately and to obtain either a renewed license or an interim operating authority as a condition to Closing.
- Add a specific long-stop date for this consent (e.g., "no later than 10 days before the anticipated Closing Date").
- **Flag for client call:** Confirm the expected Closing date with the client so we can assess whether the December 31 expiration creates a timing risk.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 8. IP Assignment Agreement – Knowledge Qualifier References Wrong Party

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 5.1(d).

**Issue:** Section 5.1(d) represents that "each registration listed on Schedule A is subsisting and, to the Knowledge of Seller, valid and enforceable." "Knowledge of Seller" is defined in the SPA § 1.1 by reference to five individuals at Seller (Bruneis Hotel Holdings). But the representation is made by the Brand Affiliate (Bruneis Brand Holdings LLC), not by Seller. The Brand Affiliate is a separate legal entity. It is unclear whether the knowledge of Seller's executives should be imputed to the Brand Affiliate's representation.

**Why it matters:** In a dispute over the enforceability of a registration, the Brand Affiliate could argue that the knowledge qualifier refers to persons who are not its employees and whose knowledge should not be attributed to it. This creates ambiguity that could narrow the scope of the representation.

**Recommendation:** Amend § 5.1(d) to replace "to the Knowledge of Seller" with "to the Knowledge of Assignor" and add a definition of "Knowledge of Assignor" in the IP Assignment Agreement referencing the relevant Brand Affiliate personnel. Alternatively, remove the knowledge qualifier entirely for the validity/enforceability element.

### 9. SABLEWICK STAYS – Intent-to-Use Application Requires Post-Closing Action

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement Schedule A, Part 2, item 2; IP Assignment Agreement § 2.3.

**Issue:** The SABLEWICK STAYS application (Serial No. 99/016,884) was filed May 21, 2026 on an intent-to-use basis (Section 1(b)). No statement of use has been filed. Under § 2.3, the application is assigned "as part of the business to which the mark pertains, which business is ongoing and existing, as required by 15 U.S.C. § 1060(a)(1)." Post-closing, the Company (as new owner) must file a statement of use (or amendment to allege use) within the statutory window, or the application will be abandoned.

**Why it matters:** If the statement of use deadline is missed, the SABLEWICK STAYS mark is lost. This mark covers Classes 35 and 43 and appears to be a newer brand extension (the domain sablewickstays.com and TikTok handle @sablewickstays are active per Disclosure Schedule § 3).

**Recommendation:**
- Calendar the statement of use deadline immediately (typically 6 months from filing, extendable in 6-month increments for up to 3 years, so the first deadline is approximately November 21, 2026—potentially before Closing).
- Confirm with Seller whether any extensions have been filed.
- Ensure the IP Assignment Agreement's further-assurances provision (§ 4.2) and the SPA § 5.9 cover cooperation on the statement of use filing.
- Assign ownership of this item to the client's IP counsel for post-closing docketing.

### 10. Press Release Commitments Exceed Contractual Obligations

**Where:** Press Release § 2.3 ("orderly transition"); § 4.4 ("intends to complete the Hotel Pellam guest-room renovation"); § 4.5 ("expects the hotels' leadership teams to remain in place"); § 6.2 (12-month wage protection).

**Issue:** The press release makes several forward-looking commitments that are not fully supported by the SPA:
- "Orderly transition" of centralized services (no TSA; see Issue 6).
- "Intends to complete" the Pellam renovation (the construction contract is with the Company, so this is the Company's obligation, not Draibeir's direct covenant; but the GMP contract could be terminated by the contractor for non-payment if the Company's cash flow is disrupted).
- "Expects the hotels' leadership teams to remain in place" (SPA § 5.11 only protects base wages for 12 months; it does not require retention of any specific individual).

**Why it matters:** If these expectations are not met, the client faces reputational risk with guests, employees, and vendors who read the press release. The wage protection in § 6.2 is contractually supported (SPA § 5.11), but the other items are aspirational.

**Recommendation:**
- Review the press release with the client before it is finalized (if not yet issued) or be prepared to manage expectations internally.
- Ensure the client's board is aware that "expects leadership teams to remain in place" is not a contractual obligation and that key-person retention should be addressed through separate retention agreements if desired.
- Confirm that the Pellam construction contract (Torrance & Vail) does not have a change-of-control termination right that could be triggered by the stock sale.
