## 1. Bottom Line

The execution draft should not be signed yet. Five problems matter most:

1. **Dr. Santangelo's non-compete, non-solicit and invention assignment end automatically at Closing** (EEA §9.2). The SPA closing condition would still be satisfied (SPA §7.2(d)).
2. **The SPA does not carry over several LOI protections.** Product recall liabilities are not indemnified at all. Pre-Closing Taxes are capped. Taxes are not fundamental. There is no cash-free, debt-free mechanism (LOI §§2, 4, 5(c)).
3. **There is no escrow, holdback or guaranty.** Recovery depends on the post-Closing credit of Glosiv.
4. **The TSA does not cover the MRI and ultrasound lines,** and its governing law, forum and liability terms conflict with the SPA.
5. **Exclusivity appears to have lapsed.** The LOI exclusivity period ran 75 days from March 9, 2026, so it expired on or about May 23, 2026 (LOI §§13, 16(c)). The folder contains no extension.

Priority 1 items must be resolved before signing. Several require client decisions, which are flagged below and consolidated in Section 6.

## 4. Priority 2: Significant Drafting and Economic Points

### 9. TSA governing law and forum conflict with the SPA

- **Where:** TSA §12.6 (New York law; exclusive jurisdiction of Manhattan courts; jury waiver). SPA §11.4 (each Ancillary Agreement controls over the SPA on its own subject matter; the TSA is an Ancillary Agreement under SPA §1.1). SPA §11.9–11.10 (Delaware law; exclusive Court of Chancery). NDA §§10.6–10.7 (Delaware).
- **Why it matters:** Under SPA §11.4, the TSA's New York clauses may govern TSA disputes, creating split forums for related claims about service failures and indemnity.
- **Recommended fix:** Conform TSA §12.6 to Delaware law and the Court of Chancery. Alternatively, carve forum and governing law out of SPA §11.4.

### 10. The TSA sharply limits Glosiv's liability for service failures

- **Where:** TSA §9.1 (indemnity only for gross negligence, willful misconduct or violation of law in performing the Services, or breach of §7 or §8). TSA §10.1 (excludes consequential damages and lost profits, with carve-outs only for third-party indemnity, §7 and §8 breaches, and gross negligence or willful misconduct). TSA §10.2 (aggregate cap equal to fees paid and payable, which may be small early in the term). TSA §2.2(a)–(c) (standard of performance). TSA §2.3(a) (complaint forwarding within two Business Days).
- **Why it matters:** An ordinary-negligence failure in field service or complaint handling that injures a customer is not indemnified. Regulatory penalties and recall costs caused by late complaint forwarding could be argued to be consequential and excluded. The fee-based cap is lowest in the early months, when risk of transition failures is highest.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Indemnify for negligence and breach of §§2.2 and 2.3.
  - Expressly exclude regulatory fines, recall and corrective-action costs, and remediation from the consequential-damages exclusion.
  - Set a fixed-dollar floor for the cap, or leave regulatory and complaint-handling failures uncapped.
  - Add service credits to Exhibit B.
- **Client decision:** **Yes.** This is a risk-allocation choice.

### 11. TSA pricing is uncapped and internally inconsistent

- **Where:** TSA §5.1 (fully loaded cost, "without markup," with overhead allocated on the pre-Closing basis). TSA §3.2 (10% fee increase during extension). TSA Exhibit B (monthly estimates: B-1 $1,150,000; B-2 $185,000; B-3 $240,000; B-4 $310,000, totaling $1,885,000 per month, or roughly $22.6 million over 12 months). TSA §5.4 (one audit per calendar year; records kept two years). LOI §6 (cost-based pricing).
- **Why it matters:** The estimated annual total is roughly 12% of the Purchase Price. Seller controls overhead allocation and there is no cap, benchmarking or not-to-exceed amount. The 10% extension premium contradicts "without markup" in §5.1.
- **Recommended fix:** Set a fixed or not-to-exceed monthly fee for the initial term. Define the overhead methodology and exclude Glosiv corporate overhead not attributable to the Company. Delete or cap the 10% premium. Increase audit frequency. Reconcile the Exhibit B estimates to the Company's historical cost base.
- **Client decision:** **Yes.** Compare the estimates to the Company's financial model.

### 12. Continuity of service software, shared systems and intercompany services

- **Where:** TSA §6.2 (each party keeps its own IP; Recipient's license to Provider systems runs only during the Service Term). TSA §6.3 (only work product created "exclusively" for Recipient is owned by Recipient). TSA §4.2 (source code access during migration, limited to repositories "that relate to the Services"). TSA Exhibit A item 4 (AurexServ). SPA §4.12 (Company "owns or has the valid right to use" service software). SPA §6.10 (all intercompany arrangements other than the TSA are terminated at Closing). TSA §2.1(d) (ERP, CRM, quality management and email). LOI §6 (TSA to cover "the services it currently provides").
- **Why it matters:**
  - If AurexServ, its hosting or remote diagnostics depend on Glosiv-owned code, the Company may lose its service software when the TSA ends.
  - The Company's quality records appear to sit on Glosiv systems (TSA §2.1(d), Exhibit B item B-4).
  - The TSA does not cover payroll, HR and benefits, insurance, finance, treasury, tax or legal services, and SPA §6.10 cuts off all intercompany arrangements other than the TSA at Closing.
  - The TSA has no right to hire the Glosiv personnel who deliver services. SPA §6.7(b) protects only Company employees.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Map every intercompany service in diligence and add the necessary services to the TSA.
  - Require assignment of AurexServ and Company-specific software to the Company, or a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free license to any Glosiv-owned component, with source code escrow.
  - Set a dated quality-records export and migration plan. Add a default migration plan if the parties do not agree within 45 days (TSA §4.2).
  - Add a right to hire dedicated field engineers and complaint staff at a stated fee.
- **Client decision:** **Yes.** Decide whether to add HR, finance and related services and at what cost.

### 13. No insurance coverage covenant for pre-Closing product claims

- **Where:** SPA §4.19 (lists policies; the rep does not address occurrence-based coverage or access). No insurance covenant in Article 6. SPA §6.10.
- **Why it matters:** Pre-Closing products may be covered by Glosiv's policies. Without a covenant, the Company may lose access to those policies after Closing.

### 14. Fraud is narrowly defined and Seller's exclusive-remedy structure is restrictive

- **Where:** SPA §1.1 "Fraud" (actual and intentional fraud under Delaware common law, in making Article 4 representations only). SPA §10.9 (exclusive remedy, with Fraud as the sole carve-out other than specific performance). SPA §10.5(b). SPA §8.2 ("willful breach" undefined).
- **Why it matters:** Fraud in closing certificates (SPA §§3.2(g), 7.2(e)), in covenants or in the Disclosure Schedule falls outside the definition. Recklessness is excluded. Undefined "willful breach" creates a second, unclear standard for pre-Closing termination liability.

### 15. The materiality scrape applies only to Losses

- **Where:** SPA §10.7 ("for purposes of calculating Losses").
- **Why it matters:** Seller can argue that materiality qualifiers still control whether a breach occurred, so a rep may be breached only if the breach is material. Applying the scrape only to Losses would narrow recovery.
- **Recommended fix:** Apply the scrape both to determining whether a breach occurred and to calculating Losses, for indemnity purposes.

### 16. Disclosure Schedule is missing, Knowledge is undefined, and general disclosure is broad

- **Where:** SPA Art. 4 lead-in ("Except as set forth in the Disclosure Schedule"). SPA §1.1 "Disclosure Schedule" (delivered on signing). SPA §11.8 (cross-disclosure where relevance is "reasonably apparent on the face"). SPA §§4.4(c), 4.5, 4.9, 4.11, 4.17, 4.19 (schedules referenced). ### 17. Interim operating covenants are generic and miss regulatory events

- **Where:** SPA §6.1 (ordinary course; preserve relationships; no amendment of Material Contracts). SPA §6.2 (access). SPA §6.3 (commercially reasonable efforts). Add consent rights over recalls, compensation changes for key employees, changes to Company Products, and amendments to Material Contracts. Confirm whether any FDA or other regulatory change-of-ownership notice is required (regulatory counsel). SPA §4.5 and §7.2(c) reference only the OEM consent and HSR filings in the schedule.

### 18. Halvorsen OEM consent: standard of effort, cost and conditions

### 21. Tax drafting and timing conflicts

- **Where:** SPA §6.8 (Seller prepares pre-Closing returns; Buyer only "cooperates reasonably"; no review or consent right). SPA §6.8 closing-of-the-books allocation "as of the end of the Closing Date." SPA §3.1 (Closing effective at 12:01 a.m. ET on the Closing Date).
