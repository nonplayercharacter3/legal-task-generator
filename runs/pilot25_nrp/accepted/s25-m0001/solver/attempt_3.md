## Issue 1 – Kestrova Change of Control Triggers Immediate Termination Right

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §17.2; Amended and Restated Service Level Schedule §8.1, §8.3.

**Explanation.** Section 17.2 of the Distribution Agreement provides that any Change of Control of Distributor "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Distributor requiring Supplier's prior written consent under Section 17.1." A Change of Control is defined to include "any transaction or series of related transactions by which any person or group acquires, directly or indirectly, more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity of Distributor or of any entity Controlling Distributor." The Kestrova transaction—sale of 100% of Sterin Field Systems' equity by Sterin Holdings, Inc.—falls squarely within prong (a) of that definition.

Section 17.2 further provides that a Change of Control without Supplier's prior written consent "is a material breach of this Agreement, and Supplier may, on written notice, immediately terminate this Agreement and suspend shipments of Products under any Purchase Order, without opportunity to cure."

Service Level Schedule §8.1 discloses the pending Stock Purchase Agreement and expected January 15, 2027 closing. Section 8.3 states expressly: "Nothing in this Section 8 constitutes Supplier's consent under Section 17.2 of the Distribution Agreement."

Pridort is therefore on notice of the transaction and has affirmatively disclaimed consent. If the Kestrova deal closes on schedule without a separate consent from Pridort, Sterin will be in immediate material breach, and Pridort can terminate and stop all shipments overnight.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** This is the single most time-critical exposure. Sterin is a distributor whose business model depends on the exclusive supply relationship. Loss of the agreement at closing would strand Sterin (or Kestrova, post-closing) with no product supply, no trademark license, and potentially with inventory it cannot sell (since the sell-off right in §16.3(b) is only 180 days). The "without opportunity to cure" language eliminates any argument that Sterin can fix the problem after the fact.

**Recommendation.** Before the Kestrova closing, Sterin must obtain Pridort's written consent under §17.2. We should:
- Open a negotiation with Pridort specifically on the consent, ideally well before the January 15, 2027 target.
- Seek to make the consent a condition to closing in the Stock Purchase Agreement (if not already).
- In the consent negotiation, request that Pridort waive the "deemed assignment" characterization and confirm that the existing agreement continues on its current terms with Kestrova as the controlling entity.
- If Pridort conditions consent on economic concessions (e.g., higher minimums, price increases), quantify those before agreeing.

**Facts we need from the client:** Has Sterin raised the consent issue with Pridort? Does the Stock Purchase Agreement contain a condition that Sterin obtain Pridort's consent? What is Pridort's stated position?

## Issue 2 – Direct Conflict on Whether Recall Costs Are Subject to the Liability Cap

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §12.3(d); Quality Agreement §9.4; Distribution Agreement §20.4 (order of precedence).

**Explanation.** The two documents are irreconcilable on this point:

- Distribution Agreement §12.3(d) states that the limitations in §§12.1 and 12.2 "shall not apply to … (d) Supplier's recall cost reimbursement under Section 9.5, which is excluded from the Section 12.1 cap."
- Quality Agreement §9.4 states: "Supplier's aggregate liability for Recall Costs is subject to the limitation set forth in Section 12.1 of the Distribution Agreement, and all amounts paid or payable by Supplier in respect of Recall Costs shall be counted toward that limitation."

Under the order of precedence in Distribution Agreement §20.4, the Quality Agreement prevails over the Distribution Agreement for "quality, nonconformance and recall matters only." A recall cost reimbursement is unambiguously a "recall matter." Therefore, as the documents are currently drafted, the Quality Agreement controls, and recall costs are subject to the 12-month-payment cap in §12.1.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** The cap in §12.1 is "the amounts paid or payable by Distributor in the 12 months preceding the claim." If Sterin is a growing distributor, 12 months of purchases might be, say, $3–5 million. A serious product recall (e.g., a battery thermal event affecting the AX-B60 across the installed base) could easily generate recall costs well in excess of that amount—customer notifications, retrieval, field labor, replacement units, refunds. Under the Quality Agreement's language, Sterin would bear the excess. The Distribution Agreement's language would have left recall costs uncapped. This is a difference of potentially millions of dollars in a worst-case scenario.

Additionally, the Parent Guaranty §2.2 mirrors Supplier's limitations: "Every limitation, exclusion and defense available to Supplier under the Guaranteed Documents is available to Guarantor." So even the Pridort Holdings guaranty does not protect Sterin above the cap.

**Recommendation.** Before the next amendment cycle or in connection with the Kestrova consent negotiation, Sterin should demand that Quality Agreement §9.4 be conformed to Distribution Agreement §12.3(d), deleting the sentence that subjects Recall Costs to the §12.1 cap. If Pridort resists, a fallback position would be to raise the cap specifically for recall costs (e.g., 24 months of payments, or a fixed dollar floor tied to the product recall insurance limit in §13.1).

**Facts we need from the client:** What was the commercial understanding during negotiation? Was the §12.3(d) exclusion from the cap a point Sterin specifically negotiated for? If so, that strengthens the argument that the Quality Agreement §9.4 language was a drafting error.

## Issue 3 – Deemed Acceptance Waives Warranty Claims; Already in Active Dispute (Lot SR-4417)

**Documents and Sections:** Quality Agreement §6.2; Distribution Agreement §9.1; Service Level Schedule, Annex B, OM-01.

**Explanation.** Quality Agreement §6.2 requires Sterin to reject any Product with a Nonconformity "discoverable at incoming inspection" within ten (10) Business Days after delivery. The consequence of missing the deadline is severe: "Any Product not rejected within that period shall be deemed accepted, and Distributor waives all claims with respect to any Nonconformity in that Product that was discoverable at incoming inspection, including warranty claims under Section 9 of the Distribution Agreement."

This effectively collapses the 12-month product warranty (DA §9.1) into a 10-Business-Day window for any defect that could be found at incoming inspection. The warranty survives only for "Latent Nonconformities" (QA §2.5: "a Nonconformity that could not reasonably be discovered through the incoming inspection procedures in Section 6.1").

**The live dispute.** Annex B, OM-01 records that Lot SR-4417 (140 AX-60 spray drones) was delivered August 4, 2026. Sterin gave written rejection notice on August 25, 2026. Pridort rejected the notice as untimely on September 3, 2026.

Counting 10 Business Days from Tuesday, August 4, 2026: August 5, 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18. The deadline was end of business Tuesday, August 18, 2026. Sterin's notice on August 25 was five Business Days late. On the plain language, Pridort's position is correct.

**However**, the incoming inspection procedure for the AX-60 (QA Schedule 2, Part A) calls for a "nozzle flow-controller static flow test on sampled units." The word "sampled" is critical. If the inspection plan tests only a subset of units, a nonconformity affecting the full lot (or a portion not sampled) may not have been "discoverable at incoming inspection" within the meaning of §6.2. If the nonconformity was latent, QA §7.1 governs, and the 10-day deadline does not apply—instead, Sterin must notify within 30 days of becoming aware.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** If Pridort's characterization prevails, Sterin has waived all claims on 140 AX-60 drones, including the 12-month warranty. If those drones subsequently fail in the field (e.g., incorrect spray application rates leading to customer crop damage), Sterin bears the cost. The financial exposure depends on the unit price and the nature of the nonconformity.

**Recommendation.**
- Immediately assess whether the nozzle flow-controller nonconformity in Lot SR-4417 was truly "discoverable" under the sampling plan. If the sampling plan tested, e.g., 3 of 140 units, and the nonconformity was not present in the sampled units, Sterin has a strong argument that it was a Latent Nonconformity.
- Preserve all evidence: the incoming inspection records, the sampling plan Pridort provided under QA §4.1, the rejection notice, and any field failure data from the 140 units.
- In the monthly operations review (SLS §9.1), raise the dispute formally and request Pridort's production of the sampling plan and inspection criteria it provided under §4.1.
- If the dispute cannot be resolved, note that QA §6.5 provides for referral to an independent testing laboratory, but that mechanism applies only to "conformance" disputes under Section 6—it does not resolve the threshold question of whether the nonconformity was "discoverable at incoming inspection" (i.e., whether §6.2 or §7.1 governs).
- Going forward, implement a strict internal protocol to ensure rejection notices go out well within the 10-Business-Day window, with a designated owner and escalation path.

**Facts we need from the client:** What was the sampling plan for Lot SR-4417? How many units were actually tested at incoming inspection? What was the specific nature of the nozzle flow-controller nonconformity (e.g., flow rate out of spec, mechanical defect)? Has Sterin received any field failures from the 140 units? What is the unit price of the AX-60?

## Issue 4 – Minimum Purchase Commitment Has No Carve-Out for Supplier Supply Failures

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §4.3; Service Level Schedule, Annex B, OM-02.

**Explanation.** Section 4.3 requires Sterin to purchase at least the minimum quantities in Exhibit D each contract year. If Sterin misses the minimum, "Supplier may, as its sole right under this Section 4.3 on written notice given within ninety (90) days after the end of that contract year, convert Distributor's appointment to non-exclusive for the remainder of the Term."

There is no exception for Supplier's failure to supply. If Pridort cannot deliver the products Sterin ordered (due to its own production issues, force majeure, or backorders), Sterin still owes the minimum purchase. The consequence—loss of exclusivity—is severe: it would allow Pridort to appoint other distributors or sell directly in the 12-state Territory.

**The live context.** OM-02 records that 220 AX-B60 batteries were backordered as of September 30, 2026, with Pridort committing to ship by November 30, 2026. If those batteries are part of Sterin's minimum purchase commitment for contract year 2026 and Pridort's delay causes Sterin to miss the minimum, Pridort could convert the appointment to non-exclusive.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** Exclusivity is the economic foundation of Sterin's investment in training, marketing, and infrastructure for the AX-series. Losing it would undermine Sterin's business case and its value to Kestrova (which is presumably acquiring Sterin in part for this distribution relationship).

**Recommendation.**
- Negotiate a supply-failure carve-out: if Sterin's failure to meet the minimum is caused by Supplier's failure to deliver accepted Purchase Orders within the applicable lead times (or by a force majeure event), the minimum should be reduced by the undelivered quantity.
- In the interim, track all backorders and delivery failures meticulously and document the causal link to any shortfall in the minimum.
- Confirm what the Exhibit D minimums actually are (the exhibit is referenced but not reproduced in the documents provided).

**Facts we need from the client:** What are the actual minimum quantities in Exhibit D for contract year 2026? How many units has Sterin purchased to date in 2026? Are the 220 backordered AX-B60 units part of the minimum? Has Sterin placed orders that Pridort has not yet filled?

## Issue 5 – Asymmetric Assignment Rights

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §17.1; Parent Guaranty §4.1(c).

**Explanation.** Section 17.1 permits Supplier to assign the Agreement without consent "to an Affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of its business relating to the Products that assumes Supplier's obligations in writing." Distributor may not assign without consent (and a Change of Control is deemed an assignment under §17.2).

Parent Guaranty §4.1(c) confirms that the guaranty is not affected by "any assignment by Supplier permitted under Section 17.1 of the Distribution Agreement." So if Pridort Aerial Robotics is sold to a third party, the new owner steps into the agreement, and Pridort Holdings' guaranty continues—but only if Pridort Holdings remains the parent. If the acquirer is not Pridort Holdings, the guaranty's continuing effect depends on §4.1(c)'s broad language ("any change in the ownership, corporate structure or existence of Supplier or Guarantor").

**Why it matters to Sterin.** Sterin has locked itself into a 5-year initial term (plus auto-renewals) with a counterparty that can be sold to anyone—including a direct competitor of Kestrova or an entity with weaker financials—without Sterin's consent. Sterin's exit is constrained by the Change of Control provision; Pridort's is not.

**Recommendation.** In connection with the Kestrova consent negotiation, seek a mutualization of the assignment provision: require Supplier to obtain Distributor's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment to a non-Affiliate successor. At minimum, require that any successor assume the Parent Guaranty or provide equivalent credit support.

## Issue 6 – Peak Season Orders Above 125% of Forecast Are Excluded from Fill-Rate Measurement

**Documents and Sections:** Amended and Restated Service Level Schedule §3.3.

**Explanation.** Section 3.3 provides that Peak Season (March 1–July 31) orders exceeding 125% of the forecast delivered 90 days earlier "are excluded from the fill-rate measure in Section 4.1." Supplier only owes "commercially reasonable efforts" on those units.

Agricultural demand is highly weather-dependent. A late winter followed by an early spring thaw can cause demand to spike well above 125% of forecast. In that scenario, Pridort can miss delivery on the incremental units without triggering service credits, and Sterin cannot claim the fill-rate failure as a service-level breach.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** Peak Season is when Sterin makes the majority of its revenue. If Pridort systematically under-forecasts capacity and then invokes the 125% exclusion, Sterin loses its primary performance remedy during the most critical period.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate to raise the threshold (e.g., 150%) or to provide that orders above the threshold still count toward the fill rate if Sterin provided the forecast at least 120 days (rather than 90) in advance. Alternatively, seek a separate service credit for peak-season shortfalls that cause Sterin to lose customer orders.

## Issue 7 – Service Level Schedule Obligations May Not Survive Termination

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §16.4, §16.5; Amended and Restated Service Level Schedule §6 (second-line support), §7 (parts and training).

**Explanation.** Section 16.4 requires Supplier to make spare parts and second-line technical support available for 5 years post-termination. However, §16.5 lists the surviving sections: "Sections 9, 11, 12, 15, 16.3, 16.4 and 20, and any other provision that by its nature should survive." The Service Level Schedule is not a "section" of the Distribution Agreement in the enumerated sense; it is Schedule 2. The specific response-time targets in SLS §6.3, the parts stocking obligation in §7.1, and the training commitment in §7.3 are not clearly captured by the §16.5 survival clause.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** After termination, Sterin will have an installed base of drones in the field that need support. If the SLA response times (1-hour initial response for Severity 1, etc.) do not survive, Pridort could argue it owes only a general obligation to "make spare parts and second-line technical support available" without any specific performance standard. For a safety-critical product (agricultural spray drones), degraded support post-termination creates regulatory and customer-relationship risk for Sterin.

**Recommendation.** Seek an express survival provision for the Service Level Schedule's support and parts obligations, or amend §16.4 to incorporate the specific SLA targets by reference.

## Issue 9 – 5% Annual Price Increase Right

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §5.1.

**Explanation.** Pridort may increase prices once per contract year on 90 days' notice, up to 5%. Over a 5-year Initial Term, compounded annual increases of 5% amount to approximately 28% total price escalation. For a distributor operating on thin margins in agricultural equipment, this is material.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** Sterin's pricing to customers is likely set at the beginning of the selling season. A mid-season price increase (even with 90 days' notice) could force Sterin to either absorb the cost or pass it through at an awkward time. Combined with the minimum purchase commitment (Issue 4), Sterin is locked into volume while facing rising unit costs.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate to tie price adjustments to a published index (e.g., PPI for industrial equipment) with a cap, or to require 180 days' notice for adjustments that take effect during Peak Season.

## Issue 10 – Force Majeure Allocation Standard Is Weak

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §18.2.

**Explanation.** During a force majeure event that reduces Supplier's capacity, Pridort must allocate available Products to Sterin "no less favorably than to any other customer." This is a parity standard, not a priority standard. If Pridort is also supplying national accounts (Exhibit C) or customers outside the Territory, Sterin could receive the same pro-rata share as any other customer—potentially far less than 100% of its orders.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** As the exclusive distributor for 12 states, Sterin's customers expect reliable supply. If a force majeure event (e.g., a factory fire, a chip shortage) causes Pridort to allocate equally across all customers, Sterin's territory could be severely under-served while out-of-territory customers are made whole.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a priority allocation for Sterin during force majeure, at least for Peak Season, given that Sterin is the exclusive channel for the Territory and has made territory-specific investments.

## Issue 11 – Parent Guaranty Mirrors All of Supplier's Limitations

**Documents and Sections:** Parent Guaranty §2.2.

**Explanation.** Section 2.2 provides that "Guarantor's liability under this Guaranty shall not exceed the liability that Supplier would have to Distributor under the Guaranteed Documents for the same matter. Every limitation, exclusion and defense available to Supplier under the Guaranteed Documents is available to Guarantor."

This means the guaranty is only as strong as the underlying agreement. If the recall cost cap issue (Issue 2) resolves against Sterin, the guaranty is also capped. If the liability cap in DA §12.1 applies, the guarantor's exposure is limited to the same amount. The guaranty adds credit support (Pridort Holdings' balance sheet) but does not expand the scope of obligations.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** The guaranty was presumably obtained to protect Sterin against Pridort Aerial Robotics' potential insolvency (it is a subsidiary, and the parent is the credit-worthy entity). But if the underlying obligations are narrowly capped, the guaranty's practical value is diminished.

**Recommendation.** In any renegotiation, seek to decouple the guaranty from certain limitations—specifically, provide that the guaranty covers recall costs and indemnification obligations without regard to the §12.1 cap (consistent with DA §12.3(a) and (d) as originally drafted).

## Issue 12 – Product Recall Insurance May Not Cover Sterin as Additional Insured

**Documents and Sections:** Distribution Agreement §13.1, §13.2.

**Explanation.** Section 13.1 requires Supplier to maintain "product recall insurance of at least $2,000,000." Section 13.2 requires each party to name the other "as an additional insured on its liability policies." The question is whether "product recall insurance" is a "liability policy" within the meaning of §13.2. Product recall insurance typically covers the cost of recalling product (retrieval, disposal, replacement) and is structured differently from traditional CGL or products liability policies. If it is not a "liability policy," Sterin may not be an additional insured on it, and the $2M coverage may not be directly accessible to Sterin.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** In a recall scenario, Sterin incurs Recall Costs (customer notification, retrieval, field labor, etc.) that it then bills to Pridort under §9.5. If Pridort is slow to pay or disputes the bill, Sterin would want to access the recall insurance directly. If Sterin is not an additional insured on that policy, it must wait for Pridort to claim and then be reimbursed.

**Recommendation.** Confirm with the broker or request a copy of Pridort's product recall insurance policy to verify whether Sterin is named as an additional insured or loss payee. If not, amend §13.2 to expressly include product recall insurance in the additional-insured requirement.

## Issue 13 – Quality Agreement §6.5 Lab Process Does Not Resolve "Discoverable" vs. "Latent" Disputes

**Documents and Sections:** Quality Agreement §6.5, §2.5, §7.1.

**Explanation.** Section 6.5 provides that unresolved disputes over rejections may be referred to an independent testing laboratory, whose findings "are binding on the Parties for purposes of this Section 6." However, the threshold question in the OM-01 dispute is not whether the product is nonconforming (it is), but whether the nonconformity was "discoverable at incoming inspection" (triggering §6.2's 10-day deadline and waiver) or was "latent" (triggering §7.1's 30-day awareness standard and preserving warranty claims). The lab process under §6.5 addresses conformance, not the discoverability characterization.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** If the dispute is framed as a §6.2 issue, the lab can determine whether the nozzle flow-controller met spec, but it cannot determine whether the defect was "discoverable" under the sampling plan. That is a question about the inspection procedure, not the product. Sterin needs a different procedural path.

**Recommendation.** In the monthly operations review, reframe the OM-01 dispute as a §7.1 Latent Nonconformity issue (arguing the sampling plan made the defect undiscoverable at incoming inspection). If Pridort insists on the §6.2 characterization, seek a joint expert determination on the discoverability question, or escalate under SLS §9.3.

## Issue 14 – NDA Non-Solicitation Has Expired; NDA Confidentiality Obligations Remain in Effect

**Documents and Sections:** Mutual NDA §8.1, §9.1–9.2.

**Explanation.** The NDA's 12-month employee non-solicitation (§8.1) expired on November 18, 2025 (12 months after the NDA Effective Date of November 18, 2024). The confidentiality obligations, however, continue: the disclosure period runs until November 18, 2026 (2 years from the NDA Effective Date), and the receiving party's obligations continue for 3 years after the end of the disclosure period (i.e., until November 18, 2029), or indefinitely for trade secrets.

**Why it matters to Sterin.** This is primarily a housekeeping point. The NDA is still operative for information exchanged between November 18, 2024 and November 18, 2026. Given that the Distribution Agreement and Quality Agreement contain their own confidentiality provisions (DA §15), the NDA's continuing effect is somewhat redundant but not harmful. The main practical point is that the NDA does not restrict Sterin's ability to complete the Kestrova transaction (see §7.3, which expressly preserves the right to compete).

**Recommendation.** No action required, but be aware that any confidential information Pridort shared during the pre-agreement evaluation period (November 2024–March 2025) remains subject to the NDA through November 2029.
