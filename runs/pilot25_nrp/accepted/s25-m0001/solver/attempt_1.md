## Issue 1 – Direct Conflict on Whether Recall Costs Are Capped

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 12.3(d): "Supplier's recall cost reimbursement under Section 9.5, which is excluded from the Section 12.1 cap."
- Quality Agreement § 9.4: "Supplier's aggregate liability for Recall Costs is subject to the limitation set forth in Section 12.1 of the Distribution Agreement, and all amounts paid or payable by Supplier in respect of Recall Costs shall be counted toward that limitation."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
These two provisions are irreconcilable. The Distribution Agreement carves recall costs out of the 12-month liability cap; the Quality Agreement pulls them back in. Under the order of precedence in Distribution Agreement § 20.4, the Quality Agreement prevails over the Distribution Agreement for "quality, nonconformance and recall matters." A recall of agricultural spray drones across a 12-state territory—covering customer notification, retrieval, field labor, replacement units, and refunds—could easily exceed 12 months of purchase volume. If the Quality Agreement controls, Sterin's recovery for a major recall is capped at that amount, and the excess falls on Sterin.

**Recommendation:**
Negotiate a conforming amendment. The cleanest fix is to delete the second and third sentences of Quality Agreement § 9.4 and replace them with: "Supplier's recall cost reimbursement obligations under this Section 9.4 are excluded from the limitation of liability in Section 12.1 of the Distribution Agreement, consistent with Section 12.3(d) thereof." If Pridort resists, a fallback is to set a separate, higher cap for recall costs (e.g., 24 months of purchases) that applies in lieu of the general cap.

**Client Question:**
Confirm whether any recall has been initiated or threatened to date, and what Sterin's current inventory and installed-base size would make a realistic worst-case recall cost.

## Issue 2 – Kestrova Change of Control Triggers Termination Right Without Consent

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 17.2: A Change of Control of Distributor (including acquisition of >50% of "any entity Controlling Distributor") is deemed an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent. Without consent, it is a material breach permitting Supplier to "immediately terminate this Agreement and suspend shipments of Products under any Purchase Order, without opportunity to cure."
- Service Level Schedule § 8.1: Discloses that Sterin Holdings, Inc. (Distributor's parent) has a Stock Purchase Agreement with Kestrova Agritech, Inc. to sell 100% of Distributor's equity, expected to close January 15, 2027.
- Service Level Schedule § 8.3 (final sentence): "Nothing in this Section 8 constitutes Supplier's consent under Section 17.2 of the Distribution Agreement."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
The Kestrova transaction is squarely a Change of Control under § 17.2 (it involves both the acquisition of 100% of Distributor's equity and of Sterin Holdings, which Controls Distributor). The documents confirm that Supplier has not consented. If the deal closes on January 15, 2027 without consent, Pridort can terminate the distribution agreement immediately, suspend all shipments, and do so without giving Sterin a cure period. This would strip Sterin (and Kestrova) of its exclusive territory, its inventory repurchase right, and its ongoing supply—potentially mid-peak-season planning.

**Recommendation:**
Sterin must obtain Pridort's written consent under § 17.2 before the Kestrova closing. We recommend:
1. Open the consent discussion immediately, well ahead of the January 15, 2027 target closing.
2. Seek to negotiate the consent as part of a package that may include, for example, a mutual agreement that the consent does not constitute a waiver of any other right, or a side letter confirming that the transition will be orderly (continued shipments through a wind-down period, cooperation on Dealer Portal migration beyond the 40 hours in § 8.3).
3. If Pridort conditions consent on commercial terms (e.g., higher minimums, price adjustment), evaluate whether those conditions are acceptable before the Kestrova closing date is locked.
4. As a contingency, explore whether the Stock Purchase Agreement with Kestrova contains a condition precedent or outside date that can be adjusted if Pridort's consent is delayed.

**Client Question:**
Has Sterin formally requested Pridort's consent under § 17.2? What is the status of any discussions? Does the Kestrova Stock Purchase Agreement contain a regulatory or third-party consent condition that could be extended? What is the outside date under the Stock Purchase Agreement?

## Issue 3 – Deemed Acceptance and Warranty Waiver Already in Dispute (Lot SR-4417)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Quality Agreement § 6.2: If Distributor does not reject a Product within 10 Business Days after delivery, the Product is "deemed accepted, and Distributor waives all claims with respect to any Nonconformity in that Product that was discoverable at incoming inspection, including warranty claims under Section 9 of the Distribution Agreement."
- Service Level Schedule, Annex B, OM-01: Lot SR-4417 (140 AX-60 spray drones) was delivered August 4, 2026. Sterin gave written rejection notice August 25, 2026. Pridort rejected the notice as untimely on September 3, 2026. Sterin disputes and reserves rights.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Counting 10 Business Days from the August 4, 2026 delivery (a Tuesday) yields a deadline of August 18, 2026. Sterin's notice on August 25 is seven calendar days (five Business Days) late. If Pridort's reading is correct, Sterin has waived all claims—including the 12-month product warranty under Distribution Agreement § 9.1—on 140 spray drones with a nozzle flow-controller nonconformity. The nozzle flow-controller is a critical component: a nonconformity here affects spray application rates, which implicates both the product warranty and potentially regulatory compliance (pesticide application equipment laws referenced in Distribution Agreement § 7.3). The financial exposure is the cost of 140 drones plus any field corrections, customer claims, or regulatory issues that flow from the nonconformity.

**Recommendation:**
1. Assess the strength of Sterin's position. Possible arguments: (a) the nonconformity was not "discoverable at incoming inspection" as defined by the sampling plan in Quality Agreement Schedule 2, Part A (the static flow test is performed on "sampled units," not all 140); (b) the 10-Business-Day period should run from when the nonconformity was actually discovered, not from delivery; (c) Pridort's own Certificate of Conformance represented the lot was conforming, which may estop it from relying on the deemed-acceptance clause.
2. If the arguments are weak, negotiate a commercial resolution (e.g., Pridort replaces the affected units or provides a credit) as part of the broader consent discussion in Issue 2.
3. Going forward, implement a strict internal process to ensure incoming inspection and rejection notices are within the 10-Business-Day window, particularly during peak season when volume is highest.

**Client Question:**
What was the specific nature of the nozzle flow-controller nonconformity? Was it discovered through the sampled-unit static flow test or through some other means? How many of the 140 units were actually sampled under the inspection plan? Has Sterin quantified its exposure (replacement cost, customer claims, potential regulatory exposure)?

## Issue 4 – Inventory Repurchase Obligation Is Subject to the Liability Cap

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 16.3(c): On termination or expiration, "Supplier shall, at Distributor's option, repurchase Distributor's unsold, undamaged inventory at the price paid."
- Distribution Agreement § 12.1: Each Party's aggregate liability is capped at "the amounts paid or payable by Distributor in the 12 months preceding the claim."
- Distribution Agreement § 12.3: The exclusions from the cap are (a) indemnification, (b) confidentiality breach, (c) gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and (d) recall cost reimbursement. The repurchase obligation is **not** listed.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
If the distribution agreement is terminated (whether by Pridort under the Change of Control provision, by Sterin for chronic service-level failure, or by mutual agreement), Sterin may hold significant inventory. The repurchase obligation is a liability of Supplier "arising out of or relating to this Agreement" and is therefore subject to the § 12.1 cap. If Sterin's trailing 12-month purchases are, for example, $1.5M but its unsold inventory at termination is $3M, Pridort's repurchase obligation would be capped at $1.5M, leaving Sterin with $1.5M of stranded inventory. This risk is amplified by Issue 2: if Pridort terminates for the Kestrova Change of Control, Sterin could be left with a full inventory and no repurchase recovery above the cap.

**Recommendation:**
Negotiate an amendment to add § 16.3(c) to the list of exclusions in § 12.3, or to create a separate, higher cap for the repurchase obligation (e.g., the full value of unsold, undamaged inventory regardless of the general cap). This is a standard provision in exclusive distribution agreements and should be a reasonable ask.

## Issue 5 – Loss of Exclusivity for a Single Year's Minimum Purchase Shortfall

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 4.3: If Distributor fails to meet the Minimum Purchase Commitment for any contract year, Supplier may convert the appointment to non-exclusive for the remainder of the Term.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Exclusivity is the core economic value of this arrangement. Sterin has built (or is building) a 12-state agricultural drone distribution business on the premise that Pridort will not sell directly or through another channel in the Territory. A single year's shortfall—whether caused by a weak agronomy season, the uncertainty of the Kestrova transition, or Pridort's own supply failures (e.g., the backorders in OM-02)—gives Pridort the right to open the Territory to competitors. The provision gives Supplier the right to convert "as its sole right," meaning Sterin has no offsetting remedy even if the shortfall was caused by Pridort's failure to supply.

**Recommendation:**
1. Negotiate a cure mechanism: allow Sterin to make up the shortfall in the following contract year before conversion is triggered.
2. Add a carve-out: if the shortfall is attributable to Supplier's failure to meet its supply obligations (e.g., chronic fill-rate failures under Service Level Schedule § 5.5), the Minimum Purchase Commitment should be reduced proportionally or the conversion right should be suspended.
3. Confirm the actual minimums in Exhibit D and model Sterin's expected purchases against them, factoring in the Kestrova transition.

**Client Question:**
What are the actual minimum quantities in Exhibit D? Is Sterin currently on track to meet them for the 2026–2027 contract year? Were any of the 220 backordered AX-B60 units (OM-02) part of Sterin's minimum commitment?

## Issue 6 – Peak Season Forecast Exclusion Undermines Fill-Rate Protection

**Documents and Sections:**
- Service Level Schedule § 3.3: "Quantities ordered for Peak Season that exceed one hundred twenty-five percent (125%) of the quantity forecast for the relevant month in the forecast delivered at least ninety (90) days earlier are excluded from the fill-rate measure in Section 4.1."
- Service Level Schedule § 2.6: "Peak Season" is March 1 through July 31.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Peak Season is precisely when agricultural spraying demand spikes and Sterin needs product availability most. If Sterin's actual orders exceed 125% of its forecast (which is likely in a strong season or if Sterin is building inventory ahead of demand), those excess units are excluded from the fill-rate calculation. This means Pridort can ship those units late without triggering service credits or the chronic-failure material breach trigger in § 5.5. The practical effect is that Pridort's supply obligation is weakest exactly when Sterin's need is greatest.

**Recommendation:**
Negotiate to raise the threshold from 125% to 150% or 200%, or to eliminate the exclusion for Peak Season entirely. Alternatively, agree that units excluded from the fill-rate measure are still subject to the Committed Ship Date, with service credits applying at a reduced rate (e.g., half the standard credit).

## Issue 7 – Parent Guaranty Does Not Provide Meaningful Additional Protection

**Documents and Sections:**
- Parent Guaranty § 2.2: "Guarantor's liability under this Guaranty shall not exceed the liability that Supplier would have to Distributor under the Guaranteed Documents for the same matter. Every limitation, exclusion and defense available to Supplier under the Guaranteed Documents is available to Guarantor."
- Parent Guaranty § 6.1: Annual financial statements delivered "on request, so long as any Guaranteed Obligation may arise."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
The guaranty is structurally a pass-through: Pridort Holdings is liable only to the extent Pridort Aerial Robotics is liable, and it inherits all of Supplier's defenses, including the liability cap. This means:
- The recall cost cap conflict (Issue 1) limits the guaranty as well.
- The repurchase cap (Issue 4) limits the guaranty as well.
- If Pridort Aerial Robotics is the operating subsidiary with limited assets, the guaranty's value depends entirely on Pridort Holdings' balance sheet, but Sterin has no financial maintenance covenant, no minimum net worth requirement, and no early-warning trigger if the parent's credit deteriorates.

**Recommendation:**
1. Seek to add a financial covenant to the guaranty (e.g., Pridort Holdings shall maintain a tangible net worth of no less than $X or a debt-to-equity ratio not exceeding Y:1).
2. Seek to exclude the recall cost reimbursement and inventory repurchase obligations from the "every limitation" language in § 2.2, so the guarantor's exposure on those items is not capped at Supplier's (already-capped) liability.
3. Request Pridort Holdings' most recent annual financial statements now, under § 6.1, to assess the guarantor's creditworthiness.

**Client Question:**
Does Sterin have any financial information on Pridort Holdings, Inc.? Has Sterin requested the annual financial statements under § 6.1?

## Issue 8 – Distributor's Insurance Requirements May Be Inadequate

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 13.1: "Distributor shall maintain commercial general liability insurance of at least $2,000,000 per occurrence."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Sterin provides first-line technical support, field service, and warranty administration (Distribution Agreement § 7.2) and handles lithium batteries (AX-B60) in storage and transport. A single incident involving a battery thermal event, a drone crash causing property damage or personal injury, or a pesticide application failure could generate claims well above $2M. The agreement requires only $2M CGL for Distributor versus $5M CGL plus $5M aviation liability plus $2M product recall insurance for Supplier. There is no requirement for Sterin to carry aviation liability, product recall, or environmental/contamination coverage.

**Recommendation:**
1. Review Sterin's current insurance program with its broker to confirm adequate limits for the risks it actually bears (field service, battery handling, pesticide application equipment).
2. Consider whether the $2M limit is sufficient given the installed base and the nature of the products. If not, negotiate with Pridort to clarify that the $2M is a minimum and that Sterin may carry higher limits, and that Pridort's product liability and recall insurance (which should respond to product defects) is primary to Sterin's CGL for product-related claims.
3. Confirm that Pridort's $5M aviation liability policy names Sterin as an additional insured (required by § 13.2) and that the certificate has been delivered.

**Client Question:**
What insurance coverage does Sterin currently carry? Has Sterin received certificates of insurance from Pridort confirming the required limits and additional insured status?

## Issue 9 – Gap Between Distribution Agreement and Quality Agreement Effective Dates

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement: Effective Date March 3, 2025.
- Quality Agreement: QA Effective Date June 1, 2025.
- Distribution Agreement § 8.1: "Distributor shall inspect Products upon receipt in accordance with the incoming inspection procedures of the Quality Agreement."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
For approximately three months (March 3 – May 31, 2025), the Distribution Agreement was in force but the Quality Agreement was not. Any Products delivered during that gap were subject to an inspection regime that did not yet exist in written form. If a nonconformity in a unit delivered in, say, April 2025 is now disputed, Pridort could argue that the 10-Business-Day deemed-acceptance window and the specific sampling plan in Quality Agreement Schedule 2 did not apply, or conversely, Sterin could argue that the absence of a defined inspection procedure means Pridort cannot rely on deemed acceptance. This is a latent ambiguity that could surface in a future dispute.

**Recommendation:**
Confirm with the client whether any Products were delivered between March 3 and June 1, 2025, and whether any nonconformities were identified in those early shipments. If so, document the inspection procedures actually used during that period. For future reference, note that the Quality Agreement's § 12.1 provides it remains in effect "for so long as the Distribution Agreement is in effect," so the gap is a one-time issue.

**Client Question:**
Were any Products delivered under the Distribution Agreement before June 1, 2025? If so, what inspection procedures were used, and were any nonconformities identified?

## Issue 10 – Service Credits Are the Exclusive Monetary Remedy for Late Delivery

**Documents and Sections:**
- Service Level Schedule § 5.4: "Service credits are a price adjustment reflecting reduced service value. They do not limit Distributor's other rights and remedies under the Distribution Agreement in respect of the same failure, except that any damages recovered for a late delivery shall be reduced by the service credits issued for it."
- Service Level Schedule § 5.3: Monthly service credits capped at 10% of amounts invoiced for Products shipped in that Measurement Period.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
While § 5.4 preserves Sterin's right to pursue other remedies (including termination for chronic failure under § 5.5), the practical effect of the 10% monthly cap on service credits is that Sterin's monetary recovery for late delivery is modest. If Sterin incurs real damages from late delivery (e.g., a grower cancels a spraying contract because the drones arrive late, and Sterin must refund or compensate the grower), those damages are reduced dollar-for-dollar by the service credits, and the service credits themselves are capped at 10% of the month's invoiced amount. The net recovery may be well below actual losses.

**Recommendation:**
This is harder to fix without undermining the service-credit structure. However, consider negotiating that the § 5.4 offset applies only to the extent the service credits were actually issued and applied, and that Sterin's right to claim actual damages (subject to the § 12.1 cap) is not further reduced by the 10% monthly maximum in § 5.3. In other words, the 10% cap should limit the credits Pridort owes, not limit Sterin's damages claim.

## Issue 11 – No Right to Terminate for Convenience; Renewal Notice Period Is Long

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 3.2: Automatic renewal for successive 2-year terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term.
- Distribution Agreement § 16: Termination only for material breach (with 30-day cure), insolvency, or (for Supplier) Change of Control of Distributor.

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Sterin has no for-convenience termination right. Once in the agreement, it is locked in for the Initial Term (5 years, through March 2, 2030) and potentially two 2-year Renewal Terms (through March 2, 2034) unless it gives 180-day non-renewal notice. The 180-day notice period means Sterin must decide by September 3, 2029 whether to renew or exit the Initial Term. If the Kestrova transaction changes Sterin's strategic direction, or if the relationship with Pridort deteriorates, Sterin has limited flexibility. Combined with the Minimum Purchase Commitment (Issue 5), Sterin is contractually committed to buying product for up to 9 years.

**Recommendation:**
This is a structural feature that is difficult to change now that the agreement is in force. However, if Sterin is negotiating the Kestrova consent (Issue 2), it could seek a mutual right to terminate for convenience on 12 months' notice with a buyout of remaining inventory at cost. At minimum, calendar the September 3, 2029 non-renewal deadline immediately.

## Issue 12 – Quality Agreement Prevails Only for "Quality, Nonconformance and Recall Matters"

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 20.4: Order of precedence is "Quality Agreement (quality, nonconformance and recall matters only), then Distribution Agreement, then Service Level Schedule, then Purchase Orders."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
The parenthetical limitation means the Quality Agreement does **not** prevail over the Distribution Agreement for matters that are quality-adjacent but not squarely "quality, nonconformance or recall." For example:
- The warranty in Distribution Agreement § 9.1 is a product warranty, but a dispute over whether a particular failure constitutes a "Nonconformity" (Quality Agreement territory) versus a "defect in design, materials or workmanship" (Distribution Agreement territory) could raise a threshold question about which document controls.
- The traceability obligations appear in both documents (Distribution Agreement § 7.4 and Quality Agreement § 8.2). A conflict in record-retention timing or scope might not be clearly a "quality" matter.

This ambiguity could be exploited by Pridort in a dispute to argue that the Distribution Agreement (with its liability cap and warranty disclaimer) controls rather than the Quality Agreement (with its more detailed remedies).

**Recommendation:**
Seek to clarify § 20.4 to state that the Quality Agreement prevails for all matters governed by it, not just those labeled "quality, nonconformance and recall." Alternatively, add a sentence: "For purposes of this Section 20.4, 'quality, nonconformance and recall matters' includes all matters addressed in the Quality Agreement, including warranty, traceability, testing, and corrective action."

## Issue 14 – Force Majeure Allocation Clause Is Narrow

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 18.2: "During any period in which Supplier's capacity to supply is reduced by a force majeure event, Supplier shall allocate available Products to Distributor no less favorably than to any other customer."

**Why It Matters to Sterin:**
Because Sterin is the exclusive distributor, "any other customer" refers only to the reserved national accounts in Exhibit C. If Pridort has significant direct sales to national accounts, the allocation clause ensures Sterin is not treated worse than those accounts—but it does not guarantee Sterin priority. In a severe supply disruption (e.g., a factory fire, a chip shortage affecting flight controllers), Sterin could receive a reduced allocation that is "no less favorable" than the national accounts but still insufficient to meet its Minimum Purchase Commitment or customer demand.

**Recommendation:**
Negotiate a priority allocation right: in the event of a force majeure supply reduction, Distributor shall receive at least [X]% of available capacity before any allocation to national accounts. Alternatively, clarify that a force majeure event does not relieve Supplier of its Minimum Purchase Commitment-related obligations or reduce the fill-rate target.

2. Track the November 30, 2026 ship date. If missed, issue a formal notice and calculate additional credits.
3. Monitor whether this backorder, combined with any other fill-rate failures, is approaching the three-in-twelve-months threshold for chronic failure under § 5.5.

**Client Question:**
