## Issue 1 — Service Level Schedule Caps Germplasm Loss Liability, Overriding the Agreement's Unlimited-Damages Protection

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §6.2; Supply Agreement §§12.2, 12.3(a); Supply Agreement §2.3 (order of precedence).

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §12.3(a) expressly carves out of the liability cap "Supplier's breach of Article 9, including any loss, diversion or unauthorized propagation of Parent Seed or other Braport Germplasm." This was clearly negotiated to give Braport uncapped recovery for the most serious category of harm.
- Service Level Schedule §6.2 states: *"Notwithstanding Section 12.3 of the Agreement, Supplier's liability for any Germplasm Loss Event, including any related Performance Failure, is subject to the limit in Section 12.2 of the Agreement."*
- Supply Agreement §2.3 provides that in the event of conflict, the Service Level Schedule governs over the Supply Agreement.

**Why it matters:** Under the documents as written, SLS §6.2 controls. Braport's recovery for a Germplasm Loss Event—the theft, diversion, or unauthorized propagation of its proprietary parent-line germplasm—is capped at the fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim. For the 2027 season, total fees are approximately $6.3 million (180,000 Units × blended ~$35.17/Unit). The actual value of Braport's germplasm—years of breeding investment, pipeline hybrids, and competitive position—likely exceeds that by an order of magnitude or more. The cap effectively converts Braport's most important protective provision into a token remedy.

The use of "Notwithstanding" makes this an intentional override, not an inadvertent conflict. It reads as if Traidir's counsel inserted it to neutralize §12.3(a) and Braport's counsel accepted it without flagging the consequence.

**Recommended fix:** Delete SLS §6.2 in its entirety. If Traidir resists, offer a compromise: cap germplasm loss liability at a multiple of annual fees (e.g., 5×) or at the stated replacement/redevelopment value of the affected parent lines, whichever is greater, but do not accept the 12-month-fee cap for Article 9 breaches.

**Proposed replacement language (if compromise is needed):**

> "6.2 Germplasm Loss Events. Supplier's liability for a Germplasm Loss Event resulting from Supplier's breach of Article 9 of the Agreement shall not be subject to the limitation in Section 12.2 of the Agreement. For purposes of this Section 6.2, 'Germplasm Loss Event' has the meaning set out in Section 9.5 of the Agreement."

## Issue 2 — Split Governing Law and Forum: Iowa for Quality Disputes, Delaware for Everything Else

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §8.1; Supply Agreement §§19.1, 19.2.

**What the documents say:**

- Supply Agreement §19.1: Delaware law governs. §19.2: exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware.
- SLS §8.1: *"This Schedule, and any dispute concerning Performance Failures, Service Credits or Seed Quality, is governed by the laws of the State of Iowa… Any such dispute will be resolved exclusively in the state or federal courts located in Polk County, Iowa."*
- Under §2.3 (order of precedence), the SLS prevails over the Agreement.

**Why it matters:** The result is a bifurcated dispute-resolution regime. A seed-quality dispute (e.g., a lot fails genetic purity) goes to Iowa courts under Iowa law. A germplasm-theft dispute goes to Delaware under Delaware law. A volume-shortfall dispute (a "Performance Failure" under SLS §1.1) goes to Iowa. Braport would need to maintain counsel in two jurisdictions, and the same factual scenario could produce different outcomes depending on how the claim is characterized. Iowa law may also differ from Delaware law on issues like the enforceability of jury waivers, the standard for material breach, or the availability of equitable relief.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SLS §8.1 to conform to the Agreement's governing law and forum provisions.

**Proposed language:**

> "8.1 Law and Forum. This Schedule is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles. Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Schedule is subject to Section 19 of the Agreement."

If Traidir insists on a local forum for operational disputes, a fallback would be to limit the Iowa forum to "routine Service Credit calculations" while reserving all other disputes (including Seed Quality rejections, Germplasm Loss Events, and material-breach determinations) to Delaware.

## Issue 3 — One-Sided Assignment: Traidir Can Transfer the Agreement to Any Successor Without Braport's Consent

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§18.1, 18.2; §4.1 (exclusivity).

**What the documents say:**

- §18.1: Braport may not assign without Traidir's consent.
- §18.2: Traidir may assign "without the consent of Customer, on 30 days' written notice to Customer, including by merger, consolidation, change of control or sale of all or part of its seed production assets."
- §4.1: Braport must purchase all Contract Seed in the Territory exclusively from Traidir (and its permitted successors under §18.3) for the Initial Term and any Renewal Term.

**Why it matters:** Braport selected Traidir for its specific agronomic capability, security infrastructure, and operational reliability. As drafted, Traidir could sell its seed-production business to a competitor of Braport, to a financially weaker entity, or to a party with inferior security controls—and Braport would be contractually locked into buying exclusively from the assignee for up to five years (plus renewals). The only protection is 30 days' notice, which is insufficient to re-source production mid-stream. There is no termination right triggered by an assignment, no requirement that the assignee meet minimum security or capability standards, and no right of first refusal.

**Recommended fix:** Amend §18.2 to require Braport's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment, or at minimum:

**Proposed language:**

> "18.2 By Supplier. Supplier may not assign or transfer this Agreement or any Statement of Work, in whole or in part, without Customer's prior written consent, which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed. Notwithstanding the foregoing, Supplier may assign this Agreement to an Affiliate without Customer's consent, provided that (a) the Affiliate assumes all of Supplier's obligations under this Agreement in writing, (b) the Affiliate maintains insurance and security measures no less protective than those required by this Agreement, and (c) Supplier remains jointly and severally liable for the Affiliate's performance. Any change of control of Supplier that results in a Party competing with Customer in the development or sale of hybrid seed shall entitle Customer to terminate this Agreement upon written notice within 90 days after the change of control."

## Issue 4 — Exclusivity Lock-In Without a Practical Exit for Underperformance

**Where:** Supply Agreement §4.1; §§16.1, 16.3; SLS §6.4.

**What the documents say:**

- §4.1: Braport must buy all Contract Seed in the Territory exclusively from Traidir during the Initial Term (5 years) and any Renewal Term.
- §16.1: Termination for material breach requires 30 days' notice and a failed cure period.
- SLS §6.4: A Service Level failure in two consecutive Production Seasons constitutes a material breach.
- There is no termination right for convenience, no "minimum performance" trigger that falls short of material breach, and no right to source from a second supplier if Traidir is underperforming but not in material breach.

**Why it matters:** This is Braport's first season with Traidir. If Traidir delivers 93% of volume in 2027 (below the 95% Service Level but not a "failure" in two consecutive seasons) and 96% first-pass quality (below 97% but again not yet a chronic failure), Braport cannot terminate. It must wait through 2028, hope the same Service Level fails again, and then still give 30 days to cure. In the meantime, Braport cannot go to a backup grower for the shortfall because §4.1 prohibits it. The practical effect is that Braport is locked in for a minimum of two full production seasons before it can exit for performance reasons, with no right to supplement from another source in the interim.

**Recommended fix:** Add a "performance threshold" termination right and a limited right to source supplemental volume:

**Proposed language (new §4.4):**

> "4.4 Performance Threshold. If in any Production Season Supplier fails to meet the volume Service Level in Section 4.2 of the Service Level Schedule by more than 10 percentage points (i.e., delivers less than 85% of the Units allocated to any hybrid), Customer may, by written notice, (a) terminate the applicable Statement of Work effective immediately, or (b) source up to 20% of the shortfall volume from an alternative grower for that Production Season without violating Section 4.1. Customer's exercise of either right shall not relieve Supplier of its obligations for the volume actually produced and accepted."

And amend §16.1 to add: "For purposes of this Section 16.1, a failure by Supplier to meet the volume Service Level in Section 4.2 of the Service Level Schedule by more than 5 percentage points in any Production Season shall constitute a material breach."

## Issue 5 — Deemed Acceptance at 30 Days Conflicts with Grow-Out Genetic Purity Testing

**Where:** Supply Agreement §8.2; SLS §2.3.

**What the documents say:**

- §8.2: "Customer may test any lot of Contract Seed and may reject any lot that does not meet the Specifications by notice to Supplier within 30 days after delivery. Lots not rejected within that period are deemed accepted."
- SLS §2.3: Genetic purity (minimum 98.0%) is "determined by grow-out or by molecular marker analysis performed by a Certified Laboratory on Customer's behalf."

**Why it matters:** A grow-out test requires planting the seed and evaluating the resulting plants, which takes an entire growing season (approximately 4–5 months for corn). If Braport relies on grow-out to verify genetic purity, the 30-day acceptance window will have long expired before results are available. The lot will be "deemed accepted," and Braport may have no contractual basis to reject it even if the grow-out reveals 96% genetic purity. The molecular-marker alternative could be completed within 30 days, but (a) the Certified Laboratory for that work has not yet been confirmed (Meeting Notes, Action Item 6, due March 31, 2027), and (b) the SOW does not specify which method will be used for the 2027 season.

**Recommended fix:** Carve out genetic purity (and any Specification that requires grow-out) from the 30-day deemed-acceptance rule.

**Proposed language (amend §8.2):**

> "8.2 Acceptance. Customer may test any lot of Contract Seed and may reject any lot that does not meet the Specifications by notice to Supplier within 30 days after delivery, except that for Specifications requiring grow-out evaluation (including genetic purity under Section 2.3 of the Service Level Schedule), the rejection period shall be 30 days after Customer receives the grow-out results. Lots not rejected within the applicable period are deemed accepted."

Additionally, the SOW should specify that molecular marker analysis will be the primary method for genetic purity verification for the 2027 season, with grow-out as a confirmatory backup, so that results are available within the standard 30-day window.

## Issue 6 — Volume Shortfall Service Credit Is Inadequate and Consequential Damages Are Excluded

**Where:** SLS §§4.2, 5.1; Supply Agreement §§12.1, 12.2.

**What the documents say:**

- SLS §4.2: Volume Service Level is 95% of allocated Units per hybrid.
- SLS §5.1: The credit for shortfall below 95% is $4.00 per Unit.
- SLS §4.2: Force Majeure shortfalls are excluded from the Service Level entirely.
- Supply Agreement §12.1: No consequential damages (including lost profits).
- Supply Agreement §12.2: Liability capped at 12 months of fees.

**Why it matters:** Consider a scenario: drought reduces Traidir's yield on BSG-4120 to 88% of allocation (61,600 Units delivered vs. 70,000 allocated). The shortfall below the 95% threshold is 3,500 Units. Service credit: 3,500 × $4.00 = $14,000. The fee value of those 3,500 Units: 3,500 × $34.50 = $120,750. Braport's actual commercial loss—having to buy replacement seed from another source at a premium, or facing lost sales to its customers—could be several times the fee value. All of that is excluded as consequential damages. And if the drought qualifies as Force Majeure (SLS §4.2), even the $14,000 credit disappears.

The $4.00/Unit credit represents roughly 11.6% of the per-Unit fee. It is a token amount that does not compensate Braport for the operational disruption of a volume shortfall.

**Recommended fix:**

(a) Increase the volume shortfall credit to a percentage of the fee for the shortfall Units (e.g., 50% of the per-Unit fee for each Unit below the 95% threshold).

(b) Add a carve-out from the consequential-damages exclusion for volume shortfalls that force Braport to purchase replacement seed:

> "12.1 (additional sentence): Notwithstanding the foregoing, if Supplier fails to deliver at least 90% of the Units allocated to a hybrid under the applicable Statement of Work and Customer is required to purchase substitute seed from a third party to meet its customer commitments, Supplier shall reimburse Customer for the difference between the cost of the substitute seed and the fee that would have been paid under the Statement of Work, up to the liability cap in Section 12.2."

(c) Clarify that a weather event that reduces yield below 85% of allocation is not a Force Majeure Event for purposes of the volume Service Level, given that Traidir's capacity commitment (§4.2 of the Agreement) implies it should have sufficient acreage buffer to absorb normal weather variation.

## Issue 7 — Insurance "Full Replacement Value" Is Inadequate for Proprietary Germplasm

**Where:** Supply Agreement §14.1(b); §7.2 (title); §9.1 (ownership).

**What the documents say:**

- §14.1(b): Traidir must maintain "crop and property insurance covering Parent Seed and Contract Seed in its care, custody and control at full replacement value."
- §7.2: Title to Parent Seed remains with Braport at all times.
- §9.1: Braport owns all right, title and interest in Braport Germplasm.

**Why it matters:** "Full replacement value" is a property-insurance concept that works for fungible assets (e.g., a combine harvester can be replaced at market price). Parent Seed is not fungible. BF-217, BF-231, BF-244, BM-063, and BM-071 are unique inbred and parent lines representing years of directed breeding. They cannot be "replaced" by purchasing equivalent material on the open market. Their true value to Braport is the value of the hybrids and pipelines they support, which is not a number that appears on any insurance schedule.

Additionally, the Agreement does not require Traidir to name Braport as additional insured or loss payee on the policy, does not specify a minimum coverage amount for the crop/property policy, and does not require the policy to specifically cover theft or diversion of germplasm (as opposed to only physical perils like fire or hail).

**Recommended fix:**

**Proposed language (amend §14.1(b)):**

> "(b) crop and property insurance covering Parent Seed and Contract Seed in its care, custody and control, with a minimum coverage amount of $[X] per occurrence, naming Customer as additional insured and loss payee, and expressly covering loss or damage by theft, diversion, fire, storm, flood or other named peril. The Parties acknowledge that Parent Seed is unique proprietary material that cannot be replaced at market value, and Supplier shall maintain a separate germplasm liability coverage (or increase the coverage under this clause (b)) in an amount not less than $[Y] to address the loss of Parent Seed due to Supplier's breach of Article 9."

**Open question for the client:** What dollar value does Braport assign to each parent line for insurance purposes? This will drive the minimum coverage amounts. I would need Braport's internal valuation or R&D cost data to recommend specific figures.

## Issue 8 — No Right to Cancel or Reduce a Signed SOW

**Where:** Supply Agreement §2.1; SOW generally.

**What the documents say:**

- §2.1: "A Statement of Work becomes binding only when signed by authorized representatives of both Parties."
- There is no provision allowing either party to cancel or reduce a signed SOW before the Production Season begins.
- §4.3 makes forecasts non-binding, but the SOW itself is not a forecast—it is a committed production plan.

**Why it matters:** Once the SOW is signed (presumably in late 2026 or early 2027), Braport is committed to 180,000 Units. If Braport's commercial plans change—for example, a key customer cancels its order, a regulatory issue affects one of the hybrids, or market conditions shift—Braport has no contractual mechanism to reduce or cancel the SOW without being in breach. Traidir, by contrast, has the Force Majeure termination right (§17.2) and the insolvency right (§16.2), but Braport has no analogous right for its own changed circumstances.

**Recommended fix:** Add a cancellation/reduction right with a reasonable notice period and a break-fee:

**Proposed language (new SOW §9.4 or Agreement §2.4):**

> "2.4 Cancellation or Reduction. Customer may cancel or reduce a Statement of Work by written notice given at least 120 days before the scheduled Parent Seed delivery date for the affected hybrid. Upon cancellation, Customer shall pay Supplier for all non-cancellable costs incurred in reliance on the Statement of Work (including land leases, input purchases and grower commitments), up to a maximum of [25%] of the estimated production fee for the cancelled volume. Upon reduction, the Statement of Work shall be amended to reflect the reduced volume, and Customer shall pay for non-cancellable costs attributable to the reduced volume on the same basis."

**Open question for the client:** What is Braport's typical lead time from decision to cancel a hybrid program? If 120 days is too short or too long, the notice period should be adjusted. Also, what percentage cap on break-fee is commercially acceptable?

## Issue 9 — Force Majeure Excludes the Volume Service Level but Braport Bears All Commercial Risk

**Where:** SLS §4.2; Supply Agreement §17.

**What the documents say:**

- SLS §4.2: "Shortfalls caused by a Force Majeure Event under Article 17 of the Agreement are excluded from this Service Level."
- §17.1 lists drought, flood, hail, windstorm, fire, epidemic, war, government action, and labor disputes.
- §17.2: If Force Majeure prevents a material part of the SOW for more than 60 days, Braport may terminate that SOW.

**Why it matters:** Corn production is inherently weather-dependent. Drought, hail, and flood are not extraordinary events in Iowa and Illinois—they are recurring risks. By excluding all Force Majeure shortfalls from the volume Service Level, the Agreement places 100% of the weather-related yield risk on Braport with zero compensation from Traidir. Traidir's only obligation is to terminate the SOW if the event lasts more than 60 days and prevents a "material part" of performance. For a localized hail event that destroys 30% of one field for two weeks, Traidir owes nothing. Braport loses the seed, loses the Parent Seed planted in that field, and must source replacement volume on the open market at a premium—all while being exclusively locked to Traidir under §4.1.

This risk allocation is aggravated by §4.2 (Capacity Commitment), which requires Traidir to "maintain sufficient acreage, isolation, equipment, Conditioning capacity and personnel to produce the volumes set out in each Statement of Work." If Traidir is committed to producing the full volume, it should bear some of the weather risk, or at minimum the parties should agree on a shared-risk mechanism.

**Recommended fix:**

(a) Narrow the Force Majeure exclusion in SLS §4.2 to "extraordinary" events (e.g., events that affect more than 50% of the total Production Fields):

> "4.2 (revised): Shortfalls caused by a Force Majeure Event are excluded from this Service Level only to the extent the Force Majeure Event affects more than 50% of the total acreage planted under the applicable Statement of Work. For Force Majeure Events affecting 50% or less of total acreage, the volume Service Level applies to the unaffected portion, and Service Credits under Section 5.1 apply to the shortfall."

(b) Alternatively, require Traidir to maintain a minimum over-plant buffer (e.g., 10% additional acreage beyond the 3,150 minimum in SOW §3.3) specifically to absorb normal weather variation, at no additional cost to Braport.

## Issue 10 — Open Items and Administrative Gaps from the Meeting Notes

**Where:** Production Planning Meeting Notes §§7.1, 9 (Action Items); SOW §§5.2, 5.3.

**What the documents say:**

- Meeting Notes §7.1: Braport has not yet decided whether to supply seed treatment materials or have Traidir purchase them at cost. The SOW (§5.2) requires Braport to designate the treatment package by February 1, 2027.
- Meeting Notes, Action Item 6: Braport must confirm the Certified Laboratory for genetic purity testing by March 31, 2027. This is critical because the lab must be available before conditioning begins (September 15, 2027 per SOW §4.3) and must be able to process all lots within the 30-day acceptance window.
- Meeting Notes, Action Item 4: Traidir must submit the Parent Seed storage access list for Braport's approval by February 15, 2027 (SLS §7.1). Braport should not approve this list without reviewing it against its security requirements.
- SOW §5.3: Braport must specify bulk box vs. single-Unit bag proportions by November 1, 2027. This is a very late date (only 6 weeks before final delivery on January 15, 2028) and may not give Traidir sufficient time to repackage if the initial packaging is wrong.

**Why it matters:** These are not legal defects in the documents, but they are operational risks. If the Certified Laboratory is not confirmed well in advance, Braport may be unable to complete genetic purity testing within the 30-day acceptance window (compounding Issue 5). If the treatment decision is delayed, it could push back the conditioning schedule. The November 1 packaging deadline is particularly tight.

**Recommended fix:**

- Treat Action Items 2, 4, and 6 as priority items for the next planning meeting (late March 2027). Build internal deadlines ahead of the contractual dates (e.g., confirm the lab by February 28, not March 31).
- Negotiate to move the SOW §5.3 packaging specification date earlier (e.g., September 1, 2027, before conditioning begins).
- **Open question for the client:** Does Braport have a preferred Certified Laboratory in mind for genetic purity testing? If so, we should confirm its capacity and turnaround time before the SOW is finalized.

## Additional Observations

**A. Incomplete or missing documents.** The folder contains the four documents listed above. I do not see:
- An executed copy of the Supply Agreement (the signature blocks are blank).
- Any exhibit or schedule identifying the "Specifications" in more detail beyond the SLS (e.g., specific isolation distances by hybrid, sizing grade designations, or treatment rates).
- Braport's "written production protocols" referenced in Agreement §5.2. These are supposed to be provided "from time to time" but are not in the folder. If they contain operational requirements that conflict with the SLS, the order of precedence (§2.3) does not address where they fit.
- Any insurance certificates from Traidir (required by §14.1 on request).

If the Agreement has not yet been executed, the issues above should be raised as negotiation points before signing. If it has been executed, the issues should be raised as amendments under §20.3.

**B. SOW §3.3 acreage floor.** The SOW requires "not less than 3,150 female-row acres in total" but does not specify a maximum. If Traidir plants 4,500 acres and yield comes in low, it could argue it met its capacity commitment. Conversely, if it plants exactly 3,150 acres and yield is average, it may miss the volume target. The acreage floor should ideally be tied to a yield assumption so that Braport can verify whether Traidir planted enough.

**C. SOW §7.3 grower advance.** Braport may advance up to 20% of the estimated production fee on July 15, 2027. There is no security for this advance (no letter of credit, no escrow, no set-off right if the advance is not credited). If Traidir becomes insolvent after receiving the advance, Braport would be an unsecured creditor. Given the assignment risk (Issue 3) and the absence of financial covenants, I recommend that any advance be secured or that Braport require Traidir to maintain a minimum net-worth or liquidity threshold as a condition of receiving advances.

**D. SLS §4.4 reporting credit is $500 per late report.** Over a production season with bi-weekly reports (approximately 20 reports from planting in late April to final delivery in mid-January), the maximum reporting credit is $10,000. This is immaterial relative to the fees and does not create meaningful incentive for timely reporting. I would increase this to $2,500 per late report or tie it to a percentage of the seasonal fees.
