## Summary

I have reviewed the four documents in the matter folder: the Supply Agreement (executed October 1, 2026), Statement of Work No. 1 for the 2027 Production Season, the Service Level Schedule, and the November 4, 2026 production planning meeting notes. The documents are substantively complete as a package, but they contain several provisions that materially disadvantage Braport, one direct conflict between documents that could create litigation risk, and a number of open operational items. The most serious issue is that the Service Level Schedule, by virtue of the order-of-precedence clause, effectively caps Braport's recovery for germplasm loss — undermining the core protective purpose of Article 9 of the Supply Agreement.

Below I set out each issue in order of severity, with the document and section where it appears, why it matters, and a recommended fix. I flag open questions where we need facts from the client.

## A. Critical Issues

### 1. SLS §6.2 Overrides the Uncapped Germplasm Liability Carve-Out

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §6.2; Supply Agreement §§12.2, 12.3(a), 2.3.

**What it says:** Supply Agreement §12.3(a) expressly carves "Supplier's breach of Article 9, including any loss, diversion or unauthorized propagation of Parent Seed or other Braport Germplasm" out of both the indirect-damages exclusion (§12.1) and the liability cap (§12.2). The intent is clear: germplasm breaches are uncapped. However, SLS §6.2 states:

> "Notwithstanding Section 12.3 of the Agreement, Supplier's liability for any Germplasm Loss Event, including any related Performance Failure, is subject to the limit in Section 12.2 of the Agreement."

Because Supply Agreement §2.3 gives the SLS priority over the Supply Agreement in the event of conflict, SLS §6.2 controls. The practical effect is that Braport's recovery for the loss, theft, or unauthorized propagation of its proprietary parent lines is capped at "the fees paid by Customer in the 12 months preceding the claim" (§12.2).

**Why it matters:** Braport's parent lines are the crown jewels of its business. They represent years of breeding investment and have no ready replacement. A cap tied to trailing 12-month fees is likely to be a small fraction of the actual value of lost germplasm. Worse, for a claim arising early in the 2027 season (e.g., a theft in April 2027), the "fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim" may be zero or near-zero, because fees are only paid upon acceptance of lots at the end of the season (Supply Agreement §6.1–6.2). This could leave Braport with no meaningful financial remedy for the single most catastrophic risk in the relationship.

**Recommended fix:** Delete SLS §6.2 in its entirety, or restate it to confirm that Germplasm Loss Events remain subject to the uncapped treatment in Supply Agreement §12.3(a). Proposed replacement language:

> "6.2 Germplasm Loss Events. Supplier's liability for any Germplasm Loss Event, including any related Performance Failure, shall not be subject to the limitations in Section 12.1 or Section 12.2 of the Agreement, consistent with Section 12.3(a) of the Agreement."

If Traidir resists full deletion, a fallback is to set a specific super-cap for germplasm events (e.g., 5× the annual fees, or a stated dollar figure reflecting the development value of the parent lines). **Open question for client:** What is Braport's internal estimate of the replacement/development value of each parent line (BF-217, BF-231, BF-244, BM-063, BM-071)? We need that figure to negotiate a meaningful super-cap if Traidir will not accept uncapped liability.

### 2. Split Governing Law and Forum (SLS §8.1 vs. Supply Agreement §19)

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §8.1; Supply Agreement §§19.1, 19.2, 2.3.

**What it says:** Supply Agreement §19.1 selects Delaware law; §19.2 selects exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware. SLS §8.1 selects Iowa law and exclusive jurisdiction in Polk County, Iowa, but only for "any dispute concerning Performance Failures, Service Credits or Seed Quality."

Because the SLS has priority (§2.3), for any quality or service-level dispute the governing law is Iowa and the forum is Polk County. For all other disputes (e.g., interpretation of Article 9, assignment, termination for insolvency), the governing law is Delaware and the forum is Wilmington.

**Why it matters:** A single real-world dispute will often implicate both categories. For example, if Traidir delivers a lot that fails genetic purity (a "Seed Quality" dispute under Iowa law in Polk County) and Braport argues the failure constitutes a Germplasm Loss Event triggering Article 9 (a "Supply Agreement" dispute under Delaware law in Wilmington), the parties could end up in two forums applying two bodies of law. This creates cost, delay, and the risk of inconsistent outcomes. It also means Braport cannot rely on Delaware's well-developed commercial law for the disputes most likely to arise in a seed production relationship.

**Recommended fix:** Conform SLS §8.1 to the Supply Agreement. Proposed replacement:

> "8.1 Law and Forum. This Schedule is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles. Any dispute arising out of or relating to this Schedule, including any dispute concerning Performance Failures, Service Credits or Seed Quality, will be resolved exclusively in the state or federal courts located in Wilmington, Delaware, and each Party submits to the jurisdiction of those courts."

If Traidir insists on a home-state forum as a condition of the deal, the alternative is to move the entire agreement to Iowa law and forum (amending Supply Agreement §19). But from Braport's perspective, a single, neutral forum is preferable to a split.

### 3. Supplier's Unilateral Right to Assign (Supply Agreement §18.2)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §18.2; compare §18.1.

**What it says:** §18.1 requires Customer's consent for Customer to assign. §18.2 permits Supplier to assign "without the consent of Customer, on 30 days' written notice," expressly including "by merger, consolidation, change of control or sale of all or part of its seed production assets."

**Why it matters:** Braport is entrusting Traidir with irreplaceable proprietary germplasm. If Traidir sells its seed production assets to a competitor of Braport, or undergoes a change of control to an entity with weaker security practices or a different strategic orientation, Braport's germplasm could end up in hostile or careless hands. Thirty days' notice is insufficient for Braport to assess the risk, negotiate new security terms, or (if it objects) wind down the relationship. The asymmetry with §18.1 is also commercially unreasonable: Braport cannot reassign without Traidir's consent, but Traidir can reassign freely.

**Recommended fix:** Amend §18.2 to require Customer's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment by Supplier, including by change of control or asset sale. Proposed language:

> "18.2 By Supplier. Supplier shall not assign or transfer this Agreement or any Statement of Work, in whole or in part, without Customer's prior written consent, including by merger, consolidation, change of control or sale of all or part of its seed production assets. Customer's consent may be conditioned on the assignee assuming all of Supplier's obligations under this Agreement in writing and maintaining the security and insurance requirements of Articles 9 and 14."

At minimum, add a right for Braport to terminate without penalty if an assignment to a named competitor occurs.

### 4. One-Way Exclusivity with Inadequate Exit (Supply Agreement §4.1; SLS §6.4)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §4.1; SLS §6.4; Supply Agreement §16.1.

**What it says:** §4.1 obligates Braport to purchase "all of its requirements of Contract Seed in the Territory exclusively from Supplier" for the Initial Term (five years) and any Renewal Term. Braport may not produce Contract Seed itself or through any other grower. The only path out for underperformance is termination for material breach (§16.1), which requires a 30-day cure period, or the "chronic failure" provision in SLS §6.4, which requires the same Service Level to be failed in **two consecutive** Production Seasons.

**Why it matters:** If Traidir systematically underdelivers on volume (e.g., 93–94% each year, just above the 95% Service Level threshold so no "failure" is triggered), or delivers late each year (incurring the capped 10% service credit), Braport is locked in for five years with no practical remedy. The two-consecutive-season requirement in §6.4 means Braport must endure at least two full growing cycles of the same problem before it can terminate. In the seed business, a missed season is a missed revenue year for Braport's customers.

**Recommended fix:**

- Lower the chronic-failure trigger in SLS §6.4 from "two consecutive Production Seasons" to "two Production Seasons within any three-consecutive-Season period," or add a cumulative-shortfall trigger (e.g., if total volume delivered across two seasons falls below 90% of allocated volume, that is a material breach).
- Add a termination-for-convenience right for Braport on 12 months' notice after the first Production Season, with a reasonable break fee (e.g., the unamortized cost of Traidir's committed acreage and inputs for the current season). This gives Braport an exit without requiring a breach finding.
- Carve out of the exclusivity obligation any volume that Traidir fails to deliver: if Traidir cannot meet a hybrid's allocation, Braport should be permitted to source the shortfall from an alternative grower without breaching §4.1.

Proposed language for the exclusivity carve-out:

> "4.1 (continued) Notwithstanding the foregoing, if Supplier fails to deliver at least 95% of the Units allocated to a hybrid under a Statement of Work, Customer may source the shortfall from an alternative grower for that Production Season without breach of this Section 4.1, provided that Customer gives Supplier written notice of the shortfall and the opportunity to cure before Customer engages the alternative grower."

### 5. Liability Cap May Be Near-Zero for Early-Season Claims (Supply Agreement §12.2)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §12.2; SLS §6.2 (see Issue 1 above).

**What it says:** The general cap is "the fees paid by Customer in the 12 months preceding the claim." Fees are paid only upon acceptance of lots (Supply Agreement §6.1), which occurs after final delivery (SOW §4.3: final delivery by January 15, 2028).

**Why it matters:** If a claim arises during the 2027 growing season — say, a germplasm security breach in June 2027, or a quality dispute over early-harvested lots in October 2027 — no fees for the 2027 season will have been paid yet. The Agreement became effective October 1, 2026, so the "12 months preceding" a June 2027 claim would cover only October–December 2026, a period in which no production occurred and no fees were paid. The cap could be effectively zero.

This issue is compounded by SLS §6.2 (Issue 1), which subjects even germplasm losses to this cap.

**Recommended fix:** In addition to fixing SLS §6.2 (Issue 1), amend §12.2 to use a more meaningful baseline. Options:

- "the fees paid or payable by Customer under all Statements of Work in the 12 months preceding the claim" (adding "payable" captures accrued but unpaid fees); or
- "the total fees for the Production Season in which the claim arises, plus any fees paid in the preceding 12 months"; or
- a stated dollar floor (e.g., "in no event less than $[X] million").

**Open question for client:** What is Braport's total expected fee spend for the 2027 season? (Rough estimate from the SOW: 180,000 Units × ~$35.50 average ≈ $6.4 million.) This gives us a benchmark for negotiating a floor.

## B. Significant Issues

### 6. Volume Shortfall Remedy Is Weak (SLS §§4.2, 5.1)

**Where:** SLS §4.2 (95% threshold), §5.1 ($4.00 per Unit of shortfall below 95%).

**Why it matters:** Traidir can short up to 5% of any hybrid's allocation and owe only $4.00 per Unit below the threshold. For BSG-4120 (70,000 Units), a 4% shortfall (2,800 Units) triggers no credit at all (it's above the 95% line). A 6% shortfall (2,800 Units below the 95% line) yields only $11,200 in credits against a total fee for that hybrid of ~$2.4 million. Meanwhile, Braport needs that seed to fulfill its own customer commitments and cannot source elsewhere (Issue 4).

**Recommended fix:**

- Lower the volume threshold from 95% to 98%.
- Increase the per-Unit credit from $4.00 to an amount that reflects Braport's actual cost of sourcing the shortfall elsewhere (e.g., the difference between the SOW fee and Braport's estimated emergency sourcing cost).
- Add a right for Braport to terminate the affected SOW (or the affected hybrid allocation) if the shortfall exceeds 10%, without penalty.

### 7. On-Time Delivery Credit Capped at 10% (SLS §5.1)

**Where:** SLS §4.1, §5.1 (first row of the credit table).

**What it says:** 1% of the fee per Unit per week of delay, capped at 10%.

**Why it matters:** If Traidir delivers a lot six or more weeks late, Braport's maximum credit is 10% of the fee (~$3.50 per Unit). But a six-week delay in January could mean Braport misses the spring planting window entirely for its downstream customers. The actual harm to Braport (lost customer contracts, reputation damage) vastly exceeds the credit. And because the indirect-damages exclusion (§12.1) applies (unless the delay rises to a germplasm breach, which it wouldn't), Braport cannot recover its consequential losses.

**Recommended fix:** Remove or raise the 10% cap for delays exceeding four weeks. Add a provision that if delay exceeds 30 days for any lot, Braport may reject that lot under §8.2 and source the volume elsewhere at Traidir's cost (subject to the exclusivity carve-out proposed in Issue 4).

---

### 8. No Service Credits for Intermediate Schedule Slippage (SOW §4.3; SLS §4)

**Where:** SOW §4.3 (schedule table uses "Target Window"); SLS §4 (Service Levels cover only final delivery, volume, first-pass quality, reporting, germplasm notification, and chain-of-custody reconciliation).

**Why it matters:** The SOW schedule sets target windows for planting (April 20–May 25), detasseling (July 1–31), harvest (September 1–October 20), and conditioning (September 15–December 15). But the only delivery-related Service Level in the SLS is §4.1 (final delivery date). If Traidir plants at the end of the window, harvests late, or conditions slowly, there is no service credit or other remedy until the final delivery date is missed. By then, it may be too late to cure.

**Recommended fix:** Add intermediate milestone Service Levels to the SLS, with modest credits or, more usefully, with a right for Braport to require a corrective action plan (under SLS §6.3) if any milestone is missed by more than 10 days. Alternatively, add a provision in the SOW that if planting is not completed by May 25, 2027, or harvest is not completed by October 20, 2027, Braport may require Traidir to submit a revised delivery schedule and, if the revised schedule pushes final delivery beyond January 15, 2028, Braport may terminate the SOW without penalty.

### 9. 30-Day Deemed Acceptance May Be Too Short (Supply Agreement §8.2)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §8.2.

**What it says:** "Customer may test any lot of Contract Seed and may reject any lot that does not meet the Specifications by notice to Supplier within 30 days after delivery. Lots not rejected within that period are deemed accepted."

**Why it matters:** Braport is receiving up to 180,000 Units across multiple lots. Testing each lot for all specifications (germination, vigor, genetic purity, physical purity, moisture, sizing, adventitious presence, treatment) takes time. Genetic purity in particular requires grow-out or molecular marker analysis, which can take weeks. If Braport is still running tests at day 30, the lot is deemed accepted. The SLS §3.4 retest mechanism only helps if Braport has already rejected and Traidir disputes; it does not extend the rejection window.

**Recommended fix:** Extend the rejection period to 45 days, or (better) tie it to completion of testing: "Customer may reject any lot that does not meet the Specifications by notice to Supplier within 30 days after delivery or within 10 days after Customer receives all test results for that lot, whichever is later." Alternatively, add a provision that lots are not deemed accepted if Customer has initiated testing before the 30-day deadline and the delay is attributable to the testing timeline.

### 10. No Termination for Convenience (Supply Agreement Article 16)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§16.1–16.3 (termination only for breach, insolvency, or germplasm breach).

**Why it matters:** Braport is committed for five years. If Braport's business strategy shifts (e.g., it acquires a competing germplasm platform, decides to in-source production, or loses a major customer), it has no clean exit. It would have to either perform for the full term or risk a breach claim by Traidir for lost fees.

**Recommended fix:** Add a termination-for-convenience right exercisable by Braport on 12 months' written notice, with payment of a break fee covering Traidir's documented, non-recoverable costs for the current Production Season (acreage commitments, inputs already purchased, labor contracts). This is a market-standard provision in long-term supply agreements and gives Braport strategic flexibility.

### 11. FCA Delivery Term Conflicts with Risk-of-Loss Provision (SOW §5.4 vs. Supply Agreement §7.3)

**Where:** SOW §5.4 ("FCA Supplier's Ames, Iowa facility"); Supply Agreement §7.3 (risk passes on "delivery to Customer under Section 7.1").

**Why it matters:** Under FCA (a UCC/Incoterms concept), risk of loss passes to the buyer when the seller places the goods at the buyer's carrier at the named origin. Under §7.3, risk passes on "delivery to Customer." Because the Supply Agreement has priority over the SOW (§2.3), §7.3 controls and risk stays with Traidir until Braport actually receives the seed. This favors Braport as written. However, the FCA language in the SOW creates an ambiguity that Traidir could exploit in a dispute (e.g., arguing that "delivery" under §7.1 occurs when the seed is made available at the Ames facility, consistent with FCA).

**Recommended fix:** Delete "FCA" from SOW §5.4 and replace with language consistent with §7.3:

> "5.4 Delivery Locations. Supplier will make Conditioned Contract Seed available for collection at its Ames, Iowa facility, or will deliver it to such other locations within Iowa and Illinois as Customer may designate in writing. Risk of loss remains with Supplier until Contract Seed is received by Customer or its designated carrier at the delivery location. Supplier will give Customer five Business Days' notice that each lot is ready for collection."

### 12. No Specific Remedy for Replacement of Lost Parent Seed (Supply Agreement Article 9; SLS Article 7)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§7.2–7.3 (title and risk), 9.4 (reconciliation and return), 9.5 (notification); SLS §7 (security measures).

**Why it matters:** The documents require Traidir to notify Braport of a Germplasm Loss Event within 24 hours and to cooperate in recovery. Title to Parent Seed remains with Braport at all times, and risk is on Traidir. But there is no provision requiring Traidir to **replace** lost Parent Seed at its cost, or to pay the development value of the germplasm if replacement is impossible. The only financial remedies are the (capped, per Issue 1) general liability and the $25,000-per-notification service credit. If a parent line is truly lost (e.g., destroyed in a fire with no backup), Braport's remedy is a damages claim subject to the cap — which, as noted, may be near-zero for an early-season event.

**Recommended fix:** Add a specific provision to Article 9 (or the SLS) requiring that, in the event of a Germplasm Loss Event, Supplier shall, at its sole cost and expense: (a) use commercially reasonable efforts to recover or replace the lost Braport Germplasm; (b) if replacement is not possible, pay Customer an amount equal to the reasonable cost of re-developing the affected parent line, as determined by an independent expert; and (c) reimburse Customer for all costs of investigating and remediating the event. This should be added to the §12.3 exclusions so it is uncapped.

**Open question for client:** Does Braport maintain backup stocks of each parent line at its own facilities or with a third-party cryobank? If so, the "replacement value" analysis changes — the loss is the cost of reconstituting from backup plus any time delay. If not, the loss is the full development cost, which is potentially very large.

## C. Moderate / Practical Issues

### 13. All Parent Seed Due on the Same Date (SOW §2.2)

**Where:** SOW §2.2 (all five parent lines "By March 15, 2027").

**Why it matters:** If Braport experiences any delay in producing or shipping even one parent line (e.g., BF-244), the entire planting schedule could be affected. There is no provision for staggered delivery or for allocating responsibility if one line arrives late.

**Recommended fix:** Add to SOW §2.2: "If Customer anticipates that any Parent Seed will not be delivered by the date above, Customer will notify Supplier at least 10 Business Days in advance, and the Parties will agree in writing on a revised delivery date for the affected line. A delay of up to 14 days in delivery of any Parent Seed will not relieve Supplier of its obligation to plant within the target window in Section 4.3, and Supplier will adjust its field preparation schedule accordingly."

---

### 14. Insurance "Full Replacement Value" for Unique Germplasm Is Undefined (Supply Agreement §14.1(b))

**Where:** Supply Agreement §14.1(b).

**What it says:** Traidir must maintain "crop and property insurance covering Parent Seed and Contract Seed in its care, custody and control at full replacement value."

**Why it matters:** "Full replacement value" for a unique, proprietary parent line is not a standard insurance concept. Insurers will likely value Parent Seed at its seed weight (a small number) rather than its development value (potentially millions). If a loss occurs, the insurance payout may be far below Braport's actual loss, and Braport would need to pursue the gap from Traidir — subject to the cap (Issues 1 and 5).

**Recommended fix:** Require Traidir to carry a specified minimum coverage amount for Parent Seed (e.g., $[X] million, to be determined by the client based on germplasm value) and to name Braport as additional insured or loss payee on the policy. Add a requirement that Traidir provide the policy (not just a certificate) to Braport before the first Parent Seed delivery.

**Open question for client:** What coverage amount does Braport consider appropriate for the Parent Seed in Traidir's custody? What is the current insurance market's willingness to write "germplasm" or "biological asset" coverage at that level?

---

### 15. Mid-Season Insolvency: Crops in the Ground (Supply Agreement §§16.2, 16.4)

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§16.2 (termination for insolvency), 16.4 (effect of termination).

**Why it matters:** §16.4(a) requires Traidir to "promptly deliver to Customer all Parent Seed, Contract Seed and Braport Germplasm in its possession or control." But if Traidir becomes insolvent in July 2027, the Contract Seed is growing in fields in Iowa and Illinois. Braport has title (§7.2), but practically harvesting, conditioning, and delivering that seed requires Traidir's equipment, personnel, and grower relationships. A bankruptcy trustee may not cooperate, and Braport would need to engage a third party to complete the harvest — at significant cost and with quality risk.

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision to §16.4 (or a new §16.6) addressing mid-season insolvency:

> "16.6 Mid-Season Insolvency. If Supplier becomes subject to an insolvency proceeding during a Production Season before harvest is complete, Customer shall have the right, at Supplier's (or its estate's) cost, to take possession of all Production Fields and to complete harvest, Conditioning and delivery through a third-party contractor. Supplier (or its estate) shall cooperate with Customer's transition, including providing access to grower agreements, field records, and equipment. The insurance required by Section 14.1(b) shall be maintained through the end of the affected Production Season."

### 16. SOW §3.4 Yield Shortfall Triggers Only a "Discussion"

**Where:** SOW §3.4.

**What it says:** If Traidir expects to produce less than 90% of a hybrid's allocation, it "will notify Customer promptly, and the Parties will discuss in good faith whether to reallocate volume among hybrids."

**Why it matters:** There is no obligation on Traidir to make up the shortfall, no penalty, and no right for Braport to source elsewhere. "Discuss in good faith" is not a binding standard. Combined with the exclusivity obligation (Issue 4), Braport has no leverage.

**Recommended fix:** Add: "If Supplier expects to produce less than 90% of the Units allocated to any hybrid, Supplier will notify Customer at least 30 days before the expected harvest date for that hybrid, with a written explanation and a recovery plan. If the shortfall is not caused by a Force Majeure Event, Supplier will deliver the shortfall at no additional cost to Customer within 60 days after the final delivery date, or Customer may source the shortfall from an alternative grower and Supplier will reimburse Customer for the incremental cost."

### 17. Germplasm Notification Service Credit Is Trivial (SLS §5.1)

**Where:** SLS §5.1 (last-but-one row: "Germplasm Notification (4.5) — Each late notification — $25,000 per notification").

**Why it matters:** A $25,000 credit for failing to notify within 24 hours of a germplasm loss is a negligible deterrent. The 24-hour notification window exists so Braport can act quickly to contain a diversion or theft. If Traidir delays notification by a day, the $25,000 credit is less than one day's operating cost for a company of Traidir's size. The real harm (loss of containment opportunity) is not addressed.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the credit to $100,000 per late notification, and add that a late notification (beyond 24 hours) that results in an expanded loss (e.g., diverted seed is sold before Braport can issue a stop-order) makes Traidir liable for the incremental loss, uncapped (consistent with the fix to Issue 1).

### 18. SLS §6.4 Chronic Failure Requires Two Consecutive Seasons

**Where:** SLS §6.4.

**Why it matters:** Already discussed in Issue 4. Braport must endure two full growing cycles before it can terminate for chronic underperformance. In the seed business, that is two years of lost revenue and customer goodwill.

**Recommended fix:** See Issue 4.
