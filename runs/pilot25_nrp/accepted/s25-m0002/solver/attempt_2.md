## Summary

I have reviewed the Supply Agreement (effective October 1, 2026), Statement of Work No. 1 (2027 Production Season), Service Level Schedule, and the November 4, 2026 production planning meeting notes. I identify **twenty-three issues** ranging from critical to moderate, organized by severity. The two most consequential are: (1) a provision in the Service Level Schedule that effectively re-imposes the general liability cap on Germplasm Loss Events, directly contradicting the Supply Agreement's express carve-out for germplasm breaches; and (2) a split governing-law and forum clause that sends quality disputes to Iowa while leaving everything else in Delaware. Both are addressed below with proposed fixes.

---

## A. Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Service Level Schedule Caps Germplasm Loss Event Liability, Overriding the Supply Agreement's Express Carve-Out

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §6.2; Supply Agreement §§12.2, 12.3(a); Supply Agreement §2.3 (order of precedence).

**What it says:**

- Supply Agreement §12.3(a) provides that the indirect-damages exclusion (§12.1) and the liability cap (§12.2) do **not** apply to "Supplier's breach of Article 9, including any loss, diversion or unauthorized propagation of Parent Seed or other Braport Germplasm."
- Service Level Schedule §6.2 states: *"Notwithstanding Section 12.3 of the Agreement, Supplier's liability for any Germplasm Loss Event, including any related Performance Failure, is subject to the limit in Section 12.2 of the Agreement."*
- Supply Agreement §2.3 gives the Service Level Schedule **first** priority in the order of precedence.

**Why it matters:** Because the SLS governs over the Supply Agreement under §2.3, SLS §6.2 controls. The practical effect is that if Traidir's employees steal or accidentally propagate Braport's proprietary parent lines, Braport's recovery is capped at the fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim (roughly $6.4 million based on 2027 volumes). The entire strategic value of the germplasm—potentially worth hundreds of millions in future hybrid revenue—would be unrecoverable above that cap. This appears to eviscerate the core protection Braport negotiated in Article 9.

**Recommended fix:** Delete SLS §6.2 in its entirety, or restate it to confirm that the §12.3(a) carve-out applies. Proposed replacement language:

> **6.2 Germplasm Loss Events.** For the avoidance of doubt, Section 12.3(a) of the Agreement applies in full to any Germplasm Loss Event. Supplier's liability for a Germplasm Loss Event is not subject to the limitations in Sections 12.1 or 12.2 of the Agreement.

If Traidir resists deletion, a fallback would be to raise the cap for Article 9 breaches to a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $50 million) or to a multiple of annual fees (e.g., 5×), but the cleanest position is to restore the uncapped treatment the Supply Agreement already provides.

### Issue 2: Split Governing Law and Forum – Quality Disputes Go to Iowa, Everything Else to Delaware

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §8.1; Supply Agreement §§19.1, 19.2.

**What it says:**

- Supply Agreement §19.1: Delaware law governs. §19.2: Exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware.
- SLS §8.1: *"This Schedule, and any dispute concerning Performance Failures, Service Credits or Seed Quality, is governed by the laws of the State of Iowa … Any such dispute will be resolved exclusively in the state or federal courts located in Polk County, Iowa."*
- Under §2.3, the SLS has priority.

**Why it matters:** Braport faces a bifurcated dispute regime. A quality claim (e.g., a lot fails germination) is an Iowa-law, Iowa-forum matter. A germplasm theft claim is a Delaware-law, Delaware-forum matter (because it falls under Article 9, not the SLS). A volume shortfall claim could be argued to be either. This creates:

- Increased litigation cost (two jurisdictions, two bodies of law, potentially inconsistent outcomes).
- Strategic disadvantage: Traidir is an Iowa LLC operating in Iowa; Braport is a Delaware corporation. Traidir has home-court advantage on the most frequent type of dispute (quality and service credits).
- Interpretive risk: Iowa and Delaware courts may construe "material breach," "good faith," and "workmanlike manner" differently, creating uncertainty about when Braport can terminate.

**Recommended fix:** Conform SLS §8.1 to the Supply Agreement. Proposed language:

> **8.1 Law and Forum.** This Schedule is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles. Any dispute concerning this Schedule, including Performance Failures, Service Credits or Seed Quality, is subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state or federal courts located in Wilmington, Delaware, as provided in Section 19.2 of the Agreement.

If Traidir insists on a neutral or convenient forum, Delaware is already neutral (neither party is a Delaware entity in the operational sense, and Braport is a Delaware corporation). Alternatively, the parties could agree to arbitration seated in a neutral city, but that is a larger structural change.

### Issue 3: Assignment Asymmetry – Traidir Can Assign Freely; Braport Cannot; No Termination Right on Assignment

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§18.1, 18.2, 4.1.

**What it says:**

- §18.1: Braport may not assign without Traidir's consent.
- §18.2: Traidir may assign **without** Braport's consent, on 30 days' notice, "including by merger, consolidation, change of control or sale of all or part of its seed production assets."
- §4.1: Braport is locked into exclusive sourcing from Traidir for five years (plus renewals).

**Why it matters:** Traidir could sell its seed-production business to a direct competitor of Braport, to a financially weaker entity, or to a private-equity buyer that strips capacity. Braport would have no consent right, no termination right, and no ability to source elsewhere (exclusivity). The 30-day notice is informational only. This is particularly acute because Braport's germplasm—its most valuable asset—would then be in the hands of an unknown entity.

**Recommended fix:** Add a termination right and a consent requirement for change-of-control assignments. Proposed language to add as new §18.4:

> **18.4 Termination on Supplier Assignment.** If Supplier assigns this Agreement or any Statement of Work pursuant to Section 18.2, Customer may terminate the affected Statement of Work or this Agreement, as applicable, by written notice within 60 days after receipt of notice of the assignment, without liability. Upon such termination, Section 16.4 applies.

Additionally, amend §18.2 to require Braport's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment resulting in a change of control of Traidir or a sale of the seed-production assets to a competitor of Braport.

### Issue 4: Five-Year Exclusivity with Limited Exit Rights

**Where:** Supply Agreement §§4.1, 16.1, 16.2, 16.3; SLS §6.4.

**What it says:**

- §4.1: Braport must purchase **all** Contract Seed in the US and Canada exclusively from Traidir for the Initial Term (5 years) and any Renewal Term.
- Termination is available only for: material breach with 30-day cure (§16.1), insolvency (§16.2), or germplasm breach (§16.3).
- SLS §6.4: Chronic failure (same service level missed in two consecutive seasons) constitutes a material breach.

**Why it matters:** If Traidir underperforms in a single season (e.g., delivers only 88% of volume, misses first-pass quality by 3 points), Braport receives modest service credits but cannot terminate. It must wait a full second season of the same failure before the chronic-breach trigger fires. During both seasons, Braport cannot source from anyone else. For a company whose revenue depends on having hybrid seed available to customers on time, a 12–18% volume shortfall with no alternative sourcing is an existential operational risk.

**Recommended fix:** Add a "material underperformance" termination right. Proposed language as new §16.6:

> **16.6 Material Underperformance.** If in any Production Season Supplier fails to deliver at least 90% of the total Units allocated across all hybrids in the applicable Statement of Work (excluding shortfalls caused by a Force Majeure Event), Customer may terminate this Agreement or the affected Statement of Work by written notice within 30 days after the final delivery date for that Production Season, without liability.

Alternatively, lower the chronic-failure threshold in SLS §6.4 from "two consecutive Production Seasons" to "two consecutive Production Seasons or any single Production Season in which Supplier fails two or more different Service Levels."

## B. High-Priority Issues

### Issue 5: Circular Definition of Production Season End / Reconciliation Deadline

**Where:** SOW §1.3; Supply Agreement §9.4.

**What it says:**

- SOW §1.3: The Production Season ends "when Supplier has delivered all Contract Seed under this SOW **and completed the Parent Seed reconciliation** under Section 9.4 of the Agreement."
- Supply Agreement §9.4: "Within **60 days after the end of each Production Season**, Supplier shall deliver to Customer a reconciliation of all Parent Seed received and used."

**Why it matters:** The reconciliation is both a condition of the Production Season ending and a deliverable due 60 days after it ends. This is logically circular. In practice, if a dispute arises about whether the reconciliation was timely, Traidir could argue the season never "ended" (because the reconciliation wasn't done), so the 60-day clock never started. Conversely, Braport could argue the season ended on final delivery and the 60 days ran from there. The ambiguity cuts against Braport because it muddies the trigger for the SLS §4.6 service credit (chain-of-custody reconciliation variance) and for the survival of Traidir's obligations.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SOW §1.3 to decouple the two events. Proposed language:

> **1.3 Production Season.** This SOW covers the 2027 Production Season, beginning on the date Supplier receives the first shipment of Parent Seed and ending on the date Supplier has delivered all Contract Seed under this SOW (the "**Season End Date**"). Supplier shall complete the Parent Seed reconciliation under Section 9.4 of the Agreement within 60 days after the Season End Date.

### Issue 6: No Security for Grower Advances

**Where:** SOW §7.3; Meeting Notes §8.3.

**What it says:** Braport may advance up to 20% of the estimated production fee per hybrid on July 15, 2027. The advance is "credited against the first invoices." No security, letter of credit, or set-off right is specified.

**Why it matters:** If Traidir becomes insolvent between July 15, 2027 and the invoicing date (potentially December 2027 or January 2028), Braport's advance becomes an unsecured claim in Traidir's bankruptcy. At 20% of ~$6.4 million, that is roughly $1.28 million at risk. The meeting notes confirm no decision has been made yet, so this is still negotiable.

**Open question for client:** Does Braport intend to request the grower advance for 2027? If yes, what security is acceptable?

**Recommended fix (if advance is used):** Add to SOW §7.3:

> Any advance under this Section 7.3 shall be secured by a first-priority lien on the Contract Seed to be produced for the applicable hybrid, and Supplier shall execute such security instruments as Customer reasonably requests. In the event of Supplier's insolvency, Customer shall be entitled to set off any unearned advance against amounts owed to Supplier.

### Issue 7: Force Majeure + Exclusivity = No Alternative Sourcing for FM-Related Shortfalls

**Where:** SLS §4.2; Supply Agreement §§4.1, 17.1, 17.2.

**What it says:**

- SLS §4.2: Volume shortfalls "caused by a Force Majeure Event … are excluded from this Service Level" (i.e., no service credit).
- §17.2: Braport may terminate the SOW only if FM prevents performance of a "material part" for more than 60 days.
- §4.1: Braport cannot source elsewhere.

**Why it matters:** If a hail event destroys 25% of BSG-4120's crop in Story County, Traidir owes no service credit (FM exclusion), the event likely doesn't prevent a "material part" of the SOW for 60+ days (so no termination under §17.2), and Braport cannot buy the missing seed from another grower (exclusivity). Braport absorbs the full commercial loss of the shortfall with no remedy.

**Recommended fix:** Carve FM-related shortfalls out of the exclusivity obligation. Proposed language as new §4.4:

> **4.4 Exclusivity Exception for Force Majeure.** If a Force Majeure Event causes Supplier to deliver less than 95% of the Units allocated to any hybrid in a Statement of Work, Customer may source the shortfall from alternative suppliers for that Production Season without breach of Section 4.1, provided Customer gives Supplier written notice of the shortfall and the alternative source.

### Issue 8: No Specific Remedy for Third-Party Isolation Breach

**Where:** SLS §2.9; SOW §4.2; Supply Agreement §17.1.

**What it says:** Traidir must maintain 660-foot isolation. If a neighboring farmer plants non-conforming corn within that distance after Traidir has selected the field, the genetic purity of the lot may be compromised. A neighbor's planting decision is not listed as a Force Majeure Event in §17.1 (which covers "drought, flood, hail, windstorm, fire, epidemic, war, government action or labor disputes").

**Why it matters:** If a neighbor's violation causes a lot to fail the 98.0% genetic purity specification, Braport can reject the lot (§8.2) and claim the first-pass quality service credit. But if the contamination is below the rejection threshold yet still reduces value (e.g., 98.2% purity vs. 99.5% in a clean field), Braport has no remedy. Traidir could argue the neighbor's action was beyond its control. Braport could argue Traidir should have selected fields with adequate buffer or monitored neighboring plantings.

**Recommended fix:** Add to SOW §4.2:

> Supplier shall monitor neighboring fields during the growing season for any planting that would reduce isolation below the Specification. If a third party's planting threatens isolation, Supplier shall (a) notify Customer within 48 hours, (b) take all commercially reasonable steps to remedy the situation (including negotiating with the neighbor or applying border rows), and (c) if the isolation cannot be restored, Customer may direct Supplier to abandon the affected field and replant at Supplier's cost.

## C. Moderate-Priority Issues

### Issue 9: Service Credit for Late Germplasm Notification Is Disproportionately Low

**Where:** SLS §5.1 (row: Germplasm Notification); Supply Agreement §9.5.

**What it says:** Late notification of a Germplasm Loss Event: $25,000 per notification.

**Why it matters:** The 24-hour notification window in §9.5 exists so Braport can act quickly to contain a germplasm breach (e.g., trace diverted seed, notify law enforcement, alert competitors). If Traidir notifies on day 3 instead of day 1, the penalty is $25,000—trivial against the potential value of the compromised germplasm. The penalty should create a meaningful incentive for prompt notification.

**Recommended fix:** Increase to $100,000 per 24-hour period of delay, or tie it to a percentage of the estimated value of the affected germplasm. Alternatively, make any late notification a standalone material breach (triggering §16.1 termination rights) in addition to the credit.

### Issue 10: SOW §3.4 Yield-Shortfall Notification Uses Non-Binding Language

**Where:** SOW §3.4.

**What it says:** "If Supplier expects to produce less than 90% of the Units allocated to any hybrid, it will notify Customer **promptly**, and the Parties will **discuss in good faith** whether to reallocate volume among hybrids."

**Why it matters:** "Promptly" is undefined. "Discuss in good faith" creates no obligation to agree or to take any particular action. If Traidir knows by August 15 (the first yield-estimate date) that BSG-4610 will yield only 80% of target, it must notify Braport—but there is no deadline for that notification, no consequence for failing to notify, and no obligation to actually reallocate. Braport could discover the shortfall only at harvest.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SOW §3.4:

> If Supplier's yield estimate for any hybrid (under Section 3.4 or otherwise) indicates expected production below 90% of the allocated Units, Supplier shall notify Customer in writing within five Business Days of forming that estimate, stating the expected shortfall and the cause. The Parties shall meet within ten Business Days of such notice to agree on a reallocation plan. If the Parties cannot agree, Customer may direct Supplier to apply commercially reasonable efforts to maximize yield on the affected hybrid, and any resulting shortfall below 95% of allocation shall be subject to the Volume Service Credit in Section 5.1 of the Service Level Schedule.

### Issue 11: No Specified Storage Conditions for Parent Seed

**Where:** SLS Article 7 (Germplasm Security Measures); SOW §2.2.

**What it says:** SLS §7.1 requires "locked, access-controlled storage separate from all other seed" with badge access and video surveillance. No temperature, humidity, or duration limits are specified.

**Why it matters:** Parent Seed is due at Traidir's facility by March 15, 2027. Planting begins April 20. That is a minimum 36-day storage period. If the storage room is not climate-controlled (e.g., temperature exceeds 15°C or humidity exceeds 30% RH), seed vigor can decline before planting, reducing yield and potentially causing lots to miss the 88% cold-germination specification. The meeting notes describe the room but do not confirm temperature/humidity controls.

**Open question for client:** What are Braport's required storage conditions for Parent Seed (temperature range, RH range, maximum storage duration)? Does Braport have written production protocols that address this? (Supply Agreement §5.2 references "Customer's written production protocols as provided to Supplier from time to time"—if such protocols exist and specify storage conditions, they may already cover this gap, but they are not in the folder.)

**Recommended fix:** Add to SLS §7.1:

> Parent Seed shall be stored at a temperature between [X]°C and [X]°C and at a relative humidity not exceeding [X]%. Supplier shall monitor and record storage conditions daily and make the records available to Customer on request.

### Issue 12: Genetic Purity Testing Cost Allocation Is Ambiguous

**Where:** SLS §2.3; SOW §7.2.

**What it says:**

- SLS §2.3: Genetic purity is "determined by grow-out or by molecular marker analysis performed by a Certified Laboratory **on Customer's behalf**."
- SOW §7.2: The production fee "covers all … testing."

**Why it matters:** "On Customer's behalf" suggests Braport arranges and pays for the Certified Laboratory. But SOW §7.2 says the fee covers "testing." If Braport reads §7.2 as including genetic purity testing, it will expect Traidir to bear the cost. If Traidir reads SLS §2.3 as putting the cost on Braport, a dispute arises. Marker testing for three hybrids across multiple lots could cost $50,000–$150,000+ per season.

**Open question for client:** Which Certified Laboratory does Braport intend to use for genetic purity testing? What is the expected cost per lot? Does Braport expect this cost to be included in the production fee or borne separately?

**Recommended fix:** Clarify in SOW §7.2:

> The production fee covers all testing performed by or on behalf of Supplier, including warm germination, cold germination, physical purity, moisture, sizing, adventitious presence, and seed treatment verification. Molecular marker analysis for genetic purity performed by a Certified Laboratory on Customer's behalf is at Customer's cost and is not included in the production fee.

(Or the reverse, depending on the commercial deal.)

### Issue 13: No Ownership or Use Rights in Production Data

**Where:** Supply Agreement Article 10; SOW §§3.3, 8.2.

**What it says:** §10.1 assigns to Braport ownership of "Braport Germplasm, Contract Seed, Customer's production protocols, hybrid designations and trademarks." It does not mention yield data, field performance records, or agronomic data generated by Traidir during production.

**Why it matters:** Over a five-year term, Traidir will generate substantial data on Braport's parent lines: yield by field, by year, by environment; germination performance; input response. This data is valuable for Braport's breeding program and for modeling future production. Without an explicit data-ownership clause, Traidir could argue it owns the data as its own operational records, or at minimum that Braport has no right to use it for purposes beyond the Agreement (e.g., in a publication, in a negotiation with another grower, or in a product-labeling claim).

**Recommended fix:** Add to Supply Agreement §10.1:

> All data, records, and information generated by or for Supplier in the course of performing this Agreement or any Statement of Work, including but not limited to yield data, field performance records, agronomic observations, test results, and Production Data, shall be the sole property of Customer. Supplier shall provide Customer with complete and accurate copies of all such data upon request and shall not use such data for any purpose other than performance of this Agreement without Customer's prior written consent.

### Issue 14: Chronic Failure Threshold Requires Two Consecutive Seasons

**Where:** SLS §6.4.

**What it says:** "If Supplier fails the same Service Level in two consecutive Production Seasons, the failure is a material breach."

**Why it matters:** As noted in Issue 4, this means Braport must endure one full season of underperformance before it has any termination right (short of the other grounds in Article 16). For a company with annual seed-customer commitments, a single bad season can mean lost revenue and damaged relationships that no service credit will restore.

**Recommended fix:** See Issue 4 for proposed alternative language. At minimum, add: "A failure of the Volume Service Level (Section 4.2) in any single Production Season by more than 10 percentage points below the 95% threshold shall constitute a material breach."

### Issue 15: No Price Adjustment Mechanism Over the Five-Year Term

**Where:** SOW §7.1; Supply Agreement §3.1.

**What it says:** Fees are fixed per unit in the SOW. The Initial Term is five years. Each year a new SOW is executed (§2.1), but there is no formula or index for adjusting prices.

**Why it matters:** If input costs (fuel, labor, land rent, fertilizer) rise 20% over the term, Traidir will have strong incentive to seek price increases or to underinvest in capacity. If costs fall, Braport has no mechanism to capture the savings. The absence of a formula makes each annual SOW negotiation a potential flashpoint.

**Recommended fix (optional, depending on commercial strategy):** Add to the Supply Agreement:

> **6.6 Price Adjustment.** The fees in each Statement of Work may be adjusted annually by mutual agreement. In the absence of agreement, either Party may propose an adjustment of up to [X]% per annum, supported by reasonable documentation of cost changes. If the Parties cannot agree within 30 days of the proposal, the fees from the prior Statement of Work shall apply for the current Production Season pending resolution.

### Issue 16: "Promptly" Used Without Definition in Multiple Places

**Where:** SOW §3.4, §4.3; Supply Agreement §9.5 (24 hours is defined), §16.4 ("promptly deliver").

**What it says:** "Promptly" appears in SOW §3.4 (yield shortfall notice), SOW §4.3 (schedule deviation notice), and Supply Agreement §16.4 (post-termination return of materials). None of these instances defines a time period.

**Why it matters:** In a dispute, "promptly" will be argued about. Traidir will say it acted within a reasonable time; Braport will say it should have been immediate. For the yield-shortfall notice (SOW §3.4), even a two-week delay could cost Braport the ability to mitigate (e.g., by adjusting customer allocations).

**Recommended fix:** Replace "promptly" with defined time periods throughout. For SOW §4.3: "within 48 hours." For SOW §3.4: see Issue 10. For Supply Agreement §16.4: "within 10 Business Days."

### Issue 17: All Parent Seed Due on the Same Date – Extended Storage Risk

**Where:** SOW §2.2; SOW §4.3.

**What it says:** All five parent lines are due at the Ames facility "By March 15, 2027." Planting begins April 20, 2027.

**Why it matters:** As noted in Issue 11, this creates a minimum 36-day storage period before planting. The longer Parent Seed sits, the greater the risk of viability loss, contamination, or security incident. A staggered delivery aligned with actual planting needs for each field would reduce the window of exposure.

**Open question for client:** Can Braport stagger Parent Seed deliveries to align with the planting schedule by field? For example, BF-217 (planted in Story and Boone Counties) could be delivered in early April, just before those fields are planted.

**Recommended fix (if feasible):** Amend SOW §2.2 to provide delivery dates by parent line aligned with the planting schedule in §4.3, with a maximum pre-planting storage period of [X] days.

### Issue 18: No Deemed-Approval or Response-Deadline Mechanism for Field Substitutions

**Where:** SOW §4.1.

**What it says:** "Supplier must obtain Customer's prior written approval before substituting or adding any field."

**Why it matters:** If Traidir requests a field substitution on April 15 and Braport's operations team is slow to respond (e.g., takes 10 days), Traidir could argue it was unreasonably delayed and that Braport's silence constituted acquiescence. Conversely, Braport could argue that no approval was given, so Traidir breached by planting in an unapproved field. The agreement is silent on response timelines.

**Recommended fix:** Add to SOW §4.1:

> Customer shall respond to any field substitution request within five Business Days of receipt. If Customer does not respond within that period, the request shall be deemed approved, provided the substitute field meets all Specifications and is in a county adjacent to a currently approved Production Field.

(Or the reverse—deemed rejected—depending on Braport's risk appetite.)

### Issue 19: Insurance "Full Replacement Value" Is Inadequate for Proprietary Germplasm

**Where:** Supply Agreement §14.1(b).

**What it says:** Traidir must maintain "crop and property insurance covering Parent Seed and Contract Seed in its care, custody and control at full replacement value."

**Why it matters:** "Full replacement value" of Parent Seed is ambiguous. The physical seed might cost $5,000–$10,000 to replace (if it could be replaced at all), but the genetic information it carries may be irreplaceable and worth far more. A standard crop/property policy will not cover the strategic value of proprietary germplasm. If the Parent Seed is destroyed (e.g., fire in the storage room), the insurance pays the physical replacement cost, not the R&D investment embedded in the genetics.

**Open question for client:** Does Braport carry its own insurance on the Parent Seed while in Traidir's custody? What is Braport's internal valuation of the 2027 Parent Seed lots?

**Recommended fix:** Require Traidir to carry a specific germplasm liability policy (or a rider on its CGL policy) with a stated minimum limit (e.g., $25 million) naming Braport as additional insured and loss payee. Add to §14.1:

> (d) germplasm liability insurance with a minimum limit of $[X] per occurrence, naming Customer as additional insured and loss payee, covering loss of, damage to, or unauthorized use of Braport Germplasm in Supplier's care, custody or control.

### Issue 20: FCA Delivery Term Shifts Transit Risk to Braport

**Where:** SOW §5.4; Supply Agreement §7.3.

**What it says:** Delivery is "FCA Supplier's Ames, Iowa facility, for collection by Customer's designated carriers." Under §7.3, Traidir bears risk "until delivery to Customer under Section 7.1." Because the SOW defines delivery as FCA at the Ames dock, risk passes to Braport when the seed is loaded onto Braport's carrier.

**Why it matters:** Braport bears all transit risk from Ames to its destination (which could be a customer's silo in another state). If a load is damaged in transit, Braport cannot claim against Traidir under the Agreement. It would need to rely on its carrier's insurance. For 180,000 units (approximately 14.4 billion kernels), even a small percentage of transit loss is significant.

**Recommended fix (if Braport wants Traidir to bear transit risk):** Change SOW §5.4 to a DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) or DAP (Delivered at Place) term to Braport's designated destination. Alternatively, require Traidir to maintain cargo/transit insurance on Contract Seed until it is received and accepted at Braport's destination.

### Issue 21: No Approval Mechanism for Traidir's Purchase of Treatment Materials

**Where:** SOW §5.2.

**What it says:** "Customer will supply the treatment materials, or Supplier will purchase them and Customer will reimburse them at Supplier's cost without markup."

**Why it matters:** If Traidir purchases the treatment materials, it selects the supplier, product formulation, and quantity. There is no requirement for Braport to approve the specific product or supplier before purchase. If Traidir buys a substandard or off-specification treatment, the seed treatment specification (SLS §2.8: ±10% of target loading) could be missed, and Braport would be out of reimbursement with no recourse.

**Open question for client:** Does Braport intend to supply the treatment materials or have Traidir purchase them? (Meeting Notes §7.1: "Braport has not yet decided.")

Supplier shall not purchase or apply any treatment material without Customer's prior written approval.

### Issue 22: No Consequence for Missing Non-Delivery Schedule Milestones

**Where:** SOW §4.3 (Schedule table).

**What it says:** The schedule lists target windows for planting, detasseling, harvest, conditioning, and final delivery. The only service level tied to timing is On-Time Delivery (SLS §4.1), which applies to the final delivery date.

**Why it matters:** If Traidir plants on May 25 (the last day of the window) instead of April 20, the crop matures later, harvest is compressed, and conditioning may run past December 15. But unless the final delivery date (January 15, 2028) is missed, no service credit applies. Braport has no remedy for the compressed timeline even if it causes quality issues (e.g., harvest in wet conditions increasing mold risk).

**Recommended fix:** Add intermediate schedule milestones to SLS Article 4 with modest credits or, at minimum, a notification-and-cure mechanism:

> **4.7 Schedule Compliance.** Supplier shall complete each activity in the SOW Section 4.3 schedule within the target window. If Supplier expects any activity to extend beyond its target window by more than seven days, Supplier shall notify Customer in writing at least 48 hours before the window closes, stating the cause and the expected completion date. Failure to provide such notice is a Performance Failure subject to a Service Credit of $[X] per day of delay.

### Issue 23: SLS §5.1 "Full Percentage Point" Rounding Favors Traidir

**Where:** SLS §5.1 (First-Pass Quality row).

**What it says:** "2% of the fees for the Production Season for each **full** percentage point below 97%."

**Why it matters:** If the first-pass rate is 96.5% (half a point below), Braport receives zero credit because it is not a "full" percentage point. If the rate is 96.0%, Braport gets 2% of season fees (~$128,000). The half-point gap creates a cliff effect that discourages Traidir from improving performance in the 96.0–96.9% range.

**Recommended fix:** Change "full percentage point" to "percentage point (rounded down to the nearest 0.5 point)" or simply "each percentage point or fraction thereof below 97%."
