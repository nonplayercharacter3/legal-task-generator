## Summary

Gloumund Water Solutions, LLC ("Gloumund" or "Distributor") faces two immediate and serious threats from Droupourt Chemical Corporation ("Droupourt" or "Supplier"): (1) a notice of material breach of the non-competition covenant (Section 2.4) that, if not cured within 30 days, permits Droupourt to terminate the Agreement; and (2) a tender of the "Newark Claim" (a February 2026 ferric sulfate tote leak at Gloumund's Newark warehouse) for which Droupourt demands that Gloumund assume the defense and indemnify Droupourt under Section 11.1.

A comparison of the executed Agreement against the prior draft (v2, December 18, 2025) reveals that several provisions were materially altered in Droupourt's favor between draft and execution. The most consequential changes are: the non-compete was expanded in scope, geography, duration, and covered entities; the Distributor's indemnity was converted from a fault-based obligation with express carve-outs to an absolute obligation "regardless of the cause"; the Supplier's indemnity was narrowed; the comparative-responsibility provision was deleted; and the liability cap was made asymmetric so that only Gloumund's indemnity obligation is uncapped. These changes directly drive Gloumund's current exposure.

The items below are ordered by significance to the client.

## Issue 1: Section 11.1 Indemnity – "Regardless of the Cause" – Uncapped Exposure for the Newark Claim

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 11.1 (final); Prior Draft v2 § 11.1; Droupourt Notice Letter §§ 3.1–3.5.

**What the final agreement says.** Section 11.1 requires Gloumund to "defend, indemnify and hold harmless Supplier … from and against all Claims and Losses, including environmental and regulatory Claims and Losses, arising out of or relating to the storage, handling, transport or resale of Products by or on behalf of Distributor, **regardless of the cause**."

**What the draft said.** Draft v2 § 11.1 limited Gloumund's indemnity to claims arising from "(a) the negligence or willful misconduct of Distributor or its employees, agents or carriers …; (b) Distributor's breach of Section 7.2; or (c) any warranty or representation about the Products that Distributor makes to its customers beyond those in Section 8.1." Critically, the draft added: "Distributor has no obligation under this Section 11.1 to the extent a Claim or Loss is caused by a breach of Section 8.1 or by the design, condition or integrity of packaging for which Supplier is responsible under Section 6.2."

**Why it matters.** The Newark Claim involves a 330-gallon IBC tote of DC-FS-50 that leaked inside Gloumund's warehouse. Section 6.2 of the final Agreement expressly provides that "Supplier is responsible for the design, condition and integrity of all totes and drums it fills." If the tote failed because of a manufacturing or design defect, the draft would have shielded Gloumund entirely. The final Agreement's "regardless of the cause" language eliminates that shield. Gloumund must indemnify Droupourt for the Newark Claim even if Droupourt's own defective tote caused the leak.

Furthermore, because Section 12.3 (final) carves out "Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1" from both the consequential-damages exclusion (§ 12.1) and the 12-month payment cap (§ 12.2), Gloumund's indemnity exposure for the Newark Claim is **uncapped** and includes all environmental cleanup costs, regulatory fines, third-party claims, and attorneys' fees.

**Recommendation.**

- Investigate the cause of the tote failure immediately. Retain an independent packaging engineer to examine the preserved tote (see Issue 7 on Droupourt's preservation demand). If the failure is attributable to the tote's design, condition, or integrity, Gloumund has a factual basis to argue that Droupourt's own Section 6.2 responsibility should limit or allocate the Newark Claim, even though the contract language is adverse.
- Assess whether Gloumund's pollution legal liability and CGL policies respond to the Newark Claim. If they do, the insurance proceeds should fund the defense and any settlement, mitigating the uncapped contractual exposure.
- In any negotiation with Droupourt, press for a cost-sharing or allocation arrangement based on the tote's condition. The "regardless of the cause" language is contractually binding, but a court may be reluctant to enforce it where the Supplier's own product defect is the proximate cause, particularly under New York law.
- **Fact needed from client:** What is the current status of the NJDEP investigation? What cleanup has been performed or is required? What are the estimated costs? Has Gloumund given notice to its insurers?

## Issue 2: Section 2.4 Non-Competition – Expanded Scope, Geography, Duration, and Affiliate Coverage

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 1.4, 2.4, 10.4 (final); Prior Draft v2 §§ 1.4, 2.4, 10.4; Droupourt Notice Letter §§ 2.1–2.5.

**What changed between draft and final:**

| Element | Draft v2 | Final Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Covered products (§ 1.4) | "any polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate coagulant that is not supplied by Supplier" | "any chemical used in the treatment of water or wastewater, **whether or not it competes with the Products**, other than Products purchased from Supplier" |
| Geographic scope | "in the Territory" (NY, NJ, PA) | "anywhere in North America" |
| Duration | "During the Term" only | "During the Term **and for eighteen (18) months after** its expiration or termination" |
| Affiliate restriction | None | "shall cause its Affiliates not to" |
| Activities | "purchase for resale, market, distribute or sell" | "manufacture, purchase for resale, market, **promote**, distribute or sell" |
| Express carve-out | "This Section 2.4 does not restrict Distributor from selling any other water or wastewater treatment chemical, in the Territory or elsewhere." | **Deleted** |
| Survival (§ 10.4) | Not listed as surviving | **Added** to survival clause |

**Why it matters.** Droupourt's March 16 notice alleges that Gloumund has been purchasing and reselling sodium hypochlorite (a disinfectant, not a coagulant) to Connecticut water utilities since at least February 2026. Under the draft, this conduct would have been perfectly permissible: the draft restricted only coagulants, only in the Territory, only during the Term, and contained an express carve-out for "any other water or wastewater treatment chemical." Under the final Agreement, sodium hypochlorite is a "Competing Product" (it is a chemical used in water treatment), Connecticut is in North America, and the restriction binds Gloumund's Affiliates. Droupourt has a colorable argument that this is a material breach.

If Gloumund does not cure within 30 days of the notice, Droupourt may terminate under Section 10.1. Upon termination, Section 2.4 survives (per § 10.4), meaning the non-compete continues for 18 months post-termination. Droupourt also reserves the right to seek injunctive relief.

**Recommendation.**

- **Fact needed from client:** Confirm whether Gloumund (or any Affiliate) actually purchased and resold sodium hypochlorite to Connecticut utilities in February 2026. If so, determine the volume, revenue, and whether the sales are ongoing. Determine whether this was a deliberate business decision or an oversight.
- If the conduct is confirmed, assess the enforceability of the covenant under New York law. A post-termination non-compete that covers all of North America for 18 months and restricts all water treatment chemicals (not just competing products) is aggressive and may be deemed an unreasonable restraint of trade. New York courts will look at whether the restriction is no broader than necessary to protect the Supplier's legitimate interests (goodwill, customer relationships, trade secrets). The "whether or not it competes with the Products" language in § 1.4 is particularly vulnerable because it restricts products that have no competitive overlap with Droupourt's coagulants.
- Consider whether to cure (cease the sodium hypochlorite sales) to avoid termination, while reserving the right to challenge the covenant's enforceability if Droupourt later seeks injunctive relief. A cure avoids the immediate termination trigger but does not waive the argument that the covenant is overbroad.
- If Gloumund cures, negotiate a written release or amendment narrowing Section 2.4 to its draft-v2 scope (coagulants only, Territory only, during the Term only) as a condition of continuing the relationship.
- **Fact needed:** Are any of Gloumund's Affiliates (including Gloumund Holdings) also selling water treatment chemicals in other states? The Affiliate restriction could implicate the parent.

## Issue 3: Section 12.3 – Asymmetric Liability Cap

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 12.3 (final); Prior Draft v2 § 12.3.

**What the final says.** "The limitations in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 apply to all liability under this Agreement, **except for Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1**."

**What the draft said.** The draft's § 12.3 excluded from the cap: (a) either Party's indemnification obligations for third-party Claims under Section 11; (b) either Party's breach of Section 14 (confidentiality); (c) either Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct; and (d) Distributor's obligation to pay for Products delivered.

**Why it matters.** In the final Agreement:

- Gloumund's Section 11.1 indemnity (including the Newark Claim) is **uncapped** and not subject to the consequential-damages exclusion.
- Droupourt's Section 11.2 indemnity (e.g., for a defective product or Specifications non-conformity) **is capped** at 12 months of payments and **is subject to** the consequential-damages exclusion.
- Droupourt's gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct **is capped** (unlike the draft, which excluded these from the cap).
- Breach of confidentiality by either party **is capped** (unlike the draft).

This creates a structural asymmetry: if Droupourt's defective tote causes a catastrophic environmental event, Gloumund's indemnity to Droupourt is unlimited, but Gloumund's recovery from Droupourt for the same event is capped at 12 months of purchases. At the current $3.5M annual minimum, the cap is approximately $3.5M – a fraction of what a major environmental cleanup could cost.

**Recommendation.**

- This is a negotiation point if the relationship continues. Seek to restore the draft's mutual exclusions from the cap (at minimum for gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct and for both parties' indemnification obligations).
- In the interim, ensure that Gloumund's insurance program (CGL, pollution legal liability) is sufficient to cover the uncapped Section 11.1 exposure. **Fact needed:** What are the current policy limits, and do they cover the Newark Claim? Are there self-insured retentions?

## Issue 4: Deletion of Section 11.4 (Comparative Responsibility) and Narrowing of Section 11.2

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 11.2 (final); Prior Draft v2 §§ 11.2, 11.4.

**What changed.**

- Draft § 11.2 gave Gloumund an indemnity from Droupourt for: (a) product defects; (b) Specifications/warranty failures; **(c) any failure of packaging to meet Supplier's obligations under Section 6.2; (d) the negligence or willful misconduct of Supplier or its employees or agents**; and (e) IP infringement.
- Final § 11.2 gives Gloumund an indemnity from Droupourt for only: (a) product defects; (b) Specifications/warranty failures; and (c) IP infringement. **Packaging failure and Supplier negligence/willful misconduct were deleted.**
- Draft § 11.4 provided: "If both Parties contribute to a Claim or Loss, each Party's obligation under this Section 11 is limited to its proportionate share of responsibility." **This was deleted entirely.**

**Why it matters.** For the Newark Claim specifically: if the tote leaked because of a packaging defect, Gloumund's only potential contractual recourse against Droupourt is through § 11.2(b) (failure to conform to Specifications, since Exhibit A requires totes to be "supplied by Supplier in clean, leak-tested condition"). There is no express packaging indemnity, no Supplier-negligence indemnity, and no comparative-fault allocation. Gloumund must indemnify Droupourt under § 11.1 "regardless of the cause," and its recovery from Droupourt (if any) is limited to the narrow § 11.2(b) theory and is subject to the cap in § 12.2.

**Recommendation.**

- Build the factual record on the tote's condition. If the tote was not leak-tested or was defective when Droupourt filled it, that is a Specifications non-conformity under § 8.1(a) and § 11.2(b).
- In any renegotiation, demand restoration of § 11.2(c) (packaging), § 11.2(d) (Supplier negligence), and § 11.4 (comparative responsibility).
- **Fact needed:** Did Gloumund inspect the tote upon receipt under § 6.4? Was any nonconformity noted? If Gloumund accepted the tote without objection within 10 business days, Droupourt may argue the tote conformed to Specifications at delivery, weakening the § 11.2(b) claim.

## Issue 5: The Newark Claim – Immediate Procedural Obligations and Deadlines

**Deadlines imposed by Droupourt's notice:**

**Why it matters.** Failure to respond within the stated deadlines could be characterized as a breach of the indemnification procedure (§ 11.3) or as bad faith, giving Droupourt additional leverage. However, Gloumund is not obligated to accept the tender as framed. Section 11.3(b) gives the *indemnifying* party (here, Gloumund, if the tender is valid) control of the defense. Gloumund should not simply "accept" Droupourt's characterization that § 11.1 applies "regardless of the cause" without reserving its rights.

**Recommendation.**

- Respond within the 10-business-day window. Acknowledge receipt of the tender. State that Gloumund is evaluating its obligations and reserving all rights, including the right to argue that the Claim is attributable to Droupourt's packaging responsibility under § 6.2 and that Droupourt's own indemnity under § 11.2 may apply.

- Comply with the preservation demand. Secure the tote, photograph and document its condition, preserve warehouse video, and collect all receiving/inspection records. **Do not repair or alter the tote.**
- Give notice to Gloumund's CGL and pollution legal liability carriers immediately. Confirm whether Droupourt is named as an additional insured and what the policy's terms provide for the insured's defense of a claim in which the additional insured is also potentially at fault.
- **Fact needed:** Has Gloumund already notified its insurers? What are the policy terms regarding additional insureds and the duty to defend? What was the result of the § 6.4 inspection of this particular tote at delivery?

## Issue 6: "Competing Products" Definition – Section 1.4

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 1.4 (final); Prior Draft v2 § 1.4.

**What changed.** The draft defined Competing Products as "any polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate coagulant that is not supplied by Supplier under this Agreement." The final defines them as "any chemical used in the treatment of water or wastewater, **whether or not it competes with the Products**, other than Products purchased from Supplier under this Agreement."

**Why it matters.** This is the definitional hook that makes the sodium hypochlorite sales a breach. Sodium hypochlorite is a disinfectant, not a coagulant. It does not compete with polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate. The phrase "whether or not it competes with the Products" confirms that Droupourt intended to capture all water treatment chemicals, not just direct competitors. This definition, combined with the North American geographic scope and the 18-month tail, effectively gives Droupourt an exclusive distribution right over the entire water treatment chemical category in North America – far broader than the "exclusive distributor of the Products" appointment in § 2.1.

**Recommendation.**

- This definition is the most vulnerable provision in the Agreement to an unenforceability challenge. In any litigation or negotiation, argue that the definition is broader than necessary to protect Droupourt's legitimate interests in its coagulant product line and that the "whether or not it competes" language is an unreasonable restraint.
- If Gloumund wishes to continue selling other water treatment chemicals (which is likely core to its business), a negotiated amendment is essential. Propose restoring the draft's definition (coagulants only) or, at minimum, limiting the restriction to products that are "functionally substitutable" for the Products.
- **Fact needed:** What is Gloumund's full product line beyond Droupourt's coagulants? How significant are non-coagulant products (disinfectants, pH adjusters, sequestrants, etc.) to Gloumund's revenue? This determines the commercial stakes of the non-compete.

## Issue 7: Droupourt's Preservation and Access Demands

**Documents:** Droupourt Notice Letter § 3.6.

**What Droupourt demands.** Gloumund must preserve the leaking tote, its contents, labeling, fragments, fittings, and all related records (receiving/inspection records under § 6.4, storage logs, warehouse video, incident reports, communications with NJDEP and the Passaic Valley Sewerage Commission). Droupourt may not dispose of, repair, or alter the tote without Droupourt's prior written consent. Droupourt requests access to inspect the tote and release area within 10 business days.

**Why it matters.** The tote is potentially the single most important piece of physical evidence. If it was defective (cracked seam, failed valve, corroded wall), that supports Gloumund's argument that Droupourt's packaging was at fault under § 6.2. If it was damaged in Gloumund's warehouse (dropped, punctured by forklift, stored incompatibly), that supports Droupourt's position. The records of the § 6.4 inspection at delivery are also critical: if Gloumund accepted the tote without noting any defect, Droupourt will argue it was in good condition when delivered.

**Recommendation.**

- Issue a litigation hold immediately covering all documents, electronic communications, video, and physical items related to the Newark incident and the sodium hypochlorite sales.
- Do not allow Droupourt personnel to handle or inspect the tote without Gloumund's own representative (and ideally independent expert) present. Droupourt's "request" for access is not a contractual right; § 11.3 gives the indemnifying party (Gloumund, if the tender is valid) control.
- If Gloumund consents to an inspection, do so under a protocol that preserves chain of custody and allows Gloumund's expert to examine the tote first or concurrently.
- **Fact needed:** What does the warehouse video show? Was the tote dropped, struck, or otherwise mishandled? What were the storage conditions? Did the tote show any signs of damage before the leak?

## Issue 8: Parent Guaranty – Structure and Exposure for Gloumund Holdings, Inc.

**Documents:** Parent Guaranty §§ 1.2, 2.1–2.4, 3.1, 9.1–9.2; Distribution Agreement § 16.1.

**Key terms:**

- **Scope (§ 1.2):** Guarantees "all present and future payment obligations of Distributor **and its Affiliates** for Products," including purchase price, invoiced charges, tote deposits, taxes, late charges, "whether due, not yet due, absolute or contingent."
- **Nature (§ 2.2):** Guaranty of payment, not collection. Droupourt need not proceed against Gloumund first.
- **Demand (§ 2.3):** Payment due within 10 business days of written demand, without set-off or counterclaim.
- **Cap (§ 3.1):** $2,000,000 aggregate.
- **Continuing (§ 2.4):** Applies to all obligations "whenever incurred" until termination under § 9.
- **Term (§ 9.1):** Remains in effect until the later of (a) expiration/termination of the Distribution Agreement and (b) payment in full of all Guaranteed Obligations incurred before that date.
- **Termination trap (§ 9.2):** Gloumund Holdings may terminate as to future obligations on 90 days' notice, but if it does, "Distributor shall be in breach of Section 16.1 of the Distribution Agreement unless a replacement guaranty reasonably acceptable to Supplier is delivered before the termination takes effect."

**Why it matters.**

- The $2M cap is less than one year's minimum purchase obligation ($3.5M under Exhibit B). If Gloumund defaults on payments, the parent's exposure is limited to $2M, but the parent's equity in the subsidiary is at risk if Droupourt terminates and pursues the subsidiary directly.
- The "Affiliates" language in § 1.2 means that if any other Gloumund entity purchases products from Droupourt (even outside the Distribution Agreement), the parent guarantees those payments too.
- The § 9.2 termination trap means the parent cannot unilaterally exit the guaranty without triggering a default under the Distribution Agreement. This locks the parent in for the duration of the relationship.
- If Droupourt terminates the Agreement for the Section 2.4 breach, the Guaranty continues (§ 9.1) until all payment obligations incurred before termination are paid. The parent cannot walk away.

**Recommendation.**

- **Fact needed:** Does Gloumund Holdings have other subsidiaries or affiliated entities that purchase products from Droupourt or its Affiliates? If so, the Guaranty's scope is broader than the Distribution Agreement itself.
- If the relationship with Droupourt is to continue, negotiate a reduction in the Guaranty's scope (remove "Affiliates," limit to obligations under the Distribution Agreement only) or an increase in the cap to match the annual minimum.
- Be aware that the Guaranty is governed by New York law (§ 11.1) and subject to exclusive jurisdiction in New York County (§ 11.2), with a jury trial waiver (§ 11.3).

## Issue 9: Termination Consequences – Section 10.3

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 10.1, 10.3, 10.4; Droupourt Notice Letter § 2.5.

**Why it matters.** If Droupourt terminates for Gloumund's breach of § 2.4:

- Under § 10.3(b), Droupourt is not required to fill accepted Purchase Orders (because it terminated under § 10.1). Gloumund loses its supply of coagulants.
- Under § 10.3(c), Gloumund may sell off remaining inventory for only 90 days.
- Under § 10.4, § 2.4 survives, so the non-compete continues for 18 months post-termination.
- Droupourt has expressly reserved the right to decline to fill Purchase Orders (§ 2.5 of the notice).
- The Parent Guaranty continues under § 9.1 until all pre-termination payment obligations are satisfied.

The combination of loss of supply, a 90-day sell-off window, and an 18-month non-compete on all water treatment chemicals in North America could be existentially threatening to Gloumund's business if it cannot source coagulants from another supplier during the restricted period.

**Recommendation.**

- **Fact needed:** Can Gloumund source polyaluminum chloride and ferric sulfate from an alternative supplier on reasonable terms? If the non-compete is enforced, Gloumund cannot purchase those products from anyone other than Droupourt for 18 months post-termination. This is a critical business continuity question.
- If termination appears likely, begin contingency planning for alternative sourcing (subject to the non-compete) and for the 90-day inventory sell-off.
- In negotiations, seek a provision that, upon termination for Supplier's convenience or for Distributor's breach, the non-compete in § 2.4 would be limited to the Territory and to the Products (coagulants) rather than all water treatment chemicals in North America.

## Issue 10: Insurance – Additional Insured Status and Coverage Gaps

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 13.1, 13.2; Droupourt Notice Letter § 4.1.

**Why it matters.** Section 13.2 requires each party to name the other as an additional insured under CGL and pollution legal liability policies. This means Droupourt is an additional insured on Gloumund's policies. If Gloumund's pollution legal liability policy responds to the Newark Claim, Droupourt may be able to claim coverage directly under that policy, potentially reducing Gloumund's out-of-pocket exposure but also giving Droupourt a direct source of recovery that Gloumund did not intend.

Additionally, many CGL and pollution policies contain exclusions or limitations where the additional insured's own act or omission caused the loss. If Droupourt's defective tote caused the leak, Gloumund's carrier may argue that Droupourt (as additional insured) is not entitled to coverage for its own negligence, or that the policy does not cover the insured's (Gloumund's) obligation to indemnify the additional insured (Droupourt) for the additional insured's own fault.

**Recommendation.**

- **Fact needed:** Obtain copies of Gloumund's CGL and pollution legal liability policies. Review the additional insured endorsements, the duty to defend provisions, and any exclusions for the insured's contractual indemnity obligations. Determine whether the policies cover Gloumund's indemnity obligation to Droupourt under § 11.1.
- Confirm that timely notice has been given to the carriers. Late notice can jeopardize coverage.
- If the policies do not cover Gloumund's contractual indemnity to Droupourt (some policies exclude "contractual liability" beyond "insured contracts"), Gloumund's uncapped § 11.1 exposure is not insured, and the full financial risk falls on the company.

## Issue 11: Section 6.4 Inspection – Potential Waiver or Estoppel Risk

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 6.4; Droupourt Notice Letter § 3.6.

**Why it matters.** Section 6.4 requires Gloumund to inspect Products within 10 business days of receipt and to notify Droupourt of any "shortage, visible damage or nonconformity with the Specifications that a reasonable inspection would reveal." If the tote that leaked was delivered more than 10 business days before February 27, 2026, and Gloumund did not report any visible defect at inspection, Droupourt will argue that the tote conformed to Specifications at delivery and that the leak was caused by Gloumund's storage or handling. This would strengthen Droupourt's position that § 11.1 applies cleanly.

Conversely, if Gloumund did note a defect or if the tote was delivered within 10 business days of the leak, Gloumund has a stronger argument that the packaging failure was latent and attributable to Droupourt.

**Recommendation.**

- **Fact needed:** When was the specific tote that leaked delivered to Gloumund's Newark warehouse? Did Gloumund inspect it within 10 business days? Was any nonconformity noted? What do the receiving records show?
- Preserve all inspection records, delivery tickets, and communications regarding that tote.

## Issue 12: Notice and Cure Period – Tactical Considerations

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 10.1, 17.1; Droupourt Notice Letter §§ 2.4, 3.4.

**Why it matters.** The notice was delivered by overnight courier and email on March 16, 2026. Under § 17.1, notice is effective upon receipt. The 30-day cure period for the § 2.4 breach and the 10-business-day response period for the Newark Claim tender are running from the date of receipt. Gloumund should confirm the actual date of receipt to calculate deadlines precisely.

Droupourt's demand for an "officer-signed" confirmation that Gloumund has ceased all Competing Product sales (§ 2.4(a) of the notice) is significant: it creates a written admission that could be used in subsequent litigation if Gloumund later argues the covenant is unenforceable.

**Recommendation.**

- Confirm the date the notice was actually received at Gloumund's Newark office.
- Do not provide the officer-signed confirmation without legal review. If Gloumund cures, a confirmation can be given, but it should be carefully drafted to avoid admitting that the conduct constituted a breach or that the covenant is enforceable as written.
- Consider whether to respond to both demands (breach cure and Newark tender) in a single letter or separately. A single response allows Gloumund to address the interrelated issues coherently.
