## Executive Summary

Gloumund Water Solutions, LLC ("Distributor" or "Gloumund") faces two simultaneous and interrelated threats from Droupourt Chemical Corporation ("Supplier"): (1) a notice of material breach of the non-competition covenant (Section 2.4) with a 30-day cure demand and express reservation of termination rights, and (2) a tender of the Newark warehouse spill claim under Section 11.1, demanding that Distributor assume the defense within 10 business days. Both threats are significantly more dangerous to Gloumund than the prior draft of the agreement (Draft v2, December 18, 2025) would have made them, because the executed agreement materially expanded the non-compete, stripped the fault-based limitation from Distributor's indemnification obligation, deleted the comparative-responsibility provision, and narrowed the exclusions from the liability cap. The parent guaranty compounds the financial exposure if the relationship terminates.

## Issue 1: Non-Competition Breach – Section 2.4 and Section 1.4 (Highest Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Final Agreement, Section 2.4 (Non-Competition) and Section 1.4 (Competing Products definition)
- Draft v2, Section 2.4 and Section 1.4
- Droupourt Notice Letter, Section 2 (¶¶ 2.1–2.5)

**What Changed from Draft to Final:**

| Element | Draft v2 | Final Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Product scope (Sec. 1.4) | "any polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate coagulant that is not supplied by Supplier" | "any chemical used in the treatment of water or wastewater, **whether or not it competes with the Products**, other than Products purchased from Supplier" |
| Geographic scope (Sec. 2.4) | "in the Territory" (NY, NJ, PA only) | "**anywhere in North America**" |
| Affiliate restriction | Not present | "shall cause its Affiliates not to" |
| Post-termination tail | Not present | "for eighteen (18) months after its expiration or termination" |
| Express carve-out | "This Section 2.4 does not restrict Distributor from selling any other water or wastewater treatment chemical, in the Territory or elsewhere." | **Deleted** |
| Survival (Sec. 10.4) | Section 2.4 not listed as surviving | Section 2.4 **added** to survival clause |

**Why It Matters:**

Under the Draft v2 language, Gloumund's sale of sodium hypochlorite in Connecticut would **not** have been a breach. Sodium hypochlorite is a disinfectant, not a polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate coagulant, so it would not have been a "Competing Product." Moreover, even if it had been, the restriction applied only "in the Territory" (NY, NJ, PA), and Connecticut is outside the Territory. The express carve-out in Draft v2 Section 2.4 would have confirmed this.

Under the executed agreement, every one of those protections is gone. Sodium hypochlorite is "a chemical used in the treatment of water or wastewater," the restriction applies "anywhere in North America," and the Affiliate restriction means even Gloumund Holdings, Inc. or any sister entity is captured. Droupourt's notice letter (¶¶ 2.2–2.3) correctly identifies this as a breach under the final language.

The practical consequences if Droupourt terminates:

- **Loss of exclusive distribution** in NY, NJ, PA for the Products (Section 2.1).
- **Supply disruption:** Under Section 10.3(b), if Supplier terminates under Section 10.1, it is expressly relieved of the obligation to fill accepted Purchase Orders. Gloumund could be cut off from its coagulant supply with no contractual right to continued delivery.
- **Sell-off period:** Only 90 days to liquidate remaining inventory (Section 10.3(c)).
- **Post-termination non-compete:** Section 2.4 survives (Section 10.4) and binds Gloumund and its Affiliates for 18 months post-termination, anywhere in North America. This means Gloumund could not sell *any* water treatment chemical (not just coagulants) for a year and a half after termination.
- **Injunctive relief and damages:** Droupourt expressly reserves the right to seek both (Notice Letter ¶ 2.5), in New York courts (Section 18.3).

**Recommendation:**

1. **Immediately assess whether the sodium hypochlorite sales can be cured within 30 days.** Droupourt demands (a) cessation of all Competing Product activity, (b) officer-signed confirmation, and (c) a customer list with quantities and invoiced amounts (Notice Letter ¶ 2.4). If Gloumund has existing contracts with Connecticut utilities for calendar year 2026, "ceasing" may mean breaching those contracts. We need to know the terms and duration of those commitments.
2. **Evaluate whether to cure or to negotiate.** If Gloumund cures, Droupourt may still terminate at its discretion (the notice says "material breach," and Droupourt is reserving the right to terminate if cure is not satisfactory). A negotiated resolution—perhaps a side letter carving out disinfectants or limiting the non-compete to coagulants—may be preferable to a clean cure that leaves the relationship poisoned.
3. **Preserve the argument that the final agreement's Section 2.4 is unenforceable as an overbroad restraint on trade.** New York law (and the law of any North American jurisdiction where Gloumund operates) may not enforce a non-compete that bars a distributor from selling *any* water treatment chemical *anywhere in North America* for 18 months post-termination, particularly where the supplier's legitimate interest is protection of its coagulant franchise in a three-state territory. This is a strong public-policy argument, though it carries litigation risk.
4. **Do not ignore the 30-day deadline.** Failure to respond or cure within the window hands Droupourt a clean termination right and weakens any later argument that the breach was cured or excused.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- What are the terms of the Connecticut sodium hypochlorite contracts? (Duration, volume, termination rights, liquidated damages?)
- When exactly did Gloumund begin purchasing and reselling sodium hypochlorite?
- Were any Affiliates (including Gloumund Holdings, Inc.) involved in the Connecticut sales?
- Was the expansion of Section 2.4 and Section 1.4 from the draft to the final version discussed with Droupourt before signing? Was there any side correspondence or email thread addressing the change? (This goes to whether there is an argument of mutual mistake or that the final language was not the agreed deal.)
- Does Gloumund sell any other water treatment chemicals (beyond coagulants and sodium hypochlorite)? If so, where?

## Issue 2: Newark Warehouse Spill – Indemnification "Regardless of Cause" (Highest Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Final Agreement, Section 11.1 (Distributor Indemnification)
- Draft v2, Section 11.1 (Distributor Indemnification)
- Final Agreement, Section 6.2 (Packaging and Labeling)
- Droupourt Notice Letter, Section 3 (¶¶ 3.1–3.7)

**What Changed from Draft to Final:**

Draft v2 Section 11.1 limited Distributor's indemnity to claims arising "to the extent" caused by:
- (a) Distributor's negligence or willful misconduct in storage, handling, transport or resale;
- (b) Distributor's breach of Section 7.2 (storage/handling obligations); or
- (c) warranties Distributor made to its customers beyond Section 8.1.

It then provided an **express carve-out**: "Distributor has no obligation under this Section 11.1 to the extent a Claim or Loss is caused by a breach of Section 8.1 or by the design, condition or integrity of packaging for which Supplier is responsible under Section 6.2."

Final Agreement Section 11.1 replaces all of the above with a single, unqualified obligation: Distributor indemnifies for "all Claims and Losses, including environmental and regulatory Claims and Losses, arising out of or relating to the storage, handling, transport or resale of Products by or on behalf of Distributor, **regardless of the cause**." The packaging carve-out is gone. The fault requirement is gone.

**Why It Matters:**

Section 6.2 of the final agreement (unchanged from the draft) states: "Supplier is responsible for the design, condition and integrity of all totes and drums it fills." If the IBC tote that leaked on February 27, 2026 was defective in its design, condition, or integrity—a fact that is entirely plausible given that it was a 330-gallon tote of liquid ferric sulfate that ruptured in a warehouse—then under the Draft v2, Gloumund would have had a complete defense to Droupourt's indemnification demand. The packaging failure would have been Supplier's responsibility, and the express carve-out in Draft Section 11.1 would have shielded Gloumund.

Under the executed agreement, that defense does not exist. "Regardless of the cause" means Gloumund must indemnify Droupourt even if the tote was defective, even if the leak was entirely due to Supplier's packaging, and even if Gloumund was not negligent in any way. The only question is whether the claim "arises out of or relating to the storage, handling, transport or resale of Products by or on behalf of Distributor." A leak in Gloumund's warehouse almost certainly meets that test.

The financial exposure is potentially severe:
- NJDEP investigation and cleanup under the New Jersey Spill Act (which imposes strict, joint and several liability on "persons" who contribute to a spill).
- Passaic Valley Sewerage Commission review of possible discharge to its system.
- Droupourt's own legal and consulting fees (Notice Letter ¶ 3.5), which Gloumund must cover as part of "Losses" (defined in Section 1.6 to include "costs of investigation, cleanup and remediation" and "reasonable attorneys' and consultants' fees").
- Any third-party claims from neighboring properties or the sewerage commission.

**Additionally:** Droupourt is demanding that Gloumund assume the defense within 10 business days (Notice Letter ¶ 3.4). If Gloumund does not accept the tender, Droupourt may argue Gloumund is in breach of Section 11.3(b) (failure to allow the indemnifying party to control the defense—here inverted, as Droupourt is the indemnitee demanding the indemnitor take over). More practically, Droupourt will continue incurring fees and billing Gloumund for them.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Do not accept the tender unconditionally.** Respond within the 10-business-day window (the letter was delivered by courier and email on or about March 16, 2026; 10 business days from receipt puts the deadline around March 30, 2026—confirm exact receipt date). The response should:
   - Acknowledge receipt of the tender.
   - State that Gloumund is investigating the cause of the tote failure.
   - Reserve all rights, including the right to assert that the leak was caused by the design, condition, or integrity of the tote for which Supplier is responsible under Section 6.2.
   - Request that the parties cooperate in the investigation and that Supplier provide the tote's manufacturing and quality-control records, the certificate of analysis for the lot, and any prior complaints about that tote or tote model.
   - Propose a joint defense arrangement or, at minimum, that Supplier participate in the defense (as Section 11.3 permits the indemnified party to do) and that costs be allocated based on the investigation findings.

2. **Immediately inspect and preserve the tote.** The notice letter demands preservation (¶ 3.6), and Gloumund should comply to avoid spoliation arguments. But Gloumund should also conduct its own independent inspection and engage its own forensic engineer to determine whether the tote failed due to a manufacturing defect, a handling issue, or some other cause. This is critical: if the tote was defective, Gloumund has a strong factual basis to resist the indemnification demand and to counterclaim under Section 11.2 (Supplier's indemnity for "any defect in the Products"—arguably the tote is part of the "Product" packaging) or in tort (negligent manufacture of the tote).

3. **Notify Gloumund's insurers immediately.** Section 13.1 requires $5M CGL (including products/completed operations) and $5M pollution legal liability. The Newark spill is exactly the type of claim these policies are designed to cover. Late notice can give insurers a defense. Confirm that Droupourt is named as an additional insured under both policies (Section 13.2) and provide certificates as demanded (Notice Letter ¶ 4.1).

4. **Assess whether Section 12.2 (the liability cap) protects Gloumund here.** The cap limits "each Party's aggregate liability arising out of or relating to this Agreement" to 12 months of payments. Section 12.3 carves out only "Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1" from the cap. This means Gloumund's indemnification obligation under Section 11.1 is **uncapped**. However, Droupourt's own liability to Gloumund (e.g., under Section 11.2 for a defective tote) **is** capped at 12 months of payments. This asymmetry is a significant problem for Gloumund.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- What was the physical condition of the tote before the leak? Any visible damage, corrosion, or prior stress?
- Was the tote a new tote or a returned/reused tote? (Exhibit B provides for a $150 tote deposit refunded on return "in serviceable condition"—suggesting totes are recycled.)
- Did Gloumund inspect the tote upon receipt under Section 6.4 (10 business days)? Was any nonconformity noted?
- What was the lot number? Can we obtain the certificate of analysis?
- What did Gloumund tell NJDEP in its initial report? Was the tote identified as the source?
- Does Gloumund's pollution legal liability policy have a self-insured retention or deductible? What is the limit?
- Has Droupourt provided an itemized statement of its fees to date (as promised in Notice Letter ¶ 3.5)?

## Issue 3: Deletion of Comparative Responsibility – Section 11.4 (High Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Draft v2, Section 11.4 (Comparative Responsibility)
- Final Agreement – Section 11.4 does not exist

**What Changed:**

Draft v2 Section 11.4 provided: "If both Parties contribute to a Claim or Loss, each Party's obligation under this Section 11 is limited to its proportionate share of responsibility."

This section was deleted in its entirety from the final agreement.

**Why It Matters:**

In the Newark spill scenario, it is entirely possible that both parties contributed: Supplier may have provided a defective tote, and Gloumund may have stored it in a manner that accelerated the failure (e.g., improper stacking, exposure to heat, failure to maintain the warehouse floor). Under the draft, the indemnification would have been allocated proportionally. Under the final agreement, there is no allocation mechanism. Combined with the "regardless of the cause" language in Section 11.1, Gloumund could be held 100% responsible even if Supplier's packaging defect was the primary cause.

**Recommendation:**

This is a negotiating point if the parties enter a side agreement or amendment. In the immediate context of the Newark claim, the absence of Section 11.4 strengthens Gloumund's argument for a joint defense and cost-sharing arrangement based on the actual facts of the tote failure. We should not concede that the "regardless of the cause" language was intended to override Section 6.2's allocation of packaging responsibility.

## Issue 4: Narrowing of Supplier's Indemnification – Section 11.2 (High Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Final Agreement, Section 11.2
- Draft v2, Section 11.2

**What Changed:**

Draft v2 Section 11.2 required Supplier to indemnify for:
- (a) any defect in the Products;
- (b) failure to conform to Specifications or Section 8.1 warranties;
- (c) **any failure of packaging to meet Supplier's obligations under Section 6.2**;
- (d) **the negligence or willful misconduct of Supplier or its employees or agents**; and
- (e) IP infringement.

Final Agreement Section 11.2 requires Supplier to indemnify for:
- (a) any defect in the Products;
- (b) failure to conform to Specifications or Section 8.1 warranties; or
- (c) IP infringement.

Subsections (c) and (d) from the draft—packaging failure and Supplier negligence—were **deleted**.

**Why It Matters:**

If the tote that leaked was defective in its design, condition, or integrity (for which Supplier is responsible under Section 6.2), Gloumund's ability to seek indemnification from Supplier under Section 11.2 is now limited to arguing that the tote defect constitutes "a defect in the Products" (subsection (a)) or "a failure of the Products to conform to the Specifications" (subsection (b)). This is a narrower argument than the draft's express packaging indemnity. A court might hold that the tote is "packaging" rather than a "Product" (defined in Section 1.7 as "the polyaluminum chloride and ferric sulfate coagulants listed in Exhibit A"), in which case Section 11.2(a) and (b) may not reach it.

Furthermore, without the Supplier negligence indemnity (draft subsection (d)), Gloumund's only recourse for a negligently manufactured tote is a common-law tort claim, which is subject to the Section 12.2 cap (12 months of payments) and the Section 12.1 exclusion of consequential damages.

**Recommendation:**

- In the Newark matter, frame the tote defect as a "defect in the Products" within the meaning of Section 11.2(a). The Products definition (Section 1.7) refers to the coagulants "listed in Exhibit A," and Exhibit A includes packaging specifications (UN-rated totes, DOT/GHS labeling, clean and leak-tested condition). One can argue that the tote is an integral part of the "Product" as specified.
- Preserve a tort claim for negligent manufacture of the tote, but be aware it is subject to the Section 12.2 cap.
- In any renegotiation, seek restoration of the packaging and negligence indemnities.

## Issue 5: Limitation of Liability – Asymmetric Exclusions (High Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Final Agreement, Section 12.1, 12.2, 12.3
- Draft v2, Section 12.1, 12.2, 12.3

**What Changed:**

Draft v2 Section 12.3 excluded from the consequential-damages waiver and the cap:
- (a) either Party's indemnification obligations for third-party Claims under Section 11;
- (b) either Party's breach of Section 14 (confidentiality);
- (c) either Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct; and
- (d) Distributor's obligation to pay for Products delivered.

Final Agreement Section 12.3 excludes only:
- "Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1."

**Why It Matters:**

The practical effect is a dramatic asymmetry:

| Obligation | Subject to Cap? | Subject to Consequential Damages Waiver? |
|---|---|---|
| Gloumund's indemnity to Droupourt (Sec. 11.1) | **No** (uncapped) | **No** |
| Droupourt's indemnity to Gloumund (Sec. 11.2) | **Yes** (capped at 12 months of payments) | **Yes** |
| Either party's gross negligence or willful misconduct | **Yes** (capped) | **Yes** |
| Either party's confidentiality breach | **Yes** (capped) | **Yes** |

This means:
- If the Newark spill causes $10M in environmental cleanup and third-party claims, Gloumund's indemnification obligation to Droupourt is uncapped. Gloumund could owe Droupourt $10M+ in Losses.
- If Gloumund seeks to recover from Droupourt for the defective tote under Section 11.2, Droupourt's maximum exposure is capped at 12 months of payments (approximately $3.5M based on the annual minimum, potentially more if actual purchases exceeded the minimum).
- There is no carve-out for Droupourt's gross negligence or willful misconduct. Even if Droupourt knowingly shipped a defective tote, Gloumund's recovery is capped.

**Recommendation:**

- This is the single most financially dangerous structural feature of the final agreement for Gloumund. In any negotiation with Droupourt (whether to resolve the non-compete breach, the Newark claim, or both), Gloumund should demand restoration of the Draft v2 Section 12.3 exclusions, or at minimum a mutual carve-out for gross negligence and willful misconduct.
- In the Newark matter, ensure that Gloumund's insurance programs (CGL and pollution legal liability) are adequate to cover an uncapped indemnification obligation. If the $5M limits are insufficient for a worst-case NJDEP/Spill Act scenario, Gloumund needs to understand its gap.
- Calculate the actual 12-month payment amount to quantify the cap. If Gloumund has purchased less than the $3.5M annual minimum in the 12 months preceding the claim, the cap is lower than $3.5M.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- What was the actual invoiced purchase amount from Droupourt in the 12 months preceding the Newark claim (i.e., roughly March 2025 – March 2026)? This determines the Section 12.2 cap.
- What are the actual limits and SIRs on Gloumund's CGL and pollution legal liability policies?
- Does Gloumund's pollution legal liability policy cover first-party cleanup costs, or only third-party liability?

## Issue 6: Parent Guaranty – Exposure of Gloumund Holdings, Inc. (High Consequence)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Parent Guaranty, Sections 1.2, 2.1–2.4, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1, 9.1–9.2
- Final Agreement, Section 16.1

**Key Features:**

- **Scope (Sec. 1.2):** Covers "all present and future payment obligations of Distributor and its Affiliates for Products," including "purchase price, invoiced charges, tote deposits, taxes payable by the purchaser, late charges and other amounts payable for Products, whether arising under the Distribution Agreement, any Purchase Order or otherwise, and whether due, not yet due, absolute or contingent."
- **Payment, not collection (Sec. 2.2):** Droupourt need not proceed against Gloumund Water Solutions first. It can demand payment directly from Gloumund Holdings.
- **10-business-day payment (Sec. 2.3):** Once Droupourt makes a written demand identifying unpaid invoices, Gloumund Holdings must pay within 10 business days, "without set-off or counterclaim."
- **Cap (Sec. 3.1):** $2,000,000 aggregate.
- **Continuing (Sec. 2.4):** Applies to all obligations "whenever incurred, until it terminates under Section 9."
- **Survives termination (Sec. 9.1):** Remains in effect until the later of (a) expiration/termination of the Distribution Agreement and (b) payment in full of all Guaranteed Obligations incurred on or before that date.
- **Not released by amendment or insolvency (Sec. 4.1):** Changes to the agreement, extensions, or even Distributor's bankruptcy do not reduce the guaranty.
- **Termination trap (Sec. 9.2):** If Gloumund Holdings gives 90 days' notice to terminate the guaranty going forward, "Distributor shall be in breach of Section 16.1 of the Distribution Agreement unless a replacement guaranty reasonably acceptable to Supplier is delivered before the termination takes effect."

**Why It Matters:**

If Droupourt terminates the agreement for the Section 2.4 breach:
1. Gloumund must pay all amounts due for Products delivered (Section 10.3(a)).
2. Any unpaid invoices become Guaranteed Obligations.
3. Droupourt can demand payment from Gloumund Holdings directly, without first collecting from Gloumund Water Solutions.
4. Gloumund Holdings must pay within 10 business days of demand, without set-off or counterclaim—even if Gloumund has a valid counterclaim (e.g., for the defective tote, for the overbroad non-compete, for lost profits from supply disruption).
5. The $2M cap is meaningful but may not cover all exposure if the annual minimum ($3.5M) was met or exceeded.

Additionally, the "without set-off or counterclaim" language (Guaranty Sec. 2.3) means Gloumund Holdings cannot withhold payment on the ground that Droupourt owes Gloumund money under the Newark claim indemnification. The two obligations are treated as separate.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Quantify current payables.** Determine exactly what Gloumund Water Solutions owes Droupourt as of today (unpaid invoices, tote deposits, late charges). This is the maximum immediate exposure under the guaranty.
2. **Do not allow the guaranty to be triggered unnecessarily.** If the parties negotiate a resolution of the non-compete issue, ensure that any settlement expressly addresses the status of the guaranty and any accrued amounts.
3. **Be aware of the Section 9.2 trap.** If Gloumund Holdings considers terminating the guaranty (e.g., as part of a broader restructuring or exit from the Droupourt relationship), it cannot do so without either (a) providing a replacement guaranty acceptable to Droupourt or (b) accepting that Gloumund Water Solutions will be in breach of Section 16.1. This effectively locks the parent into the relationship.
4. **Gloumund Holdings should understand its $2M exposure is real and immediate** if Droupourt terminates and makes a demand. The parent's board or CFO should be briefed.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- What is the current outstanding balance owed by Gloumund Water Solutions to Droupourt? (Unpaid invoices, tote deposits not yet refunded, etc.)
- Has Gloumund Holdings, Inc. reviewed and approved the guaranty at the board level? Is there a board resolution authorizing the $2M commitment?
- Does Gloumund Holdings have other material obligations or covenants (e.g., debt agreements) that could be triggered by a $2M payment to Droupourt?

## Issue 10: Draft-to-Final Discrepancies – Potential for Mutual Mistake or Unconscionability Argument (Moderate Consequence, Long-Term Value)

**Documents and Sections:**
- All sections noted above (1.4, 2.4, 10.4, 11.1, 11.2, 11.4, 12.3)

**Why It Matters:**

The changes from Draft v2 to the final agreement are not minor drafting adjustments. They represent a fundamental shift in the risk allocation between the parties:
- The non-compete went from a narrow, territory-limited coagulant restriction to a continent-wide, all-water-chemicals restriction with an 18-month tail.
- The indemnification went from fault-based with a packaging carve-out and comparative responsibility to strict liability "regardless of cause" with no allocation mechanism.
- The liability cap exclusions went from a balanced set of mutual carve-outs to a one-way exclusion that benefits only Droupourt.

If there is evidence that these changes were made without Gloumund's informed consent (e.g., the changes were introduced in a late revision that was not flagged, or that Gloumund's counsel reviewed Draft v2 and the final was presented as "minor edits"), there may be a mutual mistake or lack-of-assent argument. Even if not actionable, the discrepancy is powerful negotiating leverage: it demonstrates that the final agreement is not the deal the parties were negotiating, and it supports a request to amend.

**Recommendation:**

- Gather all drafts, redlines, and correspondence between December 18, 2025 (Draft v2 date) and January 12, 2026 (Effective Date).
- Identify who made the changes and when. Was there a Draft v3? Were the changes presented in a redline?
- Interview Thomas R. Kellerby (President, signatory) and Eleanor V. Hastrup (EVP/CFO, guaranty signatory) about what they understood the final agreement to provide, particularly regarding the non-compete and indemnification.
- If the changes were not flagged, this supports both a negotiation position ("this is not what we agreed to") and, in the worst case, a legal defense to enforcement of the expanded provisions.
