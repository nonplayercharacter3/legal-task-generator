## SUMMARY

The final Distribution Agreement (January 12, 2026) departs materially from Draft v2 (December 18, 2025) in ways that significantly increase Gloumund Water Solutions' ("GWS") exposure. Droupourt's March 16, 2026 notice letter invokes two of those expanded provisions: (1) a breach allegation under the non-compete (Section 2.4) and (2) an indemnification tender under Section 11.1 for the Newark warehouse leak. Both claims would likely fail or be substantially weaker under the draft language. The following issues are listed in order of consequence.

## ISSUE 1: Section 11.1 Indemnification Expanded to "Regardless of the Cause" – Directly Implicates the Newark Claim

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 11.1 (final); Draft v2 § 11.1; Notice Letter ¶¶ 3.1–3.5.

**What changed:**

| Element | Draft v2 § 11.1 | Final § 11.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | "to the extent arising out of" (a) Distributor's **negligence or willful misconduct** in storage/handling/transport/resale after title passes; (b) breach of § 7.2; (c) extra-contractual warranties to customers | "arising out of or relating to the storage, handling, transport or resale of Products by or on behalf of Distributor, **regardless of the cause**" |
| Packaging carve-out | "Distributor has no obligation under this Section 11.1 to the extent a Claim or Loss is caused by a breach of Section 8.1 **or by the design, condition or integrity of packaging for which Supplier is responsible under Section 6.2**" | **Deleted** |

**Why it matters:** The Newark Claim involves a 330-gallon IBC tote of DC-FS-50 that leaked inside GWS's warehouse (Notice Letter ¶ 3.1). Section 6.2 of both versions states: "Supplier is responsible for the design, condition and integrity of all totes and drums it fills." If the tote failed due to a manufacturing or design defect, the draft would have shielded GWS from indemnifying Droupourt. The final agreement removes that shield and imposes indemnification "regardless of the cause." Droupourt has already tendered the defense and demanded GWS assume it within 10 business days (Notice Letter ¶ 3.4).

Additionally, because the final § 12.3 carves out only "Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1" from the liability cap (see Issue 3), GWS's indemnification exposure here is **uncapped**.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- Determine the cause of the tote leak immediately. Retain an independent packaging engineer to inspect the tote (which Droupourt has asked GWS to preserve – Notice Letter ¶ 3.6). If the tote was defective, GWS has a potential counterclaim under § 11.2(b) (failure to conform to Specifications; Exhibit A requires totes to be "supplied by Supplier in clean, leak-tested condition").
- Do **not** simply accept the tender as framed. Respond within the 10-business-day window to preserve rights, but make the acceptance expressly conditional on the outcome of the tote inspection and reserved as to any contribution or counterclaim against Droupourt.
- Notify GWS's pollution legal liability and CGL carriers immediately (Droupourt is requesting this under Notice Letter ¶ 4.1; the agreement requires it under § 13).
- Flag for the client: if the tote is found to be defective, GWS may owe Droupourt indemnification under § 11.1 while simultaneously having a (capped) claim against Droupourt under § 11.2. The net economics depend on the § 12.2 cap (see Issue 3).

**Facts needed from client:**
- What was the condition of the tote when it was received? Was the 10-business-day inspection under § 6.4 performed? Was any nonconformity noted?
- What does the warehouse video show? Did the tote rupture spontaneously, or was there external impact?
- What is the current status of the NJDEP investigation and the Passaic Valley Sewerage Commission inquiry? Has GWS been assigned primary responsibility by either agency?
- What are GWS's pollution legal liability policy limits and does the policy cover first-party cleanup costs, or only third-party claims?

## ISSUE 2: "Competing Products" Definition and Non-Competition Covenant Dramatically Broadened – Basis for Droupourt's Breach Allegation

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 1.4 and § 2.4 (final); Draft v2 § 1.4 and § 2.4; Notice Letter ¶¶ 2.1–2.5.

**What changed:**

| Element | Draft v2 | Final |
|---|---|---|
| § 1.4 "Competing Products" | "any **polyaluminum chloride or ferric sulfate coagulant** that is not supplied by Supplier" | "any **chemical used in the treatment of water or wastewater, whether or not it competes with the Products**, other than Products purchased from Supplier" |
| § 2.4 Geographic scope | "in the **Territory**" (NY, NJ, PA only) | "**anywhere in North America**" |
| § 2.4 Temporal scope | "During the Term" only | "During the Term **and for eighteen (18) months after** its expiration or termination" |
| § 2.4 Restricted activities | "purchase for resale, market, distribute or sell" | "**manufacture**, purchase for resale, market, **promote**, distribute or sell" |
| § 2.4 Affiliate restriction | None | "shall **cause its Affiliates not to**" |
| § 2.4 Express carve-out | "This Section 2.4 **does not restrict Distributor from selling any other water or wastewater treatment chemical**, in the Territory or elsewhere." | **Deleted** |

**Why it matters:** Droupourt alleges GWS purchased and resold sodium hypochlorite to Connecticut utilities (Notice Letter ¶ 2.2). Under the draft, this conduct would **not** have been a breach for two independent reasons: (a) sodium hypochlorite is not a PAC or ferric sulfate coagulant, so it is not a "Competing Product"; and (b) even if it were, Connecticut is outside the Territory and the draft contained an express carve-out for other water treatment chemicals. Under the final agreement, every one of those protections is gone. Sodium hypochlorite is a chemical used in water treatment; Connecticut is in North America; the restriction applies during the Term (which began January 12, 2026 and runs five years); and the Affiliate restriction could implicate Gloumund Holdings, Inc. or any sister entity.

Droupourt is demanding (within 30 days): cessation of all Competing Product activity, officer-signed confirmation, and a customer list with quantities and invoiced amounts (Notice Letter ¶ 2.4). Droupourt reserves the right to terminate for material breach if GWS does not cure (Notice Letter ¶ 2.5).

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- **Assess whether the sodium hypochlorite activity is real and ongoing.** If GWS (or an Affiliate) is indeed selling sodium hypochlorite in Connecticut, GWS is in breach under the final agreement and Droupourt has a valid termination trigger. The 30-day cure period is running from receipt of the letter (delivered March 16, 2026; cure deadline approximately April 15, 2026).
- If the activity is confirmed, the client must decide whether to (a) cease the activity and cure, preserving the distribution relationship; or (b) allow Droupourt to terminate, in which case GWS loses exclusivity in the Territory and faces the 18-month post-termination non-compete tail.
- If GWS cures, it should do so in writing with the officer-signed confirmation Droupourt requests, but should **expressly reserve rights** and note that the cure is made without admission that the covenant is enforceable to its full scope (particularly the 18-month post-termination tail and the North America geographic scope, which may be vulnerable to challenge under New York law as an unreasonable restraint, especially for an LLC that is not a professional services entity).
- **Investigate whether any Affiliate** (e.g., Gloumund Holdings, Inc. or other subsidiaries) is also selling water treatment chemicals. The "cause its Affiliates not to" language is broad.
- Consider whether the sodium hypochlorite business was a pre-existing line that the client did not appreciate would be captured by the final definition. If so, this is a critical lesson for future contract negotiations.

**Facts needed from client:**
- Is GWS (or any Affiliate) currently purchasing and reselling sodium hypochlorite or any other water/wastewater treatment chemical? If so, since when, in what volumes, to what customers, and in what states?
- Was this a pre-existing business line before the Distribution Agreement was signed?
- Does Gloumund Holdings, Inc. or any other Affiliate sell water treatment chemicals?
- What is the revenue significance of the sodium hypochlorite line relative to the Droupourt distribution business?
- Does the client believe the 18-month post-termination non-compete and North America geographic scope are enforceable under New York law? (We should obtain a brief on this.)

## ISSUE 3: Liability Cap Exclusions Narrowed – One-Sided Uncapped Exposure for GWS

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 12.3 (final); Draft v2 § 12.3.

**What changed:**

| Draft v2 § 12.3 – Excluded from cap/consequential damages bar: | Final § 12.3 – Excluded from cap/consequential damages bar: |
|---|---|
| (a) Either Party's indemnification obligations for third-party Claims under § 11 | **Only:** "Distributor's obligations under Section 11.1" |
| (b) Either Party's breach of § 14 (confidentiality) | |
| (c) Either Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct | |
| (d) Distributor's obligation to pay for Products delivered | |

**Why it matters:**

- **GWS's § 11.1 indemnification is uncapped.** In the Newark Claim scenario, GWS could face unlimited indemnification liability to Droupourt for environmental cleanup, third-party claims, fines, and attorneys' fees.
- **Droupourt's § 11.2 indemnification to GWS IS capped** at the trailing 12-month payment amount. If the tote was defective and GWS has a counterclaim against Droupourt, that claim is subject to the § 12.2 cap. Given the $3.5M annual minimum, the cap is likely in the low millions – but if GWS has only been purchasing for ~2 months (since January 12), the trailing 12-month payment amount could be significantly less.
- **GWS's payment obligation for delivered Products is now subject to the cap.** Under the draft, this was explicitly excluded. In a scenario where Droupourt delivers Products and GWS disputes quality, the draft would have let Droupourt recover the full payment amount uncapped. The final technically subjects it to the cap, but since the cap is measured by "amounts paid in the 12 months before the claim," this is somewhat circular and of limited practical benefit to GWS.
- **Gross negligence, fraud, and willful misconduct are no longer carved out.** If Droupourt's gross negligence caused the tote failure, GWS's claim against Droupourt is still capped.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- In any renegotiation or amendment, seek to restore the draft's bilateral exclusions from the cap, or at minimum add back the exclusion for "either Party's gross negligence, fraud or willful misconduct" and for "Supplier's indemnification obligations under Section 11.2."
- For the immediate Newark Claim, quantify what the § 12.2 cap would be: sum of all amounts GWS has actually paid Droupourt in the 12 months before the claim (i.e., since approximately March 2025, but the agreement only began January 12, 2026, so effectively only January–March 2026 payments). This number is likely well below $3.5M and represents the ceiling on any counterclaim GWS can bring against Droupourt.
- Ensure GWS's insurance program (CGL + pollution legal liability, each $5M per § 13.1) is adequate to cover the uncapped § 11.1 exposure.

**Facts needed from client:**
- What is the total amount GWS has paid Droupourt since January 12, 2026? (This determines the practical cap on any counterclaim.)
- What are GWS's actual insurance limits and deductibles for pollution legal liability? Does the policy cover first-party cleanup or only third-party liability?
- Is there any evidence of Droupourt gross negligence or willful misconduct regarding the tote?

## ISSUE 4: Supplier Indemnification Narrowed and Comparative Responsibility Eliminated

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 11.2 (final); Draft v2 § 11.2 and § 11.4.

**What changed:**

- **§ 11.2 (Supplier's indemnity to Distributor):** The draft included five triggers: (a) defect in Products; (b) failure to conform to Specifications/warranties; (c) **failure of packaging to meet § 6.2 obligations**; (d) **negligence or willful misconduct of Supplier**; (e) IP infringement. The final retains only (a), (b), and (e). The packaging and Supplier-negligence triggers are deleted.
- **§ 11.4 (Comparative Responsibility):** The draft provided: "If both Parties contribute to a Claim or Loss, each Party's obligation under this Section 11 is limited to its proportionate share of responsibility." This section is **entirely absent** from the final.

**Why it matters:** In the Newark Claim, if the tote leaked because of a manufacturing defect (Supplier's responsibility under § 6.2) combined with perhaps some storage issue on GWS's end, the draft would have allocated responsibility proportionately. The final gives Droupourt no contractual obligation to indemnify GWS for packaging failures or its own negligence, and no mechanism for comparative allocation. GWS must indemnify Droupourt under § 11.1 "regardless of the cause," while its own remedy against Droupourt is limited to § 11.2(a)–(b) (defect/failure to conform) and is subject to the cap (Issue 3).

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- In any renegotiation, restore § 11.2(c) and (d) and § 11.4.
- For the immediate Newark Claim, GWS's best contractual hook against Droupourt is § 11.2(b) – a failure of the tote to conform to the Specifications in Exhibit A ("supplied by Supplier in clean, leak-tested condition"). This is a fact-intensive argument that depends on the tote inspection.
- Even if GWS prevails on the § 11.2(b) claim, it is capped (Issue 3). The net position could be: GWS pays Droupourt uncapped under § 11.1, then recovers from Droupourt a capped amount under § 11.2(b). The gap is GWS's problem.

## ISSUE 5: Section 2.4 Survives Termination (Added to § 10.4)

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 10.4 (final); Draft v2 § 10.4.

**What changed:** The final adds Section 2.4 to the survival list: "Sections **2.4**, 5, 8, 11, 12, 14, 17 and 18 and this Section 10.4 survive expiration or termination." The draft did not include § 2.4.

**Why it matters:** Combined with the 18-month post-termination tail in § 2.4 itself, this means that even if Droupourt terminates the agreement for GWS's alleged non-compete breach, GWS remains bound by the non-compete for 18 months after termination. Droupourt expressly reserves this right in the notice letter (¶ 2.5). So the practical effect is: GWS loses the distribution relationship AND cannot sell competing products anywhere in North America for 18 months.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- This is a significant strategic consideration in deciding whether to cure the non-compete breach or allow termination. If GWS cures, the 18-month tail is deferred (it runs from expiration or termination, not from the cure date). If GWS does not cure and Droupourt terminates, the 18-month clock starts.
- Challenge the enforceability of the 18-month post-termination non-compete as applied to an LLC (not a sale-of-business context). New York courts generally enforce post-termination non-competes in the employment context with reasonable scope, but the application to a corporate distributor relationship is less settled. The "anywhere in North America" geographic scope and the breadth of "Competing Products" (any water treatment chemical) strengthen an overbreadth argument.

## ISSUE 6: Affiliate Restriction in § 2.4 Implicates Gloumund Holdings, Inc.

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 2.4 (final); Parent Guaranty Recital B.

**What changed:** The final adds "shall cause its Affiliates not to" to § 2.4. The draft had no Affiliate restriction.

**Why it matters:** Gloumund Holdings, Inc. is GWS's parent (Parent Guaranty Recital B: "Distributor is a wholly owned subsidiary of Guarantor"). If Holdings or any other Affiliate sells water treatment chemicals (which is plausible for a holding company with multiple operating subsidiaries), that activity could constitute a breach of § 2.4 through GWS's failure to "cause" the Affiliate to comply. This creates cross-entity exposure and could complicate the parent guaranty relationship.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- Conduct an immediate internal audit of all Gloumund entities' product lines to identify any that could be characterized as "chemicals used in the treatment of water or wastewater."
- If any Affiliate has such a product line, assess whether it can be restructured or whether GWS can demonstrate it is not in a position to "cause" the Affiliate to stop (i.e., the Affiliate is independently operated).
- Note that § 1.1 defines "Affiliate" using a >50% control test, so any entity in the Gloumund corporate family is likely captured.

**Facts needed from client:**
- What other entities does Gloumund Holdings, Inc. own? Do any of them sell water treatment chemicals?
- What is the corporate structure – are there any entities that are >50% owned by Holdings but operate independently?

## ISSUE 7: Parent Guaranty – Broad "Products" and "Guaranteed Obligations" Definitions

**Documents:** Parent Guaranty §§ 1.1, 1.2.

**Analysis:** The Guaranty defines "Products" more broadly than the Distribution Agreement: "all products sold by Supplier **or any of its Affiliates** to Distributor **or any of its Affiliates**, together with all related packaging, totes, drums, deposits, freight and services." This means if Droupourt's affiliates sell other chemical products to any Gloumund entity, those payment obligations are guaranteed. "Guaranteed Obligations" covers "all present and future payment obligations" that are "due, not yet due, absolute or contingent."

The maximum liability is $2,000,000 (Guaranty § 3.1), which is less than one year's minimum purchase ($3.5M under Exhibit B). The guaranty is a continuing guaranty (§ 2.4) and survives until the later of agreement termination and payment in full of all pre-termination obligations (§ 9.1).

**Why it matters:** If Droupourt terminates the agreement and accelerates all payment obligations, Gloumund Holdings is on the hook up to $2M. The broad "Products" definition could pull in unexpected obligations if Droupourt's affiliates have been selling to other Gloumund entities.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- Confirm whether any Droupourt affiliate has sold products to any Gloumund entity.
- Note that § 9.2 of the Guaranty allows Holdings to terminate as to future obligations with 90 days' notice, but doing so puts GWS in breach of § 16.1 of the Distribution Agreement unless a replacement guaranty is delivered. This is a trap: Holdings cannot simply walk away from the guaranty without triggering a default under the distribution agreement.

## ISSUE 8: Notice Letter – Timing and Procedural Demands

**Documents:** Notice Letter ¶¶ 2.4, 3.4, 3.6, 4.1.

**Analysis:** Droupourt's letter imposes multiple deadlines:
- 30 days to cure the non-compete breach (¶ 2.4) – approximately April 15, 2026.
- 10 business days to confirm acceptance of the Newark Claim tender and identify counsel/consultant (¶ 3.4) – approximately March 30, 2026.
- 10 business days to provide insurance certificates and carrier notices (¶ 4.1) – approximately March 30, 2026.
- 10 business days for Droupourt to inspect the tote and release area (¶ 3.6).

**Why it matters:** Missing the 10-business-day tender response could be characterized as a failure to cooperate under § 11.3(c), potentially prejudicing GWS's position. However, § 11.3(a) provides that a delay in notice relieves the indemnifying party only "to the extent the delay prejudices it" – this cuts in GWS's favor if Droupourt later argues GWS's delayed response prejudiced the defense.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- Respond to the tender within 10 business days. The response should: (a) confirm GWS is evaluating the claim; (b) identify counsel and environmental consultant (or state they will be identified within a few days); (c) expressly reserve all rights, including the right to dispute the scope of § 11.1, to assert counterclaims under § 11.2, and to challenge the tote's condition; (d) request the itemized statement of Droupourt's Losses (¶ 3.5).
- Do not agree to Droupourt's framing that the claim falls squarely within § 11.1 without reservation.
- Coordinate the non-compete cure response (30 days) with the tender response (10 days) so the client is not caught between two different deadlines with inconsistent positions.

## ISSUE 9: Tension Between § 6.2 (Supplier's Tote Responsibility) and § 11.1 (Distributor Indemnifies "Regardless of Cause")

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement §§ 6.2, 11.1 (final); Draft v2 §§ 6.2, 11.1.

**Analysis:** Section 6.2 (unchanged from draft) states: "Supplier is responsible for the design, condition and integrity of all totes and drums it fills." Section 11.1 (final) states GWS indemnifies Droupourt for claims "arising out of or relating to the storage, handling, transport or resale of Products by or on behalf of Distributor, regardless of the cause." These provisions are in direct tension: if a tote leaks because of a design defect (Supplier's responsibility under § 6.2), the final § 11.1 still requires GWS to indemnify Droupourt. The draft resolved this tension with the explicit packaging carve-out; the final does not.

**Why it matters:** This is the core of the Newark Claim dispute. GWS's position should be that § 6.2 allocates responsibility for tote integrity to Droupourt, and that reading § 11.1 to override § 6.2 would render § 6.2 meaningless. While the "regardless of the cause" language is broad, a court may apply the interpretive principle that specific provisions (§ 6.2) control over general ones (§ 11.1), or that the agreement should be read as a whole to give effect to both provisions.

**Recommendation / Course of Action:**

- Preserve this argument for the Newark Claim response. Do not concede that § 11.1 applies "regardless" of whether the tote was defective.
- If the tote inspection confirms a manufacturing or design defect, this becomes GWS's strongest argument to limit or negate its § 11.1 exposure.
- In any renegotiation, restore the draft's packaging carve-out to § 11.1.

## ISSUE 10: Section 1.2 – "Provincial" Added to Applicable Law Definition

**Documents:** Distribution Agreement § 1.2 (final); Draft v2 § 1.2.

**What changed:** The final adds "provincial" to the list of governmental levels: "all federal, state, **provincial** and local laws." The draft had only "federal, state and local."

**Why it matters:** Minor, but suggests the agreement may have been adapted from a template that contemplates Canadian operations. The Territory is exclusively US states (NY, NJ, PA). This does not create substantive exposure but indicates the agreement may not have been carefully tailored. It could theoretically be invoked by Droupourt to argue that Canadian provincial regulations (e.g., if products are shipped to a Canadian customer) are covered by "Applicable Law" and thus by the warranty in § 8.1(d) and the indemnification in § 11.1.

**Recommendation:** Low priority. Note for any future amendment.

## CONCLUSION

The final agreement represents a significant shift in risk allocation from the draft, almost entirely in Droupourt's favor. The two matters Droupourt has now invoked – the non-compete breach and the Newark Claim indemnification – are precisely the areas where the final language is most aggressive. GWS faces:

- **Uncapped indemnification exposure** for the Newark Claim, even if the tote was defective;
- **A valid (under the final text) breach allegation** for the sodium hypochlorite activity, with a 30-day cure deadline and termination risk;
- **An 18-month post-termination non-compete** covering all of North America and all water treatment chemicals, binding GWS and its Affiliates; and
- **A $2M parent guaranty** that is difficult to terminate without triggering a default.

The immediate priorities are: (1) respond to the tender within 10 business days with appropriate reservations; (2) inspect the tote and determine the cause of the leak; (3) assess the sodium hypochlorite activity and decide on a cure strategy before the 30-day deadline; and (4) notify insurers. Medium-term, the client should consider whether to seek an amendment restoring the draft's more balanced indemnification and non-compete language, using the current dispute as leverage.
