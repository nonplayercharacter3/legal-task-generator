## Summary

I reviewed the five documents in the matter folder. The most significant problems are (1) the Licensee indemnity in Section 9.2 of the Agreement is dramatically broader than what the Term Sheet contemplated and is uncapped; (2) Amendment No. 1 quietly restated Section 10.3 to remove Licensor's IP indemnity from the cap exceptions and to make the confidentiality exception one-way; and (3) Namaith's pending acquisition by Orvand Partners triggers the Change of Control provision in Section 14.2, and the Amendment expressly disclaims any consent. These three issues alone warrant immediate attention. I identify ten issues in total, ranked roughly by materiality.

## Issue 1: Licensee Indemnity (Section 9.2) Is Far Broader Than the Term Sheet and Is Uncapped

**Where:** Agreement § 9.2; compare Term Sheet Item 7; Agreement § 10.3(a) (as originally drafted).

**What the Term Sheet said:** "Licensee will indemnify Licensor against third-party claims. Licensee's indemnity is limited to claims from Licensee's negligence or modifications; subject to liability cap."

**What the Agreement actually says:** Section 9.2 requires Namaith to indemnify Lavas for "any and all third-party claims, losses, damages, liabilities, costs and expenses" arising out of or relating to any building or structure incorporating glass products fabricated using the Licensed Software. It expressly includes claims alleging that "the Licensed Software, any engineering output it generated or any tempering parameter it applied was defective or erroneous." There is no negligence limitation. There is no "modifications by Licensee" limitation. And Section 10.3(a) carves Licensee's Section 9.2 obligations out of both the liability cap and the consequential-damages exclusion, making them uncapped.

**Why it matters:** This inverts the risk allocation. If GlazeCore generates a bad tempering parameter due to a software defect, and a curtain wall fails, Namaith indemnifies Lavas for the building damage, personal injury, and repair costs—potentially in the tens or hundreds of millions—while Lavas's own exposure is capped at roughly $1.24M (12 months of fees). Namaith is effectively insuring Lavas's product liability. The Term Sheet made clear this was not the deal.

**Recommended fix:** Restore the Term Sheet formulation. Section 9.2 should be limited to claims arising from (a) Namaith's negligence or willful misconduct, or (b) modifications Namaith makes to the software. Remove Section 10.3(a) so the indemnity is subject to the cap. If Lavas resists a full cap, negotiate a super-cap (e.g., 2–3× annual fees) for the Licensee indemnity, and in any event carve out claims to the extent caused by Licensor's software defect or breach of warranty.

## Issue 2: Amendment No. 1 Restates Section 10.3 to Remove Licensor's IP Indemnity from the Cap Exceptions and Make the Confidentiality Exception One-Way

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 2 (restating Agreement § 10.3); compare Term Sheet Item 7; original Agreement § 10.3.

**Original § 10.3 exceptions (uncapped, no consequential-damages bar):**
- (a) Licensee's indemnity under § 9.2
- (b) **Licensor's indemnity under § 9.1**
- (c) **Either Party's** breach of § 7
- (d) Licensee's payment obligations under § 4

**Restated § 10.3 (after Amendment):**
- (a) Licensee's indemnity under § 9.2
- (b) **Licensee's** breach of § 7
- (c) Licensee's payment obligations under § 4

Two things changed:

1. **Licensor's IP indemnity (§ 9.1) is no longer excluded from the cap.** The Term Sheet expressly provided: "Licensor's IP infringement indemnity is excluded from the liability cap (uncapped)." Now it is capped at 12 months of fees (~$1.405M after the ThermaStress add-on). If a third party asserts a patent infringement claim against Namaith's use of GlazeCore, Lavas's indemnity obligation is capped at roughly $1.4M—potentially far less than the defense costs and exposure of a patent case.

2. **The confidentiality exception is now one-way.** Originally, *either* party's breach of § 7 was uncapped. Now only *Licensee's* breach is uncapped. If Lavas breaches Namaith's confidentiality (e.g., discloses Namaith's project data or tempering recipes to a competitor), Namaith's recovery is capped at 12 months of fees and consequential damages are excluded.

**Why it matters:** The Amendment's stated purpose is to add the ThermaStress module. The § 10.3 restatement is buried in § 2 and is not referenced in the Recitals as a negotiated trade. It materially worsens Namaith's position on two fronts. The Recitals do not mention any consideration for this change other than the module addition.

**Recommended fix:** Restore original § 10.3(b) (Licensor's § 9.1 indemnity uncapped) and original § 10.3(c) (mutual confidentiality breach uncapped). If Lavas insists on keeping the restatement for some commercial reason, at minimum restore the IP indemnity exception. Document the business rationale for any concession in a side letter or recital.

## Issue 3: Namaith's Pending Acquisition Triggers Section 14.2, and the Amendment Expressly Disclaims Consent

**Where:** Agreement § 14.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital D and § 5.

**Facts:** Amendment Recital D states that Namaith has informed Lavas that Namaith Holdings Corp. (Namaith's parent) has signed a definitive agreement to be acquired by Orvand Partners Fund III, L.P. Section 14.2 provides that any direct or indirect change of Control of Licensee "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee requiring Licensor's prior written consent." If the change occurs without consent, Licensor may terminate at any time within 120 days after it becomes aware.

Section 5 of the Amendment states: "Nothing in this Amendment is a waiver of, or a consent under, any provision of the Agreement."

**Why it matters:** When the Orvand acquisition closes, Namaith will have a change of Control (the definition in § 1.4 captures changes at the parent level). Lavas will have the right to terminate the entire software license within 120 days. Namaith would then have only 90 days to wind down (§ 13.4). For a facade fabricator with multi-month project pipelines, this is an existential operational risk at the worst possible time (integration period post-close).

The fact that Lavas acknowledged the pending deal in the Amendment but declined to consent is a red flag. It preserves Lavas's termination right while Namaith is in the middle of a transition.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Orvand deal closes, obtain Lavas's written consent under § 14.2, or negotiate a waiver. If Lavas wants leverage, consider offering a consent fee or a modest fee bump in exchange. Alternatively, negotiate a provision that the change of control to a financial sponsor does not trigger the termination right so long as Namaith remains the operating entity and the license is not transferred to the acquirer's other portfolio companies. Do not allow the acquisition to close without resolving this.

## Issue 4: Parent Guaranty Is Subject to the Same Liability Cap as the License Agreement

**Where:** Guaranty § 3.2; Agreement § 10.1.

**What it says:** "Guarantor's liability for any Guaranteed Obligation is no greater than Licensor's liability for that obligation under the License Agreement, including Section 10 of the License Agreement."

**Why it matters:** The practical value of a parent guaranty is to give Namaith a deeper pocket to look to if the subsidiary (Lavas Glazing Software, LLC) cannot pay. But if the guaranty is subject to the same $1.24M–$1.405M cap, it only helps in the insolvency scenario (i.e., if the LLC has no assets but the parent does). For any claim where the cap applies, Namaith cannot recover more from the parent than it could from the subsidiary. This is particularly problematic now that Amendment No. 1 has brought Licensor's IP indemnity within the cap (Issue 2 above)—the guaranty of that indemnity (Guaranty § 1.3(c)) is also capped.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a carve-out in Guaranty § 3.2 so that the cap does not apply to Guaranteed Obligations that are themselves uncapped under the License Agreement (i.e., conform the guaranty to the License Agreement's exceptions). At minimum, ensure that if § 10.3 is restored per Issue 2, the guaranty automatically benefits.

## Issue 5: Parent Guaranty Has a Different Governing Law and Forum from the License Agreement

**Where:** Guaranty § 12 (New York law; Manhattan/SDNY forum); Agreement § 15 (Delaware law; Wilmington forum).

**Why it matters:** If Namaith needs to enforce both the License Agreement and the Guaranty (e.g., Licensor breaches and is insolvent, so Namaith sues both the LLC in Delaware and the parent in New York), it faces two proceedings, two sets of counsel, two bodies of law, and potential for inconsistent outcomes. This increases cost and complexity and creates tactical disadvantages.

**Recommended fix:** Conform the Guaranty's governing law and forum to Delaware/Wilmington to match the License Agreement. If Lavas resists, at minimum confirm that the Guaranty can be enforced in the same action as the License Agreement (e.g., by adding a consent to jurisdiction in Delaware for Guaranty-related claims).

## Issue 6: IP Indemnity "Combination" Exclusion May Swallow the Integration Right

**Where:** Agreement § 9.1 (exclusion (ii)); Agreement § 2.2.

**What it says:** Section 2.2 expressly permits Namaith to "connect the Licensed Software to its tempering furnaces, cutting tables, inspection systems and other production equipment through the interfaces described in the Documentation." But Section 9.1 excludes from the IP indemnity any claim resulting from "combination of the Licensed Software with items not supplied or specified by Licensor, where the claim would not have arisen but for that combination."

**Why it matters:** Namaith's tempering furnaces and cutting tables are almost certainly not "supplied or specified by Licensor." If a patent claim arises that is triggered by the way GlazeCore interfaces with Namaith's specific furnace controllers, Lavas could argue the combination exclusion applies and deny the indemnity. This creates a gap between what Namaith is told it can do (§ 2.2) and what is actually covered by the IP indemnity (§ 9.1).

**Recommended fix:** Add language to § 9.1 clarifying that the combination exclusion does not apply to integrations performed in accordance with § 2.2 and the Documentation. Alternatively, require Lavas to specify in the Documentation which equipment interfaces are "specified by Licensor" for purposes of the exclusion.

## Issue 7: Limited Remedy for Software Performance Failures

**Where:** Agreement § 8.2; § 10.2.

**What it says:** If the software does not perform materially in accordance with the Documentation, Namaith's sole remedy is for Lavas to fix it within the Schedule B timeframes. If Lavas cannot fix it within 60 days, Namaith may terminate and get a pro rata refund of prepaid fees. There is no damages remedy for the period of non-performance. Combined with the consequential-damages exclusion (§ 10.2), Namaith cannot recover lost production revenue, missed delivery deadlines to its customers, or customer claims resulting from software downtime.

**Why it matters:** For a production-critical system integrated with tempering furnaces, even a multi-day Severity 1 outage could cost Namaith significant money in idle furnace time, expedited shipping to meet customer deadlines, or liquidated damages under its own customer contracts. The current remedy structure gives Namaith no compensation for that.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a service-credit mechanism in Schedule B (e.g., a percentage of the monthly fee credited for each hour of Severity 1 downtime beyond the 8-hour fix target). Alternatively, add a direct-damages carve-out for Licensor's breach of § 8.2 (performance warranty) so Namaith can at least recover out-of-pocket costs (e.g., rental of temporary equipment, overtime).

## Issue 8: 90-Day Post-Termination Wind-Down May Be Insufficient

**Where:** Agreement § 13.4(a).

**What it says:** On termination, Namaith may use the software for up to 90 days "solely to complete fabrication of orders then in progress and to export Licensee Data."

**Why it matters:** Architectural glass and curtain-wall projects often have fabrication cycles of 4–8 months from order to shipment, particularly for unitized facade systems. If the Agreement is terminated (e.g., by Lavas under § 14.2 following the Orvand acquisition, or for cause), 90 days may not be enough to complete all in-progress orders. Namaith could be forced to stop production mid-project, breaching its own customer delivery commitments.

**Recommended fix:** Extend the wind-down period to 180 days, or tie it to the completion of orders in progress as of the termination date (whichever is later). At minimum, get a commitment that the 90-day period runs from the effective date of termination (not the notice date) and that Lavas will not interfere with Namaith's use during that period.

## Issue 9: No Specific Data Security or Cybersecurity Obligations on Licensor

**Where:** Agreement generally; cf. § 7 (Confidentiality); § 11.1(b) (insurance includes "network security and privacy liability").

**What's missing:** The Agreement imposes no affirmative data security obligations on Lavas. There is no requirement to maintain specific security controls, to notify Namaith of a security breach within a defined period, to comply with any security standard, or to restrict data processing to specific locations. Section 7 imposes confidentiality obligations, but those are about non-disclosure, not about protecting data from hacking or loss.

**Why it matters:** GlazeCore is integrated with Namaith's production equipment and contains Namaith's project data, customer designs, and tempering recipes. A breach at Lavas (or at Lavas's cloud infrastructure, if any) could expose this data. The insurance certificate confirms Lavas carries network security and privacy liability coverage, which implies the parties contemplated cyber risk—but the Agreement itself gives Namaith no contractual right to demand security measures or prompt breach notification.

**Recommended fix:** Add a data security exhibit or section requiring Lavas to (a) maintain reasonable administrative, technical, and physical safeguards; (b) notify Namaith within 48–72 hours of discovering a security breach affecting Namaith data; (c) cooperate in breach investigation and remediation; and (d) not process Namaith data outside the United States without consent.

## Issue 10: Licensor Can Assign to an Affiliate Without Namaith's Consent—Potential Issue if Lavas Itself Is Acquired

**Where:** Agreement § 14.1; § 1.1 (Affiliate definition).

**What it says:** Section 14.1 prohibits assignment without consent, "except that Licensor may assign this Agreement to an Affiliate that assumes all of Licensor's obligations in writing." "Affiliate" means any entity under common Control (§ 1.1).

**Why it matters:** If Lavas Technologies Holdings (the parent) is acquired by a third party, that third party becomes the parent of both Holdings and, through it, Lavas Glazing Software. The acquirer and Lavas would be Affiliates. The acquirer could then assign the License Agreement to itself (or to another Affiliate) without Namaith's consent, effectively transferring the contract to an entity Namaith never chose. There is no Change of Control provision for Licensor (only for Licensee, § 14.2).

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision that a Change of Control of Licensor (or its parent) requires Namaith's consent, or at minimum gives Namaith a termination right. Alternatively, narrow the Affiliate assignment exception to exclude assignments to entities that are not in the same line of business or that are competitors of Namaith.
