## 1. Unauthorized Change of Control Triggers Material Breach (Not Curable)

**Location:** Supply Agreement § 14.2; Side Letter, Recital B and § 3.2

**Issue.** Section 14.2 provides that any Change of Control of Customer or its ultimate parent is deemed an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent under § 14.1. A Change of Control effected without consent "shall constitute a material breach of this Agreement, **not subject to cure** under Section 13.2(a)," and Supplier may terminate with immediate effect and demand the Minimum Purchase Shortfall within 30 days.

The Side Letter (Recital B) confirms that Pounirt Holdings, Inc. (Customer's parent) entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Kestrelbay Animal Health Partners, L.P. on **August 14, 2026**. Under § 1.4(a), the acquisition of more than 50% of voting securities by a Person or group acting in concert constitutes a Change of Control. The Kestrelbay merger almost certainly qualifies.

Critically, the Side Letter does **not** obtain Supplier's written consent to the Change of Control, does not waive § 14.2, and § 3.2 of the Side Letter expressly disclaims any implied waiver. Reigein's willingness to negotiate volume relief in the Side Letter is not, under the four corners of the documents, consent to the Change of Control.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** Reigein holds a live right to terminate the Agreement immediately and demand the full Minimum Purchase Shortfall (potentially up to $3.6M for the current contract year, plus remaining years if the term has renewed—see Issue 2). Because the breach is "not subject to cure," Pounirt cannot fix this by paying late or providing notice. Pounirt's entire supply relationship is exposed to unilateral termination by Reigein at any time within 90 days of when Reigein "becomes aware" of the Change of Control (which, per the Side Letter, was no later than August 14, 2026—meaning the 90-day window may have already closed by mid-November 2026, or is closing imminently).

**Recommendation.** Obtain Reigein's express written consent to the Change of Control, either by amending the Side Letter or by a separate consent letter referencing § 14.2. If Reigein refuses, negotiate a standstill agreement preserving the status quo pending resolution. Escalate internally: if Reigein terminates under § 14.2, Pounirt loses its exclusive supply source for hematology and chemistry reagents at all four sites with no cure right, and the Analyzers (titled to Reigein under Order Form § 5.2) must be removed within 45 days (Supply Agreement § 13.3).

## 2. Non-Renewal Notice Was Untimely—Pounirt Likely Locked into a Three-Year Renewal

**Location:** Order Form § 1.2; Side Letter § 2

**Issue.** Order Form § 1.2 provides that the Agreement renews automatically for successive three-year terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal "at least 180 days before expiration of the then-current term." The Initial Term expires **February 28, 2027**. Counting back 180 days from February 28, 2027 yields **September 1, 2026** as the deadline. Pounirt delivered its Non-Renewal Notice on **September 21, 2026**—20 days late.

The Side Letter (§ 2) records that Reigein "considers the Non-Renewal Notice untimely" and that "Customer has not accepted Supplier's position." Both parties reserve rights.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** If Reigein's reading is correct (and the math supports it), the Agreement auto-renewed on March 1, 2027 for a three-year term through February 28, 2030. Pounirt would be locked in for $3.6M/year in minimum purchases (subject to the 15% Integration Period reduction only for the current contract year), 100% reagent exclusivity, and the Change of Control restriction for three additional years. The next opportunity to exit would require notice by approximately September 1, 2029.

**Recommendation.** (a) Confirm the arithmetic with the client's business team—verify the exact expiration date and count back 180 days. (b) If the notice was indeed late, assess whether Reigein's conduct (negotiating the Side Letter, not asserting the renewal in the Side Letter, continuing performance) supports an argument of waiver or estoppel, though § 3.2 of the Side Letter and § 16.5 of the Supply Agreement make this difficult. (c) If Reigein asserts the renewal, negotiate a release from the renewal term as part of any Change of Control consent (see Issue 1)—Reigein has leverage here because it also holds the § 14.2 termination right. (d) Calendar the next non-renewal deadline (approximately September 1, 2029 if the renewal took effect) with a 24-month advance reminder.

## 3. Governing Law and Venue Conflict: Order Form Imposes Minnesota Law over Delaware

**Location:** Order Form § 9; Supply Agreement §§ 15.1–15.2, 16.4; DPA § 9.4

**Issue.** The Supply Agreement (§ 15.1) selects Delaware law and (§ 15.2) exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware. The DPA (§ 9.4) incorporates those same Delaware provisions by reference. However, Order Form § 9 selects **Minnesota law** and exclusive jurisdiction in **Hennepin County, Minnesota**.

Supply Agreement § 16.4 (Order of Precedence) states: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency among the documents comprising this Agreement, the documents shall control in the following order: **Order Form, then Supply Agreement, then Data Processing Addendum.**"

The Order Form's Minnesota provision therefore overrides both the Supply Agreement's Delaware provision and the DPA's incorporation of it.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** Pounirt is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Wilmington, Delaware. All four Customer Sites are in Delaware, Ohio, North Carolina, and Colorado. Having to litigate in Hennepin County, Minnesota under Minnesota law is materially less convenient and may produce different outcomes on issues such as the enforceability of the liability cap (§ 12.1), the Change of Control consent requirement (§ 14.2), or the exclusivity obligation (Order Form § 3.1). Minnesota's commercial law, while generally well-developed, is not the same body of law the parties presumably had in mind when the Supply Agreement was negotiated.

**Recommendation.** In any renegotiation (whether to resolve the Change of Control issue or the renewal dispute), seek to conform Order Form § 9 to the Supply Agreement's Delaware governing law and venue. If Reigein resists, at minimum confirm that the client's litigation budget and counsel retainers account for Minnesota-based disputes.

## 4. "Customer Sites" Expands to All Affiliate Facilities—Now and in the Future

**Location:** Order Form § 3.2; Supply Agreement § 1.5, Exhibit C

**Issue.** Supply Agreement § 1.5 and Exhibit C define "Customer Sites" as the four specific laboratory facilities listed. However, Order Form § 3.2 redefines "Customer Sites" for purposes of the Order Form (which controls under § 16.4) as "all laboratory facilities owned or operated by Customer or **any Affiliate, now or in the future**."

The exclusivity obligation in Order Form § 3.1 (100% of hematology and chemistry reagent requirements must be sourced from Reigein) applies to all "Customer Sites" as defined in § 3.2.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** If the Kestrelbay merger brings additional laboratory facilities under common control, those facilities become "Customer Sites" by operation of the definition. Pounirt would be contractually obligated to source 100% of their hematology and chemistry reagents from Reigein, even if those facilities currently use a different vendor, different analyzer platform, or different test menus. This could: (a) force costly reagent and analyzer conversions at acquired facilities; (b) create operational disruption during the integration period the Side Letter was designed to accommodate; and (c) increase Pounirt's effective reagent spend well beyond the $3.6M Minimum Purchase Commitment (which is a floor, not a ceiling).

**Recommendation.** In the Change of Control consent negotiation, seek to either (a) exclude Kestrelbay facilities from the "Customer Sites" definition for a transition period (e.g., 18–24 months post-closing), or (b) add an exception to Order Form § 3.1 for facilities that were not Customer Sites as of the Effective Date and that use analyzer platforms other than Reigein's. At minimum, model the reagent cost implications of bringing all Kestrelbay labs onto Reigein products.

## 5. DPA Indemnification Requires Pounirt to Cover Reigein's Own Fault

**Location:** DPA § 8.2

**Issue.** DPA § 8.2 requires Customer to indemnify Processor for "all Losses arising from the Customer Data or Customer's processing instructions, including Losses arising out of third-party claims and any fines or penalties imposed by a governmental authority, **regardless of whether such Losses also arise in part from Processor's acts or omissions**."

This means that if a data breach occurs due to a combination of Pounirt's instructions and Reigein's security failure, Pounirt must still indemnify Reigein for the full amount of third-party claims and regulatory fines. The "regardless of" language eliminates any comparative-fault offset.

Compare DPA § 8.1, where Processor's indemnity to Customer is limited to breaches of § 5 (Security) only—and does not extend to Processor's breach of other DPA obligations (e.g., unauthorized subprocessor use under § 6, failure to return data under § 7.4).

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** Pounirt processes sensitive data including animal owner PII, veterinary practice information, and test results across four sites. A Security Incident could trigger state breach notification obligations, regulatory fines, and third-party class actions. Under § 8.2, Pounirt's indemnity to Reigein is uncapped (the Supply Agreement's liability cap in § 12.1 does not apply to indemnification obligations per § 12.3) and extends to Reigein's own contributory negligence. This is a one-way risk allocation that is unusual and unfavorable.

**Recommendation.** In any amendment or renegotiation, strike the "regardless of whether such Losses also arise in part from Processor's acts or omissions" language and replace with a proportionate-fault standard. Alternatively, cap Customer's DPA indemnity at the same level as the general liability cap in Supply Agreement § 12.1. Also consider expanding § 8.1 so that Processor's indemnity covers all DPA breaches, not just § 5.

## 6. RD-H7 Launch Creates Exclusivity and Pricing Gaps

**Location:** Reigein Press Release (Sept. 9, 2026), §§ 2–3; Order Form § 3.1; Supply Agreement Exhibit A, § 1.13

**Issue.** Reigein launched the RD-H7 hematology analyzer on September 9, 2026, with a "new line of RD-H7 hematology reagent cartridges, controls and calibrators." The press release states that RD-H7 pricing and placement options "are set out in individual customer agreements." The RD-H7 reagents are **not** listed in Exhibit A (Products) or in the Order Form price schedule (§ 4). The "Products" definition in Supply Agreement § 1.13 is limited to products "listed in Exhibit A, as updated by the Parties in writing."

Meanwhile, Order Form § 3.1 requires Pounirt to source 100% of its hematology reagent requirements from Reigein.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** If Pounirt wants to upgrade to the RD-H7 (which the press release markets directly at "veterinary reference laboratories"—Pounirt's exact segment), it faces several problems: (a) the RD-H7 reagents are not "Products" under the Agreement, so the pricing protections of § 3.1 (firm first year, then capped at lesser of 3% or CPI) do not apply; (b) the service levels in Exhibit A (4-hour remote response, 1-business-day on-site for analyzer-down) are tied to the RD-H5 and RD-C900 and may not extend to the RD-H7; (c) the warranty in § 8.1 applies to "Products" as defined, so RD-H7 reagents may fall outside the warranty; and (d) if Pounirt installs RD-H7 units, the exclusivity clause means it cannot use any competitor's hematology reagents, but the RD-H7 reagents are governed by a separate "individual customer agreement" whose terms are unknown.

Additionally, the press release notes the RD-H7 has "reduced reagent consumption" compared to the RD-H5. If Pounirt upgrades, its actual reagent spend per test drops, which could make hitting the $3.6M Minimum Purchase Commitment harder.

**Recommendation.** Before committing to any RD-H7 deployment, require Reigein to either (a) add the RD-H7 reagents to Exhibit A and the Order Form price schedule with the same pricing protections, service levels, and warranty terms as the RD-H5, or (b) confirm in writing that the existing Agreement terms (warranty, service levels, pricing cap, indemnification) apply to RD-H7 products. Model the impact of reduced reagent consumption on the Minimum Purchase Commitment.

## 7. Insurance: TEO/Cyber Policy Is Claims-Made and May Lapse Before All DPA Obligations Are Satisfied

**Location:** Certificate of Insurance § 5.5; DPA §§ 7.4, 9.1

**Issue.** The Technology E&O and Cyber Liability policy (Insurer C, Bluewater Specialty) is written on a **claims-made** form with a policy period of July 1, 2026 to July 1, 2027. Claims-made policies cover only claims first made during the policy period (or during any extended reporting period).

The DPA (§ 9.1) remains in effect "for as long as Processor or any Subprocessor processes Customer Data, **including after termination or expiration** of the Supply Agreement." Section 7.4 requires data return and deletion within 30 days after termination, and § 9.2 provides that § 8 (Indemnification) survives. A data breach discovered after July 1, 2027—arising from processing that occurred during the policy period—would not be covered unless Reigein purchases a tail/extended reporting period or renews the claims-made policy.

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** If the Agreement terminates (or expires) and a Security Incident is discovered or a claim is made after the TEO/Cyber policy expires, Reigein's insurer may deny coverage, leaving Pounirt's indemnity claim under DPA § 8.1 unsecured. Given the sensitive nature of Customer Data (owner PII, veterinary records), the tail risk is real.

**Recommendation.** Require Reigein to (a) maintain the TEO/Cyber policy (or equivalent) for at least three years after the last day on which Customer Data is processed, or (b) purchase a six-year extended reporting period (tail) on the current claims-made policy at the time of any termination or expiration. Add this as a condition in any Change of Control consent or settlement.

## 8. Side Letter Provides Retroactive Relief Without Addressing the Change of Control

**Location:** Side Letter §§ 1.1, 3.2; Supply Agreement § 14.2

**Issue.** The Integration Period begins October 1, 2026, but the Side Letter is dated and signed October 5, 2026. The volume relief therefore applies retroactively for four days. More importantly, the Side Letter acknowledges the Kestrelbay merger in Recital B but does not address § 14.2, does not constitute consent to the Change of Control, and § 3.2 expressly disclaims waiver. The Side Letter's practical effect is to give Reigein a documented record that it knew of the Change of Control as of October 5, 2026, while preserving its right to terminate under § 14.2 (90-day window from awareness).

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** The Side Letter is a double-edged document. On one hand, it shows Reigein chose to negotiate commercial relief rather than terminate, which supports an argument of acquiescence. On the other hand, it is a signed, written acknowledgment by Pounirt that the Change of Control occurred, which Reigein could use to establish the "awareness" date for its 90-day termination window and to defeat any argument that Reigein did not know about the merger.

**Recommendation.** Do not treat the Side Letter as resolving the § 14.2 issue. Pursue express written consent (see Issue 1). If Reigein issues a termination notice under § 14.2, the Side Letter's acknowledgment of the merger will be cited against Pounirt.

## 9. Liability Cap May Be Inadequate for Pounirt's Exposure

**Location:** Supply Agreement §§ 12.1, 12.3

**Issue.** Section 12.1 caps each Party's aggregate liability at "the fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim." At a $3.6M annual Minimum Purchase Commitment, the cap is approximately $3.6M. Section 12.3 carves out indemnification obligations, confidentiality breaches, gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and Customer's payment obligations from the cap.

However, ordinary breach of contract claims (e.g., Reigein's failure to meet service levels under Exhibit A, failure to supply per § 3.4 of the Order Form, or breach of the exclusivity-related supplier commitments) are subject to the cap and to the exclusion of consequential damages in § 12.2 (lost profits, lost revenues).

**Why it matters to Pounirt.** If Reigein's analyzers go down and the 1-business-day on-site response is not met, Pounirt's laboratory operations halt. The revenue loss from missed testing turnaround could substantially exceed $3.6M, but § 12.2 bars recovery of lost profits or revenues. Pounirt's practical remedy is limited to the cost of replacement reagents or a credit, not the downstream business impact.

**Recommendation.** This is a structural limitation that is difficult to change mid-term, but in any renegotiation (Change of Control consent, renewal release, RD-H7 addition), seek to (a) raise the cap to 24 months of fees or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $7.2M), and (b) add a carve-out from § 12.2 for Supplier's failure to meet the service levels in Exhibit A (analyzer-down response times), since that is the scenario most likely to cause Pounirt significant operational harm.
