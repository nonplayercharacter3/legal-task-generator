## 1. Change of Control Consent Not Expressly Obtained

**Location:** Supply Agreement § 14.2; § 1.4 (definition of "Change of Control"); Side Letter, Recital B.

**Why it matters:** Side Letter Recital B discloses that Pounirt Holdings, Inc. (Customer's ultimate parent) signed a merger agreement with Kestrelbay Animal Health Partners, L.P. on August 14, 2026. Under § 1.4(b), a merger after which pre-transaction holders own less than 50% of the surviving entity is a Change of Control. Section 14.2 deems any Change of Control of Customer *or of its ultimate parent* to be an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent. Without consent, it is a material breach **not subject to cure** under § 13.2(a), and Supplier may (i) terminate immediately and (ii) demand the full Minimum Purchase Shortfall within 30 days.

The Side Letter, delivered October 5, 2026—more than seven weeks after the merger agreement—addresses only volume relief and the non-renewal dispute. It contains no express consent to the Change of Control, and § 3.2 of the Side Letter expressly disclaims any implied waiver. The fact that Reigein is willing to negotiate a Side Letter is not the same as written consent under § 14.1.

If the Kestrelbay transaction closes and Pounirt has not secured express consent, Reigein holds an immediate-termination right plus a shortfall claim. That would strand Pounirt's four laboratories without analyzers (title remains with Reigein per Order Form § 5.2) and without the volume-relief protection the Side Letter was meant to provide.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Kestrelbay merger closes, obtain Reigein's written consent to the Change of Control, either as a standalone consent letter or as an added recital/section in the Side Letter. The consent should state that the Change of Control will not constitute a breach and will not trigger the remedies in § 14.2. If Reigein conditions consent on other terms (e.g., a commitment to renew, or a higher MPC), flag those to the client for business judgment.

## 2. Non-Renewal Notice Appears Untimely by ~19 Days

**Location:** Order Form § 1.2; Side Letter § 2.

**Why it matters:** Order Form § 1.2 requires non-renewal notice "at least 180 days before expiration of the then-current term." The Initial Term expires February 28, 2027. Counting back 180 days lands on approximately September 1, 2026. Pounirt's notice was delivered September 21, 2026—roughly 19 days late. Reigein has already asserted the notice is untimely (Side Letter § 2), and the parties have reserved all rights.

If the notice is invalid, the Agreement auto-renews for a three-year term (through approximately February 2030) at the $3.6 million annual MPC (Order Form § 2.1). Pounirt would be locked in for three years with full exclusivity (Order Form § 3.1) and no further volume relief (the Side Letter relief expires February 28, 2027). Given that Pounirt is in the middle of a parent-level merger and integration, a forced three-year renewal at current volumes is a significant commercial exposure.

**Recommended fix:** Two tracks:

- *Negotiated resolution:* Use the Side Letter process to agree that the non-renewal notice is effective, or that the renewal term will be on modified terms (e.g., reduced MPC, narrowed exclusivity, or a one-year renewal instead of three). Reigein's willingness to grant volume relief suggests it wants to maintain the relationship; leverage that.
- *Fallback:* If Reigein holds firm on timeliness, Pounirt should evaluate whether it can cure the defect. The notice requirement is in the Order Form, not the Supply Agreement, and § 16.5 requires amendments in writing. There is no self-help cure for a late notice, but Pounirt could argue substantial compliance or equitable estoppel if Reigein accepted performance after the notice without objection for a meaningful period. This is a weaker position; the practical path is negotiation.

## 3. Governing Law and Venue Conflict Favors Reigein

**Location:** Order Form § 9 (Minnesota law; Hennepin County venue) vs. Supply Agreement § 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington venue) vs. DPA § 9.4 (incorporates Supply Agreement § 15 by reference, i.e., Delaware).

**Why it matters:** Supply Agreement § 16.4 sets the order of precedence: Order Form > Supply Agreement > DPA. Because the Order Form's § 9 directly conflicts with the Supply Agreement's § 15, the Order Form controls. The net effect is that **all** disputes—including those arising under the DPA—are governed by Minnesota law and must be litigated in Hennepin County, Minnesota. Pounirt is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Wilmington; Reigein is a Minnesota corporation headquartered in Minneapolis. This gives Reigein a home-forum advantage and subjects Pounirt to Minnesota substantive law, which may differ from Delaware law on issues like the enforceability of the Change of Control provision, the reasonableness of the exclusivity clause, or the interpretation of the non-renewal notice deadline.

**Recommended fix:** In any renegotiation (see Issues 1 and 2), ask Reigein to conform Order Form § 9 to the Supply Agreement's Delaware forum, or at minimum to agree that the non-renewal dispute and any Change of Control dispute will be heard in Delaware. If Reigein will not budge, Pounirt should ensure its outside counsel includes a Minnesota-licensed local counsel for any litigation.

## 4. "Customer Sites" Defined Broader Than Exhibit C

**Location:** Order Form § 3.2 vs. Supply Agreement § 1.5 and Exhibit C.

**Why it matters:** The Supply Agreement defines "Customer Sites" as the four facilities in Exhibit C (Wilmington, Columbus, Raleigh, Denver). But Order Form § 3.2 redefines "Customer Sites" for purposes of the exclusivity provision (§ 3.1) as "all laboratory facilities owned or operated by Customer or any Affiliate, **now or in the future**." Because the Order Form controls (precedence rule), the broader definition applies to exclusivity.

Post-merger, Kestrelbay Animal Health Partners' laboratories would likely be "Affiliate" facilities (common control under § 1.2). If so, 100% of Kestrelbay's hematology and chemistry reagent purchases would be subject to the exclusivity obligation, and the $3.6 million MPC would need to cover those volumes. This could dramatically increase Pounirt's effective purchase obligation without any corresponding increase in the price schedule or service levels.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a cap or carve-out: either limit "Customer Sites" for exclusivity purposes to the four Exhibit C facilities plus any new Pounirt-branded facilities opened during the term, or exclude Affiliate facilities acquired in the Kestrelbay transaction from the exclusivity obligation (at least for a transition period). Alternatively, if Kestrelbay labs are included, the MPC should be adjusted to reflect the combined volumes and the price schedule should be amended to cover the additional SKUs.

## 5. DPA Indemnification by Customer Is Overly Broad

**Location:** DPA § 8.2.

**Why it matters:** Section 8.2 requires Pounirt to indemnify Reigein for all Losses "arising from the Customer Data or Customer's processing instructions, including Losses arising out of third-party claims and any fines or penalties imposed by a governmental authority, **regardless of whether such Losses also arise in part from Processor's acts or omissions**." The bolded language means that even if Reigein's own security failure contributed to a breach, Pounirt still owes indemnification. This is materially worse than the Supply Agreement's mutual indemnity structure (§ 11.1–11.2), which allocates based on the party whose conduct caused the loss.

In practical terms: if Reigein's cloud host (Northfield Cloud Services, per DPA Annex 3) suffers a breach that exposes animal-owner PII, and a state AG imposes a fine, Pounirt could be on the hook for that fine even though the breach originated in Reigein's infrastructure.

**Recommended fix:** Amend DPA § 8.2 to delete the "regardless of whether" clause and replace it with a proportionate-fault standard: Customer's indemnity should not extend to Losses to the extent caused by Processor's breach of this DPA or its negligence. This aligns the DPA with the Supply Agreement's indemnity structure.

## 6. Cyber/Technology E&O Insurance Is Claims-Made and Not Contractually Required

**Location:** Certificate of Insurance § 5.5 (claims-made form, $5M per claim / $5M aggregate, policy period July 1, 2026 – July 1, 2027); Supply Agreement § 16.3 (requires only CGL).

**Why it matters:** Two problems:

*(a) No contractual requirement.* Supply Agreement § 16.3 mandates only commercial general liability insurance. It does not require Reigein to carry technology E&O or cyber liability coverage, even though Reigein processes Pounirt's Customer Data (including animal-owner PII and veterinary practice information) under the DPA. The DPA itself (§ 5) imposes security obligations but does not require a specific insurance backstop.

*(b) Claims-made gap.* The current cyber policy is claims-made with a policy period ending July 1, 2027. A data breach that occurs in June 2027 but is not discovered or reported until August 2027 would fall outside the policy period. Unless Reigein purchases a tail (extended reporting period), there is no coverage. Pounirt's DPA indemnity claim against Reigein (§ 8.1) would then be unsecured.

**Recommended fix:** (1) Amend Supply Agreement § 16.3 (or add a new section) to require Reigein to maintain technology E&O / cyber liability coverage of not less than $5M per claim, on an occurrence or claims-made-with-tail basis, for the duration of the Agreement plus a reasonable tail period. (2) Require Reigein to provide a certificate of insurance annually and to give 30 days' notice of any cancellation or material reduction. (3) Confirm that the current policy includes a tail or that Reigein will purchase one at its expense upon non-renewal or termination.

## 7. Exclusivity May Impede Adoption of the New RD-H7 Platform

**Location:** Order Form § 3.1; Supply Agreement § 1.13 (definition of "Products"); Press Release §§ 1–3.

**Why it matters:** Reigein launched the RD-H7 on September 9, 2026, with new reagent cartridges not listed in Exhibit A. The "Products" definition (§ 1.13) covers only items "listed in Exhibit A, as updated by the Parties in writing." The RD-H7 reagents are therefore not currently "Products" under the Agreement. However, Order Form § 3.1 requires Pounirt to source "100% of its hematology and chemistry reagent requirements" from Reigein during the term. If Pounirt installs the RD-H7, it must buy RD-H7 reagents from Reigein—but those reagents aren't priced in the Order Form, aren't covered by the warranty (§ 8.1), and aren't subject to the service levels in Exhibit A. Pounirt would be in an exclusivity obligation with no corresponding pricing, quality, or service protections for the new platform.

Conversely, if Pounirt wants to evaluate a competitor's hematology system (to avoid being locked into Reigein's roadmap), the exclusivity provision prohibits it.

**Recommended fix:** If Pounirt intends to adopt the RD-H7, negotiate an amendment to Exhibit A adding the RD-H7 SKUs with pricing, shelf-life, lead times, and service levels before any installation. If Pounirt wants to preserve optionality, negotiate a carve-out from § 3.1 for platforms Reigein has not yet made available under the Agreement, or for a defined evaluation period.

## 8. Liability Cap and Consequential Damages Exclusion Limit Pounirt's Recovery

**Location:** Supply Agreement §§ 12.1, 12.2, 12.3.

**Why it matters:** Section 12.1 caps each party's aggregate liability at "fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim"—roughly $3.6M for Reigein. Section 12.2 excludes all indirect, consequential, special, exemplary or punitive damages and lost profits. The carve-outs in § 12.3 are: indemnification obligations, breach of confidentiality, gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and Customer's payment obligation.

For Pounirt, the practical effect is: if a batch of reagents is defective and Pounirt must (a) re-test samples, (b) notify veterinary clients of delayed results, (c) lose contracts with practices that switch to a competitor's lab, or (d) face a regulatory inquiry, only the direct cost of repeat testing (§ 8.3) is recoverable. Lost revenue from veterinary clients, reputational harm, and regulatory fines are all "consequential" and excluded. The $3.6M cap further limits even the direct-recovery scenario.

**Recommended fix:** This is a standard commercial provision and difficult to move in a renegotiation. However, Pounirt should: (1) ensure § 8.3's "reasonable costs of repeat testing" is interpreted broadly (include labor, client communication, and any regulatory response costs directly caused by the nonconformity); (2) confirm that a product recall under § 9.2 is not subject to the cap (it should fall within the § 11.1 indemnity, which is carved out in § 12.3); and (3) in any amendment, seek to add "data breach response costs" to the § 12.3 carve-outs so that a Reigein-caused breach does not leave Pounirt bearing regulatory fines.

## 9. Analyzer Removal Cost Shifts to Pounirt If Termination Results from Customer Breach

**Location:** Supply Agreement § 13.3; Order Form § 5.2.

**Why it matters:** Section 13.3 provides that on termination, Reigein removes the Analyzers at its expense within 45 days—**except** where termination results from Customer's uncured breach, in which case removal is at Customer's expense. Title to the Analyzers remains with Reigein (Order Form § 5.2). If the Change of Control issue (Issue 1) is not resolved and Reigein terminates under § 14.2, that termination is by reason of Customer's breach. Pounirt would then bear the cost of removing 17 analyzers (per Order Form § 5.1: 3+3+2+2+2+2+2+3 = 19 units across four sites) from four facilities, on top of the Minimum Purchase Shortfall demand.

**Recommended fix:** This is largely a consequence of Issue 1. If Pounirt secures Change of Control consent, the termination trigger disappears. As a secondary protection, negotiate that even in a breach-based termination, Analyzer removal costs are shared or capped, given that the Analyzers are integral to Pounirt's laboratory operations and their sudden removal would cause operational disruption beyond the cost of the physical removal.

## 10. Side Letter Volume Relief Is Narrow in Scope and Duration

**Location:** Side Letter §§ 1.1–1.6.

**Why it matters:** The 15% MPC reduction applies only to the contract year ending February 28, 2027, and only for the portion of that year falling within the Integration Period (October 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027—five months). The relief does not extend into any renewal term. If the non-renewal notice is held invalid (Issue 2) and the Agreement renews for three years, Pounirt faces the full $3.6M MPC from March 1, 2027 onward with no integration relief, even though operational consolidation (workflow changes, IS migration, staff transitions) may not be complete by that date.

Additionally, § 1.4 credits purchases "without regard to how ordering volumes are allocated among Customer Sites." This is helpful if integration shifts volume between sites, but it does not address the scenario where total volumes drop below the (reduced) commitment because of the merger itself.

**Recommended fix:** If Pounirt expects integration effects to extend beyond February 28, 2027, negotiate an extension of the volume relief into the first renewal contract year (or at least the first 6–12 months of any renewal). Tie the extension to objectively verifiable milestones (e.g., completion of IS migration, stabilization of volumes at each site) rather than a fixed date, to avoid another dispute.
