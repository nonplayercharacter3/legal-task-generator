## 1. Change of Control Triggered by the Kestrelbay Merger – Potential Material Breach

**Location:** Supply Agreement § 14.2; Side Letter, Recital B.

**Why it matters:** Side Letter Recital B discloses that Pounirt's parent, Pounirt Holdings, Inc., entered into a merger with Kestrelbay Animal Health Partners, L.P. on August 14, 2026. Supply Agreement § 14.2 defines a Change of Control of Customer *or of its ultimate parent* as a deemed assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent. A merger in which the pre-transaction holders end up with less than 50% of the surviving entity falls squarely within § 1.4(b). If Kestrelbay (or its partners) acquired more than 50% of Pounirt Holdings' voting securities, this is a Change of Control of Pounirt's ultimate parent.

The consequences under § 14.2 are severe: the unconsented Change of Control is a material breach **not subject to cure** under § 13.2(a). Reigein may, within 90 days of becoming aware, (a) terminate immediately and (b) demand the Minimum Purchase Shortfall "as of the date of termination." Because the shortfall is calculated over *each remaining contract year of the then-current term* (§ 1.10), if the non-renewal dispute (Issue 2 below) resolves against Pounirt and the term extends three more years, the exposure could approach $10.8 million ($3.6M × 3 years) less actual purchases.

The Side Letter is silent on the Change of Control. Section 3.2 expressly disclaims implied waiver. There is no evidence in the folder that Reigein consented to the merger under § 14.1/14.2.

**Recommendation:** Immediately confirm with Pounirt's corporate development team whether Reigein provided written consent to the Kestrelbay merger before or after closing. If not, Pounirt should seek retroactive consent in writing before Reigein's 90-day window expires. Given the Side Letter already contemplates the merger (Recital B) and Reigein signed it without reserving its § 14.2 rights, there is a factual basis to argue Reigein acquiesced, but § 3.2 undercuts that. A short, targeted consent letter is the cleanest fix.

## 2. Untimely Non-Renewal Notice – Auto-Renewal for Three Additional Years

**Location:** Order Form § 1.2; Side Letter § 2.

**Why it matters:** Order Form § 1.2 requires non-renewal notice at least 180 days before expiration of the then-current term. The Initial Term expires February 28, 2027. Counting back 180 days yields **September 2, 2026**. Pounirt delivered its Non-Renewal Notice on **September 21, 2026** – 19 days late. Reigein has expressly disputed timeliness (Side Letter § 2).

If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement auto-renews for three years (through February 28, 2030). Pounirt would be locked into: (a) the $3.6M annual minimum purchase commitment; (b) 100% exclusivity for hematology and chemistry reagents (Order Form § 3.1); and (c) the Change of Control termination trigger (Issue 1) for the entire extended term.

The Side Letter § 2 states both parties "reserve all rights" and that the Side Letter is "not an admission." Reigein's willingness to grant volume relief (Side Letter § 1) while disputing the non-renewal suggests it is positioning to argue the Agreement continues past February 28, 2027.

**Recommendation:** Assess the strength of Pounirt's position. Arguments in Pounirt's favor: (a) the notice was only 19 days late and was delivered in good faith; (b) Reigein accepted the Side Letter (which references the non-renewal) without conditioning its signature on a renewal; (c) the Side Letter's Integration Period runs only to February 28, 2027, suggesting both parties treated that as the end of the term. Arguments against: the 180-day requirement is unambiguous, and § 16.5 of the Supply Agreement requires amendments in writing – a late notice is not an amendment. Pounirt should prepare to negotiate a mutual release or a short extension with a new non-renewal date, rather than litigate. If Pounirt is in the Kestrelbay integration and wants operational flexibility, a negotiated exit (even with a modest buyout) is preferable to a three-year lock-in.

## 3. Conflicting Governing Law and Venue Clauses

**Location:** Order Form § 9 (Minnesota law; Hennepin County, MN venue) vs. Supply Agreement § 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington, DE venue). DPA § 9.4 and Side Letter § 4.1 both incorporate the Supply Agreement's Delaware provisions.

**Why it matters:** Supply Agreement § 16.4 establishes the order of precedence: Order Form > Supply Agreement > DPA. For disputes "arising out of or relating to" the Order Form, Purchase Orders, or Products supplied under it, Minnesota law and venue control. For disputes under the Supply Agreement proper (e.g., Change of Control termination, confidentiality, indemnification), Delaware law and venue control. The DPA and Side Letter point to Delaware.

This creates a practical problem: a single factual dispute (e.g., a product defect that also implicates the minimum purchase commitment and the non-renewal question) could be characterized as belonging to either forum. Reigein, as a Minnesota corporation headquartered in Minneapolis, has a home-field advantage in Hennepin County. Pounirt is a Delaware corporation with its principal office in Wilmington. The split also means potentially inconsistent interpretations of overlapping terms (e.g., "material breach" under Minnesota vs. Delaware law).

**Recommendation:** In any renegotiation (whether to resolve the non-renewal dispute or to address the Change of Control), Pounirt should seek a unified governing law and venue clause. Delaware is the natural choice – it is Pounirt's state of incorporation, the Supply Agreement already designates it, and it is a neutral forum. At minimum, Pounirt should ensure that any dispute involving the Change of Control or termination is clearly within the Delaware forum, not Minnesota.

## 4. "Customer Sites" Defined More Broadly in Order Form Than in Supply Agreement

**Location:** Order Form § 3.2 ("all laboratory facilities owned or operated by Customer or any Affiliate, **now or in the future**") vs. Supply Agreement § 1.5 ("the four Customer laboratory facilities listed in Exhibit C").

**Why it matters:** Under the § 16.4 precedence rule, the Order Form's broader definition controls. This means:

- The 100% exclusivity obligation (Order Form § 3.1) extends to any new lab Pounirt or its Affiliates open during the term.
- The Minimum Purchase Commitment is credited by purchases at *any* Customer Site (Order Form § 2.2; Side Letter § 1.4 confirms this), so new sites increase the volume Pounirt must buy from Reigein to avoid a shortfall.
- In the Kestrelbay integration context, if the combined entity opens or consolidates labs, those facilities are automatically in-scope.

This is a significant commercial trap that was likely not the focus when the parties negotiated the four-site Exhibit C.

**Recommendation:** If Pounirt negotiates a renewal or extension, it should revert "Customer Sites" to the four named facilities in Exhibit C, or at least require a mutual written amendment to add new sites (which would also trigger a re-negotiation of the minimum purchase commitment). If Pounirt exits at the end of the Initial Term, this issue becomes moot.

## 5. DPA Customer Indemnification Sweeps in Processor's Own Negligence

**Location:** DPA § 8.2.

**Why it matters:** Pounirt indemnifies Reigein for Losses "arising from the Customer Data or Customer's processing instructions, including Losses arising out of third-party claims and any fines or penalties imposed by a governmental authority, **regardless of whether such Losses also arise in part from Processor's acts or omissions**."

The final clause is unusual and one-sided. It means that if a data breach occurs due to *both* a flaw in Pounirt's data (e.g., Pounirt transmitted data it had no right to) *and* Reigein's security failure (e.g., a misconfigured firewall), Pounirt still indemnifies Reigein for the full Losses, including regulatory fines. Compare DPA § 8.1, where Reigein indemnifies Pounirt only for breach of Section 5 (Security). The asymmetry is significant given that the Customer Data includes animal owner names, contact details, and veterinary practice information – data whose breach could trigger state notification obligations and consumer claims.

**Recommendation:** In any DPA amendment (which § 9.3 requires the parties to negotiate if data protection laws change), Pounirt should strike the "regardless of whether such Losses also arise in part from Processor's acts or omissions" language and replace it with a proportionate-fault allocation. At a minimum, Pounirt's indemnity should be reduced to the extent Losses result from Processor's breach of DPA § 5.

## 6. RD-H7 Analyzer and Reagents Not Covered by Current Agreement

**Location:** Press Release §§ 2–3; Order Form § 4 (Price Schedule); Supply Agreement Exhibit A (Products).

**Why it matters:** Reigein publicly launched the RD-H7 on September 9, 2026, with new reagent cartridges. The current Order Form price schedule lists only RD-H5 and RD-C900 SKUs. Exhibit A lists only RD-H5 and RD-C900 products. The RD-H7 reagents are not "Products" under the Agreement (§ 1.13). If Pounirt wants to upgrade to the RD-H7 (for the throughput and species coverage described in the press release), it has:

- No contractual right to purchase RD-H7 reagents under this Agreement.
- No pricing certainty (the press release says pricing is "set out in individual customer agreements").
- No guarantee that Reigein will place RD-H7 analyzers under the same reagent-rental model (Order Form § 5.1).
- Exclusivity (Order Form § 3.1) that would require Pounirt to source RD-H7 reagents from Reigein if it installs the RD-H7, but at what price is unknown.

The press release's statement that Reigein "will continue to manufacture, sell and support RD-H5" is helpful for continuity but is a public statement, not a contractual commitment.

**Recommendation:** If Pounirt is evaluating the RD-H7 as part of the Kestrelbay integration (higher throughput, broader species), it should open a commercial discussion with Reigein now to negotiate RD-H7 terms (pricing, placement, service levels) before the current term ends or renews. This is also leverage in the non-renewal negotiation: Pounirt can signal willingness to continue the relationship on new terms rather than walk away.

## 7. DPA Audit Right Is Effectively a Paper Review

**Location:** DPA § 7.3.

**Why it matters:** Pounirt's audit right is limited to once per contract year, on 30 days' notice. Critically, Reigein can satisfy the audit "by providing its most recent independent security assessment report … together with reasonable responses to Customer's written questions." There is no obligation to permit an actual on-site or remote technical audit of Reigein's systems. Given that the Customer Data includes animal owner PII and is processed through Reigein's cloud infrastructure (Annex 3: Northfield Cloud Services), Pounirt has no practical way to verify compliance with Annex 2 security measures between the annual paper review.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate a right to conduct (or commission) a targeted technical assessment at least once every two years, with reasonable scope limitations to address Reigein's operational concerns. Alternatively, require Reigein to maintain a recognized certification (e.g., SOC 2 Type II) and provide the report directly to Pounirt annually, with the right to ask follow-up questions.

## 8. Security Incident Notice Runs from "Confirmation," Not Discovery

**Location:** DPA § 5.3.

**Why it matters:** Reigein must notify Pounirt "without undue delay, and in any event within seventy-two (72) hours, **after confirming** a Security Incident." The definition of Security Incident (§ 1.6) requires "confirmed unauthorized access." This means the 72-hour clock does not start until Reigein has completed its internal investigation and confirmed the incident. In a complex breach involving cloud infrastructure, that confirmation could take days or weeks. Pounirt's own state breach-notification obligations (to animal owners, veterinary practices, and regulators) are typically triggered by *discovery* or *reason to believe* a breach occurred, not by the vendor's confirmation.

**Recommendation:** Amend DPA § 5.3 to require notice within 72 hours of *discovery* (or when Reigein has reasonable grounds to suspect) a Security Incident, with a supplemental notice within 72 hours of *confirmation*. This aligns Reigein's obligation with the timeline Pounirt actually faces under state breach laws.

## 9. Cyber / Technology E&O Insurance Is Claims-Made with No Tail Requirement

**Location:** Certificate of Insurance § 5.5; Supply Agreement § 16.3.

**Why it matters:** The Technology E&O and Cyber Liability policy (Bluewater Specialty, $5M per claim / $5M aggregate) is **claims-made** with a policy period of July 1, 2026 to July 1, 2027. A claims-made policy covers only claims first made during the policy period. If a data breach occurs in June 2027 but a third-party claim is not filed until August 2027, the current policy does not respond. The Supply Agreement (§ 16.3) requires only CGL insurance; it does not require Reigein to maintain cyber/TEO coverage, let alone a tail or extended reporting period.

Pounirt's exposure: if a breach of Customer Data occurs near the end of the policy period and claims emerge after renewal, Pounirt's DPA indemnity from Reigein (§ 8.1) may be only as good as Reigein's insurance.

**Recommendation:** Add to the Supply Agreement (or a side letter) a requirement that Reigein maintain Technology E&O / Cyber Liability coverage of not less than $5M on a claims-made basis, with a 60-day notice of non-renewal or material change, and a tail (extended reporting period) of at least 12 months following any non-renewal or cancellation. Request an updated certificate of insurance annually and upon any policy change.

## 10. Side Letter Integration Period Has a Four-Day Gap

**Location:** Side Letter §§ 1.1 and letter date.

**Why it matters:** The Integration Period begins October 1, 2026, but the Side Letter is dated and delivered October 5, 2026. For the four days from October 1 through October 4, the reduced commitment in § 1.2 technically does not apply. If any shortfall is calculated for the contract year ending February 28, 2027, the 15% reduction is "prorated to the portion of that contract year falling within the Integration Period" (§ 1.2). The Integration Period is defined as October 1 – February 28, so the proration should capture those four days. However, if Reigein argues the Side Letter was not effective until October 5 (the date of delivery and countersignature), the Integration Period for proration purposes might be October 5 – February 28 (146 days) rather than October 1 – February 28 (150 days). The difference is small (about 2.7% of the proration factor) but could amount to roughly $15,000 on a $3.6M commitment.

**Recommendation:** Confirm in writing (even by email) that both parties treat the Integration Period as running from October 1, 2026, as stated in § 1.1, regardless of the October 5 delivery date. This is a low-cost clarification that avoids a footnote dispute at year-end.

## 11. No Additional Insured Status on Reigein's Policies

**Location:** Certificate of Insurance § 1 (Notice); Supply Agreement § 16.3.

**Why it matters:** The certificate is issued to Pounirt as "certificate holder" only. Section 1 of the certificate expressly states it "confers no rights on the certificate holder" and is "a matter of information only." Pounirt is not listed as an additional insured on any of the policies. If a product defect causes bodily injury or property damage at a Pounirt lab, Pounirt's recovery depends on its own insurance and on Reigein's indemnity (§ 11.1), not on direct access to Reigein's CGL policy.

**Recommendation:** In the next amendment or renewal, require Reigein to add Pounirt (and its Affiliates) as additional insureds on the CGL and Products-Completed Operations policies, with a waiver of subrogation in Pounirt's favor. This gives Pounirt direct coverage under Reigein's policy for third-party claims arising from Reigein's products.

## 12. Exclusivity Scope and Supplier's Soft Supply Commitment

**Location:** Order Form §§ 3.1 and 3.4.

**Why it matters:** Pounirt is contractually obligated to source 100% of hematology and chemistry reagents from Reigein. In return, Reigein's supply commitment (§ 3.4) is only to maintain "sufficient inventory and manufacturing capacity" for forecast requirements, and if actual requirements exceed the forecast by more than 25%, Reigein need only use "commercially reasonable efforts." There is no liquidated damage, no service-level penalty, and no termination right for Pounirt if Reigein chronically under-delivers. The only remedy for late delivery is cancellation of the affected PO (§ 5.4 of the Supply Agreement), which credits the minimum purchase commitment but does not compensate Pounirt for lost testing revenue or the cost of emergency third-party sourcing.

During the Kestrelbay integration, if Pounirt's volumes shift unpredictably (as the Side Letter acknowledges), the 25% buffer in § 3.4 may be insufficient, and Reigein's "commercially reasonable efforts" standard provides little recourse.

**Recommendation:** If the relationship continues past the Initial Term, negotiate: (a) a higher threshold before the "commercially reasonable efforts" standard applies (e.g., 40%); (b) a service-level credit or penalty for repeated delivery failures; and (c) a carve-out from exclusivity for emergency sourcing if Reigein fails to deliver within a specified window, without requiring Pounirt to first cancel under § 5.4.
