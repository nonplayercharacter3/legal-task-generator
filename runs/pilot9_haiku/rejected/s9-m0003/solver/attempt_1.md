## 4. Issues, Ranked by Importance to the Client

### Issue 1: Late non-renewal notice; the Agreement most likely auto-renewed (Critical, prompt)

**Where:** MSA §§12.1, 12.2, 16.2, 16.3; Letter §1.

**Why it matters:** Section 12.2 requires notice "not more than one hundred twenty (120) days and not less than ninety (90) days" before the end of the term. The Letter was sent by overnight courier on July 20, which is effective on receipt (MSA §16.2). Receipt was therefore about July 21, roughly 41 days before expiration. The deadline was June 2, 2026. Absent a signed written amendment (MSA §16.3), the Agreement renews for 36 months at the then-current fee. Section 16.3 also says that failure or delay in exercising a right is not a waiver, which undercuts any argument that Gaiport gave up the timing requirement. Delaware law governs (MSA §16.1), and courts generally enforce unambiguous terms as written. I have not researched whether any exception applies.

**Fix:**
- Determine whether Gaiport has responded to the Letter, and whether it treats the Agreement as renewed.
- Choose a strategy: (a) accept renewal and negotiate an amendment (see Issues 11–13); or (b) negotiate a signed early-termination amendment with a fee and transition terms. Do not assume Gaiport will accept a unilateral exit.
- Do not send further correspondence that asserts an August 31 expiration until the strategy is settled.

**Facts needed:** Courier proof of delivery date; whether Gaiport has replied; any discussions with Gaiport about renewal or extension; who made the non-renewal decision and when.

### Issue 4: Patient data is largely outside the DPA; no BAA in the folder (Critical)

**Where:** DPA §§1.1, 1.3, 1.6, 2.1, 5.1–5.4, 6.1, 9; MSA §§1.4, 7.1, 7.3, 7.4; DPA Annex A.

**Why it matters:**
- DPA §1.1 defines Customer Personal Data as excluding Patient Health Information. The DPA's operative protections (encryption §5.2, penetration testing §5.3, SOC 2 access §5.4, 72-hour notice §6.1, export and deletion §9) apply to Customer Personal Data. Annex A limits that category to staff data.
- Patient records, radiographs, and billing data are "Customer Data" under MSA §1.4, which includes PHI and radiographic images. Their protection rests on MSA §7, which is general and cross-refers to the DPA (§§7.1, 7.3, 7.4). The DPA does not cover PHI, so for patient data the cross-references lead to a gap.
- Daitend appears to be a HIPAA covered entity (confirm). Gaiport hosts PHI and must be a business associate. A business associate contract is required under 45 C.F.R. §§ 164.502(e) and 164.504(e). None is in the folder. Without a BAA, both parties may be out of compliance, and Daitend has no contractual breach-notice commitment for PHI. HIPAA's default outer limit for a business associate to report to a covered entity is 60 days (45 C.F.R. § 164.410), far longer than the DPA's 72-hour clock, which does not reach PHI.
- The clinic names in the emails (Dover, Newark, Middletown, Smyrna, Milford, Seaford) suggest Delaware locations. State-law overlays should be checked.

**Fix:**
- Confirm whether a BAA exists. If not, execute one now.
- Amend the DPA so that "Customer Personal Data" expressly includes PHI, or add a separate PHI schedule with the same protections.
- Confirm that Gaiport's subcontractors (AWS, Okta, Zendesk, and any others) are bound by equivalent BAA terms.
- Confirm whether Zendesk support tickets may contain patient information.

**Facts needed:** Whether a BAA exists; whether Daitend has a HIPAA compliance program and privacy officer; whether support tickets include PHI; any state-law analysis done to date.

### Issue 5: Daitend indemnifies Gaiport for breaches Gaiport causes, uncapped (Critical)

**Where:** MSA §§9.1(a), 9.2, 10.1, 10.3(a).

**Why it matters:** Daitend must defend and indemnify Gaiport against third-party claims and "regulatory investigations, fines, penalties" arising from Customer Data, including unauthorized access "regardless of whether caused by Gaiport" (§9.1(a)). Gaiport's indemnity covers only U.S. intellectual property claims (§9.2). Section 10.3(a) removes the liability cap for indemnity obligations. The net effect is that Gaiport could cause a breach, and Daitend would pay the defense, settlements, and regulatory fines without limit.

**Fix:**
- Strike "regardless of whether caused by Gaiport" and limit §9.1(a) to incidents caused by Daitend or its Authorized Users.
- Add a Gaiport indemnity for security incidents caused by Gaiport or its subprocessors, covering notification, credit monitoring, forensics, call-center costs, and regulatory fines to the extent insurable or lawful.
- Make the data indemnities mutual and subject them to a defined super-cap rather than leaving them uncapped.

**Facts needed:** Any incidents or near-misses since March 2024; Gaiport's cyber insurance limits; Daitend's own cyber coverage and whether it responds to vendor-caused events.

### Issue 6: Gaiport's breach liability is capped and largely excluded (High)

**Where:** MSA §§10.1, 10.2, 10.3(c); DPA §§6.3, 10.1.

**Why it matters:** Gaiport's liability is capped at fees paid in the preceding 12 months, roughly $220,800 at full payment and less early in the term (§10.1). Consequential damages are excluded (§10.2). Breach-response costs such as notification, monitoring, and forensics may be characterized as consequential. Only gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct escape the cap (§10.3(c)), and "gross negligence" is undefined. DPA §6.3 requires cooperation but does not allocate costs.

**Fix:**
- Create a data-breach super-cap set at several multiples of annual fees, or at a figure tied to Gaiport's cyber coverage.
- State that breach-response costs are direct damages.
- Define gross negligence to include a material failure to maintain the Annex B controls.

**Facts needed:** Gaiport's cyber policy limits (MSA §14.1); Daitend's exposure estimate for a patient-record breach at 22 clinics.

### Issue 8: Gaiport may assign the Agreement and Customer Data without consent or notice (High)

**Where:** MSA §§1.10, 3.1, 15.3, 15.4, 15.5.

**Why it matters:** Gaiport may transfer the Agreement "together with... Customer Data" to any Permitted Assignee without consent or notice (§15.4). Permitted Assignees include any acquirer, merger partner, lender, investor, or their agents or trustees (§1.10). PHI could therefore pass to an acquirer with weaker security, or to a competitor, without Daitend knowing. Daitend, by contrast, needs Gaiport's consent to assign (§15.3).

**Fix:** Require prior written notice of any Gaiport assignment that includes Customer Data. Require consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, for assignment to competitors or to any acquirer that does not assume the DPA and BAA in full. Give Daitend a no-fee termination right and a right to data export upon any change of control. Allow Daitend to assign to an acquirer of its clinics.

**Facts needed:** Any known Gaiport financing or ownership change; Daitend's own sale or acquisition plans.

### Issue 9: Security incident definition and notice clock are narrow (High)

**Where:** DPA §§1.6, 6.1, 6.2, 6.3; MSA §7.3.

**Why it matters:** "Security Incident" requires a "confirmed breach" (DPA §1.6). The 72-hour clock runs from awareness of a Security Incident (§6.1), so suspected incidents may not start the clock at all. Notice content is limited to what is "known at the time" (§6.2). For PHI, MSA §7.3 refers to the DPA, which does not cover PHI (Issue 4).

**Fix:** Define Security Incident to include suspected incidents. Require initial notice within 24 to 72 hours of discovery of a suspected incident, with rolling updates. Expressly cover PHI, and require Gaiport to supply the information Daitend needs for a HIPAA breach risk assessment.

**Facts needed:** Gaiport's incident history; whether Daitend's incident response plan names Gaiport as a contact.

### Issue 20: Force majeure may excuse hosting outages (Low)

**Where:** MSA §§3.5, 16.4.
