## 1. Bottom Line

Besouth's position under the Meideis documents is weaker than the file's commercial framing suggests:

- The non-renewal window has closed, so the Supply Agreement will renew for three years, with exclusivity that Amendment No. 1 has widened.
- Besouth owes a firm Calloway commitment of **$1,229,925** at SOW prices (before upcharges, freight, and tax) that it cannot reduce without Meideis's consent, and the file has no owner-suspension relief.
- Besouth's warranty recovery is capped well below the owner-side cost of a failed glass unit on a 31-story tower.
- Holdings guarantees all of this without a cap, including Besouth's uncapped facade indemnity.
- The insurance protection depends on endorsements we have not seen.
- Every document in the file is an unsigned copy.
- The spandrel (SG-1) schedule does not work, and the Amendment's submittals are due **November 10, 2026**.

Section 2 lists the items to raise with the client now. Section 3 covers the remaining issues. Section 4 lists missing documents. Section 5 states scope and assumptions.

## 2. Items for the Client Now

### 2.1 The non-renewal window has closed; the Agreement will renew to about 2030

- **Where:** Supply Agreement §3.1 (Initial Term of four years from the April 1, 2023 Effective Date); §3.2 (automatic three-year renewals unless non-renewal notice is delivered at least 180 days before expiry, under §17.2); §5.1 (100% requirements exclusivity during the Term); Amendment §2.1 (redefines "Products").

Notice had to be delivered by about **October 3, 2026** (or October 2 on the earlier reading). The file contains no notice, and today is November 2. Absent a notice, the Agreement renews for a Renewal Term to about 2030, "on the terms of this Agreement then in effect, including Section 5" (§3.2). Amendment No. 1 was signed October 20, after the window, so its expanded scope carries into renewal.
- **Fix:**
  - Ask the client whether any §17.2 notice was sent or received.
  - If the client wants out of exclusivity, the only route is a written amendment signed by both parties (§17.3). Open that discussion now, while Besouth still has the Calloway relationship as leverage.
  - Calendar the next window (about October 2–3, 2029) and the 60-day price-notice date under §6.2 (January 31, 2027).

### 2.2 Every document in the file is unsigned

- **Where:** Signature blocks in the Supply Agreement (p. 4), Calloway SOW (p. 2), Amendment No. 1 (p. 2), and Parent Guaranty (p. 2). Each shows "By: ____."
- **Why it matters:** Under §1.11, a Statement of Work must be "executed by both Parties." Under §17.3, amendments must be in a writing signed by both. The Guaranty (§6.2) and Amendment (§4.2) both contain authority representations. We cannot confirm the Calloway commitment, the SG-1 terms, or Holdings' guaranty without executed copies. Meideis may argue the parties performed on the documents regardless, but we should not rely on that.

This includes (b) Committed Quantity amounts, (e) termination payments under §§15.3 and 16.2, and (f) amounts under §11.2 (Buyer's design, fabrication, and installation indemnity).

### 2.4 Calloway take-or-pay with no relief if the project stops; change-of-control trap

- **Where:**
  - §2.3 (Buyer "shall purchase the full Committed Quantity"; reduction requires Supplier's written consent).
  - Calloway SOW §3.1 (42,000 sq ft; IG-1 36,500 at $28.40 = $1,036,600; IG-2 5,500 at $35.15 = $193,325; total $1,229,925).
  - SOW §5.3 (deferral limited to 60 days, and it does not reduce the Committed Quantity).
  - §17.5 (force majeure does not excuse payment obligations and does not list owner-driven suspensions).
  - §1.3 and §1.5 (Change of Control includes a transfer of more than 50% of Holdings, and "Control" includes control "by contract or otherwise").
  - §16.2 (Change of Control requires Supplier's consent with no reasonableness standard, unlike §16.1; on a CoC without consent, Meideis may terminate and collect the Price of all remaining Committed Quantities within 30 days, "whether or not ... ordered, manufactured or delivered").
- **Why it matters:** If the owner, Calloway Street Partners, LP, suspends or cancels the work, Besouth still owes the full commitment, and the file has no owner-delay clause. Under §16.2, the termination payment is the full Price with no credit for avoided manufacturing costs, up to about $1.23 million less amounts already delivered and paid, and the Guaranty covers it. A lender's control rights or an investor's board appointment could trigger §1.3(b) without any sale.
- **Fix:**

  - Research whether the §16.2 payment is an unenforceable penalty under UCC §2-718, and how the seller remedies in §§2-706, 2-708, and 2-709 apply given §17.1's Article 2 choice. Do not rely on this until it is researched.

### 2.5 Insurance: certificate only, endorsements missing, and coverage for incorporated glass is uncertain

- **Where:**
  - Cover letter §1.1–1.2 (ACORD 25 and endorsements described as "enclosed"; the file contains only the letter).
  - §1.2(a) (CG 20 15 04 13 Vendors and CG 20 26 04 13 Designated Person endorsements).
  - §1.2(b) (umbrella follows the underlying additional-insured status).
  - §1.2(c) (30-day cancellation notice endorsements).
  - §3.2 (broker says the endorsements cover products "distributed to or incorporated by Besouth," including products-completed operations).
  - §4 (notice relates to cancellation only).
  - §5.1 (certificate is "for information only").
  - Supply Agreement §13.1–13.2.
- **Why it matters:** On their face, the limits and ratings in cover letter §2 appear to meet §13.1. The CGL provides $2M per occurrence, $4M aggregate, and $4M products-completed operations aggregate. The umbrella provides $5M. Auto is $1M CSL, and employer's liability is $1M. Carriers are rated A or better. But Besouth's protection depends on the endorsement wording, which we have not seen. Standard ISO vendors endorsements (CG 20 15) are written for resellers and commonly exclude products that have been used "as a part or ingredient of any other thing" by or for the vendor. Besouth incorporates the IGUs into curtain-wall units, so if the issued form contains that exclusion, Besouth's additional-insured coverage for installed-facade product claims may not respond, and the umbrella follows. Section 13.2 also requires 30 days' notice of "cancellation or material reduction," and the endorsements described cover cancellation only. The three-year post-Term tail in §13.1 is not satisfied by occurrence policies unless coverage is maintained. Besouth's own insurance is not in the file.
- **Fix:**
  - Obtain the ACORD 25, the full endorsement forms, policy schedules, and the umbrella additional-insured wording now. Request them under §13.2, which allows certificates on Buyer's reasonable request.
  - Ask Meideis to confirm in writing that Besouth's additional-insured status covers ongoing and completed operations for installed systems, using a form that does not exclude incorporated products.
  - Require direct notice to Besouth for cancellation, non-renewal, and material reduction, rather than relying on the broker.
  - Require Meideis to maintain completed-operations coverage or buy discontinued-products coverage for three years after the Term.
  - Confirm Besouth's own CGL, completed-operations, umbrella, and design or professional coverage. Confirm whether the general contractor and owner must be additional insureds under Meideis's program, which Besouth's prime subcontract likely requires (not verified).

## 3. Other Issues

**3.1 Warranty remedy is capped well below owner-side exposure.**
- **Where:** §10.3 (replacement plus labor reimbursement, capped at two times the Price of the unit); §12.1 (excludes consequential damages and lost profits); §12.2 (aggregate cap equal to the prior 12 months' Prices); Amendment §2.6 (new §10.5 applies §10.3 to spandrel).
- **Why:** Illustration only: a 40 sq ft IG-1 unit has a Price of about $1,136, so the labor cap is about $2,272. That is likely less than the cost of removing and reinstalling a unitized panel on a high-rise. Owner back-charges and delay costs fall under §12.1's exclusion. The §12.2 cap is small early in the program. The §10.3 cap is also ambiguous as to whether it covers replacement glass or only labor, and "Price of that unit" is undefined.
- **Fix:** Treat removal and reinstallation costs as direct damages. Raise or remove the per-unit cap, or set it per Project. Carve out owner back-charges from §12.1 for warranty claims. Clarify §10.3 and the "contract year" wording in §12.2.

**3.2 Indemnity is one-sided and gives Meideis control of defense.**
- **Where:** §11.2(a) (Buyer indemnifies for design, fabrication, and installation claims, with no carve-out for Supplier's negligence or non-defect breach); §11.1 (Supplier indemnifies only for bodily injury and property damage caused by a defect, IP, and negligence, and not for economic loss); §11.3 (indemnifying party controls the defense); §12.1 and §12.2 (indemnities uncapped).
- **Why:** Besouth may have to defend owner-facing claims that its prime contract assigns to it, but §11.3 would let Meideis control those claims.
- **Fix:** Add a carve-out in §11.2(a) for Supplier's negligence, breach, and Product defects. Give Besouth control of owner- and GC-facing claims, with consultation rights for Meideis. Consider a mutual cap on §11 indemnities.

**3.3 Warranty periods and the unseen glazing guidelines.**
- **Where:** §10.1(c) and §10.2 (ten years from manufacture, not installation); §10.1 (warranty conditioned on installation "in accordance with Supplier's published glazing guidelines"); Amendment §2.6 (five years for laminated spandrel, with a ten-foot visual standard for interlayer defects).
- **Why:** Besouth's exposure to the owner may run longer than Meideis's warranty periods. The guidelines are not in the file and Meideis can change them unilaterally. Failure to comply with them gives Meideis a warranty defense.
- **Fix:** Obtain the current guidelines and attach them to the Agreement as of a fixed date. Measure warranty periods from installation or acceptance. Align the spandrel warranty with the IGU warranty.

**3.4 Upcharges may apply on top of the Project Prices.**
- **Where:** Exhibit B (oversize upcharge of 15% for units over 50 sq ft; fully tempered upcharge of $2.40 per lite); Calloway SOW §4.2 (upcharges "apply at the rates stated there"); Amendment §2.4 (frit upcharge of $3.10 per lite); Amendment §3.4 (SG-1 price "fixed," but silent on upcharges).
- **Why:** If Calloway units exceed 50 sq ft, the oversize upcharge is up to about **$184,489** (15% of $1,229,925), before any SG-1 effect. Maximum spandrel lite size is 72 sq ft under Exhibit A-1 §3, so some SG-1 lites may trigger the upcharge. The frit option, if the Architect requires it, adds $3.10 per lite.
- **Fix:** Get the unit-size schedule from Besouth's takeoff. Negotiate a waiver of the oversize upcharge for Calloway, or confirm in writing that it does not apply. Confirm that no upcharge applies to SG-1 and price the frit option.

**3.5 The Committed Quantity needs reconciliation to Besouth's takeoff.**
- **Where:** Calloway SOW §3.1 (42,000 sq ft total); §3.2 (each unit rounded up to the whole square foot); §2.3 (mock-up units count toward the Committed Quantity); §5.4 (attic stock counts toward the Committed Quantity, but the quantity is not stated); Supply Agreement §2.3 (Buyer may increase only with Supplier's acceptance).
- **Why:** The 42,000 sq ft figure includes mock-ups and attic stock, and the file contains no takeoff. If it is short, Besouth must buy more, and Meideis may refuse. If Besouth buys elsewhere to cover the gap, it breaches §5.1. If it is too high, Besouth is exposed under §§2.3 and 16.2. The figure also appears low for a 31-story tower glazed on four elevations, but the footprint is not in the file, so this must be verified.
- **Fix:** Reconcile the quantity to the takeoff, including mock-up, attic stock, and rounding. Obtain written confirmation of the attic stock quantity. Seek a commitment from Meideis to accept conforming increase POs.

**3.6 Specification incorporation is vague and may be overridden.**
- **Where:** Calloway SOW §2.2(a) (Architect's specifications "to the extent applicable to insulating glass units"); §2.2(b)–(e) (U-factor, roller-wave, black spacers, and adhesion test "before the first production run"); Amendment §3.2 (adds Revision 2 for SG-1 only); §17.4 (SOW ranks last in precedence).
- **Why:** The "to the extent applicable" language lets Meideis argue that some specifications do not apply to its IGUs. Revision 2 may also change IGU requirements. Under §17.4, Exhibit A could override SOW project specifications where they conflict.
- **Fix:** List the applicable specification sections. Obtain Revision 2 and confirm whether it affects IGUs. State in the SOW that its project specifications control for the Calloway Project. Confirm the adhesion test has been delivered.

**3.7 Required submittals may not have been delivered.**
- **Where:** Calloway SOW §6.1 (submittals due within 15 Business Days after March 2, 2026, which is **March 23, 2026**); §2.2(e) (adhesion test report before the first production run); §6.2 (production records retained for the warranty period).
- **Why:** The file has no evidence of delivery. If the submittals were not delivered, Besouth may have a breach that starts the §15.1 cure period. That would require a proper notice.

**3.8 Notice mechanics control several deadlines.**
- **Where:** §17.2 (all notices by hand, courier, or certified mail, with an email copy, effective on receipt); §7.3 (invoice dispute notice within 20 days); §9.1 (inspection within 10 Business Days); §10.3 (warranty claim within 60 days of discovery); §15.1 (breach notice and cure periods); Guaranty §3.1 (ten Business Days' notice before a demand).
- **Why:** Email alone may not be effective. Section 7.3 and the Guaranty's §2.4 defense depend on a proper dispute notice.
- **Fix:** Adopt a notice protocol that uses §17.2 delivery and email. Docket each window, starting with the 20-day invoice-dispute window.

**3.9 Payment and interest exposure.**

- **Why:** Short cure periods and interest make non-payment costly, and Holdings guarantees the interest.
- **Fix:** Confirm payment status of all invoices. Set an internal payment calendar and dispute process.

**3.10 Confidentiality blocks Project submittals to the owner, GC, and Architect.**
- **Where:** §14.1 (disclosure limited to employees, advisors, lenders, and insurers); Calloway SOW §6.1 and Amendment §3.6 (submittals "for submission to the Architect"); Guaranty §8.2.

**3.11 Supply continuity is thin.**
- **Where:** §15.2 (insolvency termination; bankruptcy enforceability is a research point); §5.2 (alternative sourcing limited to the affected PO); §3.3 (open SOWs continue after expiration, which helps).

**3.12 Assignment and change-of-control consent standard.**

- **Why:** Besouth may need internal reorganizations or affiliate transfers. Meideis could refuse consent to a CoC for any reason.

**3.13 The Term definition is circular and the expiry date is ambiguous.**

**3.15 Price increases outside the SOW.**
