### C. Warranty, Remedies and Indemnity

**Issue 17. SG-1 warranty runs five years and omits opacifier and color durability.** *(High)*
- **Where:** Amend. § 2.6 (new SA § 10.5: five years; delamination, edge separation, and bubbles or discoloration visible at ten feet); Amend. § 2.6 (SA §§ 10.1(c) and 10.2 do not apply to spandrel); SOW § 2.2(f).
- **Why it matters:** The IG warranties run ten years (SA §§ 10.1(c), 10.2). The SG-1 warranty has no opacifier adhesion, color-stability, or edge-seal durability term. For a 31-story tower, Besouth's exposure after year five is unprotected.
- **Recommendation:** Seek a ten-year warranty for SG-1 at least covering opacifier adhesion and durability and color stability within a stated tolerance.

**Issue 18. Labor remedy cap is likely too low.** *(High)*
- **Where:** SA § 10.3 (replacement plus reasonable labor, capped per unit at two times the unit Price); Amend. § 2.6 (applies § 10.3 to SG-1).
- **Why it matters:** The cost to remove and reinstall unitized curtain-wall panels can be many times the glass price. For IG units priced at about $28–35 per sq. ft., a cap of twice the price may not cover reinstallation, and Besouth bears the difference. "Unit" is also undefined for spandrel lites.
- **Recommendation:** Raise the labor component to documented reasonable cost without a multiple, or define the "unit" as the installed panel, at least for Calloway.

**Issue 19. Consequential exclusion and liability cap.** *(High)*
- **Where:** SA § 12.1; SA § 12.2 (cap equal to Prices paid and payable "in the twelve months preceding the event").
- **Why it matters:** Early in Calloway deliveries, the trailing twelve-month amount may be small, so the cap may be low. Section 12.2 also refers to liability "in any contract year" but measures by the preceding twelve months, which is internally inconsistent.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate a Calloway-specific cap, for example a fixed dollar figure or a percentage of SOW value, with a carve-out for delay caused by nonconforming product. Fix the measurement inconsistency.

**Issue 20. Besouth's indemnity to Meideis is uncapped and lacks a Supplier-negligence carve-out.** *(High)*
- **Where:** SA § 11.2(a) (Besouth's design, fabrication, or installation, "except to the extent caused by a defect in a Product"); SA § 11.2(b); SA §§ 12.1, 12.2 (Section 11 carved out of the exclusion and the cap); Guaranty § 1.2(f) (Holdings guarantees § 11.2 amounts).
- **Why it matters:** Besouth indemnifies Meideis and its Affiliates for all third-party claims arising from Besouth's design, fabrication, or installation, including attorneys' fees, without a cap. The only carve-out is for a Product defect. There is no carve-out for Meideis's own negligence in § 11.2(a), unlike the symmetrical limit in § 11.1(c). Holdings guarantees this exposure (Issue 25).
- **Recommendation:** Limit § 11.2(a) to claims caused by Besouth's negligence or fault, on a proportionate basis, and add an express carve-out for Meideis's negligence. Subject § 11.2 to a cap. Confirm that Besouth's own insurance responds (Issue 37).

**Issue 21. Supplier indemnity trigger is undefined.** *(Medium)*
- **Where:** SA § 11.1(a) ("caused by a defect in a Product"); SA § 10.1(a)–(b).
- **Why it matters:** "Defect" is undefined. Meideis could argue that a nonconformity is not a defect, and that breakage caused by thermal stress or design could fall outside the indemnity.
- **Recommendation:** Define "defect" to include failure to meet the Specifications. Add an express indemnity for glass breakage and fall-out.

**Issue 22. No set-off right; dispute deadline is short.** *(High, process)*
- **Where:** SA § 7.2 (payment within 45 days; interest at 1% per month); SA § 7.3 (dispute notice within 20 days of receipt); Guaranty § 2.4 (Holdings may assert only defenses Buyer could assert) and § 3.3 (no deductions except under § 2.4).
- **Why it matters:** The SA has no set-off clause, so Besouth cannot net warranty credits or delay damages against invoices. Holdings can raise only defenses Besouth itself has, and Besouth has no express set-off right. If Besouth misses the 20-day dispute window, it must pay the invoice.
- **Recommendation:** Add an express set-off right for credits and warranty amounts. Calendar the 20-day dispute deadline for every invoice. Confirm whether any invoices are outstanding.

### F. Insurance

**Issue 33. The additional-insured endorsements may not fit Besouth's role.** *(Urgent, coverage)*
- **Where:** Cover Letter § 1.2(a) (CG 20 15 04 13, Additional Insured–Vendors; CG 20 26 04 13, Additional Insured–Designated Person or Organization); Cover Letter § 3.2 (states coverage for products "distributed to or incorporated by Besouth"); SA § 13.2 (Besouth to be named on the policies in § 13.1(a) and (b)).
- **Why it matters:** The endorsement wording is not in the folder, so the broker's description cannot be verified. My understanding, subject to checking the actual forms, is that vendor endorsements are commonly limited to products sold or distributed without alteration, and often exclude products used as a part of another product. A designated-person endorsement is commonly limited to liability caused in whole or in part by the named insured. If the enclosed forms follow those patterns, Besouth's coverage may not respond to the claims most likely to arise: incorporated glass in Besouth's facade, and claims under SA § 11.1. The broker's § 3.2 statement would then be inaccurate as applied to Besouth.
- **Recommendation:** Obtain the endorsements. Have the broker or coverage counsel confirm in writing that Besouth is an additional insured for products-completed operations involving incorporated IGUs and SG-1, including claims under SA § 11.1. If not, require a broader additional-insured form by amendment.
