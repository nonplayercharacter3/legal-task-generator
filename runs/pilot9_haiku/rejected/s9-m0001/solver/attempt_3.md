**Issue 2. Escrow sole-source clause conflicts with the SPA and cuts off Seller's direct liability**

- **Where:** Escrow §6.4 ("The Escrow Funds shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery for all claims of the Buyer Indemnitees under Article IX ... except claims for actual fraud"); Escrow §6.5 (if escrow is insufficient, Escrow Agent pays what is available and has no further obligation). Compare SPA §9.5 (escrow first, then Seller directly); SPA §9.6(b) (Fundamental Representations, Pre-Closing Taxes and fraud capped at the $42,000,000 Purchase Price); SPA §11.9 (Escrow Agreement controls "with respect to the release of and recovery from the Escrow Funds").
- **Why it matters:** Under Escrow §6.4, Paibon's recovery for every Article IX claim is capped at $4,200,000. That includes breaches of the Fundamental Representations (SPA §§4.1–4.3, 4.13, 4.18), Pre-Closing Taxes (SPA §9.2(c)), undisclosed Closing Indebtedness (SPA §9.2(d)), and Equipment Affiliate claims (SPA §9.2(e)). Seller will argue that SPA §11.9 makes the Escrow Agreement control. The result would be that the SPA's direct-recourse structure is effectively void. Escrow §6.4 is also broader than a "recovery from escrow" rule, since it speaks to "the source of recovery for all claims."
- **Recommendation:** Confirm the operative text of the executed Escrow Agreement (Email 1, item 2, says it was fully executed on July 28, 2026; the folder copy is unsigned). Amend Escrow §§6.4 and 6.5 to track SPA §9.5. Under Escrow §10.2, the amendment requires the signatures of Buyer, Seller and Ostrander. Alternatively, amend SPA §11.9 to state that the escrow controls only the timing and mechanics of release, not the existence or extent of Seller's liability.

**Issue 3. Larkfield change-of-control termination right is unprotected and allocated to Buyer**

- **Where:** SPA Schedule 4.10, item 1 (Larkfield is the Company's largest customer and may terminate on a change of control unless it gives prior written consent under §14.3 of its agreement); SPA §1.1 ("Required Consents," defined by reference to Schedule 7.2(d)); SPA Schedule 7.2(d) (lists only the landlord's consent); SPA §6.4 (Seller's efforts covenant covers only the Required Consents); SPA §4.4 (no-conflicts representation is qualified by "except as disclosed"); SPA §9.2(f) (no indemnity for failure to obtain consents disclosed on the Disclosure Schedules); SPA §4.17 (customer representation, limited to notice of an intent to terminate and given as of Closing); Emails 1 through 7 (Larkfield is not mentioned in any message).
- **Why it matters:** Larkfield can terminate because of this transaction. Its consent is not a closing condition (SPA §7.2(d)). Seller has no obligation to obtain it beyond commercially reasonable efforts for the listed consents only (SPA §6.4). Because the termination right is disclosed, the no-conflicts representation is not breached (SPA §4.4), and SPA §9.2(f) bars recovery for failure to obtain a disclosed consent. If Larkfield terminates, Paibon bears the entire loss. The folder contains no revenue figures for Larkfield, and its agreement is not in the folder. The undefined term "Material Adverse Effect" (Issue 13) also makes the fallback closing condition in SPA §7.2(c) uncertain.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Obtain the Larkfield agreement (including §14.3) and Larkfield's revenue contribution for FY2025 and YTD 2026.
  - If not yet closed: add a written Larkfield consent or waiver of §14.3 to Schedule 7.2(d). Any Buyer waiver should be expressly conditioned on a specific indemnity.
  - If closed, or if Larkfield will not consent: negotiate a special indemnity or purchase price holdback for loss of the Larkfield relationship within a defined period. Carve it out of SPA §9.2(f), the $315,000 threshold in SPA §9.6(c), and the cap in SPA §9.6(a).

**Issue 4. Environmental: 180-day survival is shorter than the Phase II timeline, and there is no special indemnity**

- **Where:** SPA §4.14 (wastewater and pretreatment compliance; no release requiring investigation or remediation); SPA §9.1(b) (§4.14 survives 180 days after Closing); SPA §6.7 (Buyer shall complete the Phase II within 270 days after Closing and shall deliver the report to Seller promptly); SPA §9.2 (no separate environmental indemnity); SPA §9.6(a) and (c) (general cap of $4,200,000 and $315,000 threshold, shared with all other representation claims); Escrow §5.1 (escrow release at 18 months).
- **Why it matters:**
  - The Phase II completion deadline (270 days) falls after the representation survival period (180 days) (computed: Feb. 10, 2027 versus May 11, 2027). Under SPA §9.1, a claim must be noticed in writing before the survival period expires. Paibon must therefore give notice before it knows the results.
  - Under SPA §4.14(c), a breach requires contamination that "requires investigation or remediation under Environmental Law." Seller can argue that no regulator has required anything, so no breach has occurred.
  - Environmental losses fall under the same basket and cap as all other representation claims. A Phase II finding could be offset by unrelated items or exhaust the cap.
  - SPA §6.7 makes the Phase II mandatory once Buyer elects to conduct it. Delivering the report to Seller "promptly" also gives Seller the findings and may affect privilege.
  - The folder contains no Phase I, no permits, and no environmental allocation under the Stockton Plant Lease.
- **Recommendation:**
  - If closed: deliver a protective written claim notice under SPA §9.1 and Escrow §6.1 before February 10, 2027, describing the Stockton Plant environmental concerns with a good-faith estimate (Escrow §6.3(d) permits later updates). Commission the Phase II now.
  - Amend the SPA to extend §4.14 survival to at least three years, or to 60 days after delivery of the final Phase II report.
  - Add a special environmental indemnity from the first dollar, outside the threshold and the general cap, backed by a separate environmental escrow sized from a Phase I.
  - Define "investigation or remediation" to include investigation required by any Governmental Authority or reasonably necessary to comply with Environmental Law.
  - Obtain the Stockton Plant Lease, the wastewater permits, and any historical reports. Set a privilege protocol for delivering the Phase II report under SPA §6.7.

### Tier 3: Medium (drafting, completeness, and follow-up)

**Issue 16. Escrow Agreement drafting defects and economics**

- **Where and issue:**
  - Escrow §2.1 defines "Final Order" as described in "Section 10.4," but §10.4 is the governing-law and jurisdiction clause and describes no order. This affects Escrow §§5.4 and 6.3(c).
  - Escrow §8.2 permits the Escrow Agent to deduct unpaid fees from the Escrow Funds after 60 days. This reduces indemnity security and is in tension with the equal split in SPA §11.2.
  - Escrow §9.1 treats Buyer as the tax owner of the Escrow Funds, so Buyer pays tax on earnings the Seller ultimately receives. The cost depends on the yield and should be quantified.
  - Escrow §4.1 invests in the "Ostrander Government Obligations Money Market Fund," an affiliate of the Escrow Agent. Affiliate fees should be disclosed.
  - Escrow §10.4 selects New York law and Manhattan courts. SPA §§11.6–11.7 select Delaware law and the Delaware Court of Chancery. Disputes over escrow release could be litigated in two forums.
  - Escrow §6.6 pays claims first-in-time, so an early small claim can deplete the fund ahead of a larger one.
  - Escrow §7.7 makes Buyer and Seller jointly and severally liable to the agent, with a 50/50 split as between them.
  - Escrow Exhibit B (fees) and §10.1 (notice addresses) are blank.
- **Recommendation:** Correct the "Final Order" cross-reference. Prohibit fee deduction from escrow or require Seller to bear fees. Allocate tax on escrow earnings to Seller or require a gross-up. Require disclosure of affiliate fees. Align the forum or add a carve-out for escrow disputes. Consider pro rata payment. Complete the exhibits.
