## 3. Detailed Issues and Recommended Fixes

### Issue 1: Wire and escrow call-back controls are not in place, and Seller is initiating verification (Critical, immediate)

**Where:**
- Escrow §5.5 requires each wire, and any change to wire instructions, to be confirmed by call-back "with a person listed on Exhibit A for the relevant party."
- Escrow Exhibit A lists four authorized representatives, and every name, title, telephone number and specimen signature is "[●]" or blank.
- Escrow Exhibit B (fees) is also blank.
- Thread msg. 2 (Ebright, Aug. 3): Seller will send "the Exhibit A call-back contacts for Seller, if Ostrander does not already have them." Nothing in the file shows that they were sent, and no Buyer contacts appear anywhere.
- Thread msg. 5 (Whitcombe, Aug. 11): wire instructions "will be verified by telephone call-back to the contacts confirmed separately."
- Thread msg. 6 (Ebright, Aug. 12): "Elena [Oduya, Seller] will confirm Zanoul's wire details by phone with Theo [Lindqvist, Paibon] this afternoon."

**Why it matters:** The funds flow directs about $37.8 million to Seller plus $4.2 million to the Escrow Agent, along with the payoff amounts (Thread msg. 5; SPA §2.2). Without completed Exhibit A contacts, neither Ostrander's call-back under Escrow §5.5 nor Buyer's own verification has a reliable reference point. The msg. 6 arrangement has the Seller side initiating the call to Buyer's side. A callback is only effective if Buyer calls a number it already trusts, independent of the email chain, to confirm the account. Seller-originated confirmation is a known wire-fraud pattern. Losses can be irreversible once funds leave.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Before any wire, complete Exhibit A for both parties (names, titles, verified telephone numbers, specimen signatures) and confirm Ostrander has them.
2. Have Buyer, not Seller, initiate verification. Call a number from Exhibit A or independently sourced, not one from the email chain, and confirm Seller's wire instructions with a named Seller representative.
3. Apply the same process to the payoff lender's wire instructions.
4. Complete Exhibit B so Ostrander's fees are fixed before closing.
5. Document each call (date, time, person, number used). Treat any change to instructions as a new call-back event.
6. If the deal closed, confirm that the wires went to verified accounts. If not, contact the bank and Ostrander immediately about a recall.

**Open points:** Whether Ostrander already holds any call-back contacts; whether the msg. 6 call with Mr. Lindqvist took place and on which number; whether Seller's instructions were in writing and from which address.

### Issue 3: Escrow "sole source" clause conflicts with the SPA and limits recovery to $4.2 million (Critical)

**Where:**
- Escrow §6.4: The Escrow Funds "shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery for all claims of the Buyer Indemnitees under Article IX... except claims for actual fraud."
- SPA §9.5: Losses under §9.2 are paid first from escrow and then "directly by Seller" to the extent escrow is insufficient or released.
- SPA §9.6(a): Seller's liability under §9.2(a) is capped at the Indemnity Escrow Amount ($4.2 million).
- SPA §9.6(b): Fundamental Representations, Pre-Closing Taxes and fraud are capped at the Purchase Price, not at escrow.
- SPA §11.9: The Escrow Agreement controls "with respect to the release of and recovery from the Escrow Funds."
- SPA §9.1: Fundamental Representations survive until 60 days after the statute of limitations.
- Escrow §5.1: Escrow Funds are released to Seller 18 months after closing, less pending claims.

**Why it matters:**
- Escrow §6.4 is broader than §9.5 and §9.6. Read literally, it limits all Article IX recovery to $4.2 million, which would also eliminate the Purchase Price cap for Fundamental Representations and Pre-Closing Taxes. Seller will argue that §11.9 supports this reading, since the Escrow Agreement controls "recovery." Buyer's position under the SPA is that §11.9 concerns only recovery from the escrow account.
- Escrow §6.4 carves out only "actual fraud." SPA §9.6(b) caps fraud at the Purchase Price, which is inconsistent with the fraud carve-outs in §9.7 and Escrow §6.4. It is also unusual for a fraud claim to be capped at all.
- Escrow release at 18 months leaves no security for claims that can be brought later: Fundamental Representations and Pre-Closing Taxes (survival to the statute of limitations plus 60 days), and §9.2(c)–(e) indemnities. The file contains no credit support. Seller is a holding company that may distribute proceeds after closing.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Amend Escrow §6.4 to read "subject to Sections 9.5 and 9.6 of the Purchase Agreement," or limit it to §9.2(a) claims other than Fundamental Representations.
2. Conform SPA §11.9 so the Escrow Agreement controls only the mechanics of release from the escrow account.
3. Make fraud uncapped in SPA §9.6(b), and carve fraud out of Escrow §6.4 consistently.
4. Consider a separate tax and Fundamental Representations holdback that survives to the statute of limitations plus 60 days, or a parent guaranty or net-worth covenant from Seller.
5. Any change requires a writing signed by all three parties (Escrow §10.2; SPA §11.3).

**Open points:** Seller's post-closing net assets and any distribution plans; whether Seller has any other source of credit support.

### Issue 6: Environmental survival is shorter than the Phase II timetable, and recovery is limited (High)

**Where:**

### Issue 9: Tax exposure gaps (High)

**Where:**
- SPA §1.1, "Pre-Closing Taxes": defined as "all Taxes of the Company" for pre-closing periods.
- SPA §4.13: Taxes are a Fundamental Representation. The Company is in Seller's consolidated group.
- SPA §6.10: Seller prepares and files consolidated returns that include the Company. Buyer has no review or consent right.
- SPA §9.1: Survival covers representations; covenants survive "in accordance with their terms." §9.2(c) (Pre-Closing Taxes indemnity) has no stated survival period.
- SPA §9.6(b): Pre-Closing Taxes are capped at the Purchase Price.
- SPA §11.2: Each party bears its own costs. Transfer taxes are not allocated.
- BoS §3.1 and §4(d): Transfer of vehicles and equipment to the Company for no stated price.

**Why it matters:**
- Under Treas. Reg. §1.1502-6, each member of a consolidated group is severally liable for the group's consolidated federal income tax for each year it was a member. The Company may therefore be exposed to Seller group tax for pre-closing years beyond "Taxes of the Company."
- The §9.2(c) indemnity has no stated survival, so Seller may argue it lapses on the general 18-month period.
- The transfer of equipment and vehicles from an affiliate for no stated price may trigger California sales or use tax, DMV fees or other transfer charges. The SPA does not say who bears them.
- Buyer has no review right over the consolidated returns that include the Company, and the file does not mention termination of any tax sharing arrangement.
- The 338(h)(10) deemed-sale tax, if any, is not expressly allocated to Seller.

**Recommended fix:**
- Expand "Pre-Closing Taxes" to include any Tax of Seller or any affiliate for which the Company is liable, including under Treas. Reg. §1.1502-6, as transferee or successor, or by contract.
- Set survival of the §9.2(c) indemnity to the statute of limitations plus 60 days.
- Allocate transfer, sales and use taxes, and DMV fees to Seller.
- Give Buyer review and consent rights over any return filed by Seller that includes the Company, and require termination of tax sharing arrangements at closing.
- State expressly that Seller bears any tax from a 338(h)(10) deemed sale.

**Open points:** Whether the Company is party to a tax sharing agreement; state filing history; any pending audits; the California tax position on the equipment transfer.

### Issue 10: Governing law and forum split (Medium)

**Where:**
- SPA §11.6 and §11.7: Delaware law; exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery.
- BoS §8.2 and §8.3: Delaware law and Delaware forum.
- Escrow §10.4: New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in the state and federal courts in Manhattan. Escrow §10.5 includes a jury waiver.
- SPA §11.9: The Escrow Agreement controls release and recovery from escrow.

**Why it matters:** A dispute over an escrow release could be litigated in New York while related indemnity claims proceed in Delaware. The two courts could interpret the "sole source" question (Issue 3) differently, and Buyer could face parallel proceedings.

**Recommended fix:** Conform Escrow §10.4 to Delaware law and forum, or add a coordination clause stating that the merits of any indemnity claim are determined under SPA Article XI, with New York used only for escrow mechanics.

### Issue 11: Escrow mechanics do not track SPA limits (Medium)

**Where:** Escrow §§6.1–6.3 (claim notice, objection, payment), §6.6 (order of payment), §6.5 (insufficient funds), §7.1 (ministerial duties only); SPA §9.1 (survival), §9.4 (procedures), §9.6(c) (basket).

**Why it matters:** Under Escrow §6.3(a), the escrow agent pays a claim within five Business Days after the 30-day objection period expires, if Seller has not objected. The agent does not check the claim against the SPA basket, the cap or the survival period (§7.1). Payment can therefore occur for a claim that is outside the basket or the survival period if Seller fails to object in time. Under §6.6, claims are paid in the order received, so an early small claim can deplete the escrow before a larger claim is paid.

**Recommended fix:** Require each Claim Notice to certify that it is timely under SPA §9.1 and exceeds the §9.6(c) threshold, or to state the basis for exceeding it.
