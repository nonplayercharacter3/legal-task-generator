- Require Seller to disclose all Larkfield communications since signing (SPA §6.3).

### 3. Escrow sole-source language conflicts with the SPA and could cut off direct recovery from Seller

**Where:**
- Escrow §6.4 ("The Escrow Funds shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery for all claims of the Buyer Indemnitees under Article IX ... except claims for actual fraud.")
- SPA §9.5 (escrow first, then Seller directly)
- SPA §9.6(b) (Fundamental Representations, Pre-Closing Taxes, and fraud are not limited to the escrow and may recover up to the Purchase Price)
- SPA §9.2(b), (c), (d), and (e) (covenants, Pre-Closing Taxes, unpaid Closing Indebtedness, and Equipment Affiliate claims)
- SPA §11.9 (SPA prevails over Ancillary Documents, except that the Escrow Agreement controls "with respect to the release of and recovery from the Escrow Funds")
- Escrow §10.2 (amendment requires Buyer, Seller, and Escrow Agent)

**Why it matters.** Seller will argue that Escrow §6.4 limits all Article IX recovery to the $4.2 million escrow. Paibon will argue that §11.9 limits the Escrow Agreement to the escrow's release mechanics, so §6.4 cannot override SPA §§9.5 and 9.6(b). The ambiguity matters most for Pre-Closing Taxes, Fundamental Representations, and Equipment Affiliate claims, where the SPA allows recovery beyond the escrow. Seller is a holding company that may distribute its proceeds, so direct recovery may be the only practical remedy.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Escrow §6.4 to read that recovery is first from the Escrow Funds, then directly from Seller as provided in SPA §§9.5 and 9.6. Add an express statement that nothing in the Escrow Agreement limits Seller's obligations under the SPA. This requires signatures from Buyer, Seller, and Ostrander (Escrow §10.2). If Ostrander will not sign, obtain a Seller-Buyer side letter that confirms the SPA controls, and note the issue for the escrow agent.

**Also.** Escrow §6.3(c) holds disputed amounts until Joint Written Instructions or a Final Order. Seller can therefore block any disputed claim indefinitely by withholding instructions, and there is no deadline or escalation mechanism. Add a deadline and an expedited resolution path.

### 4. Environmental protection is misaligned across survival, Phase II timing, the cap, and the escrow

**Where:**
- SPA §4.14 (environmental reps, including (c) no release requiring remediation)
- SPA §9.1(b) (environmental reps survive 180 days)
- SPA §6.7 (Phase II may be conducted post-closing and must be completed within 270 days; report to be delivered to Seller)
- SPA §9.2(a) and §9.6(a) (environmental claims are capped at the $4.2 million escrow)
- SPA §9.6(c) ($315,000 tipping basket, applicable to environmental claims because §4.14 is not a Fundamental Representation)
- SPA §9.4 (Seller may assume the defense of third-party claims, including regulatory claims)
- Escrow §5.1 (release at 18 months)

**Why it matters.**
- **Timing gap.** Environmental claims must be noticed by February 10, 2027 (180 days after the August 14 closing). The Phase II investigation may run until May 11, 2027. Paibon may not have the findings needed to make a claim when the window closes.
- **Cap and basket.** Any environmental loss is capped at $4.2 million (10% of the price) and is subject to the $315,000 basket. The Stockton Plant processes detergents, solvents, and bleaching agents (SPA §§1.1, 4.14(b)), and wastewater permits are referenced in §4.14(a). Remediation costs could exceed the cap.
- **Defense control.** Seller could assume the defense of a regulator claim under §9.4, which is inconsistent with Paibon's control of its own operations and remediation.
- **Disclosure.** §6.7 requires Paibon to deliver the final Phase II report to Seller. This may create discoverable material and a route for Seller to contest findings. It also gives Seller information before Paibon has decided how to respond.

**Recommended fix.**
- Extend the §4.14 survival period, or add a separate environmental indemnity that survives until at least 90 days after the Phase II report is final, and in any event not less than three years.
- Make environmental losses a special indemnity that is not subject to the §9.6(a) cap or the §9.6(c) basket, or create a separate environmental holdback.
- Require Buyer control of environmental investigation, remediation, and regulator communications. Carve these claims out of §9.4.
- Shorten the Phase II deadline or move the Phase II earlier, if still feasible post-closing. Serve a protective Claim Notice under SPA §9.1 and Escrow §6.1 before February 10, 2027, based on the information then available.
- Limit §6.7's report delivery to the findings required by law or by agreement, and consider a privilege structure for the investigation.
- Consider environmental insurance (pollution legal liability).

**Open facts.** Whether any Phase I or prior environmental reports exist. Stockton Plant wastewater permit status and any transfer requirements. Whether the Stockton Plant Lease (SPA §4.9) allocates environmental responsibility to the tenant.

### 5. Section 338(h)(10) election and tax protections

**Where:**
- SPA §6.11 (Buyer's notice must be delivered within 45 days after signing, that is, by July 16, 2026; if not, Seller's obligations lapse; Form 8023 is due at Closing if the election is made)
- SPA §4.13 (the Company is a member of Seller's consolidated group)
- SPA §5.2 (Buyer is taxed as an association)
- SPA §1.1 ("Pre-Closing Taxes" means taxes "of the Company")
- SPA §6.10 (Seller prepares consolidated returns; no Buyer review rights; no straddle-period mechanics)
- SPA §9.2(c) and §9.8 (Pre-Closing Taxes indemnity; indemnity payments treated as price adjustments)
- SPA §11.2 (each party bears its own costs; no transfer-tax allocation)

**Why it matters.**
- **Lost step-up.** If no §6.11 notice was delivered by July 16, the election is unavailable, and Paibon loses the basis step-up it may have priced in. This could be a significant economic loss.
- **Consolidated-return exposure.** Under the consolidated return regulations, the Company is severally liable for the federal income tax of Seller's consolidated group for years in which it was a member (Treas. Reg. §1.1502-6). The definition of Pre-Closing Taxes refers only to taxes "of the Company," so the indemnity may not clearly reach this exposure. This should be confirmed with tax counsel.
- **Straddle period and contests.** The SPA has no straddle-period return mechanics, no tax-contest control, and no refund allocation.
- **Transfer taxes.** The transfer of route trucks and equipment from the Equipment Affiliate to the Company may trigger California sales or use tax and DMV fees. The SPA does not allocate them.

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm immediately whether a §6.11 notice was delivered. If it was not, the step-up is lost and this should be reflected in price or in discussions with Seller.
- Amend "Pre-Closing Taxes" to expressly include Seller group taxes for which the Company is liable under Treas. Reg. §1.1502-6 or similar state law.
- Add tax covenants: Buyer review and consent rights over consolidated returns that affect the Company; straddle-period allocation (closing-of-the-books); control of tax contests affecting the Company; and refund allocation.
- Allocate transfer, sales, use, and DMV costs in writing, preferably to Seller or the Equipment Affiliate.
- Confirm Form 8023 filing mechanics and the state conformity question for any state election with tax counsel.

**Open facts.** Whether the Company was an S corporation or a C corporation member of Seller's group. Seller's tax history and any open audits. Whether Seller has a state-law filing requirement.

### 6. Liens on the conveyed equipment are not cleanly resolved

**Where:**
- BOS §3.2 (title free and clear); BOS §4(c) (sole owner, free of liens)
- BOS §4(d) (no consent or filing required, except DMV filings for route trucks)
- BOS §5.2 (UCC-3 terminations "before or at Closing")
- SPA §4.8 (title to assets), §9.2(e) (Equipment Affiliate claims)
- Thread, Item 3 (Aug 5, Venkataraman): two financing statements naming Zanoul Equipment Leasing as debtor covering laundry equipment at the Stockton Plant
- Thread, Item 4 (Aug 5, Ebright): secured party has signed authorizations; UCC-3s to be filed on the morning of Closing before funds are released

**Why it matters.**
- **Termination is not the same as release.** A UCC-3 terminates a financing statement. Paibon needs written lien releases from each secured party confirming payment and release of all security interests in the Conveyed Assets. The Thread describes only authorizations and filing.
- **Underlying debt is not in the price mechanics.** The "Closing Indebtedness" payoff in SPA §2.2(a) and §3.2(a)(iv) appears to refer to the Company's revolving facility. The Equipment Affiliate's secured debt is not clearly covered. If it is not paid, the secured party's lien survives on the trucks and equipment.
- **Jurisdiction of filing.** Zanoul Equipment Leasing is a Delaware LLC, so its UCC financing statements would generally be filed with the Delaware Secretary of State. The Thread does not say which jurisdiction the searches covered. Fixture filings for equipment bolted to the Stockton Plant would be filed locally in San Joaquin County. Confirm both.
- **Rep is likely inaccurate.** BOS §4(d) says no third-party consent is required. Secured lenders typically have consent or release rights over collateral. If the secured debt is not paid, the representation is untrue at signing.
- **Seller's own shares.** SPA §§2.1 and 4.3 require Seller's Shares to be free and clear. The Thread does not mention searches against Zanoul Holdings. Any pledge of the Shares to Seller's lenders must be released at closing.
- **Company's own liens.** Once the trucks and equipment are conveyed to the Company, the Company's revolving lender may claim them as after-acquired collateral. The payoff letters must release the Company's own UCC filings, not only the Equipment Affiliate's.

**Recommended fix.**
- Obtain bring-down UCC, tax lien, and judgment searches against Zanoul Equipment Leasing (Delaware), Zanoul Holdings (Delaware), and the Company (California), plus a fixture search in San Joaquin County.
- Require payoff and release letters from each secured party for the Equipment Affiliate, in addition to the UCC-3 authorizations. Make delivery a closing condition under SPA §7.2(e).
- Require payoff letters for the Company's revolving facility to include release of the Company's UCC filings and any lien on the Conveyed Assets.
- If any lien survives closing, add a specific indemnity under SPA §9.2(e) and confirm that it is not limited by the escrow sole-source language (see Issue 3).

**Open facts.** Identity of the two secured parties and the debts they secure. Whether the debts are being paid off or remain outstanding. Whether any of the equipment is subject to a purchase-money security interest.

**Why it matters.** Paibon now knows of the Larkfield, lien, and environmental issues from the folder. If Seller argues that Paibon knew of a breach before closing and cannot recover for it, the SPA does not say otherwise. Defense control under §9.4 also needs to be carved out for tax and environmental matters (see Issues 4 and 5).

**Recommended fix.** Add an express clause stating that Buyer's knowledge does not limit indemnification. Carve out tax contests and environmental and regulatory matters from Seller's defense rights.

### 13. Restrictive covenant enforceability (lower priority)

**Where:** SPA §6.8 (five-year, California-only non-compete binding Seller and its Affiliates).

**Why it matters.** Because Seller is selling all of its shares of the Company, the sale-of-business exception in California Business and Professions Code §16601 is likely to apply. The covenant binds "Affiliates" who are not parties to the SPA, and Seller cannot control them directly. Zanoul Equipment Leasing and other Affiliates are the most likely to compete.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm enforceability under §16601 with California counsel. Obtain joinders or separate non-compete agreements from the principal Affiliates.
