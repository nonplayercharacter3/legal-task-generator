## URGENT – IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED

### Issue 1: Change of Control Consent Not Obtained

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 14.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital C.

**Explanation:** Section 14.2 requires Distributor's prior written consent to any Change of Control of Supplier. A Change of Control without consent is a material breach that is *not* subject to the 30-day cure period in § 13.1, and Distributor may terminate on 30 days' notice at any time within 120 days after the Change of Control becomes effective.

Amendment No. 1, Recital C, discloses that Paikort entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Fenwyck Pet Holdings, LLC on September 15, 2026, under which Paikort will become a wholly owned subsidiary. Closing is expected on or about December 1, 2026. This is squarely within the definition of "Change of Control" in § 1.2(a) (surviving entity becomes a subsidiary of another). The Amendment does not obtain Distributor's consent to the Change of Control, nor does it waive or modify § 14.2.

If the merger closes on December 1, 2026 without consent, Broumoux has until approximately March 31, 2027 to deliver a termination notice. Upon such termination, § 14.3 requires Paikort to: (a) repurchase all saleable inventory at cost plus documented freight, and (b) pay an Exit Fee equal to 12 months' average Gross Margin (i.e., 12 × average monthly Gross Margin over the 12 full calendar months preceding the termination notice). Given the minimum purchase commitments of $3M–$4M per year (Exhibit B, ¶ 2), the Exit Fee could be substantial.

**Recommended Fix:**

This requires going back to Broumoux. Paikort should seek Distributor's written consent to the Change of Control *before* the merger closes. Options:

1. **Preferred:** Negotiate a consent letter or a second amendment in which Broumoux consents to the Fenwyck merger and waives its § 14.2 termination right (or agrees to a reduced Exit Fee or other modified consequences). Leverage: Paikort is adding a new product line (freeze-dried) and the relationship is otherwise in good standing. Broumoux benefits from continued exclusivity.

2. **Fallback:** If Broumoux will not consent, consider whether the merger can be structured or timed to avoid triggering § 14.2 (unlikely given the plain language of § 1.2(a)).

3. **Risk mitigation if consent cannot be obtained:** Model the worst-case financial exposure (inventory buyback + Exit Fee) and determine whether Paikort is prepared to absorb it. Also assess whether Broumoux is likely to actually terminate (it may prefer to continue the relationship and forgo the Exit Fee in exchange for a buy-in).

**Drafting language for consent (if Broumoux agrees):**

> **Consent to Change of Control**
>
> Distributor hereby consents to the Change of Control of Supplier described in Recital C of Amendment No. 1 to the Distribution Agreement, pursuant to which Supplier will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Fenwyck Pet Holdings, LLC upon closing of the Agreement and Plan of Merger dated September 15, 2026. Distributor waives its right to terminate the Agreement under Section 14.2 as a result of that Change of Control and confirms that the Agreement shall continue in full force and effect on its existing terms.

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### Issue 2: Amendment No. 1 Narrows Liability Cap Exclusion – Unfavorable to Paikort

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 3 (rewriting Distribution Agreement § 11.3(b)).

**Explanation:** The original § 11.3(b) excluded *all* of Section 10.1 (Distributor's indemnification obligations) and Section 10.2 (Supplier's indemnification obligations) from the $750,000 liability cap. The Amendment narrows this to exclude only "Section 10.1(a) or Section 10.2." The practical effect:

| Indemnity Obligation | Before Amendment | After Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| § 10.1(a) – Distributor indemnity for bodily injury/animal injury from its negligence or storage/handling | Uncapped | Uncapped |
| § 10.1(b) – Distributor indemnity for breach of territory restrictions (§ 2.3) or competing products (§ 2.4) | Uncapped | **Capped at $750,000** |
| § 10.1(c) – Distributor indemnity for recall costs caused by its storage/handling failures | Uncapped | **Capped at $750,000** |
| § 10.2 – Supplier's indemnity to Distributor | Uncapped | Uncapped |

This means that if Broumoux sells Products outside the Territory or distributes competing products, Paikort's recovery is now limited to $750,000 regardless of actual damages. Similarly, if a recall is caused by Broumoux's improper storage, Paikort's indemnity recovery from Broumoux is capped at $750,000. For a distribution network covering three states with national-adjacent retail accounts, a single territory breach or a contaminated-warehouse recall could easily exceed that amount.

**Recommended Fix:**

This requires going back to Broumoux. Paikort should seek to restore the original language of § 11.3(b) or at minimum carve out § 10.1(c) (recall costs) from the cap. If Broumoux resists, consider whether the $750,000 cap is acceptable in exchange for other concessions (e.g., the Change of Control consent in Issue 1).

**Proposed language (restoring original):**

> 11.3 **Exclusions.** The limitations in Sections 11.1 and 11.2 do not apply to:
>
> (a) a Party's fraud, gross negligence or willful misconduct;
>
> (b) a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 or Section 10.2;
>
> (c) Distributor's obligation to pay for Products ordered and delivered; or
>
> (d) a Party's payment obligations under Section 14.3.

**Alternative (compromise – at minimum uncapping recall costs):**

> (b) a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1(a), Section 10.1(c) or Section 10.2;

### Issue 3: Governing Law and Venue Conflict Between Distribution Agreement and Quality Agreement

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§ 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County, DE venue) vs. Quality Agreement §§ 12.1–12.2 (New Jersey law; Essex County, NJ venue).

**Explanation:** The two agreements select different governing laws and exclusive forums. Distribution Agreement § 16.4 provides an order of precedence and states that "the Quality Agreement controls quality, recall and handling matters." However, choice-of-law and forum-selection clauses are procedural, not substantive "quality, recall and handling" provisions. A dispute that implicates both commercial terms (e.g., pricing, minimums, termination) and quality terms (e.g., a recall caused by storage failure) could be subject to a jurisdictional fight. Paikort, as the Delaware corporation with its principal office in Wilmington (per the lease), is better positioned in Delaware. Broumoux, a New Jersey corporation, has home-field advantage in Essex County.

**Recommended Fix:**

This requires going back to Broumoux. Align the Quality Agreement's governing law and venue with the Distribution Agreement (Delaware / New Castle County). In the alternative, add a provision to the Quality Agreement stating that disputes arising under both agreements shall be consolidated in the forum selected by the Distribution Agreement.

**Proposed language (amending Quality Agreement § 12):**

> ## 12. GOVERNING LAW AND VENUE
>
> 12.1 **Governing Law.** This Quality Agreement and all disputes arising under or relating to it are governed by the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.
>
> 12.2 **Venue.** Any action or proceeding arising under or relating to this Quality Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in New Castle County, Delaware, consistent with Section 15.2 of the Distribution Agreement. Each Party irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection to venue in them.

### Issue 4: Quality Agreement Review Obligation Triggered by Freeze-Dried Product Addition

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 13.3; Amendment No. 1, Recital B and § 2.

**Explanation:** Section 13.3 requires the Parties to review the Quality Agreement "on the addition of any new Product line" and make updates in writing. The freeze-dried product line was added effective October 1, 2026. There is no evidence in the folder that a review has occurred. The Quality Agreement's storage parameters (§ 4.2: max 80°F, 65% RH) were drafted for dry kibble. Freeze-dried products are far more sensitive to moisture and may require lower humidity thresholds, different packaging integrity checks, or modified traceability procedures. While Amendment § 5.4 (QA) and § 5.2 (Amendment) require Supplier to furnish product-specific handling instructions before first shipment, the base QA parameters may be inadequate if those instructions are not formally incorporated.

**Recommended Fix:**

Internal action for Paikort: (1) Confirm that Specifications and freeze-dried-specific handling instructions have been or will be delivered to Broumoux before first shipment (Amendment § 5.2). (2) Initiate the § 13.3 review and propose a written update to the Quality Agreement addressing freeze-dried product storage (e.g., reduced maximum RH, pouch integrity inspection at receiving, modified quarantine triggers). This can be done unilaterally by Paikort preparing the proposed revisions and sending them to Broumoux under the review obligation.

**Proposed language (new Quality Agreement § 4.6):**

> 4.6 **Freeze-Dried Products – Additional Requirements.** For freeze-dried Products, Distributor shall: (a) maintain relative humidity not exceeding fifty percent (50%) in storage areas dedicated to or shared with freeze-dried Products; (b) inspect each pouch for integrity (seal, puncture, moisture ingress) at receiving and before outbound shipment; (c) store freeze-dried Products in their original unopened packaging at all times; and (d) comply with all additional handling instructions set forth in the Specifications furnished by Supplier under Section 2.1 of this Quality Agreement.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### Issue 5: Automatic Renewal – 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice Deadline

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.2.

**Explanation:** After the Initial Term (March 1, 2025 – February 29, 2028), the Agreement renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. For the Initial Term, the non-renewal notice deadline is **September 2, 2027** (180 days before February 29, 2028). Paikort should calendar this date. If Paikort wants the option to exit or renegotiate at the end of the Initial Term, it must act by that date.

**Recommended Fix:**

Internal: Calendar the September 2, 2027 deadline. Decide as part of the Change of Control negotiation (Issue 1) whether Paikort wants to preserve the non-renewal option or is comfortable with automatic renewal.

### Issue 6: Limited Remedy for Failure to Meet Minimum Purchase Commitment

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 4.4.

**Explanation:** If Broumoux fails to meet the minimum purchase commitment for two consecutive contract years, Paikort's sole contractual remedy is to convert the appointment to non-exclusive on 90 days' notice. Paikort has no right to terminate, no right to damages for the shortfall, and no right to accelerate the minimums. Given that the minimums escalate from $3M to $4M per year, a distributor that chronically under-purchases locks Paikort into exclusivity in three states with no exit.

**Recommended Fix:**

This requires going back to Broumoux. Negotiate an amendment to § 4.4 adding: (a) a termination right if the minimum is missed for three consecutive contract years; and/or (b) a liquidated damages or make-up provision for the shortfall.

**Proposed language:**

> 4.4 **Minimum Purchase Commitment.** Distributor shall purchase in each contract year at least the minimum quantities of Products stated in Exhibit B. If Distributor fails to meet the minimum for two consecutive contract years, Supplier may, on ninety (90) days' written notice, convert Distributor's appointment to non-exclusive. If Distributor fails to meet the minimum for three consecutive contract years, Supplier may terminate this Agreement on ninety (90) days' written notice, in which case Section 14.3 shall not apply.

### Issue 7: Amendment No. 1 Signature Block Lacks Notice Addresses

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, signature block (compare Distribution Agreement signature block, which includes "Address for notices").

**Explanation:** The main Agreement specifies notice addresses (Attn: General Counsel for Paikort; Attn: President for Broumoux). The Amendment's signature block has blank Name/Title lines but no notice address field. While § 16.1 of the main Agreement governs notices for the Agreement "as amended," the absence of a notice address in the Amendment creates a minor ambiguity about whether the Amendment's execution constitutes a "written instrument signed by both Parties" for purposes of § 16.3 if the signature blocks are incomplete.

**Recommended Fix:**

Internal: Ensure both signature blocks are fully completed (Name, Title) and that the executed copies are retained. If the blanks are still unfilled, have both Parties initial or re-execute with completed blocks. No negotiation with Broumoux required unless the blocks remain incomplete.

### Issue 8: Insurance Limits May Be Inadequate for Expanded Product Line

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 12.1(b).

**Explanation:** Both Parties must maintain product contamination and recall insurance of not less than $1,000,000. With the addition of freeze-dried products (which carry higher contamination and recall risk due to moisture sensitivity and raw-ingredient handling), and with minimum purchase commitments now encompassing a broader product portfolio, $1M recall coverage may be insufficient for a multi-state recall event. A single recall of the freeze-dried line across NJ, NY, and PA could easily exceed $1M in retrieval, destruction, replacement, and notification costs.

**Recommended Fix:**

Internal: Paikort should review its current product recall insurance policy with its broker and consider increasing the limit to $2M–$3M, particularly now that the product line has expanded. This does not require action by Broumoux unless Paikort also wants to amend § 12.1 to raise the contractual minimum (which would require Broumoux to increase its own coverage).
