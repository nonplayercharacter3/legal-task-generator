### 1. Change of Control Consent Not Obtained – Closing Imminent

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 14.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital C.

**Issue.** Amendment No. 1, Recital C, confirms that on September 15, 2026, Paikort entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Fenwyck Pet Holdings, LLC, under which Paikort will become a wholly owned subsidiary. Closing is expected on or about December 1, 2026. Section 1.2 of the Distribution Agreement defines "Change of Control" to include, in subsection (a), a merger in which the party "survives but becomes a subsidiary of another entity." The Fenwyck transaction is squarely within that definition.

Section 14.2 requires Distributor's **prior written consent** to any Change of Control of Supplier. A Change of Control without consent is a material breach **not subject to the 30-day cure period** in § 13.1, and Distributor may terminate on 30 days' notice at any time within 120 days after the Change of Control becomes effective.

The Amendment No. 1, although executed after the merger agreement was signed (Amendment Effective Date: October 1, 2026), does **not** include Distributor's consent to the Change of Control. Recital C merely discloses the transaction.

**Why it matters.** If Paikort closes the Fenwyck merger without obtaining Broumoux's written consent under § 14.2, Broumoux will have an immediate, uncured material breach and a 120-day window to terminate. Termination triggers the consequences in § 14.3: (a) Paikort must repurchase all saleable inventory at cost plus freight within 30 days, and (b) Paikort must pay an Exit Fee equal to 12 months' average Gross Margin (calculated over the 12 full calendar months preceding the termination notice). Given minimum purchase commitments of $3–4 million per year (Exhibit B, ¶ 2), the Gross Margin exposure could be substantial.

**Recommended fix.** Obtain Broumoux's written consent to the Change of Control **before** the Fenwyck closing. This requires going back to Broumoux. If Broumoux withholds consent, Paikort should negotiate either (a) a consent with no conditions, or (b) a consent coupled with an agreement that Broumoux will not exercise its termination right under § 14.2, in exchange for some consideration (e.g., a margin adjustment, an extension of the minimum purchase commitment, or a reduced Exit Fee). If Broumoux insists on its termination right, Paikort should model the § 14.3 exposure (inventory buyback + Exit Fee) before closing.

Proposed consent language (to be inserted as a new recital or standalone provision in a further amendment or side letter):

> **Distributor's Consent to Change of Control.** Distributor hereby consents to the Change of Control of Supplier contemplated by the Agreement and Plan of Merger dated September 15, 2026, between Supplier and Fenwyck Pet Holdings, LLC, and agrees not to terminate the Agreement under Section 14.2 by reason of that Change of Control. For the avoidance of doubt, this consent does not waive Distributor's rights under any other provision of the Agreement.

### 2. Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Liability Cap Exclusion – Adverse to Paikort

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 3 (replacing Distribution Agreement § 11.3(b)); Distribution Agreement §§ 11.2, 10.1(b)–(c).

**Issue.** Original § 11.3(b) provided that the $750,000 cap in § 11.2 does not apply to "a party's indemnification obligations under **Section 10.1 or Section 10.2**" (i.e., all of Distributor's indemnification obligations in § 10.1(a), (b), and (c), plus all of Supplier's obligations in § 10.2). Amendment No. 1, § 3, replaces this with: "a Party's indemnification obligations under **Section 10.1(a) or Section 10.2**."

The practical effect: Distributor's indemnification obligations under § 10.1(b) (breach of territory restrictions in § 2.3 or competing products restriction in § 2.4) and § 10.1(c) (Recall costs caused by Distributor's storage or handling failures) are now **subject to the $750,000 aggregate cap**.

**Why it matters.**

- **Recall exposure (§ 10.1(c)):** If Broumoux's warehouse conditions cause a product recall (e.g., temperature excursion leading to spoilage, pest contamination), Paikort's recovery from Broumoux for notification, retrieval, transportation, destruction and replacement costs is now capped at $750,000. A multi-state recall of pet food products can easily exceed that amount. The Quality Agreement, § 8.3, confirms that in such a scenario the costs are "borne by Distributor as indemnifiable under Section 10.1(c) of the Distribution Agreement" – but that indemnification is now capped.

- **Territory/competing product breaches (§ 10.1(b)):** If Broumoux actively sells into out-of-territory markets or distributes a competing brand in violation of § 2.4, Paikort's indemnification recovery is capped at $750,000. While the more likely remedy for such breaches is injunctive relief (§ 15.3) or termination, the monetary recovery for lost profits or market disruption is now limited.

- **The cap is aggregate.** The $750,000 is not per-claim; it is the total across all claims subject to the cap during the life of the agreement. A single significant recall could exhaust the entire cap.

**Recommended fix.** This requires going back to Broumoux. Paikort should seek to restore the original language of § 11.3(b) (i.e., exclude all of § 10.1 from the cap) or, at minimum, exclude § 10.1(c) (recall costs) from the cap. If Broumoux resists, Paikort should consider whether the $750,000 cap is adequate given the risk profile of the freeze-dried product line (which is more sensitive to storage conditions) and the multi-state Territory.

Proposed restoration language:

> 11.3 **Exclusions.** The limitations in Sections 11.1 and 11.2 do not apply to:
>
> (a) a Party's fraud, gross negligence or willful misconduct;
>
> (b) a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 or Section 10.2;
>
> (c) Distributor's obligation to pay for Products ordered and delivered; or
>
> (d) a Party's payment obligations under Section 14.3.

Alternatively, if Broumoux will not agree to full restoration, a compromise:

> (b) a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1(a), Section 10.1(c) or Section 10.2;

This would at least keep recall-cost indemnification outside the cap.

### 3. Split Governing Law and Venue: Delaware vs. New Jersey

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County, DE venue); Quality Agreement § 12.1–12.2 (New Jersey law; Essex County, NJ venue); Distribution Agreement § 16.4 (order of precedence).

**Issue.** The Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive venue in New Castle County, DE. The Quality Agreement is governed by New Jersey law with exclusive venue in Essex County, NJ. Section 16.4 of the Distribution Agreement provides that the Quality Agreement "controls quality, recall and handling matters." This creates a bifurcated dispute-resolution framework: a recall-cost allocation dispute, for example, would be governed by NJ law and heard in Essex County, while a pricing or minimum-purchase dispute would be governed by DE law and heard in New Castle County.

**Why it matters.** If a recall occurs and the parties disagree over cost allocation, Paikort would be litigating in New Jersey under New Jersey law, even though the master agreement is a Delaware document. New Jersey law may interpret indemnification, limitation of liability, or force majeure provisions differently than Delaware law. The split also increases litigation cost and complexity, as related claims may need to be brought (or defended) in two forums.

**Recommended fix.** This requires going back to Broumoux. Paikort should seek to conform the Quality Agreement's governing law and venue to the Distribution Agreement (Delaware law, New Castle County venue). If Broumoux resists (it is a NJ corporation, so it may prefer home-court advantage), Paikort should at minimum confirm that the § 16.4 proviso does not create an unintended override of the Amendment. See Issue 4 below.

Proposed amendment to Quality Agreement § 12:

> 12.1 **Governing Law.** This Quality Agreement and all disputes arising under or relating to it are governed by the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.
>
> 12.2 **Venue.** Any action or proceeding arising under or relating to this Quality Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in New Castle County, Delaware. Each Party irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection to venue in them.

### 4. Order of Precedence Ambiguity: Does the Quality Agreement Override the Amendment?

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 16.4; Amendment No. 1, § 3.

**Issue.** Section 16.4 establishes the order of precedence as: (1) Amendments, (2) the Distribution Agreement, (3) the Quality Agreement, "provided, however, that the Quality Agreement controls quality, recall and handling matters." Amendment No. 1, § 3, modifies § 11.3(b), which governs whether recall-cost indemnification (§ 10.1(c)) is subject to the liability cap. A recall-cost dispute is arguably a "recall" matter, which the proviso assigns to the Quality Agreement. But the Quality Agreement, § 8.3, itself references § 10.1(c) of the Distribution Agreement as the indemnification mechanism. The question is whether the Amendment (which sits at the top of the precedence order) controls the cap question, or whether the proviso pulls "recall matters" into the Quality Agreement's domain, where no cap is stated.

**Why it matters.** If the proviso is read to mean the Quality Agreement controls all recall-related disputes regardless of the Amendment, then the cap modification in Amendment No. 1, § 3 might not apply to recall-cost indemnification. This creates uncertainty that could be exploited in a dispute. Paikort (which is adversely affected by the cap narrowing) would want the Amendment to control; Broumoux might argue the proviso preserves the uncapped indemnification for recalls.

**Recommended fix.** This requires going back to Broumoux for clarification. Add a sentence to § 16.4 or to the next amendment making explicit that the Amendment governs the scope of the liability cap as it applies to recall-cost indemnification. For example:

> For the avoidance of doubt, the Amendment's modification of Section 11.3(b) governs whether recall-cost indemnification under Section 10.1(c) is subject to the limitation in Section 11.2, notwithstanding the proviso in this Section 16.4.

(If Paikort successfully restores the original § 11.3(b) per Issue 2, this clarification becomes less critical but is still advisable.)

### 5. Quality Agreement Not Updated for Freeze-Dried Products

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 13.3; Amendment No. 1, §§ 2, 5.

**Issue.** Quality Agreement § 13.3 requires the parties to review the Quality Agreement "on the addition of any new Product line." Amendment No. 1 added freeze-dried dog food and cat food products (three new product lines in Exhibit A). There is no evidence in the matter folder that the Quality Agreement was reviewed or amended to reflect the different storage, handling, and traceability requirements of freeze-dried products.

The current Quality Agreement sets a maximum storage temperature of 80°F and maximum relative humidity of 65% (§ 4.2). Freeze-dried products are typically more sensitive to moisture and may require lower humidity thresholds or specific packaging integrity checks (e.g., pouch seal verification). Amendment No. 1, § 5.4, provides that Distributor shall comply with "any additional handling instructions furnished by Supplier" for freeze-dried products, and § 5.2 requires Supplier to furnish Specifications and product-specific handling instructions before first shipment. However, the Quality Agreement itself does not incorporate any freeze-dried-specific parameters.

**Why it matters.** If a storage deviation occurs with freeze-dried products and the parties dispute whether the 80°F/65% RH thresholds were appropriate, the Quality Agreement is silent on product-specific variations. This could affect recall-cost allocation under § 8.3 and indemnification under § 10.1(c).

**Recommended fix.** (a) Internally: Ensure Paikort's quality team prepares and delivers written Specifications and handling instructions for the freeze-dried products before first shipment, as required by Amendment No. 1, § 5.2. (b) With Broumoux: Amend the Quality Agreement to add a section or exhibit specifying storage parameters for freeze-dried products (e.g., maximum temperature, maximum humidity, pouch integrity inspection requirements). This requires going back to Broumoux but can be done as a routine update under § 13.3.

### 6. Exhibit C Schedules Not Attached

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Exhibit C, ¶¶ 1 and 2.

**Issue.** Exhibit C identifies (a) Reserved Accounts (national mass-merchant and pet specialty chain accounts) and (b) Existing Distributor Lines (third-party brands Broumoux may continue to distribute under § 2.4). Both are described as "the schedule agreed by the Parties on the Effective Date," but no actual schedule is attached to the Agreement or referenced by a specific document.

**Why it matters.** If a dispute arises over whether a particular retail account is a Reserved Account (and thus available for Paikort to sell directly) or whether a particular competing brand is a permitted "existing line," there is no definitive list in the contract. Paikort could find its direct sales to a national chain challenged as a breach of exclusivity (§ 2.1), or Broumoux could argue it is carrying a competing brand that was not on the agreed schedule.

**Recommended fix.** Obtain the schedules that were "agreed by the Parties on the Effective Date" and attach them to the Agreement as a further exhibit or confirmatory amendment. This requires going back to Broumoux to confirm the agreed lists and execute a brief confirmatory document.

### 7. Auto-Renewal and Non-Renewal Notice Window

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.1–3.2.

**Issue.** The Initial Term is five years from March 1, 2025, expiring February 28, 2030. Thereafter, the Agreement renews automatically for successive one-year terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. The first non-renewal notice deadline is therefore approximately September 1, 2029 (180 days before February 28, 2030).

**Why it matters.** If the Fenwyck acquisition changes Paikort's strategic direction for the mid-Atlantic territory, Paikort needs to be aware of this deadline well in advance. Missing it locks Paikort into another year. Additionally, if Paikort wants to renegotiate terms (e.g., the liability cap, the Change of Control provision) as leverage for renewal, the 180-day window is the natural point to raise those issues.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the September 1, 2029 deadline (and subsequent annual deadlines) in Paikort's contract management system. No action required now, but flag for the client's business team.

### 8. Insurance Requirements – Confirm Adequacy for Expanded Product Line

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 12.1.

**Issue.** Both parties must maintain (a) CGL with products/completed operations coverage of $2M per occurrence / $5M aggregate, and (b) product contamination and recall insurance of at least $1M. These requirements were negotiated for a dry pet food product line. The addition of freeze-dried products (which carry different contamination and spoilage risk profiles) may warrant a review of whether the $1M recall insurance limit is adequate.

**Why it matters.** If a recall of freeze-dried products exceeds the $1M insurance limit, the excess would fall on the parties per the indemnification and cost-allocation provisions. Given that the liability cap (as amended) is only $750,000, the insurance is Paikort's primary backstop.

**Recommended fix.** Internally: Have Paikort's risk management team confirm that its current product contamination and recall insurance policy covers the freeze-dried product lines and that the $1M limit is adequate given the expanded SKU count and risk profile. If additional coverage is needed, update the policy before the first freeze-dried shipment. No action required with Broumoux unless Paikort wants to negotiate a higher mutual insurance requirement.

### 9. Paikort's Obligation to Furnish Freeze-Dried Specifications Before First Shipment

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 5.2; Quality Agreement § 2.1.

**Issue.** Amendment No. 1, § 5.2, requires Paikort to furnish Specifications and product-specific handling instructions for freeze-dried products **before** the first shipment. Quality Agreement § 2.1 similarly makes Supplier responsible for "furnishing to Distributor any product-specific storage and handling instructions."

**Why it matters.** If Paikort ships freeze-dried products before delivering these documents, it may be in breach of the Amendment, and any subsequent quality issue could be complicated by the argument that Broumoux did not have adequate instructions. This is a Paikort-side action item.

**Recommended fix.** Internally: Confirm with Paikort's product/quality team that Specifications and handling instructions for all three freeze-dried product lines (Raw-Crafted Freeze-Dried Recipe Dog, Freeze-Dried Toppers Dog, Raw-Crafted Freeze-Dried Recipe Cat) are drafted, approved, and delivered to Broumoux in writing before the first Purchase Order is accepted for shipment. Track delivery and obtain Broumoux's written acknowledgment of receipt.
