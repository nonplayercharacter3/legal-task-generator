## URGENT ITEMS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ATTENTION

**1. Fenwyck Merger – Missing Change-of-Control Consent**

Amendment No. 1, Recital C (amendment_no_1.md) discloses that Paikort entered into a merger agreement with Fenwyck Pet Holdings, LLC on September 15, 2026, with closing expected on or about December 1, 2026. Section 14.2 of the Distribution Agreement (distribution_agreement.md, § 14.2) requires **Distributor's prior written consent** for any Change of Control of Supplier. A Change of Control is defined in § 1.2 to include a merger in which the party "survives but becomes a subsidiary of another entity" — which is exactly what will happen here.

The Amendment does not obtain that consent. A recital is not an operative grant of consent. If the Fenwyck closing occurs without Broumoux's written consent under § 14.2, Paikort will be in material breach that is **not subject to the 30-day cure period** (§ 14.2, last sentence), and Broumoux may terminate at any time within 120 days after the CoC becomes effective. Upon such termination, § 14.3 triggers:

- **Inventory buyback:** Paikort must repurchase all saleable inventory at Broumoux's cost plus documented freight, with delivery within 30 days (§ 14.3(a)).
- **Exit Fee:** Paikort must pay 12 months' average Gross Margin, calculated as Broumoux's average monthly Gross Margin over the preceding 12 full calendar months, multiplied by 12 (§ 14.3(b)).

Given the minimum purchase commitment of $4,000,000 per year (Exhibit B, ¶ 2) and the margin structure, the Exit Fee could be a seven-figure exposure.

**Recommended fix:** Obtain Broumoux's written consent to the Fenwyck Change of Control **before** the December 1, 2026 closing. This requires going back to Broumoux. I recommend we draft a short consent letter or, preferably, a second amendment that (a) grants consent under § 14.2, (b) confirms the parties' intent that the recital in Amendment No. 1 was not itself consent, and (c) addresses any conditions Broumoux may want (e.g., a representation that Fenwyck will honor the Agreement, or a right to terminate without the Exit Fee if the relationship is to continue under new ownership). If Broumoux resists, we need to understand their leverage and whether the Exit Fee is negotiable.

## ADDITIONAL ISSUES

### 2. Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Liability Cap Exclusion (Paikort's Recovery Is Now Capped at $750,000 for Two Important Scenarios)

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 11.2 (cap of $750,000 per party) and § 11.3(b) as amended by Amendment No. 1, § 3 (amendment_no_1.md).

**Original § 11.3(b):** Excluded "a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 **or** Section 10.2" from the cap. This meant **all** of Broumoux's indemnification obligations to Paikort (territory breaches, competing products, and recall costs caused by Broumoux's handling failures) were uncapped.

**Amended § 11.3(b):** Excludes only "a Party's indemnification obligations under Section **10.1(a)** or Section 10.2." This means:

| Broumoux Indemnity Obligation | Before Amendment | After Amendment |
|---|---|---|
| § 10.1(a) – Bodily injury/animal death from Broumoux negligence or handling | Uncapped | Uncapped |
| § 10.1(b) – Breach of territory restrictions (§ 2.3) or competing products (§ 2.4) | Uncapped | **Capped at $750,000** |
| § 10.1(c) – Recall costs caused by Broumoux's storage/handling failure | Uncapped | **Capped at $750,000** |

**Why it matters:** A recall across three states (NJ, NY, PA) involving retrieval, destruction, and replacement of pet food could easily exceed $750,000 in costs if caused by Broumoux's handling failure. Similarly, if Broumoux sells into out-of-territory markets or distributes competing products, Paikort's recovery for that breach is now limited to $750,000 regardless of actual damages.

**Recommended fix:** This requires going back to Broumoux. I recommend negotiating a second amendment (or folding this into the CoC consent discussed above) that restores the original language of § 11.3(b) to read: "a Party's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 or Section 10.2." If Broumoux resists, a fallback position would be to raise the cap specifically for § 10.1(c) (recall costs) to a level that reflects realistic recall exposure (e.g., $2,000,000 or $5,000,000), given that the Quality Agreement at § 8.3 allocates those costs to Broumoux when caused by its handling failures.

### 3. Conflicting Governing Law and Venue Provisions

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County, DE venue) vs. Quality Agreement § 12.1–12.2 (New Jersey law; Essex County, NJ venue).

**Why it matters:** Distribution Agreement § 16.4 provides that the Quality Agreement "controls quality, recall and handling matters." So a dispute over recall cost allocation or a storage deviation would be governed by New Jersey law and heard in Essex County, NJ, while a dispute over pricing, minimum purchases, or the Change of Control provision would be governed by Delaware law and heard in New Castle County, DE. This split creates:

- Practical complexity and cost if a single incident (e.g., a recall caused by a storage deviation) raises both quality/handling issues and commercial issues (e.g., whether the recall was properly allocated, whether Broumoux's handling failure also constitutes a material breach under the Distribution Agreement).
- Risk of inconsistent outcomes under different states' substantive law.
- Potential forum-fighting by Broumoux, which is a New Jersey corporation and would have home-court advantage in Essex County.

**Recommended fix:** This requires going back to Broumoux. I recommend amending Quality Agreement § 12 to conform to the Distribution Agreement's Delaware law and New Castle County venue, or alternatively amending Distribution Agreement § 15 to conform to the Quality Agreement. Given that Paikort is a Delaware corporation and the Distribution Agreement is the primary commercial document, I would recommend making everything Delaware. Proposed language for Quality Agreement § 12:

> **12.1 Governing Law.** This Quality Agreement and all disputes arising under or relating to it are governed by the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.
>
> **12.2 Venue.** Any action or proceeding arising under or relating to this Quality Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the state or federal courts located in New Castle County, Delaware. Each Party irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of those courts and waives any objection to venue in them.

### 4. Quality Agreement Not Formally Amended for Freeze-Dried Products

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 13.3 (requires review "on the addition of any new Product line"); Amendment No. 1, § 5 (addresses freeze-dried products in the context of the Distribution Agreement but does not execute a formal amendment to the Quality Agreement).

**Why it matters:** The Quality Agreement as it appears in the folder already includes § 5.4 (handling of freeze-dried products) and § 4.2 sets temperature (80°F max) and humidity (65% max) parameters. However:

- There is no formal amendment to the Quality Agreement in the matter folder documenting the addition of § 5.4 or any other freeze-dried-specific changes. If § 5.4 was added by side letter or informal agreement, the documentation is incomplete.
- The general parameters in § 4.2 (80°F / 65% RH) may not be appropriate for freeze-dried products, which are typically more moisture-sensitive. Amendment No. 1, § 5.2 obligates Paikort to furnish "Specifications and any product-specific handling instructions" before first shipment. If those instructions specify tighter parameters (e.g., 60°F max, 40% RH max), there is a potential conflict with § 4.2 of the Quality Agreement, which sets the general ceiling.
- Quality Agreement § 13.3 requires the parties to "make any updates in writing" upon addition of a new product line. This obligation has not been formally discharged.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Confirm whether a written amendment to the Quality Agreement was executed when § 5.4 was added; if not, execute one now. (b) Ensure that Paikort's product-specific handling instructions for the freeze-dried line (required by Amendment No. 1, § 5.2) are delivered to Broumoux in writing before the first shipment and are consistent with or supersede the general parameters in Quality Agreement § 4.2. (c) Add a sentence to the freeze-dried handling instructions or a Quality Agreement amendment clarifying that product-specific instructions supersede the general parameters in § 4.2 where they are more restrictive. Proposed language:

> "Where Supplier's product-specific handling instructions for a particular Product impose storage, temperature, or humidity parameters that are more restrictive than those set forth in Section 4.2, the product-specific instructions shall control for that Product."

### 5. Auto-Renewal with 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.2.

**Why it matters:** After the five-year Initial Term (expiring February 29, 2030), the Agreement renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. For the first renewal, Paikort would need to deliver notice by approximately **September 2, 2029**. If Paikort wants the option to walk away at the end of the Initial Term (or any renewal term), it must calendar this deadline well in advance.

**Recommended fix:** Internal action. Calendar the 180-day non-renewal notice deadline for each term. No drafting needed, but if Paikort wants a shorter notice period or a mutual right to terminate for convenience at the end of the Initial Term, that would require going back to Broumoux.

### 6. Limited Remedy for Chronic Failure to Meet Minimum Purchases

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 4.4.

**Why it matters:** If Broumoux fails to meet the minimum purchase commitment for two consecutive contract years, Paikort's **only** contractual remedy is to convert the appointment to non-exclusive on 90 days' notice. Paikort has no right to terminate for this failure, no right to claim damages for lost volume, and no right to impose a liquidated shortfall fee. If Broumoux chronically under-purchases but still meets the minimum by a narrow margin (or if the minimums are not aggressive enough), Paikort is locked into the exclusive arrangement for the full Term.

**Recommended fix:** This requires going back to Broumoux if Paikort wants stronger protections. Options include: (a) adding a right to terminate (not just convert to non-exclusive) after two consecutive years of failure to meet minimums; (b) adding a shortfall payment obligation (e.g., Broumoux pays Paikort the difference between actual purchases and the minimum, calculated at a specified margin); or (c) lowering the consecutive-year threshold from two to one. I would recommend option (a) as the minimum ask, with (b) as a negotiating chip.

### 7. Insurance Compliance – Verification Needed

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 12.1–12.2.

**Why it matters:** Both parties must maintain CGL ($2M per occurrence / $5M aggregate) and product contamination/recall insurance ($1M minimum), with the other party named as additional insured on the CGL policy. Given the issues identified above (particularly the $750,000 cap on Broumoux's recall-cost indemnity under amended § 11.3(b)), Paikort's ability to recover from Broumoux's insurance becomes more important. If Broumoux is not actually maintaining the required coverage, Paikort's practical recovery in a recall scenario could be severely limited.

**Recommended fix:** Internal action. Request current certificates of insurance from Broumoux under § 12.2 and verify that (a) the policies are in force, (b) the limits meet § 12.1, and (c) Paikort is named as additional insured on the CGL policy. If Broumoux cannot produce compliant certificates, this is a potential material breach under § 13.1.

### 8. Audit Rights May Be Insufficient

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 7.6.

**Why it matters:** Paikort may audit Broumoux's warehouses and records only **once per contract year** (except following a Recall or suspected breach). For a three-state territory carrying both dry and freeze-dried products with strict temperature/humidity requirements, one audit per year may be inadequate to ensure ongoing compliance, particularly if Broumoux uses multiple facilities (see Quality Agreement § 2.4, which requires Paikort's approval for third-party warehouses).

**Recommended fix:** This requires going back to Broumoux if Paikort wants more frequent audits. A reasonable ask would be to increase the frequency to twice per contract year, or to allow additional audits upon reasonable suspicion of a Deviation (not just a full Recall). Alternatively, Paikort could require Broumoux to provide quarterly temperature/humidity monitoring summaries as a less intrusive alternative.
