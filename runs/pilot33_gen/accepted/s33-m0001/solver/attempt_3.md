## Summary

## Issues Ranked by Significance

### 1. Change of Control – Merger Consent Not Obtained

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Recital C; Distribution Agreement §15.3 (page 3–4).

**What the documents say:** Recital C of the Amendment discloses that Sorund Holdings, Inc. (Supplier's parent) entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Ostrava Animal Health plc on September 15, 2026, with closing expected in Q1 2027. Section 15.3 provides that a Change of Control of Supplier (including of any parent) "shall be deemed an assignment requiring Distributor's prior written consent." If it occurs without consent, Distributor may (a) terminate the Agreement by notice within 180 days of learning of the Change of Control, or (b) require Supplier to repurchase all of Distributor's then-current inventory at Distributor's landed cost, payable within 30 days.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** The merger closing in Q1 2027 will almost certainly constitute a Change of Control under §1.3 (acquisition of >50% of voting securities of a parent). The Amendment discloses the merger in a recital but does not obtain consent, does not waive the consent requirement, and does not address the repurchase or termination remedies. If the merger closes without consent, Bairuth has a clean contractual right to walk away or demand a full inventory buyback at landed cost (which includes freight, insurance, and any markups Bairuth has built in). Given the $2.4M annual minimum and the 36-month Initial Term (expiring March 31, 2028), the inventory repurchase could be a material cash outlay on top of losing the distribution channel.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Ostrava merger closes, Sorund should seek written consent from Bairuth under §15.3. The negotiation leverage is better now (pre-closing) than after. If Bairuth resists, Sorund should be prepared to offer a limited concession (e.g., a right to terminate with a shorter wind-down, or a cap on the repurchase price) in exchange for consent. Document the consent as an Amendment or side letter to avoid any argument that the recital in Amendment No. 1 constitutes consent (a recital is generally not an operative provision).

**Fact to confirm:** What is the current status and expected closing date of the Ostrava merger? Has Sorund's board or deal team already engaged Bairuth on this point?

### 2. Reference-Laboratory Services Pulled into "Products" by Amendment No. 1

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §1.1 (page 1); Statement of Work §1.3 (page 1); Distribution Agreement §2.4 (page 1).

**What the documents say:**

- Agreement §2.4 (Reserved Services): "Supplier reserves the right to provide reference-laboratory testing services, directly or through its Affiliates, to veterinary clinics and other customers inside and outside the Territory. Reference-laboratory testing services are not Products. They fall outside the scope of Distributor's appointment and exclusivity under this Section 2."
- SOW §1.3 (Connected Services): "all software, cloud reporting, telemedicine integrations **and reference laboratory testing services** offered by Supplier, whether provided directly by Supplier or through its Affiliates, together with any related onboarding, data connectivity and support services."
- Amendment §1.1 (revised §1.9): "Products" now means the items in Exhibit A "and includes the Connected Services and the SorundLink Platform."
- Agreement §16.4 (Order of Precedence): Amendments prevail over the Distribution Agreement.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** By the four corners of the documents, the Amendment's expanded definition of "Products" (which incorporates "Connected Services" from the SOW, which in turn includes reference-laboratory testing) overrides §2.4 under the precedence rule. This means reference-lab testing services are now "Products" subject to Bairuth's exclusive distribution right under §2.1–2.2. Sorund could be contractually barred from selling its own reference-lab services directly to veterinary clinics in New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, or Connecticut. That is a business line Sorund expressly reserved in the base agreement.

Even if Sorund argues that §2.4's reservation was specific enough to survive, the ambiguity creates litigation risk and gives Bairuth a colorable claim that Sorund's direct reference-lab sales in the Territory are a breach of exclusivity.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a clarifying amendment or side letter that either (a) carves reference-laboratory testing services out of the "Connected Services" definition in the SOW, or (b) adds an express override to §2.4 stating that the Amendment does not alter the Reserved Services exclusion. If Sorund intends to let Bairuth resell reference-lab services (unlikely, given the operational model), that should be done deliberately with pricing and support terms, not by definitional accident.

**Fact to confirm:** What is Sorund's current reference-lab revenue in the four-state Territory? Is Sorund actively soliciting reference-lab customers there? This will inform how urgently the fix is needed and what fallback position to take.

### 3. Asymmetric Assignment Rights

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §15.1 (page 3).

**What the documents say:** "Supplier shall not assign or transfer this Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations under it, without Distributor's prior written consent. Distributor may assign this Agreement, or any of its rights under it, to any Affiliate, successor or third party upon written notice to Supplier."

**Why it matters for Sorund:** Sorund is locked in; Bairuth is not. Bairuth could assign the distribution rights to a direct competitor of Sorund's other channel partners, to a financially weaker entity, or to an Affiliate with different operational capabilities, and Sorund's only recourse is a material-breach claim under §12.3(a) (which requires 30-day cure). Combined with Issue 1 (Change of Control), the asymmetry means Sorund bears all the structural risk of ownership changes while Bairuth faces none.

**Recommended fix:** In the next amendment (ideally the one addressing Issues 1 and 2), seek to make assignment rights mutual: either both parties require consent, or at minimum restrict Distributor's assignment to Affiliates with equivalent financial and operational capability, with a right for Sorund to terminate if the assignee is unsuitable.

### 4. Press Release – Approval Under §8.2

**Where:** Bairuth press release (April 8, 2025); Distribution Agreement §8.2 (page 2).

**What the documents say:** §8.2 requires Distributor to "submit to Supplier for prior approval all marketing materials and press releases that use the Trademarks or refer to Supplier." The press release uses "Sorund Veterinary Diagnostics, Inc.," "Sorund," "Sorund VC-300," and "Sorund VC-120" throughout, includes a direct quote from Sorund's CEO (Margaret L. Ventris), and lists a Sorund media contact.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** If Sorund did not formally approve this release under §8.2, two problems arise: (a) Bairuth may argue that Sorund's participation in drafting the CEO quote and providing a media contact constitutes implied approval or waiver of the §8.2 right for this release; and (b) going forward, Sorund needs to be clear that §8.2 applies to all future releases, especially now that the Amendment adds SorundLink to the product line and the merger is pending. Conversely, if Sorund did approve, we should confirm the approval is documented (email, signed form) so there is no later dispute.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm internally whether §8.2 approval was given for the April 8 release. If it was, file the approval with the contract. If it was not, raise the point with Bairuth in the next commercial discussion (not as a breach claim, but as a process reminder) and implement a simple written-approval workflow for all future press releases and marketing materials.

**Fact to confirm:** Did Sorund's communications team or General Counsel approve the April 8 press release? Is there an email or other record of approval?

### 5. SorundLink Terms – Unilateral Update Right Creates Friction Risk

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §1.18, §2.4 (page 1).

**What the documents say:** "SorundLink Terms" means Supplier's standard end-user subscription terms "as Supplier publishes and updates them from time to time." Section 2.4 requires Distributor to ensure each Customer accepts the SorundLink Terms before activation and prohibits Distributor from making commitments inconsistent with those terms.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** Sorund has the unilateral right to update the terms, but the operational burden of getting Customer acceptance falls on Bairuth. If Sorund makes a material change (e.g., data-use provisions, liability limits, auto-renewal mechanics), Bairuth may push back on passing it through to existing or prospective Customers, creating a commercial dispute that the Agreement does not cleanly resolve. There is no mechanism for Sorund to enforce term updates on already-subscribed Customers through Bairuth.

**Recommended fix:** In the next amendment, add a provision clarifying that (a) Sorund may update the SorundLink Terms on reasonable notice to Distributor, (b) updates apply to renewals and new subscriptions automatically, and (c) for existing subscriptions, the updated terms apply at the next renewal unless the Customer opts out. Also consider giving Sorund the right to communicate directly with Customers about platform changes (security patches, service outages) without going through Bairuth.

### 6. Demonstration Units – 50% Buy Option Excluded from Minimums

**Where:** SOW §4.3 (page 1); Order Form §4.2(c) (page 1).

**What the documents say:** At the end of the Launch Period, Distributor may buy Demonstration Units at 50% of the Exhibit B price. Order Form §4.2(c) expressly excludes "any purchase of Demonstration Units under Section 4.3 of the Statement of Work" from the minimum purchase commitment measurement.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** With up to 12 demo units (6 VC-300s at $14,250 and 6 VC-120s at $8,900), the maximum value of units Bairuth could acquire at half price is $142,800 — and none of it counts toward the $2.4M annual minimum. More importantly, the 50% discount creates a below-market price point that Bairuth could reference in negotiations for regular inventory. The exclusion from minimums also means that if Bairuth relies heavily on demo-unit conversions, its effective purchase obligation is lower than the headline number suggests.

**Recommended fix:** This is a relatively minor dollar issue, but in the next commercial discussion Sorund should (a) confirm that the 50% price is a one-time launch incentive and not a precedent for future pricing, and (b) consider whether the exclusion from minimums should be capped or time-limited. If the Launch Period has already ended (it would have ended ~September 28, 2025), confirm whether Bairuth exercised the buy option and at what price.

**Fact to confirm:** Did Bairuth purchase any Demonstration Units at the 50% discount? If so, how many and at what price?

### 7. Ongoing Free SorundLink Demo Accounts – No Sunset

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 §4.2 (page 2).

**What the documents say:** "Supplier shall provide Distributor with up to ten (10) demonstration accounts on the SorundLink Platform without charge **for as long as this Amendment remains in effect**."

**Why it matters for Sorund:** Unlike the SOW (which is tied to the 180-day Launch Period), this obligation has no defined end date. It runs for the full Term, including any 5-year Renewal Terms. Ten free accounts is a modest cost, but the "as long as this Amendment remains in effect" language means Sorund cannot unilaterally stop providing them without amending or terminating. If the business model for SorundLink changes (e.g., per-analyzer pricing instead of per-location), the free accounts become more expensive.

**Recommended fix:** In the next amendment, tie the demo account obligation to a defined period (e.g., 24 months from the Amendment Effective Date) or to the number of active SorundLink subscriptions (e.g., "up to 10% of active subscription locations, capped at 10").

### 8. Limited Remedy for Minimum Purchase Shortfall

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §7.2 (page 2); Order Form §4.4 (page 1).

**What the documents say:** If Distributor misses the $2.4M annual minimum, Supplier's **sole remedy** is to (a) convert the appointment to non-exclusive for the remainder of the Term, or (b) terminate under §12.3(c). No monetary penalty, no buy-out of the shortfall, no specific performance.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** If Bairuth purchases $1.8M in a Contract Year (a 25% shortfall), Sorund's only options are to open the Territory to other distributors or end the relationship entirely. There is no way to recover the revenue gap. Given the 36-month Initial Term and 5-year auto-renewals, a chronic pattern of under-purchasing (e.g., $2.0M per year) would leave Sorund with an exclusive distributor who is not delivering the committed volume, and no financial recourse short of termination.

**Recommended fix:** In the next amendment, add a shortfall payment provision: if Distributor's purchases fall below the annual minimum, Distributor pays the difference (or a percentage of it) as a liquidated payment to Supplier, in addition to or in lieu of the non-exclusivity/termination remedies. Alternatively, add a "clawback" of the price-increase restriction in §5.2 for the following year if the shortfall exceeds a threshold.

### 9. Auto-Renewal – 5-Year Terms with Narrow Notice Window

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §12.2 (page 3); Order Form §2.2 (page 1).

**What the documents say:** After the 36-month Initial Term (expiring March 31, 2028), the Agreement auto-renews for successive **five-year** terms. Either Party must give non-renewal notice no earlier than 120 days and no later than 90 days before expiry of the then-current term.

**Why it matters for Sorund:** A 5-year auto-renewal is a long lock-in. The notice window (December 1, 2027 – January 30, 2028 for the first renewal) is narrow and easy to miss. If Sorund wants to exit or renegotiate at the end of the Initial Term, it must act in that specific 30-day window. Given the pending merger (Issue 1) and the reference-lab conflict (Issue 2), Sorund may want the flexibility to renegotiate at the March 2028 mark, but missing the window commits it to another 5 years.

**Recommended fix:** Calendar the notice window immediately (December 1, 2027 – January 30, 2028). If Sorund anticipates wanting to renegotiate, consider giving notice of non-renewal and then re-papering on new terms, rather than allowing the auto-renewal to trigger and then trying to amend.

### 10. Reagent Shelf-Life Requirement – 9 Months at Delivery

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §6.4 (page 2).

**What the documents say:** "At delivery, each reagent and consumable shall have at least nine (9) months of remaining shelf life."

**Why it matters for Sorund:** This is a hard operational constraint on Sorund's inventory management. If Sorund's manufacturing batch process yields reagents with, say, 18-month shelf life, Sorund must hold inventory for up to 9 months before shipping to ensure compliance. This ties up working capital and creates waste if batches expire before they can be shipped. The clause does not provide a cure period or a mechanism for Sorund to notify Bairuth of a shortfall before delivery.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm with Sorund's operations team whether 9 months is achievable given current batch sizes and shelf-life profiles. If it is tight, negotiate to reduce to 6 months or add a provision allowing Sorund to ship with less than 9 months' shelf life if it provides a corresponding price credit or extended warranty.

**Fact to confirm:** What is the typical total shelf life of Sorund's reagent rotors? Can operations consistently meet the 9-month-at-delivery requirement without excess inventory carrying costs?
