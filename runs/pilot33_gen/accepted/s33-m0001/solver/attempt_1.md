## Executive Summary

Review of the five documents in the matter folder reveals two issues requiring prompt attention: (1) the pending Ostrava merger will trigger Bairuth's termination and inventory-buyback rights under Section 15.3 unless Sorund obtains consent before closing, and (2) the Amendment's redefinition of "Products" to include "Connected Services" appears to override Sorund's reserved right to provide reference-laboratory testing directly, creating a direct conflict with Section 2.4. Several other structural imbalances warrant negotiation at the next amendment cycle.

## Issue 1 – PENDING MERGER TRIGGERS SECTION 15.3 TERMINATION AND INVENTORY-BUYBACK RIGHTS

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.3; Amendment No. 1, Recital C; Agreement § 1.3 (definition of "Change of Control").

**What the documents say:**

- § 1.3 defines "Change of Control" to include acquisition of more than 50% of the voting securities of Supplier **or of any parent of Supplier**.
- § 15.3 provides that a Change of Control of Supplier (including a parent) "shall be deemed an assignment requiring Distributor's prior written consent." If it occurs without consent, Distributor may (a) terminate within 180 days of learning of the Change of Control, or (b) require Supplier to repurchase all of Distributor's then-current inventory at Distributor's landed cost, payable within 30 days.
- Amendment No. 1, Recital C states that "Supplier has informed Distributor that its parent, Sorund Holdings, Inc., entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with Ostrava Animal Health plc on September 15, 2026. Closing of the merger is expected in the first quarter of 2027."
- The Amendment does **not** modify § 15.3, does **not** grant consent, and does **not** waive the consent requirement. Recital C is informational only.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

When the Ostrava merger closes (expected Q1 2027), a Change of Control of a parent of Supplier will have occurred. Unless Sorund obtains Bairuth's prior written consent before that date, Bairuth will have the unilateral right to either walk away from the entire distribution relationship or force Sorund to buy back all of Bairuth's inventory at landed cost. The inventory buyback could be material: at the $2.4M annual minimum, Bairuth could reasonably hold $600K–$1.2M of product at any given time (30-day inventory obligation under § 3.2 plus pipeline), plus the initial stocking order of $694,850 (Order Form § 5.1) if not yet turned over. "Landed cost" is not defined in the Agreement, creating additional exposure for freight, insurance, and handling charges.

Furthermore, if Bairuth terminates, Sorund loses its exclusive distribution channel in the four-state Territory with no transition period, no sell-off rights for Bairuth's customers, and no ability to reappoint a distributor quickly given the 30-day inventory and training obligations.

**Recommended fix:**

Sorund should seek Bairuth's written consent to the Change of Control **before** the Ostrava merger closes. This could be structured as a short side letter or Amendment No. 2. In exchange, Sorund could offer:
- A commitment to maintain the distribution relationship post-merger (e.g., no termination by Sorund for 12–18 months post-closing);
- Confirmation that Ostrava Animal Health plc (or its successor) will assume the Agreement;
- A cap on the inventory buyback if consent is later withdrawn.

**Prompt attention required.** The merger is expected to close in Q1 2027. Sorund should open this conversation with Bairuth immediately to allow time for negotiation and execution before closing.

**Fact to confirm with client:** Has Sorund discussed the § 15.3 consent issue with Bairuth beyond the Recital C disclosure? Is there any side correspondence (email, call notes) in which Bairuth indicated it would consent? What is the current inventory level at Bairuth?

## Issue 2 – AMENDMENT REDEFINITION OF "PRODUCTS" APPEARS TO ELIMINATE SORUND'S RESERVED REFERENCE-LABORATORY RIGHTS

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 1.1 (redefining "Products"); SOW § 1.3 (defining "Connected Services"); Distribution Agreement § 2.4 (Reserved Services); Agreement § 16.4 (Order of Precedence).

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 1.3 defines "Connected Services" as "all software, cloud reporting, telemedicine integrations **and reference laboratory testing services** offered by Supplier, whether provided directly by Supplier or through its Affiliates, together with any related onboarding, data connectivity and support services."
- Amendment § 1.1 redefines "Products" to include "the Connected Services and the SorundLink Platform."
- Agreement § 2.4 states: "Supplier reserves the right to provide reference-laboratory testing services, directly or through its Affiliates, to veterinary clinics and other customers inside and outside the Territory. Reference-laboratory testing services are **not Products**. They fall outside the scope of Distributor's appointment and exclusivity under this Section 2."
- Agreement § 16.4 provides the order of precedence: **Amendments** > Distribution Agreement > Order Form > Statement of Work.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

Under the order of precedence, the Amendment's definition of "Products" (which incorporates "Connected Services," defined in the SOW to include reference-laboratory testing services) overrides § 2.4's express statement that reference-lab services are "not Products." If reference-lab services are now "Products," then:

- § 2.2 (Exclusivity) prevents Sorund from selling reference-lab services directly to Customers in the Territory;
- § 2.4's reservation is effectively nullified;
- Sorund's reference-lab business in the four-state Territory is captured by the distribution arrangement, meaning Bairuth could argue it has exclusive rights to distribute those services.

This appears to be an inadvertent drafting consequence of using the SOW's broad "Connected Services" definition (which was written for a different purpose – to describe what Supplier personnel might discuss during training) rather than a tailored definition limited to the SorundLink Platform and software connectivity.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend § 1.1 of the Agreement (via Amendment No. 2 or a corrective amendment) to either:
- Narrow the definition of "Connected Services" as used in the "Products" definition to exclude reference-laboratory testing services (e.g., "Connected Services means the software, cloud reporting, and telemedicine integrations offered by Supplier, but excludes reference-laboratory testing services"); or
- Add an express carve-out: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, reference-laboratory testing services are not Products and remain reserved to Supplier under Section 2.4."

**Prompt attention required.** This should be addressed in the same amendment package as Issue 1. If the Ostrava merger triggers a consent negotiation, this is the natural moment to correct the definition.

**Fact to confirm with client:** What is the revenue from Sorund's reference-laboratory testing business in the four-state Territory? Are there existing direct reference-lab customers in the Territory that would be affected? Was the inclusion of "Connected Services" in the Products definition intentional or a drafting oversight?

## Issue 3 – ASYMMETRIC ASSIGNMENT RIGHTS FAVOR BAIRUTH

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.1.

**What the documents say:**

- Supplier "shall not assign or transfer this Agreement, or any of its rights or obligations under it, without Distributor's prior written consent."
- Distributor "may assign this Agreement, or any of its rights under it, to any Affiliate, successor or third party upon written notice to Supplier."

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

Bairuth can transfer the entire distribution relationship – including the exclusive appointment, the trademark license, and the minimum purchase obligation – to any third party (including a competitor of Sorund or an entity with inferior capabilities) on notice alone. Sorund has no consent right, no right to object, and no termination trigger if the assignment is to an unsuitable party. This is particularly concerning because:

- § 3.5 (Competing Products) restricts Distributor from distributing competing analyzers, but if Bairuth assigns to a third party that distributes competing products, Sorund's remedy is unclear;
- The quality of distribution (sales force, training, customer support under § 3.1–3.3) is critical to Sorund's brand, and a weak assignee could damage it;
- Combined with Issue 1, if the Ostrava merger is consented to, Sorund may feel locked in with a counterparty it cannot control.

**Recommended fix:**

Negotiate a mutual consent standard: Distributor may assign only to an Affiliate or to a successor in a Change of Control of Distributor, and in each case with Supplier's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld). Alternatively, add a termination right for Sorund if Distributor assigns to a party that is in breach of § 3.5 or that fails to meet minimum capability standards.

**Fact to confirm with client:** Does Sorund have any commercial concern about who might acquire Bairuth or to whom Bairuth might assign? Is there a specific competitive threat in mind?

## Issue 4 – FIVE-YEAR AUTO-RENEWAL WITH NARROW NOTICE WINDOW

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 12.2; Order Form § 2.1 (Initial Term = 36 months).

**What the documents say:**

- The Initial Term runs from April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2028.
- On expiry, the Agreement "shall automatically renew for successive five (5) year Renewal Terms" unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal "no earlier than one hundred twenty (120) days and no later than ninety (90) days before expiry."
- The notice window for the first renewal is therefore **December 1, 2027 through January 30, 2028**.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

If Sorund misses the narrow ~2-month window, it is locked in for five additional years (through March 2033) on the same terms. Given that:
- the Ostrava merger will have closed by then (Q1 2027);
- the market for veterinary point-of-care diagnostics may have shifted;
- Sorund may want to restructure its distribution model post-merger;

a missed notice could be very costly. Moreover, the auto-renewal is at "the prices set out in Exhibit B then in effect" – it does not provide for price renegotiation at renewal, only the § 5.2 mechanism (one 4% increase per Contract Year).

**Recommended fix:**

- Calendar the notice window immediately (December 1, 2027 – January 30, 2028) and assign internal ownership.
- In the next amendment (see Issue 1), consider negotiating: (a) a longer notice window (e.g., 180–120 days); (b) a price reset or renegotiation right at each renewal; or (c) a mutual option to renew for a shorter period (e.g., 2 years) rather than 5.

**Fact to confirm with client:** Does Sorund currently intend to renew beyond the Initial Term, or is it evaluating alternatives (e.g., direct sales, a different distributor, or a post-merger restructuring)?

## Issue 5 – NO MONETARY REMEDY FOR MINIMUM PURCHASE SHORTFALL

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 7.2; Order Form § 4.1 ($2.4M annual minimum); Order Form § 4.3 (quarterly targets are not separate commitments).

**What the documents say:**

If Distributor fails to meet the $2.4M annual minimum, Supplier's **sole remedy** is, on written notice within 60 days after the Contract Year ends, to either (a) convert the appointment to non-exclusive for the remainder of the Term, or (b) terminate under § 12.3(c). There is no liquidated damages, no shortfall payment, and no right to the difference between the minimum and actual purchases.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

- The 60-day exercise window is short and runs from the end of the Contract Year (i.e., Sorund has until ~June 1 to act on a shortfall for the year ending March 31).
- Converting to non-exclusive may not be commercially useful if Sorund does not have its own direct sales capability in the Territory (it currently relies on the house accounts only).
- Termination is nuclear – it ends the relationship and triggers the § 12.4/12.5 wind-down.
- There is no intermediate remedy (e.g., a buy-out of the shortfall at a discount, or a reduction in the Territory).

**Recommended fix:**

In the next amendment, negotiate a shortfall payment: if Distributor's purchases fall below the minimum by more than a de minimis threshold (e.g., 10%), Distributor pays Supplier the difference between actual and minimum purchases at a discounted rate (e.g., 50% of the shortfall). This gives Sorund a monetary remedy without forcing the all-or-nothing choice.

**Fact to confirm with client:** What has Bairuth's actual purchase trajectory been to date? Are there any indicators it will miss the $2.4M in the current or next Contract Year? Does Sorund have internal direct-sales capability in the Territory that would make the non-exclusive conversion a viable fallback?

## Issue 6 – PRESS RELEASE PUBLISHED WITHOUT DOCUMENTED APPROVAL

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 8.2; Bairuth Press Release (dated April 8, 2025).

**What the documents say:**

- § 8.2: "Distributor shall submit to Supplier for prior approval all marketing materials and press releases that use the Trademarks or refer to Supplier."
- The Bairuth press release refers to Sorund by name, quotes its CEO (Margaret L. Ventris), and describes the partnership. It was published on April 8, 2025, one week after the Agreement took effect.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

The quote from Ms. Ventris suggests Sorund was substantively involved in the release, which would likely establish approval as a matter of course. However, there is no documented approval in the file. If the release contained any inaccurate statement (e.g., overpromising on product capabilities or timeline), Sorund could face reputational or regulatory exposure without a clear record that it approved the language. This is a governance hygiene issue rather than a substantive legal risk, but it sets a precedent for future marketing materials.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm with the client that the press release was reviewed and approved by Sorund's communications team before publication.
- Going forward, implement a simple written approval process (email is sufficient under the Agreement's notice provisions) for any Bairuth marketing material that references Sorund.

**Fact to confirm with client:** Was the press release formally approved by Sorund before publication? If so, is the approval documented (email, sign-off sheet)?

## Issue 7 – DEMONSTRATION UNIT BUYOUT AT 50% DISCOUNT, EXCLUDED FROM MINIMUM PURCHASE

**Where:** SOW § 4.3; Order Form § 4.2(c); Agreement Exhibit B.

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 4.3: Within 30 days after the Launch Period ends, Distributor may buy Demonstration Units "at fifty percent (50%) of the Exhibit B price, at Distributor's election."
- Order Form § 4.2(c): Purchases of Demonstration Units under SOW § 4.3 are **excluded** from the minimum purchase commitment measurement.

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

Sorund is giving a 50% discount on up to 12 analyzers (6 VC-300s at $14,250 + 6 VC-120s at $8,900 = $141,300 list value, so ~$70,650 in discounts) and those units do not count toward the $2.4M minimum. The Launch Period ended approximately September 28, 2025 (180 days from April 1, 2025), so the buyout election window (30 days after) closed around October 28, 2025.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm whether Bairuth elected to buy the demo units and, if so, at what price. If the buyout occurred, ensure it was invoiced correctly and that the exclusion from the minimum was applied.
- For any future SOWs or launch programs, negotiate that demo unit buyouts count toward the minimum (even at the discounted price) or that the discount is limited to a smaller percentage.

**Fact to confirm with client:** Did Bairuth elect to purchase the Demonstration Units? If so, how many and at what price? Were they invoiced and paid?

## Issue 8 – SORUNDLINK FREE DEMONSTRATION ACCOUNTS ARE OPEN-ENDED

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 4.2.

**What the documents say:**

"Supplier shall provide Distributor with up to ten (10) demonstration accounts on the SorundLink Platform without charge for as long as this Amendment remains in effect."

**Why it matters for Sorund:**

Given the 5-year auto-renewal (Issue 4), "as long as this Amendment remains in effect" could mean 13+ years (36-month Initial Term + 5-year Renewal Term + potential further renewals). Ten free SaaS accounts is a modest cost, but the open-ended duration is inconsistent with the time-limited nature of the other launch support (the SOW Launch Period was 180 days).

**Recommended fix:**

In the next amendment, tie the free demonstration accounts to a defined period (e.g., the first 12 months after the Amendment Effective Date, or the duration of the Initial Term) and require Distributor to pay the Exhibit B price thereafter.
