## Issues Ranked by Significance

### 1. CRITICAL – Amendment No. 1 Inadvertently Makes Reference-Lab Testing a "Product" Subject to Exclusivity

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 1.1 (replacing Agreement § 1.9); SOW § 1.3 (definition of "Connected Services"); Agreement § 2.4 (Reserved Services); Agreement § 16.4 (Order of Precedence).

**The problem.** Amendment No. 1 § 1.1 redefines "Products" to include "the Connected Services and the SorundLink Platform." The term "Connected Services" is not defined in the Amendment; it takes its meaning from SOW § 1.3, which defines Connected Services as "all software, cloud reporting, telemedicine integrations **and reference laboratory testing services** offered by Supplier, whether provided directly by Supplier or through its Affiliates, together with any related onboarding, data connectivity and support services."

Agreement § 2.4 expressly reserves Sorund's right to provide reference-laboratory testing directly and states: "Reference-laboratory testing services are not Products. They fall outside the scope of Distributor's appointment and exclusivity under this Section 2."

Under the order of precedence (Agreement § 16.4), the Amendment controls over the Distribution Agreement. Read together, the Amendment's expanded Products definition overrides § 2.4. The practical effect is that reference-laboratory testing services in the four-state Territory would now fall within Bairuth's exclusive distribution rights, and Sorund's continued direct provision of those services would breach the exclusivity grant in § 2.2.

**Why it matters.** Sorund's reference-lab business is a core revenue stream (Recital A). If § 2.4 is effectively nullified, Sorund either (a) must stop providing reference-lab testing directly to clinics in NY, NJ, PA and CT, or (b) is in ongoing breach of the exclusivity clause, exposing it to a termination-for-cause claim under § 12.3(a) and potentially to damages (subject to the § 11.2 cap).

**Recommended fix.** Amend the definition in Amendment No. 1 § 1.1 to carve out reference-laboratory testing services, mirroring the language in § 2.4. For example: "…and includes the SorundLink Platform, but excludes reference-laboratory testing services as described in Section 2.4." Alternatively, add a new sentence to § 2.4 confirming it survives the Amendment. This should be raised with Bairuth immediately and documented in a further amendment or side letter before the Ostrava closing.

**Facts to confirm with client:** Whether Sorund has been providing reference-lab testing directly to customers in the Territory since October 1, 2026 (the Amendment Effective Date), and if so, at what volume.

### 2. CRITICAL – Ostrava Merger Triggers Change of Control Rights; No Consent in Place

**Where:** Agreement § 15.3 (Change of Control of Supplier); Agreement § 1.3 (definition of "Change of Control"); Amendment No. 1, Recital C (disclosure of merger).

**The problem.** Amendment No. 1 Recital C discloses that Sorund's parent, Sorund Holdings, Inc., signed a merger agreement with Ostrava Animal Health plc on September 15, 2026, with closing expected in Q1 2027. The Amendment was executed October 1, 2026—sixteen days later—but it does not obtain Bairuth's consent to the Change of Control, waive the repurchase right, or modify § 15.3 in any respect.

Section 15.3 provides that a Change of Control of Supplier (including any parent) "shall be deemed an assignment requiring Distributor's prior written consent." If the CoC occurs without consent, Bairuth may:

- (a) terminate the Agreement by notice within 180 days after it learns of the CoC; **or**
- (b) require Sorund to repurchase all of Bairuth's then-current inventory at **Distributor's landed cost**, payable within 30 days of receiving an inventory statement.

Because the merger has not yet closed, the CoC has not yet occurred. Consent must be obtained **before** closing to avoid triggering these rights.

**Why it matters.** This is Sorund's most immediate financial and commercial exposure. If Bairuth demands an inventory repurchase, the cost could be substantial. At the $2.4M annual minimum (Order Form § 4.1), Bairuth likely carries several hundred thousand dollars of inventory at any given time; "landed cost" adds freight and handling on top of invoice price. The 30-day payment window creates cash-flow pressure. Alternatively, if Bairuth terminates, Sorund loses its exclusive distribution channel in the four-state Territory at the worst possible moment (mid-merger integration).

**Recommended fix.**

- **Before Ostrava closing:** Negotiate and document Bairuth's written consent to the Change of Control under § 15.3. Leverage the fact that the Amendment was signed with full knowledge of the pending merger (Recital C) to argue that consent was implicitly part of the commercial deal, but do not rely on that—get it in writing.
- **In the consent negotiation:** Seek a waiver of the inventory-repurchase right, or at minimum a cap on the repurchase amount and an extended payment period (e.g., 90 days rather than 30).
- **If consent is withheld:** Model the financial impact of both the termination scenario and the repurchase scenario. Assess whether Sorund can absorb the inventory buyback or whether it needs to arrange bridge financing.

**Facts to confirm with client:**

- Current expected closing date for the Ostrava merger and whether it remains on track for Q1 2027.
- Whether any discussions about § 15.3 consent have taken place with Bairuth since the Amendment was signed.
- Bairuth's current inventory levels (Sorund may have visibility through quarterly reports under § 3.4).
- Whether Sorund has quantified the potential repurchase exposure.

### 3. HIGH – Asymmetric Assignment Rights Give Bairuth Unilateral Flexibility

**Where:** Agreement § 15.1.

**The problem.** Section 15.1 provides:

- **Supplier** shall not assign without Distributor's **prior written consent**.
- **Distributor** may assign to "any Affiliate, successor or third party upon **written notice** to Supplier."

Sorund is locked in; Bairuth is not. Bairuth can transfer the entire distribution relationship—including the exclusive appointment, the trademark license, the minimum purchase obligation, and the SorundLink resale rights—to any entity it chooses, with nothing more than notice.

**Why it matters.** In the context of the Ostrava merger, this asymmetry gives Bairuth significant negotiating leverage. Sorund cannot restructure, sell a product line, or move the agreement to an affiliate without Bairuth's consent, while Bairuth can do the reverse freely. If Bairuth assigns to a competitor of Sorund (or to an entity with competing interests), Sorund would be contractually bound to supply that entity exclusively in the Territory. The non-compete in § 3.5 would bind the assignee, but the commercial relationship would be with an unfamiliar and potentially adversarial counterparty.

**Recommended fix.** In the same negotiation as the § 15.3 consent (Issue 2), seek to tighten § 15.1 to require Sorund's consent for any assignment by Bairuth, or at minimum to prohibit assignment to a direct competitor of Sorund. If Bairuth resists, accept a "no assignment to a competitor" carve-out as a floor.

### 4. MEDIUM – Press Release Creates Public Commitment Beyond the Agreement

**Where:** Bairuth Press Release § 3.1 (Oyelaran quote); Agreement § 8.2 (approval requirement); Agreement § 3.2 (inventory obligation).

**The problem.** The press release quote from Bairuth's Managing Member states the partnership is "backed by Bairuth's local service and **next-day reagent supply**." The Agreement requires Bairuth to maintain 30 days of inventory (§ 3.2) but does not obligate next-day delivery. The press release was issued April 8, 2025, one week after the Effective Date.

**Why it matters for Sorund.** Two concerns:

- **Approval.** Section 8.2 requires Bairuth to submit all press releases that "refer to Supplier" for Sorund's prior approval. The release names Sorund, uses its product names, and includes a Sorund CEO quote. If Sorund did not formally approve it, Bairuth is in (technical) breach. If Sorund did approve, the "next-day reagent supply" language is now a public representation that customers may rely on. If supply-chain disruptions prevent next-day delivery, customers may look to Sorund as the manufacturer, creating reputational risk and potential support burden.
- **No SorundLink mention.** The release describes the product line without SorundLink (understandable, as it predated the Amendment). If SorundLink is now a "Product," the public description of the partnership is incomplete, though this is a minor point.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm whether Sorund approved the release under § 8.2. If yes, no action needed beyond awareness. If no, note the breach but do not escalate given the circumstances (Sorund's CEO is quoted, suggesting involvement). Going forward, ensure all Bairuth marketing materials referencing Sorund are routed through § 8.2 approval.

**Facts to confirm with client:** Did Sorund's communications team or General Counsel approve the April 8, 2025 press release? Was the "next-day reagent supply" language specifically reviewed?

### 5. MEDIUM – Five-Year Auto-Renewal: Notice Window Opens December 2027

**Where:** Agreement § 12.2; Order Form § 2.1.

**The problem.** The Initial Term is 36 months, ending March 31, 2028. The Agreement then auto-renews for successive **five-year** terms unless either party gives non-renewal notice between 120 and 90 days before expiry—that is, between **December 1, 2027** and **January 30, 2028**.

**Why it matters.** If the Ostrava merger closes in Q1 2027 and the relationship continues (i.e., Sorund obtains consent and Bairuth does not terminate), Sorund will face a decision in late 2027 about whether to lock itself into another five years. Post-merger, Sorund's strategy for the Territory may be uncertain. Missing the notice window means a five-year commitment with limited exit rights (only for-cause termination under § 12.3).

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the notice window now (December 1 – January 30, 2027). Build the renewal decision into the post-merger integration plan. If Sorund wants flexibility, consider negotiating a shorter renewal term (e.g., one or two years instead of five) as part of the § 15.3 consent discussion.

### 6. MEDIUM – SorundLink Warranty Tied to Undefined "Published Documentation"

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 3.4.

**The problem.** The platform warranty states Sorund warrants the SorundLink Platform "will perform materially in accordance with its **published documentation** during each subscription period." The term "published documentation" is not defined anywhere in the deal documents. It could encompass user guides, API documentation, marketing materials, release notes, or internal specifications that happen to be shared.

**Why it matters.** In a warranty dispute, Bairuth (or a Customer looking through) could argue that any document Sorund has made available describes required functionality. The "materially" qualifier helps, but the undefined baseline creates uncertainty. The remedy (correct or refund unused portion) is limited and favorable to Sorund, so the financial exposure is bounded, but the definitional gap could generate disputes and support burden.

**Recommended fix.** In the next amendment (which will be needed anyway to fix Issue 1), add a definition of "published documentation" limited to specific documents (e.g., "the SorundLink User Guide and API Reference as posted on Sorund's customer portal on the subscription start date"). Alternatively, state that the warranty is measured against the documentation version in effect at the start of the applicable subscription period.

### 7. LOW – Demonstration Unit Return/Buyback Deadline Has Passed

**Where:** SOW § 4.3; Order Form § 4.2(c).

**The problem.** The Launch Period was 180 days from April 1, 2025, ending approximately September 28, 2025. SOW § 4.3 required Bairuth to either return the demo units (at Sorund's cost) or purchase them at 50% of Exhibit B price within 30 days after the Launch Period ended—i.e., by approximately October 28, 2025. That deadline has passed.

Additionally, Order Form § 4.2(c) excludes any purchase of Demonstration Units from the minimum purchase measurement, so even if Bairuth bought them, the discounted revenue does not count toward the $2.4M commitment.

**Why it matters.** If Bairuth neither returned nor purchased the units, the units are sitting at Bairuth's facility with title still in Sorund (SOW § 4.2). Sorund should confirm the units' status and, if not returned, issue a reminder or demand return. The financial exposure is modest (up to 12 analyzers at 50% of $14,250/$8,900), but the units represent working capital and could be at risk if the relationship deteriorates.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm with the client what happened. If the units were not returned or purchased, send a polite but firm reminder citing SOW § 4.3.

**Facts to confirm with client:** Did Bairuth return the demonstration units or elect to purchase them by the October 28, 2025 deadline? If purchased, at what price and were they invoiced?

### 8. LOW – SorundLink Terms Allow Unilateral Modification

**Where:** Amendment No. 1 § 1.18 (definition of "SorundLink Terms"); § 2.4 (End-User Terms).

**The problem.** "SorundLink Terms" are defined as Sorund's standard terms "as Supplier publishes and updates them from time to time." This gives Sorund a unilateral right to modify the end-user subscription terms at any time. Section 2.4 requires Bairuth to ensure each Customer accepts the current SorundLink Terms before activation and prohibits Bairuth from making commitments inconsistent with them.

**Why it matters for Sorund.** Operationally, this is favorable—Sorund can update terms without renegotiating the Agreement. The risk is relational: if Sorund makes a change that Customers perceive as adverse (e.g., adding data-use permissions, changing termination rights, or modifying support levels), Bairuth could face customer pushback and look to Sorund as the source. This could strain the distribution relationship, particularly during the sensitive period surrounding the Ostrava merger.

**Recommended fix.** No contractual change needed. As a practical matter, give Bairuth advance notice (e.g., 30 days) of any material changes to the SorundLink Terms, even though the contract does not require it. This is a relationship-management recommendation, not a legal one.
