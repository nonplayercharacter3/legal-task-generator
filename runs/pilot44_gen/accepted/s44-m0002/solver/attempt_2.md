## Executive Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (the "SPA"), the Escrow Agreement, the Talin Board Consent, the IP Assignment Agreement, and the prior draft of the Escrow Agreement. The package is substantially complete but contains two critical issues that should be resolved before we go back to the other side: (1) a significant scope gap between the SPA's definition of "Company IP" and what the IP Assignment Agreement actually conveys, and (2) a 120-day survival period for Seller's representations and warranties that is far too short for an $84 million acquisition of a festival business whose key asset (the 2027 Lanternfall Festival) occurs roughly eight and a half months after the expected Closing Date. Several other issues and open questions are addressed below.

## I. CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. IP Assignment Agreement Conveys Only a Subset of "Company IP"

**Documents:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Company IP"); IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Company IP") and Exhibit A; SPA § 11.9 (order of precedence).

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Company IP" broadly to include:

> (a) the LANTERNFALL marks and all other trademarks, service marks, logos and trade dress used in connection with the Lanternfall Festival;
> (b) the Company's fan contact database; and
> (c) the Company's ticketing software.

*(SPA § 1.1, "Company IP")*

The IP Assignment Agreement, however, redefines "Company IP" as:

> "solely the registered trademarks and service marks set out in Exhibit A, together with all registrations and pending applications for them."

*(IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1)*

Exhibit A lists six USPTO/CIPO trademark registrations and one pending application. It does **not** include:

- Trade dress (expressly referenced in the SPA definition);
- The fan contact database;
- The ticketing software;
- Unregistered trademarks, trade names, or domain names;
- Social media accounts;
- Copyrights;
- Trade secrets or know-how.

SPA § 11.9 provides that "the IP Assignment Agreement shall govern the conveyance of Intellectual Property." This means the narrow definition in the IP Assignment Agreement controls what is actually assigned. The practical result is that Talin receives a representation in SPA § 4.10 that "the Company owns or has a valid right to use all Company IP" (using the SPA's broad definition), but the IP Assignment Agreement only conveys the six registered marks and one pending application held by Nasund Brands LLC.

**Why it matters for Talin.** The fan contact database and ticketing software are likely material to the business. If either is held by Nasund Brands LLC (or another Seller affiliate) rather than by the Company itself, Talin will not receive it at Closing. Even if the Company already holds those assets, the gap creates ambiguity about what Talin is actually acquiring and weakens the indemnification backstop: a claim that the database or software was not properly conveyed would fall outside the IP Assignment Agreement's scope and could be disputed.

**Recommendation.**

- Confirm through diligence whether the fan contact database and ticketing software are owned by the Company or by Nasund Brands LLC (or another affiliate).
- If held by Nasund Brands LLC, expand the IP Assignment Agreement's definition of "Company IP" and Exhibit A to include them, or add a separate assignment schedule.
- At minimum, add the following to the IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 definition:

> "Company IP" means all Intellectual Property used in the business of Assignee, including (a) the trademarks, service marks, logos and trade dress listed in Exhibit A and all other trademarks, service marks, logos and trade dress used in connection with the Lanternfall Festival; (b) Assignee's fan contact database; (c) Assignee's ticketing software; and (d) all domain names, social media accounts, copyrights, trade secrets and know-how used in Assignee's business, together with all registrations and applications for any of the foregoing.

- If the database and software are already owned by the Company, add a specific representation in the IP Assignment Agreement (or rely on SPA § 4.10) confirming that Nasund Brands LLC holds no interest in them, and confirm that no license or other arrangement with Nasund Brands LLC is required for the Company's use.

### 2. Survival Period of 120 Days Is Inadequate

**Documents:** SPA § 9.1; Escrow Agreement § 4.1; SPA § 9.4 (claims procedure).

**Issue.** SPA § 9.1 provides that Seller's representations and warranties "shall survive the Closing until the Escrow Release Date." The Escrow Agreement § 4.1 sets the Escrow Release Date at **120 days after the Closing Date**. With a December 1, 2026 Closing, the survival period expires on or about **March 31, 2027**.

**Why it matters for Talin.**

- The 2027 Lanternfall Festival is held in **August 2027** (Board Consent § 1.2). Talin will not know whether the festival's talent agreements, venue arrangements, permits, and operational readiness are as represented until the festival actually occurs or is substantially prepared—well after the survival period has lapsed.
- The first post-closing audit of Keelhaven will not be conducted until after the fiscal year ending **June 30, 2027** (Board Consent § 1.5(d)), again after the survival period.
- The working capital adjustment calculation is due within 90 days (SPA § 2.4(b)), which fits within the 120-day window, but that is a separate mechanism from the indemnity for breach of reps.
- SPA § 9.4 provides that a claim for which a Claim Notice is not delivered before the survival period expires is "irrevocably waived." This is a hard deadline with no cure period.

A 120-day survival period is well below market for a transaction of this size. Typical survival periods for general representations in middle-market deals are 18 to 24 months, with fundamental reps (organization, capitalization, authority) and tax reps surviving until the applicable statute of limitations.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a tiered survival structure. Proposed language for SPA § 9.1:

> The representations and warranties of Seller contained in Section 4.1 (Organization and Authority), Section 4.2 (Organization of the Company), Section 4.3 (Capitalization), and Section 4.4 (No Conflicts; Consents) shall survive the Closing until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations. The representations and warranties of Seller contained in Section 4.13 (Taxes) shall survive the Closing until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations plus 60 days. All other representations and warranties of Seller shall survive the Closing until the first anniversary of the Closing Date.

- Correspondingly, amend Escrow Agreement § 4.1 to set the Escrow Release Date at the later of (a) 12 months after the Closing Date or (b) the date on which all claims for which the survival period has not yet expired have been resolved. Alternatively, split the escrow: release 50% at 120 days (to cover the working capital adjustment and early claims) and hold the remaining 50% until 18 months.

- If Seller resists extending survival beyond 12 months, consider at minimum extending it to 18 months and adding a specific representation that all permits, licenses, and venue agreements necessary for the 2027 Lanternfall Festival are in place and in full force, with survival through the festival date.

## II. HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 3. Ambiguity in Closing Condition Regarding IP Assignment

**Documents:** SPA § 7.2(d); IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 and Exhibit A; SPA § 11.9.

**Issue.** SPA § 7.2(d) makes it a condition to Buyer's obligation to close that "Nasund Brands LLC has executed and delivered the IP Assignment Agreement, assigning **all Company IP** to the Company." The phrase "all Company IP" uses the SPA's broad definition (which includes the database and software). But the IP Assignment Agreement only assigns the narrow set in Exhibit A.

Under SPA § 11.9, the IP Assignment Agreement "shall govern the conveyance of Intellectual Property." One could argue that the condition is satisfied if the IP Assignment Agreement is delivered, because that agreement defines the scope of the conveyance. But the condition's plain text says "all Company IP," which is broader.

**Why it matters for Talin.** If Seller delivers the IP Assignment Agreement with only the six marks in Exhibit A, Seller could argue the condition is met. Talin would then be obligated to close without the database or software (if they are held by Nasund Brands LLC). This creates a leverage problem at Closing.

**Recommendation.**

- Amend SPA § 7.2(d) to read: "Nasund Brands LLC has executed and delivered the IP Assignment Agreement, assigning all Intellectual Property held by Nasund Brands LLC that is used in the business of the Company to the Company."
- Alternatively, if the database and software are confirmed to be owned by the Company (not Nasund Brands LLC), the condition as drafted is technically satisfied, but the IP scope gap in Issue 1 should still be addressed to avoid post-closing disputes.

### 4. Non-Compete Is Narrower Than the Business Risk Warrants

**Documents:** SPA § 6.6.

**Issue.** The non-compete prohibits Seller and its Affiliates from "promot[ing] or produ[c]ing any multi-day music festival within 250 miles of the Lanternfall Festival site" for three years.

Gaps:

- **Single-day concerts and other events** are not covered. A Nasund affiliate could promote a competing single-day music event at the same venue.
- **Non-music festivals** (e.g., food, arts, or general entertainment festivals) are not covered.
- **The 250-mile radius** may be too narrow if the festival draws from a broader regional or national audience.
- **Individual principals** of Seller are not bound—only Seller and its Affiliates. If the managing members of Nasund Partners LLC form a new entity that is not technically an "Affiliate" (e.g., after divesting their interest in Nasund Partners), they could compete.

**Why it matters for Talin.** The Lanternfall Festival is the core asset. If the Seller's principals can launch a competing festival or concert series under a new entity, Talin's $84 million investment is at risk.

**Recommendation.**

- Expand the restricted activities to include "promote, produce, book, or manage any music festival (whether multi-day or single-day), music concert series, or similar live entertainment event."
- Add the managing members of Seller (by name) as restricted persons, or add a covenant that Seller shall cause its managing members to be bound by the same restriction.
- Consider expanding the radius to 300 or 500 miles, depending on the festival's draw region.
- Proposed replacement language:

> For three years after the Closing Date, Seller, its Affiliates, and each of [named managing members of Seller] shall not, directly or indirectly, promote, produce, book, manage or invest in any multi-day music festival, music concert series of three or more dates, or similar live entertainment event within 300 miles of the Lanternfall Festival site.

### 5. Disclosure Schedule Not in the Folder

**Documents:** SPA § 1.1 (definition); referenced throughout Article 4.

**Issue.** The SPA is riddled with "except as set out in Section [X] of the Disclosure Schedule" qualifiers. Without the Disclosure Schedule, we cannot assess the scope of exceptions to Seller's representations. In particular:

- § 4.4 (No Conflicts; Consents) — what consents are required?
- § 4.8 (Material Contracts) — what contracts are listed? Are there any with change-of-control provisions?
- § 4.9 (Talent Agreements) — what are the actual guarantees and deposit terms for post-closing shows?
- § 4.10 (Intellectual Property) — what IP is listed as registered vs. unregistered?

**Recommendation.** Request the complete Disclosure Schedule immediately. It is essential to evaluating the quality of Seller's representations and to identifying post-closing risks (particularly the magnitude of Post-Closing Show Liabilities under SPA § 9.3(b)).

## III. MODERATE-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 6. Board Consent Language Re: IP Assignment Acceptance

**Documents:** Board Consent § 3.2; IP Assignment Agreement § 2.1.

**Issue.** Board Consent § 3.2 authorizes the officers to "cause Keelhaven, **after the closing**, to accept and perform the IP Assignment Agreement as assignee." However, the IP Assignment Agreement § 2.1 provides that the assignment is effective "immediately before the Closing," and the SPA § 3.2(c) requires delivery of the executed IP Assignment Agreement **at** the Closing. Keelhaven's acceptance occurs at or before Closing, not after.

**Why it matters.** This is a minor drafting inconsistency. If a strict reading were applied, Keelhaven's acceptance at Closing would not be covered by the board authorization. In practice, this is unlikely to be an issue because § 6.1 of the Board Consent grants broad general authority, but it should be cleaned up to avoid any argument about corporate authority.

**Recommendation.** Amend Board Consent § 3.2 to read: "the Authorized Officers are each authorized to cause Keelhaven, at or after the closing, to accept and perform the IP Assignment Agreement as assignee."

---

### 7. Post-Closing Show Liabilities — Uncapped Buyer Indemnity

**Documents:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Post-Closing Show Liabilities"); SPA § 9.3(b); SPA § 9.5(a).

**Issue.** SPA § 9.3(b) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller for all Post-Closing Show Liabilities. This indemnity is **not** subject to the cap in § 9.5(a) (which caps only § 9.3(a) claims at the Escrow Amount). The definition of Post-Closing Show Liabilities is broad: "all Liabilities and obligations under the Talent Agreements in respect of Shows scheduled to occur after the Closing Date, including artist guarantees, deposits, advances, bonuses, rider and production obligations, and settlement obligations, **whether arising before or after the Closing Date**."

**Why it matters for Talin.** This is effectively Talin's own business risk for post-closing shows, and the uncapped nature is appropriate in that sense. However, the "whether arising before or after the Closing Date" language means that if a Talent Agreement signed before Closing (and thus potentially subject to Seller's pre-closing conduct) gives rise to a claim after Closing, Talin bears the economic loss. The practical exposure depends entirely on the Talent Agreements listed in the Disclosure Schedule (which we do not have). If the 2027 Lanternfall Festival has large artist guarantees (e.g., $5M+ headliners), Talin's uncapped exposure could be substantial if the festival underperforms.

**Recommendation.**

- Review the Disclosure Schedule (when received) to quantify the total Post-Closing Show Liabilities exposure.
- Consider adding a provision that if a Post-Closing Show Liability arises from a breach of a Talent Agreement by the Company **before** the Closing Date (i.e., a pre-closing act or omission), Seller remains liable. Proposed language:

> Notwithstanding the foregoing, Seller shall remain liable for any Post-Closing Show Liability to the extent arising from a breach of a Talent Agreement by the Company or any of its officers or employees occurring before the Closing Date.

- Ensure the integration plan accounts for the timing of artist payments and guarantees relative to the festival date.

### 8. Escrow Agent Fee Split

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement § 6.8; Schedule A.

**Issue.** The Escrow Agent's fees (approximately $8,500 in year one: $3,500 acceptance + $5,000 annual admin) are split 50/50 between Buyer and Seller. Talin's share is approximately $4,250 in year one.

**Why it matters.** This is a minor economic point. However, the "annual administration fee" continues "on each anniversary while any portion of the Escrow Fund is held." If claims remain unresolved past 120 days (which is likely given the short survival period), the fee continues to accrue and is still split 50/50.

**Recommendation.** Acceptable as drafted. If we negotiate a longer escrow period (see Issue 2), confirm that the annual fee does not increase.
