## Executive Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA), the Escrow Agreement, the IP Assignment Agreement, the Talin Board Consent, and the prior draft of the Escrow Agreement. The deal package has several material issues that require attention before we go back to the other side. The most significant is a critical gap between the SPA's definition of "Company IP" and the scope of the IP Assignment Agreement, which means Talin may not be receiving the fan contact database or ticketing software that the SPA represents are part of the business. The survival period is also unusually short at 120 days, and there are several other issues detailed below.

## Issue 1: IP Assignment Agreement Scope Is Far Narrower Than the SPA's Definition of Company IP

**Documents:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Company IP"); IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Company IP") and Exhibit A; SPA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence)

**The problem.** The SPA defines "Company IP" to include three categories:

- (a) the LANTERNFALL marks and all other trademarks, service marks, logos and trade dress used in connection with the Lanternfall Festival;
- (b) the Company's fan contact database; and
- (c) the Company's ticketing software.

The IP Assignment Agreement, however, defines "Company IP" as "solely the registered trademarks and service marks set out in Exhibit A, together with all registrations and pending applications for them." Exhibit A lists six registered marks and one pending application. The fan contact database and the ticketing software are not in Exhibit A and are not covered by the IP Assignment Agreement's definition.

SPA § 11.9 provides that in the event of a conflict between the SPA and an Ancillary Agreement, the SPA prevails, **except** that "the IP Assignment Agreement shall govern the conveyance of Intellectual Property." This means the narrower definition in the IP Assignment Agreement controls the actual transfer of IP.

**Why it matters for Talin.** Talin is paying $84 million for a company whose value is substantially derived from the Lanternfall Festival brand, its fan database, and its ticketing platform. If Nasund Brands LLC holds any record title or ownership interest in the fan contact database or the ticketing software, those assets are not being assigned to the Company under the IP Assignment Agreement. SPA § 4.10 represents that "Upon consummation of the IP Assignment Agreement, the Company will own all Company IP held by Nasund Brands LLC," but that representation is only as true as the scope of the IP Assignment Agreement allows. If the database and software are held by Nasund Brands, the IP Assignment Agreement does not convey them, and the representation in § 4.10 would be inaccurate—but Talin's remedy for that inaccuracy is limited to the $8.4 million escrow (see Issue 2).

Additionally, even for the registered marks, the IP Assignment Agreement only covers what is in Exhibit A. Any unregistered trade dress, domain names, social media accounts, or copyrights used in the business that are held by Nasund Brands would not be conveyed.

**Recommendation.**

1. Confirm with the client whether Nasund Brands LLC actually holds any interest in the fan contact database or the ticketing software. If it does, the IP Assignment Agreement must be expanded to include them.
2. Amend the IP Assignment Agreement's definition of "Company IP" to match the SPA's broader definition, or add the database and software as separate categories of Assigned Rights in § 2.1.
3. If the database and software are owned by the Company itself (not Nasund Brands), then the IP Assignment Agreement's narrow scope is less problematic, but we should confirm this through the disclosure schedule and diligence records.
4. In any event, add a specific representation in the IP Assignment Agreement that Assignor does not hold, and has not licensed to any third party, any interest in the fan contact database or ticketing software.

**Proposed language for IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (amended definition):**

> "**Company IP**" means all Intellectual Property used in the business of Assignee, including: (a) the registered trademarks and service marks set out in Exhibit A, together with all registrations and pending applications for them; (b) all unregistered trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos, trade dress, domain names, and social media accounts used in connection with the Lanternfall Festival or Assignee's business; (c) Assignee's fan contact database; (d) Assignee's ticketing software; and (e) all copyrights, trade secrets, and other proprietary rights used in Assignee's business.

## Issue 2: Survival Period of 120 Days Is Unacceptably Short

**Documents:** SPA § 9.1; Escrow Agreement § 4.1; SPA § 9.4

**The problem.** SPA § 9.1 provides that all representations and warranties survive only "until the Escrow Release Date." The Escrow Release Date is defined in Escrow Agreement § 4.1 as 120 days after the Closing Date. With a Closing Date of December 1, 2026, the Escrow Release Date is approximately March 31, 2027.

SPA § 9.4 further provides that no claim may be made under § 9.2(a) (breach of representations) unless a Claim Notice is delivered before the survival period expires, and any claim not so delivered "is irrevocably waived."

**Why it matters for Talin.**

- The most recent financial statements are as of June 30, 2026 (SPA § 4.5). That is five months before closing. Any financial deterioration or undisclosed liability that arises in the July–November 2026 period may not be discoverable within 120 days of closing.
- The 2027 Lanternfall Festival is in August 2027 (Board Consent § 1.2). The first post-closing audit covers the fiscal year ending June 30, 2027 (Board Consent § 1.5(d)). Talin will not have audited post-closing financials until well after the survival period has expired.
- For a festival business, many liabilities (talent guarantees, production costs, sponsorship obligations) are incurred in the months leading up to the festival. If the 2027 festival is in August 2027, the bulk of the relevant talent agreements and production commitments will be finalized and performed after the March 31, 2027 survival deadline.
- The 120-day period also means Talin has only ~4 months to complete post-closing integration diligence, identify any issues with the fan database, ticketing software, or talent agreements, and deliver claim notices.

**Recommendation.**

1. Extend the survival period for general representations and warranties to at least 18 months post-closing (i.e., through June 30, 2028, which would cover the 2027 festival and the first post-closing audit).
2. Provide for a longer survival (statute of limitations) for fundamental representations: organization, authority, capitalization, and the IP representations.
3. Conform the Escrow Release Date in the Escrow Agreement to the extended survival period, or provide for a partial release (e.g., 50% at 120 days, 50% at 18 months).
4. If Seller resists extending survival, at minimum extend it to cover the 2027 Lanternfall Festival (i.e., through September 30, 2027).

**Proposed language for SPA § 9.1 (amended):**

> The representations and warranties of Seller contained in Article 4 shall survive the Closing as follows: (a) the representations in Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.10, and 4.13 shall survive until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations; (b) the representations in Sections 4.5, 4.6, 4.11, 4.12, and 4.15 shall survive until eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date; and (c) all other representations and warranties of Seller shall survive until eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date. The representations and warranties of Buyer shall survive the Closing until eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date. The covenants and agreements of the parties shall survive the Closing until fully performed in accordance with their terms.

The Escrow Agreement § 4.1 would need to be conformed accordingly.

## Issue 3: No Requirement for Interim Financial Statements

**Documents:** SPA § 4.5; SPA § 6.1; Board Consent § 1.5

**The problem.** The SPA requires delivery of audited financial statements as of June 30, 2025 and June 30, 2026 (SPA § 4.5). The Closing Date is December 1, 2026. There is no covenant requiring Seller to deliver interim financial statements (e.g., for the period July 1 – November 30, 2026) before or at closing. The conduct of business covenants in § 6.1 are limited to four specific restrictions and do not include a financial reporting obligation.

**Why it matters for Talin.** Talin is closing with a five-month gap in financial visibility. For a seasonal business where the fiscal year ends June 30, the July–November period likely includes significant planning and commitment activity for the 2027 Lanternfall Festival (talent bookings, venue contracts, sponsorship sales). Without interim financials, Talin cannot verify that the working capital target ($2.5M under § 2.4) is being met, that no material liabilities have been incurred, or that the business is operating as expected. The Estimated Working Capital to be delivered under § 2.4(a) is a good-faith estimate, but without underlying interim financials, Talin has no basis to challenge it.

**Recommendation.**

Add a covenant in Article 6 requiring Seller to deliver unaudited interim financial statements (balance sheet and income statement) for the period from July 1, 2026 through the last day of the month preceding the Closing Date, at least 15 days before the Closing. Alternatively, require delivery of a management-prepared balance sheet as of November 30, 2026.

**Proposed language (new SPA § 6.8):**

> **6.8 Interim Financial Statements.** At least fifteen (15) days before the Closing Date, Seller shall deliver to Buyer unaudited financial statements of the Company, consisting of a balance sheet as of November 30, 2026 and related statements of income and cash flows for the period from July 1, 2026 through November 30, 2026, prepared on a basis consistent with the Financial Statements. Seller shall use commercially reasonable efforts to deliver such statements by the foregoing date.

## Issue 4: Buyer's Uncapped Indemnification for Post-Closing Show Liabilities

**Documents:** SPA § 9.3(b); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Post-Closing Show Liabilities"); SPA § 9.5(a)

**The problem.** SPA § 9.3(b) provides that Buyer indemnifies Seller for "any Post-Closing Show Liabilities, including any Post-Closing Show Liabilities for which Seller or any of its Affiliates is or becomes liable as guarantor, co-promoter or otherwise." This indemnity is **not** subject to the cap in § 9.5(a) (which only caps claims under § 9.3(a), i.e., breach of Buyer's representations). It is also not subject to the basket in § 9.5(b).

"Post-Closing Show Liabilities" is defined in § 1.1 as "all Liabilities and obligations under the Talent Agreements in respect of Shows scheduled to occur after the Closing Date, including artist guarantees, deposits, advances, bonuses, rider and production obligations, and settlement obligations, whether arising before or after the Closing Date."

**Why it matters for Talin.** This is effectively an uncapped guarantee by Talin of Seller's exposure under any talent agreement for post-closing shows. If Seller or its affiliates (e.g., Nasund Brands LLC or another Nasund entity) are named as guarantors or co-promoters on talent agreements for the 2027 Lanternfall Festival or other 2027 shows, and the Company fails to perform (for whatever reason), Talin must indemnify Seller for the full amount. This could be very substantial for a festival of this scale.

The practical effect is that Talin is underwriting Seller's credit risk as a guarantor on the Company's talent deals, in addition to bearing the Company's own obligations as the principal.

**Recommendation.**

1. Require disclosure of all instances where Seller or its Affiliates are guarantors, co-promoters, or otherwise secondarily liable under any Talent Agreement. This should be in the Disclosure Schedule to § 4.9.
2. Cap this indemnity or limit it to Talent Agreements that are specifically identified in the Disclosure Schedule.
3. Add a condition that Seller cause its Affiliates to be released from any guarantees or secondary obligations under Talent Agreements at or before the Closing, to the extent consent of the artist/agent is obtainable.
4. If the indemnity must remain, limit it to Talent Agreements with a guarantee exceeding a specified threshold (e.g., $250,000) that are identified in the Disclosure Schedule.

**Proposed language for SPA § 9.3(b) (amended):**

> (b) any Post-Closing Show Liabilities for which Seller or any of its Affiliates is or becomes liable as guarantor, co-promoter or otherwise, **solely to the extent arising under the Talent Agreements identified in Section 4.9 of the Disclosure Schedule as to which Seller or its Affiliates is a guarantor or co-promoter, and solely for the period during which such guarantee or co-promotion obligation remains in effect after the Closing Date.**

Additionally, add to SPA § 6.1:

> (e) cause Seller or any of its Affiliates to be released from any guarantee, co-promotion or other secondary obligation under any Talent Agreement, to the extent consent of the counterparty is required and reasonably obtainable.

## Issue 5: Non-Compete Is Too Narrow

**Documents:** SPA § 6.6

**The problem.** The non-compete in § 6.6 prohibits Seller (and its Affiliates) from promoting or producing "any multi-day music festival within 250 miles of the Lanternfall Festival site" for three years. This does not prohibit:

- Single-day concerts or tour dates
- Other types of live events (theater, comedy, sports entertainment)
- Use of the Lanternfall name or brand in any capacity (though the IP Assignment Agreement § 5.2 covers cessation of use of the Company IP, that is a separate obligation and may be harder to enforce)
- Activities beyond 250 miles
- Soliciting or poaching the Company's talent relationships, sponsors, or venue relationships

**Why it matters for Talin.** The primary value of this acquisition is the Lanternfall Festival and the Company's ability to promote live events in the region. If Seller can launch a competing single-day concert series or a one-day festival within the same market, or if Seller's affiliates can use their relationships with the same artists and sponsors to build a competing operation, Talin's investment is at risk. The 250-mile radius may also be insufficient if the festival draws from a wider geographic area.

**Recommendation.**

1. Expand the restricted activities to include all live music events (not just multi-day festivals).
2. Add a non-solicitation covenant covering the Company's talent, sponsors, venues, and key employees for the same period.
3. Consider expanding the geographic scope to the market area from which the festival draws attendees (which may be wider than 250 miles).

**Proposed language for SPA § 6.6 (amended):**

> **6.6 Non-Competition; Non-Solicitation.** For three years after the Closing Date, Seller shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, directly or indirectly: (a) promote, produce, co-promote, or co-produce any live music event (whether a concert, festival, tour date, or other live musical performance) within 300 miles of the Lanternfall Festival site; (b) solicit or attempt to solicit any artist, performer, agent, manager, or loan-out company that is a party to a Talent Agreement of the Company as of the Closing Date; (c) solicit or attempt to solicit any sponsor or venue that is a party to a Material Contract of the Company as of the Closing Date; or (d) hire or solicit for employment any employee of the Company who is involved in talent booking, production, or festival operations.

## Issue 6: Escrow Earnings Are Taxable to Buyer

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement § 3.4

**The problem.** Section 3.4 provides that "For tax purposes, Buyer shall be treated as the owner of the Escrow Fund, and all earnings shall be reported as income of Buyer, until disbursement." This means Talin will recognize interest income on the $8.4 million escrow (invested in a government money market fund per § 3.1) even though the funds are held as security for Seller's indemnification obligations and may ultimately be disbursed to Seller.

**Why it matters for Talin.** At current money market rates, $8.4 million could generate approximately $300,000–$400,000 in annual interest. Talin would owe corporate income tax on that amount (21% federal, plus any state tax) even if the principal is ultimately paid to Seller. This is a real economic cost to Talin that is not offset by any corresponding benefit.

**Recommendation.**

1. Negotiate for a tax gross-up: Seller should indemnify Buyer for any tax cost arising from Buyer's treatment as owner of the Escrow Fund.
2. Alternatively, allocate the escrow earnings to Seller (i.e., Buyer is not taxed on earnings that inure to Seller's benefit).
3. If neither is achievable, at minimum ensure the tax cost is factored into the overall deal economics.

**Proposed language (new Escrow Agreement § 3.5):**

> **3.5 Tax Gross-Up.** Seller shall indemnify and hold Buyer harmless from any income, excise, or other Tax imposed on Buyer by reason of Buyer's treatment as the owner of the Escrow Fund under Section 3.4, to the extent such Tax is attributable to earnings on the portion of the Escrow Fund that is ultimately disbursed to Seller under this Agreement.

## Issue 7: Working Capital Adjustment Mechanics May Be Inadequate for a Seasonal Business

**Documents:** SPA § 2.4

**The problem.** The working capital adjustment uses a target of $2.5 million and a dollar-for-dollar true-up. Buyer has 90 days post-closing to deliver its calculation of actual net working capital "as of the Closing." However:

- The Company's fiscal year ends June 30. Working capital in a festival business is highly seasonal—cash and receivables spike in the months before a festival (ticket sales, sponsorship payments) while payables (talent advances, production costs) build up. A working capital snapshot as of December 1, 2026 may not be representative.
- The definition of "net working capital" is not provided in the SPA. It is presumably derived from the Financial Statements methodology, but the specific components (e.g., whether ticket receivables for the 2027 festival are included, whether deferred revenue from advance ticket sales is netted against them) are not specified.
- The 90-day window for Buyer's calculation (SPA § 2.4(b)) means Talin must deliver its number by approximately March 1, 2027—before the 2027 festival planning is fully underway and before any post-closing audit.

**Why it matters for Talin.** If the working capital definition is ambiguous, Seller could argue that certain items (e.g., deferred ticket revenue for the 2027 festival, or talent deposits paid in advance) should or should not be included, creating a material dollar-for-dollar impact on the Purchase Price.

**Recommendation.**

1. Add a schedule to the SPA defining the components of "net working capital" (current assets minus current liabilities, with specific inclusions and exclusions).
2. Clarify how deferred revenue from advance ticket sales and pre-paid talent deposits are treated.
3. Consider extending the calculation window to 120 days to align with the (recommended) extended survival period.

**Proposed language (new SPA § 2.4(c)):**

> (c) For purposes of this Section 2.4, "net working capital" means current assets minus current liabilities, determined as of the Closing Date, excluding: (i) cash and cash equivalents; (ii) deferred revenue from advance ticket sales for Shows scheduled to occur after the Closing Date; (iii) pre-paid deposits and advances under Talent Agreements for Shows scheduled to occur after the Closing Date; and (iv) any Indebtedness to be paid off at the Closing under Section 2.3(a).

## Issue 8: Conduct of Business Covenants Are Inadequate for the Interim Period

**Documents:** SPA § 6.1

**The problem.** The interim period runs from October 14, 2026 (signing) to December 1, 2026 (closing)—approximately 7 weeks. The covenants in § 6.1 restrict only:

- (a) Talent agreements with guarantees exceeding $150,000;
- (b) Incurrence of Indebtedness;
- (c) Disposal of Company IP; and
- (d) Dividends or distributions.

Missing restrictions include:

- Changes in management or key personnel
- Increases in compensation or benefits
- Entry into material contracts other than Talent Agreements (e.g., new venue leases, sponsorship agreements, production service contracts)
- Capital expenditures above a threshold
- Changes in the Company's business plan or festival format
- Sale of material assets
- Amendments to existing Material Contracts

**Why it matters for Talin.** Even a 7-week interim period is long enough for management to make decisions that affect the Company's value or Talin's post-closing plans. For example, management could sign a new sponsorship deal, commit to a production vendor, or make changes to the 2027 festival format. The Board's integration plan (Board Consent § 1.5) assumes Keelhaven will continue to operate "under its existing name and management through the 2027 Lanternfall Festival," but nothing in the SPA covenants protects that assumption during the interim period.

**Recommendation.**

Expand § 6.1 to include:

> (e) make any change in the officers, directors, or key employees of the Company, or increase any compensation, bonus, or benefit payable to any of them;
> (f) enter into any Material Contract or amend any existing Material Contract in a manner that increases the Company's obligations;
> (g) make any capital expenditure in excess of $100,000 individually or $250,000 in the aggregate;
> (h) sell, transfer, or otherwise dispose of any material asset of the Company; or
> (i) change the format, date, or location of the Lanternfall Festival or any other Show.

## Summary of Recommended Actions (Priority Order)

1. **Resolve the IP scope gap** (Issue 1) – Confirm what Nasund Brands actually holds; amend the IP Assignment Agreement if necessary. This is a condition to closing (SPA § 7.2(d)), so it must be fixed before Talin is obligated to close.
2. **Extend the survival period** (Issue 2) – Negotiate at least 18 months for general reps; conform the Escrow Release Date.
3. **Require interim financials** (Issue 3) – Add a covenant for November 30, 2026 financials.
4. **Limit or condition the Post-Closing Show Liabilities indemnity** (Issue 4) – Require disclosure of all Seller/Affiliate guarantees; cap or narrow the indemnity.
5. **Broaden the non-compete** (Issue 5) – Add non-solicitation; expand restricted activities.
6. **Address escrow tax allocation** (Issue 6) – Negotiate a gross-up or earnings allocation.
7. **Define working capital components** (Issue 7) – Add a schedule specifying inclusions/exclusions.
8. **Expand interim covenants** (Issue 8) – Add management, capex, and material contract restrictions.
9. **Obtain missing documents** (Issue 9) – Disclosure Schedule, payoff letters, talent agreements, credit facility.
