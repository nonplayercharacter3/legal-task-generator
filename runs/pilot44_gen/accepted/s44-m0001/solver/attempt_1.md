## Summary

Vepous faces three interlocking problems that have converged in the past 60 days: (1) a pending change of control of its parent that triggers a material breach under the Supply Agreement with no cure period and exposes Vepous to up to approximately $7.4 million in liquidated damages; (2) an amendment that quietly narrowed the limitation-of-liability carve-outs, capping the supplier's indemnity for product-defect bodily injury claims at roughly 12 months of fees; and (3) an assignment of the Supply Agreement to an affiliate of a student-transportation competitor, which may strip Vepous of its parent guaranty and lock it into exclusive supply with a competitive threat. ## Issue 1 – Change of Control of Customer's Parent Triggers Material Breach and Liquidated Damages

**Documents and sections:**
- Supply Agreement § 1.4 (definition of "Change of Control"); § 14.3 (consequences); § 3.4 (Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment); § 2.1 (Initial Term)
- Milon Notice Letter §§ 3.1–3.3, 4.1

**What happened.** On September 28, 2026, Vepous Transit Holdings, Inc. (Vepous's parent) signed a stock purchase agreement with Larchfield Mobility Partners, L.P. under which Larchfield will acquire a controlling interest in Vepous Transit Holdings (Milon Notice Letter § 3.1). Section 1.4 of the Supply Agreement defines "Change of Control" to include "a sale, transfer or issuance of a controlling interest in Customer or in any entity that directly or indirectly controls Customer." The Larchfield Transaction falls squarely within that definition.

**Why it matters.** Section 14.3 provides that a Change of Control without Supplier's prior written consent is a material breach to which the 30-day cure period in § 13.1 does not apply. Upon that breach, Supplier (or its assignee, Rookwood) may:

- Terminate the Agreement on 30 days' written notice; and
- Recover liquidated damages equal to the AMPC for the then-current Contract Year plus each remaining Contract Year of the Initial Term, less the invoiced value of Products purchased in the then-current Contract Year.

The Initial Term runs through February 28, 2030 (five years from March 1, 2025). As of the expected closing (which we do not yet know), Vepous is in Contract Year 2 (March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027). The maximum liquidated damages exposure is:

| Contract Year | AMPC |
|---|---|
| CY 2 (remainder) | $1,850,000 |
| CY 3 | $1,850,000 |
| CY 4 | $1,850,000 |
| CY 5 | $1,850,000 |
| **Gross** | **$7,400,000** |
| Less: CY 2 purchases to date | (to be confirmed) |
| **Net exposure** | **up to ~$7.4M** |

In addition, termination would end Vepous's exclusive supply relationship at a moment when it has no alternative source for brake, steering, suspension, and child-check alarm components. For a school bus operator with 339 vehicles (Schedule A, Part 3), a sudden loss of supply creates immediate safety and operational risk.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the Larchfield Transaction closes, seek written consent from Milon (or, post-assignment, from Rookwood) under § 14.3.** Consent eliminates the breach entirely. Approach this as a commercial negotiation: Vepous can offer to reaffirm the AMPC, extend the Initial Term, or accept a modest price adjustment in exchange for consent. The leverage Vepous has is that the supplier also wants the revenue; a termination benefits neither party.

2. **If consent is not forthcoming, negotiate a side letter or amendment that (a) waives the breach arising from the Larchfield Transaction specifically, and (b) either deletes or caps the liquidated damages provision.** Proposed language for a targeted waiver:

   > The Parties agree that the acquisition of a controlling interest in Vepous Transit Holdings, Inc. by Larchfield Mobility Partners, L.P. (or its affiliates or designees) pursuant to the Stock Purchase Agreement dated September 28, 2026 shall not constitute a Change of Control of Customer for purposes of Section 14.3 of the Agreement, and Supplier waives any right to terminate the Agreement or claim liquidated damages arising from that transaction.

3. **If no consent or waiver is obtained before closing, Vepous should be prepared for a termination notice within 30 days of closing.** In that scenario, immediately invoke § 5.4 (substitute purchase rights) for any accepted POs that Rookwood fails to fill, and begin qualifying an alternative supplier for the products not covered by the exclusivity (which ends with the Agreement).

4. **Do not voluntarily disclose the expected closing date to Milon without first securing consent or a waiver.** See Issue 5 below.

## Issue 2 – Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Limitation-of-Liability Carve-Outs, Capping Product-Defect Bodily Injury Indemnity

**Documents and sections:**
- Supply Agreement § 10.1(b) (product-defect bodily injury indemnity); § 11.1 (consequential damages exclusion); § 11.2 (cap); original § 11.3(a)
- Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 (replaced § 11.3)

**What happened.** The original § 11.3(a) excluded "all Section 10.1 indemnities, including 10.1(b) product-defect bodily injury claims" from both the consequential damages exclusion (§ 11.1) and the 12-month-fee cap (§ 11.2). Amendment No. 1, § 3.1 replaced § 11.3 with a version in which subsection (a) now reads:

> "Supplier's indemnification obligations for third-party claims arising **solely** under Section 10.1(a)"

This is a material narrowing. The practical effect:

| Claim type | Original § 11.3 | Amended § 11.3 | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10.1(a) IP infringement | Uncapped, no consequential damages exclusion | Uncapped, no consequential damages exclusion | Unchanged |
| 10.1(b) Product-defect bodily injury / property damage | Uncapped, no consequential damages exclusion | **Subject to § 11.1 and § 11.2 cap** | **Severely weakened** |
| 10.1(c) Regulatory breach / gross negligence / willful misconduct | Uncapped (via 10.1 broadly) | Uncapped only if it constitutes gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct (§ 11.3(d)); otherwise capped | **Weakened** |

**Why it matters.** Vepous operates school buses carrying children. A brake failure, steering component failure, or child-check alarm malfunction that causes a bodily injury is the single highest-severity risk in this relationship. Under the amended provision, Vepous's recovery for a third-party bodily injury claim caused by a defective product is:

- Capped at the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim (approximately $1.85M based on the AMPC, or the actual invoiced amount if lower); and
- Deprived of consequential damages (which would include vehicle downtime, substitute transportation costs, and potentially lost school district contract revenue).

The § 10.1(b) indemnity still exists on paper, but its economic value is now limited to the cap. For a catastrophic multi-vehicle incident, the cap is grossly inadequate.

Additionally, the word "solely" in amended § 11.3(a) creates a trap: if a product defect claim also has an IP component (e.g., the defective part infringes a patent), the claim does not arise "solely" under § 10.1(a), and even the IP indemnity could be argued to be capped.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Negotiate a restoration of the original § 11.3(a) scope as part of the consent/waiver discussion in Issue 1.** Vepous should not give consent to the Larchfield Transaction without also restoring the liability protections. Proposed replacement language:

   > 11.3 **Exclusions.** Sections 11.1 and 11.2 do not apply to (a) all of Supplier's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1, including third-party claims for bodily injury, death, or property damage arising from a Defect in a Product; (b) Supplier's obligations under Section 7.3; (c) a Party's breach of Section 9; (d) a Party's gross negligence, fraud, or willful misconduct; or (e) Customer's payment obligations for Products delivered.

2. **If Milon/Rookwood resists full restoration, at minimum negotiate to add 10.1(b) back as a separate carve-out:**

   > (a-1) Supplier's indemnification obligations for third-party claims under Section 10.1(b) (product-defect bodily injury, death, or tangible property damage);

3. **Delete the word "solely" from § 11.3(a)** to avoid the mixed-claim trap.

4. **Confirm with the client** whether the Amendment was negotiated with the understanding that 10.1(b) would remain uncapped. If the client believed the original protections were preserved, this may be a basis to argue the amendment was not intended to have this effect (though § 15.6 requires amendments in writing, so this is a weak position as a matter of contract law; it is better used as negotiating leverage).

## Issue 3 – Parent Guaranty May Not Survive the Assignment to Rookwood

**Documents and sections:**
- Supply Agreement § 15.3 (Parent Guaranty as condition); § 14.2 (Supplier assignment)
- Parent Guaranty §§ 1.2, 2.1, 7.1
- Milon Notice Letter § 2.1 (assignment to Rookwood)

**What happened.** Section 15.3 of the Supply Agreement requires that "Supplier shall deliver to Customer a guaranty of Supplier's obligations under this Agreement, executed by **Milon Industrial Holdings, Inc.**" The Parent Guaranty was given by Milon Industrial Holdings, Inc. as the parent of Milon Fleet Parts Corp. (Guaranty, Recital B). The Guaranty covers "all obligations and liabilities of Supplier to Customer under the Supply Agreement" (Guaranty § 1.2).

Milon is assigning the entire Supply Agreement to Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC, an affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. (Milon Notice Letter § 2.2). After the Assignment Date (December 1, 2026), "Supplier" under the Agreement will be Rookwood.

**Why it matters.**

- The Guaranty was given by Milon Industrial Holdings as parent of Milon Fleet Parts Corp. It is not clear that Milon Industrial Holdings has any ownership relationship with Rookwood or Kestrel Student Coach. The Guaranty does not contain a provision stating that it follows the Supply Agreement upon assignment to a non-affiliate.
- Guaranty § 7.1 prohibits the Guarantor from assigning its obligations without Customer's consent, but that addresses the Guarantor's voluntary assignment, not the effect of the underlying agreement being assigned to a third party.
- If the Guaranty does not extend to Rookwood's obligations, Vepous loses its credit support for all of Rookwood's performance, warranty, indemnification, and recall obligations.
- The Milon Notice Letter makes no mention of the Guaranty or of any replacement guaranty from Kestrel or Rookwood's parent.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the Assignment Date (December 1, 2026), require Rookwood (or Kestrel Student Coach, Inc., or Rookwood's parent) to deliver a replacement parent guaranty** in substantially the same form as the existing Milon Industrial Holdings guaranty, covering Rookwood's obligations as Supplier under the Agreement. This should be a condition of Vepous's consent to the assignment (if Vepous is giving consent) or a condition of Vepous's continued performance.

2. **Alternatively, require Milon Industrial Holdings to confirm in writing that its existing Guaranty continues to cover Rookwood's obligations post-assignment.** If Milon Industrial Holdings has no equity relationship with Rookwood, this confirmation would be unusual and the Guarantor may resist.

3. **Proposed language for a replacement guaranty requirement (to be added as a condition in a side letter or in the consent to the Larchfield Transaction):**

   > As a condition to Customer's consent to the assignment of the Supply Agreement to Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC, Rookwood shall cause its parent entity, [Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. / applicable parent], to execute and deliver to Customer a guaranty of Rookwood's obligations under the Supply Agreement in form and substance substantially equivalent to the Parent Guaranty dated March 1, 2025 executed by Milon Industrial Holdings, Inc., no later than the Assignment Date.

4. **Confirm with the client** whether it has received the written assumption of obligations from Rookwood that Milon's letter promises (Milon Notice Letter § 2.3). If not, follow up immediately.

## Issue 4 – Assignment to an Affiliate of a Competitor Creates Information and Competitive Risks

**Documents and sections:**
- Supply Agreement § 3.1 (exclusive supply); § 9.1 (confidentiality – "route and depot data"); Schedule A Part 3 (fleet profile)
- Milon Notice Letter §§ 2.2, 2.4(c) (Rookwood is affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach; transfer of records including pricing, PO history, forecasts, volumes, depot delivery info, traceability records)
- Mutual NDA § 1.1(b)–(c) (fleet size, maintenance records, parts consumption, route, depot, school district contract information)

**What happened.** Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC is an affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. (Milon Notice Letter § 2.2). The name "Kestrel Student Coach" strongly suggests it is in the student transportation business, potentially competing with Vepous for school district contracts on Long Island. Upon assignment, Rookwood will receive all of Vepous's records under the Agreement, including pricing, Purchase Order history, forecasts, volumes, Covered Depot delivery information, and traceability records (Milon Notice Letter § 2.4(c)).

**Why it matters.**

- **Exclusivity lock-in:** Section 3.1 requires Vepous to buy all Fleet Components exclusively from the Supplier. Post-assignment, that means exclusively from Rookwood, an entity controlled by (or under common control with) a potential competitor. Vepous cannot source from a third party even if Rookwood underperforms, unless § 5.4 is triggered (which requires a failure to fill an accepted PO within the delivery period).
- **Information asymmetry:** Rookwood/Kestrel will know Vepous's complete parts consumption by depot, fleet composition (140 + 118 Type C; 22 + 19 Type A), maintenance patterns, and cost structure. If Kestrel is bidding for school district contracts that Vepous currently serves or may pursue, this information is competitively sensitive.
- **NDA protection is limited:** The Mutual NDA's disclosure period ends January 13, 2027 (two years from the January 13, 2025 NDA Effective Date) (NDA § 7.1). After that, the NDA no longer governs new disclosures. The Supply Agreement's § 9 confidentiality survives three years after the Agreement ends (§ 9.3), but its definition of "Confidential Information" (§ 9.1) is narrower than the NDA's (§ 1.1) — it does not expressly cover "maintenance records, parts consumption data" or "school district contract" information, though "route and depot data" and "volumes" are included.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Confirm with the client whether Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. is a competitor** in Vepous's school district contracts or in adjacent markets. If it is, this elevates the issue significantly.

2. **Negotiate a data-use restriction as part of the consent/assignment discussion.** Proposed language:

   > Rookwood and its Affiliates (including Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. and its subsidiaries) shall use Customer's Confidential Information received under or in connection with the Supply Agreement solely for the purpose of performing Supplier's obligations under the Supply Agreement and shall not use such information for any competitive purpose, including in connection with any bid, proposal, or contract negotiation for pupil transportation services.

3. **Extend the Supply Agreement's confidentiality definition** to match the NDA's broader scope, at least for the duration of the Term. Proposed amendment to § 9.1:

   > Each Party shall keep confidential all non-public information that the other Party discloses under this Agreement, including pricing, volumes, forecasts, route and depot data, fleet size and composition, maintenance records, parts consumption data, school district contract information, and technical information ("Confidential Information").

4. **Consider whether to invoke § 13.1 (termination for material breach)** if Rookwood/Kestrel uses Vepous's information competitively. This is a high-bar remedy but preserves the option.

## Issue 5 – Milon's Information Request: Strategic Response Required by ~November 9, 2026

**Documents and sections:**
- Milon Notice Letter § 5.1 (request for (a) expected closing date of Larchfield Transaction; (b) list of post-closing Affiliates); § 15.5 of Supply Agreement (notices effective on receipt)

**What happened.** Milon's October 19, 2026 letter requests, within 15 business days (approximately November 9, 2026): (a) the expected closing date of the Larchfield Transaction, and (b) a list of entities that will be Affiliates of Customer after closing.

**Why it matters.**

- **Disclosing the closing date** gives Milon/Rookwood the ability to time a § 14.3 termination notice to land immediately after closing, maximizing disruption and minimizing Vepous's ability to negotiate.
- **Disclosing the Affiliate list** confirms the corporate structure and may reveal whether Kestrel or other competitors will be Affiliates of Vepous post-closing (which would further complicate the exclusivity and competitive information issues in Issue 4).

However, the letter is framed as a "request," not a contractual obligation with a stated remedy for non-compliance. The Supply Agreement does not contain a general information-sharing covenant that would be breached by silence.
- **The request is also a signal:** Milon is documenting that it gave notice, that Vepous was aware of the Change of Control, and that Vepous had an opportunity to seek consent but did not. This builds Milon's (or Rookwood's) position that any later consent request comes from a party in breach.

1. **Respond before the deadline, but do not volunteer the closing date or full Affiliate list without protective language.** A measured response:

   > (b) We will provide a list of Customer's Affiliates following the closing of the Larchfield Transaction, subject to execution of a mutual confidentiality understanding with respect to that information.
   >
   > We note that Customer is in active discussions with Supplier regarding (i) Supplier's consent to the Larchfield Transaction under Section 14.3 of the Agreement, and (ii) the terms on which the Assignment to Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC will be effective, including the delivery of a replacement parent guaranty. We look forward to continuing those discussions.
   >
   > Sincerely,
   > [General Counsel]

2. **Use the response to open the consent negotiation** (Issue 1) and the guaranty replacement demand (Issue 3) simultaneously. Frame it as a package: Vepous will provide the requested information and seek consent in exchange for (a) a written consent/waiver of § 14.3, (b) restoration of the original § 11.3 liability carve-outs, and (c) a replacement parent guaranty.

3. **Do not agree to provide the closing date until a consent or waiver is in hand.** If Milon threatens to terminate without consent, the response is to note that termination does not relieve Vepous of the obligation to pay for Products delivered, and that Vepous will pursue any termination as a breach if it interferes with the safe operation of its fleet during the school year.

## Issue 6 – The Word "Solely" in Amended § 11.3(a) Creates a Mixed-Claim Gap

**Documents and sections:**
- Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 (replaced § 11.3(a): "third-party claims arising **solely** under Section 10.1(a)")

**What happened.** The amended § 11.3(a) carves out from the cap and consequential damages exclusion only those indemnification obligations "for third-party claims arising solely under Section 10.1(a)" (IP infringement). If a single product gives rise to both an IP claim and a bodily injury claim (e.g., a brake component that infringes a patent and also fails, causing an injury), the bodily injury component does not arise "solely" under § 10.1(a), and the IP component may be argued to be tainted by the non-IP element. In either case, the uncapped treatment may not apply.

**Why it matters.** This is a technical drafting flaw that could allow the supplier to argue that even the IP indemnity is capped in mixed-claim scenarios. While mixed claims are less common than pure product-defect claims, the word "solely" creates unnecessary litigation risk.

**Recommendation.** Delete the word "solely" as part of the § 11.3 restoration negotiated under Issue 2. The corrected language should read: "Supplier's indemnification obligations for third-party claims under Section 10.1(a)."

## Issue 7 – Mutual NDA Disclosure Period Expires January 13, 2027

**Documents and sections:**
- Mutual NDA § 7.1 (disclosure period: two years from NDA Effective Date of January 13, 2025, i.e., until January 13, 2027); § 7.2 (obligations continue three years after disclosure period ends, i.e., until January 13, 2030 for non-trade-secret information)
- Supply Agreement § 9.3 (confidentiality survives three years after Agreement ends)

**What happens.** After January 13, 2027, the NDA no longer applies to new disclosures. The Supply Agreement's § 9 will continue to govern, but as noted in Issue 4, its definition of Confidential Information is narrower than the NDA's.

**Why it matters.** This is a moderate gap. Information such as "school district contract" details and "maintenance records" that were protected under the NDA may fall outside § 9.1 of the Supply Agreement if disclosed after January 13, 2027.

**Recommendation.** As part of the broader renegotiation (Issues 1–4), amend Supply Agreement § 9.1 to incorporate the NDA's broader definition (see proposed language in Issue 4, Recommendation 3). Alternatively, extend the NDA's disclosure period by amendment to align with the Supply Agreement's Term.

**What happened.** While most product prices were reduced in Amendment No. 1, the front leaf spring assembly was increased by $14.00 per unit (2.8%).

## Issue 9 – Operational Continuity Risks Post-Assignment

**Documents and sections:**
- Supply Agreement § 5.2 (vehicle-down delivery: 1 business day); § 5.5 (30 days safety stock at Hauppauge facility)
- Milon Notice Letter § 2.2 (Rookwood will acquire the Hauppauge distribution facility)

**What happens.** Rookwood will acquire the Hauppauge, New York distribution facility as part of the transaction. The Agreement requires safety stock of 30 days of forecasted requirements for brake components and child-check alarm systems to be maintained at that facility (§ 5.5), and vehicle-down items to be delivered within 1 business day (§ 5.2).

**Why it matters.** If Rookwood's operational capabilities differ from Milon's (e.g., different staffing, different inventory management, different service-level commitments to its other customers), Vepous's critical delivery obligations could be at risk. For a school bus operator, a one-day delay in delivering a brake component to a vehicle-down bus is a safety issue.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Require Rookwood to confirm in writing, as part of its assumption of obligations, that it will maintain the § 5.5 safety stock levels and the § 5.2 delivery timelines** at the Hauppauge facility (or an equivalent facility within the same geographic area).

2. **Consider adding a service-level remedy:** If Rookwood fails to meet the vehicle-down delivery time in more than two instances in any 90-day period, Vepous may source substitute parts under § 5.4 without first issuing a formal notice of breach.
