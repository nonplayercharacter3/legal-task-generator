## Summary

Vepous faces two acute, interlocking problems arising from events in the fall of 2026: (1) the pending Larchfield acquisition of Vepous's parent triggers the Change of Control clause in Section 14.3 of the Supply Agreement, which Milon has already flagged and reserved the right to enforce; and (2) Milon's assignment of the Agreement to Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC—an affiliate of a competing school bus operator—strips Vepous of its parent guaranty and creates a structural competitive conflict. In addition, Amendment No. 1 quietly narrowed the uncapped indemnity for product-defect bodily injury claims, which is the single most important risk category for a school bus operator. I identify eleven issues below, in order of importance to the client.

## Issue 1 – Change of Control Breach: Larchfield Transaction

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 1.4, § 14.3; Milon Notice Letter §§ 3.1–3.3, 4.1

**What the documents say.** Section 1.4 defines "Change of Control" to include "a sale, transfer or issuance of a controlling interest in Customer or in any entity that directly or indirectly controls Customer." Section 14.3 provides that a Change of Control of Customer without Supplier's prior written consent is a material breach to which the 30-day cure period in § 13.1 does **not** apply. Upon that breach, Supplier may (a) terminate on 30 days' written notice and (b) recover, as liquidated damages, the Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment shortfall for the remainder of the Initial Term—calculated as $1,850,000 for the then-current Contract Year plus each remaining Contract Year, less invoiced purchases in the then-current year, payable within 30 days of termination.

Milon's October 19, 2026 letter (§§ 3.1–3.3) confirms it is aware of the September 28, 2026 stock purchase agreement between Vepous Transit Holdings, Inc. and Larchfield Mobility Partners, L.P., and expressly states that no consent has been given. Section 4.1 reserves all rights under § 14.3, including termination and the liquidated damages claim.

**Why it matters.** If the Larchfield Transaction closes without Vepous first obtaining Milon's (or, post-assignment, Rookwood's) written consent, Vepous will be in an uncured material breach. The exposure is termination of the exclusive supply relationship in the middle of the school year plus a liquidated damages payment that, depending on timing, could range from approximately $3.7 million (if closing late in Contract Year 2, leaving three full remaining years) to $7.4 million (if closing early in Contract Year 2, leaving four years). The lack of a cure period means Vepous cannot simply pay the shortfall to cure; the breach is irrevocable once the Change of Control occurs absent consent.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the Larchfield Transaction closes**, Vepous must seek written consent from Milon (and, because the assignment will be effective December 1, 2026, from Rookwood as well) under § 14.3. Frame the request as a condition to closing. If the SPA with Larchfield has a closing condition that allows for this, use it.

2. **Open a negotiation with Milon/Rookwood** to obtain consent in exchange for consideration. Possible quid pro quo:
   - A modest increase to the Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment (e.g., from $1.85M to $2.0M), which gives the supplier a revenue bump in exchange for waiving the Change of Control right.
   - A commitment to extend the Initial Term by one year.
   - A one-time fee (e.g., $250,000–$500,000) characterized as a consent fee.

3. **If consent cannot be obtained**, evaluate whether Vepous can restructure the Larchfield Transaction so that it does not constitute a Change of Control under § 1.4. This is difficult because the definition captures any entity that "directly or indirectly controls Customer," and Larchfield will acquire a controlling interest in Vepous Transit Holdings, Inc. The only structural workaround would be for Larchfield to take a non-controlling stake in the parent, which likely defeats the purpose of the transaction.

4. **Assess the enforceability of the liquidated damages clause** as a fallback defense. Under New York law, a liquidated damages provision is unenforceable as a penalty if the amount is grossly disproportionate to the actual loss foreseeable at the time of contracting (*Hoch v. Central Fire Ins. Co.*, 225 N.Y. 68 (1918); *Mallory v. Rochelle Gen. Ins. Co.*, 20 A.D.3d 515 (2d Dep't 2005)). The clause measures damages as the full remaining minimum purchase commitment, which approximates the supplier's lost gross revenue—not lost profit. If the supplier's margin on these parts is, say, 30–40%, the actual lost profit would be $2.2M–$3.0M on a $7.4M face amount. This is a viable but uncertain defense; it should be preserved, not led with.

5. **Respond to Milon's information request** (Notice Letter § 5) strategically. See Issue 6 below.

## Issue 2 – Loss of Parent Guaranty Upon Assignment to Rookwood

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 15.3; Milon Parent Guaranty §§ 1.2, 2.1, 4.2, 7.1; Milon Notice Letter § 2

**What the documents say.** Section 15.3 of the Supply Agreement makes a parent guaranty "a condition to this Agreement," requiring that "Supplier shall deliver to Customer a guaranty of Supplier's obligations under this Agreement, executed by Milon Industrial Holdings, Inc." The existing Guaranty (§ 1.2) defines "Guaranteed Obligations" as "all obligations and liabilities of **Supplier** to Customer under the Supply Agreement." "Supplier" is defined in the Supply Agreement as Milon Fleet Parts Corp. The Guaranty does not contain language extending it to an assignee of Supplier or to the assignee's parent.

Milon's Notice Letter (§ 2.1) confirms the assignment of the Agreement in whole to Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC, effective December 1, 2026. Rookwood is an affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach, Inc.—not of Milon Industrial Holdings, Inc.

**Why it matters.** After December 1, 2026, Milon Fleet Parts Corp. will no longer be the Supplier. Its obligations will have transferred to Rookwood. The Guaranty from Milon Industrial Holdings covers only "Supplier's" (i.e., Milon Fleet Parts Corp.'s) obligations. There is no guaranty from Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. or from Rookwood's actual parent. Vepous loses its credit enhancement at the exact moment it most needs it—while simultaneously facing the Change of Control exposure in Issue 1. If Rookwood underperforms, becomes insolvent, or disputes a claim, Vepous has no parent to look to.

Additionally, § 15.3 states the guaranty is "a condition to this Agreement." One can argue that when the Supplier changes by assignment, the condition is re-triggered: the new Supplier (Rookwood) must deliver a guaranty from its parent as a condition to the continued effectiveness of the Agreement.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the Assignment Date (December 1, 2026), demand that Rookwood deliver a parent guaranty** in substantially the same form as the existing Milon Parent Guaranty, executed by Kestrel Student Coach, Inc. (or whichever entity directly or indirectly controls Rookwood). Cite § 15.3 as the contractual basis.

2. **Propose the following language** for the new guaranty's scope clause, to be inserted in a new Parent Guaranty from Kestrel:

   > "1.2 'Guaranteed Obligations' means all obligations and liabilities of Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC, in its capacity as Supplier under the Supply Agreement, whether now existing or arising later, including payment, delivery, warranty, recall, indemnification and reimbursement obligations. For the avoidance of doubt, this Guaranty covers obligations assumed by Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC pursuant to Section 14.2 of the Supply Agreement."

3. **Alternatively, if Kestrel will not provide a guaranty**, negotiate a confirmation from Milon Industrial Holdings that the existing Guaranty continues to cover Rookwood's obligations as assignee. This would require an amendment to the Guaranty (per § 8.5, a writing signed by Guarantor and Customer). Proposed amendment language:

   > "Section 1.2 is amended by inserting after 'Supplier' the following: 'or, following any assignment of the Supply Agreement pursuant to Section 14.2, the assignee of Supplier.'"

4. **If neither parent will provide a guaranty**, consider whether Vepous has the right to refuse the assignment or to terminate. Section 14.2 does not give Customer a consent right, so Vepous cannot block the assignment per se. However, if the failure to deliver a replacement guaranty constitutes a breach of § 15.3 (a condition to the Agreement), Vepous could argue the assignment is ineffective or that it has a termination right under § 13.1. This is a stronger negotiating lever than a legal claim; use it to extract the guaranty.

5. **Request Rookwood's financial statements** before the Assignment Date to assess creditworthiness, analogous to the information rights in § 6.1 of the existing Guaranty.

## Issue 3 – Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Uncapped Indemnity for Product-Defect Bodily Injury Claims

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 10.1(b), § 11.1, § 11.2, § 11.3 (original); Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 (replacing § 11.3)

**What the documents say.** Original § 11.3(a) provided that the consequential damages exclusion (§ 11.1) and the liability cap (§ 11.2) do not apply to "all Section 10.1 indemnities, including 10.1(b) product-defect bodily injury claims." Amendment No. 1, § 3.1, replaced § 11.3(a) with: "Supplier's indemnification obligations for third-party claims arising **solely** under Section 10.1(a)."

Section 10.1(a) covers IP infringement. Section 10.1(b) covers bodily injury, death, or property damage from a Product Defect. Section 10.1(c) covers regulatory compliance breaches and gross negligence/willful misconduct.

**Why it matters.** This is the most significant substantive change in the Amendment, and it cuts directly against Vepous's core risk. Vepous operates school buses carrying children. A brake failure, steering component failure, or child-check alarm malfunction that injures or kills a student will generate a § 10.1(b) claim. Under the original Agreement, Supplier's indemnity for that claim was uncapped. Under the Amendment, it is subject to:

- The § 11.1 exclusion of consequential damages (which could be argued to bar vehicle downtime costs, substitute transportation, and emotional distress damages passed through from a third-party plaintiff), and
- The § 11.2 cap of "fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim" (approximately $1.85 million, based on the minimum purchase commitment, though actual spend may be higher).

A single serious student injury claim can easily exceed $5 million in damages. Vepous would bear the excess.

The word "solely" in the amended § 11.3(a) further narrows even the IP indemnity: if a claim alleges both IP infringement and product defect, it does not arise "solely" under § 10.1(a) and falls back into the cap.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Flag this to the client immediately** so they understand the current risk posture. The Amendment was signed in June 2026; the client may not have appreciated the full impact.

2. **Seek to restore the original § 11.3(a)** in connection with the negotiation over the Change of Control consent (Issue 1) or the assignment (Issue 2). Package it: "We will consent to the Larchfield Transaction / accept the assignment on the condition that § 11.3(a) is restored to its pre-Amendment form."

3. **Proposed restoration language** (Amendment No. 2 or side letter):

   > "Section 11.3(a) of the Agreement is hereby amended to read as follows: '(a) all of Supplier's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1, including Section 10.1(b) product-defect bodily injury and property damage claims and Section 10.1(c) regulatory compliance and gross negligence claims;'"

4. **If the supplier resists full restoration**, negotiate a middle ground: raise the cap for § 10.1(b) claims to a stated dollar amount (e.g., $10 million) or to the supplier's insurance limits ($5 million CGL per § 12.1). Proposed language:

   > "Notwithstanding Section 11.2, Supplier's aggregate liability for third-party claims under Section 10.1(b) shall not be less than $10,000,000 per claim and $20,000,000 in the aggregate."

5. **Confirm insurance adequacy.** Section 12.1 requires $5 million CGL. If the indemnity cap is restored to align with insurance, Vepous should also require that the insurance policy name Vepous as additional insured (already required by § 12.2) and that the supplier provide a certificate before the Assignment Date.

## Issue 4 – Competitive Conflict: Rookwood Is an Affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach, Inc.

**Documents:** Milon Notice Letter § 2.2; Supply Agreement §§ 3.1, 9.1; NDA § 1.1

**What the documents say.** The Notice Letter (§ 2.2) identifies Rookwood as "an affiliate of Kestrel Student Coach, Inc." Kestrel is, by its name and the context of the transaction (acquiring a school bus parts distribution business), a school bus operator—i.e., a direct competitor of Vepous. Section 3.1 of the Supply Agreement requires Vepous to purchase **all** Fleet Components exclusively from Supplier (now Rookwood). Section 9.1 makes "pricing, volumes, forecasts, route and depot data and technical information" Confidential Information.

**Why it matters.** From December 1, 2026, Vepous's exclusive parts supplier will be a subsidiary of a competitor. Rookwood will have ongoing access to:

- Vepous's exact parts consumption by depot and vehicle type (revealing fleet utilization and maintenance costs),
- Vepous's rolling 12-month forecasts (revealing planned fleet changes),
- Vepous's route and depot data (revealing operational footprint),
- Vepous's pricing (revealing cost structure for bidding on school district contracts).

While § 9.1 of the Supply Agreement imposes confidentiality on Rookwood, it does not impose **information barriers** between Rookwood and its affiliate Kestrel. Section 9.2 permits disclosure to "Affiliates" who "need to know it and who are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as this Section 9." The definition of "Affiliate" (§ 1.2) is broad and would capture Kestrel. In practice, enforcing confidentiality against a corporate parent that is a direct competitor is extremely difficult.

The NDA's broader protections (which specifically enumerate "school district contract and operational information" in § 1.1(c)) do not bind Rookwood or Kestrel, as the NDA is a bilateral agreement between Vepous and Milon.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Negotiate an information barrier provision** as a condition of accepting the assignment. Proposed language for a new section or amendment to § 9:

   > "9.4 **Affiliate Firewall.** Supplier shall not disclose Customer's Confidential Information to any Affiliate of Supplier that operates a school bus fleet or provides pupil transportation services, or to any employee, officer or director of any such Affiliate, except to the extent strictly necessary to perform Supplier's obligations under this Agreement and only to personnel who have no operational role in any such Affiliate's transportation business. Supplier shall maintain written policies and technical safeguards to enforce this restriction and shall certify compliance annually upon Customer's request."

2. **Limit the scope of data shared.** Negotiate a data-minimization protocol: Vepous should share only the minimum forecast and depot data necessary for Rookwood to fulfill orders, and should not share school district contract terms, bid pricing, or strategic fleet planning information.

3. **Consider whether the exclusivity obligation (§ 3.1) should be narrowed** in connection with the assignment. If Vepous cannot trust Rookwood/Kestrel with its data, the exclusivity obligation (which is the quid pro quo for the fixed pricing and supply commitment) becomes harder to justify. At minimum, negotiate a right to source from a third party if Rookwood breaches the information barrier.

4. **Monitor Kestrel's bidding activity** on school district contracts in Vepous's territory. If Vepous detects that Kestrel is using Vepous's cost data to underbid, that is a § 9 breach and potentially a tortious interference claim. Document any such instances.

## Issue 5 – Insurance Continuity After Assignment

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 12.1–12.2; Milon Notice Letter § 2

**What the documents say.** Section 12.1 requires Supplier to maintain $5 million CGL (including products-completed operations) and $2 million product recall insurance during the Term and for three years after. Section 12.2 requires Supplier to name Customer as additional insured and to provide 30 days' notice of cancellation or material reduction.

The Notice Letter does not address Rookwood's insurance program.

**Why it matters.** If Rookwood's insurance is less than the required limits, or if it does not name Vepous as additional insured, Vepous's recovery in a product liability claim depends entirely on Rookwood's balance sheet (which is unguaranteed—see Issue 2). Given that Rookwood is a newly formed or recently restructured entity acquiring a business from Milon, its insurance program may not yet be in place.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the Assignment Date, require Rookwood to deliver certificates of insurance** confirming:
   - CGL of at least $5 million per occurrence and in the aggregate (including products-completed operations);
   - Product recall insurance of at least $2 million;
   - Vepous named as additional insured on the CGL policy;
   - 30 days' notice of cancellation or material reduction.

2. **Make delivery of these certificates a condition to Vepous's acceptance of the assignment.** If Rookwood cannot evidence compliant coverage by December 1, 2026, Vepous should notify Milon that the assignment is not effective as to Vepous until compliance is achieved, and reserve all rights.

If so, that information is competitively sensitive and should be shared only under a confidentiality undertaking or in a form that does not identify the specific entities.

## Issue 7 – Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment: Price Decreases Make the Dollar Threshold Harder to Meet

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 3.4; Amendment No. 1 § 2.1, § 2.4

**What the documents say.** The Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment is $1,850,000 in **invoiced value** per Contract Year (§ 3.4). Amendment No. 1 reduced most unit prices effective July 1, 2026 (e.g., brake pads from $186 to $179.50; steering gear from $1,145 to $1,098; child-check alarm kit from $689 to $664). Section 2.4 confirms the $1,850,000 threshold is unchanged and that products "count toward the Annual Minimum Purchase Commitment at their invoiced value."

**Why it matters.** Because the minimum is a dollar figure, not a unit figure, lower prices mean Vepous must purchase more units to reach $1.85 million. If Vepous's actual parts consumption (in units) remains constant, its invoiced spend will decline proportionally with the price cuts. For example, if Vepous was on track to spend $1.95 million in Contract Year 2 at old prices, a 4% average price reduction brings that to approximately $1.87 million—still above the threshold but with a much thinner margin. If consumption dips slightly (fewer breakdowns, a smaller fleet for a season), Vepous could fall below $1.85 million and owe a shortfall invoice under § 3.4.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Model Vepous's expected Contract Year 2 spend** (March 1, 2026 – February 28, 2027) using the amended prices and actual year-to-date purchase data. Identify the gap (if any) to $1.85 million.

2. **If the gap is material**, consider negotiating an increase in order volume (e.g., pulling forward maintenance work) or asking Rookwood to agree that the minimum for Contract Year 2 should be prorated for the period before July 1, 2026 at old prices and after at new prices.

3. **Going forward**, monitor quarterly. The forecast obligation under § 3.3 (due the 15th of each quarter) is non-binding, but it is the natural mechanism to flag a potential shortfall early.

## Issue 8 – Section 14.2 Assignment: No Customer Consent Right, but Notice Requirements Should Be Policed

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 14.2; Milon Notice Letter § 2

**What the documents say.** Section 14.2 permits Supplier to assign to "any Affiliate or to any purchaser of all or part of its business or assets" without Customer's consent, on 30 days' written notice. The assignee "shall assume Supplier's obligations under this Agreement in writing."

Milon's Notice Letter gives notice of assignment to Rookwood effective December 1, 2026—43 days after the letter date, satisfying the 30-day minimum. The letter states that "Before the Assignment Date, Rookwood will deliver to Customer a written instrument assuming Supplier's obligations."

**Why it matters.** The Assignment Date is December 1, 2026. Vepous should verify before that date that:
- Rookwood has actually delivered the written assumption instrument (as promised in Notice Letter § 2.3);
- The assumption instrument covers **all** of Supplier's obligations, including the insurance obligations (§ 12), the safety stock obligation (§ 5.5), the quality system certification (§ 7.1), and the indemnification obligations (§ 10.1);
- Rookwood has provided its notice address under § 15.5.

If the assumption instrument is not delivered or is deficient, the assignment may not be effective as to Vepous, and Milon remains the Supplier of record.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Send a pre-assignment checklist to Milon and Rookwood by November 15, 2026**, requiring delivery before December 1 of:
   - The written assumption instrument (signed by Rookwood);
   - Certificates of insurance (per Issue 5);
   - Rookwood's notice address and contact for Purchase Orders;
   - Confirmation that Rookwood will maintain the Hauppauge safety stock (§ 5.5);
   - The parent guaranty or amendment (per Issue 2).

2. **If any item is not delivered by December 1**, send a notice stating that Vepous does not recognize the assignment as effective until all conditions are met, and that all Purchase Orders and communications will continue to be directed to Milon in the interim.

## Issue 9 – NDA Does Not Bind Rookwood or Kestrel

**Documents:** Mutual NDA (entire); Supply Agreement § 9

**What the documents say.** The NDA is a bilateral agreement between Vepous and Milon. Its disclosure period runs two years from January 13, 2025 (i.e., until January 13, 2027), with obligations continuing three years after (until January 13, 2030, or indefinitely for trade secrets). The NDA does not contain a successor-or-assign clause that would bind Rookwood.

The Supply Agreement's § 9 confidentiality provisions bind "Supplier" (which after assignment is Rookwood) and survive for three years after the Agreement ends. However, § 9 is narrower than the NDA: it covers "pricing, volumes, forecasts, route and depot data and technical information" but does not explicitly cover "fleet size and composition, maintenance records, parts consumption data" or "school district contract" information the way NDA § 1.1 does.

**Why it matters.** Information Vepous shared with Milon during the pre-contract negotiation phase (covered by the NDA) may not be fully protected under the Supply Agreement's § 9 once Milon is no longer a party. If Rookwood or Kestrel accesses that pre-contract information (e.g., Vepous's fleet composition data, maintenance records, or school district contract terms shared during the evaluation phase), the NDA is Vepous's only remedy—and it runs only against Milon, not Rookwood.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Request that Milon, before the Assignment Date, certify that all Confidential Information disclosed under the NDA has been returned or destroyed** (NDA § 5.1), or that it has been transferred to Rookwood subject to the NDA's terms. If transferred, require Rookwood to execute a joinder to the NDA.

2. **Proposed NDA joinder language:**

   > "Rookwood Transit Supply, LLC ('Rookwood'), by its signature below, agrees to be bound by the Mutual Nondisclosure Agreement dated January 13, 2025 between Vepous Student Transportation, LLC and Milon Fleet Parts Corp. (the 'NDA') as if Rookwood were a Party thereto, with respect to all Confidential Information disclosed under the NDA or transferred to Rookwood in connection with the assignment of the Supply Agreement effective December 1, 2026. Rookwood assumes all obligations of Milon Fleet Parts Corp. under the NDA."

3. **Broaden § 9 of the Supply Agreement** (via Amendment No. 2 or the assumption instrument) to match the NDA's definition of Confidential Information, adding "fleet size and composition, maintenance records, parts consumption data, and school district contract information."

## Issue 11 – Section 13.3 Safety Termination: A Useful but Narrow Exit Right

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 13.3

**What the documents say.** Vepous may terminate on 30 days' notice if (a) Products are subject to two or more safety recalls in any Contract Year, or (b) a Defect causes a bodily injury incident involving a student.

**Why it matters.** This is Vepous's only unilateral termination right that does not depend on the supplier's breach. It is a safety valve, but it is narrow: it requires either two recalls in a year or a student injury. It does not cover a single recall, a near-miss, a regulatory citation, or a pattern of quality issues that falls short of a "Defect" as defined in § 1.9.

If the Change of Control negotiation (Issue 1) breaks down and Milon/Rookwood terminates under § 14.3, Vepous would lose its supply agreement and face the challenge of re-sourcing all Fleet Components on an emergency basis. The § 13.3 right is not available to Vepous in that scenario because it is the supplier, not Vepous, that is terminating.

**Recommendation.** No immediate action. But if Vepous is negotiating consent to the Larchfield Transaction (Issue 1), consider requesting that § 13.3 be broadened to include termination for "a pattern of quality non-conformance" or "loss of ISO 9001/IATF 16949 certification" (which is currently only a notice obligation under § 7.1, not a termination trigger). This would give Vepous more flexibility if Rookwood's quality standards are lower than Milon's.
