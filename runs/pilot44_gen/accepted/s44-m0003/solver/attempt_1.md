## 1. Quality Agreement Caps Vehicle Damage at $2,500 Per Vehicle, Overriding the Uncapped Position in the MSA and Term Sheet

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 8.4; MSA § 10.3(d); Term Sheet § 7.3(d); MSA § 1.4 (order of precedence).

**The problem.** MSA § 10.3(d) expressly excludes "loss of or damage to Vehicles in Provider's care, custody or control" from the 12-month fee cap in § 10.2. Term Sheet § 7.3(d) confirms this was the negotiated deal: vehicle damage is uncapped. Quality Agreement § 8.4 then states:

> "Notwithstanding Section 10.3(d) of the MSA, Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage, including Vehicle Damage arising from any failure to meet the Inspection Standards, is subject to the limitation of liability in Section 10.2 of the MSA. That limitation is fees paid or payable to Provider in the 12 months preceding the claim. Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage is further limited, for each Vehicle, to the reasonable cost of repairing that Vehicle, not to exceed $2,500 per Vehicle."

Because MSA § 1.4 gives the executed Quality Agreement top priority in the order of precedence, § 8.4 would override § 10.3(d). The practical effect is that if Fanul destroys, steals, or fails to inspect a vehicle worth $40,000, Butex's direct recovery is capped at $2,500.

**Why it matters.** Butex is a wholesale auction operator. Vehicles in its custody at any given time can range from $8,000 to well over $100,000. A $2,500 per-vehicle sub-cap is commercially unreasonable for the risk Fanul assumes under MSA § 6 (care, custody and control) and the QA's own inspection and lot-management obligations. It also creates an ambiguity with the indemnity: MSA § 10.3(a) excludes indemnification obligations from the cap, and QA § 8.4 only carves out § 10.3(d), not § 10.3(a). If a Consignor's vehicle is stolen and the Consignor sues Butex, Butex can arguably still recover in full from Fanul under the § 10.1(a) indemnity. But if Butex itself bears the loss (e.g., it has already made the Consignor whole out of pocket and is seeking reimbursement as a direct claimant), the $2,500 cap applies. This split creates litigation risk and undermines the clean allocation the term sheet intended.

**Recommendation.** Delete QA § 8.4 in its entirety. If Fanul resists, the minimum acceptable position is to strike the "Notwithstanding Section 10.3(d)" language and the $2,500 per-vehicle sub-cap, restoring the MSA's uncapped treatment of vehicle damage. Raise this as a deal-point with Fanul before execution.

## 2. Quality Agreement Imposes Texas Governing Law and Dallas Venue, Conflicting with the MSA and Term Sheet

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 11.1–11.2; MSA § 16.1–16.2; Term Sheet § 12.1–12.2.

**The problem.** The MSA and term sheet both provide for Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. Quality Agreement § 11 provides:

> § 11.1: "The laws of the State of Texas govern this Quality Agreement and all disputes arising out of or relating to the Services, without regard to conflict-of-laws principles."
>
> § 11.2: "Each Party submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in Dallas County, Texas for any action arising out of or relating to this Quality Agreement or the Services."

Because the QA has top precedence under MSA § 1.4, and because the QA's scope (§ 1.2) covers all four categories of Services, this provision would effectively pull virtually every operational dispute into Texas courts under Texas law.

**Why it matters.** Butex is a Delaware corporation. Its facilities are in Delaware (implied by the governing-law choice and the New Castle County forum). Litigating in Dallas under Texas law increases cost, eliminates home-court familiarity, and creates the risk that Texas substantive law (which may differ on issues like the standard of care, indemnity scope, or enforceability of liability caps) governs the very disputes the MSA was designed to resolve in Delaware.

**Recommendation.** Strike QA § 11 in its entirety. The MSA's governing law and forum provisions (§ 16.1–16.2) should control for all documents in the agreement stack. If Fanul insists on a separate governing-law clause for the QA, it must be conformed to Delaware law and New Castle County venue.

## 3. MSA Triples the Renewal Term and Narrows the Non-Renewal Notice Window Relative to the Term Sheet

**Where:** MSA § 12.2; Term Sheet § 4.2 and § 4.3.

**The problem.** The term sheet provided:

- § 4.2: automatic renewal for **one-year** renewal terms;
- § 4.3: non-renewal notice "at any time at least **60 days** before the end of the current term."

The MSA provides:

- § 12.2: automatic renewal for successive **three-year** Renewal Terms;
- § 12.2 (same section): non-renewal notice in a window of "not more than one hundred twenty (120) and not less than ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term."

**Why it matters.** The three-year renewal term locks Butex in for a much longer period than negotiated. If service quality degrades in year four, Butex cannot simply let the agreement lapse after one year; it is committed for three. The narrow 90–120-day non-renewal window (as opposed to "at least 60 days") creates a trap: if Butex's legal team misses the 90-day outside date by even one day, the non-renewal notice is ineffective and the agreement auto-renews for another three years. The term sheet's "at any time at least 60 days" formulation was far more forgiving.

**Recommendation.** Amend MSA § 12.2 to restore one-year renewal terms and the "at least 60 days" non-renewal notice standard from the term sheet. If Fanul insists on longer renewal terms, Butex should at minimum secure (a) a broader non-renewal window (e.g., "at least 90 days," with no outside date) and (b) a right to terminate for convenience on 180 days' notice at the end of any renewal term.

## 4. MSA Assignment Clause Is One-Way and Omits the "Competing Auction Operator" Restriction

**Where:** MSA § 15.2; Term Sheet § 9.1–9.2.

**The problem.** Term Sheet § 9.1 provided: "Neither party may assign without the other's consent; never to a Competing Auction Operator." Section 9.2 defined "Competing Auction Operator" as any person or affiliate operating a wholesale used-vehicle auction in competition with Butex.

MSA § 15.2 provides only: "Customer shall not assign this Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, without Provider's prior written consent, except to an affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates."

The MSA imposes no restriction on Provider's assignment. Fanul could assign the MSA to a subsidiary, a successor, or—even under the current language—an entity that Butex has no relationship with and no ability to vet, without Butex's consent.

**Why it matters.** Butex is giving Fanul access to its facilities, its vehicles, its consignor data, and its gate-release systems. An uncontrolled assignment could place all of that in the hands of an unknown or even competing entity. The term sheet's "never to a Competing Auction Operator" restriction was a material protection for Butex that has been dropped.

**Recommendation.** Amend MSA § 15.2 to make the assignment restriction mutual and to reinstate the "Competing Auction Operator" prohibition from Term Sheet § 9. Suggested language: "Neither Party shall assign this Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, without the other Party's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), and in no event to a Competing Auction Operator. 'Competing Auction Operator' has the meaning set out in [defined term]."

## 5. Workers' Compensation Insurer Does Not Meet the MSA's Minimum A.M. Best Rating

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 2.1 (table); MSA § 8.1 (preamble); Term Sheet § 13(a).

**The problem.** MSA § 8.1 requires all insurers to be "rated A- VII or better by A.M. Best." The cover letter's table lists:

| Insurer | Rating | Meets A- VII or better? |
|---|---|---|
| Trinity Mutual Casualty Co. | A VIII | Yes (A category is above A-) |
| Brazos Specialty Insurance Co. | A- VII | Yes (meets minimum exactly) |
| **Pecos Workers Indemnity Co.** | **A- VIII** | **No (A- VIII is below A- VII)** |
| Red River Excess Insurance Co. | A IX | Yes (A category is above A-) |

Pecos Workers Indemnity Company, which carries the workers' compensation and employer's liability policies, is rated A- VIII—one sub-level below the required A- VII. Cover Letter § 2.2 nonetheless states: "Each insurer listed above carries an A.M. Best rating of A- VII or better, as required by Section 8.1 of the MSA." That representation is inaccurate.

**Why it matters.** Workers' compensation coverage is mandatory under MSA § 8.1(d). If Fanul's personnel are injured on Butex's property, Butex faces potential third-party claims. An insurer below the agreed rating threshold presents a collectability risk. Additionally, Term Sheet § 13(a) makes "satisfactory due diligence by Butex on Provider's licensing, insurance and operational capacity" a condition to MSA completion. Butex may be able to argue the condition has not been satisfied.

**Recommendation.** Require Fanul to either (a) replace Pecos with an insurer rated A- VII or better, or (b) obtain Butex's written waiver of the rating requirement for this specific policy. Do not accept the cover letter's inaccurate § 2.2 representation at face value. Flag this to the client as a potential condition-precedent issue under Term Sheet § 13(a).

## 6. Quality Agreement Schedules A and B Are Missing

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 4.1, § 4.2, § 4.3, § 4.4, § 6.2, and § 13.2; signature page.

**The problem.** The QA's core operational standards are contained in Schedule A (Condition Grading Scale, Inspection Points and Photo Protocol) and Schedule B (Reconditioning Workmanship Standards). Both are marked "[To be attached.]" on the draft. QA § 4.1 provides that from the QA Effective Date, "the standards in this Section 4 and in Schedule A are the Inspection Standards for all purposes of the MSA." Without Schedule A, the Inspection Standards are undefined, and Provider's grading, photo, and inspection-point obligations (§§ 4.2–4.4) have no operative content.

**Why it matters.** Butex cannot execute a quality agreement whose central standards are placeholder text. If the QA is signed as-is, there is a risk that Fanul later supplies a Schedule A that is less protective than Butex expects, and Butex will have already agreed that those (unknown) standards "replace any inspection standards previously set out in a Statement of Work" (§ 4.1).

**Recommendation.** Do not execute the QA until Schedules A and B are attached and reviewed. Butex should confirm that the grading scale, the 36-photograph protocol, the inspection-point list, and the reconditioning workmanship standards meet Butex's operational requirements before signing.

## 7. Quality Agreement Incorporates Only Three MSA Sections, Creating a Fragmented Framework

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 13.1.

**The problem.** QA § 13.1 states: "This Quality Agreement is part of the MSA. Sections 9 (Confidentiality), 14 (Notices) and 15.6 (Counterparts) of the MSA apply to this Quality Agreement." It does not incorporate the MSA's indemnification (§ 10), limitation of liability (§ 10.2–10.4), representation and warranty (§ 11), or dispute-resolution (§ 16) provisions. Combined with the QA's own Texas governing law (§ 11), this means that for disputes "arising out of or relating to the Services" (the QA's entire subject matter), the MSA's indemnity and liability framework may not apply.

**Why it matters.** If a court or arbitrator reads the QA as a self-contained agreement governed by Texas law with only three MSA sections incorporated, Butex could lose the benefit of the MSA's indemnity, the consequential-damages waiver (which cuts both ways), and the Delaware forum. This compounds Issue 2 above.

**Recommendation.** Amend QA § 13.1 to incorporate the full MSA by reference, or at minimum to incorporate §§ 10, 11, and 16. Alternatively, delete QA § 11 and add a provision stating that the MSA's governing law, venue, and dispute-resolution provisions apply to the QA.

## 8. MSA Lacks a Convenience Termination Right for Butex

**Where:** MSA § 12 (Term and Termination); Term Sheet § 4.4.

**The problem.** The MSA provides termination only for cause (material breach with 30-day cure, insolvency) and for repeated service-level failures (§ 12.4, which applies to individual SOWs, not the MSA itself). There is no termination for convenience. The term sheet did not include one either, so this is not a deviation from the negotiated deal—but it is a structural gap worth flagging.

**Why it matters.** Butex is committing to a three-year initial term (and, under the current MSA, three-year renewals). If Butex's business strategy changes, if it decides to insource the services, or if Fanul's performance is chronically mediocre (failing to meet the § 12.4 threshold of three failures in a rolling six-month period), Butex has no clean exit short of proving material breach.

**Recommendation.** This is a business decision for the client. If Butex wants flexibility, negotiate a termination-for-convenience right exercisable on 180 days' notice after the first 12 months, with a reasonable transition fee. At minimum, confirm with the client that it is comfortable with the current termination framework.
