## Summary

I have reviewed the four documents in the matter folder: the Master Services Agreement ("MSA"), the Term Sheet, Fanul's draft Quality Agreement ("QA"), and the insurance certificate cover letter. The MSA is broadly consistent with the Term Sheet on most commercial points, but the Quality Agreement contains two provisions that materially alter the risk allocation Butex negotiated, and the MSA deviates from the Term Sheet in three areas that favor Fanul. I have identified seven issues, listed in order of significance.

---

## Issue 1 – Quality Agreement Caps Vehicle Damage at $2,500 Per Vehicle, Overriding the MSA's Uncapped Protection

**Where:** QA § 8.4; MSA § 10.3(d); Term Sheet § 7.3(d)

**Explanation:** MSA § 10.3(d) expressly excludes "loss of or damage to Vehicles in Provider's care, custody or control" from the general liability cap in § 10.2. The Term Sheet § 7.3(d) confirms this was a negotiated point. However, QA § 8.4 states:

> "Notwithstanding Section 10.3(d) of the MSA, Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage, including Vehicle Damage arising from any failure to meet the Inspection Standards, is subject to the limitation of liability in Section 10.2 of the MSA."

It then adds a further per-vehicle sub-cap: "Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage is further limited, for each Vehicle, to the reasonable cost of repairing that Vehicle, not to exceed $2,500 per Vehicle."

Because MSA § 1.4 gives the executed Quality Agreement top priority in the order of precedence, signing the QA as drafted would strip Butex of the uncapped vehicle-damage protection and replace it with a $2,500-per-vehicle ceiling. At a wholesale auction where individual vehicles routinely carry values well above $2,500, a single yard collision or reconditioning error could leave Butex absorbing most of the loss.

**Recommendation:** Delete QA § 8.4 in its entirety, or revise it to confirm that MSA § 10.3(d) applies and that Vehicle Damage is excluded from the § 10.2 cap. Do not accept any per-vehicle sub-cap. Raise this as a deal-breaker with Fanul before execution.

---

## Issue 2 – Quality Agreement Specifies Texas Governing Law and Venue, Contradicting the MSA and Term Sheet

**Where:** QA § 11.1–11.2; MSA § 16.1–16.2; Term Sheet § 12.1–12.2

**Explanation:** The MSA and Term Sheet both specify Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware. QA § 11.1 provides that "the laws of the State of Texas govern this Quality Agreement and all disputes arising out of or relating to the Services," and § 11.2 submits the parties to "the state and federal courts located in Dallas County, Texas."

Under the MSA's order of precedence (§ 1.4), the QA controls over the MSA. As drafted, this would create a split-regime: quality and service-level disputes (which are likely the most frequent in this relationship) would be litigated in Dallas under Texas law, while other disputes (e.g., payment, confidentiality) would remain in Delaware. This increases Butex's litigation cost and complexity and cedes home-field advantage to Fanul on the issues most likely to arise.

**Recommendation:** Amend QA § 11 to conform to MSA § 16 (Delaware law, New Castle County venue). Alternatively, delete QA § 11 entirely and rely on MSA § 16, which already governs "any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement" (and the QA is part of the Agreement under QA § 13.1).

## Issue 3 – MSA Auto-Renews for Three-Year Terms Instead of the One-Year Terms in the Term Sheet, with a Narrower Non-Renewal Window

**Where:** MSA § 12.2; Term Sheet §§ 4.2–4.3

**Explanation:** The Term Sheet § 4.2 provides for "one-year renewal terms," and § 4.3 allows either party to prevent renewal by notice "at any time at least 60 days before the end of the current term." The MSA § 12.2 instead provides for "successive three (3) year Renewal Terms" and requires non-renewal notice to be delivered "not more than one hundred twenty (120) and not less than ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term."

This is a significant deviation. If Butex misses the 90-day deadline (for example, because the decision is delayed internally), it is locked in for another three years rather than one. The narrower 90–120 day window also creates a trap: notice given at 89 days is ineffective, and notice given at 121 days is likewise ineffective.

**Recommendation:** Amend MSA § 12.2 to (a) reduce the renewal term to one year, consistent with the Term Sheet, and (b) broaden the non-renewal notice window to "at least 60 days before the end of the then-current term" without an outer limit. If Fanul insists on three-year renewals, Butex should at minimum get the outer limit removed so that notice can be given early without risk of being "too soon."

## Issue 4 – MSA Assignment Clause Restricts Only Customer; Omits the "Competing Auction Operator" Prohibition from the Term Sheet

**Where:** MSA § 15.2; Term Sheet §§ 9.1–9.2

**Explanation:** Term Sheet § 9.1 provides that "[n]either party may assign without the other's consent; never to a Competing Auction Operator," and § 9.2 defines "Competing Auction Operator" as any person operating a wholesale used-vehicle auction in competition with Butex.

MSA § 15.2 restricts only Customer's assignment (requiring Provider's consent, with exceptions for affiliates and successors). It contains no restriction on Provider's assignment and no prohibition on assignment to a competitor. As drafted, Fanul could assign the MSA to a company that operates a competing auction, potentially giving that competitor access to Butex's facilities, data, and operational knowledge.

**Recommendation:** Rewrite MSA § 15.2 to make the assignment restriction mutual and to add the "Competing Auction Operator" prohibition from Term Sheet § 9. Suggested language: "Neither Party shall assign this Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, without the other Party's prior written consent, which shall not be unreasonably withheld. No Party may assign to a Competing Auction Operator [defined per Term Sheet § 9.2]. Customer may assign to an affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates without consent."

## Issue 5 – Quality Agreement Schedules A and B Are Missing

**Where:** QA § 13.2; QA pp. 3 (Schedule A and B placeholders)

**Explanation:** The QA references two schedules that are integral to its operation: Schedule A (Condition Grading Scale, Inspection Points and Photo Protocol) and Schedule B (Reconditioning Workmanship Standards). Both are marked "[To be attached.]" Without these schedules, the Inspection Standards (QA § 4.1) are undefined, the Condition Grading scale (QA § 4.2) has no reference points, the 36-photograph protocol (QA § 4.4) has no angle/lighting specifications, and the reconditioning workmanship standards (QA § 6.2) are empty.

QA § 4.1 states that the standards in Section 4 and Schedule A "replace any inspection standards previously set out in a Statement of Work." If the QA is executed without the schedules, the replacement language could be read as vacating the SOW-based inspection standards while providing no substitute, leaving a gap in the contractual basis for evaluating condition-report accuracy.

**Recommendation:** Do not execute the QA until Schedules A and B are attached and reviewed. Confirm with the client what grading scale and photo protocol Butex expects (these should reflect Butex's existing internal standards, not Fanul's). Also confirm that the SOW-based inspection standards remain in effect until the QA (with complete schedules) is executed, consistent with MSA § 1.1(f).

## Issue 6 – MSA Conditions Ownership of Deliverables on Payment in Full

**Where:** MSA § 7.2; Term Sheet § 10.2

**Explanation:** MSA § 7.2 provides that condition reports, photographs, inspection data and other deliverables "become the property of Customer upon payment in full." The Term Sheet § 10.2 uses the same formulation.

The practical risk: Butex has the right to withhold disputed amounts in good faith (MSA § 4.5). If Butex withholds a portion of an invoice in dispute, Fanul could argue that the deliverables covered by that invoice have not yet vested in Butex. In a wholesale auction context, condition reports are time-sensitive; if ownership is in limbo during a payment dispute, Butex could be hampered in using the reports for ongoing auctions or in responding to buyer or consignor claims.

**Recommendation:** Revise MSA § 7.2 to vest ownership in Customer upon delivery of the deliverable, not upon payment. Payment remains an obligation under § 4, but ownership should not be held hostage to it. Suggested language: "Condition reports, photographs, inspection data and other deliverables prepared for Customer under this Agreement are the property of Customer upon delivery."

## Issue 7 – Insurance Certificate: Verify Red River Excess Rating Meets the MSA Threshold

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 2.1 (table), § 2.2; MSA § 8.1

**Explanation:** MSA § 8.1 requires insurers rated "A- VII or better by A.M. Best." The cover letter's table lists Red River Excess Insurance Company (umbrella policy) with a rating of "A IX." Cover letter § 2.2 asserts that "[e]ach insurer listed above carries an A.M. Best rating of A- VII or better."

The rating "A IX" is ambiguous relative to the "A- VII" threshold. If the Roman numerals denote sub-levels where a higher number indicates a weaker rating (i.e., A- I is stronger than A- VII), then "A IX" would fall below the required minimum and the § 2.2 assertion would be incorrect. I cannot resolve this from the documents alone because the rating scale is not defined in any of the four files.

**Recommendation:** Ask the client to confirm with Lonestar (or directly with A.M. Best) where "A IX" falls relative to "A- VII" on the applicable scale. If Red River Excess does not meet the threshold, require Fanul to replace the umbrella carrier or obtain a waiver. Also confirm that the garage keepers location schedule (Enclosure 5) lists all initial SOW Facilities.

## Items I Could Not Resolve from the Documents Alone

1. **Insurance rating scale (Issue 7):** The relative strength of "A IX" versus "A- VII" is not defined in any document provided.

2. **SOW content:** No Statements of Work are in the folder. Several MSA and QA provisions (turnaround times, staffing levels, Facility-specific requirements, driving record standards) are deferred to the SOWs. Butex's exposure on those points cannot be assessed until the SOWs are drafted.

3. **Butex's existing inspection standards:** QA § 4.1 replaces SOW-based standards with the QA standards, but we do not have Butex's current internal grading protocol to confirm that Schedule A (when provided) will match or exceed it.

4. **Facility count and locations:** The insurance certificate references "initial Statements of Work" for the garage keepers schedule, but we do not know how many Facilities are in scope or whether all are listed on Enclosure 5.
