## Executive Summary

I have reviewed the four documents in the matter folder: the Master Services Agreement (MSA), the Term Sheet, Fanul's draft Quality Agreement (QA), and the insurance certificate cover letter. The MSA is broadly consistent with the Term Sheet, but Fanul's draft Quality Agreement contains two provisions that fundamentally alter the risk allocation Butex agreed to in the Term Sheet, and the MSA itself departs from the Term Sheet in several material respects. The most urgent issue is that the QA, if executed as drafted, would cap Fanul's liability for vehicle damage at $2,500 per vehicle—directly contradicting the Term Sheet and the MSA's own exclusion of vehicle damage from the liability cap.

---

## Issue 1 – Quality Agreement Caps Vehicle Damage Liability at $2,500 Per Vehicle, Overriding the MSA

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Section 8.4; MSA, Section 10.3(d); Term Sheet, Section 7.3(d).

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 7.3(d) agreed that "loss of or damage to vehicles in Provider's care, custody or control" would be **uncapped**.
- MSA § 10.3(d) implements that agreement: the liability cap in § 10.2 "does not apply to … loss of or damage to Vehicles in Provider's care, custody or control."
- QA § 8.4 states: "Notwithstanding Section 10.3(d) of the MSA, Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage … is subject to the limitation of liability in Section 10.2 of the MSA." It then adds a further per-vehicle cap: "Provider's liability for Vehicle Damage is further limited, for each Vehicle, to the reasonable cost of repairing that Vehicle, not to exceed **$2,500 per Vehicle**."

Because MSA § 1.4 gives the executed Quality Agreement top priority in the order of precedence, QA § 8.4 would control over MSA § 10.3(d) if the QA is signed as drafted.

**Why it matters:** Butex operates wholesale auctions where vehicles in Fanul's custody can range from $8,000 to well over $50,000. A $2,500 per-vehicle cap means that if Fanul's driver backs a forklift into a $40,000 truck, or a reconditioning bay fire destroys a lot, Butex's maximum recovery from Fanul is $2,500 per damaged vehicle. The garage keepers insurance ($1.5M per location) would be the primary recovery source, but if the insurer denies or limits a claim, Butex's contractual recourse against Fanul is negligible. This is a fundamental departure from the risk allocation Butex agreed to in the Term Sheet.

**Recommendation:**

- Delete QA § 8.4 in its entirety, or revise it to confirm that vehicle damage remains uncapped per MSA § 10.3(d).
- If Fanul resists full uncapped exposure, a possible compromise is to raise the per-vehicle sub-limit to a figure that reflects actual vehicle values (e.g., $50,000 per vehicle) and to make the garage keepers insurance the exclusive remedy only up to the policy limit, with Fanul remaining liable above that.
- This point should be raised with Fanul before the QA is executed. Do not sign the QA as drafted.

## Issue 2 – Quality Agreement Imposes Texas Governing Law and Venue, Contradicting the MSA

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Section 11.1–11.2; MSA, Section 16.1–16.2; Term Sheet, Section 12.1–12.2.

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 12.1: Delaware law. § 12.2: exclusive forum in New Castle County, Delaware.
- MSA § 16.1: Delaware law. § 16.2: exclusive jurisdiction in New Castle County, Delaware.
- QA § 11.1: "The laws of the State of **Texas** govern this Quality Agreement and **all disputes arising out of or relating to the Services**."
- QA § 11.2: exclusive jurisdiction in **Dallas County, Texas**.

The QA's governing-law clause is drafted to cover not just the QA itself but "all disputes arising out of or relating to the Services"—which would sweep in the MSA and the SOWs. Combined with the MSA § 1.4 order of precedence (QA > MSA > SOW), this would effectively move the entire relationship to Texas law and Dallas venue.

**Why it matters:** Butex is a Delaware corporation. Its operations, its consignors, its buyers, and its facilities are in Delaware. Delaware law and forum were a negotiated term. Texas law may differ on issues of bailment, indemnification enforceability, and limitation-of-liability interpretation. More practically, litigating in Dallas County imposes significant travel and cost burdens on Butex and its counsel.

**Recommendation:**

- Delete QA § 11 in its entirety. The MSA already contains governing law and forum provisions that apply to the entire agreement (including the QA, which is "part of the MSA" per QA § 13.1).
- If Fanul insists on a standalone governing-law clause in the QA, it must conform to Delaware law and New Castle County venue.
- Raise this with Fanul immediately; it appears to be a boilerplate insertion from Fanul's Texas-based counsel that was not negotiated.

## Issue 3 – MSA Provides Three-Year Automatic Renewal Terms Instead of the Agreed One-Year Terms

**Where:** MSA, Section 12.2; Term Sheet, Section 4.2.

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 4.2: "After the initial term, the MSA will renew automatically for **one-year** renewal terms."
- MSA § 12.2: "this Agreement shall automatically renew for successive **three (3) year** Renewal Terms."

**Why it matters:** A three-year auto-renewal locks Butex into a long commitment with a service provider it may want to replace. If Fanul's performance is mediocre but not terminable (see Issue 7 below), Butex would be stuck for three years rather than one. The longer the renewal term, the less leverage Butex has at renewal to renegotiate pricing or service levels.

**Recommendation:**

- Amend MSA § 12.2 to provide for one-year renewal terms, consistent with the Term Sheet.
- Alternatively, if Fanul insists on longer renewal terms, shorten the non-renewal notice window (see Issue 4) and add a price-escalation cap for renewal terms.

## Issue 4 – Non-Renewal Notice Window Is Longer and Narrower Than Agreed

**Where:** MSA, Section 12.2; Term Sheet, Section 4.3.

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 4.3: "Either Party may prevent renewal by written notice at any time **at least 60 days** before the end of the current term."
- MSA § 12.2: notice must be given "not more than **one hundred twenty (120)** and not less than **ninety (90)** days before the end of the then-current term."

The MSA creates a narrow 30-day window (days 90–120 before term end) during which notice must be given. If Butex's general counsel is on leave or the decision is delayed past day 120, the renewal is locked in. The Term Sheet allowed notice at any time up to 60 days before term end—a much more flexible arrangement.

**Why it matters:** Combined with the three-year renewal term (Issue 3), missing the 90–120 day window means Butex is committed for another three years. This is a significant operational risk.

**Recommendation:**

- Amend MSA § 12.2 to restore the Term Sheet's formulation: notice at any time at least 60 days before the end of the then-current term. Eliminate the "not more than 120 days" outer limit.

## Issue 5 – Assignment Clause Protects Only Butex; Missing "Competing Auction Operator" Bar

**Where:** MSA, Section 15.2; Term Sheet, Sections 9.1–9.2.

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 9.1: "**Neither party** may assign without the other's consent; **never to a Competing Auction Operator**."
- Term Sheet § 9.2 defines "Competing Auction Operator" as any person or affiliate that operates a wholesale used-vehicle auction in competition with Butex.
- MSA § 15.2: "**Customer** shall not assign this Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, without Provider's prior written consent, except to an affiliate or to a successor …"

The MSA restricts only Butex's assignment. It is silent on Provider's assignment. The absolute bar on assignment to a Competing Auction Operator—present in the Term Sheet—is entirely absent from the MSA.

**Why it matters:** Fanul could sell or assign the contract to a competitor of Butex (or to an entity whose parent operates a competing auction) without Butex's consent. Butex's consignor relationships, vehicle data, and operational workflows would then be in the hands of a direct competitor. The Term Sheet clearly contemplated this risk and addressed it; the MSA does not.

**Recommendation:**

- Amend MSA § 15.2 to make the assignment restriction mutual and to add the Term Sheet's "Competing Auction Operator" definition and absolute bar. Suggested language: "Neither Party shall assign this Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, without the other Party's prior written consent. Notwithstanding the foregoing, neither Party may assign this Agreement to a Competing Auction Operator. 'Competing Auction Operator' means any person, or any affiliate of a person, that operates or owns a wholesale used-vehicle auction, whether physical or online, in competition with Customer."
- Add a carve-out for Butex's assignment to an affiliate or successor (as currently drafted), but no equivalent carve-out for Fanul.

## Issue 6 – Quality Agreement Schedules A and B Are Missing, Yet QA § 4.1 Replaces SOW Inspection Standards

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Sections 4.1, 13.2; signature page (Schedules A and B marked "[To be attached]").

**Analysis:**

QA § 4.1 provides: "From the QA Effective Date, the standards in this Section 4 and in Schedule A are the Inspection Standards for all purposes of the MSA. **They replace any inspection standards previously set out in a Statement of Work.**"

However, Schedule A (Condition Grading Scale, Inspection Points and Photo Protocol) and Schedule B (Reconditioning Workmanship Standards) are both marked "[To be attached]" and are not included in the draft.

**Why it matters:** If the QA is executed without the Schedules, the "Inspection Standards" would be undefined, yet they have already displaced whatever standards were in the SOWs. This creates a gap: Fanul would be performing inspections against no defined standard, and Butex would have no objective basis to claim a condition report is inaccurate (QA § 5.1) or to enforce the 36-photograph requirement (QA § 4.4). The SOWs that currently contain inspection standards would be superseded with nothing in their place.

**Recommendation:**

- Do not execute the QA until Schedules A and B are attached and reviewed.
- Butex should confirm that the grading scale, inspection points, photo protocol, and workmanship standards in the Schedules are consistent with what Butex's auction operations require.
- Add a condition in the QA that it does not take effect (or that § 4.1 does not operate to replace SOW standards) until both Schedules are attached and initialed by both Parties.

## Issue 7 – Termination for Repeated Service Failures Is Narrower Than the Term Sheet Contemplates

**Where:** MSA, Section 12.4; Term Sheet, Section 4.4.

**Analysis:**

- Term Sheet § 4.4: "Butex may also terminate an affected Statement of Work for **repeated service-level failures**." (No further qualification.)
- MSA § 12.4: "Customer may terminate the affected Statement of Work by written notice if Provider fails **the same service level** in any **three (3) months within a rolling six (6) month period**."

The MSA requires the *same* service level to fail in 3 out of 6 months. If Fanul misses condition-report turnaround in January, lot-location accuracy in March, and reconditioning completion in May, Butex cannot terminate under § 12.4 because no single service level failed three times.

**Why it matters:** A provider that chronically underperforms across multiple service levels—without any single level failing three times—would be insulated from this termination right. Butex would be limited to the general material-breach termination in § 12.3(a), which requires a 30-day cure period and a higher threshold of "materiality."

**Recommendation:**

- Amend MSA § 12.4 to allow termination if Provider fails **any** service level (not just the same one) in three months within a rolling six-month period. Alternatively, add a separate trigger: "or if Provider fails two or more different Service Levels in any three consecutive months."

## Issue 8 – Quality Agreement Incorporates Only Three MSA Sections, Creating Ambiguity

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Section 13.1.

**Analysis:**

QA § 13.1 states: "This Quality Agreement is part of the MSA. Sections 9 (Confidentiality), 14 (Notices) and 15.6 (Counterparts) of the MSA apply to this Quality Agreement."

It does not expressly incorporate the MSA's indemnification (§ 10), limitation of liability (§ 10.2–10.4), insurance (§ 8), or any other protective provisions. While one could argue that because the QA is "part of the MSA" all MSA provisions apply by default, the express incorporation of only three sections suggests the drafter intended a limited set of MSA provisions to apply. This ambiguity could be exploited by Fanul to argue, for example, that the MSA's indemnification obligation in § 10.1 does not extend to claims arising under the QA.

**Why it matters:** In a dispute over vehicle damage or a data breach, Fanul could argue that the QA's own terms (including its liability cap in § 8.4) govern exclusively, and that the MSA's broader indemnification and uncapped-liability provisions do not apply to QA-related claims.

**Recommendation:**

- Amend QA § 13.1 to state that all provisions of the MSA apply to the Quality Agreement unless expressly modified by the QA. Alternatively, expressly incorporate MSA §§ 6, 8, 10, and 11.
- This is particularly important given Issues 1 and 2 above, where the QA expressly overrides MSA provisions. The incorporation clause should make clear that any override must be express and specific.

## Issue 9 – Deliverables Ownership Conditioned on Payment in Full

**Where:** MSA, Section 7.2; Term Sheet, Section 10.2.

**Analysis:**

MSA § 7.2: "Condition reports, photographs, inspection data and other deliverables prepared for Customer under this Agreement become the property of Customer **upon payment in full**."

The Term Sheet (§ 10.2) uses the same formulation ("once paid for"), so this is not a deviation from the Term Sheet. However, it creates a potential leverage point: if Butex withholds payment on a disputed invoice (per MSA § 4.5), Fanul could argue that condition reports prepared during the dispute period do not yet belong to Butex.

**Why it matters:** In a wholesale auction, condition reports must be available to buyers in real time. If Fanul could withhold or claim ownership of reports during a billing dispute, Butex's auction operations would be disrupted.

**Recommendation:**

- Consider amending MSA § 7.2 to provide that deliverables become Customer's property upon creation (or upon delivery to Customer's platform), with payment in full as a condition only to Fanul's right to retain copies for its own records. Alternatively, add: "A good-faith dispute over any portion of an invoice shall not delay transfer of ownership of deliverables."

## Issue 10 – Insurance Certificate: A.M. Best Rating Notation Warrants Verification

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter, Section 2.1 (table); MSA, Section 8.1.

**Analysis:**

The MSA requires insurers rated "A- VII or better by A.M. Best." The certificate lists:

| Insurer | Rating Shown |
|---|---|
| Trinity Mutual Casualty Company | A VIII |
| Brazos Specialty Insurance Company | A- VII |
| Pecos Workers Indemnity Company | A- VIII |
| Red River Excess Insurance Company | A IX |

The broker's cover letter (§ 2.2) states: "Each insurer listed above carries an A.M. Best rating of A- VII or better, as required by Section 8.1 of the MSA."

The notation "A VIII" and "A IX" is non-standard for A.M. Best (which typically uses A++, A+, A, A-, B++, etc.). If the rating scale here is such that higher Roman numerals within a letter category denote weaker financial strength (i.e., A- IX is weaker than A- VII), then the umbrella insurer (Red River Excess, A IX) and the CGL/auto insurer (Trinity, A VIII) may not meet the "A- VII or better" threshold. However, the broker affirmatively states compliance.

**Why it matters:** If any insurer is actually below the required rating, the insurance program may not satisfy MSA § 8.1, and Butex could be exposed if a claim exceeds the financial capacity of a weaker insurer.

**Recommendation:**

- Flag for the client: verify the A.M. Best ratings directly with A.M. Best or request that Fanul's broker provide a written confirmation of the exact rating designation and what "A- VII or better" means on the scale used. This cannot be fully resolved from the documents alone.

## Issue 11 – Quality Agreement Section 5.1: 0.5-Grade Accuracy Tolerance May Be Too Generous

**Where:** Quality Agreement, Section 5.1(a).

**Analysis:**

QA § 5.1(a) defines an accurate condition report as one where "the Condition Grade is within **0.5** of the grade determined on re-inspection." On a 0.0–5.0 scale, a 0.5 tolerance represents 10% of the full range. A vehicle that is truly a 2.0 (significant damage) could be reported as a 2.5 (moderate damage) and still be "accurate."

**Why it matters:** In a wholesale auction, buyers price vehicles based on condition grades. A half-grade inflation systematically overstates vehicle condition, which can lead to buyer disputes, arbitration claims, and reputational harm to Butex's auction. The 0.5 tolerance may be too generous to protect Butex's buyer relationships.

**Recommendation:**

- This is a business judgment call for the client. If Butex's operations require tighter accuracy, propose reducing the tolerance to 0.3 or 0.25.
- Note that QA § 5.2 already requires Fanul to investigate buyer arbitration claims alleging inaccuracy, so Butex has a remedial path even if the report was technically "accurate" under § 5.1.
