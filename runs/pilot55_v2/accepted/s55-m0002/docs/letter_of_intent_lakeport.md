**Re: Proposed Acquisition of Lakeport Skykitchen, Inc.**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

This letter of intent (this "**Letter**") sets out the principal terms on which **Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC**, a Delaware limited liability company ("**Buyer**"), proposes to acquire all of the issued and outstanding capital stock (the "**Shares**") of **Lakeport Skykitchen, Inc.**, a Delaware corporation (the "**Company**"). The Shares are currently owned by **Dulaix Hospitality Holdings, Inc.**, a Delaware corporation ("**Seller**"). We refer to the proposed acquisition as the "**Transaction**."

Except as provided in Section 12, this Letter is an expression of intent only. It is not a binding agreement to complete the Transaction.

## 1. Structure

Buyer would purchase from Seller all of the Shares, free and clear of all liens, under a definitive stock purchase agreement (the "**Purchase Agreement**"). After the closing (the "**Closing**"), the Company would become a wholly owned subsidiary of Buyer. It would continue to conduct its airline catering, onboard provisioning and galley services business (the "**Business**").

## 2. Purchase Price

**2.1 Amount.** The aggregate purchase price for the Shares would be $48,000,000 (the "**Purchase Price**"), payable as follows:

(a) $42,000,000 in cash at the Closing, subject to the adjustments described in Section 3; and

(b) $6,000,000 by delivery of a subordinated promissory note made by Buyer in favor of Seller (the "**Seller Note**").

**2.2 Basis.** The Purchase Price assumes that the Company will be delivered on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normalized level of net working capital. Indebtedness for borrowed money of the Company would be repaid at the Closing from the cash portion of the Purchase Price under customary payoff letters.

## 3. Working Capital Adjustment

The Purchase Agreement would provide for a customary net working capital adjustment against a target of $5,400,000. Seller would deliver an estimate before the Closing, and the cash payable at the Closing would be adjusted for any excess or shortfall. After the Closing, Seller would prepare a closing statement. Buyer would then have a period to review and dispute it, and any unresolved items would go to an independent accounting firm. The parties would set the specific periods and accounting principles in the Purchase Agreement.

## 4. Seller Note

The Seller Note would have the following principal terms:

(a) *Principal:* $6,000,000.

(b) *Interest:* six percent (6%) per annum, payable quarterly in arrears.

(c) *Maturity:* twelve (12) months after the Closing, with prepayment permitted at any time without premium or penalty.

(d) *Subordination:* subordinated to Buyer's senior acquisition financing under a subordination agreement with Buyer's senior lender on terms customary for seller financing.

(e) *Setoff:* Buyer may set off amounts finally determined to be owed to Buyer under the indemnification and purchase price adjustment provisions of the Purchase Agreement.

The remaining terms of the Seller Note, including events of default, would be negotiated in the definitive documentation.

## 5. Representations, Warranties and Indemnification

The Purchase Agreement would contain representations, warranties, covenants and indemnification provisions customary for a transaction of this type. These would include representations regarding organization, authority, capitalization, financial statements, material contracts, customers, employees, taxes, compliance and permits (including food safety and airport security requirements), litigation and environmental matters. Survival periods, deductibles and caps would be negotiated in the Purchase Agreement on terms customary for transactions of this size.

## 6. Key Employee

Buyer regards the continued service of Marisol Okafor-Brandt, the Company's Vice President of Operations, as essential to the Transaction. It would be a condition to Closing that Ms. Okafor-Brandt enter into a key employee agreement with the Company (the "**Key Employee Agreement**"), effective as of the Closing. Buyer anticipates the Key Employee Agreement would include the following terms:

(a) continued employment as Vice President of Operations for an initial term of two (2) years;

(b) base salary, annual bonus opportunity and a retention bonus to be agreed with Ms. Okafor-Brandt;

(c) customary severance on a termination without cause or a resignation for good reason, conditioned on a general release;

(d) a covenant not to solicit all customers of the Company, during employment and for twenty-four (24) months after employment ends;

(e) a covenant not to solicit or hire Company employees at the level of supervisor or above for the same period; and

(f) customary confidentiality and work product provisions.

Buyer would be entitled to enforce the restrictive covenants in the Key Employee Agreement directly.

## 7. Seller Covenants

The Purchase Agreement would include a covenant by Seller and its Affiliates not to compete with the Business in the United States, and not to solicit Company employees at the level of manager or above, for three (3) years after the Closing.

## 8. Seller Credit Support

Buyer understands that Seller has provided guarantees and other credit support in respect of certain of the Company's airport concession agreements and commissary leases. The parties would address the release or replacement of that credit support after the Closing, and the allocation of related responsibilities, in the Purchase Agreement.

## 9. Galley Equipment

Buyer understands that certain galley equipment used in the Business is owned by Seller rather than the Company. At the Closing, Seller would transfer that equipment to the Company under a bill of sale, for no consideration beyond the Purchase Price.

## 10. Due Diligence; Conditions

**10.1 Diligence.** Buyer's obligations would be subject to completion of a business, financial, tax, legal, operational and environmental due diligence review satisfactory to Buyer. Seller would cause the Company to give Buyer and its representatives reasonable access to the Company's facilities, books, records, contracts and personnel.

**10.2 Conditions.** The Closing would be subject to customary conditions. These would include the accuracy of representations and warranties, performance of covenants, receipt of required third-party consents (including airline customers and airport authorities, as applicable), execution of the Key Employee Agreement and the absence of any material adverse change in the Business.

**10.3 Financing.** Buyer intends to fund the cash portion of the Purchase Price with a combination of equity and senior debt financing. The Purchase Agreement would not include a financing condition.

## 11. Timing

The parties would aim to sign the Purchase Agreement by July 15, 2026 and to close the Transaction on or about August 3, 2026, subject to completion of due diligence and satisfaction of closing conditions.

## 12. Binding Provisions

Only this Section 12 and Sections 13 through 17 (the "**Binding Provisions**") are legally binding on the parties. No other provision of this Letter creates any binding obligation. No party has any obligation to negotiate or complete the Transaction except under a Purchase Agreement executed and delivered by both parties.

## 13. Exclusivity

From the date Seller countersigns this Letter until the earlier of (a) July 31, 2026 and (b) the date either party notifies the other in writing that it has ended negotiations (the "**Exclusivity Period**"), Seller shall not, and shall cause the Company and their respective representatives not to, solicit, encourage, negotiate or enter into any agreement with any Person other than Buyer regarding any sale of the Company, the Shares or any material portion of the Business. Seller shall promptly inform Buyer of any unsolicited proposal of that kind received during the Exclusivity Period.

## 14. Confidentiality

The terms of this Letter and all information exchanged in connection with the Transaction are subject to the Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement between Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc. and Seller. That agreement remains in full force and effect in accordance with its terms.

## 15. Expenses

Each party shall bear its own expenses in connection with this Letter and the Transaction, whether or not the Transaction is completed.

## 16. Governing Law

This Letter shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles.

## 17. General

This Letter may be executed in counterparts, including by electronic signature. Each counterpart is an original, and all of them together constitute one instrument. This proposal expires if Seller has not countersigned and returned this Letter by 5:00 p.m. Eastern Time on May 25, 2026.
