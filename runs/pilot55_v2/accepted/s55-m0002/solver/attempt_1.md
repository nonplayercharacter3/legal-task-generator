## Issue 1: Dispute Notice Delivered Outside the Contractual Deadline

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(b); Trenex Letter to Dulaix (Oct. 9, 2026), ¶ 1.1

**Explanation:** SPA § 2.5(b) requires Buyer to deliver a Dispute Notice "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement." The Closing Statement was delivered by email on September 2, 2026 (Letter ¶ 1.1). Thirty days from September 2 is **October 2, 2026**. The Dispute Notice was delivered **October 9, 2026**—seven days late.

Section 2.5(b) provides that if Buyer does not deliver a timely Dispute Notice, "the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Net Working Capital shall be final and binding on the parties." Seller has a strong textual argument that the entire $1,150,000 dispute (obsolete inventory, aged receivables, omitted payroll accruals, and the concession fee accrual) is time-barred and that the $5,620,000 figure in the Closing Statement is final.

This matters because it directly puts at risk Trenex's recovery of $1,150,000 that it overpaid at Closing (the difference between the $5,620,000 Seller reported and the $4,470,000 Trenex calculates under the Accounting Principles in Schedule 2.4).

**Recommended Fix:**

- Immediately assess whether there is any basis to argue the deadline was tolled, extended, or waived (e.g., course of dealing, Seller's conduct in preparing the Closing Statement, or a mutual understanding). Review all correspondence between September 2 and October 9 for any statement by Seller that could constitute a waiver or estoppel.
- If no such basis exists, consider whether to (a) litigate the timeliness issue (risk: court enforces the plain language), or (b) negotiate a commercial resolution with Seller, using the strength of the underlying documentation (Attachments 1–4) as leverage. The items are factually well-supported; a court or the Independent Accountant would likely side with Trenex on the merits if the threshold timeliness issue were resolved.
- For future transactions, implement an internal calendar with hard stops at day 20 and day 25 of any dispute window to avoid recurrence.

## Issue 2: Parent Merger Triggers a Change-of-Control Event of Default Under the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note § 1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 5.1(g), § 5.2; SPA § 6.12, § 11.10

**Explanation:** SPA § 6.12 acknowledges that Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc. (Buyer's parent) is being acquired by Osprey Ridge Capital Partners, expected to close in Q1 2027. Section 6.12 provides that the Parent Merger "shall not constitute a breach of this Agreement" and shall not give Seller "any right to terminate this Agreement or to claim indemnification under this Agreement."

However, the Seller Note independently defines "Change of Control" to include:

> "(b) any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker ceases to control Maker, or a change occurs in the Persons who control any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker"

Osprey Ridge's acquisition of Trenex Aviation Services Group (which directly controls Maker, Trenex Flight Provisions) squarely fits prong (b). Seller Note § 5.1(g) makes a Change of Control "without the prior written consent of the Holder" an Event of Default. Upon an Event of Default, § 5.2 permits Holder to declare the entire $6,000,000 balance immediately due and payable.

SPA § 11.10 (Order of Precedence) provides that "the Seller Note shall control as to its payment, maturity and subordination terms." Acceleration is a maturity/payment term. Therefore, the Seller Note's Change-of-Control default provision likely controls over SPA § 6.12's carve-out, which only addresses breach of "this Agreement" (i.e., the SPA).

The Parent Merger is expected to close in Q1 2027—**before** the Seller Note's Maturity Date of August 3, 2027. If Dulaix treats the Parent Merger as an Event of Default, it can accelerate the note, demand immediate repayment of $6,000,000 plus accrued interest, and assert default-rate interest (8% per annum) under § 2.3. It could also invoke the costs-of-collection provision (§ 7.1).

**Recommended Fix:**

- **Before the Parent Merger closes**, obtain Dulaix's prior written consent to the Change of Control, as required by Seller Note § 5.1(g). This is the cleanest solution and is explicitly contemplated by the Note's language ("without the prior written consent of the Holder").
- Alternatively, amend the Seller Note (pursuant to § 7.3) to add a carve-out from the Change of Control definition for the Osprey Ridge transaction, mirroring the protection in SPA § 6.12.
- If neither is achievable before the Parent Merger closes, be prepared for Dulaix to declare a default and accelerate. Trenex should ensure it has the liquidity to repay $6,000,000 on short notice or that the Senior Lender's consent would be required for any refinancing.
- Note that § 6(c) of the Seller Note prohibits Maker from declaring or paying any distribution on its equity interests while an Event of Default is continuing—this could restrict value extraction by Osprey Ridge post-merger if a default is declared.

## Issue 3: Uncapped Indemnification Exposure for Seller Credit Support Obligations

**Where:** SPA § 9.3(c), § 9.5(b), § 6.9; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Seller Credit Support Obligations")

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.3(c) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller Indemnitees for "the Seller Credit Support Obligations, including any payment made or liability incurred by any Seller Indemnitee under the Seller Credit Support Obligations." Section 9.5(b) caps Buyer's aggregate liability under § 9.3 at $4,800,000, **but expressly carves out § 9.3(c)**: "provided that this limitation shall not apply to Losses under Section 9.3(c)."

The "Seller Credit Support Obligations" are defined broadly (SPA § 1.1) to include "all guarantees, letters of credit, surety bonds and other credit support provided by Seller or any of its Affiliates…in respect of the obligations of the Company under the airport concession agreements and commissary leases listed in Schedule 6.9, together with all obligations of Seller or such Affiliate under that credit support, **whether arising before or after the Closing**."

This means Trenex has **unlimited** indemnification exposure if Dulaix or its affiliates are called on any guarantee, letter of credit, or surety bond supporting the Company's airport concession agreements or commissary leases. Airport concession fees and lease obligations can be substantial and long-duration. The "whether arising before or after the Closing" language extends the exposure indefinitely.

Compounding this, SPA § 6.9 requires Buyer to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain Seller's release from these obligations post-Closing, but only "commercially reasonable efforts"—not a guarantee. If release negotiations stall or the counterparty (airport authority) refuses to accept replacement credit support, Dulaix remains exposed, and Trenex's uncapped indemnity follows.

**Recommended Fix:**

- Prioritize the § 6.9 release process immediately. Identify each concession agreement and commissary lease in Schedule 6.9, determine the amount of credit support in place, and begin discussions with the airport authorities about accepting the Company's (now Trenex-owned) credit or a third-party letter of credit in lieu of Dulaix's.
- Negotiate a cap or sunset on the § 9.3(c) indemnity. For example, limit it to credit support obligations in existence as of the Closing Date (delete "whether arising before or after the Closing"), or set a dollar cap tied to the actual face amount of the credit support listed in Schedule 6.9.
- If a cap cannot be negotiated, consider requiring Dulaix to maintain the credit support at its own expense until release, with Trenex's indemnity triggered only if Dulaix fails to do so.

## Issue 4: Title Representation Gap for Galley Equipment in the Bill of Sale

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 4; SPA § 4.10

**Explanation:** Bill of Sale § 4 states: "The representations and warranties of Seller concerning title to and condition of the assets used in the Business, **including those in Section 4.10 of the Purchase Agreement**, apply to the Galley Equipment."

However, SPA § 4.10 provides: "The **Company** has good title to, or a valid leasehold interest in, all tangible assets used in the Business, free and clear of liens other than permitted liens."

This representation is about the **Company's** title to assets the Company owns. The Galley Equipment, by contrast, is owned by **Seller** (Dulaix) and is being transferred to the Company for the first time at Closing (Bill of Sale § 2.1; SPA § 3.2(a)(iv)). There is no representation anywhere in the SPA or the Bill of Sale that **Seller** has good title to the Galley Equipment, free and clear of liens.

If any item in Schedule A (e.g., the 420 full-size trolleys, the conveyor lines, or the cold room panels) is subject to a security interest, lease, or other encumbrance held by a third party, Trenex would lack a clear contractual basis to claim indemnification from Dulaix. The incorporated § 4.10 rep speaks to the Company's title, which the Company did not have pre-Closing because it didn't own the equipment.

**Recommended Fix:**

- Add a specific representation in the Bill of Sale (or as an amendment to the SPA) that Seller has good and marketable title to the Galley Equipment, free and clear of all liens, encumbrances, and security interests, and that the transfer does not violate any lease, financing agreement, or other arrangement to which Seller is a party.
- Run UCC searches and, for titled equipment (e.g., the Aerobox truck bodies), verify registration status before Closing to confirm no third-party interests exist.
- If the deal has already closed (as the October letter suggests), assess whether any items in Schedule A show evidence of third-party liens and, if so, raise the issue under the Bill of Sale's further-assurances provision (§ 5) or as a general breach of the SPA's title representations.

## Issue 5: Customer Non-Solicitation Narrowed to "Key Customers" Only

**Where:** Key Employee Agreement § 5.2; LOI § 6(d); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Key Customers")

**Explanation:** The LOI (non-binding, § 6(d)) contemplated that Okafor-Brandt would be restricted from soliciting "all customers of the Company." The executed Key Employee Agreement § 5.2 limits the restriction to "Key Customer (as defined in the Purchase Agreement)"—i.e., only the **ten largest** airline customers by 2025 revenue (SPA § 1.1).

If the Company serves more than ten airline customers (likely, given the scale of the operation), Okafor-Brandt is free to solicit, induce, or interfere with relationships with any customer outside the top ten. In an airline catering business, even mid-tier customers can represent meaningful revenue and operational complexity (unique specs, dedicated trolley configurations, etc.).

**Recommended Fix:**

- Amend Key Employee Agreement § 5.2 to cover all customers of the Company, or at minimum all customers with annual revenue exceeding a specified threshold (e.g., $100,000).
- If an amendment is not feasible (Okafor-Brandt may resist), consider whether the Company's own customer contracts contain non-solicitation or exclusivity provisions that would provide supplemental protection.
- Document the business rationale for the narrowing in the deal file in case the issue becomes relevant in a dispute.

## Issue 6: NDA Party Mismatch – Agreement Is With Parent, Not Buyer Entity

**Where:** Mutual NDA (preamble); SPA § 6.3; NDA § 2.1, § 11.4

**Explanation:** The Mutual NDA is between **Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc.** (the parent) and Dulaix. The SPA is between **Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC** (the buyer/subsidiary) and Dulaix. SPA § 6.3 states that the NDA "shall continue to apply to information furnished under this Agreement until the Closing."

While NDA § 2.1 defines "Affiliate" to include Trenex Flight Provisions, and § 11.4 permits Trenex (parent) to assign the NDA to "any Affiliate that pursues the Proposed Transaction," there is no evidence in the folder of a formal assignment. The SPA's reference to the NDA in § 6.3 creates an implicit assumption that it applies to the buyer entity, but the contractual chain is:

- NDA binds Trenex Aviation Services Group (parent) ↔ Dulaix
- SPA binds Trenex Flight Provisions (buyer) ↔ Dulaix
- No documented assignment bridges the two

Post-Closing, SPA § 6.3 creates a standalone confidentiality obligation on Seller ("Seller shall keep confidential all non-public information concerning the Company and the Business"), so the NDA mismatch is less acute. But pre-Closing, if Dulaix were to claim that Trenex Flight Provisions (the entity that actually received and used the confidential information during diligence) is not a party to the NDA, there could be an argument that the NDA's protections (including the non-solicitation of employees in NDA § 6 and the fee-shifting in § 10.2) do not directly bind or benefit the buyer entity.

**Recommended Fix:**

- Execute a short-form assignment of the NDA from Trenex Aviation Services Group to Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC, pursuant to NDA § 11.4, with Trenex Aviation Services Group remaining secondarily liable. This is a low-cost, low-risk fix that eliminates the ambiguity.
- Alternatively, add a sentence to the SPA (or a side letter) confirming that the NDA is incorporated by reference and applies to both Trenex entities for purposes of the transaction.

## Issue 7: Tax Representation Survival Period May Be Inadequate

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(b); Seller Note § 2.1 (Maturity Date); SPA § 4.14

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.1(b) provides that the tax representations in § 4.14 "shall survive the Closing until the Maturity Date of the Seller Note"—i.e., **August 3, 2027**, only 12 months after Closing.

Section 4.14(a) represents that the Company timely filed all material tax returns for **2023 through 2025**. The federal statute of limitations for the 2024 tax year (filed by April 15, 2025) does not expire until **April 15, 2028**. The 2025 return (filed by April 15, 2026) does not expire until **April 15, 2029**. State statutes of limitations may be longer.

If the IRS or a state authority opens an audit of the Company's 2024 or 2025 tax year after August 3, 2027, Trenex would lose its right to claim indemnification based on a breach of the tax reps (because the rep would no longer be "surviving").

**Mitigating factor:** SPA § 9.2(c) provides a standalone indemnity for "any Taxes of the Company attributable to any period, or portion of any period, ending on or before the Closing Date." This indemnity is not expressly limited by the § 9.1(b) survival period (which governs "representations and warranties"). If the § 9.2(c) indemnity is read as independent of the rep survival, it would cover pre-Closing taxes regardless of when the liability is assessed. However, this reading is not certain, and Seller could argue that the § 9.2(c) indemnity is inextricably linked to the § 4.14 reps and subject to the same survival limit.

**Recommended Fix:**

- Amend SPA § 9.1(b) to extend the survival of the tax representations to the later of (a) the Maturity Date or (b) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations (including extensions) for the relevant tax year, plus 60 days. This is market-standard for tax reps.
- If Seller resists, at minimum add clarifying language to § 9.2(c) confirming that the tax indemnity is not subject to the survival limitations in § 9.1 and survives until all pre-Closing tax liabilities are finally resolved.
