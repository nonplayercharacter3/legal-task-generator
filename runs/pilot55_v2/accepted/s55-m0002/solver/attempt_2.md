## I. CRITICAL ISSUES

### Issue 1: Dispute Notice Delivered Outside the Contractual Deadline

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(b); Trenex Letter to Dulaix, Oct. 9, 2026, § 1

**Facts.** Seller delivered the Closing Statement by email to finance@trenexflight.com on September 2, 2026 (a Wednesday). SPA § 2.5(b) requires Buyer to deliver a Dispute Notice "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement." The SPA's notice provision (§ 11.2) deems email delivered on the day sent if before 5:00 p.m. recipient's local time on a Business Day. Thirty calendar days from September 2, 2026 is **October 2, 2026**. Trenex's Dispute Notice is dated **October 9, 2026**—seven days late.

**Why it matters.** Section 2.5(b) provides: "If Buyer does not deliver a Dispute Notice within that period, the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Net Working Capital shall be final and binding on the parties." Seller's Closing Statement shows Closing Net Working Capital of $5,620,000 (i.e., $220,000 *above* target). If that figure is final, Trenex owes Seller $220,000 under § 2.5(d) rather than collecting the $1,150,000 it believes is owed. The swing is **$1,370,000**.

**Note on "days" vs. "Business Days."** The SPA consistently uses "Business Days" when it means business days (§ 2.4: "three Business Days"; § 2.5(d): "five Business Days"; § 3.1: "Business Day" defined). Section 2.5(b) uses unqualified "days," strongly indicating calendar days. If, contrary to the better reading, the period were 30 *business* days, the deadline would fall on October 14, 2026, and the October 9 notice would be timely. But we should not plan around that reading.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Immediately** contact Dulaix's counsel to raise the issue and propose that the parties treat the October 9 letter as timely, or that Dulaix waive the deadline. Frame it cooperatively: the substance of the dispute is well-documented and the attachments are specific.
2. If Dulaix resists, evaluate whether Dulaix is estopped from asserting the deadline (e.g., if it engaged in good-faith negotiations after receiving the letter without objecting to timeliness). SPA § 2.5(c) contemplates a 30-day negotiation period after a "timely Dispute Notice"; if Dulaix participates in that process, it may be hard for it to later argue the notice was untimely.
3. In the alternative, assess whether the Closing Statement's delivery was defective (wrong address, after 5:00 p.m., not in writing) to toll or restart the 30-day period. The letter states it was sent "by email from Seller's finance team to finance@trenexflight.com"—confirm the exact timestamp and sender.
4. Preserve all arguments in writing. Do not concede the deadline in any correspondence.

### Issue 2: Parent Merger Triggers Change-of-Control Event of Default Under the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note § 1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 5.1(g), § 5.2; SPA § 6.12, § 11.10

**Facts.** SPA § 6.12 acknowledges that Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc. (Buyer's parent) has agreed to merge with Osprey Ridge Capital Partners, expected to close in Q1 2027. The Seller Note matures August 3, 2027—*after* the expected Parent Merger closing. The Seller Note defines "Change of Control" to include:

> "(a) any Person or group of Persons acting together acquires, directly or indirectly, beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting equity interests of Maker or of any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker; (b) any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker ceases to control Maker, or a change occurs in the Persons who control any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker…"

When Osprey Ridge acquires Trenex Aviation Services Group, both prongs (a) and (b) are satisfied. Seller Note § 5.1(g) makes a Change of Control "without the prior written consent of the Holder" an Event of Default. Under § 5.2, Dulaix may then accelerate the entire $6,000,000 note.

SPA § 6.12 states that the Parent Merger "shall not constitute a breach of this Agreement" and "shall not give rise to any right of Seller to terminate this Agreement or to claim indemnification under this Agreement." However:

- § 6.12 addresses breach of the *SPA*, not default under the *Seller Note*. The Seller Note is a separate instrument with its own default and remedy provisions.
- SPA § 11.10 (Order of Precedence) provides that "the Seller Note shall control as to its payment, maturity and subordination terms." An acceleration triggered by a Change of Control Event of Default is arguably a "maturity" term (it advances the date payment is due). Even if not, § 6.12 does not purport to amend or consent to the Seller Note's terms.
- There is no provision in either document in which Dulaix consents to the Parent Merger for purposes of Seller Note § 5.1(g).

**Why it matters.** If the Parent Merger closes in Q1 2027 without Dulaix's prior written consent, Dulaix has an immediate Event of Default. It can accelerate the $6M note, demand payment, and (under § 7.1) charge Trenex its collection costs and attorneys' fees. Because the note is subordinated to Senior Debt (§ 4.1), acceleration also puts pressure on Trenex's senior lender relationship.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Before the SPA closes (August 3, 2026),** obtain Dulaix's written consent to the Parent Merger as a Change of Control under Seller Note § 5.1(g). This can be done by:
   - Adding a sentence to SPA § 6.12: "Seller hereby consents to the Parent Merger for purposes of Section 5.1(g) of the Seller Note, and the consummation of the Parent Merger shall not constitute a Change of Control or an Event of Default under the Seller Note."
   - Or executing a separate consent letter from Dulaix referencing the Seller Note.
2. If Dulaix resists, negotiate a carve-out in the Seller Note's Change of Control definition for the specific Osprey Ridge transaction.
3. Do **not** rely on SPA § 6.12 alone. It is insufficient to protect against a Seller Note default.

## II. SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### Issue 3: Uncapped Indemnity for Seller Credit Support Obligations

**Where:** SPA § 9.3(c), § 9.5(b), § 6.9; Definition of "Seller Credit Support Obligations" in § 1.1

**Facts.** Section 9.3(c) requires Buyer (Trenex) to indemnify Seller for "the Seller Credit Support Obligations, including any payment made or liability incurred by any Seller Indemnitee under the Seller Credit Support Obligations." Section 9.5(b) caps Buyer's indemnity at $4,800,000 "provided that this limitation shall not apply to Losses under Section 9.3(c)." The Seller Credit Support Obligations are "all guarantees, letters of credit, surety bonds and other credit support provided by Seller or any of its Affiliates…in respect of the obligations of the Company under the airport concession agreements and commissary leases listed in Schedule 6.9, together with all obligations of Seller or such Affiliate under that credit support, **whether arising before or after the Closing**."

**Why it matters.** Trenex's exposure here is **uncapped** and extends to obligations arising *after* Closing. If an airport concession agreement generates a large claim (e.g., a multi-year revenue shortfall triggering a guarantee), Dulaix can look to Trenex for the full amount with no ceiling. Section 6.9 compounds this by requiring Trenex to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain a release and restricting Trenex from amending the underlying agreements in a way that increases Dulaix's exposure.

**Recommendation.**

1. Before closing, review Schedule 6.9 in detail to quantify the maximum exposure under each guarantee, letter of credit, and surety bond.
2. Negotiate a cap on the § 9.3(c) indemnity (e.g., limited to the face amount of the credit support instruments listed in Schedule 6.9 as of Closing).
3. Clarify in § 6.9 that "commercially reasonable efforts" does not obligate Trenex to provide replacement credit support at its own cost if the airport authority requires terms Trenex finds unreasonable.
4. Confirm that the release obligation in § 6.9 has a time limit (e.g., "within 18 months after Closing") so it doesn't become a perpetual covenant.

### Issue 4: Tax Representation Survival Shorter Than General Representation Survival

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(a) vs. § 9.1(b)

**Facts.** General representations survive 18 months post-Closing (i.e., until February 3, 2028) under § 9.1(a). Tax representations (§ 4.14) survive only until the "Maturity Date of the Seller Note"—August 3, 2027—under § 9.1(b). That is **12 months**, six months shorter than the general survival period.

**Why it matters.** Tax audits and assessments can arise well after 12 months. If a pre-Closing tax issue (e.g., an unfiled return, an underpayment, or a disallowed deduction) is identified in month 14, Trenex would have no claim for breach of the § 4.14 representation because it has expired. The standalone tax indemnity in § 9.2(c) may still be available (see Issue 5), but the shorter rep survival weakens Trenex's negotiating position and creates ambiguity.

**Recommendation.** Amend § 9.1(b) to provide that the § 4.14 representations survive until the later of (a) the Maturity Date or (b) 18 months after Closing, consistent with the general survival period. Ideally, tax reps should survive until the applicable statute of limitations expires (as is customary), but at minimum they should not expire *before* the other reps.

### Issue 5: Ambiguous Survival of Standalone Tax Indemnity (§ 9.2(c))

**Where:** SPA § 9.2(c), § 9.1

**Facts.** Section 9.2(c) indemnifies Buyer for "any Taxes of the Company attributable to any period, or portion of any period, ending on or before the Closing Date." This is a standalone indemnity obligation, not tied to a specific representation. Section 9.1 addresses survival of "representations and warranties" and "covenants," but does not explicitly state the survival period for indemnification obligations under § 9.2(c).

**Why it matters.** If Dulaix argues that the § 9.2(c) indemnity is subject to the 12-month survival of the tax reps (§ 9.1(b)) or the 18-month general survival (§ 9.1(a)), Trenex's protection for pre-Closing tax liabilities ends in 2027 or early 2028—well before many tax statutes of limitations expire. A tax period ending on the Closing Date (August 3, 2026) could remain open for assessment until 2029 or later.

**Recommendation.** Add clarifying language to § 9.1: "The indemnification obligations under Section 9.2(c) shall survive the Closing until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations (including any extensions) for the relevant Tax period, plus sixty (60) days."

### Issue 6: Seller Note Cross-Default to Senior Debt Acceleration

**Where:** Seller Note § 5.1(f)

**Facts.** If the Senior Lender accelerates the Senior Debt following an event of default under the Senior Credit Agreement (and the acceleration is not rescinded), that constitutes an Event of Default under the Seller Note. Dulaix can then also accelerate the $6M note.

**Why it matters.** This creates a cascading default: a single trip on the senior credit facility (e.g., a covenant breach, a rating downgrade trigger, or a payment default) could give Dulaix the right to demand immediate repayment of the Seller Note, on top of the senior lender's acceleration. Given that the Parent Merger (Issue 2) will change Trenex's capital structure and potentially its ability to service debt, this cross-default is a material risk.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a cure period or a materiality threshold in § 5.1(f) (e.g., the senior acceleration must result in a payment default actually made, not merely a technical covenant breach). At minimum, ensure the consent obtained for the Parent Merger (Issue 2) also addresses any potential interaction with the Senior Credit Agreement's change-of-control or assignment provisions.

### Issue 7: Seller Note Cross-Default to Judgments Against the Company

**Where:** Seller Note § 5.1(h)

**Facts.** A final judgment for more than $2,500,000 against "Maker or the Company" that remains undischarged and unstayed for 60 days is an Event of Default under the Seller Note.

**Why it matters.** After Closing, the Company (Lakeport Skykitchen) is Trenex's subsidiary. Any large judgment against the Company—whether from a customer dispute, an employee claim, a food-safety incident, or an airport authority—could trigger a default on the Seller Note, giving Dulaix the right to accelerate. The $2.5M threshold is relatively low for a business with $48M in enterprise value and multiple airport concession agreements.

**Recommendation.** Raise the judgment threshold to $5,000,000 or tie it to a percentage of the note's principal (e.g., 75% = $4.5M). Alternatively, limit the cross-default to judgments against Maker (Trenex Flight Provisions) only, excluding the Company, since the Company's operations are now Trenex's to manage.

## III. MODERATE ISSUES

### Issue 8: Key Employee Non-Solicitation Narrower Than LOI Contemplated

**Where:** Key Employee Agreement § 5.2; LOI § 6(d)

**Facts.** The LOI stated that the Key Employee Agreement would include "a covenant not to solicit **all customers** of the Company." The definitive agreement limits the non-solicitation to "Key Customer (as defined in the Purchase Agreement)"—i.e., only the ten largest airline customers by 2025 revenue (SPA § 1.1).

**Why it matters.** If Okafor-Brandt departs and solicits an 11th- or 12th-largest customer (or a smaller but strategically important customer), Trenex has no contractual remedy under the Key Employee Agreement. Given that the Company's value is heavily dependent on airline customer relationships, this gap reduces the protection Trenex is paying for (the $150,000 Retention Bonus and the two-year lock-in).

**Recommendation.** If the deal has not yet closed, negotiate to expand § 5.2 to cover all customers with which the Company had revenue in excess of a threshold (e.g., $50,000) in the trailing 12 months. If the deal has closed, document the limitation and consider whether the SPA's Seller non-compete (§ 6.7) or the NDA's non-solicitation (§ 6) provide any supplemental protection (they do not, as they address different parties).

### Issue 9: Bill of Sale Incorporates SPA § 4.10 (Company Title Rep) for Seller-Owned Equipment

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 4; SPA § 4.10

**Facts.** Bill of Sale § 4 states: "The representations and warranties of Seller concerning title to and condition of the assets used in the Business, **including those in Section 4.10 of the Purchase Agreement**, apply to the Galley Equipment." However, SPA § 4.10 represents that "**The Company** has good title to, or a valid leasehold interest in, all tangible assets used in the Business, free and clear of liens other than permitted liens." The Galley Equipment is owned by *Seller*, not the Company, pre-Closing. So § 4.10, as drafted, does not directly represent that *Seller* has good title to the equipment it is transferring.

**Why it matters.** If the Galley Equipment is subject to a lien (e.g., a security interest held by a vendor or the Senior Lender), the Company's post-Closing title could be defective. The Bill of Sale's attempt to incorporate § 4.10 is inapt because § 4.10 speaks to the Company's title, not Seller's. Trenex's indemnity claim for a title defect would need to rest on § 9.2(a) (breach of a representation), but the relevant representation (§ 4.10) is arguably inapplicable to Seller-owned assets.

**Recommendation.** Amend Bill of Sale § 4 to add an express representation by Seller: "Seller has good and marketable title to the Galley Equipment, free and clear of all liens, encumbrances and security interests, and has the full right and authority to transfer the Galley Equipment to the Company." This gives Trenex a clean indemnity hook under SPA § 9.2(a).

### Issue 10: Seller Note Requires Delivery of Trenex Financial Statements to Dulaix

**Where:** Seller Note § 6(b)

**Facts.** "Maker shall…deliver to Holder, within one hundred twenty (120) days after the end of each fiscal year, the annual financial statements of Maker that Maker delivers to the Senior Lender."

**Why it matters.** This gives Dulaix visibility into Trenex Flight Provisions' (and by extension, the Company's) financial performance for up to 12 months post-Closing. While this is common in seller-note arrangements, it means Dulaix can monitor whether Trenex is generating sufficient cash flow to service the note—and potentially use that information in negotiating leverage over the working capital dispute (Issue 1) or the credit support release (Issue 3).

**Recommendation.** Accept this as a standard term but be aware of the information asymmetry it creates. Ensure that the financial statements delivered are the same (and no more detailed) than those provided to the Senior Lender. Consider whether the Parent Merger (Issue 2) would change what financial statements are "delivered to the Senior Lender."

### Issue 11: No Financing Condition Combined with Sufficiency-of-Funds Representation

**Where:** SPA § 5.4; LOI § 10.3

**Facts.** The LOI stated "The Purchase Agreement would not include a financing condition." The SPA has no financing condition. However, SPA § 5.4 represents that "Buyer has, and at the Closing will have, sufficient funds to pay the cash portion of the Purchase Price." The $42M cash is to be funded by a combination of equity and senior debt (per LOI § 10.3), and the Senior Credit Agreement is dated August 3, 2026 (same as Closing).

**Why it matters.** If the senior financing falls through or is delayed, Trenex is in breach of § 5.4 (a representation) and potentially in default under the SPA. There is no "out" for Trenex. This was a negotiated trade-off (Dulaix required certainty of closing), but it means any financing delay is a material risk.

**Recommendation.** Confirm that the Senior Credit Agreement is fully executed and all conditions to funding are satisfied *before* the Closing Date. If there is any risk of a funding delay, consider whether a short extension of the Closing Date (permitted by SPA § 3.1: "or on such other date as the parties may agree in writing") should be pre-negotiated.
