## Issue 1 – CRITICAL: Dispute Notice Delivered Outside the 30-Day Window

**Where:** SPA § 2.5(b); Trenex Letter to Dulaix (Oct. 9, 2026), § 1.1

**Facts.** SPA § 2.5(a) requires Seller to deliver the Closing Statement within 30 days after the Closing. Seller delivered it by email on **September 2, 2026** (a Wednesday and a Business Day). SPA § 2.5(b) provides that Buyer "may deliver to Seller a written notice of dispute **within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement**" and that "[i]f Buyer does not deliver a Dispute Notice within that period, the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Net Working Capital shall be **final and binding** on the parties."

Thirty days after September 2, 2026 is **October 2, 2026**. Even if the September 2 email was sent after 5:00 p.m. (triggering next-Business-Day effectiveness under SPA § 11.2), the deadline would be October 3, 2026. Trenex's Dispute Notice was delivered **October 9, 2026** — six to seven days late.

**Why it matters.** The $1,150,000 working capital shortfall (comprising $410,000 in unreserved obsolete/obsolete inventory, $385,000 in unreserved aged/disputed receivables, $230,000 in omitted accrued payroll, and $125,000 in omitted airport concession fees) may be contractually barred. SPA § 2.5(b) is unambiguous: an untimely Dispute Notice makes Seller's calculation "final and binding." There is no cure period, no "material prejudice" qualifier, and no equitable exception stated in the text.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Immediately** assess whether any factual basis exists to argue the notice was timely (e.g., confirm the exact send-time of the September 2 email; if it was sent after 5:00 p.m. Eastern on a non-Business Day, the clock may have started later). Given September 2 was a Wednesday, this argument is unlikely to succeed.
2. If the notice is clearly late, **engage Seller promptly** to seek a written waiver or agreement to accept the Dispute Notice. Frame it commercially: the items are objectively supportable (inventory counts, AR aging, payroll registers, concession fee statements are all documented in the attachments), and litigating a 7-day technicality over $1.15M is costly for both sides.
3. In parallel, evaluate **alternative theories of recovery**: (a) whether Seller's preparation of the Closing Statement in knowing disregard of Schedule 2.4 Accounting Principles constitutes a breach of the § 4.5 (Financial Statements) or § 4.6 (Undisclosed Liabilities) representation, giving rise to an indemnification claim under § 9.2(a) (subject to the $360,000 deductible and 18-month survival); (b) whether the overstatement rises to the level of fraud (which is carved out of the exclusive remedy provision in § 9.7). These are weaker but preserve leverage in the negotiation with Seller.
4. Document all communications regarding the timing issue.

## Issue 2 – CRITICAL: Parent Merger Likely Triggers Change of Control Default on the Seller Note

**Where:** Seller Note § 1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 5.1(g); SPA § 6.12, § 11.10

**Facts.** SPA § 6.12 acknowledges that Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc. (Buyer's parent) has signed a merger agreement with Osprey Ridge Capital Partners, expected to close in **Q1 2027**. The provision states that the Parent Merger "shall not constitute a breach of **this Agreement**" and shall not give Seller the right to terminate or claim indemnification "under **this Agreement**."

However, the Seller Note defines "Change of Control" to include "(b) any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker **ceases to control Maker**, or a change occurs in the Persons who control any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker." When Osprey Ridge acquires Trenex Aviation Services Group in Q1 2027, the person controlling Maker (Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC) changes. This is a Change of Control under the Note.

Seller Note § 5.1(g) provides that "[m]aker consummates a Change of Control without the prior written consent of the Holder" is an **Event of Default**. Upon an Event of Default, § 5.2 permits Holder to accelerate the entire $6,000,000 balance.

**The order-of-precedence problem.** SPA § 11.10 states: "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall control. **However, the Seller Note shall control as to its payment, maturity and subordination terms.**" Acceleration of the Note is a payment/maturity term. Therefore, the Seller Note's Change of Control default provision likely overrides the protection in SPA § 6.12.

**Why it matters.** If the Parent Merger closes in Q1 2027 (before the Note's Maturity Date of August 3, 2027), Dulaix could declare an Event of Default and demand immediate repayment of the full $6,000,000 plus accrued interest. This would create a significant liquidity event for Trenex at exactly the time it is integrating the Osprey Ridge transaction. Additionally, while an Event of Default is continuing, Seller Note § 6(c) prohibits Maker from declaring or paying any distribution on its equity interests, which could complicate the Parent Merger mechanics.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Before the Parent Merger closes**, obtain Dulaix's **prior written consent** to the Change of Control, as required by Seller Note § 5.1(g). This is the cleanest solution and is expressly contemplated by the Note's language ("without the prior written consent of the Holder").
2. Alternatively, negotiate an **amendment to the Seller Note** (under § 7.3, which requires Maker and Holder signatures) to carve out the Osprey Ridge Parent Merger from the Change of Control definition, or to provide that the Parent Merger shall not constitute an Event of Default.
3. As a fallback, seek a **clarifying side letter** from Dulaix confirming that it will not treat the Parent Merger as a Change of Control default, citing SPA § 6.12. (This is weaker than an amendment but better than nothing.)
4. Ensure the consent or amendment is in place **before** the Parent Merger closes, not after.

## Issue 3 – HIGH: Uncapped Indemnification Exposure for Seller Credit Support Obligations

**Where:** SPA §§ 9.3(c), 9.5(b), 6.9; Definition of "Seller Credit Support Obligations" in § 1.1

**Facts.** SPA § 9.3(c) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller Indemnitees for "the Seller Credit Support Obligations, including any payment made or liability incurred by any Seller Indemnitee under the Seller Credit Support Obligations." SPA § 9.5(b) caps Buyer's aggregate indemnification liability at $4,800,000, **but expressly provides that "this limitation shall not apply to Losses under Section 9.3(c)."** The Seller Credit Support Obligations are therefore **uncapped**.

The defined term covers "all guarantees, letters of credit, surety bonds and other credit support provided by Seller or any of its Affiliates … in respect of the obligations of the Company under the airport concession agreements and commissary leases listed in Schedule 6.9, together with all obligations of Seller or such Affiliate under that credit support, **whether arising before or after the Closing**."

SPA § 6.9 requires Buyer to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to cause Seller's release from these obligations, including by offering replacement credit support.

**Why it matters.** If the Company defaults under an airport concession agreement or commissary lease post-closing, and the airport authority calls on Seller's guarantee, Seller can pass that entire loss through to Trenex with no cap. The "whether arising before or after the Closing" language means even post-closing operational failures at the Company could trigger this indemnity. For an airline catering business with multiple airport concessions, the potential exposure could be substantial.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Review **Schedule 6.9** (not in the folder) to quantify the actual credit support amounts and the underlying concession agreements.
2. Prioritize obtaining **releases or replacements** of the Seller Credit Support Obligations as soon as practicable post-closing, per § 6.9. The longer these remain in place, the longer Trenex's uncapped exposure persists.
3. If the releases cannot be obtained quickly, consider negotiating with Dulaix a **cap or sunset** on the § 9.3(c) indemnity (e.g., limited to the face amount of the credit support actually provided, or expiring upon the release).
4. Ensure the Company's operational compliance with the concession agreements is a priority in the integration plan to avoid triggering the credit support.

## Issue 4 – HIGH: Tax Representation Survival Tied to Note Maturity (Only 12 Months)

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(b); Seller Note § 2.1

**Facts.** SPA § 9.1(b) provides that the tax representations in § 4.14 "shall survive the Closing until the **Maturity Date of the Seller Note**." The Maturity Date is fixed at **August 3, 2027** (12 months after the August 3, 2026 Closing). This means the tax reps expire in just one year.

**Why it matters.** The federal income tax statute of limitations is generally three years from filing. State taxes can be longer. If a pre-closing tax issue (e.g., an unfiled return, an underpayment, or an audit adjustment) surfaces in year 2 or year 3 post-closing, Trenex will have **no indemnification recourse** against Dulaix under § 9.2(a) because the tax rep will have expired. The Company's pre-closing tax years (2023–2025 per § 4.14(a)) remain open for audit well past August 2027.

This is an unusual and short survival period. Market practice for tax reps is survival until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations (plus a 60-day tail), which would typically be 3–6+ years.

**Recommended fix.**

1. This is a post-closing issue (the deal has closed), so renegotiation is unlikely. However, if the working capital dispute (Issue 1) is being negotiated with Dulaix, consider raising this as a related point — if Dulaix is going to be paying Trenex $1.15M on the NWC adjustment, there may be room to agree to an extended tax rep survival in a side agreement.
2. In the interim, ensure the Company's tax function is robust. Review all pre-closing tax returns (2023–2025) for accuracy. If any issues are identified, document them now while there may still be a basis to assert a claim (e.g., if the issue relates to a rep that was inaccurate at Closing, and the 18-month general survival in § 9.1(a) has not yet expired — the general survival runs to February 3, 2028).
3. Note that § 9.1(a) provides 18-month survival for non-fundamental, non-tax reps. The tax rep's 12-month survival is actually *shorter* than the general 18-month period, which is anomalous and suggests it may have been a drafting compromise. If there is any ambiguity, argue that the tax rep should survive at least as long as the general reps (18 months, i.e., until February 3, 2028).

## Issue 5 – MEDIUM: Customer Non-Solicitation Narrowed from "All Customers" to "Key Customers"

**Where:** Key Employee Agreement § 5.2; Letter of Intent § 6(d)

**Facts.** The LOI (non-binding, § 12) proposed that the Key Employee Agreement include "a covenant not to solicit **all customers** of the Company, during employment and for twenty-four (24) months after employment ends." The executed Key Employee Agreement § 5.2 limits the non-solicitation to "**Key Customers**" only — defined in SPA § 1.1 as "the ten largest airline customers of the Company, measured by revenue … for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025."

**Why it matters.** Marisol Okafor-Brandt is the VP of Operations with direct knowledge of all customer relationships, pricing, and specifications. If she departs, she is free to solicit any airline customer outside the top 10. For a business with fragmented airline customers, the #11 through #30 customers could represent meaningful revenue. The narrowing reduces Trenex's protection against customer attrition following a key employee departure.

**Recommended fix.**

1. The agreement is already executed and in effect. Renegotiation is unlikely absent a breach.
2. Ensure the **retention bonus** (§ 3.3: $150,000 paid over two years) and the **severance package** (§ 4.3: 12 months salary + COBRA) provide sufficient economic incentive for Okafor-Brandt to remain through the Initial Term.
3. Strengthen **operational protections**: ensure customer relationships are documented and not solely in Okafor-Brandt's head. Cross-train other staff on smaller accounts.
4. If Okafor-Brandt's employment ends and she moves to a competitor, monitor whether her solicitation of non-Key Customers causes revenue loss that could support a claim under the broader SPA § 6.7 (Seller non-compete) or under common-law theories (though the latter would be weak given the express limitation in § 5.2).

## Issue 6 – MEDIUM: Cross-Default to Judgments Against the Company

**Where:** Seller Note § 5.1(h)

**Facts.** Seller Note § 5.1(h) provides that an Event of Default occurs if "a final judgment for the payment of money in excess of **$2,500,000** is entered against **Maker or the Company** and remains undischarged and unstayed for sixty (60) days."

**Why it matters.** The Company (Lakeport Skykitchen) is now owned by Trenex. If the Company is sued by a customer, supplier, employee, or airport authority and loses a judgment over $2.5M, that judgment triggers a default on the Seller Note — even though the judgment has nothing to do with Trenex's ability or willingness to pay Dulaix. This effectively gives Dulaix a security interest in the Company's litigation outcomes. The $2.5M threshold is relatively low for an airline catering business with significant contract values.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Review the Company's **insurance program** (SPA § 4.18, Disclosure Schedule) to confirm that general liability, professional liability, and aviation-specific coverages are in place with limits sufficient to absorb a $2.5M+ judgment without it remaining "undischarged."
2. In the integration plan, prioritize **litigation risk management**: ensure the Company's contract portfolio (particularly the airport concession agreements and airline catering agreements) has robust limitation-of-liability and indemnification provisions.
3. If a significant claim arises, consider whether it can be **stayed or settled** within 60 days to avoid triggering the default.
4. If renegotiating the Note (e.g., in connection with the Change of Control consent under Issue 2), seek to **raise the threshold** to $5M or limit the cross-default to judgments against Maker only (excluding the Company).

## Issue 7 – LOW: SPA Confidentiality Covenant References NDA to Which Buyer Is Not a Direct Party

**Where:** SPA § 6.3; Mutual NDA §§ 1, 2.1, 11.4

**Facts.** SPA § 6.3 states: "The Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement between **Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc.** and Seller shall continue to apply to information furnished under this Agreement until the Closing." The NDA is between Trenex Aviation Services Group, Inc. (the parent) and Dulaix. Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC (the Buyer under the SPA) is not a signatory to the NDA, though it is covered as an "Affiliate" under NDA § 2.1.

**Why it matters.** This is a minor structural issue. If a dispute arose over post-closing confidentiality, Dulaix could theoretically argue that the SPA's confidentiality covenant (which references the NDA) binds only the NDA's signatories, not the Buyer directly. However, the NDA's assignment provision (§ 11.4) permits Trenex to assign rights and obligations to any Affiliate pursuing the transaction, and the Buyer is expressly identified as an Affiliate. The risk is low.

**Recommended fix.** No immediate action required. If a side agreement is negotiated with Dulaix (e.g., regarding the Change of Control consent or the Dispute Notice timing), consider adding a provision confirming that the NDA's protections extend to and are enforceable by Trenex Flight Provisions, LLC directly.

## Issue 8 – LOW: Indemnity Deductible Operates as a True Deductible, Not a Tipping Basket

**Where:** SPA § 9.5(a)

**Facts.** SPA § 9.5(a) provides: "Seller shall not be liable under Section 9.2(a), other than for breaches of Fundamental Representations, until the aggregate Losses exceed **$360,000**, after which Seller shall be liable **only for Losses in excess of that amount**."

This is a **true deductible**: Trenex recovers only the amount by which total losses exceed $360,000. It is not a **tipping basket** (or "threshold"), under which once losses exceed $360,000, Trenex could recover the full amount including the first $360,000.

**Why it matters.** If Trenex's total indemnification losses (excluding Fundamental Rep breaches) come to, say, $500,000, Trenex recovers only $140,000 under a true deductible, versus the full $500,000 under a tipping basket. The $360,000 deductible equals 10% of the Purchase Price, which is at the higher end of market. Combined with the $4,800,000 cap (also 10% of Purchase Price), Trenex's practical recovery window for non-fundamental rep breaches is $4.44M ($4.8M cap minus $360K deductible).

**Recommended fix.** This is a negotiated term and the deal has closed. No fix is available. For future transactions, consider negotiating a tipping basket structure or a lower deductible. For this deal, ensure that any claims are aggregated carefully to maximize the amount above the deductible.

## Additional Observations (Not Standalone Issues)

- **SPA § 2.5(d) payment direction:** If final NWC is less than Estimated NWC, *Seller pays Buyer* the shortfall. This means the $1,150,000 (if the dispute is resolved in Trenex's favor) would be a cash payment from Dulaix to Trenex, or a setoff against the Seller Note under SPA § 9.6 and Seller Note § 3.1. Trenex should plan for both scenarios.

- **Seller Note § 4.2 (Permitted Payments):** Regular scheduled payments on the Note (interest quarterly, principal at maturity) are permitted even while subordinated, so long as no default exists under the Senior Credit Agreement. This means Trenex can service the Note on schedule unless the senior lender accelerates.

- **Key Employee Agreement § 7.2 (Third-Party Beneficiary):** Buyer (Trenex) is expressly named as a third-party beneficiary of the restrictive covenants (§§ 5 and 6) and may enforce them directly. This is important because the agreement is between the Company and Okafor-Brandt, not between Trenex and Okafor-Brandt.

- **Bill of Sale Schedule A:** The schedule references serial numbers "as recorded in Seller's fixed asset register as of the Closing Date" and states "A copy has been delivered to the Company." Ensure this copy is in the closing file. If any equipment is later found to be missing or not matching the register, the Bill of Sale's representation in § 2.1 (transfer "free and clear of all liens") and the incorporated SPA § 4.10 rep would be the basis for a claim.
