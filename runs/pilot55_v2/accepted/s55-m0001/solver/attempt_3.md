## 1. Parent Guaranty Does Not Cover the Entity Actually Performing SOW No. 1

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §1.1 (definition of "Provider"); SOW No. 1 preamble and §2.1; MSA §1.1 (definition of "Provider").

**Issue:** The MSA defines "Provider" broadly: "Geilaind and any Affiliate of Geilaind that executes an SOW, in each case as the Provider under that SOW" (MSA §1.1). SOW No. 1 is executed by **Geilaind Ship Management (Cyprus) Ltd**, an Affiliate. However, the Parent Guaranty defines "Provider" narrowly as "Geilaind Marine Services LLC, a limited liability company, and its successors and permitted assigns" (Guaranty §1.1). The Cyprus entity is neither Geilaind Marine Services LLC nor its successor or assign. The "Guaranteed Obligations" (Guaranty §1.1) are therefore obligations of "Provider" as defined in the Guaranty—i.e., only Geilaind Marine Services LLC.

**Why it matters:** The entity actually performing the services, incurring the obligations, and holding the Management Accounts is the Cyprus company. If the Cyprus entity defaults, Zilain may have no enforceable claim under the Guaranty because the Cyprus entity is not "Provider" within the meaning of the Guaranty. The Guaranty was a condition of engagement under MSA §2.5, so Zilain is relying on it for credit support that may not cover the real obligor.

**Recommended fix:** Amend the Guaranty's definition of "Provider" to track the MSA definition: "Geilaind Marine Services LLC and any Affiliate of Geilaind Marine Services LLC that executes a SOW, in each case as the Provider under that SOW." Alternatively, have the Cyprus entity execute a separate guaranty or accession to the existing one.

## 2. "Environmental Losses" Definition Is Too Narrow—Major Pollution Liability Gap

**Where:** Quality Agreement §1.2 (definition of "Environmental Losses"); MSA §12.3(c) (exclusion from liability cap); MSA §12.2 (liability cap).

**Issue:** "Environmental Losses" is defined as "fines, penalties and assessments imposed by a Governmental Authority under MARPOL or OPA 90 arising from a Pollution Incident" (QA §1.2). MSA §12.3(c) excludes "Environmental Losses" from the liability cap in §12.2. But the cap in §12.2 is "Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event"—for a new SOW, that is roughly $1.86 million (SOW §6.1).

The definition covers only **regulatory fines and penalties**. It does **not** cover:
- Third-party claims for pollution damage (e.g., coastal communities, fisheries, tourism operators);
- Cleanup and remediation costs not imposed as a regulatory fine;
- Natural resource damages claimed by a private party or a state in its sovereign capacity (as opposed to a regulatory assessment);
- Loss of use claims.

**Why it matters:** A serious pollution incident on a Gulf feeder vessel could generate tens or hundreds of millions of dollars in third-party liability. Because those losses are not "Environmental Losses" as defined, they fall within the §12.2 cap. Zilain's recovery from Provider (and, per the Guaranty, from the parent) would be limited to approximately $1.86 million, even if the incident is entirely Provider's fault. Zilain, as vessel owner, faces the full exposure to third parties and the regulators.

**Recommended fix:** Broaden the definition of "Environmental Losses" in the Quality Agreement to include all losses, damages, liabilities, costs and expenses arising from or in connection with a Pollution Incident, whether imposed by a Governmental Authority or claimed by a third party, including cleanup costs, natural resource damages, and loss of use. Alternatively, add a separate carve-out in MSA §12.3 for "all Losses arising from a Pollution Incident to the extent caused by Provider's negligence or breach."

## 3. Loss of Hire Excluded from Recovery

**Where:** MSA §12.1 (exclusion of indirect losses).

**Issue:** Section 12.1 excludes "loss of profit, loss of hire, loss of charter or business" from recoverable damages. For a technical manager, the most financially significant failure is one that takes a vessel off-hire—engine breakdown, class deficiency, crew failure. Zilain cannot recover the hire it loses while the vessel is idle due to Provider's technical default.

**Why it matters:** A feeder vessel earning, say, $15,000–$25,000 per day in charter hire loses substantial revenue during any technical off-hire. KPI No. 8 in Schedule 1 of the Quality Agreement tracks "Unplanned off-hire attributable to technical failure" with a target of ≤ 3 days per vessel per year, but there is no financial remedy if the KPI is missed (see Issue 5 below). The exclusion in §12.1 means that even if Zilain proves Provider's negligence caused a 30-day off-hire, it cannot recover the lost hire.

**Recommended fix:** Carve out loss of hire from the §12.1 exclusion where the off-hire is attributable to Provider's negligence or breach of the Services. Alternatively, introduce a service-credit mechanism (e.g., a daily fee abatement for each day of unplanned off-hire above the KPI threshold) in the SOW or Quality Agreement.

## 4. SOW Exclusivity Is Dramatically Broader Than the MSA and Extends 24 Months Post-Term

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §§5.1–5.3; MSA §2.4; MSA §2.3 (order of precedence).

**Issue:** MSA §2.4 limits exclusivity to "the technical management of the Covered Vessels under that SOW" during the SOW term. SOW §5.2 replaces this with: "Customer shall, and shall cause its Affiliates to, engage Provider exclusively for **all ship management, crewing and drydock supervision services** for **any vessel owned, operated or chartered** by Customer or any of its Affiliates, **in any trading area**," during the SOW Term **and for 24 months thereafter**.

The SOW validly modifies the MSA because §5.1 expressly identifies MSA §2.4 as the section being modified, satisfying the condition in MSA §2.3.

**Why it matters:**
- **Scope:** Zilain is locked in to Provider for *all* ship management services (not just technical management) for *all* vessels (not just the six Covered Vessels), across *all* trading areas, for *all* Affiliates. If Zilain grows its fleet or enters a new route, it must use Geilaind.
- **Duration:** The 24-month tail means that even after the SOW expires or is terminated, Zilain cannot use another manager for any vessel for two years.
- **New-vessel hold-up (SOW §5.3):** When Zilain acquires or charters in a new vessel, it must notify Provider within 30 days and the parties must "negotiate in good faith" terms. But §5.2's exclusivity obligation is absolute. If negotiations break down, Zilain is in breach of §5.2 the moment it engages anyone else. Provider has no obligation to agree on terms, creating a hold-up position.
- **Affiliate reach:** The obligation extends to vessels owned, operated or chartered by *any Affiliate*. If Zilain's group structure changes (new subsidiary, joint venture), the exclusivity automatically applies.

**Recommended fix:**
- Narrow the exclusivity to the Covered Vessels (and vessels added by amendment) rather than all vessels of Customer and Affiliates.
- Delete or shorten the 24-month post-term tail.
- In §5.3, add a mechanism for the case where good-faith negotiations fail (e.g., a base-fee schedule, or a right for Customer to engage a third party if no agreement is reached within 60 days).
- Limit the Affiliate reach to vessels in which Customer holds a majority interest.

## 5. No Financial Remedies for KPI Failures

**Where:** Quality Agreement §7.3 (remediation plan only); SOW No. 1 §9.1 (termination right limited to KPIs 3 and 4).

**Issue:** The KPI regime in Schedule 1 of the Quality Agreement sets 11 performance targets. The only consequences for missing them are:
- A remediation plan if a KPI is missed in two consecutive quarters (QA §7.3); and
- A termination right (SOW §9.1) limited to KPI No. 3 (Pollution Incidents) and No. 4 (Detentions), and only after two failures in a 12-month period, or failure to implement a remediation plan.

There are no service credits, fee reductions, liquidated damages, or other financial consequences for KPI failures.

**Why it matters:** KPI No. 8 (unplanned off-hire ≤ 3 days/vessel/year), KPI No. 5 (PSC deficiencies ≤ 1.0 per inspection), and KPI No. 9 (crew retention ≥ 80%) are directly tied to Zilain's commercial performance. If Provider consistently underperforms on these KPIs but never triggers the narrow termination right, Zilain has no leverage other than to endure the underperformance or terminate for "material breach" under MSA §14.3(a)—a high threshold that requires proving the KPI failure rises to the level of a material breach of the Agreement.

**Recommended fix:** Add a service-credit schedule to the SOW or Quality Agreement. For example: for each day of unplanned off-hire above the KPI No. 8 threshold, Provider credits a daily amount against the management fee. For each PSC detention (KPI No. 4), a fixed credit per day of detention. This gives Zilain a practical, automatic remedy without needing to prove material breach.

## 6. Liability Cap Is Inadequate in the Early Months of a New SOW

**Where:** MSA §12.2; SOW No. 1 §6.1.

**Issue:** The cap is "Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event giving rise to the claim." SOW No. 1 became effective March 9, 2026, with takeovers beginning April 1, 2026. If a serious incident occurs in, say, June 2026 (three months into the SOW), the cap is only three months of fees—approximately $465,000 ($155,000 × 3).

**Why it matters:** The most dangerous period is often the transition, when Provider is still familiarizing with the vessels, the crew, and the systems. A low cap during exactly this period provides minimal protection.

**Recommended fix:** Provide that for the first 12 months of the SOW, the cap shall be no less than 12 months of the annual management fee (i.e., $1,860,000), regardless of how many months have elapsed. Alternatively, use a fixed dollar cap for the first year that steps down to the trailing-12-months formula thereafter.

## 7. MSA §12.4 "Package Limitation" Preserves Provider's Statutory Rights to Limit

**Where:** MSA §12.4.

**Issue:** "Nothing in this Agreement deprives Provider of any right to limit liability that is available to a ship manager under any applicable convention or statute." This means that even if the contractual cap in §12.2 is found inapplicable or inadequate, Provider can still invoke any lower statutory or conventional limit. The contractual cap is a floor of protection for Provider, not a ceiling.

**Why it matters:** In a U.S. jurisdiction, there may be statutory limits available to ship managers (e.g., under the Limitation of Liability Act, 46 U.S.C. §30501 et seq., though its application to ship managers is uncertain). If a court finds that a statutory limit is lower than the contractual cap, Provider can invoke it. Zilain's recovery could be further reduced below the already-low contractual cap.

**Recommended fix:** Delete §12.4 or replace it with: "Nothing in this Agreement limits Provider's liability below the cap set out in Section 12.2." If the clause must stay for market reasons, add: "provided that no such right shall reduce Provider's liability below the amount of the Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event."

## 8. Agency vs. Independent Contractor Tension Creates Vicarious Liability Risk

**Where:** MSA §3.1 (Provider acts "as agent for and on behalf of Customer"); MSA §19.2 (Provider is an "independent contractor").

**Issue:** Section 3.1 states that Provider performs the Services "as agent for and on behalf of Customer or the relevant registered owner." Section 19.2 states that Provider "performs the Services as an independent contractor." These are in tension. In agency, the principal can be vicariously liable for the agent's torts committed within the scope of authority. As an independent contractor, the general rule is that the engaging party is not vicariously liable.

**Why it matters:** If a third party (e.g., a cargo interest, a port worker, a member of the public) sues Zilain for an injury caused by Provider's superintendent or crew, Zilain's ability to assert the independent-contractor defense depends on how a court reconciles these two provisions. The agency language in §3.1 undercuts the independent-contractor position. Zilain could be held vicariously liable and then have to rely on the (narrow) indemnity in §11.1 and the (capped) right of contribution.

**Recommended fix:** Clarify that Provider acts as agent only for the specific purposes enumerated in §3.1 (ISM/ISPS compliance, class maintenance, etc.) and that for all other purposes Provider is an independent contractor. Add: "Nothing in this Agreement makes Provider an agent of Customer for the purpose of creating vicarious liability of Customer for the acts or omissions of Provider or its personnel, except to the extent expressly stated in this Agreement."

## 9. Takeover Protocol: Provider Unilaterally Classifies Defects; Customer Has No Approval Right

**Where:** Vessel Takeover Protocol §§4.3–4.4.

**Issue:** Section 4.3 requires Provider to deliver an inspection report that "classify[es] each item as one that must be rectified before takeover, one to be rectified after takeover, or one for monitoring." Section 4.4 then says "The Parties shall agree in good faith which items are to be rectified before the takeover date." But the initial classification is Provider's alone, and there is no mechanism if the parties cannot agree.

Furthermore, §4.4 provides that "Rectification work carried out after the Takeover Time shall be included in the vessel's operating budget or approved under Section 7.2 of the MSA." This means pre-existing defects that Provider classifies as "rectify after takeover" become Customer's cost through the Management Accounts.

**Why it matters:** Provider has an incentive to classify more items as "rectify after takeover" or "for monitoring" because those costs fall on Customer's budget. Customer has no formal veto over the classification and no escalation mechanism if it disagrees.

**Recommended fix:** Amend §4.3 to require that the classification be "agreed by the Parties" rather than unilaterally determined by Provider. Add: "If the Parties cannot agree on the classification of any item within five (5) Business Days of delivery of the inspection report, the item shall be treated as 'must be rectified before takeover' and the cost shall be borne by Provider." Alternatively, give Customer the right to require pre-takeover rectification of any item it identifies as safety-critical.

## 10. Order of Precedence Places the Quality Agreement Last

**Where:** MSA §2.3.

**Issue:** The order is: (a) MSA; (b) SOW; (c) Quality Agreement. The Quality Agreement contains the detailed operational standards (crew qualifications, environmental compliance, incident reporting timelines, KPIs). If any provision of the Quality Agreement is inconsistent with the MSA, the MSA prevails.

**Why it matters:** For example, MSA §3.1(c) sets the standard as "sound ship management practice, with the degree of skill, care and diligence expected of a competent and experienced third-party technical manager." The Quality Agreement sets specific, measurable standards (e.g., DPA reachable 24/7, internal audits every 12 months, PSC deficiencies closed within 30 days). If a court finds that a specific QA standard is "inconsistent" with the MSA's general standard of care, the MSA's more flexible language prevails, undermining the specificity Zilain negotiated.

**Recommended fix:** Reverse the order so that the Quality Agreement prevails over the MSA on matters of safety, environmental, technical and performance standards. Alternatively, add to MSA §2.3: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Quality Agreement prevails over this Agreement on all matters within its scope (safety, environmental, technical and performance standards)."

## 11. Automatic Renewal with 120-Day Notice Period

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §2.2.

**Issue:** After the initial 3-year term, the SOW renews automatically for successive 1-year periods unless either party gives notice of non-renewal at least 120 days before the end of the then-current period.

**Why it matters:** 120 days is a long notice period. If Zilain wants to exit at the end of year 3, it must give notice by approximately mid-October of year 3 (120 days before the March 9 anniversary). Missing this deadline locks Zilain in for another year—and, per Issue 4 above, the broad exclusivity in §5.2 continues for the duration of the SOW Term plus 24 months.

**Recommended fix:** Reduce the non-renewal notice period to 90 days. Add a calendar reminder and internal ownership for this deadline. Consider whether the automatic renewal should be deleted in favor of a mutual-agreement renewal.

## 12. SMS Materials Ownership Ambiguity

**Where:** MSA §10.1 (records are Customer's property, including "SMS records"); MSA §10.2 (Provider's "SMS manuals, procedures and software" remain Provider's property).

**Issue:** The boundary between "SMS records" (Customer's property under §10.1) and "SMS manuals, procedures and software" (Provider's property under §10.2) is unclear. In practice, the SMS is a living document: procedures are updated based on audit findings, incident investigations, and operational experience. Over a 3-year term, the SMS will be significantly tailored to Zilain's fleet. On termination, Provider retains the "manuals and procedures" but must deliver the "records."

**Why it matters:** If Zilain terminates and transitions to a new manager, the new manager will need the SMS to operate the vessels. If the tailored procedures (which reflect Zilain's specific fleet, trade, and risk profile) are Provider's "pre-existing materials" or fall within the §10.2 carve-out, Zilain may not have a clean right to them. The new manager would have to rebuild the SMS from scratch or license it from Provider.

**Recommended fix:** Clarify that vessel-specific SMS procedures, tailored during the term to the Covered Vessels, are "Deliverables" under MSA §10.3 and become Customer's property. Limit §10.2 to Provider's generic, fleet-agnostic SMS framework and software platform.

## 13. Force Majeure: 60-Day Threshold and Continued Payment Obligation

**Where:** MSA §16.1.

**Issue:** A force majeure event must prevent performance for more than 60 consecutive days before either party may terminate. Payment obligations are expressly excluded from force majeure relief ("other than payment obligations").

**Why it matters:** For a feeder vessel, 60 days of non-performance is a severe operational disruption. During that time, Zilain must continue paying the management fee ($155,000/month for the full fleet) even though Provider is not performing. If the event is, for example, a prolonged port closure or a cyber-attack on Provider's systems, Zilain bears the cost of inaction.

**Recommended fix:** Reduce the termination threshold to 30 consecutive days. Add: "During any force majeure event, the management fee shall be abated pro rata for the period during which Provider is unable to perform the Services in respect of the affected Covered Vessel."

## 14. MSA §14.3(a): 30-Day Cure Period with No Exception for Safety-Critical Breaches

**Where:** MSA §14.3(a).

**Issue:** Either party may terminate for material breach only after the breaching party "fails to cure the breach within thirty (30) days after written notice." There is no exception for breaches that are incurable or that pose an immediate risk to safety, the vessel, or the environment.

**Why it matters:** If a vessel is detained for 20 days due to Provider's failure to maintain class (a material breach), Zilain must wait 30 days from the notice before it can terminate—even if the vessel is still detained and generating no revenue. The specific termination right in MSA §14.4(b) (detention > 14 days) helps, but only applies where the detention is "because of Provider's failure to perform the Services," which requires a causation analysis.

**Recommended fix:** Add: "The cure period in this Section 14.3(a) shall not apply where the breach (i) is not capable of being cured within the cure period, (ii) relates to the safety of a Covered Vessel or its crew, or (iii) results in the detention of a Covered Vessel by a Governmental Authority."

## 15. SOW §7.2 Budget Threshold Is Ambiguous

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §7.2; MSA §7.2.

**Issue:** SOW §7.2 sets the threshold at "five percent (5%) of the approved annual budget for the relevant Covered Vessel, or US$50,000 for any single unbudgeted item, whichever is lower." The phrase "whichever is lower" is ambiguous. It could mean:
- (a) The overall variance threshold is the lower of 5% of annual budget or $50,000; or
- (b) There are two separate thresholds: 5% of annual budget for aggregate overruns, and $50,000 for any single item, and the stricter of the two applies in each case.

**Why it matters:** For a vessel with a $1.5 million annual budget, 5% = $75,000. Under interpretation (a), the threshold is $50,000 (the lower). Under interpretation (b), Provider can incur up to $75,000 in aggregate overruns without approval, but no single item can exceed $50,000. The difference matters for operational flexibility and Customer's control over spending.

**Recommended fix:** Rewrite for clarity: "Provider shall not incur (a) aggregate expenditure in any month that exceeds the approved monthly budget by more than five percent (5%) of the approved annual budget for the relevant Covered Vessel, or (b) any single unbudgeted item exceeding US$50,000, in each case without Customer's prior written approval."

## 16. Kickoff Meeting Notes Are Non-Contractual—Transition-Period Commitments Unenforceable

**Where:** Kickoff Meeting Notes §2.2 ("Contractual terms remain as set out in the MSA, SOW No. 1 and the Quality Agreement"); Action Items table (§9).

**Issue:** The notes contain specific time-bound commitments by Provider (e.g., CVs by March 25, DPA notification by March 31, updated VRPs before each takeover date). These are marked as non-contractual.

**Why it matters:** During the transition period (March–May 2026), Zilain is operationally dependent on Provider meeting these deadlines. If Provider misses them (e.g., fails to update the Vessel Response Plan before takeover), Zilain has no contractual remedy short of arguing that the delay constitutes a material breach of the MSA or SOW—a high bar for a pre-takeover planning item.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) incorporate the action items into the Vessel Takeover Protocol as binding obligations with specific consequences for delay, or (b) add a provision to the Protocol stating that failure to complete pre-takeover steps by the agreed deadlines entitles Customer to postpone the takeover date and abate the management fee for the affected vessel until the steps are complete.

## 17. MSA §6.4 Late-Payment Interest at 1% per Month

**Where:** MSA §6.4.

**Issue:** Undisputed amounts not paid when due bear interest at 1% per month (12% per annum).

**Why it matters:** This is on the higher end for a commercial services agreement. More practically, combined with §6.3 (disputed amounts), if Zilain withholds an invoice in good faith and the dispute is resolved in Provider's favor, Provider could argue that the amount was "undisputed" as of the resolution date and that interest should run from the original due date. The interplay between §6.3 and §6.4 is not addressed.

**Recommended fix:** Add to §6.3: "While an amount is subject to a good-faith dispute under this Section 6.3, interest under Section 6.4 shall not accrue on that amount. Interest shall begin to accrue only from the date the dispute is resolved in Provider's favor."

## 18. MSA §8.2: Provider's E&O Insurance Limit ($10M) May Be Inadequate

**Where:** MSA §8.2(a).

**Issue:** Provider must maintain ship managers' errors and omissions liability insurance of not less than US$10,000,000 per claim.

**Why it matters:** While $10M is a reasonable minimum for a single-claim E&O policy, it is relevant in the context of the liability cap (Issue 6) and the narrow Environmental Losses definition (Issue 2). If a claim exceeds $10M (e.g., a major pollution incident with multiple third-party claimants), the insurance does not cover the excess, and the contractual cap limits Zilain's recovery to ~$1.86M. The insurance is a backstop for Provider, not a source of recovery for Zilain (Zilain is not a named insured or loss payee).

**Recommended fix:** Require Provider to name Zilain as an additional insured or loss payee under the E&O policy, at least for claims arising from Provider's negligence or breach. Alternatively, increase the minimum limit to $25M given the Gulf operating area and OPA 90 exposure.

5. **Internal actions for Zilain:** Calendar the 120-day non-renewal deadline; confirm the Management Accounts bank selection (due March 18); and assign internal ownership for the action items in the Kickoff Notes.
