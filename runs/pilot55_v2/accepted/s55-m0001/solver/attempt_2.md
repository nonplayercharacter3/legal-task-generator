## 1. Parent Guaranty Does Not Cover the Actual Performing Entity

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, §1.1 (definition of "Provider"); MSA §2.2 (last sentence); SOW No. 1, §2.1 (contracting entity).

**Issue:** The SOW is executed by and performed by **Geilaind Ship Management (Cyprus) Ltd**, an Affiliate. MSA §2.2 is explicit: "Only that Provider entity is responsible for the obligations under that SOW." However, the Parent Guaranty defines "Provider" as only "Geilaind Marine Services LLC, a limited liability company, and its successors and permitted assigns." The Cyprus entity is not Geilaind Marine Services LLC, nor is it a successor or assign. The Guarantor (Geilaind Holdings plc) therefore does not guarantee the obligations of the entity that is actually performing the services.

**Why it matters:** If the Cyprus entity defaults, becomes insolvent, or simply fails to perform, Zilain's only recourse is against that entity directly. The parent guaranty—the credit support Zilain was given to accept a relatively small LLC as counterparty—is unavailable. This defeats the purpose of the guaranty entirely.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Parent Guaranty §1.1 to define "Provider" as "Geilaind Marine Services LLC, any Affiliate of Geilaind Marine Services LLC that executes a SOW, and their respective successors and permitted assigns." Alternatively, require Geilaind Marine Services LLC to co-sign each SOW as a guarantor of the Affiliate's obligations, and confirm the Parent Guaranty covers Geilaind Marine Services LLC's resulting liability.

## 2. SOW Exclusivity Is Dramatically Broader Than the MSA

**Where:** SOW No. 1, §§5.1–5.3; MSA §2.4.

**Issue:** MSA §2.4 limits exclusivity to "the technical management of the Covered Vessels under that SOW" during the SOW term. SOW §5.2 replaces this with: Customer (and **all Affiliates**) must engage Provider exclusively for **all ship management, crewing and drydock supervision** for **any vessel** owned, operated or chartered by Customer or any Affiliate, **in any trading area**, for the SOW Term **plus 24 months thereafter**. SOW §5.3 adds an obligation to notify Provider within 30 days of acquiring or chartering in any vessel and to "negotiate in good faith" terms for it.

**Why it matters:** This locks Zilain's entire corporate family into Geilaind for every vessel, everywhere, for up to three years (initial SOW term) plus 24 months of post-term exclusivity—potentially five years of fleet-wide lock-in. If Zilain grows its fleet, acquires another operator, or enters a new trade, it cannot use a different manager. The "negotiate in good faith" obligation in §5.3 has no deadline and no consequence for failure to agree, creating a potential deadlock: Zilain must use Provider (per §5.2) but may not be able to agree on terms (per §5.3).

**Recommended fix:**
- Limit §5.2 to the Covered Vessels under this SOW (or vessels added by amendment), not all vessels of all Affiliates in all trades.
- Delete or shorten the 24-month post-term tail.
- Add a deadline and a walk-away right to §5.3: if the parties have not agreed terms within 60 days of notification, Customer may engage a third party for that vessel without breaching §5.2.

## 3. Loss of Hire Is Excluded from Recovery

**Where:** MSA §12.1.

**Issue:** The exclusion of indirect losses expressly includes "loss of hire." For a technical management agreement, the most probable and costly failure mode is a technical breakdown that takes a vessel off-hire. If Provider's ordinary negligence (e.g., missed maintenance, poor drydock supervision) causes a vessel to be off-hire for 30 days, Zilain cannot recover the lost charter revenue.

**Why it matters:** A 1,100–1,400 TEU feeder in U.S. Gulf service might earn $15,000–$25,000 per day. A 30-day off-hire event represents $450,000–$750,000 in lost revenue per vessel. This loss is entirely on Zilain unless it can prove gross negligence or wilful misconduct (which carve out of the exclusion under §12.3(b)).

**Recommended fix:** Carve "loss of hire directly resulting from Provider's failure to perform planned maintenance or to maintain the vessel in class" out of §12.1, or at minimum cap the exclusion at a threshold (e.g., Zilain bears the first 5 days of off-hire per event, and Provider is liable for days beyond that, subject to the §12.2 cap). Alternatively, require Provider's E&O insurance (MSA §8.2(a), $10M per claim) to expressly cover loss of hire and require a certificate confirming this.

## 4. Liability Cap Is Low Relative to the Risk

**Where:** MSA §12.2.

**Issue:** Provider's total liability is capped at "Fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event." For SOW No. 1, that is approximately **$1,860,000** (the annual management fee for six vessels). This cap applies to all claims not carved out in §12.3 (i.e., ordinary negligence, breach of contract short of the indemnity, etc.).

**Why it matters:** A single serious technical failure (e.g., a main engine breakdown caused by inadequate maintenance supervision) could result in repair costs, off-hire, and consequential losses well in excess of $1.86M. Zilain's recovery from Provider is limited to that amount. The P&I and hull insurance (arranged by Zilain under MSA §8.1) would cover the physical loss, but the insurer would subrogate to Provider—and recover at most $1.86M—leaving a gap if the loss exceeds the insurance plus the cap.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the cap to at least 24 months' fees, or tie it to the insured value of the fleet. At minimum, confirm that the E&O insurance limit ($10M per claim under MSA §8.2(a)) is intended to backstop the cap, and add a provision requiring Provider to maintain that cover for the full term with notice of cancellation.

## 5. "Environmental Losses" Carve-Out From the Cap Is Too Narrow

**Where:** Quality Agreement §1.2 (definition of "Environmental Losses"); MSA §12.3(c).

**Issue:** "Environmental Losses" is defined as "fines, penalties and assessments imposed by a Governmental Authority under MARPOL or OPA 90 arising from a Pollution Incident." This is the only environmental category excluded from the liability cap. It does **not** include:
- Clean-up and removal costs;
- Third-party claims for property damage or economic loss from pollution;
- Natural resource damages;
- Costs of implementing the Vessel Response Plan.

**Why it matters:** In a significant spill, the largest costs are typically clean-up and third-party liability, not regulatory fines. Those costs would be subject to the $1.86M cap (if attributable to Provider's negligence) or to Customer's indemnity (MSA §11.2) if the claim is framed as "relating to the Covered Vessels." The P&I insurance would respond, but again the subrogation recovery from Provider is capped.

**Recommended fix:** Broaden the definition of "Environmental Losses" in the Quality Agreement to include "all costs, expenses, losses and liabilities arising from or in connection with a Pollution Incident, including clean-up costs, third-party claims, natural resource damages, and fines or penalties." Alternatively, add a separate carve-out in MSA §12.3 for "all Losses arising from a Pollution Incident to the extent caused by Provider's negligence or breach."

## 6. Split Governing Law and Jurisdiction (MSA vs. Guaranty)

**Where:** MSA §§18.1–18.2 (Delaware law; New Castle County courts); Parent Guaranty §§9.1–9.2 (English law; English courts).

**Issue:** The underlying service contract is governed by Delaware law and litigated in Delaware. The credit support (the Guaranty) is governed by English law and litigated in England. If Zilain needs to enforce both simultaneously (e.g., Provider defaults and Zilain calls on the Guaranty), it must run parallel proceedings in two jurisdictions under two legal systems.

**Why it matters:** This increases cost, creates timing mismatches, and introduces the risk that the English court reaches a different conclusion on the scope of the "Guaranteed Obligations" than a Delaware court would on the underlying breach. It also complicates the interaction between the MSA's liability cap and the Guaranty's mirror cap (§2.5).

**Recommended fix:** Align the Guaranty's governing law and jurisdiction with the MSA (Delaware). If Geilaind Holdings plc resists (as a UK-listed entity it may have group-wide English-law documentation), at minimum add a provision that the English court will give effect to the MSA's limitation of liability and that a Delaware judgment on the underlying obligations is admissible and conclusive in the English proceedings.

## 7. No Financial Remedy for KPI Shortfalls Short of Termination

**Where:** Quality Agreement §7.3 (remediation plan); SOW No. 1 §9.1 (termination for KPI failure).

**Issue:** If Provider misses a KPI for two consecutive quarters, the only contractual consequence is a remediation plan (QA §7.3). Termination is available only for KPI No. 3 (Pollution Incidents) or No. 4 (Detentions) missed on two or more occasions in 12 months, or for failure to implement a remediation plan (SOW §9.1). There is no fee reduction, service credit, or liquidated damages mechanism for any KPI failure.

**Why it matters:** KPI No. 8 (unplanned off-hire ≤ 3 days per vessel per year) and KPI No. 6 (zero overdue critical maintenance) are the ones most directly tied to Zilain's commercial performance. If Provider chronically misses these, Zilain suffers off-hire and charter damage but has no financial recourse short of the nuclear option of termination (which disrupts the entire fleet's management).

**Recommended fix:** Add a service-credit schedule to SOW No. 1: for each KPI missed in a quarter, a specified percentage of the monthly management fee is credited (e.g., 5% per KPI, capped at 25% of the monthly fee per quarter). This gives Zilain a tangible remedy and creates a financial incentive for Provider to maintain performance.

## 8. Pollution Incident Termination Threshold Requires Two Events

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §9.1(a).

**Issue:** Customer may terminate for KPI No. 3 failure only on "two (2) or more occasions in any twelve (12) month period." The KPI target is zero pollution incidents. This means a single (even catastrophic) pollution incident does not, by itself, give Zilain the right to terminate under this clause.

**Why it matters:** A single major spill could destroy a vessel's reputation, trigger regulatory scrutiny, increase insurance premiums, and result in the vessel being denied entry to certain ports. Zilain may want to terminate after one serious incident to protect its brand and charterers' confidence, but the clause requires two.

**Recommended fix:** Add a separate termination right for a single Pollution Incident that results in (a) a vessel detention, (b) a regulatory fine exceeding a specified threshold (e.g., $100,000), or (c) a PSC inspection score below a specified level. Alternatively, lower the threshold to one occasion for KPI No. 3.

## 9. Pre-Existing Vessel Condition Not Clearly Allocated at Takeover

**Where:** Vessel Takeover Protocol §4.4; MSA §7.2.

**Issue:** Protocol §4.4 states: "The Parties shall agree in good faith which items are to be rectified before the takeover date." There is no mechanism if they cannot agree. It further provides that "Rectification work carried out after the Takeover Time shall be included in the vessel's operating budget or approved under Section 7.2 of the MSA." This means pre-existing defects that are not rectified before takeover become budget items—i.e., Zilain's cost through the Management Accounts.

**Why it matters:** The vessels are 10–15 years old. They will have outstanding defects at takeover. If the parties disagree on what must be fixed before the takeover date, the default is that everything becomes a post-takeover budget item. Zilain effectively pays for pre-existing conditions through its operating budget, with no cap and no allocation of responsibility to Provider.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a deadline: if the parties have not agreed on pre-takeover rectification items by [X] days before the takeover date, the items identified in the inspection report as "must be rectified before takeover" are deemed accepted as-is, and Provider warrants that the vessel is in the condition described in the report.
- Add a "takeover condition" schedule (annexed to the protocol for each vessel) that lists the agreed state of the vessel at takeover, with a cap on the value of pre-existing defects that can be passed to the budget (e.g., $50,000 per vessel).
- Clarify that defects existing before the Takeover Time that are not listed in the inspection report remain Provider's responsibility if they manifest within 90 days after takeover.

## 10. Provider Can Replace Key Personnel Without Customer Consent

**Where:** MSA §4.1.

**Issue:** "Provider may replace assigned personnel on reasonable notice to Customer. The replacement must have equivalent qualifications." There is no requirement for Customer's consent or approval.

**Why it matters:** The SOW requires a fleet manager with 10+ years' experience (SOW §4.1) and a Houston-based superintendent (SOW §4.2). These are relationship-critical roles. If Provider replaces the Houston superintendent with someone of "equivalent qualifications" but less local knowledge, or replaces the fleet manager mid-contract, Zilain has no contractual right to object. "Reasonable notice" is undefined.

**Recommended fix:** Add: "Provider shall not replace the Fleet Manager or the Houston-based Superintendent without Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. Any other personnel replacement requires at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice." Define "reasonable notice" as a minimum of 30 days for shore personnel and 60 days for the fleet manager.

## 11. Customer Indemnity Is Overly Broad

**Where:** MSA §11.2.

**Issue:** Customer indemnifies Provider against "all Losses arising from third-party claims relating to the Covered Vessels or the Services." The carve-back applies only to the extent the claim "result[s] from Provider's negligence, breach of this Agreement or wilful misconduct." The phrase "relating to" is extremely broad—it could encompass cargo claims, bunker claims, port claims, and crew claims, all of which "relate to" the vessels or the services.

**Why it matters:** In a cargo damage claim, the carrier (Zilain) is prima facie liable to the cargo interest. If the cause is ambiguous (e.g., was it a stowage issue—Provider's responsibility—or a weather issue—no one's fault?), the indemnity could be triggered. Zilain would have to prove Provider's negligence to avoid the indemnity, which is a high bar in a dispute where the facts are contested.

**Recommended fix:** Narrow the trigger from "relating to" to "to the extent caused by or resulting from the acts or omissions of Customer, its personnel, or its independent contractors (other than Provider)." Alternatively, add: "This indemnity does not apply to any claim to the extent the underlying facts are within Provider's control or responsibility under the Services."

## 12. Force Majeure Definition Is Broad and Asymmetric in Practice

**Where:** MSA §16.1.

**Issue:** Force majeure includes "government action" and "named storms." There is no exclusion for events that a competent ship manager should have planned for (e.g., hurricane-season preparations, regulatory changes that were foreseeable). The 60-day termination trigger is the only consequence; there is no obligation on Provider to use alternative means of performance or to mitigate beyond "reasonable efforts."

**Why it matters:** In U.S. Gulf service, named storms (hurricanes) are a known, recurring risk. A competent manager plans for them (securing vessels, pre-positioning spares, adjusting schedules). If Provider invokes force majeure for a hurricane that it should have planned for, it is excused from performance for up to 60 days with no liability. Zilain's vessels sit idle and it cannot claim.

**Recommended fix:** Add: "Force majeure does not include events that a competent third-party technical manager of container feeder vessels operating in the U.S. Gulf would reasonably be expected to plan for and mitigate, including seasonal weather patterns and foreseeable regulatory changes." Also add an obligation: "Provider shall use commercially reasonable efforts to resume performance as soon as practicable after the force majeure event ceases."

## 13. Interim ISM/ISPS Certificates: No Consequence for Delay

**Where:** Vessel Takeover Protocol §3.2(c); MSA §3.2.

**Issue:** Provider must apply for interim Safety Management Certificates and interim International Ship Security Certificates before takeover. But the protocol does not specify what happens if the Flag State or class society does not issue them by the takeover date. MSA §3.2 requires Provider to "hold the required Document of Compliance and maintain the Safety Management System," but a DoC is a shore-based certificate; the vessel-level SMS certificate is what's at issue.

**Why it matters:** If the interim certificates are not in place at the Takeover Time, the vessel is technically operating without a valid ISM certificate in the new manager's name. This is a regulatory breach that could result in PSC detention. The risk and cost fall on Zilain (the registered owner), even though the delay may be attributable to Provider's late application or to the Flag State's processing time.

**Recommended fix:** Add to the protocol: "Provider shall submit all applications for interim certificates no later than [30] days before the applicable takeover date. If the interim certificates have not been issued by the Takeover Time due to Provider's failure to submit the application in time, Provider shall bear all costs and losses resulting from the delay, including any detention. If the delay is due to the Flag State or class society, the takeover date shall be postponed by the period of delay at no cost to either Party."

## 14. Disputed Amounts: Notice Must Precede the Due Date

**Where:** MSA §6.3.

**Issue:** To withhold a disputed amount, Customer "must give Provider written notice of the dispute, with reasonable detail, **before the due date**." If Zilain discovers a billing error after the 30-day payment period has expired (e.g., through the annual audit under §6.6), it cannot withhold—it must pay and then seek a refund.

**Why it matters:** In practice, discrepancies in vessel operating expenses (the Management Accounts) often surface during monthly accounting review (MSA §7.4 gives 20 days after month-end) or during the annual audit. If the invoice was due 30 days after receipt and the discrepancy is found at day 35, Zilain has already missed the window. It must pay the full amount and then chase a refund, losing leverage.

**Recommended fix:** Change "before the due date" to "within ten (10) Business Days after the due date." Alternatively, provide that Customer may withhold any amount that is the subject of a pending audit under §6.6 without prior notice, and the audit results shall determine the amount due.

## 15. Superintendent Coverage: Two of Three Based in Cyprus

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §4.2; Kickoff Meeting Notes §4.2.

**Issue:** The SOW requires one superintendent per two vessels (three total for six vessels) and that "at least one superintendent shall be based in Houston, Texas." The kickoff notes confirm: one in Houston, two in Limassol. The two Cyprus-based superintendents cover four of the six vessels and must travel to the Gulf "as required."

**Why it matters:** For a fleet trading daily in U.S. Gulf ports, having only one local superintendent is a responsiveness risk. In an emergency (casualty, PSC inspection, drydock issue), the Cyprus-based superintendent may be 9–12 hours away by air. The MSA requires "prompt" notification and response (MSA §3.4), and the Quality Agreement requires close-out of deficiencies within authority-set timeframes (QA §3.4). Time-zone differences (EST vs. EET, 7–8 hours) compound the delay.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SOW §4.2 to require at least two superintendents based in the U.S. Gulf (Houston or another Gulf port) for the SOW Term. If cost is the concern, this could be structured as one full-time Houston-based superintendent plus one who is based in the Gulf for at least 50% of each month.

## 16. MSA §12.4: Package Limitation Savings Clause

**Where:** MSA §12.4.

**Issue:** "Nothing in this Agreement deprives Provider of any right to limit liability that is available to a ship manager under any applicable convention or statute." This is a broad savings clause that preserves any statutory or conventional limitation of liability available to a ship manager, regardless of what the MSA's own cap and exclusions provide.

**Why it matters:** It is unclear what conventions or statutes this refers to. The Limitation of Liability Act (46 U.S.C. §30501 et seq.) applies to "owners" and "masters" of vessels, not typically to third-party managers. However, if a court were to find that Provider, as the ISM "Company," has a statutory limitation available to it, this clause would preserve it even if it is lower than the MSA cap. The clause creates uncertainty about which limitation applies.

**Recommended fix:** Delete §12.4, or replace it with: "Nothing in this Agreement limits Provider's liability beyond the cap set out in Section 12.2, except as provided in Section 12.3." If Provider insists on retaining a savings clause, limit it to specific identified conventions (e.g., "the Limitation of Liability Act, 46 U.S.C. §30501 et seq., to the extent applicable to a ship manager") and provide that the MSA cap applies to the extent it is more favorable to Customer.

## 17. No Requirement for Provider to Give Notice of Insurance Cancellation or Material Change

**Where:** MSA §8.2.

**Issue:** Provider must maintain E&O, CGL, and employers' liability insurance. The only monitoring right is: "On request, Provider shall provide certificates of insurance to Customer." There is no obligation on Provider to notify Customer of cancellation, non-renewal, material reduction in coverage, or a change in insurer.

**Why it matters:** If Provider's E&O policy is cancelled (e.g., for non-payment of premium or a change in risk profile) and Zilain does not find out until after a loss occurs, Zilain's subrogation recovery (via its own P&I insurer) may be uninsured. The $10M E&O cover is the primary backstop for the liability cap, and its absence would leave Zilain exposed.

**Recommended fix:** Add: "Provider shall give Customer written notice at least thirty (30) days before any cancellation, non-renewal, or material reduction in the insurance required by this Section 8.2. Provider shall provide updated certificates of insurance annually and upon any material change."

## 18. SMS Documentation License Ends on Termination

**Where:** MSA §10.2; MSA §15.1(a).

**Issue:** Provider retains ownership of its SMS manuals, procedures, and software. Customer's license is "to the extent necessary to receive and use the Services" during the SOW term. On termination, Provider must deliver "SMS documentation" (MSA §15.1(a)), but the license does not extend post-termination.

**Why it matters:** In practice, the SMS is embedded in the vessel's operations. Crew are trained on it, procedures are followed under it, and internal audits are conducted against it. After termination, Zilain (or its new manager) cannot legally use Provider's SMS documentation. The new manager must implement its own SMS from scratch, which takes time and creates a gap in ISM compliance. The "delivery" of documentation on handover is of limited use if Zilain cannot use it.

**Recommended fix:** Add a post-termination license: "For a period of twelve (12) months after termination of the applicable SOW, Customer and its successor manager shall have a non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use Provider's SMS manuals and procedures solely to maintain ISM compliance during the transition to a new Safety Management System."

## 19. No Cap on Pre-Takeover Rectification Passed to Budget

**Where:** Vessel Takeover Protocol §4.4; SOW No. 1 §7.2 (expenditure threshold).

**Issue:** As noted in Issue 9, pre-existing defects not rectified before takeover go into the operating budget. The SOW's expenditure threshold (§7.2) is 5% of the annual budget or $50,000 per item, whichever is lower. But this threshold applies to *unbudgeted* expenditure during the term. If the defects are included in the *approved* budget (which Provider prepares under SOW §7.1), there is no threshold at all—Provider can build any amount of pre-existing rectification into the budget, and Zilain must approve it or the work doesn't get done.

**Why it matters:** Provider has an incentive to include as much pre-existing rectification work as possible in the initial budget, because it is funded by Zilain through the Management Accounts. There is no benchmark or cap. Zilain's only protection is its approval right, but if it doesn't approve, the work doesn't happen and the vessel's condition deteriorates.

**Recommended fix:** Add to the protocol or SOW: "The total value of pre-existing rectification items included in the initial budget for any Covered Vessel shall not exceed [e.g., $100,000] per vessel without Customer's prior written approval. Items exceeding this amount shall be treated as capital expenditure and require separate Customer authorization."

## 20. Automatic Renewal Combined with Post-Term Exclusivity Creates Extended Lock-In

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §2.2 (auto-renewal, 120-day notice); SOW No. 1 §5.2 (24-month post-term exclusivity).

**Issue:** The SOW renews automatically for one-year periods unless 120 days' notice is given. If Zilain misses the notice deadline, it is locked in for another year. At the end of that year (whether initial term or renewal), the 24-month exclusivity tail in §5.2 kicks in. In the worst case, Zilain could be bound for: 3-year initial term + 1-year renewal (missed notice) + 24-month exclusivity tail = **6 years** of fleet-wide exclusivity.

**Why it matters:** This is an unusually long effective commitment for a technical management arrangement, particularly given that the MSA itself can be terminated on 90 days' notice when no SOW is in effect (MSA §14.2). The auto-renewal plus exclusivity tail converts what should be a flexible framework into a long-term lock-in.

**Recommended fix:**
- Reduce the non-renewal notice period from 120 days to 60 days, and add a calendar reminder obligation on Provider to notify Customer of the upcoming renewal date at least 150 days in advance.
- Reduce or eliminate the 24-month post-term exclusivity tail (see Issue 2).
- Add: "Customer may terminate any renewal period on ninety (90) days' written notice, without penalty, if Provider has failed to meet two or more KPIs in the preceding twelve (12) months."

## 21. Crew Insurance Arrangements Are Undefined

**Where:** SOW No. 1 §3.2; MSA §8.1(b).

**Issue:** The SOW provides that Provider handles "crew insurance arrangements, other than the vessel P&I cover arranged by Customer." It does not specify what "crew insurance arrangements" means—whether it includes P&I cover for crew claims (death, injury, illness), crew repatriation costs, or separate crew insurance policies. MSA §8.1(b) requires Customer to arrange P&I insurance "including pollution cover," but it is unclear whether the P&I cover also extends to crew claims (as is typical in a P&I entry) or whether Provider must arrange separate crew cover.

**Why it matters:** If there is a gap in crew insurance (e.g., Provider assumes it is covered by Customer's P&I entry, but the P&I entry does not cover a particular crew risk), the cost falls on Zilain as employer of record or registered owner. The ambiguity could also create a dispute between Provider and Zilain's P&I club over who is responsible for a crew claim.

**Recommended fix:** Add a schedule to the SOW specifying: (a) what crew insurance Provider is responsible for arranging and maintaining (with minimum cover levels); (b) what is covered under Customer's P&I entry; and (c) that Provider shall provide certificates of crew insurance to Customer before the first crew changeover.

## 22. MSA §19.2 Tension: Agent vs. Independent Contractor

**Where:** MSA §3.1 (first sentence); MSA §19.2.

**Issue:** Section 3.1 states Provider "shall perform the Services as agent for and on behalf of Customer or the relevant registered owner." Section 19.2 states "Provider performs the Services as an independent contractor. Provider acts as agent for Customer or the registered owner only to the extent necessary to perform the Services." These provisions are in tension.

**Why it matters:** If Provider is an agent, its acts and omissions may be attributable to Zilain as principal in third-party claims (e.g., a port state control deficiency caused by Provider's instructions could be treated as Zilain's own breach). If Provider is an independent contractor, Zilain has less control but also less vicarious liability. The ambiguity means that in a dispute with a third party, the counterparty will argue for the characterization most favorable to it (i.e., that Zilain is the principal and therefore liable).

**Recommended fix:** Clarify §19.2: "Provider is an independent contractor. To the extent Provider acts as agent for Customer or the registered owner (including for the purposes of ISM and ISPS compliance), Provider's authority is limited to the specific acts described in Section 3.1 and the applicable SOW. Provider shall not bind Customer or the registered owner to any obligation without Customer's prior written consent."

## 23. No Specific Consequence for Provider's Failure to Maintain Class

**Where:** MSA §3.3(a); Quality Agreement §3.1; Schedule 1 KPI No. 7.

**Issue:** Provider is required to keep each vessel in class. The KPI target is zero overdue conditions of class. If Provider fails, the consequences are: (a) a remediation plan if missed for two consecutive quarters (QA §7.3); (b) potential termination under SOW §9.1 only if it results in a detention (KPI No. 4). There is no specific fee adjustment, no liquidated damages, and no termination right for a class deficiency that does not result in a detention.

**Why it matters:** A vessel out of class cannot be chartered to most cargo owners (charter parties require vessels to be in class). If Provider's failure to maintain class results in a vessel being uncharterable for 30 days, Zilain loses the charter but cannot recover from Provider (loss of hire is excluded under MSA §12.1, and the cap limits recovery to ~$1.86M).

**Recommended fix:** Add to the SOW: "If a Covered Vessel is not in class for more than [10] consecutive days due to Provider's failure to perform its obligations under MSA §3.3(a), Customer shall be entitled to a fee credit of [X]% of the monthly management fee for that vessel for each day the vessel remains out of class, up to a maximum of [Y]% of the monthly fee."

## 25. MSA §14.4(b): Causation Requirement for Detention Termination

**Where:** MSA §14.4(b).

**Issue:** Customer may terminate if a vessel is detained for more than 14 days "because of Provider's failure to perform the Services." The word "because of" imposes a causation requirement.

**Why it matters:** If a vessel is detained for a deficiency that existed before takeover (e.g., a class condition that was noted in the pre-takeover inspection but classified as "monitor" rather than "rectify before takeover"), Provider will argue the detention was not "because of" its failure to perform. Zilain would then have no termination right despite a 14+ day detention.

**Recommended fix:** Change "because of Provider's failure to perform the Services" to "where Provider is responsible for the deficiency giving rise to the detention, or where the deficiency arose after the Takeover Time." This captures both post-takeover failures and pre-existing deficiencies that Provider accepted responsibility for at takeover.
