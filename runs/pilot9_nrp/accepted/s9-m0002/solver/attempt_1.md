## ISSUE 1 – Change of Control: Pellworth Capital Partners

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 16.2, § 16.3; Parent Guaranty § 8.2, § 8.3

**What the documents say:**

The Agreement defines "Change of Control" as, inter alia, any transaction in which a person acquires more than 50% of the voting equity of Buyer **or of any direct or indirect parent of Buyer** (Supply Agreement § 1.1). A Change of Control at the parent level is deemed an assignment requiring Meideis's prior written consent (§ 16.2). If it occurs without consent, Meideis may terminate the Agreement and all SOWs on 30 days' notice, exercisable at any time within 12 months after Meideis learns of it (§ 16.3). Buyer must give Meideis written notice of any *proposed* Change of Control at least 30 days before completion (§ 16.2).

Separately, the Parent Guaranty provides that a change of control of the Guarantor (Besouth Holdings LLC) does not release the Guaranty (§ 8.2), but if the Guarantor ceases to own a majority of the Buyer, the Guaranty can be terminated for future obligations (§ 8.3).

**Why it matters:**

Pellworth Capital Partners is identified in your instruction as a party involved in this matter, but it does not appear in any of the five documents. If Pellworth is acquiring (or has acquired) a controlling stake in Besouth Holdings LLC or in Besouth Facade Systems, Inc., the following consequences follow:

- Meideis has a termination right over the entire Agreement and SOW No. 1 (Calloway Pier Tower), which would strand the project mid-construction.
- The 30-day advance notice obligation in § 16.2 may already have been breached if the transaction closed without notice.
- The Parent Guaranty survives a CoC of the Guarantor, so the parent's $4.5M exposure continues regardless.

**Recommendation:**

**[CLIENT DECISION NEEDED]** We need to understand Pellworth Capital Partners' role. Specifically:
- Is Pellworth acquiring an equity interest in Besouth Holdings LLC or Besouth Facade Systems, Inc.?
- What percentage, and has the transaction closed or is it pending?
- Did Besouth provide the 30-day advance notice to Meideis required by § 16.2?
- If the transaction is pending, we should advise Besouth to give notice immediately and seek Meideis's written consent before closing.
- If the transaction has already closed without notice, we need to assess exposure under § 16.3 and consider whether to approach Meideis proactively to obtain retroactive consent.

I am not assuming anything about Pellworth's role. Please confirm the facts.

## ISSUE 3 – 7% Price Increase Applies to SOW No. 1 Despite SOW's Price-Protection Clause

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1 § 7.6; SOW § 4.4; Supply Agreement § 18.2 (order of precedence)

**What the documents say:**

SOW § 4.4 states: "Prices under this SOW are subject to adjustment **only** as provided in Section 7.2 of the Agreement." Section 7.2 permits adjustment once per contract year, capped at the PPI change for flat glass.

Amendment No. 1 § 7.6 adds a new mechanism: "With effect from the first day of each Renewal Term, the prices in Exhibit B, **and the prices in each Statement of Work** for Products delivered during that Renewal Term, increase by 7% over the prices in effect on the last day of the preceding term."

Supply Agreement § 18.2 establishes the order of precedence: **Amendments > Supply Agreement > Statements of Work.**

**Why it matters:**

Under the order of precedence, Amendment § 7.6 controls over SOW § 4.4. The 7% increase applies to the Calloway Pier Tower SOW for any Products delivered during the Renewal Term (i.e., on or after February 1, 2027). The SOW's "only as provided in Section 7.2" language is overridden.

Additionally, if the project is still in production during the Renewal Term, only the units delivered after February 1, 2027 would be subject to the increase. The client should model how many units fall in each period.

## ISSUE 4 – Exclusive Purchase Obligation Is Far Broader Than the SOW

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 3.1; SOW § 2.1

**What the documents say:**

Supply Agreement § 3.1 requires Buyer to purchase **all** of its requirements for architectural glass products **throughout North America** from Meideis, including IGUs, laminated glass, heat-strengthened and fully tempered glass, and coated and spandrel glass. Buyer and its Affiliates "shall not purchase these products from any other source during the Term."

SOW § 2.1 limits exclusivity to "insulated glass units for the Calloway Pier Tower project, Baltimore, Maryland."

**Why it matters:**

The SOW's narrow exclusivity does not carve out from the Agreement's broad one. Under the order of precedence (Supply Agreement § 18.2), the Agreement prevails over the SOW. Besouth is contractually locked into buying all architectural glass from Meideis for all North American projects for the duration of the Term (including any Renewal Term). This is a significant commercial constraint that may not be fully appreciated by the client, particularly if it has other projects in the pipeline where it would prefer a different glass supplier.

**Recommendation:**

- Flag to the client that the exclusivity in § 3.1 is not project-limited.
- If the client wants flexibility, this would need to be addressed in a future amendment or at renewal.
- Note that the Affiliates are also bound by § 3.1 ("Buyer shall, and shall cause its Affiliates to, purchase from Supplier all of its requirements"). If Besouth Holdings has other operating subsidiaries, they are covered.

## ISSUE 5 – Parent Guaranty: Scope, Cap, and Interaction with Change of Control

**Documents:** Parent Guaranty §§ 1.2, 2.1, 3, 4.3, 6, 8.1–8.3; Supply Agreement § 16.2–16.3

**What the documents say:**

- Besouth Holdings LLC (parent) has given an absolute, unconditional, irrevocable guaranty of payment (Guaranty § 2.1).
- Guaranteed Obligations include all payment obligations under the Supply Agreement, every SOW, and every PO (§ 1.2), including prices, freight, rack charges, late-payment interest, and termination amounts.
- Cap: $4,500,000 plus enforcement costs (§ 3).
- Not released by amendments, renewals, price adjustments, or waivers (§ 4.3).
- No subrogation until all Guaranteed Obligations are paid in full (§ 6).
- Continuing until the later of Agreement expiry/termination and completion of all SOWs (§ 8.1).
- A CoC of the Guarantor does not release it (§ 8.2). If the Guarantor ceases to own a majority of the Buyer, it may terminate for future obligations (§ 8.3).

**Why it matters:**

1. **Adequacy of cap:** The Calloway Pier Tower SOW alone represents approximately $5.7M in product value (see Issue 3 calculation at current prices, before the 7% increase). The $4.5M cap is less than the SOW value, meaning the Guaranty does not fully backstop even one project. If Meideis is relying on the Guaranty for credit, it may press for a higher cap or additional security as obligations grow.

2. **Interaction with CoC (Issue 1):** If Pellworth acquires Besouth Holdings, the Guaranty continues (§ 8.2), but the Supply Agreement may be terminated by Meideis (§ 16.3). If the Agreement is terminated, the Guaranty continues only for pre-termination obligations (§ 8.1(a)). However, SOWs continue under § 2.5, so obligations under SOW No. 1 survive.

3. **Subrogation lockout (§ 6):** Until *all* Guaranteed Obligations are paid in full, Besouth Holdings cannot seek reimbursement from Besouth Facade Systems. This could be a significant cash-flow issue for the parent if it is called on to pay.

4. **No release for amendments (§ 4.3):** The 7% price increase and any future amendments expand the Guaranteed Obligations without releasing the Guarantor. The cap in § 3 still applies, but the scope of what's guaranteed grows.

**Recommendation:**

- The client should be aware that the parent's exposure is $4.5M and that this is a continuing, non-cancelable obligation (short of the § 8.3 trigger).
- If the CoC in Issue 1 proceeds, we should model the Guaranty's survival and whether the § 8.3 trigger (loss of majority ownership of Buyer) is implicated.
- Consider whether the subrogation lockout in § 6 is acceptable to the parent, particularly if the parent is in a different financial position than the subsidiary.

## ISSUE 6 – SOW Lead Times Create Scheduling Risk for Podium and Penthouse

**Documents:** SOW § 5.2, § 5.3; Supply Agreement § 5.2

**What the documents say:**

- Standard lead times (Agreement § 5.2): 6 weeks for standard IGUs; 10 weeks for laminated or custom-coated units.
- SOW § 5.3: IGU-2 (podium storefront, laminated) and IGU-4 (penthouse, triple-pane) have a **12-week** lead time.
- SOW § 5.2: Buyer must release each floor sequence at least **8 weeks** before the required delivery date.

**Why it matters:**

For IGU-2 and IGU-4, Besouth must release the PO at least 8 weeks before needed delivery, but the lead time is 12 weeks. This means the PO must actually be released **at least 20 weeks** before the units are needed on site (8-week release buffer + 12-week fabrication). If the project schedule compresses, Besouth has limited flexibility.

The podium (levels 1–2) is typically installed early in the project sequence, and the penthouse (level 31) may be scheduled for later. If either is on the critical path, the 12-week lead time is a constraint the client's project team needs to manage.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client's project manager (Renata Kluge) when the podium and penthouse glass are needed on the critical path.
- Ensure POs for IGU-2 and IGU-4 are released no later than 20 weeks before the required delivery date.
- Note that SOW § 5.5 gives priority to remakes (3 weeks), which provides some relief for breakage but not for initial scheduling.

## ISSUE 7 – Mock-Up Is a Critical-Path Gate

**Documents:** SOW § 3.4, § 6.1

**What the documents say:**

- Production will not begin until the Architect of Record approves the mock-up and visual samples (SOW § 3.4).
- Before first production shipment, Supplier must deliver IGCC/IGMA certification, ASTM E2190 test reports, and structural silicone adhesion/compatibility reports (SOW § 6.1).
- Mock-up glass is to be supplied within 6 weeks after the PO for mock-up glass (SOW § 3.4).

**Why it matters:**

The mock-up approval is a hard gate. If the Architect delays approval, or if the silicone compatibility tests (required by SOW § 3.3(d)) fail and require retesting, the entire production schedule slips. The 6-week mock-up lead time plus the Architect's review period plus the 12-week production lead time for complex units means the mock-up PO should be placed as early as possible.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm the mock-up PO has been issued and the timeline for Architect approval.
- Ensure the structural silicone compatibility testing (SOW § 3.3(d)) is underway, as this is a prerequisite for both mock-up and production.
- If the mock-up is not yet approved, flag the risk to the client's overall project schedule.

## ISSUE 8 – Freight Reimbursement "At Cost" Is Undefined

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 5.1; SOW § 4.5

**What the documents say:**

Where Supplier arranges freight to the Delivery Point (as it does under SOW § 4.5 for the Calloway project), Buyer reimburses freight "at cost." The term "cost" is not defined in the Agreement or the SOW.

**Why it matters:**

"At cost" could mean the carrier's invoice price, or it could be disputed if the carrier includes a margin, fuel surcharge, or administrative fee. With 6,720 units being delivered to a Baltimore facility, freight is a non-trivial expense. An undefined standard invites dispute.

**Recommendation:**

- Ask the client whether it has a historical understanding of what "at cost" means in its dealings with Meideis.
- If the relationship is new, consider proposing a clarification (e.g., "at cost means the amount actually paid to the carrier, documented by carrier invoice, excluding Supplier's administrative fees").
- This is a low-priority item but worth addressing before the first freight invoice comes due.

## ISSUE 10 – Dispute Resolution Escalation May Be Too Slow for Urgent Delivery Issues

**Documents:** Supply Agreement § 17.1, § 17.2; SOW § 5.5

**What the documents say:**

- Disputes must first be referred to senior executives, who meet within 15 days of written request (§ 17.1).
- If unresolved 30 days after that request, either party may litigate in New Castle County, Delaware (§ 17.2).
- Total minimum escalation period: 45 days before court is available.
- Injunctive relief is available in any court (§ 17.3).

**Why it matters:**

If Meideis fails to deliver on time and the project is at risk, the 45-day escalation period is long. The client's only fast remedy is injunctive relief (§ 17.3), which requires demonstrating irreparable harm. For a construction project, schedule delay may or may not qualify.

The SOW does provide for priority remakes within 3 weeks (SOW § 5.5), which is a useful operational remedy for breakage, but it doesn't address initial delivery delays.

**Recommendation:**

- Note that Agreement § 5.3 gives Buyer the right to require expedited shipment at Supplier's cost for late delivery (non-Force Majeure). This is an operational remedy that doesn't require the dispute escalation process.
- If a delivery delay threatens the project schedule, the client should invoke § 5.3 first before escalating under § 17.
- The 45-day window is a risk to be aware of, not necessarily to fix now.
