## Issue 1 – Automatic Renewal Has Been Triggered; Non-Renewal Deadline Has Passed

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, § 2.2; Supply Agreement, § 2.1

The Initial Term runs three years from February 1, 2024, expiring **February 1, 2027**. Amendment No. 1, § 2.2 provides that the Agreement "will automatically renew for successive renewal terms of 24 months each" unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal **at least 180 days before expiry** of the then-current term.

180 days before February 1, 2027 is approximately **August 5, 2026**. The other documents in the file are dated October 2026 (insurance certificate, October 20; parent guaranty, October 5). The non-renewal deadline has therefore **already passed**. The Agreement will renew automatically on February 1, 2027 for a 24-month Renewal Term (through February 1, 2029).

**Why it matters:** This locks Besouth into:
- The exclusive purchase obligation (Supply Agreement § 3.1) for another 24 months;
- The 7% mandatory price increase (see Issue 2);
- The Parent Guaranty (see Issue 8), which is not released by renewal (Guaranty § 4.3(a));
- The Change of Control consent requirement (see Issue 3).

The next opportunity to exit will be 180 days before February 1, 2029, i.e., approximately **August 5, 2028**.

**Recommendation:** If the client is comfortable continuing with Meideis, no action is required, but it should calendar the August 2028 non-renewal deadline. If the client wants out or wants to renegotiate, the only lever is to negotiate a new amendment with Meideis now (e.g., a buyout, a revised pricing structure, or a narrowed exclusivity). There is no unilateral exit right in the documents.

## Issue 2 – Mandatory 7% Price Increase at Renewal, Stacking on PPI Adjustments

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1, § 7.6; Supply Agreement, § 7.2; SOW § 4.4; Supply Agreement § 18.2 (order of precedence)

Amendment No. 1, § 7.6 provides that "with effect from the first day of each Renewal Term, the prices in Exhibit B, **and the prices in each Statement of Work** for Products delivered during that Renewal Term, increase by 7% over the prices in effect on the last day of the preceding term." It further states that any PPI adjustment under § 7.2 "applies to the prices as increased under this Section 7.6."

So in the Renewal Term (beginning February 1, 2027), Besouth faces:
1. An immediate 7% across-the-board increase, **plus**
2. A further PPI-based adjustment (up to the percentage change in the BLS PPI for flat glass) that Meideis can exercise once per contract year on 60 days' notice.

**SOW § 4.4** attempts to limit price adjustments: "Prices under this SOW are subject to adjustment only as provided in Section 7.2 of the Agreement." However, **Supply Agreement § 18.2** establishes the order of precedence as: Amendments → Supply Agreement → Statement of Work. The Amendment (which adds § 7.6) therefore **overrides** the SOW's limitation. The 7% increase applies to the Calloway Pier Tower SOW prices.

**Illustrative impact on SOW pricing (effective February 1, 2027):**

| Type | Current Price | +7% |
|------|--------------|-----|
| IGU-1 | $27.75 | $29.69 |
| IGU-2 | $46.10 | $49.33 |
| IGU-3 | $33.40 | $35.74 |
| IGU-4 | $45.25 | $48.42 |

On the estimated 186,000 sq. ft., this represents roughly **$130,000–$140,000 in additional cost** for the project, before any PPI adjustment.

**Recommendation:**
- If the project schedule extends into the Renewal Term (likely, given a 31-story tower), Besouth should build the 7% increase into its project budget and, if possible, pass it through to the Owner via a change order or price escalation clause in its subcontract.
- In any renegotiation with Meideis (see Issue 1), seek to cap the combined § 7.6 + § 7.2 increase (e.g., 5% all-in per year) or make the 7% increase conditional on a PPI threshold.

## Issue 3 – Change of Control Provisions May Be Triggered by Pellworth Capital Partners

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, §§ 1.1 ("Change of Control"), 16.2, 16.3; Guaranty, § 8.2

Supply Agreement § 16.2 provides that any Change of Control of Buyer, "including a Change of Control occurring at the level of any direct or indirect parent of Buyer," is **deemed an assignment** requiring Meideis's prior written consent. Buyer must notify Meideis at least 30 days before the transaction closes.

Under § 16.3, if a Change of Control occurs **without** Meideis's consent, Meideis may terminate the Agreement and any or all SOWs on 30 days' notice, at any time within 12 months after it learns of the Change of Control.

The "Change of Control" definition (§ 1.1) is triggered when any person or group acquires more than 50% of the voting equity of Buyer **or of any direct or indirect parent of Buyer**.

**Pellworth Capital Partners** is identified in your instruction as a party involved, but **none of the five documents mentions Pellworth**. We do not know:
- Whether Pellworth is an existing investor in Besouth Holdings LLC;
- Whether a transaction is contemplated or in progress;
- What percentage stake is involved;
- The expected timing.

**Why it matters:** If Pellworth (or any other person/group) acquires more than 50% of Besouth Holdings LLC (the direct parent of Buyer), that is a Change of Control under § 1.1. Without Meideis's consent, Meideis can terminate the entire supply relationship—including the Calloway Pier Tower SOW—within 12 months. For a 31-story tower mid-construction, that would be catastrophic.

Additionally, Guaranty § 8.2 provides that a change of control of the Guarantor (Besouth Holdings) does **not** release the guaranty. So even if Pellworth takes control of the parent, the guaranty continues (subject to the $4.5M cap).

**Recommendation:**
- If a Pellworth transaction is contemplated, Besouth must give Meideis written notice at least 30 days before closing (§ 16.2) and obtain Meideis's written consent **before** the transaction completes.
- The consent request should be made early in the process to avoid a situation where the transaction closes and Meideis later asserts a termination right.
- Consider whether Meideis will use this as leverage to extract pricing concessions.

## Issue 4 – Order of Precedence Favors the Master Agreement Over the SOW

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 18.2

Section 18.2 states: "If there is a conflict among the transaction documents, they prevail in this order: **Amendments, then the Supply Agreement, then any Statement of Work**."

This means the general master agreement overrides project-specific SOW terms. While this is not unusual in all contexts, it creates specific problems here:

- SOW § 4.4 limits price adjustments to § 7.2 only, but Amendment § 7.6 (which prevails) adds the 7% increase (see Issue 2).
- If a future SOW includes more favorable delivery, quality, or remedy terms, the master agreement can override them.
- The SOW's project-specific exclusivity (§ 2.1, limited to IGUs for Calloway Pier Tower) sits alongside the Agreement's much broader exclusivity (§ 3.1, all glass, all projects, all of North America, Buyer and Affiliates). The broader term governs.

**Recommendation:** In any renegotiation, seek to reverse the order of precedence so that the SOW (as the more specific document) prevails over the master agreement for project-specific terms. At minimum, add a provision that the SOW controls where it expressly states an intention to override a specific section of the Agreement.

**[DECISION NEEDED:** Is the client willing to raise this with Meideis as part of any renewal negotiation?]

## Issue 5 – SOW Release Schedule Inconsistent with Lead Times for IGU-2 and IGU-4

**Documents:** SOW, §§ 5.2 and 5.3

SOW § 5.2 states: "Buyer will release each tower floor sequence **at least eight weeks** before the required delivery date."

SOW § 5.3 states that IGU-2 (podium storefront) and IGU-4 (mechanical penthouse acoustic zone) have a lead time of **twelve weeks** after Supplier accepts the Purchase Order.

If a floor sequence includes IGU-2 or IGU-4 units, an eight-week release gives Meideis only eight weeks to fulfill a 12-week lead time. The SOW does not provide for a longer release period for these unit types.

**Why it matters:** This creates a structural scheduling gap. If Besouth releases a podium or penthouse sequence on the standard eight-week timeline, Meideis will be contractually unable to meet the delivery date. This could result in:
- Meideis arguing the delivery date was unreasonable (undercutting any late-delivery claim by Besouth);
- Project schedule delays with cascading impacts on the GC and Owner;
- Disputes over whether the delay was Meideis's fault or Besouth's failure to release in time.

**Recommendation:** Amend SOW § 5.2 to require a **14-week** minimum release for any floor sequence containing IGU-2 or IGU-4 units (12-week lead time + 2-week buffer). Alternatively, add a sentence: "For floor sequences containing IGU-2 or IGU-4 units, Buyer will release Purchase Orders at least fourteen weeks before the required delivery date."

**[DECISION NEEDED:** Has the project team confirmed that the current release schedule actually accommodates the 12-week lead times for podium and penthouse glass? If the project is already underway, what is the current status of those releases?]

## Issue 6 – Force Majeure Clause Is Broad and Includes Upstream Glass Supply Failures

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 14

The Force Majeure definition includes "failure of float glass supply from primary manufacturers." This is a significant carve-out because:
- It allows Meideis to excuse delivery delays if its upstream float glass suppliers (e.g., AGC, Saint-Gobain, Guardian) experience production issues, labor disputes, or allocation problems.
- The 90-day termination right (§ 14) means that if a float glass supply disruption lasts more than 90 days, **either** party can terminate the affected SOW. For a project like Calloway Pier Tower, this could strand Besouth with no glass supplier mid-construction.
- There is no obligation on Meideis to source from alternative manufacturers or to maintain safety stock.

**Why it matters:** Architectural glass supply is subject to periodic disruptions (plant outages, trade restrictions, energy crises). If a disruption hits during the Calloway Pier Tower build, Meideis could invoke Force Majeure to excuse delays, and Besouth would have no remedy under § 5.3 (late delivery) because FM is expressly excluded.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a mitigation obligation: if float glass supply from Meideis's primary source fails, Meideis must use reasonable efforts to source from alternative manufacturers within a specified period (e.g., 30 days) before invoking Force Majeure.
- Shorten the 90-day termination trigger or add a requirement that Meideis provide a recovery plan.
- Confirm whether Besouth's subcontract with the GC/Owner has a corresponding FM clause that would protect Besouth from liquidated damages if Meideis invokes FM.

**[INFO NEEDED:** Does Besouth's curtainwall subcontract for Calloway Pier Tower include a Force Majeure clause? If so, does it mirror the supply agreement's FM definition? We need to assess the gap between what Meideis can excuse and what the Owner/GC will excuse to Besouth.]

## Issue 7 – Warranty Remedy Gives Supplier the Option to Repair or Replace

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 9.4

Section 9.4 provides: "For any Product that breaches a warranty, Supplier shall, **at its option**, repair or replace it, and shall reimburse Buyer's reasonable documented costs of removal and reinstallation, up to the amount stated in Section 11.2."

This gives Meideis the right to choose repair over replacement. For a facade project, this can be problematic:
- A seal failure in an installed IGU may not be repairable in the field; the unit must be removed and replaced.
- If Meideis elects "repair" but the repair is impractical or aesthetically unacceptable, Besouth is stuck.
- The reimbursement for removal/reinstallation is capped at the § 11.2 liability cap (total amounts paid and payable under that SOW), which is generous in theory but could be disputed.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate to give **Buyer** the option (repair vs. replace), or at minimum require that Meideis's election of repair is subject to Buyer's reasonable approval. For the Calloway Pier Tower specifically, the 10-year seal warranty (§ 9.2) is the most likely warranty to be invoked, and field repair of a seal failure is often not feasible.

## Issue 8 – Parent Guaranty: Cap May Be Inadequate; Exposure Continues Through Renewal

**Documents:** Guaranty, §§ 3, 4.3(a), 8.1

**Cap adequacy.** The Guarantor's (Besouth Holdings LLC's) maximum liability is **$4,500,000** (Guaranty § 3). This is a fixed cap that does not adjust for:
- The 7% price increase at renewal (Amendment § 7.6);
- Additional SOWs that may be issued during the Renewal Term;
- PPI adjustments under § 7.2.

The Calloway Pier Tower SOW alone involves an estimated 186,000 sq. ft. At current blended prices (roughly $32–$35/sq. ft.), total product value is approximately **$6–$6.5 million**, before freight. If Besouth defaults on payment, the $4.5M cap may not cover all outstanding obligations, leaving the parent exposed to a shortfall that Meideis could pursue against Besouth directly (and potentially through bankruptcy if Besouth is insolvent).

**Continuing exposure.** Guaranty § 4.3(a) provides that the guaranty is **not released** by any renewal, extension, or amendment of the Supply Agreement. Combined with the automatic renewal (Issue 1), Besouth Holdings is locked in for at least 24 additional months (through February 1, 2029) plus the tail of any SOWs then in effect (Guaranty § 8.1).

**Subrogation restriction.** Guaranty § 6 provides that until all Guaranteed Obligations "then due" are paid in full, the Guarantor cannot exercise subrogation, reimbursement, or contribution against the Buyer. This means if Besouth Holdings pays Meideis under the guaranty, it cannot immediately seek reimbursement from Besouth Facade Systems until **all** current obligations are satisfied—not just the ones it paid.

**Recommendation:**
- If the parent is comfortable with the exposure, no action is needed, but the parent should model worst-case payment obligations over the Renewal Term to confirm the $4.5M cap is sufficient.
- If the cap is inadequate, negotiate an increase or a mechanism that adjusts the cap with price increases.
- Negotiate a release or cap reset at the end of the Initial Term (i.e., the guaranty should not automatically extend into the Renewal Term without the parent's affirmative consent).
- Clarify the subrogation restriction: it should apply only to the specific obligations the Guarantor paid, not to all "then due" obligations.

**[DECISION NEEDED:** Is Besouth Holdings (the parent) comfortable with its continuing exposure through the Renewal Term? Does the parent want to renegotiate the cap or the duration?]

## Issue 10 – Exclusive Purchase Obligation Is Very Broad

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 3.1

Section 3.1 requires Buyer to purchase **all** of its requirements for architectural glass products "throughout North America" from Meideis, and extends this obligation to Buyer's **Affiliates**. The covered products include "insulated glass units, laminated glass, heat-strengthened and fully tempered glass, and coated and spandrel glass, whether for curtainwall, storefront, window wall or other facade applications."

**Why it matters:**
- If Besouth or any Affiliate sources glass from another supplier for any project (even a small one), it is in breach of § 3.1.
- The SOW's project-specific exclusivity (§ 2.1, limited to IGUs for Calloway Pier Tower) does not narrow the Agreement's broader obligation; it adds to it.
- A breach of § 3.1 would be a material breach giving Meideis termination rights under § 15.1, and potentially a claim for damages (though consequential damages are excluded under § 11.1).
- The obligation continues through the Renewal Term (see Issue 1).

**Recommendation:**
- Conduct an internal audit to confirm that no Besouth Affiliate is currently sourcing architectural glass from any other supplier.
- If any Affiliate has existing glass supply relationships, either (a) wind them down before the Renewal Term, or (b) negotiate a carve-out in an amendment.
- Ensure that all project teams are aware of the exclusivity obligation so that no one inadvertently places a glass order with another supplier.

**[INFO NEEDED:** Does Besouth have any Affiliates (other than Besouth Facade Systems, Inc.) that currently purchase architectural glass from other suppliers? What is the corporate structure—does Besouth Holdings LLC have other operating subsidiaries?]

## Issue 11 – Late Delivery Remedy Is Weak

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 5.3

If Meideis fails to deliver by the confirmed delivery date (for reasons other than FM or Buyer's act/omission), the **only** remedy is that Meideis must "expedite shipment at its own cost" at Buyer's election. There are:
- No liquidated damages;
- No right to terminate for a single late delivery (short of a pattern constituting material breach under § 15.1);
- No right to source cover from another supplier at Meideis's expense (which would also violate § 3.1 exclusivity).

**Why it matters:** On a construction project, a delay in glass delivery can cause idle labor, crane rebooking, and schedule compression. The cost of a one-week delay on a 31-story tower can be substantial, but Besouth has no contractual remedy beyond Meideis paying for expedited freight.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate a liquidated damages clause (e.g., $X per day of delay per floor sequence, up to a cap).
- Alternatively, add a right to terminate the affected SOW if delivery is more than 30 days late (without constituting a "material breach" requiring the 30-day cure period under § 15.1).
- In the interim, ensure the project schedule has adequate float to absorb minor delivery delays.

**[DECISION NEEDED:** Has Meideis been delivering on time to date? If there have been delays, how were they handled?]

## Issue 12 – Rack Return Obligation and Cost Exposure

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 5.4; Exhibit B (rack replacement costs)

Buyer must return Meideis's racks in good condition within 60 days after delivery. Failure to do so allows Meideis to invoice the replacement cost: **$1,850 per A-frame rack** or **$2,600 per L-frame rack**.

**Why it matters:** On a project of this scale (6,720 units), there will be a significant number of racks in circulation. If racks are lost, damaged, or held beyond 60 days (e.g., because units are in storage pending installation), the replacement costs can accumulate quickly. For example, 50 lost A-frame racks = $92,500.

**Recommendation:**
- Implement a rack tracking system at the unitizing facility (2950 Broening Highway) and the project site.
- Calendar the 60-day return deadline for each delivery.
- If racks will be held longer (e.g., for phased installation), negotiate a rack extension with Meideis before the 60 days expire.

## Issue 13 – Liability Cap Is Per-SOW and May Be Inadequate for Cross-SOW Claims

**Documents:** Supply Agreement, § 11.2

Each party's total liability "under a Statement of Work is limited to the total amounts paid and payable under **that** Statement of Work." This means:
- Meideis's liability for a defect in Calloway Pier Tower glass is capped at the amounts paid/payable under SOW No. 1.
- If a single Meideis defect affects products under multiple SOWs, the caps are separate and Meideis's total exposure is the sum of the individual SOW caps.
- Conversely, if Besouth's breach (e.g., non-payment) affects the relationship broadly, Meideis's recovery from Besouth is also capped per SOW.

**Why it matters:** For the Calloway Pier Tower, the cap should be approximately equal to the total SOW value (~$6–$6.5M at current pricing, more after the 7% increase). This is likely adequate for product claims but may be insufficient if a catastrophic failure (e.g., a glass facade collapse) results in third-party bodily injury claims that exceed the SOW value. However, § 11.1 excludes the cap for § 10 indemnification obligations, so third-party claims are not capped.

**Recommendation:** Confirm that the insurance program (CGL $2M per occurrence / $4M aggregate + $5M umbrella) is adequate to cover a worst-case third-party claim that exceeds the SOW cap. The current insurance appears sufficient for most scenarios, but for a 31-story tower, consider whether the limits should be increased.
