## Issue 1 – Non-Renewal Notice Deadline Is Imminent

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, §2.2 (replacing Agreement §2.2); Agreement §2.1.

**What it says:** The Initial Term runs three years from February 1, 2024, expiring January 31, 2027. The Amendment provides that the Agreement "will automatically renew for successive renewal terms of 24 months each, unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before expiry of the then-current term."

**Why it matters:** Counting back 180 days from January 31, 2027 puts the non-renewal deadline at approximately **August 4, 2026**. The Amendment was signed June 15, 2026, so Besouth had only about 50 days from signing to make the decision. If Besouth wants out of the relationship after the Initial Term, it must deliver written notice under Agreement §18.1 (to Meideis at 2200 Furnace Street, Allentown, PA, Attention: President) by that date. Missing the deadline locks Besouth into a 24-month Renewal Term beginning February 1, 2027, with the 7% price increase described in Issue 2.

**Recommendation:** Confirm with the client immediately whether it intends to renew. If the answer is no, prepare and send the non-renewal notice well before August 4, 2026. If the answer is yes (or undecided), the client should be aware that inaction equals renewal.

**Client decision needed:** Renew or exit?

## Issue 2 – 7% Renewal Price Increase and Conflict with SOW §4.4

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, §3.1 (adding Agreement §7.6); SOW §4.4; Agreement §18.2 (order of precedence).

**What it says:**

- Amendment §3.1 adds §7.6: "With effect from the first day of each Renewal Term, the prices in Exhibit B, **and the prices in each Statement of Work** for Products delivered during that Renewal Term, increase by 7% over the prices in effect on the last day of the preceding term."
- SOW §4.4: "Prices under this SOW are subject to adjustment **only as provided in Section 7.2 of the Agreement**."
- Agreement §18.2 (order of precedence): "Amendments, then the Supply Agreement, then any Statement of Work."

**Why it matters:** The Amendment's 7% increase explicitly applies to "each Statement of Work," which includes the Calloway Pier Tower SOW. The SOW's "only Section 7.2" language was drafted before the Amendment existed and does not anticipate §7.6. Under the order of precedence, the Amendment controls, so the 7% increase almost certainly applies. However, the SOW's specific "only" language gives Besouth a textual argument that the parties intended to limit SOW price adjustments to the PPI mechanism in §7.2. This is not a strong argument given the Amendment's express reference to SOWs and the precedence rule, but it is not frivolous.

Financial impact: The estimated SOW glass value is approximately $5.72 million (calculated from SOW §§3.2 and 4.1). A 7% increase on undelivered units entering the Renewal Term could add roughly **$400,000 or more** to the project, depending on how much glass remains undelivered after January 31, 2027. The increase also stacks on top of any PPI adjustment under §7.2 (Amendment §3.1, second sentence).

**Recommendation:**

- If Besouth renews, model the 7% increase into the Calloway Pier Tower project budget for all glass not yet delivered as of February 1, 2027.
- If Besouth believes the SOW §4.4 "only" language should limit adjustments to PPI, raise the point with Meideis before the Renewal Term begins. Do not rely on this argument without a discussion—Meideis will almost certainly cite the Amendment's express language and the order of precedence.
- Consider whether the 7% increase is negotiable as a condition of renewal.

**Client decision needed:** Is the 7% increase acceptable, or should it be a bargaining chip in the renewal discussion?

## Issue 3 – Pellworth Capital Partners: Role Unknown; Change of Control Risk

**Where:** Agreement §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), §§16.2–16.3; Guaranty §8.2. No document in the folder mentions Pellworth.

**What it says:**

- "Change of Control" includes any transaction in which a person or group acquires more than 50% of the voting equity of Buyer **or of any direct or indirect parent of Buyer** (Agreement §1.1).
- A Change of Control of Buyer "is deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Buyer and requires Supplier's prior written consent" (Agreement §16.2). Buyer must give 30 days' advance notice of any proposed Change of Control.
- If a Change of Control occurs without consent, Meideis may terminate the Agreement and any SOWs on 30 days' notice, exercisable within 12 months after Meideis learns of the transaction (Agreement §16.3).
- The Guaranty is not released by a change of control of the Guarantor (Besouth Holdings) (Guaranty §8.2).

**Why it matters:** You identified Pellworth Capital Partners as a party involved, but it does not appear in any of the five documents. If Pellworth is planning to acquire a majority stake in Besouth Holdings LLC (the parent of Besouth Facade Systems), that transaction would constitute a Change of Control of Buyer under the Agreement. Meideis could refuse consent and terminate the supply relationship, which would be catastrophic for the Calloway Pier Tower project mid-construction. Even if Meideis consents, the 30-day advance notice requirement means Besouth must engage Meideis before closing.

**Recommendation:**

- **Immediate follow-up with client:** What is Pellworth's role? Is it a prospective acquirer of Besouth Holdings, a lender, a project investor, or something else? We cannot assess the Change of Control risk without knowing.
- If Pellworth is a potential acquirer of Besouth Holdings, begin a dialogue with Meideis well in advance of any transaction to secure consent under §16.2. Factor the 30-day notice period into any deal timeline.
- Note that the Guaranty survives a change of control of the Guarantor (Guaranty §8.2), so the parent guaranty would remain in effect even after a Pellworth acquisition—this is relevant to Pellworth's underwriting.

**Client decision needed:** Clarify Pellworth's role and whether a parent-level transaction is contemplated.

## Issue 4 – Parent Guaranty: Subrogation Waiver Is One-Way and Potentially Costly

**Where:** Guaranty §6.

**What it says:** "Until the Guaranteed Obligations then due have been paid in full, the Guarantor shall not exercise any right of subrogation, reimbursement or contribution against the Buyer arising from payments under this Guaranty."

**Why it matters:** If Besouth Holdings pays Meideis under the Guaranty (because Besouth Facade Systems failed to pay invoices), the parent **cannot** seek reimbursement from its subsidiary until *all* guaranteed obligations then due are paid in full. In practice, this means the parent is making a permanent capital contribution to the subsidiary for any amount it pays under the Guaranty, at least until the entire payment stack is cleared. For a $4.5 million cap, this is a material exposure for Besouth Holdings.

**Recommendation:**

- Flag to Besouth Holdings' management (and to Pellworth, if it is a prospective investor or acquirer) that the subrogation waiver effectively converts any guaranty payment into equity.
- If Besouth is negotiating the renewal or any new SOWs, consider whether the subrogation waiver can be limited (e.g., to a specific period or to the extent of the particular obligation paid).
- Ensure internal accounting treats any guaranty payment as a capital contribution, not a receivable from the subsidiary.

## Issue 5 – Parent Guaranty: $4.5 Million Cap May Be Inadequate for the Calloway Pier Tower Project Alone

**Where:** Guaranty §3; SOW §§2.2, 3.2, 4.1; Amendment §3.1.

**What it says:** The Guarantor's total aggregate liability is capped at $4,500,000 (plus enforcement costs under §10.2). The cap is reduced by Guarantor payments but not by Buyer payments (Guaranty §3).

**Why it matters:** The estimated glass value for Calloway Pier Tower is approximately $5.72 million (SOW §§3.2, 4.1). If the project extends into the Renewal Term with the 7% increase (Issue 2), the total could exceed $6 million. The $4.5 million cap does not cover the full project value. This creates two risks:

1. **Meideis may demand a higher cap or additional security** before extending trade credit for the Renewal Term, which could disrupt the supply relationship.
2. **If Besouth Facade Systems defaults**, Meideis's recovery from the parent is limited to $4.5 million, leaving a gap. Meideis may then look to terminate under Agreement §15.1 (material breach) or §15.2 (insolvency), which would trigger the work-in-process payment obligation under §15.3 and potentially leave the Calloway Pier Tower project without a glass supplier mid-construction.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client whether Meideis has raised the cap adequacy as a condition of the Renewal Term. If not, anticipate that it may.
- If Besouth wants to keep the cap at $4.5 million, be prepared to explain to Meideis how the gap will be covered (e.g., project-specific security, letter of credit, or the Owner's payment flow).
- Note that the cap covers *all* SOWs and Purchase Orders, not just Calloway Pier Tower. If Besouth plans additional projects during the Renewal Term, the cap will be consumed faster.

**Client decision needed:** Is the $4.5 million cap sufficient given the project pipeline, or should Besouth be prepared to negotiate a higher cap?

## Issue 6 – Broad Exclusivity Obligation Extends to All Affiliates and All of North America

**Where:** Agreement §3.1.

**What it says:** "During the Term, Buyer shall, **and shall cause its Affiliates to**, purchase from Supplier **all of its requirements for architectural glass products throughout North America**, including insulated glass units, laminated glass, heat-strengthened and fully tempered glass, and coated and spandrel glass, whether for curtainwall, storefront, window wall or other facade applications. Buyer and its Affiliates shall not purchase these products from any other source during the Term."

**Why it matters:** This is an exceptionally broad exclusivity. It covers:

- All Affiliates (defined in §1.1 as any entity under common control, including Besouth Holdings and any sister companies).
- All product types (not just IGUs).
- All of North America (not just projects where Besouth is the curtainwall subcontractor).
- The entire Term (which, with auto-renewal, could extend indefinitely in 24-month increments).

If Besouth has other subsidiaries or affiliates that source glass for their own projects, they are all locked into Meideis. If Besouth wants to use a different glass supplier for a non-Calloway project, it would be in breach of §3.1. The SOW's narrower exclusivity (limited to IGUs for the Calloway Pier Tower project, SOW §2.1) does not override this—the master Agreement prevails over the SOW under §18.2.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client whether Besouth has other Affiliates with glass requirements. If so, ensure those entities are aware of the exclusivity obligation.
- If the breadth of §3.1 is a commercial concern, raise it in the renewal negotiation. A reasonable ask might be to limit exclusivity to projects where Besouth is the curtainwall or facade subcontractor, or to carve out specific product categories or geographies.
- Note that the exclusivity runs for the entire Term, including any Renewal Terms. If Besouth renews, it is committing to this breadth for another 24 months.

## Issue 7 – SOW §4.4 "Only Section 7.2" Language Creates Ambiguity

**Where:** SOW §4.4; Amendment §3.1 (new Agreement §7.6); Agreement §18.2.

**What it says:** SOW §4.4 limits price adjustments to "Section 7.2 of the Agreement" only. The Amendment adds §7.6, which explicitly applies to "each Statement of Work." The order of precedence (§18.2) gives the Amendment priority.

**Why it matters:** This is the same conflict identified in Issue 2, but I separate it here because it is a drafting issue that could be cleaned up. The SOW was signed in March 2024; the Amendment was signed in June 2026. The SOW's drafter could not have anticipated §7.6. The Amendment's drafter knew about the SOW (it is referenced in Amendment Recital A and §4.1) and chose to apply the 7% increase to "each Statement of Work" notwithstanding the SOW's "only" language. This suggests the Amendment's position is the intended one.

**Recommendation:** If Besouth renews, consider executing a clarifying amendment to SOW §4.4 that either (a) expressly incorporates §7.6, eliminating any ambiguity, or (b) carves out the Calloway Pier Tower SOW from the 7% increase if that is the commercial deal. Do not leave the conflict unresolved if the project extends into the Renewal Term.

## Issue 9 – Oversize and Custom Unit Pricing Is Open

**Where:** SOW §4.2; Agreement Exhibit B (last paragraph).

**What it says:**

- SOW §4.2: "Units over 50 sq. ft. carry a surcharge of $4.10 per sq. ft. on the whole unit area. Units over 85 sq. ft. or over 144 inches on either dimension will be priced by written quote before Buyer issues the Purchase Order."
- Agreement Exhibit B: "Base prices apply to units up to 50 sq. ft. Oversize, shaped and custom-coated units are priced in the applicable Statement of Work."

**Why it matters:** For a 31-story tower, it is likely that some units will exceed 50 sq. ft. (the surcharge threshold) and some may exceed 85 sq. ft. or 144 inches (the quote threshold). The SOW does not estimate how many units will fall into these categories or what the surcharge will add to the total project cost. The $4.10/sq. ft. surcharge applies to the *whole unit area*, not just the excess over 50 sq. ft., so a 60 sq. ft. unit pays the surcharge on all 60 sq. ft. ($246 per unit). This is a significant and unquantified cost driver.

**Recommendation:**

- Work with the design team to identify how many units in the glass schedule will exceed 50 sq. ft. and how many will exceed 85 sq. ft. or 144 inches.
- For units requiring a written quote (over 85 sq. ft. or 144 inches), build time into the release schedule to obtain quotes before issuing Purchase Orders.
- Factor the surcharge into the project budget. If a significant number of units are oversize, this could add tens of thousands of dollars.

**What it says:** "Buyer shall return Supplier's racks in good condition within 60 days after delivery, failing which Supplier may invoice Buyer the replacement cost stated in Exhibit B." Replacement costs: $1,850 per A-frame rack; $2,600 per L-frame rack.

## Issue 12 – Insurance Policy Period Does Not Align with Agreement Term

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter, §2.1; Agreement §2.1, §13.2.

**What it says:** The renewed policies run October 1, 2026 to October 1, 2027. The Agreement's Initial Term runs February 1, 2024 to January 31, 2027. The first Renewal Term (if it occurs) would run February 1, 2027 to January 31, 2029.

**Why it matters:** This is not a compliance issue—Agreement §13.2 requires certificates "on each policy renewal," and Meideis has delivered them. However, the misalignment means that during the first Renewal Term, there will be a policy renewal on October 1, 2027, and Besouth should expect another certificate delivery at that time. If Besouth is tracking insurance compliance for the project, it should calendar both the October 2027 renewal and any subsequent renewals.

**Recommendation:** Calendar the next expected insurance certificate delivery (October 2027) and verify that coverage limits and additional insured status remain compliant when the new certificates arrive.
