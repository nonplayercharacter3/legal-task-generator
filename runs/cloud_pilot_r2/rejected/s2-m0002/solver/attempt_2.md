## 1. Bottom Line

All three transaction drafts are labeled "Provider Draft for Discussion" (MSA header; SOW header; DPA header). They depart from the signed Term Sheet in ways that favor Bellweather. The Term Sheet is non-binding except for confidentiality, costs and governing law (Term Sheet §§ 12.1–12.2), so Harrowgate cannot enforce those departures away. It is, however, strong negotiating leverage and should be our reference point on every deviation.

The five most serious problems are:

1. **Exclusivity** (MSA § 3.2): nationwide and covering all warehousing, freight and delivery, against a Term Sheet deal limited to NYC-metro last-mile delivery (Term Sheet §§ 4.1–4.2).
2. **Liability architecture** (MSA §§ 10.2, 11): Harrowgate's indemnity is broad and uncapped, while Bellweather's exposure, including for lost goods and data breaches, sits under a cap of roughly $2.2M with no carve-outs.
3. **Lock-in** (MSA §§ 14.1–14.2): an auto-renewal of three years, with a 15-day non-renewal window that is easy to miss and no termination for convenience. It is coupled with the exclusivity.
4. **DPA governed by Delaware law, with Delaware courts, and ranked above the MSA** (DPA § 9.1; MSA § 2.3), contrary to the Term Sheet (§ 11) and MSA § 18.1.
5. **Asymmetric assignment** (MSA § 17.4; SOW § 8.2). Harrowgate is locked in, including on a change of control, while Bellweather may assign freely.

The insurance package also needs work before Harrowgate relies on it (Section 3, Issue 7).

## 3. Detailed Issues

### Issue 1: Exclusivity far exceeds the deal in the Term Sheet (Critical)

**Where:** MSA § 3.2; Term Sheet §§ 4.1–4.2; SOW §§ 1.3, 4.1, 9.3; MSA § 16.1(c).

**What the documents say.**
- MSA § 3.2 makes Bellweather Harrowgate's "exclusive provider of all warehousing, freight transportation and delivery services required by Customer and its Affiliates throughout the United States."
- It bars Harrowgate and its Affiliates from using any third party "or perform[ing] itself" any such services.
- Term Sheet § 4.1 limits exclusivity to "last-mile home delivery within the New York City metropolitan area only."
- Term Sheet § 4.2 says "[n]o other services are subject to exclusivity" and that Customer remains free to use others or self-perform.

**Why it matters.**
- *Wrong scope.* The exclusivity covers services that are not in SOW No. 1. The SOW covers one Secaucus facility and a NY/NJ Service Area (SOW §§ 3.1, 4.1), and it expressly excludes line-haul (SOW § 1.3).
- *No reciprocal obligation.* MSA § 2.1 obliges Bellweather to perform only services described in an executed SOW. Harrowgate would therefore be barred from using anyone else for services Bellweather has no obligation to provide, at no agreed rates or service levels.
- *Immediate breach risk.*
  - SOW § 9.3 contemplates that Harrowgate arranges its own inbound freight, which is "freight transportation" under MSA § 3.2.
  - Harrowgate's own store deliveries, customer pickups, other distribution centers and vendors' inbound freight would likewise be caught.
- *Affiliates.* "Affiliate" (MSA § 1.1) includes controlling entities and sister companies. Harrowgate cannot "cause" a parent or sibling to comply (MSA § 3.2).
- *Warranty conflict.* Harrowgate warrants that signing and performing does not conflict with any other agreement (MSA § 16.1(c)). Existing logistics contracts could make that warranty untrue on day one.
- *Duration.* Exclusivity runs for the whole Term (MSA § 3.2), which can extend through renewals (Issue 4).
- *Stranded exclusivity.* If Harrowgate terminates SOW No. 1 for cause but the MSA continues (MSA § 14.3), the exclusivity still binds (Issue 11).
- *Geographic ambiguity.* The Term Sheet's "NYC metropolitan area" is undefined. SOW § 4.1 defines a specific Service Area of 5 boroughs and 8 counties.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 3.2):*
> "3.2 Exclusivity. During the Term, Customer shall use Provider as its exclusive provider of last-mile home delivery services to end customers located within the Service Area defined in Section 4.1 of Statement of Work No. 1. Nothing in this Agreement restricts Customer or its Affiliates from engaging any other provider, or performing itself, any other warehousing, freight transportation, line-haul or delivery services, including inbound freight, store replenishment, customer pick-up, and deliveries outside the Service Area. Customer's obligation under this Section 3.2 is suspended (a) for any month in which Provider fails to meet the on-time delivery service level in SOW No. 1 and for the following month, (b) during any force majeure event affecting Provider, and (c) as to any volume or delivery that Provider is unable or declines to accept."

*Request to Bellweather:* Point to Term Sheet §§ 4.1–4.2 and ask Bellweather to explain the departure. Delete the "Affiliates" language from the exclusivity clause.

*Client question:* See Section 5, items 1 and 2.

### Issue 2: Customer's uncapped indemnity is overbroad and conflicts with Provider's indemnity (Critical)

**Where:** MSA §§ 10.1, 10.2, 11.3, 10.3; Term Sheet § 8.2.

**What the documents say.**
- *Provider's indemnity* (MSA § 10.1) covers only third-party claims "to the extent arising from" Provider's negligence or willful misconduct causing bodily injury or property damage, Provider's violation of law, and its personnel's wage claims.
- *Customer's indemnity* (MSA § 10.2) covers any third-party claim "arising out of or relating to" (a) Customer Goods, (b) "any Customer premises or end-customer premises at which Provider performs Services," and (c) "Customer's use of the Services."
- *Cap carve-out* (MSA § 11.3): the § 10.2 indemnity "shall apply without limitation."
- *Term Sheet* § 8.2 says indemnities are "[t]o be negotiated."

**Why it matters.**
- Clause (b) covers every home-delivery claim, because Provider performs the Services at end-customer premises. A Bellweather crew's negligence in a customer's home could produce an indemnity claim against Harrowgate that is uncapped.
- That claim would sit alongside, and arguably override, Bellweather's own § 10.1(a) duty for the same event.
- Clause (c) is open-ended, since all Services are Harrowgate's "use of the Services."
- Clause (a) lacks a carve-out for damage Bellweather causes through its handling, for example a product-liability claim after Bellweather mis-assembles an item.
- There is no "except to the extent caused by" exception for Bellweather's fault. Because the clause is drafted as "arising out of or relating to," an argument that it reaches the indemnitee's own negligence is likely. Please confirm how NY courts treat such language [verify].
- Provider's indemnity does not cover data or confidentiality breaches, or claims by Bellweather's subcontractors' employees beyond wage claims.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 10.2):*
> "10.2 By Customer. Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Provider … from third-party claims to the extent arising from (a) a claim that Customer Goods, as designed, manufactured and supplied by or for Customer and not as a result of Provider's handling, storage, assembly or delivery, are defective or infringe a third party's intellectual property rights, in each case excluding any claim to the extent caused by the negligence, willful misconduct or breach of this Agreement by Provider, its personnel or subcontractors." *(Delete clauses (b) and (c).)*

*Redline (MSA § 10.1):* Add:
> "(d) any Security Incident or breach of Section 8 or Section 9 or the DPA by Provider or its Subprocessors; and (e) any claim arising from the acts or omissions of Provider's personnel or subcontractors while on end-customer premises."

*MSA § 11.3:* Make § 10.2 subject to the § 11.1 cap, or at a minimum make the caps mutual (see Issue 3). The Term Sheet contemplated a mutual cap (TS § 8.1), and the draft does not deliver one.

*Procedure (MSA § 10.3(b)):* Add that Harrowgate may take over the defense of any claim that implicates its brand, regulators or end customers, or that seeks non-monetary relief, at the indemnitor's cost.

### Issue 3: The liability cap and exclusions leave Harrowgate underprotected (Critical)

**Where:** MSA §§ 11.1–11.3, 6.3; SOW § 6.3; DPA § 5.5; Term Sheet § 8.1; MSA § 12.

**What the documents say.**
- *Cap* (MSA § 11.1): each Party's total liability is limited to "the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim."
- *Exceptions* (MSA § 11.3):
  - They are limited to Customer's § 10.2 indemnity, fraud, gross negligence and willful misconduct, and Customer's fee obligations.
  - No exception is provided for Bellweather's indemnity, loss of or damage to Customer Goods, confidentiality or data breaches.
- *Excluded damages* (MSA § 11.2): indirect and consequential damages and lost profits or revenue are excluded.
- *Term Sheet* § 8.1: a mutual cap, "subject to customary exceptions to be agreed."

**Why it matters.**
- *Size of the cap.* At the fixed $184,500 monthly fee (SOW § 5.1), 12 months of fees is about $2,214,000 (12 × $184,500), plus any excess-volume and pass-through charges. In the first months of the Term, "paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim" is ambiguous and may be far lower.
- *Goods.* Bellweather is responsible for loss and damage at landed cost (MSA § 6.3; SOW § 6.3), yet that liability is within the cap. SOW § 3.2 provides for at least 180,000 sq ft of Harrowgate inventory. Bellweather's own warehouse legal liability coverage is $5,000,000 per occurrence (MSA § 12.1(c)). Harrowgate could therefore be limited to a recovery well below both the value of the goods and the insurance Bellweather carries.
- *Data.*
  - Bellweather's breach-cost reimbursement (DPA § 5.5) is capped by the MSA, because the DPA has no liability section of its own.
  - It is arguably also hit by the consequential-damages exclusion. Notification, call-center and credit-monitoring costs are in doubt.
  - Required cyber coverage is $3,000,000 (MSA § 12.1(f)), above the cap.
- *Asymmetry.* Only Harrowgate's indemnity is uncapped (MSA § 11.3).
- *Lost profits.* Failed or late deliveries to customers produce lost margin, which § 11.2 excludes. Landed-cost reimbursement (MSA § 6.3) also excludes retail margin.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 11.1):*
> "…shall not exceed the greater of (a) the Fees paid and payable for the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim (or, if fewer than twelve months have elapsed, the Fees that would be payable for the first twelve months), and (b) $[__]."

*Redline (MSA § 11.3):* Add as exceptions to §§ 11.1 and 11.2:
> "(i) Provider's obligations under Section 10.1; (ii) Provider's liability for loss of or damage to Customer Goods under Section 6.3 [alternatively: subject to a separate cap not less than the greater of the replacement cost of the affected Customer Goods and the limits of insurance required by Section 12.1(c) and (d)]; (iii) breach of Section 8 (Confidentiality); (iv) breach of Section 9 or the DPA, subject to a separate cap of the greater of $[__] and [three] times the Fees paid and payable in the preceding twelve months [alternatively: unlimited]; and (v) any amount recovered or recoverable under insurance Provider is required to maintain."

*Redline (MSA § 11.2):* Add:
> "The following are direct damages and are not excluded: costs of cover (reasonable costs of procuring substitute services); costs of re-delivery or re-processing; Security Incident costs under DPA § 5.5 (investigation, notification, call center, credit monitoring, regulatory fines and penalties); and the replacement cost of lost or damaged Customer Goods."

*Request:* Ask that the loss-and-damage measure in MSA § 6.3 and SOW § 6.3 be "the greater of landed cost and Customer's documented replacement cost," or at least that goods already sold to end customers be reimbursed at the sale price (a commercial point for the client).

### Issue 4: Three-year auto-renewal, a narrow notice window, and no exit rights (Critical)

**Where:** MSA §§ 14.1–14.5, 17.1; Term Sheet §§ 5, 10.1–10.2; MSA § 3.2.

**What the documents say.**
- *Initial term* (MSA § 14.1): two years "from the MSA Effective Date." That phrase is not a defined term, since the defined term is "Effective Date."
- *Term Sheet* §§ 5 and 10.2: initial term of two years, with renewal "to be negotiated."
- *Renewal* (MSA § 14.2): automatic renewal for successive three-year terms, unless a party gives notice "during the fifteen (15) day period ending one hundred twenty (120) days before the expiry." Notice at any other time "shall be of no effect."
- *Notice mechanics* (MSA § 17.1): notices must be by hand, overnight courier or certified mail, with an email copy, and are effective on receipt.
- *Exit rights:* termination is for uncured material breach (30 days, § 14.3) or insolvency (§ 14.4). There is no right to terminate for convenience, for chronic service-level failure or on a Provider change of control.

**Why it matters.**
- With a January 1, 2027 effective date, the initial term ends about January 1, 2029 (the exact date is ambiguous, because "2 years from" could mean December 31, 2028 or January 1, 2029). The non-renewal window would be roughly August 20 to September 3, 2028.
- Notice must be *received* inside that 15-day window by a prescribed method, and any other notice is void.
- A missed window means another 36 months at the fixed fee, which is $6,642,000 (36 × $184,500). Exclusivity would continue throughout.
- The three-year renewal length appears nowhere in the Term Sheet. The draft therefore claims more than the Term Sheet records as agreed.
- SOW service credits are modest, at most 10% of the monthly fee, or $18,450 (SOW § 6.2). Without a chronic-failure termination right, Harrowgate has no real remedy for persistently poor performance short of litigating "material breach."

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA §§ 14.1–14.2):*
> "14.1 … an initial term of two (2) years from the Effective Date…"
> "14.2 … renewal terms of one (1) year each, unless either Party delivers written notice of non-renewal at least sixty (60) days before the expiry of the then-current term. Provider shall send Customer a written reminder of the non-renewal deadline between 150 and 120 days before expiry; if Provider fails to do so, Customer may give notice of non-renewal at any time before expiry and the then-current term shall expire on the date specified in that notice, not earlier than 60 days after delivery."

*Add termination rights (MSA § 14):*
> "14.6 Customer may terminate this Agreement or any Statement of Work (a) for convenience on ninety (90) days' written notice [Customer's fallback: after the first twelve months]; (b) on written notice if Provider fails to meet any two service levels in any three consecutive months or the same service level in any three months in a twelve-month period; (c) on written notice if Provider fails to maintain the insurance required by Section 12, or ceases to hold required operating authority (Section 16.2); or (d) on written notice upon a Provider change of control or assignment."

*Fix "MSA Effective Date"* in § 14.1 to "Effective Date." Add email as an effective notice method in § 17.1 for operational notices.

### Issue 5: DPA is governed by Delaware law and Delaware courts, and ranks above the MSA (High)

**Where:** DPA § 9.1; MSA §§ 2.3, 18.1, 18.3, 18.4; Term Sheet § 11.

**What the documents say.**
- *DPA* § 9.1: Delaware law governs "any dispute relating to the Processing of Personal Data or any Security Incident," with exclusive jurisdiction in the state and federal courts in Wilmington, Delaware.
- *MSA* § 18.1 and *Term Sheet* § 11: New York law and the courts in New York County, for "[t]he Definitive Agreements."
- *Order of precedence* (MSA § 2.3): the DPA first, then the MSA, then the SOW.

**Why it matters.**
- The DPA's Delaware clause conflicts with the Term Sheet and with the MSA. Because the DPA ranks first (MSA § 2.3), the Delaware clause would override the MSA for data disputes.
- A single Security Incident could generate parallel proceedings. These would include a New York case under the MSA for the commercial and goods claims and a Delaware case under the DPA for data claims, with different governing law.
- The MSA's own provisions for data matters would be split in the same way: the injunctive-relief provision for Sections 8 and 9 (MSA § 18.3) and the jury waiver (§ 18.4, which is drafted for proceedings "relating to this Agreement").
- Harrowgate's regulators, customers and witnesses are in New York, and the DPA's own security standard is a New York statute (DPA § 4.1, N.Y. GBL § 899-bb).
- A top-ranking DPA will also override MSA provisions in other ways, for example the data-return timing (Issue 8).

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (DPA § 9.1):* Replace in its entirety:
> "9.1 Governing Law and Forum. Section 18 (Governing Law and Dispute Resolution) of the Agreement applies to this DPA and to any dispute relating to the Processing of Personal Data or any Security Incident."

*Redline (MSA § 2.3):* Limit the DPA's priority:
> "…the Data Processing Addendum shall prevail over this Agreement and any Statement of Work solely as to the Processing of Personal Data and Security Incident response, provided that Sections 11 and 18 of this Agreement shall govern all questions of liability and governing law and forum."

*Request:* Ask Bellweather why Delaware was chosen and confirm that there is no operational reason for it. Cite Term Sheet § 11.

### Issue 6: One-way assignment; Provider may assign without consent (High)

**Where:** MSA § 17.4; SOW § 8.2; MSA § 7.2; SOW § 8.1; DPA § 6.1; Term Sheet § 10.2.

**What the documents say.**
- *Customer* (MSA § 17.4): Harrowgate may not assign, delegate or transfer, "by merger or change of control or otherwise," without Bellweather's prior written consent, and any violation is void.
- *Provider* (SOW § 8.2): Bellweather may assign the SOW "and any of its rights and obligations under this SOW and under the Agreement, in whole or in part, to any third party, without Customer's consent," with notice within 30 days after the assignment is effective.
- The MSA is silent on Provider assignment. Because it is silent, there is no conflict for the order of precedence (MSA § 2.3) to resolve, and SOW § 8.2 would operate as written.
- *Term Sheet* § 10.2: assignment terms "to be negotiated."

**Why it matters.**
- *Harrowgate's constraint.* Harrowgate would need Bellweather's consent for any corporate reorganization, sale or merger (even a change of control), financing, or internal affiliate transfer. This gives Bellweather a hold-up right at any deal.
- *Bellweather's freedom.* Bellweather could pass the account to a competitor of Harrowgate, an undercapitalized entity, or an entity without the required licenses or insurance. Harrowgate would hear after the fact.
- *Control leakage.* SOW § 8.2 conflicts with the consent protections for subcontractors (MSA § 7.2; SOW § 8.1) and Subprocessors (DPA § 6.1). An assignee would obtain access to the Facility, the goods and the personal data without any of those consents.
- *Notice.* The 30-day post-assignment notice is no protection.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 17.4):* Replace with a mutual clause.
> "Neither Party may assign, delegate or transfer this Agreement or any Statement of Work without the other Party's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed), except that Customer may, on written notice, assign to an Affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business or assets to which this Agreement relates, whether by merger, change of control, sale of assets or otherwise. Any assignee of Provider must hold all required licenses and insurance and be bound by the DPA. A change of control of Provider is deemed an assignment."

*Redline (SOW § 8.2):* Replace with: "Assignment is governed exclusively by Section 17.4 of the Agreement."

### Issue 7: Insurance: protections are not yet in place, and adequacy is untested (High)

**Where:** MSA §§ 12.1–12.2; Quillmore letter §§ 2.1–2.2, 3.1–3.2, 4.1–4.2, 5.1; SOW Schedule A; Term Sheet § 8.3.

**What the documents say.**
- *Requirements* (MSA § 12.1): CGL $2M/$4M; auto $5M CSL; warehouse legal liability $5M per occurrence; motor truck cargo $250,000 per vehicle; employer's liability $1M; cyber $3M per claim.
- *Additional insured and certificates* (MSA § 12.2): Harrowgate is to be named additional insured on the CGL and auto policies only. Certificates are due before the first SOW's start date and on each renewal. Cancellation notice is 30 days.
- *Broker's letter:*
  - Every limit it reports exactly equals the MSA minimum (letter § 2.1; compare MSA § 12.1).
  - It states that the insurers "have confirmed that the endorsements **will be issued** … on execution of the Agreement" (§ 3.1). The Certificate nonetheless "names" Harrowgate as additional insured on the CGL and auto policies.
  - An updated certificate is promised by January 11, 2027, per SOW Schedule A (§ 4.1).
  - The policies expire July 1, 2027, six months into the Term (§ 4.2).
  - The letter says that the Certificate is "information only" and does not "amend, extend or alter" coverage (§ 5.1).
  - It says that copies of the policies and endorsements are available "on request through Bellweather" (§ 5.1).
- *Letter formalities.* The signature line on the copy in the folder is blank.

**Why it matters.**
- *Additional-insured status.*
  - It does not exist today. A certificate is not an endorsement, and by the letter's own terms it creates no rights (§ 5.1). The endorsements are only promised, "on execution."
  - The MSA does not require delivery of the endorsements, only certificates (MSA § 12.2).
- *Gaps in the requirements.*
  - No primary and non-contributory wording.
  - No waiver of subrogation.
  - No loss-payee status for Harrowgate on the warehouse legal liability and cargo policies, the two policies that protect Harrowgate's own goods.
  - No umbrella or excess requirement.
  - No crime or employee-dishonesty cover, although crews enter customers' homes (MSA § 7.1).
- *Adequacy.* The limits equal the minimums, so they look sized to Bellweather's existing program rather than to Harrowgate's exposure.
  - $250,000 per vehicle for cargo may be low for a loaded furniture truck.
  - The $5,000,000 warehouse limit is stated per occurrence with no aggregate or sublimits shown. It appears to be shared with Bellweather's other customers' goods at the same Facility, so one fire could exhaust it.
  - The cyber policy is claims-made (letter § 2.1). We do not know its retroactive date, and MSA § 12.1 requires coverage for one year after the Term.
- *Renewal.* The first renewal falls on July 1, 2027. The renewal-certificate promise is the broker's, who is not a party to the MSA.
- *Missing exhibit.* I could not review the Certificate itself.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 12.2):* Add:
> "Provider shall deliver to Customer, before the Start Date of the first Statement of Work, copies of the endorsements naming Customer and its Affiliates as additional insureds on the policies in Sections 12.1(a) and (b) on a primary and non-contributory basis, with waiver of subrogation, and naming Customer as loss payee as its interests may appear on the policies in Sections 12.1(c) and (d). Customer is not required to tender Customer Goods, and shall have no liability for any resulting delay, until such endorsements are delivered. Provider's failure to maintain required insurance is a material breach not capable of cure."

Also:
- Require umbrella/excess coverage and crime coverage.
- State the cyber retroactive date and extended reporting period.
- Provide that failure to maintain required insurance is a material breach not capable of cure (as above).

*Requests to Quillmore and Bellweather (via Bellweather):*
- The enclosed Certificate.
- Endorsement copies.
- Warehouse legal liability sublimits, deductibles and aggregate, and whether the policy limit is shared across Bellweather's customers.
- Cyber retroactive date.
- Whether any Bellweather affiliate shares limits.
- Confirmation that the policy provides cover for Bellweather's subcontracted drivers, if any.

### Issue 8: Data-breach terms: timing, cost recovery, return vs. transition, blank Annex 3 (High)

**Where:** DPA §§ 5.1, 5.5, 6.1, 8.1, 3.3, Annexes 1 and 3; MSA §§ 9.3, 15.1(b), 15.2; SOW § 4.5; Term Sheet § 9.

**What the documents say.**
- *Notice* (DPA § 5.1): notice of a Security Incident "within forty-eight (48) hours" after becoming aware. The Term Sheet (§ 9) said "promptly."
- *Costs* (DPA § 5.5): Bellweather reimburses Harrowgate's "reasonable costs" for an incident "caused by a breach of this DPA by Provider or its Subprocessors." These include notification, call-center and credit-monitoring costs.
- *Return* (DPA § 8.1): Customer Data is returned within 30 days after termination or expiration. MSA § 15.2 gives Harrowgate up to 90 days of transition assistance after termination or expiration, at its request.
- *Subprocessors* (DPA § 6.1, Annex 3): the Annex 3 list is blank ("[To be completed by Provider and approved by Customer before the Start Date]").
- *Sensitive data* (Annex 1): "None. Customer will not provide payment card data, government identifiers or health information."

**Why it matters.**
- *Timing.*
  - The 48-hour period begins when Bellweather "becom[es] aware." As a service provider holding data it does not own, Bellweather may have a statutory duty under N.Y. GBL § 899-aa to notify the data owner more promptly. The DPA itself cites § 899-aa (DPA § 1.1) [verify the current text and any notice deadlines].
  - Harrowgate's own notification clock may start earlier than the 48-hour mark.
- *Breach causation.* "Caused by a breach of this DPA" narrows recovery. If the incident arises in Bellweather's systems but no DPA clause was demonstrably breached, recovery is contestable. Regulatory fines and penalties are not named.
- *Cap interplay.* See Issue 3.
- *Return vs. transition.* Because the DPA outranks the MSA (MSA § 2.3), the 30-day return could force the data back and into deletion (DPA § 8.2) while Bellweather is still performing transition services during the 90-day period. Bellweather would then lack the data it needs to serve Harrowgate.
- *Portal license.* The Provider portal license ends with the Term (MSA § 13.2), yet proof of delivery is delivered through that portal (SOW § 4.5). Access during transition is unprotected.
- *Annex 3.* Making the subprocessor list a post-signing item defeats the consent right in DPA § 6.1, which requires consent before engagement. Together with SOW § 8.2 (Issue 6), subprocessing could be set up without scrutiny.
- *Annex 1.* The "Customer will not provide" language reads as a covenant. Proof-of-delivery photographs and signatures taken inside homes (SOW § 4.5; DPA § 1.2) may also capture images of occupants or interiors. Please confirm with the client's privacy lead whether that data needs to be addressed.

**Recommendation.**

### Issue 9: Go-live risk: fees run from a fixed date, and Schedule A is only a target (High)

**Where:** SOW §§ 2.1, 2.2, 5.4, Schedule A.

**What the documents say.**
- The Start Date is a fixed date: January 15, 2027 (SOW § 2.1).
- The January fee is pro-rated "from the Start Date" (SOW § 5.4).
- Schedule A milestones are labeled "Target Completion." They run from kick-off on January 4 to go-live on January 15, with the initial inventory transfer "received and reconciled" on January 14.
- Nothing provides a remedy for a late milestone.

**Why it matters.**
- If implementation slips, for example if system integration testing is not finished, Harrowgate still owes the full monthly fee of $184,500 from January 15, because that is the defined Start Date (SOW §§ 2.1, 5.4). The Term Sheet describes January 15 as a "target" (Term Sheet § 5).
- The timetable has about 11 days between kick-off and go-live, including a one-day buffer between inventory reconciliation and go-live. Inventory must move from Harrowgate's current facility (SOW § 2.2). Harrowgate may have contractual obligations at that facility that we have not seen.
- Harrowgate cannot safely move all its stock until it knows Bellweather is insured (Issue 7), has cleared the subprocessor list (Issue 8) and has landlord protection (Issue 13).

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (SOW § 2.1 and § 5.4):*
> "The Start Date means the later of January 15, 2027 and the date on which Provider has completed the Schedule A milestones and Customer has confirmed in writing that the Services have commenced. Fees accrue only from the Start Date."

*Add:* A credit for each day of delay not caused by Customer (for example 1/30 of the Monthly Service Fee per day), and a right for Customer to terminate without liability if go-live is more than 30 days late.

*Add:* Delivery of the insurance endorsements and the completed Annex 3 as conditions precedent to the transfer of inventory.

*Client question:* Is a mid-January go-live realistic? See Section 5, item 5.

### Issue 10: Force majeure: Harrowgate keeps paying and has no clean exit (Medium)

**Where:** MSA § 17.2; MSA § 3.2; SOW § 5.1.

**What the documents say.**
- Payment obligations are excluded from relief (MSA § 17.2).
- After 15 days of non-performance of a material portion of the Services, Customer "may procure substitute services for the duration of the event."
- The fee is a fixed monthly service fee (SOW § 5.1).

**Why it matters.**
- Harrowgate would keep paying the fixed fee while Bellweather is excused, and would also pay for any substitute provider.
- There is no right to terminate if the event is prolonged.
- The clause does not exclude failures of Bellweather's own subcontractors or labor disputes among its workforce, which could be argued to be "beyond its reasonable control."
- The substitution right in § 17.2 sits awkwardly with the broad exclusivity in § 3.2 (Issue 1), so one would argue the substitution right is an exception to § 3.2. A narrowed § 3.2 resolves this.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 17.2):* Add:
> "Fees shall abate proportionately for any Services not performed. The following are not force majeure events: labor disputes involving Provider's or its subcontractors' personnel, failures of Provider's subcontractors or suppliers (unless themselves caused by a force majeure event), and equipment or system failures. If a force majeure event prevents Provider from performing a material portion of the Services for more than thirty (30) days, Customer may terminate the affected Statement of Work or this Agreement without liability, and Customer's exclusivity obligations are suspended for the duration of the event."

### Issue 11: Exit mechanics: stranded exclusivity, exit fees, survival (Medium)

**Where:** MSA §§ 14.3, 14.5, 15.1–15.4, 13.2; SOW §§ 5.6, 2.3.

**What the documents say.**
- Either Party may terminate "this Agreement or any Statement of Work" for uncured material breach (MSA § 14.3). Termination of the MSA ends all SOWs (§ 14.5). Termination of one SOW does not end the MSA.
- Transition assistance is charged at the SOW § 5.6 rates ($115, $48 and $140 per hour), and outbound handling of goods on release "shall be charged at the inbound pallet rate" ($9.50 per pallet; SOW § 5.3).
- The survival clause (MSA § 15.4) lists §§ 5.5, 6.1, 8, 10, 11, 13.1, 15, 17 and 18. It does not list §§ 6.2–6.3 (goods), 9 (data), or 13.2 (Customer's ownership of Customer Data and reports).

**Why it matters.**
- *Stranded exclusivity.* If Harrowgate terminates SOW No. 1 for Bellweather's breach, the MSA and the § 3.2 exclusivity continue. Harrowgate could then be barred from using anyone else, with no SOW in place.
- *Exit charges.* Harrowgate would pay transition rates and outbound handling fees even where it terminates for Bellweather's breach or insolvency, and Bellweather may recover them against disputed amounts.
- *Survival.* Bellweather's goods-loss obligations (§ 6.3) for losses occurring during transition, the data protections (§ 9) and Harrowgate's ownership of data (§ 13.2) are not on the survival list. (The DPA has its own term clause, § 10.1.)

**Recommendation.**
- Add to MSA § 14.5: if SOW No. 1 is terminated or expires, exclusivity ends automatically.
- Add to SOW § 5.6 and MSA § 15.2: transition assistance and outbound handling are free of charge where termination is by Customer for Provider's breach or insolvency, or where the MSA is terminated under the new rights in Issue 4.
- *Redline (MSA § 15.4):* "Sections 5.5, 6.1 through 6.3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 (for the period stated), 13, 15, 17 and 18…".

### Issue 12: SOW commercial ambiguities and gaps (Medium)

**Where:** SOW §§ 1.2(d), 5.2–5.3, 6.2, 6.3, 9.4; MSA § 6.3.

1. **White Glove baseline** (SOW §§ 5.2(c), 5.3). The baseline is 6,500 deliveries, "of which not more than 1,500 are White Glove." The excess charges apply to "volumes in excess of the Baseline Volumes." It is unclear whether White Glove deliveries beyond 1,500 are charged at $129 even if total deliveries are below 6,500. *Fix:* state expressly that excess charges apply only when total deliveries exceed 6,500 and, for White Glove, add a defined substitute treatment (for example, a $[__] upcharge over the Room of Choice rate).
2. **Haul-away scope.** SOW § 1.2(d) covers "haul-away of packaging," but the rate table includes "[h]aul-away of end customer's existing furniture" at $45 per item (SOW § 5.3). It is unclear whether that service is in scope. *Fix:* conform § 1.2(d), or delete the line item.
3. **Items over 350 pounds or requiring disassembly** (SOW § 9.4). The Fees "assume" that no item exceeds 350 lb or needs disassembly. Items beyond that go through a change order. For a furniture retailer this assumption may be commercially significant. *Fix:* ask the client how frequent these items are, and agree a rate card for them now.
4. **Service credits** (SOW § 6.2). They cap at 10% of the monthly fee ($18,450 on $184,500), and are applied to "the next invoice," so credits accruing in the final month are never paid out. *Fix:* credits are paid in cash at expiry or termination, and a chronic-failure termination right is added (Issue 4).
5. **Loss and damage** (SOW § 6.3; MSA § 6.3). Landed cost means purchase price plus freight, duties and customs. Recovery therefore excludes lost margin (see Issue 3). Payment within 30 days of a claim is fine.
6. **Annual physical inventory** (SOW § 3.6(b)) is set for January each year, so the first one coincides with go-live. *Fix:* start in January 2028, or agree a baseline count on transfer.
7. **No annual price adjustment** appears in the draft. This is favorable to Harrowgate. We should confirm that Bellweather does not intend to add escalators or fuel surcharges in later turns.

### Issue 13: Liens, landlord risk and counterparty diligence (Medium)

**Where:** MSA § 6.1; preamble; MSA § 16.1(a); SOW § 3.1; Term Sheet § 3.1.

**What the documents say.**
- Bellweather keeps Customer Goods free of liens "other than any lien that cannot be waived under applicable law" (MSA § 6.1).
- Bellweather is described only as "a limited liability company." No jurisdiction of organization or address is given (preamble), although it warrants good standing "under the laws of its jurisdiction of organization" (MSA § 16.1(a)).
- The Facility is described as Bellweather's distribution center at 400 Meadowlands Parkway, Secaucus, New Jersey (SOW § 3.1). The documents do not say whether Bellweather owns or leases it.

**Why it matters.**
- The carve-out for non-waivable liens is vague, and a warehouse or carrier lien could be asserted against Harrowgate's goods in a fee dispute, even though MSA § 5.4 requires Bellweather to continue performing during a dispute. Harrowgate's stock is its leverage and its working capital.
- If the Facility is leased, a landlord or mortgagee could assert rights against goods in the premises. We should look for a waiver or non-disturbance protection.
- If Bellweather becomes insolvent, the insolvency termination right (MSA § 14.4) will not by itself get the goods out. The release and access rights should be clear in the contract.
- Harrowgate is entrusting inventory and personal data to an entity we know very little about.

**Recommendation.**

*Redline (MSA § 6.1):* Replace the end of the sentence:
> "…and Provider waives, and shall not assert, any lien (including any statutory warehouse or carrier lien) against Customer Goods for any amount, whether or not related to Customer Goods, and shall not withhold, delay the release of, or condition the release of Customer Goods on payment of any disputed amount. If Provider contends a non-waivable lien applies, Provider shall identify it in writing before signing."

*Add to SOW § 3.1:* a representation about whether Bellweather owns or leases the Facility, and, if leased, delivery of a landlord waiver and acknowledgment from the landlord (and any mortgagee) that Customer Goods are not subject to its lien or claims. Include a right of access to retrieve goods in an insolvency or breach.

*Diligence request:* Preamble to name the state of organization and address; good-standing certificate; recent financial statements; consideration of a parent guaranty. *Client question:* what diligence has Harrowgate already done?
