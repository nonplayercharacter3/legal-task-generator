# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Supervising Partner
**FROM:** Associate
**DATE:** October 18, 2026
**RE:** Harrowgate Home Goods, Inc. / Bellweather Freight Logistics LLC: Review of Provider Draft MSA, SOW No. 1 and DPA against the Signed Term Sheet and Insurance Broker Letter (New York law)

---

## 1. Bottom Line

The Provider's drafts (MSA, SOW No. 1, DPA) are one-sided and depart from the signed Term Sheet in several places. Five points should be fixed before Harrowgate signs:

1. **Exclusivity is far broader than agreed.** MSA §3.2 covers all warehousing, freight and delivery services nationwide, including Affiliates and self-performance. Term Sheet §§4.1–4.2 limits exclusivity to NYC-area last-mile home delivery.
2. **Customer's indemnity is uncapped and Provider's is not.** MSA §§10.2 and 11.3 make Harrowgate indemnify Provider without limit for claims tied to end-customer premises, which is where Provider's crews work.
3. **The liability cap is weak and unclear.** It is tied to a vague trailing-12-month "fees" figure with no floor and no carve-outs for data breach or Provider's indemnity (MSA §11.1).
4. **The lock-in is long.** There is a three-year auto-renewal on a 15-day notice window, with no termination for convenience or chronic-failure exit (MSA §§14.1–14.3).
5. **The DPA moves data disputes to Delaware and outranks the MSA** (DPA §9.1; MSA §2.3), contrary to Term Sheet §11.

The broker letter shows the coverage limits meet MSA §12.1. However, the additional-insured endorsements do not yet exist, the enclosed certificate itself is not in our folder, and the program has gaps (see Issue 7).

**Leverage.** Term Sheet §§12.1 and 12.3 make the commercial terms non-binding and let either party walk away without liability. Harrowgate is not obliged to sign anything resembling this draft. Several asks below are simply a return to what Provider signed on September 22, 2026.

## 2. Issues in Order of Importance

### Tier 1: Must Fix Before Signing

#### Issue 1. Exclusivity is nationwide, all-services and binds Affiliates

- **Where:** MSA §3.2, read with §1.1 (Affiliate), §17.2 (force majeure), §2.3 (precedence); SOW §§1.3, 4.1, 9.3, 9.4; Term Sheet §§4.1–4.2.
- **What the documents say:**
  - MSA §3.2 makes Provider Customer's exclusive provider of "all warehousing, freight transportation and delivery services … throughout the United States." It bars Customer and its Affiliates from using any third party and from performing the services themselves.
  - Term Sheet §4.1 limits exclusivity to "last-mile home delivery within the New York City metropolitan area only." Term Sheet §4.2 says "[n]o other services are subject to exclusivity."
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Scope.** The exclusivity covers services Provider has not agreed to supply. SOW §1.3 puts line-haul outside the Service Area out of scope, and SOW §4.1 limits delivery to NYC and eight NY/NJ counties. Harrowgate would be locked into Provider for national freight with no commitment, price or service level from Provider.
  - **Immediate breach risk.** Literally read, the clause bars Harrowgate from running its own stores' stockrooms or trucks, and from using parcel carriers or inbound vendor freight.
  - **Affiliates.** It binds Affiliates (any >50% group entity, MSA §1.1) that Harrowgate may not control or may not be able to bind.
  - **Timing.** It runs "during the Term" from January 1, 2027 (MSA §§14.1, 3.2). Services do not start until January 15 (SOW §2.1). Harrowgate could not use its current facility or carriers during the transition.
  - **Conflicts with SOW.** SOW §9.3 has Customer arranging inbound transport to the Facility, which would breach §3.2 if a third party hauls. SOW §9.4 leaves oversized or disassembly-required items to a priced-later change order. Provider could refuse the work while exclusivity blocks Harrowgate from using anyone else.
  - **Length.** It continues through any three-year Renewal Term (MSA §14.2). Because the MSA outranks the SOW (§2.3), nothing in the SOW cures this.
  - **Term Sheet.** The Term Sheet is non-binding (§12.1), so this is a negotiating point rather than a breach claim. It is still Provider's own signed statement of the deal.
- **Recommended fix (replace MSA §3.2):**

  > "3.2 Limited Exclusivity. From the Start Date of Statement of Work No. 1 and during the Term, Customer shall use Provider as its exclusive provider of last-mile home delivery of Customer Goods to end customers located in the New York City metropolitan area [Service Area, as defined in SOW No. 1] (the 'Exclusive Services'). No other services are subject to exclusivity, and Customer and its Affiliates remain free to use any other provider, or to perform themselves, all other warehousing, freight transportation, line-haul and delivery services. Customer's Affiliates are not bound by this Section 3.2. Customer may use an alternative provider for any Exclusive Service that Provider (a) fails or declines to perform, (b) cannot perform within agreed service levels or capacity, or (c) is excused from performing under Section 17.2, and for any month in which Provider misses two or more service levels in SOW No. 1."

  Also revise the SOW to price oversized and disassembly items (Issue 11).
- **Client question:** Does Harrowgate want exclusivity at all? Does the Service Area (which includes Nassau, Suffolk, Westchester, Rockland and four NJ counties) match its understanding of "NYC metropolitan area"? Which existing carrier and warehouse contracts would exclusivity breach?

#### Issue 2. Harrowgate's indemnity is uncapped and covers Provider's own work; Provider's is capped

- **Where:** MSA §§10.1, 10.2, 11.1, 11.3, 10.3; Term Sheet §§8.1–8.2.
- **What the documents say:**
  - MSA §10.2 requires Customer to indemnify Provider against any third-party claim "arising out of or relating to" (a) Customer Goods, (b) "any Customer premises or end-customer premises at which Provider performs Services," and (c) "Customer's use of the Services."
  - There is no carve-out for Provider's negligence.
  - MSA §11.3 makes §10.2 apply "without limitation." Provider's §10.1 indemnity has no such exclusion and sits under the §11.1 cap.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Contradiction with §10.1(a).** If Provider's crew injures a customer or damages a home, §10.1(a) has Provider indemnify, while §10.2(b) has Harrowgate indemnify Provider for the same claim. Provider will say the more specific, uncapped clause controls. New York courts construe indemnities strictly and require clear language before shifting an indemnitee's own negligence. Harrowgate should not rely on that rule when a clause this broad is on the page. (Authority not in the folder; to be confirmed.)
  - **Open-ended exposure.** §10.2(c) ("use of the Services") has no limiting principle, and the indemnity is unlimited in amount.
  - **Not what the parties agreed.** Term Sheet §8.1 contemplates a mutual cap with "customary exceptions." §8.2 left indemnities "to be negotiated," so nothing supports an asymmetric uncapped customer indemnity.
  - **Narrow Provider indemnity.** §10.1 omits Security Incidents and breach of §§8–9 or the DPA. It also omits Provider's gross negligence beyond the §11.3 cap carve-out, and claims relating to Provider's systems or IP.
  - **Control of defense.** §10.3 gives the indemnifying party sole control. For claims that could affect Harrowgate's brand or customers, Harrowgate should keep approval rights over settlements involving admissions, injunctive relief or publicity. The no-admission protection in §10.3 does not reach monetary terms or non-party effects.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **MSA §10.2:** add at the end of the lead-in:

    > "…, in each case except to the extent the claim arises from the negligence, willful misconduct or breach of this Agreement by Provider, its Affiliates, personnel or subcontractors."

    Delete §10.2(b) and (c), or narrow (b) to "a hazardous condition at a Customer premises not caused by Provider." Limit (a) to claims that Customer Goods are defective or infringe, not claims arising from Provider's handling.
  - **MSA §11.3:** delete the first sentence ("shall apply without limitation").
  - **MSA §10.1:** add (d) "any Security Incident or breach of Section 8, Section 9 or the DPA" and (e) "any claim arising from Provider's gross negligence or willful misconduct."
  - **Position:** the preferred position is that both indemnities for third-party claims sit outside the cap. The fallback is a symmetrical super-cap (see Issue 3).
  - **MSA §10.3:** add:

    > "The indemnifying Party shall not, without the indemnified Party's consent, enter into any settlement that includes an admission of fault, non-monetary relief or public statement concerning the indemnified Party."

#### Issue 3. Liability cap is poorly defined, has no floor and lacks data/goods carve-outs

- **Where:** MSA §§11.1, 11.2, 11.3, 6.3; SOW §§5.1, 6.2, 6.3; DPA §5.5; MSA §2.3; MSA §12.1.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Early-term gap.** MSA §11.1 caps liability at "the fees paid or payable in the 12 months before the claim." In early months this figure is very small. For a claim in, say, March 2027, only a few months of fees have been paid or accrued. The Monthly Service Fee is $184,500 (SOW §5.1), so a full year is about $2.21M. The cap should not depend on how long the contract has run.
  - **Ambiguity.** "fees" is lowercase and not the defined term (§1.5). "Before the claim" does not say whether the measuring date is accrual, assertion or breach.
  - **Cap vs. insurance.** The cap (about $2.2M at maturity) is below the required warehouse legal liability limit of $5M (§12.1(c)) and auto of $5M (§12.1(b)). Provider could carry insurance but still cap Harrowgate's recovery below it.
  - **Goods.** Loss of Customer Goods at 180,000 sq ft of storage (SOW §3.2) could exceed the cap. The agreed landed-cost reimbursement (MSA §6.3; SOW §6.3; Term Sheet §7) is itself subject to the cap, which undercuts a bargained-for remedy.
  - **Data and consequential damages.** MSA §11.2 excludes "indirect… consequential" damages and lost profits. A Provider could argue that notification, credit monitoring and re-delivery or cover costs fall within it. DPA §5.5 reimburses "reasonable costs of investigating and responding," including notification, call center and credit monitoring. Neither the DPA nor the MSA says whether those costs sit inside or outside the cap. The DPA outranks the MSA in a conflict (MSA §2.3) but is silent on this point.
  - **Credits.** SOW §6.2 credits are "without prejudice" to other remedies, but nothing says they do not erode the cap.
- **Recommended fix (MSA §11.1):**

  > "…shall not exceed the greater of (a) the Fees paid and payable in the twelve (12) months preceding the first event giving rise to liability (or, if fewer than twelve months have elapsed, twelve (12) times the Monthly Service Fee) and (b) $[●]."

  Add to §11.3 as uncapped (or subject to a separate super-cap of $[●], not less than the §12.1 cyber limit of $3M):
  - Provider's indemnity obligations under §10.1.
  - Loss of or damage to Customer Goods (to the extent of landed cost and insurance proceeds).
  - Breach of §8 or §9 or the DPA, including DPA §5.5 costs.
  - Provider's abandonment or wrongful withholding of Customer Goods.

  Add: "Amounts under DPA §5.5 and service credits under SOW §6.2 are direct damages and do not count toward any cap." Replace "fees" with "Fees."

#### Issue 4. Three-year auto-renewal on a tight window; no exit rights

- **Where:** MSA §§14.1–14.5, 17.1; SOW §6.2; Term Sheet §§5, 10.1–10.2.
- **What the documents say:**
  - The initial term is two years (MSA §14.1), matching Term Sheet §5.
  - The agreement then auto-renews for **three-year** terms unless non-renewal notice is delivered "during the fifteen (15) day period ending one hundred twenty (120) days before the expiry" (§14.2). Notice at any other time "shall be of no effect."
  - Term Sheet §10.2 left renewal "to be negotiated," so three-year renewals were never agreed.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Narrow window.** If the Initial Term ends December 31, 2028, the window is roughly August 19 to September 2, 2028. Notice is effective only on receipt by hand, courier or certified mail (§17.1), so a mailing error forfeits it.
  - **Commitment.** Missing the window commits Harrowgate to roughly $6.6M more in fixed fees (36 × $184,500) plus US-wide exclusivity through December 2031.
  - **No other exit.** There is no termination for convenience. The only exits are material breach with a 30-day cure (§14.3) and insolvency (§14.4). There is no right to terminate for chronic SLA failure, loss of insurance or operating authority, Provider change of control or force majeure.
  - **Drafting error.** §14.1 refers to the undefined "MSA Effective Date" (the defined term is "Effective Date"), which creates uncertainty over the end date.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **§14.2:** renewal terms of one year; non-renewal notice at least 60 days before expiry; Provider must send a written reminder 90–150 days before expiry, failing which the Agreement does not renew. Alternatively delete auto-renewal.
  - **§14.1:** replace "MSA Effective Date" with "Effective Date."
  - **New §14.6 (Customer termination):** for convenience on 90 days' notice after month 12 (Provider may ask for a stated fee), and immediately on notice for (a) chronic SLA failure (for example, any SLA missed in three of any six months or two or more SLAs missed in two consecutive months), (b) lapse of required insurance or operating authority, (c) a Security Incident caused by Provider's breach, or (d) Provider's assignment or change of control to a Customer competitor.
  - **Notice:** allow notice by email with confirmation, and fill in the notice addresses (signature page is blank).
- **Client question:** What is Harrowgate's expected volume trend and its tolerance for a minimum spend of about $4.43M over 24 months (24 × $184,500)? Does it want a flexible exit?

#### Issue 5. DPA governing law and forum diverge from the MSA, and the DPA outranks the MSA

- **Where:** DPA §9.1; MSA §§2.3, 18.1–18.4, 9.1; Term Sheet §11.
- **What the documents say:**
  - DPA §9.1 applies **Delaware** law and exclusive Wilmington, Delaware courts to "any dispute relating to the Processing of Personal Data or any Security Incident."
  - The MSA (§18.1) and Term Sheet (§11) specify New York law and New York County courts.
  - MSA §2.3 ranks the DPA first, above the MSA and SOW.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Split proceedings.** A single data incident could produce parallel disputes in two states under different laws. The DPA has none of the MSA's protections: escalation (§18.2), jury waiver (§18.4) or injunctive-relief language (§18.3).
  - **Cost and leverage.** Provider can invoke Delaware to move the most likely high-value dispute away from Harrowgate's home forum.
  - **Statutory anchors.** The DPA itself cites New York's SHIELD Act (N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §§899-aa, 899-bb) as the security benchmark (DPA §§1.1, 4.1).
  - **Precedence.** Because the DPA is first in the hierarchy, it could override MSA terms beyond data protection. The ranking should be limited to privacy and security topics.
  - **Enforceability support.** On this contract size, New York's choice-of-law and forum statutes (N.Y. Gen. Oblig. Law §§5-1401, 5-1402) should support enforcing New York law and forum. This should be confirmed.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **DPA §9.1:** replace with "This DPA and any dispute arising out of or relating to it are governed by Section 18 of the Agreement."
  - **MSA §2.3:** replace with:

    > "In the event of conflict, (a) the DPA prevails solely as to the Processing and security of Personal Data, (b) otherwise this Agreement prevails over any Statement of Work, except that a Statement of Work may expressly override a specified provision of this Agreement if it identifies the provision and states the intent to override."
- **Note:** The SOW is currently subordinate in all respects. As drafted, even negotiated SOW-specific terms (for example, SOW §6.2 credits) are vulnerable to the MSA.

#### Issue 6. Assignment and change of control restrict only Harrowgate

- **Where:** MSA §17.4; SOW §§8.1–8.2; MSA §7.2; DPA §6.1; Term Sheet §10.2.
- **What the documents say:**
  - MSA §17.4 bars only **Customer** from assigning, "whether voluntarily, by operation of law, by merger or change of control," without Provider's consent. It contains no restriction on Provider.
  - SOW §8.2 lets Provider assign or delegate "this SOW, and any of its rights and obligations under this SOW and under the Agreement… to any third party, without Customer's consent," with notice only within 30 days after.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **M&A hold-up.** Provider holds a consent right over any Harrowgate sale, merger or restructuring. Combined with Issue 1 (exclusivity) and Issue 4 (lock-in), that is a hold-up risk in a deal.
  - **Provider substitution.** Provider can transfer the relationship to an unvetted party that may lack the insurance, financial strength or security controls Harrowgate relied on. This undercuts the consent rights in MSA §7.2 and DPA §6.1, and the Facility commitment in SOW §3.1.
  - **Precedence.** SOW §8.2 and MSA §17.4 are not formally in conflict, so §2.3 does not help.
  - **Open item.** Term Sheet §10.2 left assignment "to be negotiated."
- **Recommended fix (MSA §17.4):**

  > "Neither Party may assign or delegate this Agreement or any Statement of Work without the other Party's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, except that either Party may assign to an Affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice, provided the assignee assumes all obligations in writing. Provider shall not assign or undergo a change of control in favor of a competitor of Customer without Customer's consent, and Customer may terminate on thirty (30) days' notice following any assignment by, or change of control of, Provider."

  Delete SOW §8.2 and conform it to MSA §17.4.

#### Issue 7. Insurance: limits meet the draft, but the program has timing, verification and coverage gaps

- **Where:** Quillmore Insurance Brokers letter dated October 16, 2026 (§§2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.2, 4.1, 4.2, 5.1; enclosure not in folder); MSA §12.1–12.2; SOW Schedule A; Term Sheet §8.3.
- **What checks out.** I compared each limit in the broker's table (§2.1) with MSA §12.1:

  | Coverage | MSA requirement | Broker table | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | CGL | §12.1(a): $2M per occurrence / $4M aggregate | $2M / $4M | Meets |
  | Auto | §12.1(b): $5M CSL | $5M CSL | Meets |
  | Warehouse legal liability | §12.1(c): $5M per occurrence | $5M | Meets |
  | Motor truck cargo | §12.1(d): $250,000 per vehicle | $250,000 | Meets |
  | Workers' comp / EL | §12.1(e): statutory / $1M | Statutory (NY, NJ) / $1M | Meets |
  | Cyber | §12.1(f): $3M per claim | $3M per claim | Meets |

  All insurers are rated A- VII or better. The lowest is Granite Ridge at A- VIII, which is within "A- VII or better."
- **Problems:**
  - **Additional-insured status does not yet exist.** The letter (§3.1) says insurers "have confirmed that the endorsements **will be issued** … on execution." The endorsements are not in place. The certificate shows Harrowgate as an additional insured, but §5.1 states the certificate is "information only" and does not amend or extend coverage, and the policy terms govern. The broker will forward the endorsements only after issuance. We have no protection until we see them.
  - **Cancellation notice.** Broker §3.2 says the policies "will be endorsed" to give Harrowgate 30 days' notice as certificate holder. That is a promise, not an issued endorsement. Insurers often will not provide notice to certificate holders. MSA §12.2 places the obligation on Provider, so keep that and also require the endorsements.
  - **Certificate not reviewed.** The Certificate of Liability Insurance is listed as an enclosure but is not in our folder. The broker's letter is unsigned in our copy. I have verified only the broker's summary, not the certificate or any policy.
  - **Policy term.** The policies expire July 1, 2027, six months into the term (letter §4.2). The broker promises renewal certificates "on or before that expiry date," which is not a commitment to deliver before the lapse.
  - **Gaps in MSA §12.**
    - Additional-insured status is limited to CGL and auto (§12.2). There is no requirement for primary and non-contributory wording, waiver of subrogation, or loss-payee status for Customer on warehouse legal liability and cargo.
    - There is no required umbrella or excess coverage.
    - There is no insurance requirement for subcontractors.
    - Cyber is claims-made (letter §2.1). There is no required retroactive date or extended reporting period.
    - Cyber is stated only "per claim," with no aggregate. Retentions and deductibles are not disclosed.
  - **Adequacy of limits.** Cargo at $250,000 per vehicle may fall short for a furniture load. $5M for warehouse legal liability may fall short of the inventory value in the Secaucus Facility (SOW §3.1). We do not know the stock value. The cap (Issue 3) can also reduce recovery below insurance.
  - **Timing.** Certificates are due before the first SOW start date (MSA §12.2), with a Schedule A target of January 11, 2027. The broker has committed to that date (letter §4.1). The AI endorsements should be delivered before signing, not after.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **Request to Provider and broker:** obtain the enclosed certificate and copies of the AI and notice-of-cancellation endorsements, plus the declarations pages, before signing.
  - **MSA §12.2 additions:** primary and non-contributory wording, waiver of subrogation in Customer's favor, Customer as loss payee on warehouse legal liability and cargo, and a subcontractor insurance requirement.
  - **Limit increases:** an umbrella policy of at least $[●] and cyber retroactive date and tail provisions.
  - **Condition precedent:** delivery of the AI endorsements as a condition to Provider's performance and to exclusivity.
- **Client question:** What is the typical and peak value of inventory at the Secaucus Facility? Does Harrowgate's own property or stock-throughput policy cover goods at third-party warehouses and in transit?

#### Issue 8. Data security terms: costs, subprocessors and return timing

- **Where:** DPA §§5.1, 5.5, 6.1, Annex 3, 8.1, 8.2, 3.5, 4.1; MSA §§9, 15.1(b), 15.2, 15.4; SOW §4.5.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Reimbursement trigger.** DPA §5.5 applies only to incidents "caused by a breach of this DPA by Provider or its Subprocessors." Harrowgate would have to prove breach. It should apply to any incident in Provider's or Subprocessors' possession or control, except where caused by Customer. It also covers no third-party claims, regulatory fines or attorneys' fees. See Issue 3 for the cap interaction.
  - **Annex 3 is blank.** It reads "[To be completed by Provider and approved by Customer before the Start Date]." Under DPA §6.1 no Subprocessor may be engaged without consent. Provider will in practice rely on at least a warehouse management system host, a driver app and a portal vendor (SOW §§2.2, 4.5). The list must be settled before signing, or Provider will either breach on day one or press for a blanket approval.
  - **Return vs. transition.** DPA §8.1 requires return within 30 days after termination, but MSA §15.2 gives Harrowgate up to 90 days of transition assistance. Data could be returned and deleted while Provider is still running operations, or Provider could argue the opposite.
  - **Survival.** MSA §15.4 does not list §9, though DPA §10.1 keeps the DPA alive while data is held.
  - **Notice period.** 48 hours (DPA §5.1) is acceptable but should be checked against Harrowgate's own statutory deadlines under N.Y. Gen. Bus. Law §899-aa and the New Jersey statute.
  - **Photographs.** SOW §4.5 requires time-stamped photographs of delivered items "in place," which may capture the interior of customers' homes. The DPA lists photographs as Personal Data (§1.2) but sets no retention period during the Term.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **DPA §5.5:** reimburse "all costs … of any Security Incident, other than to the extent caused by Customer," expressly including legal fees, forensic costs, regulatory fines and third-party claims.
  - **Annex 3:** completed and approved before execution.
  - **DPA §8.1:** return data within 30 days after the later of termination and the end of transition assistance.
  - **DPA:** add a photo retention limit, for example 90 days unless Customer instructs otherwise.
  - **MSA §15.4:** add §9 and the DPA.
- **Request to Provider:** list all Subprocessors and any offshore access (cf. DPA §3.5, US-only).

#### Issue 9. Fixed fees continue through force majeure and implementation delay

- **Where:** MSA §17.2, §3.3, §14.1, §3.2; SOW §§2.1, 2.2, 5.1, 5.4, Schedule A.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Force majeure.** MSA §17.2 excuses Provider for events "beyond its reasonable control" but gives Customer only the right to "procure substitute services" after 15 days. Harrowgate still pays the fixed $184,500 fee (§5.1 of the SOW) and has no termination right. The list does not carve out Provider's labor disputes, subcontractor failures or cyber events.
  - **Go-live.** Provider's implementation (Schedule A) is tight: initial inventory transfer is January 14 with go-live on January 15. There is no remedy for Provider's delay. Fees accrue from the Start Date (SOW §5.4), the exclusivity clock arguably runs from January 1, and there is no termination right for failure to launch.
  - **Peak season.** The first weeks fall in a seasonal transition period.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **MSA §17.2:**
    - Add fee abatement pro rata for any period in which Services are not performed.
    - Customer may terminate the affected SOW after 30 days.
    - Exclude Provider's labor disputes, subcontractor failures and cyber incidents that would have been prevented by compliance with the DPA and Annex 2.
  - **SOW §2.1:** if go-live slips because of Provider, no Fees accrue, Provider pays any documented transition costs, and Customer may terminate if go-live is more than 30 days late.
  - **Exclusivity:** starts at go-live (Issue 1).
- **Client question:** What is Harrowgate's contingency plan for January 1–15, 2027, and are its current facility lease and carrier commitments flexible enough for the January 14–15 handover?

#### Issue 10. Customer Goods: liens, inventory variance, and release at termination

- **Where:** MSA §§6.1, 6.3, 6.4, 14.4, 15.1, 15.2; SOW §§3.6, 5.6, 6.1, 6.3; Term Sheet §7.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Lien waiver.** MSA §6.1 bars liens "arising by, through or under Provider, other than any lien that cannot be waived under applicable law." Warehouse and carrier liens are typically statutory and generally waivable by agreement (UCC Article 7; to be confirmed for New York and New Jersey). The carve-out invites Provider to assert them in a fee dispute. MSA §5.4 requires Provider to keep performing during a dispute, but there is no express bar on holding goods.
  - **Insolvency.** MSA §14.4 allows termination on insolvency. Termination rights triggered by bankruptcy are often unenforceable in the bankruptcy itself. Goods in a bankrupt warehouse operator's possession would be a practical risk. Protection is better built through lien waivers and direct access rights.
  - **Release charges.** On release, "[o]utbound handling… shall be charged at the inbound pallet rate" ($9.50 per pallet, SOW §5.6). That is a charge to retrieve Harrowgate's own goods, including after Provider's breach.
  - **Unexplained shrink.** MSA §6.3 makes Provider liable for loss "occurring while in Provider's possession." Harrowgate would have to prove when loss occurred. Count discrepancies found in cycle counts or the annual physical inventory (SOW §3.6) have no stated treatment. The 99.5% inventory accuracy SLA (SOW §6.1) carries only a small credit.
  - **Packaging and receiving.** SOW §3.4 and MSA §4.2 put packaging compliance on Customer, and MSA §6.3 excludes loss "caused by Customer's acts or omissions." Provider could blame packaging for damage. SOW §3.3 requires Provider to note visible damage at receipt, which helps. Nothing says unexcepted receipt is conclusive.
  - **End of custody.** The chain of custody for delivery should end on signed proof of delivery (SOW §4.5), and returned or refused items under SOW §4.6 should stay Provider's responsibility.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **MSA §6.1:** replace the carve-out with "Provider waives, and shall not assert, any warehouse, carrier, possessory or other lien or right of set-off against Customer Goods, including statutory liens, whether for amounts under this Agreement or otherwise."
  - **MSA §15.1:** add that release is not conditioned on payment of any disputed amount, and that outbound handling is free of charge if termination follows Provider's breach or insolvency.
  - **New §6.5 (Inventory variance):** unexplained shrink or variance above [0.x]% at any count is presumed Provider's responsibility at landed cost.
  - **New §6.6:** goods accepted at receipt without noted exception are presumed to have been in good condition.
  - **Consider:** a bailee acknowledgment or precautionary UCC filing, and a review of the Facility lease for landlord lien rights. We have not seen the lease.

#### Issue 11. SOW pricing ambiguities

- **Where:** SOW §§5.1–5.3, 9.4, 1.2(d), 4.2(c), 9.1.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **White Glove baseline.** The baseline includes "not more than 1,500" White Glove deliveries within 6,500 total (§5.2). It is unclear whether White Glove volume above 1,500 is charged at $129 each even when total deliveries are under 6,500. Excess charges apply to "[v]olumes in excess of the Baseline Volumes" (§5.3).
  - **Haul-away.** The $45 per item haul-away appears only in the excess table (§5.3). §1.2(d) says haul-away is "where ordered," so it is unclear whether it is ever included.
  - **Item parameters.** §9.4 assumes no item over 350 lbs and none requiring disassembly. For a furniture retailer this is likely to occur regularly, and no rates are set for these items (see Issue 1).
  - **No downward adjustment.** There is no credit if volumes fall below baseline, and no rate cap or escalator limit for excess volumes.
  - **Tolls.** Pass-throughs at cost (§5.5) are fine but uncapped.
  - **Forecasts.** SOW §9.1 requires a 13-week rolling forecast weekly. There should be language that it is a non-binding estimate.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Clarify that excess charges apply only to **total** volumes above 6,500 and that White Glove deliveries above 1,500 are charged at the White Glove rate only to the extent total volume exceeds 6,500, or otherwise as the client prefers.
  - Add pre-agreed rates for oversize and disassembly items.
  - State whether haul-away is ever included.
  - Add that forecasts are non-binding good-faith estimates.
  - Add that rates are fixed for the Initial Term, with renewal increases capped at [●]% on 90 days' notice.
- **Client question:** What share of Harrowgate's products exceeds 350 lbs or needs disassembly? What are expected volumes against the baselines?

#### Issue 12. Service levels and credits have little teeth

- **Where:** SOW §§6.1–6.2, 7.1(d); MSA §§3.3, 14.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Credit size.** Each missed service level earns a 2% credit (about $3,690), capped at 10% (about $18,450) a month (SOW §6.2). That is small against a missed on-time delivery rate, which affects Harrowgate's own customers.
  - **No exit for repeat failure.** Repeated misses are not a material breach by definition, so Harrowgate must prove material breach and wait out a 30-day cure (MSA §14.3).
  - **Final invoice.** Credits are applied "against the next invoice" (MSA §3.3), so credits earned in the final month are lost unless paid in cash.
  - **Measurement.** The scorecard is Provider-prepared (SOW §7.1(d)) with no audit right specific to SLA data.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Treat chronic failure (see Issue 4) as a termination right.
  - Raise credits, for example to 4–5% per missed SLA, with a cap of 20%+.
  - Add that credits are payable in cash on termination or expiry.
  - Add that SLA data is auditable under MSA §5.5.
  - Add that credits are not Customer's sole remedy (the SOW says so but MSA §3.3 should too).

#### Issue 13. No integration clause; relationship to the Term Sheet

- **Where:** MSA §17 (no entire-agreement clause); Term Sheet §§2.1, 12.1–12.3.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Gap.** MSA §17 has no entire-agreement or supersession clause. The Term Sheet's binding confidentiality provisions (§12.2(a)) survive for two years (§12.3) and sit alongside MSA §8 with no statement of how they interact.
  - **Both directions.** Provider could cite the Term Sheet or negotiating history to support its reading of an ambiguity. Harrowgate would want the final documents to carry the agreed positions.
  - **Walk-away.** Term Sheet §§12.1 and 12.3 make clear no one is obliged to sign, so refusing Provider's draft carries no liability risk. We should confirm no emails or conduct suggest otherwise.
- **Recommended fix:** add MSA §17.9:

  > "This Agreement, each Statement of Work and the DPA constitute the entire agreement of the Parties concerning their subject matter and supersede all prior agreements and understandings, including the term sheet dated September 22, 2026, provided that Section 12.2 of that term sheet continues to govern information exchanged before the Effective Date."

  The key Term Sheet positions that favor Harrowgate (exclusivity, cap parity, New York law) should be embedded in the final documents first.
