## I. Summary

I reviewed the four documents individually and against each other. All cross-references to the APA are correct, and the economics tie (see Part V). The substantive problems are these:

1. **The Bill of Sale redefines "Purchased Assets" without the APA's carve-outs.** On its face it conveys cash, the Albany facility and the HOLLIS mark, which the APA excludes. APA §10.12 may let the Bill of Sale win.
2. **APA §9.4(c) removes the cap and basket for every representation claim.** The 10% cap now protects Seller only on covenant and Excluded Liability claims, which is the opposite of the usual structure.
3. **The Escrow Agreement's claim mechanics override the APA's protections.** It pays out automatically if Seller misses a 30-day objection window, pays unproven estimates, and ignores the cap and basket.
4. **The TSA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive Delaware courts.** Every other document uses New York, and you told me the package is New York-governed.
5. **The Bill of Sale contains an irrevocable power of attorney and free-standing title and liability covenants.** Neither exists in the APA, and both sit outside Article IX's limits.
6. **There are further gaps** in the consents condition, the working capital mechanics, the integration and non-reliance language, the TSA's cost recovery and credit exposure, and Seller's post-closing access to records.

Business decisions for the client are collected in Part III. Lower-priority conforming points are in Part IV.

## II. Issues (most significant first)

### 1. Bill of Sale §1.1(b) redefines "Purchased Assets" and omits the Excluded Assets

**Where:** Bill of Sale §1.1(b), §1.1(b)(iv), §2.1, and Recital A. Compare APA §2.1, §2.2 and §10.12.

**Issue:**
- The Bill of Sale's definition conveys "all assets, properties and rights of every kind and nature, whether real, personal or mixed, tangible or intangible, wherever located, used or held for use in the Business."
- Unlike APA §2.1, it has no "other than the Excluded Assets" qualifier, and the Bill of Sale never mentions Excluded Assets.
- Its list also conveys "trademarks, trade names" in clause (iv), where the APA says "intellectual property."
- Read literally, §2.1 of the Bill of Sale ("hereby irrevocably sells…") presently transfers:
  - Seller's cash (APA §2.2);
  - the Albany manufacturing facility (real property, APA §2.2); and
  - the HOLLIS trademark (APA §2.2).
- Bill of Sale §1.1 says capitalized terms "not defined" take APA meanings, but Purchased Assets *is* defined here, so the override argument is available.
- APA §10.12 says the APA controls "except that each Ancillary Agreement controls as to its specific subject matter." Conveyance is the Bill of Sale's specific subject matter.

**Why it matters:** Buyer gets a present-tense conveyance document that contradicts the deal. Seller would be litigating the carve-outs against its own signed instrument. A real property transfer would also need separate deed formalities, but we should not leave this ambiguity in a recordable-looking instrument.

**Fix and language:**
- Replace Bill of Sale §1.1(b) with: *"'Purchased Assets' has the meaning given in Section 2.1 of the Purchase Agreement and, for the avoidance of doubt, excludes the Excluded Assets."* Delete the (i)–(vii) restatement, or conform it exactly to APA §2.1(a)–(g).
- Add new §2.5: *"Excluded Assets. Notwithstanding anything in this Bill of Sale, Seller retains, and nothing in this Bill of Sale sells, assigns or conveys, any right, title or interest in the Excluded Assets, including Seller's cash, the Albany manufacturing facility and the HOLLIS trademark."*
- Add new §5.9: *"Relationship to Purchase Agreement. This Bill of Sale is delivered pursuant to the Purchase Agreement and does not expand, limit or modify any representation, warranty, covenant, limitation or remedy in it. In the event of conflict, the Purchase Agreement controls. Seller makes no representation or warranty in this Bill of Sale."*
- Fix APA §10.12 (see Issue 9).

### 2. APA §9.4(c) swallows the cap and basket for all representation claims

**Where:** APA §9.4(a)–(c), §9.2, §1.1(m).

**Issue:**
- §9.4(a) caps Seller's aggregate liability under all of §9.2 at 10% of the Purchase Price ($1,840,000, equal to the Escrow Amount).
- §9.4(b) applies the $184,000 basket only to §9.2(a) (rep breaches).
- §9.4(c) then provides that both limits "shall not apply to any claim under Section 9.2(a)."
- Result: the basket has no work left to do, and every rep claim is uncapped, not just Fundamental Representations. The cap survives only for §9.2(b) (covenants) and §9.2(c) (Excluded Assets and Liabilities).
- This looks like a drafting slip. §1.1(m) defines "Fundamental Representations" but §9.4(c) never uses the term.

**Why it matters:** This is the largest economic exposure in the package. A $1.84 million "cap" becomes unlimited exposure for ordinary rep claims, including flat reps such as §4.6 (condition and adequacy of assets), §4.9 (non-infringement, unqualified by knowledge) and §4.10 (environmental compliance).

**Fix and language:** Replace §9.4(c) with:
*"The limitations in Section 9.4(a) and Section 9.4(b) shall not apply to any claim under Section 9.2(a) in respect of a Fundamental Representation, or to any claim arising from Fraud; provided that Seller's aggregate liability under Section 9.2(a) in respect of Fundamental Representations shall not exceed the Purchase Price actually received by Seller."*
- Define "Fraud" as *"actual and intentional common-law fraud under New York law in the making of an express representation in Article IV, and not constructive fraud, recklessness or negligent misrepresentation."* The term appears in §9.4(c) and §9.8 but is undefined.
- Note for strategy: as drafted, the cap currently also limits §9.2(c) (Excluded Liabilities) at $1.84 million. Buyer will likely seek to uncap §9.2(c) once §9.4(c) is corrected. See Part III, item 1.

### 3. Escrow Agreement §§3.1–3.3, 4.1 override APA protections and set traps

**Where:** Escrow §3.1(a), §3.2, §3.3, §3.5, §4.1, §5.1, §7.4; APA §9.4, §9.5, §9.7, §10.12.

**Issue (five problems):**
- **Deemed acceptance.** If Seller does not deliver an Objection Notice within 30 days of receipt, the Escrow Agent pays "the amount claimed" on the fifth Business Day (Escrow §3.3). APA §9.5 has no deemed-acceptance rule. Because Escrow §5.1 says the Agent has no duty to inquire into the APA, it will not apply the basket, the cap, the survival period or any defense. Under APA §10.12 the Escrow Agreement arguably controls "as to its specific subject matter."
- **Estimates paid as if final.** A Claim Notice may state "a good-faith estimate" (§3.1(a)). §3.3 pays "the amount claimed" absent objection, and nothing requires a refund if actual Losses are lower.
- **Receipt and notice mismatch.** The 30-day period runs from "Seller's receipt" (§3.2), but notices to Seller are deemed given on emailing or courier deposit (§7.4). Notices to the Agent are effective only on actual receipt (§7.4). The Agent cannot know when Seller received the notice. APA §10.2 lists no email addresses and gives no copy to counsel, so a notice to the CEO alone starts the clock.
- **Inflated estimates can freeze the whole escrow.** At the Release Date, the Agent holds back "the aggregate amount of all claims stated in Claim Notices" (§4.1). Nothing tests reasonableness, so a large estimate could freeze the entire fund. APA §9.7 ("less the amount of any pending claims") is equally open.
- **Basket and cap not applied.** There is no mechanism for the Agent to apply the $184,000 basket or the 10% cap.

**Why it matters:** Seller's money leaves escrow on a procedural default. Recovering funds already paid out means suing Buyer in New York County.

**Fix and language:**
- Escrow §3.2: change to *"within 45 days after the later of the Escrow Agent's and Seller's receipt of the Claim Notice."*
- Escrow §3.1: add *"Buyer shall deliver each Claim Notice to Seller by hand or nationally recognized overnight courier, with a copy to Seller's counsel (to be named in APA §10.2), and shall certify to the Escrow Agent the date and method of delivery. The Escrow Agent shall promptly forward a copy of each Claim Notice it receives to Seller."*
- Escrow §3.1: add a Buyer certification that *"the aggregate Losses claimed exceed the amount in Section 9.4(b) of the Purchase Agreement and the claim is made within the applicable survival period under Section 9.1."*
- Escrow §3.3: change to *"…the amount claimed, to the extent finally determined. Any amount stated only as an estimate shall be held until an updated Claim Notice under Section 3.5 states Losses as finally determined."*
- Escrow §3.3: add *"A payment under this Section 3.3 is not an admission or waiver by Seller under the Purchase Agreement. Buyer shall return, within five Business Days after demand, any amount received that exceeds Losses finally determined to be owed."*
- Escrow §4.1: limit the Pending Claims Amount to *"the amount reasonably and in good faith estimated by Buyer in its Claim Notice, which Seller may dispute under Section 3.2."*
- Add APA §9.10 mirroring the above, so the two documents agree.

### 4. TSA §9.3 selects Delaware law and exclusive Delaware courts

**Where:** TSA §9.3. Compare APA §10.8–10.9, Bill of Sale §5.5–5.6, Escrow §7.1–7.2.

**Issue:**
- The TSA alone chooses Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware courts. It also covers claims "relating to… the Services."
- The same business dispute (for example, a data issue during payroll migration that Buyer also frames as a rep breach or an Excluded Liability) could be litigated in two states under two bodies of law.
- TSA §7.3 imports APA §9.5's procedures and the APA's defined term "Losses," yet is governed by different law.

**Why it matters:**
- Dual-forum risk and inconsistent interpretation of the same defined terms.
- The package should be consistent with the New York governance you described.
- The TSA's fees total roughly $285,000 over its term plus pass-throughs (Schedule A). Whether New York's statutory choice-of-law and forum-selection provisions would apply is something I would verify rather than assume.

**Fix and language:** Replace §9.3 with the APA §10.8 and §10.9 text, adapted: *"This Agreement, and all claims arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Services, are governed by the laws of the State of New York… Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the State and federal courts located in New York County, New York…"* This is likely a template carry-over.

### 5. Bill of Sale §2.3: irrevocable, broad power of attorney with no APA basis

**Where:** Bill of Sale §2.3. Compare APA §6.7, §2.1.

**Issue:**
- Seller appoints Buyer and its "successors and assigns" as attorney-in-fact, coupled with an interest and irrevocable.
- Buyer may demand and receive assets, give releases, "institute and prosecute any proceedings that Buyer considers proper," and endorse checks payable to Seller.
- The APA contains no power of attorney; its only post-closing mechanism is further assurances "reasonably required" (§6.7).
- The power runs to "Purchased Assets," which the Bill of Sale over-defines (Issue 1), so it could reach Excluded Assets.
- There is no indemnity from Buyer, no notice to Seller, no restriction on settlements binding Seller, and nothing limiting the power in time. Because Seller's name is used, litigation can implicate Seller's reputation and costs.

**Why it matters:** An irrevocable, assignable power to sue and settle in Seller's name, and to endorse checks, is open-ended exposure for Seller after it has sold its operating business.

**Fix and language:** Preferably delete §2.3, since APA §6.7 and Bill of Sale §4.1 already cover further assurances. Alternatively, replace with:
*"Limited Power. Solely to the extent necessary to collect or enforce Purchased Assets (and expressly excluding any Excluded Asset or Excluded Liability), and only after ten Business Days' written notice to Seller, Seller appoints Buyer as its attorney-in-fact to demand and receive Purchased Assets and to endorse, without recourse, checks payable to Seller in respect of Purchased Assets. Buyer shall not, in Seller's name, commence any proceeding, assert any claim against a customer or supplier of Seller that would reasonably be expected to expose Seller to liability, or settle any matter that binds Seller, without Seller's prior written consent. Buyer shall indemnify Seller for all Losses arising from Buyer's exercise of this power. This power terminates on the second anniversary of the Closing Date."* Delete "coupled with an interest and… irrevocable."

### 6. Bill of Sale operates outside Article IX's risk allocation

**Where:** Bill of Sale §2.1 (free and clear), §3.3, §4.1; APA §9.1, §9.4, §9.8.

**Issue:**
- Bill of Sale §2.1 repeats the "free and clear of… liens other than permitted liens" statement.
- §3.3 says "Seller shall remain responsible for all Excluded Liabilities," which reads as a free-standing covenant to Buyer.
- §4.1 imposes open-ended further-assurances duties "without further consideration."
- APA §9.8 makes Article IX the exclusive remedy only for "breach of the representations, warranties, covenants and agreements in this Agreement." A claim under the Bill of Sale's own text arguably escapes the cap, the basket, the 18-month survival and the escrow-first rule (§9.6).
- "Permitted liens" is used in APA §2.1, §4.5 and Bill of Sale §2.1, but is not defined in APA Article I.

**Why it matters:** Buyer gets a second route to the same claim without the APA's limits. Title is a Fundamental Representation (§4.5), so this overlap sits on the most sensitive rep.

**Fix and language:**
- Add to the Bill of Sale: *"Any claim arising out of this Bill of Sale, including Sections 2.1, 3.3 and 4.1, shall be brought solely under, and is subject to the limitations of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement."*
- Amend APA §9.8 to extend to the Ancillary Agreements, other than the TSA and the Escrow Agreement.
- Add to APA §1.1: *"'Permitted Liens' means the liens listed in Section 2.1 of the Disclosure Schedule,"* and conform §2.1 and §4.5.
- Bill of Sale §4.1: add *"at Buyer's cost, to the extent reasonable, and not later than 24 months after the Closing Date."*

### 7. Consents are a hard closing deliverable, though the efforts covenant and Bill of Sale §3.4 assume closing without them

**Where:** APA §3.2(e), §7.1(d), §6.3, §4.4, §4.3; Bill of Sale §3.4; TSA §3.6.

**Issue:**
- APA §3.2(e) requires Seller to deliver "the third-party consents listed in Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule."
- APA §7.1(d) makes delivery of all §3.2 items a condition to Buyer's obligation to close.
- Meanwhile §6.3 requires only "commercially reasonable efforts," and Bill of Sale §3.4 sets up a hold-and-pass-through mechanism for unconsented contracts, which only makes sense if Closing can occur without consents.
- The pass-through mechanism sits only in the Bill of Sale. The APA has none, and the Bill of Sale is delivered at Closing.
- Costs of obtaining consents are not allocated in the APA or Bill of Sale, though TSA §3.6 allocates them to Buyer.
- Bill of Sale §3.4 has Buyer perform post-closing obligations but gives Seller no indemnity.
- §4.4 also states a flat rep that Schedule 4.4 lists "each consent required."

**Why it matters:** One missing consent gives Buyer a free walk right or renegotiation leverage, and Seller's remedy is to chase consents at its own expense.

**Fix and language:**
- §3.2(e): *"…the third-party consents listed in Part A of Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule that have been obtained as of the Closing."* Business to define Part A (key consents only).
- §7.1(d): *"delivery of the items in Section 3.2 (other than Section 3.2(e))."*
- §6.3: add *"Seller is not required to pay any consideration, commence any litigation or agree to any amendment to obtain a consent, and the failure to obtain any consent does not breach Section 4.3 or 4.4 or fail any condition in Article VII."*
- Move the §3.4 mechanism into the APA as new §2.9, and add to it: *"Buyer shall indemnify Seller for Losses arising from the holding, performance or non-assignment of any such contract after the Closing. All third-party costs of obtaining any consent shall be borne by Buyer."* This aligns with TSA §3.6.

### 8. Working capital adjustment (APA §2.7) is under-specified, and §2.3(b) is tied to it

**Where:** APA §2.7, §2.3(b), §2.6, §2.8, §6.2.

**Issue:**
- §2.7 sets a $2,150,000 target but does not define "net working capital," has no accounting principles or illustrative calculation, and has no estimated closing statement or collar.
- The accounting firm is not named, and the allocation of its fees is not stated.
- Buyer has 60 days to deliver the statement, but no consequence follows if it does not.
- No payment deadline or interest applies to the settlement payment.
- All books and records transfer to Buyer (§2.1(g)), and Seller has no express right to access them to review the statement.
- §2.3(b) assumes trade payables "reflected in the Closing statement under Section 2.7," but the statement is delivered up to 60 days after Closing. It is unclear what a "Closing statement" is at the Closing. If Buyer omits a payable, it stays with Seller as an Excluded Liability (§2.4).
- Nothing says whether the true-up may be recovered from the Escrow Amount, which is reserved for indemnity (§2.6(a)).
- Cash is excluded (§2.2), and the 12:01 a.m. effective time (§3.1) creates cut-off issues for same-day collections.

**Why it matters:** A shortfall payment is uncapped and uncollared. Buyer controls the numbers and the books.

**Fix and language:**
- Add a definition: *"'Net Working Capital' means the sum of inventory and accounts receivable included in the Purchased Assets, less the trade accounts payable included in the Assumed Liabilities, in each case calculated in accordance with the Accounting Principles in Schedule 2.7."*
- Add: *"If Buyer fails to deliver the statement within 60 days, the statement is deemed delivered at the target and no adjustment is owed."*
- Add: *"Buyer shall give Seller and its advisors reasonable access to the books, records and personnel of the Business during the review period."*
- Amend §2.3(b): *"…trade accounts payable of the Business incurred in the ordinary course and included in Net Working Capital as finally determined."*
- Add: *"Payment of any adjustment is due within five Business Days after final determination. Adjustment payments shall not be satisfied from, or replenish, the Escrow Funds. The accounting firm's fees shall be allocated in inverse proportion to success."* Name the firm.

### 9. No integration or non-reliance clause; §10.12 precedence clause is circular

**Where:** APA Article X (no integration clause), §10.12, §4, §9.8; Bill of Sale, TSA and Escrow Agreement (no precedence clauses).

**Issue:**
- Article X has no entire-agreement clause and no non-reliance or as-is disclaimer. Prior documents (NDA, term sheet, diligence presentations) are not superseded.
- Article IV reps are flat, and Buyer has diligence access under §6.2.
- §10.12's "except… specific subject matter" exception can swallow the rule (see Issues 1 and 3).
- None of the three ancillaries says the APA controls, so the precedence rule runs one way in a document the ancillary parties do not all sign (the Escrow Agent is not a party to the APA).

**Why it matters:** Without these provisions, extra-contractual statements become fraud or misrepresentation theories that sit outside the cap. Without a clear hierarchy, each ancillary becomes a possible vehicle for overriding the APA.

**Fix and language:**
- Add APA §10.15: *"Entire Agreement; Non-Reliance. This Agreement, the Disclosure Schedule and the Ancillary Agreements are the entire agreement of the Parties and supersede all prior agreements and understandings. Except for the representations and warranties expressly set forth in Article IV (as qualified by the Disclosure Schedule), Seller makes no representation or warranty, express or implied, and Buyer acknowledges that it has not relied on any other statement or information. The Purchased Assets are otherwise sold 'AS IS, WHERE IS.'"*
- Replace §10.12: *"…this Agreement controls, except that (a) the Escrow Agreement controls as to the rights, duties and protections of the Escrow Agent and (b) the Transition Services Agreement controls as to the performance of, and fees for, the Services. No Ancillary Agreement expands the Purchased Assets or Assumed Liabilities, or any representation, warranty, covenant or liability of Seller."*
- Add a mirror clause to each ancillary.
- Add anti-sandbagging language to Article IX (for example, no recovery for breaches Buyer knew of at Closing) and a mitigation duty. Both are customary seller asks, subject to Buyer's pushback.

### 10. TSA economics and credit exposure: cost recovery, funding, personnel and shared systems

**Where:** TSA §5.1, §5.2, §3.2, §3.5, Schedule A (Services 1–5), §2.2, §5.5; APA §6.4, §2.1.

**Issue:**
- **Cost recovery.** Fees "reflect Seller's fully loaded cost… without markup" (§5.1). Seller supplies five services for roughly $53,500 per month at cost, with no true-up and no cost-escalation protection. Migration cooperation under §3.5 reimburses only out-of-pocket costs, not staff time.
- **Credit and funding risk.** Invoices are monthly in arrears, payable in 30 days (§5.2). Seller therefore funds wages, payroll taxes and benefits for Service 2, and supplier purchases (linerboard, medium, starch) for Service 4, for up to about two months. Buyer's credit is not secured, and Seller has no suspension right for non-payment before breach and cure (§2.3).
- **Interest.** Late interest runs only on "undisputed" amounts (§5.5). Disputed amounts later resolved for Seller carry no interest.
- **Partial termination.** Buyer may terminate any Service "in whole or in part" on 30 days' notice (§2.2) with no wind-down costs, no repricing mechanism and no minimum.
- **Staffing.** Service 5 relies on "Seller's existing customer service personnel," and Services 1 and 3 on finance and IT staff. APA §6.4 has Buyer offer employment to listed employees. If the Disclosure Schedule §6.4 list includes the people who run these services, Seller cannot perform to the §3.2 standard.
- **Shared systems.** Service 3 hosts "Seller's ERP, email and order-management systems." APA §2.1 sweeps in "all assets used or held for use in the Business," and Seller keeps the Albany facility. If those systems are also used by Seller's retained operations, the APA does not say whether they are Purchased Assets, Excluded Assets or licensed. TSA §6.2 also says Service Recipient Data is owned by Buyer "as part of the Purchased Assets."
- **Payroll risk.** There is no Buyer responsibility for the accuracy of payroll inputs, and no indemnity for Seller's reliance on Buyer data.

**Why it matters:** At-cost pricing with arrears billing and no funding means Seller carries Buyer's credit and operational risk after the sale. If staff transfer, Seller faces a service-level breach it cannot cure.

**Fix and language:**
- **Funding.** *"Buyer shall fund Seller (or Seller's payroll provider), no later than three Business Days before each payroll date, the gross payroll, employer taxes and benefit premiums for Service 2 and, for Service 4, the cost of purchases at least two Business Days before Seller is obliged to pay the supplier. Seller has no obligation to perform a Service for which funding is outstanding."*
- **Suspension.** *"Seller may suspend a Service on five Business Days' notice if Buyer fails to pay undisputed amounts when due."*
- **Interest.** Extend §5.5 to disputed amounts later determined to be owing.
- **Inputs.** *"Buyer is responsible for the accuracy of all data and instructions it provides, and shall indemnify Seller for Losses arising from Seller's reliance on them in providing the Services."*
- **Staffing.** Add to APA §6.4 and TSA §3.1: *"The employees listed in Schedule A-1 shall be excluded from the offers under Section 6.4 of the Purchase Agreement until the end of the applicable Service; Buyer shall not solicit them during the Term and for six months afterward."*
- **Systems.** Confirm the treatment of the ERP, email and order-management systems with the client and add them to APA §2.2 or to a shared-asset license.
- **Cost increases.** Add a mechanism to adjust fees for pass-through cost increases and for out-of-scope work, and a markup. See Part III, item 4.

### 11. TSA liability interplay with the APA, and TSA §7.2 carve-out

**Where:** TSA §7.1–7.3; APA §2.4, §9.2(c), §9.4, §9.6.

**Issue:**
- **End-run through APA §9.2(c).** TSA §7.2 caps Seller's liability at Service Fees paid and payable, with exceptions for gross negligence and willful misconduct. But APA §2.4 makes every Seller liability not expressly assumed an "Excluded Liability," and §9.2(c) indemnifies Buyer for any Excluded Liability, including recovery against the Escrow Funds (§9.6). A Buyer claim for poor performance of Services could be pleaded as an Excluded Liability claim under the APA, and TSA §7.2 does not clearly foreclose it.
- **Section 6 carve-out.** The consequential-damages exclusion does not apply to "breach of Section 6" (§7.2). A data or confidentiality incident during Services 1, 3 or 5 then opens consequential damages, and the clause is not limited to Seller breaches. The §7.2 cap still applies to Seller (the cap's only exceptions are gross negligence and willful misconduct), so the structure is internally inconsistent.
- **Set-off.** Neither document bars Buyer from setting off TSA or APA claims against Service Fees.

**Why it matters:** Buyer could bypass the TSA's liability limits and recover from the escrow, which is for indemnity only.

**Fix and language:**
- Add to TSA §7.2: *"The rights and remedies under this Agreement are the exclusive remedy for any claim arising out of the Services. No such claim is an Excluded Liability, or a claim under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, or recoverable from the Escrow Funds."*
- Add a matching sentence to APA §2.4.
- Delete "or breach of Section 6" from the §7.2 exception, or replace it with a super-cap (for example, 2x total Service Fees) for data and confidentiality breaches.
- Add: *"Buyer shall pay all Service Fees without set-off or deduction."*

### 12. Seller loses records access; TSA data and confidentiality clauses compound it

**Where:** APA §2.1(g), §2.4, §2.7, §9.5; TSA §2.4(b), §6.1, §6.2(b)–(c).

**Issue:**
- All "books, records, customer lists and supplier lists relating to the Business" convey to Buyer (APA §2.1(g)). The APA contains no covenant giving Seller continuing access or a right to retain copies.
- Seller keeps pre-Closing taxes and employment liabilities (§2.4), must object to the working-capital statement (§2.7), and defends indemnity claims (§9.5). It needs those records.
- TSA §6.2(b)–(c) bars Seller from using Service Recipient Data "except to provide the Services" and requires return or destruction at Buyer's election within 30 days after the Term.
- TSA §6.1 limits use of the other side's non-public information to "perform or receive the Services."
- "Service Recipient Data" includes data Seller "generates," so it could capture Seller's own tax and accounting records.

**Why it matters:** Seller could be unable to prepare tax returns, defend claims or satisfy retention laws, and could breach the TSA if it did so.

**Fix and language:**
- Add APA §6.8: *"For seven years after the Closing, Buyer shall preserve the books and records included in the Purchased Assets and give Seller and its representatives reasonable access to, and the right to copy, them for Tax, accounting, litigation, Excluded Liability and Article IX purposes. Seller may retain copies of any records relating to the Business."*
- TSA §6.2: carve out *"Seller may retain and use copies of Service Recipient Data as required by law or its record-retention policies, or as reasonably necessary to prepare Tax returns, administer Excluded Liabilities, or defend or prosecute claims, subject to Section 6.1."*
- Add the same carve-out to the TSA §6.1 use restriction.

### 13. Excluded Assets create practical gaps: Albany access and HOLLIS

**Where:** APA §2.1(b), §2.2, §4.6, §4.10, §6.5; Bill of Sale §2.2, §1.1(b)(iv).

**Issue:**
- Seller retains the Albany manufacturing facility (APA §2.2), but Buyer buys the corrugators, converting equipment and tooling (§2.1(b)). The documents do not say where that equipment sits.
- Bill of Sale §2.2 delivers possession "at the locations where those assets are located on the Closing Date." If any equipment is at Albany, no lease, license, access right, removal period, risk-of-loss allocation or insurance obligation exists.
- Environmental and operating permits typically attach to the facility, while §2.1(f) conveys "all permits, to the extent transferable."
- HOLLIS is retained, but products and customer relationships may carry the brand. There is no transition license, supply or labeling arrangement.

**Why it matters:** Seller could end up as an unpaid landlord and bailee, with liability for Buyer's equipment and personnel on its site, or Buyer could claim an implied right.

**Fix and language:** This needs client input (Part III, item 3). If equipment is at Albany, add to the APA or a short ancillary: *"A license (or lease) of the Albany facility for a period of [__] months, solely to operate or remove the Purchased Assets, at Buyer's cost and risk, with Buyer to insure the premises and indemnify Seller."* Add a HOLLIS transition license or wind-down right if needed.

### 14. Non-compete (APA §6.5)

**Where:** APA §6.5, §10.7.

**Issue:**
- Three years, nationwide, covering the "manufacture or sale of corrugated packaging products," with a carve-out only for "ownership of the Excluded Assets in a manner that does not compete with the Business."
- Seller keeps a manufacturing facility, so the carve-out permits owning but arguably not operating it, and the HOLLIS mark.
- There is no carve-out for passive investments or for acquisitions of businesses with incidental competing revenue.
- The duty falls on Seller alone, without reference to Affiliates, and the scope is not tied to the Business's actual markets.

**Why it matters:** A broad restriction on a company whose remaining assets are a plant and a brand is likely to be unenforceable as to some of what it covers (§10.7 allows reformation), and it may strand Seller's residual value. Under New York law, courts generally scrutinize non-competes for reasonableness; sale-of-business covenants are treated more leniently, but scope and territory still matter.

**Fix and language:**
- Narrow to the Business's actual product lines and territories.
- Replace the carve-out: *"…except (i) the ownership and operation of the Excluded Assets, other than the manufacture and sale of products to customers of the Business; (ii) passive ownership of up to 5% of any public company; and (iii) the acquisition of any business of which less than [10]% of revenues derive from competing activities."*
- See Part III, item 2.

### 15. Closing logistics and date mismatches across the documents

**Where:** APA §3.1–3.3, §10.2, §1.1(i); TSA preamble, §2.1; Bill of Sale preamble; Escrow preamble, §2.4, §6.1–6.2, Schedule A.

**Issue:**
- **Escrow Agent signature.** The Escrow Agent signs the Escrow Agreement but is not in the APA closing deliveries (§3.2(c) and §3.3(b) cover only Seller and Buyer). Escrow Schedule A (authorized representatives, specimen signatures, wire instructions) is blank "to be completed before the Closing," and KYC deliverables (Escrow §6.1, §2.4 W-9s) have no APA covenant behind them. A Closing could stall on Escrow Agent onboarding.
- **Hard-coded dates.** The TSA, Bill of Sale and Escrow Agreement are each dated "as of November 20, 2026." APA §3.1 allows a different Closing Date by agreement, and §8.1(b) permits closing up to December 31, 2026. If Closing slips, those documents are mis-dated, and Bill of Sale §2.1 and the Escrow's definitions refer to the "Closing Date."
- **Notices.** APA §10.2 permits email notice but lists no email addresses and no copy to counsel. The ancillaries incorporate §10.2 (TSA §9.1, Bill of Sale §5.3, Escrow §7.4).
- **Effective time.** The 12:01 a.m. effectiveness (APA §3.1, Bill of Sale §2.1) conveys assets, including receivables, retroactively through a day Seller operated. This creates cut-off questions for cash and for Seller's last operating day.

**Fix and language:**
- Add to APA §3.2 and §3.3: *"(g) the Escrow Agreement duly executed by the Escrow Agent, with Schedules A and B completed, and the Know-Your-Customer information in Section 6.1 of the Escrow Agreement."*
- Change each ancillary's preamble to *"dated as of the Closing Date."*
- Add email addresses and a counsel copy to §10.2.
- Clarify the effective-time mechanics (cash and collections before the Closing are Seller's).

### 16. Escrow Agreement §§2.4, 5.4, 2.3, 5.5: tax, agent indemnity and investment

**Where:** Escrow §2.4, §5.4, §5.5, §2.3, Schedule B, Schedule C.

**Issue:**
- **Tax mismatch.** Buyer is treated as owner of the Escrow Funds and receives the Form 1099 on earnings (§2.4), yet "Escrow Funds" include earnings and are released to Seller (§1.1(c), §4.1). Buyer is taxed on income that Seller receives, and the document has no tax-distribution mechanism, which Buyer will likely request, reducing Seller's release. The APA does not address escrow tax treatment (APA §9.9 covers only indemnity payments). Seller's tax advisers should confirm the installment-sale and imputed-interest treatment of escrow releases.
- **Joint and several agent indemnity.** Seller and Buyer indemnify the Agent "jointly and severally" (§5.4), with 50/50 sharing between themselves only. Seller could be liable for 100% of an indemnity based on Buyer's conduct, with only a contribution claim against Buyer.
- **Fees.** Fees are split equally (§5.5). APA §10.1 says each Party bears its own costs, so the split is a business choice.
- **Investment.** Schedule B names an affiliated-looking Corbel money market fund, which is fine, but the alternative deposit account option in §2.3 would exceed FDIC limits on $1.84 million.

**Fix and language:**
- Add to §5.4: *"As between Seller and Buyer, Buyer shall indemnify Seller for any amount Seller pays under this Section 5.4 in excess of its share, and Seller's liability to the Escrow Agent is limited to its share except to the extent caused by Seller's own conduct."*
- Ask the tax team for input on §2.4 and on whether to allow a limited tax distribution.
- Delete the deposit-account alternative in §2.3.

## IV. Lower-priority and conforming points

- **Bring-down and schedule updates.** Reps are made at signing and at Closing (APA Art. IV lead-in), and Buyer's condition is accuracy "in all material respects" (§7.1(a)). There is no right to update the Disclosure Schedule for post-signing events. §7.1(c) "no Material Adverse Effect" has no measurement date. Add "since the date of this Agreement" and a limited update right.
- **Seller conditions.** Article VII gives Seller no condition on absence of legal prohibition or litigation. Consider adding.
- **Rep scope.** §4.9 (non-infringement) is unqualified by knowledge. §4.6 ("adequate for the uses") and §4.7 (unaudited interim statements "in accordance with GAAP," without a year-end-adjustment and footnote exception) are broad. Seek knowledge qualifiers and customary exceptions.
- **"Losses" definition** (§1.1(n)) includes penalties and fines and does not exclude punitive or consequential damages, lost profits or multiple-based damages. Seek exclusions, a mitigation covenant and a tax-benefit offset. §9.4(e) nets insurance proceeds only if "actually received."
- **Allocation (§2.8).** Buyer prepares the §1060 schedule, "reasonably acceptable to Seller," with no dispute mechanism or consistent-reporting covenant. Add accountant resolution and a covenant on Form 8594 filing.
- **Survival clause under New York law.** The last sentence of APA §9.1 lets a written claim survive "until finally resolved." Consider whether Seller prefers a requirement that Buyer commence suit within a stated period after notice. We should confirm how New York courts will read the survival language before we negotiate it.
- **Undefined terms.** "Indemnified Party" is used in §9.4(e) before its definition in §9.5. "Accounting Principles," "Fraud" and "Permitted Liens" are undefined.
- **Bill of Sale §5.6** has the New York forum clause but lacks the venue/inconvenient-forum waiver and jury waiver found in APA §10.9–10.10, TSA §9.4 and Escrow §7.3. Conform.
- **TSA §2.2** refers to refunding "advance" payments although fees are billed in arrears (§5.2). Delete or conform.
- **TSA §9.6 vs. APA §10.4.** The TSA permits assignment only to an Affiliate that acquires substantially all the Purchased Assets. The APA allows assignment to any Affiliate and collateral assignment to lenders. Conform, keeping Buyer liable.
- **Escrow §4.1 vs. APA §9.7.** APA refers to the "remaining Escrow Amount." The Escrow Agreement releases all Escrow Funds, including earnings. This favors Seller, so it should be preserved and the APA conformed.
- **TSA §5.3.** Add a rule that undisputed invoices are deemed accepted if Buyer does not notify within 20 days, and an escalation path for disputes.
- **Bill of Sale §4.2** (payments received after Closing are held in trust for Buyer) has no mirror image for Buyer's receipts relating to Excluded Assets. Add a reciprocal obligation and a right of set-off for amounts Buyer owes Seller.
- **Bulk-sale and tax notices.** I did not review any tax-clearance or bulk-sale requirements, as the folder is silent. Tax counsel should confirm whether any filing is needed before the November 20 Closing.
