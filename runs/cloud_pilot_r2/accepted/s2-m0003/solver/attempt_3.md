## 2. Executive summary

The ancillaries do not line up with the APA, and several gaps favor Buyer. The five most important points:

1. **The BoS redefines "Purchased Assets" and drops the Excluded Assets carve-out** (BoS § 1.1(b)). Read literally, it conveys the Albany facility (real property), the HOLLIS trademark and possibly cash. APA § 10.12 may let the BoS control on conveyance because it is the "specific subject matter" of that document.
2. **APA § 9.4(c) cancels the cap and basket for all representation claims.** Exempting "any claim under Section 9.2(a)" removes the 10% cap and the $184,000 basket from every rep claim, not just Fundamental Representations. That is almost certainly not the intended deal.
3. **Seller's exposure leaks outside Article IX.** The exclusive-remedy clause covers only the APA (§ 9.8). Buyer can pursue the BoS "free and clear" language and further assurances, and APA § 6.6 (a covenant to provide the TSA services), outside the cap, survival period and escrow mechanics.
4. **The TSA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive Delaware courts** (TSA § 9.3). Every other document uses New York law and New York County courts. That splits forums for what is really one transaction.
5. **Closing certainty.** APA § 3.2(e) makes delivery of all listed third-party consents a Seller closing deliverable and a condition to Buyer's obligation (§ 7.1(d)). The covenant in § 6.3 and the BoS § 3.4 mechanism both assume the deal can close without every consent.

## 3. Issues list

### TIER 1: Significant; resolve before going back to Buyer

#### 1. BoS redefines "Purchased Assets" and omits the Excluded Assets

- **Where:** BoS §§ 1.1(b), 2.1 and 2.3 versus APA §§ 1.1(r), 2.1, 2.2 and 10.12.
- **Problem:**
  - APA § 2.1 conveys assets "used or held for use in the Business, **other than the Excluded Assets**." APA § 2.2 retains Seller's cash, the Albany manufacturing facility and the HOLLIS trademark.
  - BoS § 1.1(b) uses a different definition. It covers "all assets, properties and rights of every kind and nature, whether **real**, personal or mixed," with no Excluded Assets exception. Its list includes "all intellectual property, including **trademarks, trade names**" (§ 1.1(b)(iv)).
  - BoS § 1.1 says terms defined in the BoS are the BoS's own. So the BoS definition governs the BoS and overrides the APA definition for purposes of the BoS.
  - BoS § 2.1 then "hereby irrevocably" conveys all Purchased Assets as so defined.
  - APA § 10.12 says the APA controls conflicts "except that each Ancillary Agreement controls as to its specific subject matter." The BoS's subject matter is the conveyance. Buyer would argue the BoS controls on exactly this point.
- **Why it matters to Seller:** An irrevocable present conveyance of Albany, HOLLIS and any cash would strip Seller of assets it kept in APA § 2.2. It would also undercut the § 6.5 non-compete carve-out, which depends on Seller owning the Excluded Assets. Buyer would then use the BoS § 2.3 power of attorney (Issue 6) to enforce the broader claim.
- **Fix:**
  1. Delete the BoS definition of "Purchased Assets" and the (i) to (vii) list. Cross-refer to the APA.
  2. Add an express carve-out.
  3. Amend APA § 10.12 so the APA controls on any conflict over what is conveyed.

  *BoS § 1.1(b) (replace):* "'Purchased Assets' has the meaning given in the Purchase Agreement and, for the avoidance of doubt, excludes the Excluded Assets (including Seller's cash, the Albany manufacturing facility and the HOLLIS trademark and all goodwill associated with it)."

  *BoS new § 2.5:* "Nothing in this Bill of Sale conveys, assigns or transfers any Excluded Asset or assumes or releases any Excluded Liability, and this Bill of Sale shall be construed accordingly."

  *APA § 10.12 (replace):* "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement controls, and no Ancillary Agreement shall expand, limit or modify the rights, obligations, representations, warranties, indemnities or limitations set forth in this Agreement, except that the Escrow Agreement controls as to the rights and duties of the Escrow Agent."

#### 2. APA § 9.4(c) nullifies the cap and basket for all representation breaches

- **Where:** APA §§ 9.4(a), 9.4(b), 9.4(c), 1.1(m), 9.1.
- **Problem:**
  - Section 9.4(c) says the cap and basket "shall not apply to any claim under **Section 9.2(a)**" or to fraud. Section 9.2(a) covers every rep breach, not just Fundamental Representations.
  - The effect is that rep claims are uncapped and have no basket. The basket in § 9.4(b) applies only to § 9.2(a) claims, so § 9.4(c) leaves it with no function.
  - What remains capped at 10% is § 9.2(b) (covenants) and § 9.2(c) (Excluded Assets and Liabilities).
  - "Fundamental Representations" is defined in § 1.1(m) but is not used in § 9.4. It appears only in the survival clause (§ 9.1).
  - The cap is 10% of $18,400,000, which is $1,840,000. That equals the Escrow Amount in § 2.6(a), which suggests the parties meant the escrow to be the recourse limit for general reps.
- **Why it matters:** As written, Seller faces uncapped, first-dollar exposure on every rep, including flat reps such as non-infringement (§ 4.9), asset condition (§ 4.6) and environmental compliance (§ 4.10). The cap-and-basket structure appears to be a drafting error. Buyer will likely correct it in the other direction, for example by making Excluded Liabilities uncapped.
- **Fix:** Limit the exceptions to Fundamental Representations and fraud. Make the escrow the exclusive source of recovery for general reps. Consider capping Fundamental Representations at the Purchase Price.

  *APA § 9.4(c) (replace):* "The limitations in Section 9.4(a) and Section 9.4(b) shall not apply to Losses arising from (i) any breach of or inaccuracy in a Fundamental Representation, or (ii) Fraud; provided that Seller's aggregate liability for all matters under Section 9.2 (including for Fundamental Representations, but excluding Fraud) shall not exceed the Purchase Price."

  *APA § 9.6 (add):* "Recovery for any breach of or inaccuracy in a representation or warranty (other than a Fundamental Representation) shall be limited to the funds then held by the Escrow Agent."

  *Also:* Define "Fraud" (actual and intentional misrepresentation of a representation in Article IV, excluding recklessness and constructive fraud). The APA uses the word undefined in §§ 9.4(c) and 9.8.
  *Also:* Add a reciprocal Buyer basket to § 9.4(d). Buyer's cap there applies only to § 9.3(a).
  *Also:* Define the basket as a true deductible (it reads as one now).
- **Trade-off:** Expect Buyer to ask for Excluded Liabilities and covenants to sit outside the cap. That is a reasonable concession if we hold the line on rep claims (see Section 5).

#### 3. Seller's exposure is not contained by Article IX: Ancillary Agreements and TSA covenant route

- **Where:** APA §§ 9.2(b), 9.8, 6.6, 1.1(n); BoS §§ 2.1, 4.1; TSA §§ 7.2, 7.3.
- **Problem:**
  - The exclusive-remedy clause (§ 9.8) limits remedies only for breach of "this Agreement." The cap, basket and survival period in Article IX are likewise tied to the APA. The BoS has no limitation, no survival period and no exclusive-remedy clause.
  - Buyer could sue directly on the BoS "free and clear of all liens" statement in § 2.1. That duplicates the APA § 4.5 title rep, which is a Fundamental Representation.
  - Buyer could also sue on the open-ended further-assurances covenant in BoS § 4.1.
  - APA § 6.6 is itself a covenant: "Seller shall provide certain services." A TSA service failure can be framed as a § 6.6 breach and then brought through § 9.2(b) and the escrow. That would bypass the TSA's consequential-damages exclusion and fee-based cap (TSA § 7.2).
  - "Losses" (§ 1.1(n)) has no exclusion for consequential, special, punitive or diminution-in-value damages. The TSA excludes consequential damages, but the APA does not.
- **Fix:**
  - Extend § 9.8 and Article IX to the BoS and Escrow.
  - State that TSA remedies are exclusively under the TSA.
  - Add a "no double recovery" clause.
  - Add a consequential-damages exclusion to the APA.
  - Add a clause to the BoS confirming it creates no representations beyond the APA.

  *APA § 9.8 (add at end):* "The foregoing applies equally to claims arising out of the Bill of Sale and the Escrow Agreement. Claims arising out of the Transition Services Agreement shall be governed exclusively by the Transition Services Agreement, and no breach or alleged breach of Section 6.6 or of the Transition Services Agreement shall give rise to a claim under Section 9.2 or be recoverable from the Escrow Funds."

  *APA § 9.4 (new (f)):* "No Party shall be liable under this Article IX for punitive or exemplary damages or, except to the extent paid to a third party, for consequential, special or indirect damages or lost profits."

  *BoS new § 5.9:* "This Bill of Sale is delivered pursuant to the Purchase Agreement and does not enlarge, modify or supplement any representation, warranty, covenant or indemnity in the Purchase Agreement. Any claim arising out of the transfer of the Purchased Assets is subject to Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, including its survival periods and limitations."

#### 4. TSA governing law and forum conflict with every other document

- **Where:** TSA § 9.3 versus APA §§ 10.8–10.9, BoS §§ 5.5–5.6, Escrow §§ 7.1–7.2; APA § 10.12.

#### 7. Escrow claim mechanics deviate from the APA and favor Buyer

- **Where:** Escrow §§ 3.1–3.6, 4.1–4.2, 4.3, 7.4, 1.1(d); APA §§ 9.4, 9.5, 9.7.
- **Problems:**
  1. *Deemed acceptance.* Under Escrow § 3.3, if no Objection Notice reaches the Escrow Agent within 30 days of Seller's receipt of a Claim Notice, the Escrow Agent pays the full claim on the fifth Business Day. The APA has no deemed-acceptance rule (§ 9.5), and the Escrow Agent has no duty to check what the APA says (Escrow § 5.1).
     - Notice by email with confirmation of transmission counts as given (Escrow § 7.4), and Seller's only notice address is its CEO (APA § 10.2).
     - Seller's Objection Notice to the Escrow Agent is "effective only on actual receipt" (Escrow § 7.4).
     - A missed email could therefore mean paying out money with no right to contest it in the escrow.
  2. *No basket or cap check.* The Escrow Agent pays on claims with no regard to the $184,000 basket (APA § 9.4(b)) or the cap.
  3. *Pending claims.* Escrow § 4.1 withholds "the aggregate amount of all claims stated in Claim Notices." A Claim Notice can state a "good-faith estimate" (§ 3.1(a)), so a large estimated claim can freeze the whole escrow at the Release Date. APA § 9.7 says "pending claims" and does not define them. Buyer has no deadline to supply support or to update the estimate (§ 3.5).
  4. *Slow release.* Disputes are resolved only by Joint Written Instructions or a non-appealable Final Order (§§ 1.1(d), 3.4). The APA has no covenant requiring Buyer to sign joint instructions when a claim is resolved or the Release Date arrives. Without one, Seller must litigate to a final non-appealable order.
  5. *Mismatch with APA survival.* Under APA § 9.1, a claim survives if asserted in writing before the survival period ends. Under Escrow § 4.1, only Claim Notices delivered to the Escrow Agent by the Release Date hold back funds. That works in Seller's favor if Buyer is careless, but Buyer will notice and fix it.
- **Fix:**
  - *Escrow § 3.1:* Require Buyer to certify that it delivered the Claim Notice to Seller at the § 10.2 address with a copy to Seller's counsel, and require the Escrow Agent to forward each Claim Notice to Seller.
  - *Escrow § 3.2:* Extend the objection period to 45 days.
  - *Escrow § 3.3:* Add, "Payment under this Section 3.3 is without prejudice to Seller's rights under the Purchase Agreement, and failure to deliver an Objection Notice is not an admission of liability."
  - *Escrow § 3.1(b):* Require each Claim Notice to state that aggregate Losses exceed the basket and to attach reasonable supporting detail.
  - *Escrow § 4.1:* Limit Pending Claims Amount to "the reasonable, good-faith estimate of Losses, supported in reasonable detail, with respect to claims that Buyer has asserted in compliance with Section 3.1."
  - *APA § 9.7 (add):* "Buyer and Seller shall deliver Joint Written Instructions within five Business Days after (i) any agreement as to, or final determination of, a claim, or (ii) the Release Date as to all amounts not subject to a Pending Claim."
  - Also add to the Escrow that the Escrow Funds cannot be applied to any amount owed under APA § 2.7 (working capital). APA § 2.6(a) limits the escrow to Article IX security, but the Escrow does not say so.

  - Narrow Service Recipient Data to data "of the Business relating to the Purchased Assets."
  - Add a segregation and no-access covenant.

  *TSA § 4.2 (add):* "Buyer shall not, and shall cause its personnel not to, access or use any information of Seller that does not relate to the Business, and shall promptly notify Seller of any such access. Seller may suspend access to the extent reasonably necessary to protect such information."

- **Fix:** Confirm where the equipment sits. If any is at Albany, add a short access, removal and occupancy covenant (fixed removal deadline, insurance, indemnity, Seller's right to charge reasonable costs). If Buyer operates there for a period, consider a limited license or lease.
- **Business input needed:** Where the Business's assets and operations are located (Section 5).

#### 15. TSA terms that put Seller at risk in performance

- **Where:** TSA §§ 2.2, 2.3, 2.4, 5.1, 5.2, 5.3, 6.2, 7.2, 7.3, 8.1, Schedule A.
- **Problems:**
  1. *Payroll funding (Schedule A, Service 2; § 5.2).* Seller processes payroll for "transferred employees" through its payroll provider but invoices only monthly in arrears. The Schedule lists only the Service Fee and the provider's charges as pass-throughs. Nothing requires Buyer to pre-fund wages, withholding and benefits. Buyer is the employer, but Seller's payroll provider and accounts are involved. Seller could be left carrying payroll cash or payroll tax exposure.
  2. *"Fully loaded cost, without markup" (§ 5.1).* Fees are fixed in Schedule A but described as intended to reflect Seller's fully loaded cost. That invites audit demands or fee-reduction claims, and gives Seller no right to recover actual costs above the fixed figures.
  3. *Setoff.* No clause prevents Buyer from withholding TSA fees against APA indemnity or working capital claims. Disputes are limited to a 20-day invoice dispute window with no stated effect (§ 5.3). Nothing lets Seller suspend services for non-payment.
  4. *Termination and data (§§ 2.3, 2.4(b), 6.2(c)).* Section 2.4(b) requires Seller to deliver all Service Recipient Data on termination, even if Buyer has not paid. Section 6.2(c) requires Seller to "return or destroy" the data within 30 days after the Term at Buyer's election, which conflicts with Seller's need to keep records for pre-Closing taxes, employment liabilities, Excluded Liabilities, indemnity defense and legal holds. The APA has no records-access or retention covenant for Seller.
  5. *Early termination (§ 2.2).* Buyer can terminate any Service on 30 days' notice. Seller has no right to recover stranded or wind-down costs, such as non-cancellable vendor commitments. The refund of "prepaid" fees assumes advance payment, but § 5.2 is monthly in arrears, so the sentence is inconsistent.
  6. *Buyer-side indemnity.* Section 7.3 covers only gross negligence or willful misconduct. Seller hosts Buyer's customer data and gives Buyer personnel access to Seller's systems (§ 4.2), but Buyer gives no indemnity for its own acts, its personnel, or third-party claims arising from Buyer's business.
  7. *Cap and carve-out (§ 7.2).* The cap on Seller's liability (fees paid and payable) is helpful, but the consequential-damages exception for breach of Section 6 is not matched by any limit on exposure from data incidents. Buyer's liability is not capped. Section 4.1 has no relief for Seller when Buyer fails to cooperate.
  8. *Force majeure (§ 8.1).* Fees are suspended for any affected Service even if Seller's fixed costs continue.
  9. *Missing protections.* No compliance-with-laws allocation, no right to subcontract beyond § 3.1 (which keeps Seller responsible), no Seller relief for Buyer-caused delay, no cost relief for migration time beyond out-of-pocket costs (§ 3.5), and no extension mechanic.
- **Fix (key language):**

  *TSA § 5.2 (add):* "For Service 2, Buyer shall transfer to the account designated by Seller, at least two Business Days before each payroll date, funds sufficient to pay all wages, withholding, employer taxes and benefit contributions for that payroll. Buyer, as employer, is responsible for all payroll tax filings and employment-related liabilities for the transferred employees."

  *TSA § 5.1 (replace second sentence):* delete it, or replace it with "The Service Fees are fixed. Seller has no obligation to adjust them by reference to its actual costs."

  *TSA § 5.3 (add):* "Buyer shall pay all amounts due under this Agreement without setoff, counterclaim or deduction, including for any claim under the Purchase Agreement. If Buyer fails to pay undisputed fees within ten days after notice, Seller may suspend the affected Services until payment."

  *TSA § 2.4(b) (add):* "...provided that Seller's obligation to deliver is conditioned on payment of all undisputed Service Fees then due."

  *TSA § 6.2(c) (add):* "Seller may retain copies of Service Recipient Data to the extent required by law, bona fide document-retention policy, tax or accounting requirements, or to defend or assert claims, subject to Section 6.1."

  *TSA § 7.3 (add):* "Buyer shall indemnify Seller against Losses from third-party claims arising from Buyer's operation of the Business, Buyer's instructions to Seller, or any act or omission of Buyer personnel with access under Section 4.2."

  *TSA § 4.1 (add):* "Seller is excused from any failure or delay to the extent caused by Buyer's failure to perform its obligations."

  *TSA § 2.2 (add):* "Buyer shall reimburse Seller's reasonable, documented costs of any non-cancellable commitment that Seller entered into to provide the terminated Service."
- **Business input needed:** Fee levels and markup, and capacity (Section 5).

#### 16. BoS covenants one-sided

- **Where:** BoS §§ 4.1, 4.2, 3.4; APA § 6.7.
- **Problems:**
  - Further assurances in BoS § 4.1 run only against Seller, are "without further consideration," have no end date, and are not limited to what is "reasonably required" in the same way as APA § 6.7 (which is mutual). Seller carries all costs of IP assignments and title certificates.
  - Section 4.2 requires Seller to hold post-Closing receipts "in trust" and remit within 10 Business Days, with no reciprocal duty for Buyer to remit receipts belonging to Seller (Excluded Assets, cash). No right to net amounts Buyer owes.
  - Section 3.4 has no end date, no indemnity and no cost allocation.
- **Fix:** Make §§ 4.1 and 4.2 mutual. Add reimbursement of Seller's out-of-pocket costs for further assurances beyond a de minimis level. Add a sunset (for example, 24 months) for § 4.1. Let Seller deduct amounts Buyer owes under the Transaction Documents from pass-throughs.

  *BoS § 4.2 (add):* "If Buyer receives any payment in respect of any Excluded Asset, Buyer shall hold it in trust for Seller and pay it over within ten Business Days after receipt."

#### 17. Purchase price allocation and transfer taxes

- **Where:** APA §§ 2.8, 10.1, 9.9.
- **Problem:** Buyer prepares the Section 1060 allocation within 90 days after Closing. Seller's consent is limited to "reasonably acceptable." There is no dispute mechanism, no consistent-reporting covenant, and no clarity on what is allocated to the non-compete (§ 6.5) or to inventory and receivables, which can affect Seller's tax character. Transfer taxes are split 50/50 (§ 10.1).
- **Fix:** Add an accountant-based dispute procedure (the same one as § 2.7). Add a covenant that both parties file consistently (including Form 8594). Consider shifting transfer taxes to Buyer, or capping Seller's share. Please have tax review the allocation.
- **Business input needed:** Transfer taxes and allocation priorities (Section 5).

#### 18. Escrow mechanics and housekeeping

- **Where:** Escrow §§ 2.4, 5.4, 5.5, 4.4, 6.1, 6.2, 7.4, Schedules A–C; APA §§ 2.6, 3.2(f), 10.2.
- **Problems:**
  - *Tax and earnings.* Buyer is treated as owner of the Escrow Funds for tax purposes, with earnings reported to Buyer (§ 2.4). The Escrow Funds include earnings, which go to Seller on release (§§ 1.1(c), 4.1). Buyer will pay tax on income it does not receive and will likely ask for a tax distribution from the fund. Please agree how to handle this now. The APA says nothing.
  - *Joint and several.* Seller is jointly and several liable to the Escrow Agent for indemnity (§ 5.4). The 50/50 allocation between Seller and Buyer is helpful, with a "conduct" override. Seller should add a right of contribution against Buyer and a carve-out for Buyer-caused matters.
  - *Fees.* The agent's fees are split 50/50 (§ 5.5), which is consistent with APA § 10.1. The annual administration fee is payable "at Closing and on each anniversary" (Schedule C), so Seller's share is a known amount. Confirm this is acceptable.
  - *Blank schedules.* Schedule A (authorized representatives, specimen signatures, call-back numbers, wire instructions) is blank, and Seller must complete it before Closing. Wire instructions are a fraud risk. Corbel's KYC (§ 6.1) will take time. Seller's designated account under APA § 2.6(b) is due two Business Days before Closing.
  - *W-9 timing.* Escrow § 2.4 requires each party's W-9 **before** the Closing Date. APA § 3.2(f) requires Seller's W-9 as a Closing deliverable to Buyer.
  - *Notices.* There is no counsel copy for Seller anywhere in the notice clauses (APA § 10.2, Escrow § 7.4, TSA § 9.1).
  - *Duplicate definition.* "Escrow Amount" is defined in both APA § 2.6(a) and Escrow § 2.1, in matching terms. It would be cleaner to define it once.
  - *Escrow Agent not required to sign.* APA §§ 3.2 and 3.3 require only Seller and Buyer to deliver executed copies, not the Escrow Agent. Corbel's signature should be a closing deliverable or at least a condition.
- **Fix:** Address these in the Escrow draft and the APA closing deliverables. Add a counsel copy line for Seller in each notice clause. Agree a tax distribution mechanism or confirm that earnings go to Seller net of Buyer's tax.
