## I. CRITICAL ISSUES

### Issue 1: Buyer's Notice of Disagreement Was Delivered 8 Days Late

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 2.4(b); Post-Closing Side Letter, Recital B and § 1.1

**Explanation:** Section 2.4(b) requires Buyer to deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within forty-five (45) days after delivery of the Closing Statement." Per the Side Letter's Recital B, Seller delivered the Closing Statement by email on March 30, 2026, and Buyer delivered its Notice of Disagreement by email on May 22, 2026. Counting from March 30: one day remaining in March + 30 days in April + 14 days in May = 45 days, making the deadline **May 14, 2026**. Buyer's notice was therefore **8 days late**.

Even under the most favorable reading of the SPA's notice provision (§ 11.2), which deems email notice sent after 5:00 p.m. effective the next business day, the deadline would shift to May 15 at the latest. The notice remains untimely.

Section 2.4(b) states: "If Buyer does not deliver a Notice of Disagreement within that period, the Closing Statement will be final and binding on the parties." Seller's Closing Statement calculated Closing Net Working Capital at $2,318,400 (exceeding the $2,150,000 Target by $168,400). Buyer's position is that NWC is approximately $1,118,400 (below Target by $1,031,600). The swing is approximately **$1,200,000**. If the notice is deemed untimely, the Closing Statement is final and binding, and Buyer owes Seller the $168,400 excess rather than collecting the $1,031,600 shortfall.

The Side Letter (dated May 26, 2026) records the disputed items and schedules a meeting, but § 4.1 expressly states it "does not amend, modify or supplement the Agreement." It does not waive the deadline, deem the notice timely, or otherwise cure the defect.

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately seek a written waiver or ratification from Seller confirming that the Notice of Disagreement is effective despite the 8-day delay. Frame the request around the fact that Seller has already engaged substantively (the Side Letter acknowledges the items and schedules a meeting), which supports an argument of implied waiver or estoppel.
- If Seller resists, prepare a litigation analysis under Delaware law on whether (a) the 45-day period is a condition or a claim-filing deadline, (b) Seller's conduct under the Side Letter constitutes a waiver, and (c) whether the "final and binding" language is enforceable as a liquidated consequence of a procedural default.
- Preserve all evidence of when the Closing Statement was actually received (email headers, transmission confirmations) in case the delivery date is disputed.

### Issue 2: Structural Mismatch Between Escrow Release Date and General Representation Survival Period

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement §§ 9.1, 9.6(a); Escrow Agreement § 4.2

**Explanation:** Section 9.1 provides that General Representations survive for **18 months** after the Closing Date (until September 2, 2027). Section 9.6(a) provides that Losses for breaches of General Representations "shall be recovered **solely and exclusively** from the Indemnity Escrow Fund." However, the Escrow Agreement § 4.2 provides that the Release Date is **9 months** after the Closing Date (December 2, 2026), at which point the Escrow Agent disburses all remaining funds to Seller (less Pending Claim Amounts).

This creates a **9-month gap** (December 2, 2026 through September 2, 2027) during which:
- General Representations are still within their survival period;
- A breach could be discovered and a claim asserted;
- But the exclusive source of recovery (the Escrow Fund) has already been released to Seller.

In practical terms, if Kateix discovers a breach of a General Representation (e.g., an undisclosed liability under § 4.8, a material contract issue under § 4.5, or a healthcare compliance problem under § 4.12) in month 10 through month 18, it has **no remedy** because the escrow is gone and § 9.6 bars recovery from any other source for General Rep claims.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate an amendment to either (a) extend the Escrow Release Date to 18 months to align with the General Rep survival period, or (b) modify § 9.6 to permit recovery directly from Seller for General Rep breaches that arise after the escrow release.
- If Seller resists, at minimum ensure that any claims that are reasonably identifiable before the Release Date are asserted via Claim Notice under Escrow Agreement § 5.1 so they become "Pending Claim Amounts" held back from release.
- As a near-term action, conduct a focused review of all General Representations now to identify any potential breaches before December 2, 2026, and file Claim Notices for any that are found.

### Issue 3: Pellucid Consent Not Required as a Closing Deliverable

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement §§ 3.2(e), 6.4; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.5(b) § 5.1

**Explanation:** Schedule 4.5(b) § 5.1 discloses that the Pellucid Network Participation Agreement (representing approximately **38% of the Company's net patient revenue** for FY 2025) treats a change of control as an assignment requiring **prior written consent** of Pellucid. If the change of control occurs without consent, Pellucid may terminate on 30 days' written notice.

However, the SPA's closing deliverables in § 3.2(e) require only "the third-party consents described in Section 6.4," and § 6.4 addresses **only landlord consents** under the Clinic Leases. The Pellucid consent is not a closing deliverable, not a condition to closing, and not separately represented. The deal closed on March 2, 2026, sign-and-close simultaneously (Recital C; § 3.1), with no mechanism to confirm that Pellucid consented.

If Pellucid has not consented, it has a contractual right to terminate the agreement within 30 days of the change of control, potentially as early as April 1, 2026. Loss of 38% of revenue would be catastrophic to the business Kateix just acquired.

The representation in § 4.5(c) that each Material Contract is "in full force and effect" was made as of the Closing Date, when the contract was technically still in force (Pellucid had not yet exercised its termination right). This makes a post-closing indemnification claim for breach of representation difficult to sustain.

**Recommendation:**
- **Immediately** confirm whether Pellucid's written consent was obtained before or at closing. Check the data room, closing files, and any correspondence with Seller.
- If consent was not obtained, contact Pellucid urgently to seek retroactive consent. Given that 38% of revenue is at stake, this should be treated as an emergency.
- If Pellucid threatens or issues a termination notice, evaluate: (a) whether the termination right has been waived by Pellucid's conduct (e.g., continued billing and payment after the change of control); (b) whether Seller breached § 4.5(c) by representing the contract was "in full force and effect" when it knew consent had not been obtained; and (c) whether the claim can be funded from the escrow before the Release Date.
- For future transactions, ensure that all change-of-control consents identified in Schedule 4.5(b) are expressly listed as closing deliverables or conditions.

### Issue 4: Transition Services Agreement – Uncapped, One-Sided Indemnity in Favor of Seller

**Location:** Transition Services Agreement §§ 8.1, 8.2, 9.1

**Explanation:** The TSA indemnification structure is severely asymmetric:

- **Provider's (Seller's) indemnity to Recipient (Kateix) (§ 8.1):** Limited to third-party claims "caused by the **gross negligence or willful misconduct** of Provider." This is a high threshold.

- **Recipient's (Kateix's) indemnity to Provider (Seller) (§ 8.2):** Covers "all Losses arising out of or relating to any third-party claim arising out of or relating to the Services **or the operation of the Clinics**, including any claim by any patient, payer or Governmental Authority." This is essentially unlimited in scope and standard.

- **Cap (§ 9.1):** The liability cap (6 months of Service Fees = $510,000) expressly **excludes** "obligations under Section 8.2." Kateix's indemnity to Seller is therefore **uncapped**.

The phrase "the operation of the Clinics" is extraordinarily broad. During the 9-month transition, any patient injury claim, payer audit, or government investigation that names Seller as a party (even perfunctorily) could trigger Kateix's uncapped indemnity obligation. Since Kateix now owns and operates the Clinics, it is effectively insuring Seller against the operational risks of Kateix's own business.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate an amendment to § 8.2 to (a) limit Kateix's indemnity to claims "caused by the negligence or willful misconduct of Recipient, the Company, or their personnel," mirroring the fault-based standard in § 8.1; and (b) delete the phrase "or the operation of the Clinics" or replace it with "or the operation of the Clinics to the extent caused by Recipient's acts or omissions."
- Alternatively, include § 8.2 obligations within the § 9.1 cap (i.e., delete the express exclusion of Section 8.2 from the cap).
- If Seller resists, consider whether the TSA can be terminated early under § 10.2 (termination for convenience on 30 days' notice) to limit the exposure window, though this should be weighed against the operational need for transition services.

## II. SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### Issue 5: Transition Services Agreement – Unrestricted Delegation by Provider

**Location:** Transition Services Agreement § 12.3

**Explanation:** Section 12.3 provides: "Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate the performance of any of the Services in whole or in part, to **any Person** without the consent of Recipient."

The Services include revenue-cycle and billing (Schedule A.1), PM System hosting and patient records (A.2), and IT support (A.3). These involve access to protected health information and direct interaction with payers. Allowing Seller to delegate to any Person—without Kateix's consent—creates:
- **HIPAA risk:** A subcontractor may not be a covered entity or business associate with appropriate safeguards.
- **Quality risk:** An unknown subcontractor may not maintain the service levels the Clinics require.
- **Payer compliance risk:** Billing errors by an unvetted subcontractor could trigger payer audits or overpayment demands.

Compare with the SPA's assignment provision (§ 11.6), which requires consent for assignment (with a carve-out for collateral assignment to lenders). The TSA's unrestricted delegation is inconsistent with the risk profile of the services.

**Recommendation:**
- Amend § 12.3 to require that any delegatee (a) enter into a business associate agreement with the Company if it will access Patient Data, (b) maintain insurance and compliance standards no less protective than those in the TSA, and (c) be reasonably acceptable to Recipient (consent not to be unreasonably withheld).
- Add a requirement that Provider remain liable for the acts and omissions of any delegatee as if they were its own.

### Issue 6: Seller Note – Prohibition on Set-Off

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note § 3.1; Stock Purchase Agreement § 9.6

**Explanation:** Note § 3.1 requires all payments "in full, without set-off, counterclaim or deduction." SPA § 9.6 further provides that for General Representation breaches, "Buyer shall have no right of set-off against the Seller Note for those Losses."

The combined effect is that Kateix must make full Note payments ($2,000,000 per year beginning March 2, 2028, plus quarterly interest) even if:
- The NWC adjustment results in Seller owing Kateix money (under SPA § 2.4(e), Seller must "pay the shortfall to Buyer" by wire—this is a separate payment obligation, not a set-off, so this is manageable);
- Kateix has an indemnification claim against Seller that is unpaid.

While the NWC true-up is structured as a separate wire payment (not a set-off), any other amount Kateix is owed from Seller (e.g., a covenant breach indemnity under § 9.2(b) that exceeds the escrow) cannot be netted against Note payments. Kateix must pay the Note in full and then pursue a separate collection action against Seller.

**Recommendation:**
- This is difficult to change post-closing given the Note is already executed. However, if the NWC dispute resolves in Kateix's favor, ensure Seller's payment obligation under § 2.4(e) is tracked as a separate receivable and that Kateix does not inadvertently accept a "set-off" characterization that could be used against it later.
- For the $1.2M NWC dispute specifically, if Kateix prevails, the recovery should come from Seller by wire per § 2.4(e), not from the escrow (since NWC disputes are carved out of the exclusive remedy in § 9.7 and resolved under § 2.4).
- Monitor Seller's financial condition. If Seller is unlikely to pay a NWC shortfall or indemnity obligation, Kateix's lack of set-off rights against the Note is a meaningful credit risk.

### Issue 7: Seller Note – Title References "Subordinated" but No Subordination Language in Body

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note, title page and full text

**Explanation:** The Note is titled "SECURED-FREE **SUBORDINATED** PROMISSORY NOTE." However, the body of the Note contains no subordination provision. There is no section stating that the Note is subordinated to any other debt of Kateix or the Company, no recognition of a senior lender's rights, and no intercreditor mechanics.

This discrepancy creates ambiguity:
- If the Note was intended to be subordinated to future Company debt (e.g., a term loan Kateix might obtain to refinance), the subordination is not documented and may be unenforceable.
- If the title is simply a drafting error, it could confuse a court or a future lender reviewing the Note.
- A future lender providing financing to Kateix or the Company may rely on the "subordinated" label in the title and refuse to extend credit, or may demand a separate subordination agreement.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm with the deal team whether subordination was intended. If not, execute a correction agreement or a side letter clarifying that the word "Subordinated" in the title is a scrivener's error and the Note is not subordinated to any other obligation.
- If subordination was intended (e.g., to a specific lender), the subordination terms must be documented in a separate agreement or added to the Note by amendment.

### Issue 8: Only One of Fourteen Landlord Estoppels in the Folder

**Location:** Landlord Estoppel Certificate (Clinic 1 only); Stock Purchase Agreement § 6.4; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9

**Explanation:** The SPA § 6.4 represents that "Seller delivered to Buyer at the Closing the consents of the landlords under the Clinic Leases to the change in ownership of the Company." Schedule 4.9 lists **14 Clinic Leases** across 14 different landlords. The folder contains only one estoppel and consent certificate—for Clinic 1 (Alamo Heights / Broadway Crossing Retail, LP).

While the SPA representation confirms all 14 were delivered, the absence of the other 13 from the working file creates:
- A documentation gap if the file is reviewed by a lender, insurer, or in litigation.
- Inability to verify the terms of the other consents (e.g., whether any landlord imposed conditions, fees, or additional security).
- Risk that one or more consents were not actually obtained, which would be a breach of § 6.4 and potentially trigger a landlord's right to treat the transfer as an unauthorized assignment.

**Recommendation:**
- Request that all 14 landlord estoppel/consent certificates be collected and placed in the permanent transaction file.
- Review each for: (a) confirmation of consent; (b) any conditions or fees imposed; (c) any SNDA or subordination; (d) any reservation of rights.
- If any consent is missing or conditional, assess the exposure and consider whether a § 9.2(b) indemnification claim (covenant breach) is available, noting that such claims are recoverable from the escrow first and then directly from Seller under § 9.6.

## III. MODERATE ISSUES

### Issue 9: Transition Services Agreement – Low Liability Cap for Provider

**Location:** Transition Services Agreement § 9.1

**Explanation:** Provider's (Seller's) aggregate liability under the TSA is capped at "the total Service Fees paid in the six (6) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim." At $85,000/month, the maximum cap is **$510,000**.

Given that the PM System hosts all patient records for 14 clinics and Provider performs all billing and payer submissions, a significant data breach, billing error, or system outage could generate losses well in excess of $510,000. The cap excludes only § 8.2 obligations (Kateix's indemnity to Provider) and fraud.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate to increase the cap to at least 12 months of Service Fees ($1,020,000) or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $2,000,000).
- Add carve-outs from the cap for (a) breaches of § 6.2 (Patient Data safeguards) and (b) Provider's failure to maintain required insurance.
- Confirm that Provider carries adequate cyber liability and professional liability insurance for the duration of the Term.

### Issue 10: Escrow Tax Treatment – Kateix Taxed on Escrow Earnings

**Location:** Escrow Agreement § 3.3

**Explanation:** "Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds for income Tax purposes until disbursement, and all earnings on the Escrow Funds will be reported as income of Buyer."

Kateix will receive a 1099-INT (or similar) for earnings on the $3,000,000 escrow and must include that income in its taxable income, even though the funds are security for Seller's indemnification obligations and may ultimately be disbursed to Seller. If the escrow earns, for example, 4% annually, Kateix would be taxed on approximately $120,000 of interest that it may never retain.

**Recommendation:**
- This is a standard (if economically unfavorable) provision and is difficult to change post-closing. Ensure Kateix's tax department is aware of the 1099 reporting and can claim any applicable deduction or credit.
- If the escrow is invested in a money market fund (permitted under § 3.1), the earnings may be characterized differently for tax purposes. Confirm the investment vehicle with the Escrow Agent.

### Issue 11: Seller Note – Cross-Default Triggered by Company Debt

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note § 6.1(f)

**Explanation:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker or the **Company** defaults in the payment of any indebtedness for borrowed money in an aggregate principal amount of more than $2,500,000, and the default results in the acceleration of that indebtedness."

Although the Company's existing debt (Granite Plains Bank loan and Ridgeway Equipment Finance agreement) was paid off at closing (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.5(a) §§ 4.11–4.12), the Company may incur new debt in the future (e.g., a working capital line of credit, equipment financing, or an acquisition loan). If the Company defaults on more than $2.5M of new debt and it is accelerated, the entire $6M Note becomes an Event of Default, potentially accelerating the Note as well.

**Recommendation:**
- Ensure the Company's credit facilities (if any are established post-closing) include adequate notice and cure periods to avoid technical defaults.
- Consider whether the $2.5M threshold is appropriate for a 14-clinic dental practice. If the Company is likely to borrow more than $2.5M, negotiate to raise the threshold or add a materiality qualifier.
- Track the Company's debt levels and compliance with any covenants in its credit agreements.

### Issue 12: Seller Note – Restriction on Company Stock or Asset Sales

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note § 5(c)

**Explanation:** Kateix covenants not to "sell, transfer or otherwise dispose of all or substantially all of the shares of capital stock of the Company, or cause or permit the Company to sell all or substantially all of its assets, unless this Note is repaid in full at or before the closing of that transaction."

This restricts Kateix's ability to:
- Sell the Company as a going concern (e.g., in a portfolio sale or strategic exit) without first repaying the $6M Note;
- Conduct a major asset sale (e.g., divesting underperforming clinics) if it constitutes "substantially all" assets.

The phrase "all or substantially all" is not defined, creating uncertainty about what percentage of assets or clinics would trigger the restriction.

**Recommendation:**
- If Kateix has a planned exit strategy or restructuring that could involve a sale of the Company or a significant portion of its assets, model the cost and timing of repaying the Note.
- Consider negotiating a definition of "substantially all" (e.g., 80% or more of fair market value of assets) to provide clarity.
- Note that the prepayment provision (§ 3.4) allows Kateix to prepay without premium or penalty, so the economic cost is limited to the time value of money.
