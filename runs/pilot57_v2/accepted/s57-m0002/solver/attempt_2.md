## Issue 1: Escrow Release Date Precedes General Representation Survival Period

**Location:** Escrow Agreement §4.2 (Release Date = 9 months post-Closing); SPA §9.1 (General Representations survive 18 months); SPA §9.6 (General Rep breaches recoverable *solely and exclusively* from the Indemnity Escrow Fund).

**Why it matters:** The Escrow Agreement releases all remaining funds to Seller on December 2, 2026 (nine months after the March 2, 2026 Closing). However, Kateix has until September 2, 2027 (eighteen months) to assert claims for breaches of General Representations. SPA §9.6 provides that those claims may be recovered *only* from the Indemnity Escrow Fund. Once the escrow is released at month nine, Kateix has no security and, by the plain terms of §9.6, no source of recovery for General Rep breaches discovered in months ten through eighteen. This creates a nine-month window in which Kateix is effectively without a remedy for the most common category of post-Closing indemnification claims (e.g., undisclosed liabilities under §4.8, healthcare compliance issues under §4.12, employee matters under §4.13).

**Recommendation:** Amend Escrow Agreement §4.2 to align the Release Date with the 18-month survival period for General Representations (i.e., September 2, 2027), or alternatively amend SPA §9.6 to permit recovery of General Rep losses from Seller directly (not solely from the escrow) after the Release Date. If Seller resists extending the full escrow, a compromise would be to release 50% at nine months and hold the remaining 50% until eighteen months.

## Issue 2: Notice of Disagreement Appears to Be Late Under SPA §2.4(b)

**Location:** SPA §2.4(b) (45-day dispute window); Post-Closing Side Letter, Recital B (Closing Statement delivered March 30, 2026; Buyer Notice delivered May 22, 2026).

**Why it matters:** The Closing Statement was delivered on March 30, 2026. SPA §2.4(b) provides that Buyer must deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within forty-five (45) days after delivery of the Closing Statement," and that "[i]f Buyer does not deliver a Notice of Disagreement within that period, the Closing Statement will be final and binding on the parties." Counting from March 30, the 45th day falls on May 14, 2026. The Buyer Notice was delivered on May 22, 2026—eight days after the deadline. On its face, this means Seller's Closing Statement (which calculates NWC at $2,318,400 rather than the ~$1,118,400 Kateix asserts) is final and binding, and Kateix may owe Seller the difference rather than the reverse.

The Post-Closing Side Letter (dated May 26, 2026) records the dispute and schedules a meeting, but §4.1 expressly states the letter "does not amend, modify or supplement the Agreement." Seller's countersignature of the side letter could support an argument of implied waiver or estoppel, but it is not an express waiver of the §2.4(b) deadline, and Seller has already stated in §1.2 that it "has not accepted any of the items."

**Recommendation:** (a) Immediately send a written communication to Seller expressly requesting that Seller waive the §2.4(b) deadline and confirm that the Closing Statement is not final and binding, or (b) if Seller refuses, prepare a litigation hold and assess the strength of an estoppel/waiver argument based on Seller's participation in the side letter and the scheduled June 12 meeting. In the longer term, ensure the side letter is amended to include an express waiver by Seller of the §2.4(b) deadline. The $1.2 million delta makes this the highest-priority item.

## Issue 3: TSA §8.2 Indemnification by Recipient Is Overly Broad and Uncapped

**Location:** TSA §8.2 (Recipient indemnifies Provider for "any third-party claim arising out of or relating to the Services **or the operation of the Clinics**, including any claim by any patient, payer or Governmental Authority"); TSA §9.1 (liability cap excludes §8.2 obligations).

**Why it matters:** This provision requires Kateix to indemnify Seller for virtually any third-party claim connected to clinic operations—patient malpractice, payer recoupments, regulatory actions—regardless of who caused the underlying issue. This directly conflicts with the SPA's allocation of risk: under SPA §9.2(a) and §7.1, Seller is supposed to indemnify Kateix for pre-Closing breaches of representation and pre-Closing taxes. A pre-Closing billing error that triggers a payer recoupment post-Closing would, under TSA §8.2, require Kateix to indemnize *Seller* for the claim, even though the SPA would require Seller to indemnify Kateix. The result is a circular or net-zero indemnity that effectively strips Kateix of its SPA protection.

Compounding the problem, TSA §9.1 carves §8.2 out of the liability cap, so Kateix's exposure under this provision is unlimited.

**Recommendation:** Amend TSA §8.2 to limit Recipient's indemnity to third-party claims *caused by* Recipient's or the Company's negligence, willful misconduct, or breach of the TSA (mirroring the structure of §8.1). Delete the phrase "or the operation of the Clinics" or replace it with "or Recipient's operation of the Clinics after the Closing." Add §8.2 to the list of capped obligations in §9.1, or at minimum cap it at a stated dollar amount.

## Issue 4: Pellucid Consent Not Included in SPA Closing Deliverables

**Location:** SPA §3.2(e) (Seller to deliver "the third-party consents described in Section 6.4"); SPA §6.4 (covers only "the consents of the landlords under the Clinic Leases"); Disclosure Schedule §5.1(a)–(b) (Pellucid agreement requires prior written consent for change of control; without consent, Pellucid may terminate on 30 days' notice).

**Why it matters:** The Pellucid Network Participation Agreement accounts for approximately 38% of the Company's net patient revenue (Disclosure Schedule §4.1). The Disclosure Schedule identifies that a change of control requires Pellucid's prior written consent and that, absent consent, Pellucid may terminate on 30 days' notice. However, SPA §6.4—and therefore the closing deliverable in §3.2(e)—references only landlord consents. There is no contractual obligation on Seller to deliver the Pellucid consent at Closing. If the consent was not obtained, the Company is operating in breach of the Pellucid agreement, and 38% of revenue is exposed to termination on 30 days' notice.

**Recommendation:** Confirm immediately whether the Pellucid consent was in fact obtained and delivered. If not, (a) require Seller to obtain it as a post-Closing covenant with a specific deadline, (b) add the Pellucid consent (and any other consents identified in Schedule 4.5(b)) to the SPA's closing deliverables via amendment or side letter, and (c) assess whether the absence of the consent constitutes a breach of Seller's representation in SPA §4.5(b) (that each listed contract requires consent) or §4.4 (no conflicts/consents), which would support an indemnification claim.

## Issue 5: No Set-Off Against the Seller Note for Indemnification Losses

**Location:** SPA §9.6 (Buyer "shall have no right of set-off against the Seller Note for those Losses" [General Rep breaches]); Seller Note §3.1 (Maker shall pay "without set-off, counterclaim or deduction").

**Why it matters:** Kateix is obligated to pay Seller $6,000,000 over three years via the Seller Note. If Seller breaches a General Representation (e.g., an undisclosed liability surfaces), Kateix must recover *only* from the $3,000,000 escrow and must continue making full note payments to Seller without any reduction. This is a significant cash-flow and leverage problem: Kateix is paying Seller $2,000,000 per year while its sole remedy for the most common category of breach is a $3,000,000 escrow that (per Issue 1) may already be released. The restriction is one-directional—nothing prevents Seller from offsetting amounts it owes Kateix (e.g., a purchase price adjustment payment under SPA §2.4(e)) against the note.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate a carve-out in SPA §9.6 and/or Note §3.1 permitting Kateix to set off undisputed indemnification amounts (or amounts determined by the Independent Accountant or a court) against note payments. At minimum, permit set-off for Fundamental Representation breaches and covenant breaches (which under §9.6 are already recoverable from Seller directly), so the no-set-off restriction applies only to General Rep claims while the escrow is intact.

## Issue 6: TSA Permits Provider to Assign or Delegate Services Without Recipient's Consent

**Location:** TSA §12.3 ("Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate the performance of any of the Services in whole or in part, to any Person **without the consent of Recipient**").

**Why it matters:** The transition services include revenue-cycle billing (claims submission to Medicaid/CHIP and private payers), hosting of the PM System containing all Patient Data (PHI), and IT support for 14 clinics. If Seller assigns or delegates these services to a third party without Kateix's approval, Kateix could end up with an unknown provider handling sensitive patient data and government payer claims. HIPAA obligations, billing accuracy, and service continuity are all at stake. The one-way assignment right (Recipient cannot assign without Provider's consent) makes this particularly inequitable.

**Recommendation:** Amend TSA §12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment or delegation, with a specific prohibition on delegating Services involving Patient Data or government payer billing without consent. If Seller resists, at minimum require that any delegatee assume all of Provider's obligations under Articles 5, 6, 8, and 9 of the TSA and that Provider remains liable for the delegatee's performance.

## Issue 7: Indemnity Deductible Is a True Deductible, Not a Tipping Basket

**Location:** SPA §9.5(a) (Seller not liable for General Rep breaches "until the aggregate Losses for those breaches exceed $363,750 … and then only for Losses **in excess of** the Deductible").

**Why it matters:** The $363,750 deductible (7.5% of the $48.5M Base Purchase Price) operates as a true deductible: Kateix absorbs the first $363,750 of aggregate General Rep losses and recovers only the amount above that threshold. In a tipping-basket structure, once losses exceed the threshold, the entire amount is recoverable. For a 14-clinic dental practice, it is plausible that multiple moderate issues (e.g., a few undiscovered liabilities, a minor compliance gap, an employee benefit plan issue) could aggregate to $400,000–$500,000, in which case Kateix would recover only $37,000–$137,000 rather than the full amount.

**Recommendation:** If the deal is not yet closed (or if a post-Closing amendment is feasible), negotiate to convert the deductible to a tipping basket (i.e., once aggregate losses exceed $363,750, Seller is liable for the full amount from dollar one). If the deal is closed and the escrow is the sole source of recovery, this point is less actionable, but it should inform Kateix's decision-making about whether to pursue smaller claims.

## Issue 8: Seller Note Restricts Kateix's Ability to Sell or Restructure the Company

**Location:** Seller Note §5(c) (Maker shall not "sell, transfer or otherwise dispose of all or substantially all of the shares of capital stock of the Company, or cause or permit the Company to sell all or substantially all of its assets, unless this Note is repaid in full at or before the closing of that transaction").

**Why it matters:** Kateix acquired 14 dental clinics with $48.5M in enterprise value and a $6M seller-financed note. If Kateix wishes to sell the platform (or a substantial portion of it) within the three-year note term (through March 2030), it must repay the entire remaining note balance from sale proceeds before closing. This reduces net proceeds and could affect the ability to attract a buyer or structure a transaction. The restriction also applies to asset sales, which could limit Kateix's ability to divest underperforming clinics.

**Recommendation:** Negotiate an amendment to Note §5(c) to (a) raise the "substantially all" threshold (e.g., 80% of revenue or 12 of 14 clinics), (b) permit sales of individual clinics or small groups without triggering the repayment requirement, or (c) allow the note to be assumed by a qualified successor buyer with Seller's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld).

## Issue 9: Only One Landlord Estoppel in the File; SPA Requires All 14

**Location:** Landlord Estoppel Certificate (covers only Clinic 1 – Alamo Heights / Broadway Crossing Retail, LP); SPA §6.4 (Seller to deliver "the consents of the landlords under the **Clinic Leases**" [plural, all 14]); Disclosure Schedule §7 (lists 14 Clinic Leases).

**Why it matters:** The file contains an estoppel and consent from only one of the 14 landlords. SPA §6.4 and §3.2(e) require delivery of landlord consents for all Clinic Leases at Closing. If the remaining 13 consents were not delivered, there is a potential gap in the closing deliverables. Without estoppels, Kateix has no assurance that the other 13 leases are in good standing, that rent is current, that no defaults exist, or that the landlords have consented to the change of control. Any of the 14 leases could contain a change-of-control consent requirement (per Disclosure Schedule §5.4, each does), and failure to obtain consent could give a landlord termination or recapture rights.

**Recommendation:** Confirm that all 14 landlord consents were delivered at Closing. If any are missing, demand delivery immediately and assess whether the failure constitutes a breach of SPA §6.4 (a covenant, recoverable under §9.2(b) and not subject to the escrow-only restriction in §9.6). For leases where no estoppel was obtained, consider requesting estoppels post-Closing to confirm lease status.

## Issue 10: TSA Term Ends Simultaneously with Escrow Release, Compounding the Survival Gap

**Location:** TSA §2.1 (Term = 9 months post-Closing, i.e., through December 2, 2026); Escrow Agreement §4.2 (Release Date = 9 months post-Closing).

**Why it matters:** The TSA terminates at the same time the escrow is released to Seller. If a billing error or compliance issue arises from the transition services (e.g., a claim submitted incorrectly during the TSA term that triggers a payer recoupment 10 months post-Closing), Kateix would want to (a) assert a claim under the TSA and (b) recover from the escrow. But both are unavailable: the TSA has terminated (though Articles 8 and 9 survive per §10.3), and the escrow has been released. More practically, the simultaneous expiration means that if the migration of billing systems is not complete by month nine, Kateix loses both the contractual right to services and the security for indemnification claims in the same week.

**Recommendation:** Extend the TSA term to at least 12 months to provide a buffer beyond the escrow release, or (better) fix the escrow release date per Issue 1 so that both align with the 18-month survival period. Add a post-termination cooperation covenant in the TSA requiring Provider to assist with any claims or audits arising from services performed during the Term for at least 18 months post-Closing.
