## 1. Escrow Release Date Inconsistent with General Representation Survival and Exclusive-Remedy Structure

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA) § 9.1 (survival), § 9.6 (sources of recovery); Escrow Agreement § 4.2 (release).

**Issue.** SPA § 9.1 provides that General Representations survive for 18 months after the Closing Date (i.e., until September 2, 2027). SPA § 9.6 provides that Losses for breaches of General Representations "shall be recovered solely and exclusively from the Indemnity Escrow Fund." However, Escrow Agreement § 4.2 directs the Escrow Agent to release all remaining Escrow Funds to Seller on the Release Date—nine (9) months after the Closing Date (i.e., December 2, 2026)—less only the Pending Claim Amount for claims already noticed.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** After December 2, 2026, the escrow will be empty (absent pending claims), yet General Representations remain technically "alive" until September 2, 2027. Because § 9.6 restricts recovery to the escrow, Kateix has no practical remedy for any General Rep breach discovered between month 9 and month 18. This effectively shortens the survival period for General Representations from 18 months to 9 months. Given that the healthcare compliance rep (SPA § 4.12) is a General Representation, Kateix is exposed to undetected billing or coding issues that surface after month 9 with no recourse.

**Recommendation.** Amend Escrow Agreement § 4.2 to align the Release Date with the 18-month survival period in SPA § 9.1, or amend SPA § 9.6 to permit recovery directly from Seller (not solely from the escrow) for General Rep claims asserted after the escrow is released. If Seller resists extending the escrow hold, consider a shorter survival period for General Reps (e.g., 12 months) with the escrow released at 12 months, so the two are at least consistent.

## 2. Pellucid Network Participation Agreement Consent Not a Closing Deliverable

**Location:** SPA §§ 3.2(e), 6.4; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.5(b) § 5.1.

**Issue.** Disclosure Schedule § 5.1(a) states that the Pellucid Network Participation Agreement (which generated approximately 38% of the Company's net patient revenue in FY 2025, per Schedule 4.5(a) item 4.1) treats a change of control as an assignment requiring "the prior written consent of Pellucid." If the change of control occurs without that consent, Pellucid may terminate on 30 days' notice (§ 5.1(b)). However, SPA § 6.4 requires Seller to deliver only "the consents of the landlords under the Clinic Leases" at Closing, and SPA § 3.2(e) incorporates that limited list. Pellucid consent is not a closing deliverable or a condition to closing.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** If Pellucid consent was not obtained before March 2, 2026, Pellucid has the right to terminate the network agreement on 30 days' notice. Loss of 38% of net patient revenue would be catastrophic and would likely constitute a Material Adverse Effect. Because the consent was not a closing condition, Kateix may have closed without it and now faces a termination right that Pellucid can exercise at any time within the 30-day notice window.

**Recommendation.** Immediately confirm whether Pellucid written consent was in fact obtained before the Closing Date. If not, request that Seller use its relationship to obtain retroactive consent or a waiver of the termination right. Going forward, if the consent is not secured, consider whether this constitutes a breach of Seller's representation in SPA § 4.5(b) (that each Material Contract is valid and in full force) or § 4.4 (no conflicts), and preserve indemnification claims. Also note that the SPA's order-of-precedence clause (§ 11.10) would not help here because the gap is in the SPA itself, not a conflict with an ancillary agreement.

## 3. Transition Services Agreement: Broad, Uncapped Indemnity from Kateix to Seller

**Location:** Transition Services Agreement (TSA) §§ 8.1, 8.2, 9.1.

**Issue.** TSA § 8.1 limits Provider's (Seller's) indemnity to Recipient (Kateix) to third-party claims "caused by the gross negligence or willful misconduct of Provider in performing the Services." By contrast, TSA § 8.2 requires Recipient to indemnify Provider for "all Losses arising out of or relating to any third-party claim arising out of or relating to the Services **or the operation of the Clinics**, including any claim by any patient, payer or Governmental Authority." Section 9.1 caps each party's aggregate liability at six months of Service Fees ($510,000) but expressly carves out "obligations under Section 8.2" from the cap.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** Kateix has agreed to an uncapped indemnity to Seller for virtually any third-party claim relating to the operation of the 14 clinics during the 9-month TSA term. This includes patient malpractice claims, payer audits, and government enforcement actions—matters that are Kateix's own operational risks as the clinic owner. The asymmetry is stark: Seller's indemnity to Kateix is limited to gross negligence/willful misconduct in performing the transition services, while Kateix's indemnity to Seller is unlimited in scope and amount. In practical terms, if a payer recoupment or a malpractice verdict arises during the TSA term, Kateix must absorb the loss and also indemnify Seller against any claim Seller faces in connection with it.

**Recommendation.** Amend TSA § 8.2 to (a) delete "or the operation of the Clinics" so that Kateix's indemnity is limited to claims arising from the Services themselves, and (b) add a parallel gross negligence/willful misconduct standard or at minimum limit Kateix's indemnity to claims "caused by" Recipient's acts or omissions. Alternatively, bring § 8.2 within the § 9.1 cap by deleting the carve-out in § 9.1(i). If Seller will not agree to a mutual standard, negotiate a cap on § 8.2 exposure (e.g., the total Service Fees for the remaining Term).

## 4. Transition Services Agreement: Provider May Assign or Delegate Without Kateix's Consent

**Location:** TSA § 12.3.

**Issue.** TSA § 12.3 provides: "Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate the performance of any of the Services in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient." Recipient, by contrast, "may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under this Agreement without the prior written consent of Provider."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** The Services include revenue-cycle and billing (TSA Schedule A.1), PM System hosting and patient records (A.2), and IT support (A.3). These involve continuous access to Patient Data (defined in TSA § 1.1 to include protected health information). Seller can unilaterally hand off all of these functions—including custody of patient data and control of insurance billing—to any third party of its choosing, without Kateix's approval. This creates HIPAA business-associate risk (Kateix would need to ensure any delegatee is a proper business associate), operational risk (a substandard replacement could disrupt billing across 14 clinics), and a conflict with the data-protection obligations in TSA § 6.2, which are framed as obligations of "Provider" but could be diluted by delegation.

**Recommendation.** Amend TSA § 12.3 to require Provider to obtain Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) before assigning the Agreement or delegating any Service to a third party. At minimum, require that any delegatee (a) enter into a HIPAA business associate agreement with the Company, (b) assume Provider's obligations under Articles 5, 6, and 8, and (c) be financially responsible for the quality of the delegated Service. Make the asymmetry mutual: if Provider can assign freely, Recipient should be able to as well.

## 5. Net Working Capital Dispute – Approximately $1.2 Million

**Location:** Post-Closing Side Letter §§ 1.1, 2.1; SPA § 2.4; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.4 §§ 2.2(a), (b), (e).

**Issue.** Seller's Closing Statement (delivered March 30, 2026) calculated Closing Net Working Capital at $2,318,400. Buyer's Notice of Disagreement (delivered May 22, 2026) asserts the correct figure is approximately $1,118,400—a $1,200,000 swing. The three principal items are:

- **(a) Accounts receivable reserve (~$690,000):** Buyer asserts Seller failed to apply the aging-based reserve percentages required by Schedule 2.4 § 2.2(a), specifically failing to reserve 100% for receivables aged more than 180 days from date of service.
- **(b) Payer credit balances (~$315,000):** Buyer asserts Seller omitted credit balances and identified overpayments from current liabilities, contrary to Schedule 2.4 § 2.2(b).
- **(c) Accrued dentist production bonuses (~$195,000):** Buyer asserts Seller failed to include accrued but unpaid pre-Closing production bonuses, contrary to Schedule 2.4 § 2.2(e) and the Associate Dentist Production Bonus Policy (disclosed in Schedule 4.13, item 10.5).

**Why it matters for Kateix.** The Target Net Working Capital is $2,150,000 (SPA § 1.1; Schedule 2.4 § 2.3). Under Seller's calculation, NWC exceeds target by $168,400, meaning Kateix owes Seller that amount. Under Buyer's calculation, NWC falls short of target by $1,031,600, meaning Seller owes Kateix that amount. The total economic swing is approximately $1.2 million. Each of the three disputed items is directly addressed by a specific provision of the agreed Accounting Principles, which gives Kateix a strong textual basis.

**Recommendation.** (1) Prepare a detailed work paper for the June 12 meeting (per Side Letter § 2.1) that maps each disputed item to the specific Accounting Principle provision and quantifies the adjustment with supporting data from the Company's books. (2) Ensure the Company's A/R aging report, payer credit balance detail, and accrued bonus calculations are pulled and organized before the meeting, per Side Letter § 2.2. (3) If the 30-day negotiation period under SPA § 2.4(c) expires without resolution, promptly propose an Independent Accountant. (4) Note that the Side Letter § 3.2 designates discussions as settlement communications—ensure all internal communications about the dispute are marked accordingly to preserve that protection.

## 6. Seller Note: Restriction on Sale of the Company

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note § 5(c).

**Issue.** Note § 5(c) provides that while any amount remains unpaid, Maker (Kateix) shall "not sell, transfer or otherwise dispose of all or substantially all of the shares of capital stock of the Company, or cause or permit the Company to sell all or substantially all of its assets, unless this Note is repaid in full at or before the closing of that transaction."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** Kateix has $6,000,000 in note obligations maturing in installments through March 2, 2030. This covenant effectively requires Kateix to pay off the entire remaining note balance before it can sell the Company or substantially all of its assets. If Kateix's business plan involves a future sale or recapitalization of the dental group, this is a significant liquidity constraint. It also means that in a distress scenario, Kateix cannot sell the Company to a strategic buyer without first raising $6M (or the remaining balance) to retire the note.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate an amendment to Note § 5(c) to permit a sale if the proceeds are used to repay the note at closing (which is the current requirement) OR if the buyer assumes the note with Seller's consent. Alternatively, seek to reduce the restriction to "all" (rather than "all or substantially all") of the shares or assets, to preserve flexibility for partial divestitures of individual clinics. If Seller will not agree to any change, ensure the finance team models the liquidity impact of the prepayment requirement in any future exit scenario.

## 7. Seller Note: Automatic Acceleration on Company Bankruptcy

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note §§ 6.1(d), 6.1(e), 6.2.

**Issue.** Note § 6.1(d) makes it an Event of Default if "Maker **or the Company**" (i) makes a general assignment for the benefit of creditors, (ii) commences a voluntary bankruptcy case, or (iii) admits in writing its inability to pay debts. Note § 6.1(e) extends this to involuntary cases against Maker or the Company that are not dismissed within 60 days. Critically, Note § 6.2 provides that upon an Event of Default under § 6.1(d) or (e), the entire unpaid principal "becomes immediately due and payable **without notice or any other action by Holder**."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** The Company is a separate legal entity that Kateix now owns. If the Company encounters financial difficulty and files for (or is forced into) bankruptcy, the entire remaining balance of the $6M note accelerates automatically against Kateix (the parent) with no opportunity to cure. This creates a structural subordination problem: the Company's creditors would be paid from the Company's estate, but Kateix would simultaneously owe Seller the full note balance. In a downside scenario, Kateix could be forced into its own bankruptcy to meet the accelerated obligation.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate an amendment to Note § 6.1(d) and (e) to limit the bankruptcy trigger to Maker (Kateix) only, deleting "or the Company." If Seller insists on including the Company, at minimum (a) require that the automatic acceleration in § 6.2 not apply to Company-only bankruptcy events (i.e., give Kateix a cure period or notice-and-cure right), and (b) add a provision that if the Company files for bankruptcy, the note is not accelerated but instead payment is suspended during the bankruptcy case and resumes afterward.

## 8. No Set-Off Against the Seller Note for Indemnification Claims

**Location:** SPA § 9.6; Seller Promissory Note § 3.1.

**Issue.** SPA § 9.6 provides that for General Rep breaches, "Buyer shall have no right of set-off against the Seller Note for those Losses." Note § 3.1 independently requires that "Maker shall make each payment in full, without set-off, counterclaim or deduction."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** Kateix is making quarterly interest payments and biennial principal installments on the $6M note through 2030. If Kateix has a valid indemnification claim against Seller (e.g., for a breach of a General Representation), it cannot net that claim against note payments. Kateix must pay the note in full and separately pursue the claim through the escrow (and, per Issue 1 above, only while the escrow is funded). This forces Kateix to use cash to service the note even when Seller owes Kateix money, creating an unnecessary liquidity drain.

**Recommendation.** This is difficult to change post-closing without Seller's agreement, but if the parties are already in negotiation over the NWC dispute (Issue 5), consider raising the set-off restriction as a bargaining chip. Ideally, amend SPA § 9.6 to permit set-off against the Seller Note for any claim that is (a) finally resolved in Buyer's favor and (b) not recoverable from the escrow because the escrow has been exhausted or released. If that is not achievable, at minimum ensure that the NWC adjustment payment (SPA § 2.4(e)) is made by Seller directly to Kateix by wire and is not netted against any note payment, to avoid any ambiguity.

## 9. Healthcare Compliance Representation Survives Only 18 Months

**Location:** SPA §§ 1.1 (definition of "General Representations"), 4.12, 9.1.

**Issue.** SPA § 4.12 (Healthcare Compliance) is not a Fundamental Representation and is not the Tax rep (§ 4.10), so it is a "General Representation" under the definition in § 1.1. General Representations survive only 18 months after closing (SPA § 9.1). The rep covers compliance with Healthcare Laws, accuracy of claims submitted since January 1, 2023, absence of audits or overpayment demands, and HIPAA compliance.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** Dental billing audits, Medicaid recoupments, and OIG/State AG investigations frequently arise 18–36 months after the relevant services are rendered. The Company has 14 clinics billing Texas Medicaid/CHIP and multiple private payers. A systemic coding or billing issue could surface in year 2 or 3, after the 18-month survival period has expired, leaving Kateix with no indemnification recourse against Seller. Combined with Issue 1 (escrow released at 9 months), the practical window to recover for a healthcare compliance breach may be as short as 9 months.

**Recommendation.** If the parties are amenable to a side amendment (the NWC dispute negotiation provides a natural forum), seek to extend the survival period for § 4.12 specifically to the later of (a) 24 months after closing or (b) 60 days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations for the relevant Healthcare Law violation. If that is not achievable, ensure the compliance team conducts a thorough post-closing billing audit within the first 9 months (while the escrow is still funded) to identify and preserve any claims before the escrow is released.

## 10. Deductible Structure Limits Recovery for Smaller Breaches

**Location:** SPA § 9.5(a).

**Issue.** SPA § 9.5(a) provides a true deductible of $363,750 for General Representation breaches: "Seller is not liable under Section 9.2(a) for breaches of General Representations until the aggregate Losses for those breaches exceed $363,750 (the 'Deductible'), and then only for Losses in excess of the Deductible."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** The deductible is approximately 12% of the $3M escrow and 0.75% of the $48.5M Base Purchase Price. Because it is a true deductible (not a tipping basket), Kateix absorbs the first $363,750 of losses dollar-for-dollar, and even after the threshold is crossed, Seller is liable only for the excess. For a portfolio of 14 clinics, multiple smaller breaches (e.g., a few undisclosed liabilities, minor contract issues, small compliance gaps) could collectively fall below the deductible, leaving Kateix with no recovery at all.

**Recommendation.** This is a negotiated economic term and difficult to change post-closing. However, be aware of it when evaluating whether to pursue any indemnification claim: the claim must exceed $363,750 in the aggregate (across all General Rep breaches) before any recovery is available, and then only for the amount above the threshold. Track all potential claims in a centralized log to ensure that smaller items are aggregated for deductible purposes.

## 11. Landlord Estoppel: Only One of Fourteen Clinics Covered

**Location:** SPA § 6.4; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9; Landlord Estoppel Certificate (Clinic 1 only).

**Issue.** SPA § 6.4 represents that Seller delivered landlord consents for all Clinic Leases at Closing. The Disclosure Schedule lists 14 Clinic Leases (Schedule 4.9). The matter folder contains only one landlord estoppel and consent certificate—for Clinic 1 (Mesquite Ridge – Alamo Heights, Broadway Crossing Retail, LP). No estoppels or consents for the other 13 clinics are in the folder.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** Without estoppels, Kateix has no landlord confirmation that (a) the leases are in full force, (b) no defaults exist, (c) rent is current, or (d) the landlord consents to the change of control. If any of the 13 other landlords disputes the consent or asserts a pre-existing default, Kateix could face lease termination or rent abatement disputes at a clinic it now owns. The SPA representation in § 4.9 (that each Clinic Lease is valid and in full force) is a General Representation subject to the 18-month survival and escrow-only recovery limitations discussed above.

**Recommendation.** Request from Seller (or obtain directly from landlords) estoppel certificates for all 13 remaining Clinic Leases as soon as practicable. If Seller resists, note that SPA § 6.5 (Further Assurances) obligates Seller to "execute and deliver any further instruments, and take any further actions, that the other party reasonably requests to carry out the transfer of the Shares." A post-closing request for estoppels is a reasonable further assurance. Prioritize the leases with the nearest expiration dates (Clinic 4 – New Braunfels, expiring May 31, 2028; Clinic 11 – Temple, expiring October 31, 2028) and any lease where the landlord is a different entity type that might raise consent issues.

## 12. Seller Note Cross-Default Threshold

**Location:** Seller Promissory Note § 6.1(f).

**Issue.** Note § 6.1(f) provides that an Event of Default occurs if "Maker or the Company defaults in the payment of any indebtedness for borrowed money in an aggregate principal amount of more than $2,500,000, and the default results in the acceleration of that indebtedness."

**Why it matters for Kateix.** The Company will presumably incur new debt post-closing (working capital facilities, equipment financing, etc.). If the Company takes on a credit facility in excess of $2.5M and defaults on it (triggering acceleration by the lender), that default automatically becomes an Event of Default under the Seller Note. Kateix could find itself in a position where a routine credit facility issue at the operating company level triggers acceleration of the $6M Seller Note to the former owner.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate an amendment to raise the cross-default threshold (e.g., to $5M or $7.5M) or to exclude the Company's indebtedness from the cross-default trigger (limiting it to Maker/Kateix only). If the note cannot be amended, ensure that any new Company credit facility is structured with adequate covenant headroom to avoid an acceleration that would cascade into the Seller Note.

## 13. TSA Term Aligns with Escrow Release – Migration Risk

**Location:** TSA § 2.1 (9-month term); Escrow Agreement § 4.2 (9-month release).

**Issue.** Both the TSA and the escrow release occur at 9 months post-closing (December 2, 2026). TSA § 3.5 requires Provider to cooperate in migrating the PM System data and Patient Data to Kateix's systems "before the end of the Term." If the migration is not complete by December 2, the TSA expires, the escrow is released, and Kateix may be left without (a) the billing/PM/IT services it needs for 14 clinics and (b) the escrow security for any claims arising from an incomplete or botched migration.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** A PM system migration for 14 dental clinics (including electronic dental records, imaging archives, clearinghouse connections, and patient data) is a complex project. If it slips past December 2, Kateix would need to negotiate a TSA extension (giving Seller leverage) or find a replacement vendor on an emergency basis, with potential disruption to patient care and revenue cycle.

**Recommendation.** (1) Develop a detailed migration project plan with milestones and a buffer of at least 4–6 weeks before the 9-month deadline. (2) Designate a project owner at Kateix with authority to escalate. (3) If the migration is not on track by month 7 (October 2026), proactively request a written TSA extension (even 30–60 days) while the escrow is still funded, so that any migration-related claims can be asserted against the escrow. (4) Ensure that the "commercially reasonable, non-proprietary format" data extract requirement in TSA § 3.5 is tested early (e.g., a trial extract by month 3) to identify format issues before they become critical.

## 14. TSA Pass-Through Costs Lack a Cap or Audit Right

**Location:** TSA § 4.4.

**Issue.** TSA § 4.4 requires Kateix to reimburse Provider "at cost, without markup, for third-party clearinghouse transaction fees, postage for patient statements, and per-user license fees for the PM System, in each case to the extent incurred in providing the Services and supported by reasonable documentation." There is no cap on these pass-through costs and no express audit right for Kateix.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** For 14 clinics, clearinghouse fees and per-user PM System license fees can be substantial. Without a cap or audit right, Kateix is exposed to overbilling by Provider on these items. The "reasonable documentation" standard is vague and may not be sufficient to detect inflated charges.

**Recommendation.** Add a provision requiring Provider to deliver a monthly itemized statement of pass-through costs with the invoice, and granting Kateix the right to audit those costs (or to have a third-party auditor review them) on reasonable notice. Consider setting a not-to-exceed amount based on the trailing 12-month actuals for these cost categories, with any excess requiring Kateix's prior written approval.

## 15. SPA Order of Precedence Favors the SPA over Ancillary Agreements

**Location:** SPA § 11.10.

**Issue.** SPA § 11.10 provides: "If this Agreement conflicts with any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement controls over the conflicting Ancillary Agreement." The "Ancillary Agreements" are defined in SPA § 1.1 to include the Escrow Agreement, the TSA, and the Seller Note.

**Why it matters for Kateix.** This is a double-edged sword. It protects Kateix where the SPA gives Kateix rights (e.g., the indemnification framework, the NWC adjustment mechanism) and an ancillary agreement might be read to limit them. However, it also means that if the TSA or Note contains a provision that is more favorable to Kateix than the SPA (e.g., if the Note had a broader prepayment right or the TSA had a longer term), the SPA would control and override it. In the current documents, the SPA's restrictions (e.g., § 9.6 limiting recovery to escrow, § 9.5(a) deductible) will trump any contrary language in the ancillary agreements.

**Recommendation.** Be aware of this hierarchy when interpreting any ambiguity across the document set. If Kateix seeks to amend any ancillary agreement to add protections (per the recommendations above), ensure the amendment does not create a "conflict" with the SPA that would be resolved against Kateix under § 11.10. Any amendment to an ancillary agreement should be accompanied by a conforming amendment to the SPA if necessary, or should be structured as an addition that does not contradict existing SPA language.
