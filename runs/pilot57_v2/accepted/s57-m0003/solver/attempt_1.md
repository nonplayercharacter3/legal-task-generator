## URGENT ITEMS REQUIRING IMMEDIATE ATTENTION

### 1. Late Section 4.4 Notice – Likely Loss of Conversion Right

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 4.4; Notice Letter dated October 28, 2026, ¶ 2.

**Issue:** Section 4.4 provides that if Distributor fails to meet the Minimum Purchase Commitment for a Contract Year, Supplier may convert the appointment to non-exclusive "by written notice to Distributor given **within sixty (60) days after the end of the applicable Contract Year**." Contract Year 3 ended June 30, 2026. The 60-day deadline therefore fell on **August 29, 2026**. The notice letter is dated **October 28, 2026** — approximately 60 days after the deadline.

**Why it matters:** The 60-day window is best read as a condition on the exercise of the conversion right. By missing it, Fougeil has likely forfeited its contractual right to convert Treireis to a non-exclusive appointment. The no-waiver-by-inaction clause in § 17.4 ("no failure or delay in exercising any right operates as a waiver") addresses implied waiver through conduct but does not extend a right that is expressly conditioned on timely notice. If the conversion right is lost, Fougeil remains locked into the exclusive appointment for the duration of the Term (and any Renewal Terms), while still bound by the Supplier Exclusivity Covenant in § 2.3.

**Recommended fix:**
- Assess whether any equitable or estoppel argument preserves the right (e.g., if Treireis led Fougeil to believe the deadline was extended, or if Treireis has since acted in a manner inconsistent with objecting to timeliness).
- Evaluate whether the MPC shortfall could support a termination for material breach under § 13.1 (60-day cure period), though the specific remedy in § 4.4 may be read as the exclusive remedy for this particular breach.
- If the right is lost, consider whether a commercial negotiation is warranted: e.g., a voluntary conversion in exchange for a revised MPC for Contract Year 4, or a new side letter addressing the exclusivity issue.
- Document the internal timeline for why the notice was not sent by August 29 to preserve any potential arguments.

### 2. Section 12.3(a) Cross-Reference Error – Liability Cap Misapplied

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 12.3(a); First Amendment § 2 (restating § 11).

**Issue:** Section 12.3(a) provides that the exclusion of indirect damages (§ 12.1) and the liability cap (§ 12.2) do not apply to "a party's obligations under **Section 11.1**." In the original Agreement, § 11.1 was the **Distributor Indemnification** (i.e., Treireis's obligation to indemnify Fougeil). The First Amendment deleted and restated § 11, renumbering so that **§ 11.1 is now the Supplier Indemnification** (Fougeil's obligation to indemnify Treireis) and **§ 11.2 is now the Distributor Indemnification**. The cross-reference in § 12.3(a) was not updated.

**Why it matters:** As drafted, the uncapped treatment now applies to *Fougeil's* indemnification obligations (new § 11.1), meaning Fougeil's exposure under the indemnity is unlimited. Simultaneously, *Treireis's* indemnification obligations (new § 11.2) are now subject to the 12-month payment cap in § 12.2, significantly reducing the value of the indemnity Fougeil can claim from Treireis. Both outcomes are adverse to Fougeil. The prior draft's § 12.3(a) read "each party's indemnification obligations under Section 11," which would have excluded both parties' indemnities from the cap.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a corrective amendment to § 12.3(a) restoring the original intent. The cleanest fix is to change the reference to "Section 11" (covering both 11.1 and 11.2) or to "Sections 11.1 and 11.2."
- If Treireis resists, at minimum seek to add § 11.2 to the list of exclusions so that the Distributor's indemnification obligations are uncapped.
- This should be raised promptly, ideally in connection with any negotiation arising from Issue 1 above.

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 3. Supplier Exclusivity Covenant (§ 2.3) – Dramatically Broadened from Draft

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 2.3 and § 1.2 (definition of "Competing Product"); compare Prior Draft v3 §§ 2.3 and 1.2.

**Issue:** The Prior Draft limited the covenant to the Territory (United States) and the Field (companion-animal point-of-care chemistry and hematology), and expressly stated: "This Section 2.3 does not restrict Supplier or its Affiliates from selling any product outside the Territory or outside the Field." The final Agreement:
- Expanded the geographic scope to "**anywhere in the world**";
- Expanded the product scope via the "Competing Product" definition (§ 1.2) to cover "any analyzer, instrument, test, assay, reagent, cartridge or other consumable intended for use in **veterinary diagnostics**, whether for **companion-animal, livestock, equine or other species**, and whether for **point-of-care or reference-laboratory use**";
- Added a **24-month post-termination tail**;
- The covenant survives termination under § 13.5.

**Why it matters:** Fougeil is prohibited from selling *any* veterinary diagnostic product *anywhere in the world* for the entire Term (60 months + any 60-month Renewal Terms) plus 24 months after termination. If Fougeil has or develops products for livestock diagnostics, equine testing, or reference-laboratory use, it cannot sell them through any channel. This is an extraordinary restriction that goes far beyond protecting Treireis's exclusive U.S. companion-animal distribution rights.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a narrowing amendment: restore the geographic limitation to the Territory and the product limitation to the Field (or at minimum to companion-animal point-of-care diagnostics).
- If the broad covenant is non-negotiable, seek a carve-out for products sold outside the U.S. or for non-point-of-care applications, and consider whether the 24-month tail should be reduced.
- This issue is compounded by the late-notice problem (Issue 1): if Fougeil cannot convert to non-exclusive, it remains subject to both the exclusive appointment *and* the global exclusivity covenant.

### 4. Renewal Term Length (§ 3.2) – Changed from 1 Year to 60 Months

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.2; compare Prior Draft v3 § 3.2.

**Issue:** The Prior Draft provided for automatic renewals of "one (1) year each." The final Agreement provides for renewals "each equal in length to the Initial Term" (60 months, per Order Form § 2).

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil misses the 90-day non-renewal notice deadline at the end of the Initial Term (or any Renewal Term), it is locked in for another five years. Combined with the broad exclusivity covenant (Issue 3) and the one-way assignment restriction (Issue 5), a missed renewal notice could trap Fougeil in an unfavorable arrangement for an extended period.

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (90 days before the end of the then-current term) immediately. The Initial Term ends June 30, 2028, so the non-renewal notice must be given by **April 1, 2028**.
- Negotiate a reduction in the renewal term length to one or two years, or at minimum add a right for Fougeil to terminate for convenience on 12 months' notice after the second Renewal Term.

### 5. Assignment Restriction (§ 15.1) – One-Way; Loss of Change-of-Control Protection

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.1; compare Prior Draft v3 § 15.1.

**Issue:** The Prior Draft provided:
- Mutual assignment restrictions (neither party could assign without the other's consent);
- A change-of-control provision: "A change of control of Distributor is treated as an assignment for purposes of this Section 15.1";
- A termination right: "Supplier may terminate this Agreement on thirty (30) days' written notice if Distributor assigns this Agreement to, or comes under the control of, a manufacturer or distributor of a Competing Product."

The final Agreement contains only: "Supplier shall not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it, whether voluntarily, by operation of law or otherwise, without Distributor's prior written consent." There is no restriction on Distributor's assignment, no change-of-control trigger, and no termination right if Distributor is acquired by a competitor.

**Why it matters:** If Treireis Holdings sells the Distributor to a competitor (or to a private equity firm that then sells it to a competitor), Fougeil has no contractual right to object, terminate, or even require consent. The Distributor's obligations (including the MPC) would transfer to the acquirer without Fougeil's approval. Additionally, Fougeil cannot assign the Agreement (e.g., in connection with a divestiture of its veterinary diagnostics division) without Treireis's consent, creating an asymmetry that could impair Fougeil's corporate flexibility.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a restated § 15.1 that: (a) makes the restriction mutual; (b) treats a change of control of Distributor as an assignment requiring Supplier's consent; and (c) restores the termination right if Distributor comes under the control of a competitor.
- At minimum, add a provision requiring Distributor to give Fougeil prior written notice of any change of control and granting Fougeil a termination right if the new controlling party is a competitor.

### 6. First Amendment § 11.1(c) – Broad Labeling/IFU Indemnification

**Where:** First Amendment § 2, new § 11.1(c).

**Issue:** The Amendment added a new indemnification trigger: Supplier must indemnify Distributor for "any statement in Supplier's labeling, instructions for use or approved marketing materials for a Product that is **alleged** to be false or misleading or to violate applicable law." The key word is "alleged" — Fougeil must defend and indemnify even if the allegation is baseless.

**Why it matters:** In the veterinary diagnostics space, labeling and IFU claims can trigger regulatory scrutiny (FDA, USDA-CVS) and product liability litigation. This provision means Fougeil bears the cost of defending *any* third-party claim that its labeling is allegedly misleading, regardless of merit, and must pay any resulting judgment or settlement. Combined with the Section 12.3(a) error (Issue 2), this indemnity is currently *uncapped*.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a deletion of "alleged" or a replacement with "found by a final, non-appealable court decision to be" false or misleading.
- Alternatively, add a requirement that the claim be "in violation of applicable law as finally determined by a court of competent jurisdiction."
- Ensure the Section 12.3(a) fix (Issue 2) is implemented so that at least the Distributor's indemnification obligations are also uncapped, restoring balance.

### 7. First Amendment § 11.4 – Counsel Acceptability Requirement

**Where:** First Amendment § 2, new § 11.4 (Procedure).

**Issue:** The restated procedure provides that "The indemnifying party shall control the defense and settlement of the claim with **counsel reasonably acceptable to the indemnified party**." In the original § 11.3, the indemnifying party simply "shall control the defense and settlement of the claim" with no acceptability requirement.

**Why it matters:** When Fougeil is the indemnifying party (under new § 11.1), Treireis has a veto over Fougeil's choice of defense counsel. In complex product liability or regulatory matters, Fougeil may want to use specialized counsel; Treireis could demand (on a "reasonableness" standard) that Fougeil use counsel with which it is more comfortable, potentially increasing cost or compromising defense strategy.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate deletion of "reasonably acceptable to the indemnified party" or replacement with "counsel of the indemnifying party's choosing, provided that the indemnified party may participate with its own counsel at its own expense" (which is already in the last sentence of § 11.4).
- If the acceptability standard remains, add that it may not be unreasonably withheld and that cost is the indemnifying party's responsibility regardless.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 8. Service Level Schedule § 6.5 – Credits Do Not Limit Other Remedies

**Where:** Service Level Schedule § 6.5.

**Issue:** "Service credits are a price adjustment that reflects the reduced value of the services. They do not limit Distributor's other rights or remedies under the Agreement."

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil consistently misses service levels (e.g., On-Time Shipment Rate below 95% for multiple consecutive quarters), Treireis could argue this constitutes a material breach supporting termination under § 13.1 of the Agreement, in addition to claiming the modest service credits. The credits ($5,000–$10,000 per miss, capped at 5% of quarterly shipments) are small relative to the revenue at stake, but the termination right is not.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an addition to § 6.5: "Repeated failure to meet a service level in three (3) consecutive Measurement Periods shall not, by itself, constitute a material breach of the Agreement. Distributor's remedy for service level failures is limited to the service credits in Section 6, unless the failure results from Supplier's willful misconduct or gross negligence."
- Ensure internal operations are aligned to meet the service levels, and document any Distributor-caused failures under § 7 of the Schedule.

### 9. Guaranty § 9 – New York Governing Law and Forum

**Where:** Parent Guaranty § 9.1–9.2; compare Distribution Agreement § 16.1–16.2.

**Issue:** The main Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware. The Guaranty is governed by **New York law** with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County / SDNY**.

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil needs to enforce the Guaranty (e.g., after a Distributor default or bankruptcy), it must litigate in New York under New York law, while any related dispute under the main Agreement is in Delaware. This creates:
- Procedural complexity (potential for inconsistent rulings);
- Higher enforcement costs;
- Strategic disadvantage if Treireis can forum-shop or create procedural delays.

New York guaranty law is generally favorable to creditors (e.g., *UCC § 3-418* and New York's sureship statutes), so the substantive law choice may not be problematic. But the forum split is inconvenient.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the next amendment opportunity, negotiate to conform the Guaranty's governing law and forum to Delaware (matching the main Agreement).
- If not feasible, ensure that the Guaranty's § 10.2 (assignment) provision is used strategically: Fougeil should maintain the ability to assign the Guaranty together with the Agreement to preserve package enforcement.

### 10. Launch Side Letter § 3.5 – Demo Unit Refurbishment at Supplier's Cost

**Where:** Launch Support Side Letter § 3.5.

**Issue:** When Distributor converts a Demo Unit to a sale, "The unit must first be refurbished and re-verified by Supplier or its authorized service provider **at Supplier's cost**."

**Why it matters:** Demo Units are in Distributor's possession for up to the full Term (60+ months). They are used for customer demonstrations, evaluations (up to 30 days at customer sites), and internal training. By the time of conversion, they may be significantly worn. Fougeil bears all refurbishment and re-verification costs regardless of the unit's condition or the cause of wear. With up to 35 Demo Units (20 C200 + 15 H50), this could represent a meaningful cost center.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a provision that if the Demo Unit requires refurbishment beyond routine cleaning and software re-imaging due to Distributor's misuse or failure to maintain per the Specifications and Quality Agreement, the cost is borne by Distributor.
- Alternatively, cap Supplier's refurbishment cost per unit or require Distributor to pay a restocking/condition fee if the unit is returned in poor condition.

### 11. Section 5.2 – 5% Annual Price Increase Cap

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 5.2.

**Issue:** "No single annual increase may exceed five percent (5%) for any Product, except for documented increases in the cost of raw materials or components outside Supplier's reasonable control."

**Why it matters:** In an inflationary environment or if input costs (e.g., reagent chemicals, electronic components) rise sharply, Fougeil's ability to maintain margins is constrained. The exception for "documented increases in the cost of raw materials or components outside Supplier's reasonable control" is helpful but requires documentation and may be disputed.

**Recommended fix:**
- Maintain a file of cost documentation for any year in which Fougeil seeks to exceed the 5% cap.
- Consider negotiating a "most-favored-nation" cost pass-through provision or a coll
ar (e.g., prices may decrease by up to 5% but increase by up to 7% with documentation).
- The current CY4 price list (Product Price List 2026, § 3.2) asserts compliance with the 5% cap; verify against the CY3 price list (not in the folder) to confirm.

### 12. Order Form § 6.3 – Supplier Supply Shortfall Reduction of MPC

**Where:** Order Form § 6.3.

**Issue:** "The Minimum Purchase Commitment for a Contract Year is reduced by the net invoice value of any conforming Purchase Order that Supplier rejected, or failed to ship within sixty (60) days after the requested delivery date, other than because of a breach by Distributor."

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil experiences supply disruptions (e.g., component shortages, manufacturing issues) and cannot ship within 60 days of the requested delivery date, the MPC is reduced dollar-for-dollar. This means Fougeil's own supply failures reduce the revenue benchmark against which Treireis is measured, potentially making it harder to demonstrate an MPC shortfall in future years. The 60-day grace period is generous.

**Recommended fix:**
- Shorten the grace period to 30 days or tie it to the standard lead times in Order Form § 7.2 (10 business days for analyzers, 5 for consumables).
- Add a cap on the MPC reduction (e.g., no more than 10% of the annual MPC).
- Ensure that force majeure events (which are excluded from service level calculations under SLS § 2.8(c)) are also excluded from this MPC reduction, or at least that the 60-day period is tolled during force majeure.

### 13. Quality Agreement § 4.5 – One-Business-Day Record Production for Priority 1 Cases

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 4.5.

**Issue:** "In the event of a recall or an investigation of a Priority 1 support case under the Service Level Schedule, it will do so [provide copies of records] within **one (1) business day**."

**Why it matters:** While this provision applies to both parties, Fougeil's device history records, lot release records, and distribution records (§ 4.3) may be voluminous. Producing them within one business day for a Priority 1 case (analyzer inoperable or producing unreliable results) could strain the quality team, particularly if the case involves multiple lots or serial numbers.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a two-business-day window for initial production, with a commitment to produce the complete record set within five business days.
- Clarify that "records" means records *directly relevant* to the specific serial number(s) or lot(s) in question, not the entire device history file.

### 14. Notice Letter – Reference to Service Level Schedule § 5.2 for Business Review

**Where:** Notice Letter ¶ 5.

**Issue:** The letter proposes that the parties "discuss Distributor's Contract Year 4 forecast and sales plan at the next quarterly business review under **Section 5.2 of the Service Level Schedule**." Section 5.2 of the SLS is indeed the "Business Reviews" provision, so the reference is correct. However, the SLS § 5.2 business reviews are scheduled "within forty-five (45) days after the end of each Measurement Period" (calendar quarter). The next review after the October 28, 2026 notice would be the Q3 2026 review (quarter ended September 30, 2026), which should have already occurred by mid-November 2026.

**Why it matters:** Minor timing issue. If the Q3 review has already taken place before the notice was received, the proposal to discuss at "the next" review would push the conversation to the Q4 2026 review (early 2027), delaying any commercial resolution.

**Recommended fix:**
- Propose an ad hoc business review within 30 days of the notice, rather than waiting for the next scheduled quarterly review.
