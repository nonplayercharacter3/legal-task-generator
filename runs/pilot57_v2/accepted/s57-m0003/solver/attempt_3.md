## URGENT – IMMEDIATE ACTION REQUIRED

### 1. Missed Deadline for Minimum Purchase Commitment Conversion Notice

**Where:** `fougeil_notice_letter_2026-10-28.md`, ¶2; `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §4.4; `order_form_initial_appointment.md`, §6.1

**Issue:** Section 4.4 of the Agreement requires Supplier to give written notice of a Minimum Purchase Commitment (MPC) shortfall "within sixty (60) days after the end of the applicable Contract Year." Contract Year 3 ended June 30, 2026. The 60-day deadline therefore expired on **August 29, 2026**. The notice letter is dated **October 28, 2026** — approximately 60 days past the deadline.

**Why it matters:** Fougeil has likely forfeited its right to convert Distributor's appointment from exclusive to non-exclusive for the CY3 shortfall of $1,287,520. The conversion was the *only* contractual remedy for an MPC miss (there is no termination right, no liquidated damages, and no buy-back at a discount). If the notice is time-barred, Fougeil must continue to honor Distributor's exclusive appointment in the U.S. companion-animal POC chemistry/hematology field through the remainder of the Initial Term (and any Renewal Term), despite Distributor's underperformance.

**Recommended fix:**
- Immediately assess whether the deadline can be salvaged (e.g., argument that the 60-day period runs from when Supplier *discovered* the shortfall rather than the calendar deadline, though the plain text supports the calendar reading).
- Prepare a fallback strategy: even if the CY3 conversion fails, ensure the CY4 MPC notice (due within 60 days after June 30, 2027, i.e., by **August 29, 2027**) is calendared and not missed.
- Consider whether Distributor's chronic underperformance (CY3 miss of ~17%) combined with other breaches gives rise to a termination right under §13.1 (material breach with 60-day cure).
- Send a follow-up letter to Distributor acknowledging the late notice and reserving all rights, to prevent any argument of waiver or estoppel.

## HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 2. Worldwide Supplier Exclusivity Covenant – Dramatically Broader Than Negotiated Draft

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §§1.2, 2.3, 13.5; compare `distribution_agreement_prior_draft_v3.md`, §§1.2, 2.3

**Issue:** The final Agreement's §2.3 prohibits Supplier from selling, distributing, licensing or supplying **any Competing Product anywhere in the world** during the Term and for 24 months thereafter. The defined term "Competing Product" (§1.2) covers "any analyzer, instrument, test, assay, reagent, cartridge or other consumable intended for use in veterinary diagnostics, **whether for companion-animal, livestock, equine or other species, and whether for point-of-care or reference-laboratory use**."

By contrast, draft v3 §2.3 restricted Supplier only from selling "the Products or a Competing Product **in the Territory and the Field**," and draft v3 §1.2 defined Competing Product narrowly as "any point-of-care chemistry or hematology analyzer **for companion-animal use**, and any reagent or consumable designed for use with such an analyzer."

The final agreement thus expands the restriction from (a) the U.S. companion-animal POC chemistry/hematology space to (b) all veterinary diagnostics globally, across all species and all use cases (POC and reference lab).

**Why it matters:** Fougeil cannot launch or sell *any* new veterinary diagnostic product in *any* market (including its home market, Europe, Asia, livestock, reference lab) for the 60-month Initial Term plus any Renewal Terms plus 24 months post-termination. This could block significant revenue opportunities and M&A integration. The survival clause (§13.5) expressly preserves §2.3 after termination, extending the worldwide restriction 24 months beyond the end of the relationship.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to narrow §2.3 to the Territory and Field (as in draft v3) or, at minimum, to the Territory (U.S.) and the species/use-case scope of the Field.
- If the counterparty resists, seek carve-outs for: (a) products not in the same product category (e.g., reference-lab instruments); (b) non-U.S. territories; (c) products launched before a specified date.
- Review the 24-month post-termination tail and argue for reduction to 12 months or elimination.

### 3. Cross-Reference Error in Liability Cap Exclusion – Supplier's Indemnity Now Uncapped

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §12.3(a); `first_amendment_distribution_agreement.md`, §2 (replacing §11)

**Issue:** Original Agreement §12.3(a) provides that the liability cap in §12.2 does not apply to "a party's obligations under **Section 11.1**." In the original Agreement, §11.1 was *Distributor Indemnification* (i.e., the cap exclusion protected Fougeil by making Distributor's indemnity to Fougeil uncapped).

The First Amendment deleted and replaced all of Section 11. In the amended Section 11, **§11.1 is now *Supplier Indemnification*** and §11.2 is Distributor Indemnification. The Amendment did not conform the cross-reference in §12.3(a).

**Result:** Post-amendment, §12.3(a) excludes *Supplier's* indemnification obligations (product defect, IP infringement, labeling, negligence) from the liability cap. Meanwhile, *Distributor's* indemnification obligations (§11.2) remain subject to the 12-month payment cap in §12.2. This is the inverse of the original intent and significantly increases Fougeil's exposure.

**Why it matters:** Fougeil's indemnity for product defects, IP claims, and (per the new §11.1(c)) labeling/IFU claims is now theoretically uncapped. Given the broad "alleged" language in §11.1(c) (see Issue 5 below), this creates substantial unbounded financial risk.

**Recommended fix:**
- Execute a second amendment correcting §12.3(a) to read "a party's obligations under Section 11.2" (Distributor Indemnification), restoring the original intent that Distributor's indemnity to Supplier is uncapped while Supplier's indemnity to Distributor remains subject to the cap.
- Alternatively, amend §12.3(a) to read "each party's indemnification obligations under Section 11" (as in draft v3), which would make both parties' indemnities uncapped — less ideal for Fougeil but at least symmetric.
- In the interim, ensure insurance coverage (§17.6) is adequate to address uncapped indemnity exposure.

### 4. Assignment Restriction – No M&A Carve-Out, No Reasonableness Standard

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §15.1; compare `distribution_agreement_prior_draft_v3.md`, §15.1

**Issue:** Final Agreement §15.1 provides: "Supplier shall not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it, whether voluntarily, **by operation of law or otherwise**, without Distributor's prior written consent." There is no "not to be unreasonably withheld" qualifier and no exception for a sale of all or substantially all of Supplier's business.

Draft v3 §15.1 had: (a) a mutual restriction with "not unreasonably withheld"; (b) an express carve-out allowing either party to assign "to a successor to all or substantially all of its business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice"; and (c) a termination right for Supplier if Distributor came under the control of a competitor.

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil is acquired by, or merges with, another entity, the Agreement cannot be assigned to the successor without Distributor's consent, which Distributor can withhold for any reason (or no reason). The phrase "by operation of law" could even capture a change of control that results in the Agreement being treated as assigned under applicable law. This creates deal risk and gives Distributor leverage in any future transaction involving Fougeil.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment adding: (a) "which consent shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed"; (b) a carve-out permitting assignment to a successor in interest to all or substantially all of Supplier's relevant business assets or equity, on written notice; and (c) a corresponding right for Supplier to terminate if Distributor is acquired by a competitor (mirroring the protection Fougeil lost from draft v3).

### 5. First Amendment §11.1(c) – Labeling/IFU Indemnity Triggered by "Allegation"

**Where:** `first_amendment_distribution_agreement.md`, §2 (new §11.1(c))

**Issue:** The new Supplier Indemnification covers "any statement in Supplier's labeling, instructions for use or approved marketing materials for a Product that is **alleged** to be false or misleading or to violate applicable law." The trigger is an *allegation*, not a finding or adjudication.

**Why it matters:** In the veterinary diagnostics regulatory environment (FDA, USDA/CVBD, state boards), a single informal inquiry, a customer complaint characterizing a label claim as misleading, or a competitor's baseless assertion could trigger Fougeil's duty to defend and indemnify Distributor. Combined with Issue 3 (uncapped indemnity), this creates open-ended regulatory defense costs. The §11.3 exclusions (modification, unapproved combination, continued sale after notice) apply *only* to §11.1(b) (IP claims), not to §11.1(c) (labeling). So even if Distributor modified a label or combined the product with unapproved reagents, Fougeil may still owe an indemnity for a labeling allegation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to §11.1(c) changing "alleged to be" to "finally adjudicated to be" or "determined by a regulatory authority to be." At minimum, add a requirement that the statement actually be false/misleading/violative, not merely alleged.
- Extend the §11.3 exclusions to apply to §11.1(c) as well (e.g., no indemnity if the claim arises from Distributor's modification of labeling or use with unapproved products).
- Confirm that Fougeil's product liability and regulatory defense insurance covers this exposure.

### 6. Sixty-Month Auto-Renewal Terms

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §§3.2, 3.3; `order_form_initial_appointment.md`, §2

**Issue:** The Initial Term is 60 months (§2 of Order Form). Upon expiry, the Agreement "automatically renews for successive renewal terms, **each equal in length to the Initial Term**" (§3.2). Draft v3 had 1-year renewal terms. The non-renewal notice deadline is 90 days before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil misses the 90-day non-renewal window at the end of the Initial Term (i.e., by approximately April 2, 2028 for a June 30, 2028 expiry), it is locked in for another 60 months. Combined with the worldwide exclusivity covenant (Issue 2) and the assignment restriction (Issue 4), a missed renewal notice could bind Fougeil to an unfavorable structure for five additional years.

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (90 days before June 30, 2028 = **April 2, 2028**) with multiple internal reminders.
- If the business relationship is not performing well (see the CY3 MPC shortfall), consider giving non-renewal notice well before the deadline to preserve negotiating leverage.
- Negotiate an amendment to shorten renewal terms to 12 months (as in draft v3) or to require affirmative mutual consent for renewal rather than auto-renewal.

## MEDIUM-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 7. Guaranty Governed by New York Law and NY Forum – Inconsistent with Agreement

**Where:** `treireis_holdings_parent_guaranty.md`, §§9.1–9.2; `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §§16.1–16.2

**Issue:** The Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware. The Guaranty is governed by **New York** law with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York County / SDNY**.

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil needs to enforce the Guaranty in connection with a dispute under the Agreement (e.g., Distributor fails to pay, Fougeil terminates under Delaware law, then seeks payment from the parent under the Guaranty), it may face: (a) different substantive standards under NY vs. DE law for when an obligation is "due"; (b) the need to bring separate actions in different forums; and (c) potential inconsistent judgments. The Guaranty's broad "no set-off" clause (§8.1) and primary obligation language (§2.2) mitigate some risk, but the forum split creates practical complexity and cost.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to the Guaranty conforming governing law and forum to Delaware/Wilmington, matching the Agreement.
- If the parent resists (it may prefer NY law as more guarantor-friendly in some respects), at minimum add a provision permitting Supplier to join a Guaranty enforcement action to any pending Agreement action in Delaware, or to file the Guaranty action in Delaware as a related proceeding.

### 8. Survival of Exclusivity Covenant Post-Termination

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §§2.3, 13.5

**Issue:** §13.5 lists §2.3 among the sections that survive termination or expiration. §2.3 itself imposes the worldwide restriction "during the Term and for twenty-four (24) months thereafter."

**Why it matters:** Even if the Agreement is terminated early (e.g., for Distributor's insolvency under §13.2), Fougeil remains bound by the worldwide exclusivity covenant for 24 months post-termination. This means a failed distribution relationship still blocks Fougeil from selling competing veterinary diagnostics globally for two years.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment providing that §2.3 does not survive termination for Distributor's breach or insolvency (i.e., the post-termination tail applies only upon expiration without cause or Supplier's breach).
- Alternatively, reduce the post-termination tail to 12 months.

### 9. Service Credits Are Not the Exclusive Remedy for Service Level Failures

**Where:** `service_level_schedule.md`, §6.5

**Issue:** §6.5 states: "Service credits are a price adjustment that reflects the reduced value of the services. They **do not limit Distributor's other rights or remedies under the Agreement**."

**Why it matters:** If Fougeil chronically misses service levels (e.g., On-Time Shipment Rate below 95% for multiple quarters), Distributor can accumulate service credits *and* argue that the pattern constitutes a material breach under §13.1 of the Agreement, giving Distributor a termination right. The credits do not operate as a safe harbor or exclusive remedy.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to §6.5 providing that service credits are the sole and exclusive remedy for failure to meet the service levels set out in the Schedule, except in cases of willful misconduct or gross negligence.
- In the interim, monitor service level performance closely and ensure the quarterly reporting (§5.1) and business reviews (§5.2) are used proactively to address any trends before they become material.

### 10. Five-Year Supply Obligation for Discontinued Analyzers

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §5.3

**Issue:** If Fougeil discontinues an analyzer, it "shall continue to supply reagents and consumables for any discontinued analyzer for at least five (5) years after the discontinuance date."

**Why it matters:** This is a long-tail manufacturing and supply commitment. If Fougeil exits a product line (e.g., due to technology obsolescence, a patent expiration, or a strategic pivot), it must keep the reagent/consumable supply chain running for five years. This could be disproportionately costly for lower-volume products and conflicts with the 180-day discontinuance notice (which is the only advance warning Distributor gets).

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment adding: (a) a right to wind down the 5-year supply obligation if the discontinued analyzer's installed base falls below a specified threshold; (b) a requirement that Distributor purchase a reasonable volume of the reagents/consumables during the 5-year period (to prevent Fougeil from being forced to maintain a supply chain for a handful of units); or (c) a buy-out option allowing Fougeil to pay a lump sum to Distributor in lieu of the 5-year supply.

### 11. Demo Unit Conversion at 50% for the Entire Term

**Where:** `launch_support_side_letter.md`, §3.5; `product_price_list_2026.md`, §6

**Issue:** Distributor may convert Demo Units to owned units "at any time during the Term" at 50% of the then-current price, with Fougeil bearing the cost of refurbishment and re-verification. The Term is 60 months plus potential 60-month renewals.

**Why it matters:** Fougeil has a multi-year obligation to refurbish and sell analyzers at half price on demand. If Distributor holds onto Demo Units for years and then converts them just before a renewal decision, Fougeil bears the refurbishment cost and the 50% revenue haircut. The CY4 price list (§6) confirms the conversion prices ($5,320 for C200; $6,160 for H50), showing this is an ongoing, priced obligation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment limiting the conversion window (e.g., 24 months from delivery of the Demo Unit) or capping the total number of units convertible per Contract Year.
- Require Distributor to give Fougeil advance notice (e.g., 30 days) before requesting conversion, so Fougeil can plan refurbishment capacity.

### 12. Definition of "Products" – Cross-Reference to §5.2 Instead of §5.3

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §1.9; compare `distribution_agreement_prior_draft_v3.md`, §1.9

**Issue:** §1.9 defines "Products" as items "listed in the Order Form or in Supplier's then-current price list, as updated by Supplier under **Section 5.2**." Section 5.2 governs *price changes*. Section 5.3 governs *product additions, modifications and discontinuances*. Draft v3 §1.9 referenced "Section 5.3."

**Why it matters:** While the practical impact is limited (new products would appear in the price list regardless), the incorrect cross-reference could create ambiguity about the mechanism by which new Products are added to the agreement. If a dispute arose over whether a newly added item is a "Product" under the Agreement, the reference to §5.2 (pricing) rather than §5.3 (product changes) could be exploited.

**Recommended fix:**
- Correct via amendment to reference §5.3, or to read "as updated by Supplier under Section 5.2 or Section 5.3."

## LOWER-PRIORITY / OBSERVATIONS

### 13. Quality Agreement Applies to Demo Units

**Where:** `quality_agreement.md`, §1.2

**Issue:** The Quality Agreement's full scope (storage, traceability, complaint handling, adverse event reporting, recalls, audits) applies to Demo Units, even though they are not invoiced and do not count toward the MPC.

**Why it matters:** Fougeil bears the administrative and regulatory burden of tracking complaints, adverse events, and recall effectiveness for non-revenue units. A complaint on a Demo Unit triggers the same 2-business-day notification and investigation obligations as a complaint on a sold unit.

**Recommended fix:**
- Acceptable as drafted given the safety/regulatory context, but ensure internal processes are in place to track Demo Unit serial numbers and locations (the Side Letter §3.4 requires Distributor to maintain a log). Consider whether the Quality Agreement could be amended to provide a simplified complaint pathway for Demo Units (e.g., 5 business days instead of 2).

### 14. Guaranty §1.2(d) – Reference to "Section 11" Post-Amendment

**Where:** `treireis_holdings_parent_guaranty.md`, §1.2(d); `first_amendment_distribution_agreement.md`, §2

**Issue:** The Guaranty's definition of "Guaranteed Obligations" includes "amounts payable by Distributor under **Section 11** of the Agreement." After the First Amendment, Section 11 contains both Supplier Indemnification (§11.1) and Distributor Indemnification (§11.2). On its face, "amounts payable by Distributor under Section 11" should be read as limited to §11.2 (Distributor's indemnity), since §11.1 creates obligations payable *by* Supplier *to* Distributor, not the reverse.

**Why it matters:** Low risk in practice, but if a creative argument were made that the Guaranty covers "Section 11" broadly, it could create confusion in a guaranty enforcement action. The Guaranty's §3.2(a) (obligations not affected by amendments) means the Guaranty automatically tracks the amended Section 11 without Guarantor's consent, which is favorable to Fougeil.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the next amendment cycle, conform the reference to "Section 11.2" for clarity. Low urgency given the plain meaning.

### 15. Service Level Schedule – Corrective Action Plan Obligation

**Where:** `service_level_schedule.md`, §5.3

**Issue:** If Fougeil misses the same service level in two consecutive Measurement Periods, it must deliver a written corrective action plan within 20 business days of Distributor's request and implement it "with reasonable diligence."

**Why it matters:** This creates a documented record of service deficiencies that could be used by Distributor to support a material breach/termination argument (see Issue 9). It also imposes an ongoing operational obligation that may require resource allocation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Ensure internal monitoring of service levels so that any trend toward a second consecutive miss is identified early and addressed before Distributor requests a plan.
- If a plan is requested, ensure it is realistic and scoped narrowly to avoid creating an enforceable commitment to specific performance levels beyond the Schedule.

### 16. Order of Precedence – Side Letter Ranks Above Agreement

**Where:** `fougeil_treireis_distribution_agreement.md`, §17.3

**Issue:** §17.3 establishes the order of precedence as: (1) signed amendments and side letters; (2) the Distribution Agreement; (3) the Order Form; (4) the Quality Agreement; (5) the Service Level Schedule.

**Why it matters:** The Launch Support Side Letter (a "side letter") outranks the main Agreement. If there is any conflict between the Side Letter and the Agreement, the Side Letter controls. This is generally standard, but it means the Side Letter's terms (e.g., Demo Unit conversion at 50%, marketing fund) cannot be overridden by the Agreement's general provisions without amending the Side Letter itself.

**Recommended fix:**
- No action needed, but be aware that any future amendment to the Agreement that inadvertently conflicts with the Side Letter will not override the Side Letter. Ensure the amendment process checks for Side Letter consistency.
