## Summary

The deal package contains two issues that are potentially deal-threatening and several that require negotiation before signing. The most serious is that the Membrane Technology License Agreement—a core asset of the shell product line—cannot be assigned without Fjellbrand AG's written consent, yet the APA neither requires that consent as a closing condition nor covenants Seller to obtain it, and Seller's board has affirmatively authorized closing without it. The second is that Seller's member approval, which the APA requires Seller to represent as already obtained, has not in fact been obtained as of the date of the board consent.

---

## Issue 1 (Critical): Membrane Technology License – Assignment Without Required Consent

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 2.1(c): The Membrane Technology License Agreement is listed as a Purchased Asset (Assigned Contract).
- APA § 4.4 (Consents): Seller represents that no consents are required "except for the Landlord Consent and except as set forth in Schedule 4.4."
- APA Schedule 4.4: States "None."
- APA § 7.2(d): The only third-party consent made a condition to Buyer's closing obligation is the Landlord Consent. There is no condition requiring Fjellbrand's consent.
- Seller Board Consent § 1.4(a): Acknowledges that Section 14.2 of the Membrane Technology License Agreement prohibits assignment without Fjellbrand's prior written consent, including in connection with a sale of the shell business.
- Seller Board Consent § 1.4(b): Acknowledges that under Section 14.3, Fjellbrand may terminate the license on written notice if any assignment is made without consent.
- Seller Board Consent § 4.2: Authorizes Seller's officers to "proceed to close without first obtaining the prior written consent of Fjellbrand."
- Bill of Sale § 2.1(c): Conveys the Membrane Technology License Agreement as part of the Purchased Assets, with no reference to any consent condition.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The membrane technology license is the IP foundation of the shell product line. Without it, Stepair cannot manufacture or sell the core product it is paying $18.4 million to acquire. If Fjellbrand terminates the license post-closing (which it has the contractual right to do under § 14.3), Stepair loses the technology with no recourse under the APA because:

- The APA contains no covenant requiring Seller to obtain Fjellbrand's consent before closing.
- Fjellbrand's consent is not a condition to Stepair's closing obligation, so Stepair cannot simply walk if consent is not delivered.
- Seller's representation in § 4.4 (no consents required other than Landlord's) would be false, but the indemnification cap of $1.84 million (APA § 8.4) is a fraction of the value of the license.
- The Board Consent shows Seller is *willing* to close without the consent, meaning Seller may not be motivated to obtain it.

**Recommendation:**

Before signing, we should require the following amendments to the APA:

1. Add Fjellbrand's written consent to the assignment of the Membrane Technology License Agreement as a **condition to Buyer's closing obligation** in § 7.2 (alongside the existing Landlord Consent condition in § 7.2(d)).
2. Add a **covenant** in Article VI requiring Seller to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain Fjellbrand's consent prior to the Closing Date, and to deliver such consent as a Seller closing deliverable under § 3.2.
3. Amend **Schedule 4.4** to list Fjellbrand's consent.
4. Consider adding a specific **representation** that, as of the signing date, no event has occurred that would give Fjellbrand the right to terminate the license or that would reasonably be expected to result in Fjellbrand withholding consent.
5. If Seller resists making Fjellbrand's consent a hard condition (e.g., if Fjellbrand's response is uncertain), negotiate at minimum: (a) a specific indemnity from Seller for loss of the license that is *outside* the general cap in § 8.4, and (b) a termination right for Buyer if Fjellbrand's consent is not delivered by a date certain (e.g., 10 business days before the target Closing Date).

**Open question:** Have we had any direct contact with Fjellbrand or its counsel regarding the consent? What is Fjellbrand's commercial relationship with the business, and is there any indication it would withhold consent? If Fjellbrand has already been informally approached, that changes the risk calculus.

## Issue 2 (Critical): Seller Member Approval Not Yet Obtained

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 4.2 (Authority): Seller represents that "the execution, delivery and performance of this Agreement and the Ancillary Documents have been duly authorized by all requisite action of Seller's board of managers **and members**."
- Seller Board Consent § 5.1: The Board "recommends that the members of the Company approve the sale."
- Seller Board Consent § 5.2: "The Secretary is directed to submit the transaction to the members for approval by written consent."
- Seller Board Consent § 1.2: References the sale as being "under an Asset Purchase Agreement between the Company and Buyer, in substantially the form presented to the Board."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The APA is dated September 14, 2026. The Board Consent is dated September 11, 2026, and it makes clear that member approval is a *future* step ("directed to submit"). If Stepair signs the APA before the members have approved, Seller's representation in § 4.2 is false at signing. This creates several problems:

- Stepair's closing condition in § 7.2(a) (reps true and correct in all material respects) would not be satisfied, giving Stepair a technical right not to close—but that is a weak position to be in after signing.
- More practically, if the members do not approve, the deal cannot close, and Stepair has spent time and money on diligence and negotiation with no enforceable remedy (the exclusive remedy provision in § 8.6 would likely bar a tort claim for negligent misrepresentation).
- The § 4.2 representation is one of the "fundamental" reps carved out from the indemnification cap (APA § 8.4), so Stepair would have a claim—but only *after* closing, and only if Stepair closes despite knowing the rep is false, which is an awkward position.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Do not sign the APA until written member consent has been delivered.** Request that Seller provide the executed member consent as a condition to Stepair's obligation to sign, or at minimum as a closing deliverable under § 3.2.
2. Alternatively, amend § 4.2 to state that authorization by members will be obtained prior to the Closing Date, and add delivery of the member consent to § 3.2 (Seller Closing Deliverables) and § 7.2 (Conditions to Buyer's Obligations).
3. Confirm with Seller's counsel the expected timing of member approval. If members are expected to approve promptly, a short bridge (sign now, member consent before closing) may be acceptable—but the APA should be amended to reflect that the rep is as of the Closing Date, not the signing date, for this specific item.

**Open question:** How many members does Risel have, and is there any indication of dissent? If a member is likely to withhold consent, this is a deal-breaker risk that should be assessed before Stepair invests further.

## Issue 3 (High): Seller Can Assign the APA and Be Released from Indemnification and Non-Compete

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 11.5 (Successors and Assigns): "Seller may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, without the consent of Buyer to any Affiliate or to any Person acquiring all or substantially all of Seller's remaining assets. Any such assignment shall include Seller's obligations under Article VIII (Indemnification) and Section 6.8 (Non-Competition), and upon such assignment Seller shall be released from those obligations."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

After the closing, Seller retains its insulated apparel and footwear lines (APA § 2.2, Schedule 2.2). If Seller sells those remaining assets to a third party, that third party steps into Seller's indemnification and non-compete obligations, and **Seller is released**. This means:

- Stepair's $1.84 million indemnification recovery (or up to $18.4 million for fundamental reps) would depend on the creditworthiness of an unknown acquirer of Seller's remaining business.
- The 5-year, North America/Europe non-compete in § 6.8 would bind the unknown acquirer, not Seller. If the acquirer is a smaller entity or a financial buyer with no apparel operations, the non-compete is effectively worthless.
- Stepair has no consent right over the assignment.

**Recommendation:**

Amend § 11.5 to provide that:

1. Seller may not assign the Agreement (or any portion of it) without Buyer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld; **or**
2. If Seller assigns to a Person acquiring its remaining assets, the assignee must (a) have a tangible net worth of no less than $[X] (a figure to be negotiated, perhaps $5–10 million), (b) expressly assume all of Seller's obligations under Article VIII and § 6.8 in a written instrument delivered to Buyer, and (c) provide a parent guarantee or letter of credit backing the indemnification obligations.
3. In no event should Seller be released from its obligations unless the assignee meets the above conditions.

## Issue 4 (High): Bill of Sale – Conflicting Governing Law and Jurisdiction

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 11.7: New York governing law.
- APA § 11.8: Exclusive jurisdiction in New York County state or federal courts.
- Bill of Sale § 6.1: Delaware governing law.
- Bill of Sale § 6.2: Exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery.
- APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence): "In the event of any inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Document, the Ancillary Documents control as to matters specifically addressed therein."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

Because the Bill of Sale is an Ancillary Document, § 11.9 means the Bill of Sale's Delaware law and Court of Chancery jurisdiction provisions control "as to matters specifically addressed therein"—which arguably includes all disputes about title to and conveyance of the Purchased Assets. This creates:

- **Legal uncertainty:** A title dispute would be governed by Delaware law, while a dispute about, say, Seller's breach of a representation in the APA would be governed by New York law. The same factual scenario could yield different outcomes depending on how the claim is characterized.
- **Jurisdictional problem:** The Delaware Court of Chancery has limited subject-matter jurisdiction (primarily equitable and corporate governance matters). A breach-of-contract claim over the Bill of Sale might not belong in Chancery at all, creating a threshold fight about where the case can be filed.
- **Practical burden:** Stepair would potentially need to maintain Delaware counsel and litigate in two jurisdictions.

**Recommendation:**

Conform Bill of Sale §§ 6.1 and 6.2 to match the APA: New York governing law and exclusive jurisdiction in New York County courts. If Seller's counsel insists on Delaware law for the Bill of Sale (perhaps for tax or corporate reasons), at minimum conform the jurisdiction to the Delaware Superior Court (which has general jurisdiction) rather than the Court of Chancery, and add a provision that the APA's order of precedence (§ 11.9) does not apply to governing law and jurisdiction clauses.

## Issue 5 (High): Indemnification Cap and Survival Period

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 8.4: Seller's liability for rep/warranty breaches is subject to a $184,000 threshold (basket) and a $1,840,000 cap (10% of the Purchase Price).
- APA § 8.1: General survival period is 18 months post-closing. Fundamental reps (§§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.5, 4.14) survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

- The 10% cap is at the low end of market for an $18.4 million transaction. If Stepair discovers post-closing that a key supplier contract was in breach, that inventory was significantly obsolete, or that an IP asset is encumbered, its maximum recovery (for non-fundamental reps) is $1.84 million—less than 10% of what it paid.
- The 18-month survival period is relatively short. Latent defects in inventory, undisclosed IP infringement claims, or contract issues that take time to manifest may not surface within 18 months.
- The fundamental rep carve-out (organization, authority, title, taxes) is helpful but narrow. For example, the IP representation (§ 4.8) and the material contracts representation (§ 4.7) are *not* fundamental and are subject to both the cap and the 18-month survival.

**Recommendation:**

1. Negotiate the cap up to at least 15–20% of the Purchase Price ($2.76M–$3.68M).
2. Extend the general survival period to 24 months.
3. Add § 4.7 (Material Contracts) and § 4.8 (Intellectual Property) to the list of fundamental reps in § 8.1 and § 8.4, so they survive for the full statute of limitations and are not subject to the cap.
4. Consider a specific indemnity (outside the cap) for any loss of the Membrane Technology License (ties to Issue 1).

## Issue 6 (Moderate): Security Deposit – Unclear Treatment

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 4.1: Security Deposit is $65,250 (3 months' Base Rent), held by Landlord.
- Lease § 4.4: On a permitted assignment, Landlord shall transfer the Security Deposit to the assignee "provided the assignee delivers a replacement security deposit or Tenant authorizes the transfer in writing."
- APA § 2.1 (Purchased Assets): Does not explicitly include the Security Deposit.
- APA § 2.2 / Schedule 2.2 (Excluded Assets): Does not explicitly exclude the Security Deposit.
- APA § 2.6 (Purchase Price): $18.4 million, with no adjustment mechanism for the Security Deposit.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The Security Deposit is $65,250 of Seller's money held by Landlord. It is not clearly a Purchased Asset (it is not inventory, IP, a contract, equipment, books and records, or goodwill). It is not clearly an Excluded Asset either. If it is not a Purchased Asset:

- Seller retains the right to the deposit.
- Under Lease § 4.4, Stepair (as assignee) must either post a *new* $65,250 deposit or obtain Seller's written authorization for Landlord to transfer the existing deposit to Stepair's account.
- The APA's closing mechanics do not address this.

**Recommendation:**

1. Add the Security Deposit (or the right to its transfer) to the Purchased Assets in § 2.1, or add it to the Assumed Liabilities / purchase price adjustment mechanism.
2. Add a Seller closing deliverable requiring Seller to execute a written authorization under Lease § 4.4 directing Landlord to transfer the Security Deposit to Stepair.
3. Alternatively, reduce the Purchase Price by $65,250 and require Stepair to post a new deposit at closing.

## Issue 7 (Moderate): Lease Assignment – Tangible Net Worth Test

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 8.3(b): Landlord shall not withhold consent to an assignment in connection with a sale of the business if "the assignee has a tangible net worth, immediately after the transaction, at least equal to that of Tenant on the Effective Date."
- Lease § 1: Effective Date is February 1, 2024.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

Stepair must have a tangible net worth (immediately after the transaction) at least equal to Risel's tangible net worth on February 1, 2024. We do not know what Risel's tangible net worth was on that date. If Risel was well-capitalized at the start of the lease (e.g., $10M+ in tangible assets), Stepair may need to demonstrate that level of net worth to qualify for the mandatory consent provision. If Stepair cannot meet the test, Landlord has discretion to withhold consent (subject to the "not unreasonably withhold" standard in § 8.1), which creates closing risk.

**Recommendation:**

1. Request from Seller a balance sheet or financial statement as of February 1, 2024, to determine Risel's tangible net worth on the Effective Date.
2. Confirm Stepair's projected tangible net worth immediately after the transaction (i.e., after paying the $18.4M Purchase Price and assuming the Assumed Liabilities).
3. If there is a gap, negotiate with Landlord for a modified test (e.g., a fixed dollar amount, or a ratio to annual rent) before the APA is signed, so that the Landlord Consent condition in § 7.2(d) is achievable.

**Open question:** What is Stepair's current balance sheet, and what is its capital plan for the transaction? This needs to be confirmed with the client before we can assess whether § 8.3(b) is a real obstacle.

## Issue 8 (Moderate): Lease – Permitted Use Restriction

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 5.1: Permitted Use is limited to "executive and administrative offices, apparel design studio, pattern-making and sample-room use (including sewing, cutting and seam-taping equipment customary for apparel sample production), product showroom use by appointment." Expressly prohibited: "retail sales to the public or production manufacturing."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If Stepair's business plan for the Brooklyn space includes any scale production manufacturing (as opposed to sample-room work), it would be in breach of the lease from day one. The distinction between "sample-room use" and "production manufacturing" can be fact-specific and disputed.

**Recommendation:**

1. Confirm with the client exactly what operations it intends to conduct at the Brooklyn space.
2. If the intended use is within the Permitted Use, no action needed.
3. If the intended use is borderline or outside the Permitted Use, request an amendment to the lease (or a side letter from Landlord) expanding the Permitted Use before closing. This should be a condition to the Landlord Consent.

**Open question:** What is Stepair's operational plan for the Brooklyn space? Does it involve any production beyond sample-making?

## Issue 9 (Moderate): Lease – Subordination and Non-Disturbance

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 12.3: The lease is subordinate to any mortgage now or later encumbering the Building, "provided the mortgagee delivers a commercially reasonable non-disturbance agreement under which Tenant's possession will not be disturbed so long as no Event of Default exists."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If the Building is mortgaged and the mortgagee forecloses, Stepair's leasehold is subordinate unless it has a non-disturbance agreement (NDA). The lease contemplates an NDA but does not state that one has been delivered. Without an NDA, Stepair could lose its lease in a foreclosure.

**Recommendation:**

1. Request from Seller (or Landlord) confirmation of whether the Building is currently encumbered by a mortgage.
2. If so, request a copy of the existing non-disturbance agreement. If none exists, make delivery of an NDA from the mortgagee a condition to the Landlord Consent (and thus to closing under § 7.2(d)).
3. Add the NDA to Seller's closing deliverables in § 3.2.

**Open question:** Is the Building mortgaged? We need this information from Landlord or from a title search.

## Issue 10 (Lower): No Purchase Price Adjustment Mechanism

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 2.6: Purchase Price is a fixed $18,400,000, payable by wire at Closing.
- There is no working capital adjustment, no inventory adjustment, and no post-closing true-up mechanism in the APA.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If the inventory at closing is significantly different in quantity or quality from what was represented (e.g., more obsolete stock, less finished goods), Stepair has no mechanism to adjust the price. Its only recourse is the indemnification claim, subject to the $184K basket and $1.84M cap. For an asset purchase where inventory is a major component of value, a fixed price with no adjustment is risky.

**Recommendation:**

1. Conduct a physical inventory count before closing (the APA gives Stepair access rights under § 6.2). Compare the count to Seller's books.
2. If the inventory is a material component of the purchase price, negotiate a post-closing inventory adjustment (e.g., price adjusts dollar-for-dollar for inventory variance beyond a de minimis threshold).
3. At minimum, ensure the pre-closing inventory count is a condition to closing or is performed within a narrow window before the Closing Date.

## Issue 11 (Lower): MAE Definition Lacks Standard Carve-Outs

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 1.1(k): "Material Adverse Effect" is defined without any exclusions for general economic conditions, industry-wide downturns, changes in law, or the pendency of the transaction itself.
- APA § 7.2(e): No MAE is a condition to Buyer's closing obligation.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The absence of carve-outs actually *favors* the buyer (a broader MAE definition gives Stepair more grounds to walk if conditions deteriorate). However, it also means Seller could argue that a general market downturn in the outdoor apparel industry is an MAE that prevents closing—cutting both ways. The bigger practical risk is that without a carve-out for the effects of the announcement or pendency of the transaction, Seller could argue that customer or supplier hesitation caused by the pending deal constitutes an MAE, giving Seller an argument that *Stepair's* condition is not satisfied (though this would be unusual).

**Recommendation:**

This is a lower-priority item. If we are already negotiating the MAE condition, consider adding standard carve-outs (general economic conditions, industry-wide trends, changes in GAAP) to make the definition more balanced and less likely to be disputed. If the definition is left as-is, it is marginally favorable to Stepair as buyer.

## Issue 12 (Lower): Bulk Sales Waiver

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 6.7: "The Parties waive compliance with any applicable bulk sales law. Any liability arising from noncompliance is an Excluded Liability."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

New York has a bulk sales statute (UCC Article 6, though NY's version was repealed effective 2015 for most transactions). If the waiver is ineffective or if a creditor of Seller claims it was prejudiced by the lack of bulk sales notice, the liability falls on Seller as an Excluded Liability. This is acceptable for Stepair, but worth confirming that NY's bulk sales law is indeed inapplicable or that the waiver is effective.

**Recommendation:**

Confirm with NY counsel that the bulk sales waiver is appropriate and that no bulk sales filing is required for this transaction. If a filing is required, add it to Seller's pre-closing covenants.
