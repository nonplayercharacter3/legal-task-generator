## 1. CRITICAL: Membrane Technology License – No Fjellbrand Consent, False Rep, and Inadequate Protection

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 2.1(c) and Schedule 2.1(c) item 1: The Membrane Technology License Agreement (dated March 3, 2023, between Fjellbrand AG and Seller) is an Assigned Contract and a Purchased Asset.
- APA § 4.4 and Schedule 4.4: Seller represents that no third-party consent is required for the assignment of any Assigned Contract, other than the Landlord Consent. Schedule 4.4 states "None."
- APA § 7.2: No condition to Buyer's closing obligation requires delivery of Fjellbrand's consent.
- Seller Board Consent § 1.4(a)–(b): The Seller's own board consent expressly acknowledges that § 14.2 of the license prohibits assignment without Fjellbrand's prior written consent (including by operation of law or in connection with a sale of substantially all assets), and that § 14.3 allows Fjellbrand to terminate the license upon an unconsented assignment.
- Seller Board Consent § 4.2: The Board authorizes officers to "proceed to close without first obtaining the prior written consent of Fjellbrand."
- APA § 8.4: The indemnification cap for breaches of Seller's representations (including § 4.4) is $1,840,000 (10% of the Purchase Price). The consent rep (§ 4.4) is not among the reps excepted from the cap (only §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.5, and 4.14 are).

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The membrane license is the technology underlying the entire shell product line. If Fjellbrand terminates the license after an unconsented assignment, Stepair pays $18.4 million for a business whose core IP is no longer available. The APA's consent rep is flatly contradicted by the Seller's own board consent, making it a known inaccuracy at signing. Because § 4.4 is not carved out of the indemnification cap, Stepair's maximum recovery for this breach is $1.84 million—roughly one-tenth of the purchase price—while the actual loss could be the entire enterprise value of the shell line. There is no closing condition that would allow Stepair to walk if Fjellbrand's consent is not in hand.

**Recommendation:**

- **Before signing:** Require the Seller to obtain Fjellbrand's written consent to the assignment and deliver it as a condition to closing (add to § 7.2). If the Seller resists, require at minimum that Fjellbrand's consent be a closing deliverable under § 3.2 and that the APA be amended to (a) delete "None" from Schedule 4.4 and add Fjellbrand's consent, and (b) add § 4.4 to the list of reps excepted from the indemnification cap in § 8.4.
- **If Fjellbrand consent cannot be obtained before signing:** Consider whether the deal can proceed at all. The membrane technology is the heart of the business. If the license is terminable at Fjellbrand's option upon an unconsented assignment, the "Purchased Assets" as defined may not include a valid, continuing right to the technology. Stepair should not close without this resolved.
- **Open question:** We have not seen the full text of the Membrane Technology License Agreement. We need to confirm whether § 14.2 contains any carve-out (e.g., consent "not to be unreasonably withheld") or whether Fjellbrand has any economic incentive to consent. We should request a copy from the Seller's data room.

## 2. Seller Member Approval Not Yet Obtained – Rep Already Inaccurate at Signing

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 4.2: Seller represents that the transaction "has been duly authorized by all requisite action of Seller's board of managers **and members**."
- Seller Board Consent § 5.1–5.2: The Board *recommends* member approval and *directs the Secretary to submit* the transaction to the members for written consent. The consent itself is dated September 11, 2026; the APA is dated September 14, 2026.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

Under the Operating Agreement (referenced in the board consent), member approval is required for this sale. The board consent makes clear that member approval has not yet been obtained—it is still to be solicited. Yet the APA, signed three days later, already represents that member authorization is complete. If the members do not approve, or if approval is conditioned on terms different from the APA, the § 4.2 rep is false at signing and the Seller may lack authority to close. This also creates a gap between signing and closing during which the deal could be undermined.

**Recommendation:**

- Require delivery of the executed member written consent as a condition to closing (add to § 7.2) or as a closing deliverable under § 3.2.
- Alternatively, if the members have already informally agreed, require that the written consent be executed and delivered before the APA is signed, so that the § 4.2 rep is true when made.
- **Open question:** We do not have the Operating Agreement. We should confirm the exact threshold for member approval (unanimous? majority by capital? majority by headcount?) and whether any member has a veto or appraisal right.

## 3. Bill of Sale – Conflicting Governing Law and Jurisdiction

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 11.7: New York law governs the APA.
- APA § 11.8: Exclusive jurisdiction in New York County state or federal courts.
- Bill of Sale § 6.1: Delaware law governs the Bill of Sale.
- Bill of Sale § 6.2: Exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery.
- APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence): In the event of inconsistency, the Ancillary Documents control "as to matters specifically addressed therein."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

Under the APA's own precedence rule, the Bill of Sale's Delaware law and Chancery Court jurisdiction would govern disputes about the conveyance of the Purchased Assets. This creates several problems:

- The Court of Chancery has limited subject-matter jurisdiction (primarily fiduciary duties, internal corporate matters, and certain equitable claims). A dispute over whether inventory or equipment was properly conveyed, or over a breach of the further-assurances covenant (Bill of Sale § 5.1), may not be cognizable in Chancery, leaving a jurisdictional gap.
- Splitting governing law (NY for the APA, DE for the Bill of Sale) and forum (NY County for the APA, Chancery for the Bill of Sale) complicates any multi-issue dispute and increases litigation cost.
- Delaware law on the conveyance of personal property may differ from New York law in ways that affect title (e.g., statutory short forms, merger doctrine).

**Recommendation:**

- Amend Bill of Sale § 6 to conform to the APA: New York governing law and New York County jurisdiction. If the Seller insists on Delaware law for the Bill of Sale (perhaps for tax or entity-law reasons), at minimum carve out claims for breach of the further-assurances covenant and claims for specific performance to the NY forum, and confirm that the Chancery Court would actually have jurisdiction over the types of disputes likely to arise.
- **Open question:** Confirm with the Seller why Delaware law was selected for the Bill of Sale. It may be a drafting error carried over from a template.

## 4. Studio Lease – No Estoppel Certificate Required at Closing

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 3.2 and § 7.2: The Seller's closing deliverables and Buyer's conditions to closing include the Landlord Consent but do not require an estoppel certificate from the Landlord.
- Lease § 12.2: Either party may request an estoppel certificate, and the other must deliver it within 10 business days.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

Stepair is paying $18.4 million for a business that includes a lease with approximately 4.3 years remaining (through February 28, 2031) plus a renewal option. Without an estoppel certificate, Stepair has no independent confirmation from the Landlord that (a) the lease is in full force and effect, (b) rent is current, (c) no defaults exist, and (d) the Security Deposit is on file in the stated amount. The Seller's rep in § 4.7 (Material Contracts) covers this, but a rep is only as good as the indemnity behind it—and the indemnity cap is $1.84 million.

**Recommendation:**

- Add to § 7.2 (conditions to Buyer's closing) the requirement that Stepair receive an estoppel certificate from the Landlord, dated within 30 days of the Closing Date, confirming the lease is in full force, stating the rent due and the date through which rent has been paid, and confirming no defaults.
- Coordinate with the Landlord Consent process: when the Landlord responds to the assignment request under Lease § 8.2, request the estoppel certificate at the same time to avoid a second round of communication.

## 5. Studio Lease – Security Deposit Not Addressed in the Deal Documents

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 4.1: Security Deposit is $65,250 (three months' Base Rent).
- Lease § 4.4: On a permitted assignment, the Landlord shall transfer the Security Deposit to the assignee's account, "provided the assignee delivers a replacement security deposit or Tenant authorizes the transfer in writing."
- APA: The Security Deposit is not listed as a Purchased Asset, an Assumed Liability, or a closing deliverable. It is not mentioned anywhere in the APA or the Bill of Sale.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The $65,250 deposit belongs to the tenant (Seller) as security for its lease obligations. Upon assignment, the deposit should either transfer to Stepair or Stepair should put up a new one. The deal documents are silent. If the Lease Assignment (which we have not seen in final form) does not address this, there is a risk that (a) the Landlord retains the deposit and requires Stepair to post a new one (a $65,250 cash outlay not reflected in the Purchase Price), or (b) the deposit is returned to the Seller, giving the Seller a windfall at Stepair's expense.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm that the Lease Assignment (a closing deliverable under APA § 3.2(b)) includes a provision for the transfer of the Security Deposit to Stepair, or that the Seller authorizes the transfer in writing per Lease § 4.4.
- If the deposit will not transfer, add it to the Assumed Liabilities or adjust the Purchase Price accordingly.
- **Open question:** We have not seen the form of Lease Assignment. We need to review it to confirm how the deposit is handled.

## 6. Studio Lease – Tangible Net Worth Test May Block Mandatory Consent

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 8.3: The Landlord "shall not withhold its consent" to an assignment in connection with a sale of substantially all assets if, among other things, "(b) the assignee has a tangible net worth, immediately after the transaction, at least equal to that of Tenant on the Effective Date."
- The Effective Date of the lease is February 1, 2024 (Lease § preamble).

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

This is the provision that converts the Landlord's consent from discretionary to mandatory. If Stepair's tangible net worth after the transaction (i.e., after paying the $18.4 million Purchase Price) is less than Seller's tangible net worth on February 1, 2024, the Landlord is not obligated to consent and could withhold it on that basis. We do not know what Seller's tangible net worth was on that date. If Stepair is using significant debt or has a thinner balance sheet than Seller did two and a half years ago, this test could fail.

**Recommendation:**

- **Open question:** Request Seller's balance sheet as of February 1, 2024 (or the nearest available date) to determine the tangible net worth benchmark. Compare it to Stepair's projected post-closing tangible net worth.
- If there is a gap, negotiate with the Landlord in advance to agree that the test will be satisfied, or seek an amendment to § 8.3(b) (e.g., a fixed dollar threshold rather than a comparison to Seller's historical net worth).
- In any event, start the Landlord consent process early (Lease § 8.2 requires 20 days' notice before the proposed effective date) and include Stepair's most recent financial statements with the request.

## 7. Studio Lease – Permitted Use May Not Cover Stepair's Plans

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 5.1: Permitted Use is limited to "executive and administrative offices, apparel design studio, pattern-making and sample-room use (including sewing, cutting and seam-taping equipment customary for apparel sample production), product showroom use by appointment." It expressly excludes "retail sales to the public or production manufacturing."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If Stepair intends to use the space for anything beyond sample-room operations and administrative work—for example, small-batch production, a retail pop-up, or a larger showroom open to the public without appointment—it would be in breach of the lease from day one. The APA's rep in § 4.6 (Sufficiency of Assets) states that the Purchased Assets are "necessary to conduct the Business as currently conducted," but if Stepair plans to conduct the business differently from Seller, the lease may not support that.

**Recommendation:**

- **Open question:** Confirm with the client what it intends to do in the space. If the use is within the current Permitted Use, no action is needed. If not, require a lease amendment expanding the Permitted Use as a condition to closing, or exclude the lease from the Purchased Assets and have Stepair negotiate its own space.

## 8. Excluded ERP Software – Potential Gap in "Sufficiency of Assets" Rep

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 2.2 and Schedule 2.2 item 3: "Seller's corporate-level enterprise resource planning software licenses" are Excluded Assets.
- APA § 4.6: "The Purchased Assets constitute all of the rights, property and assets necessary to conduct the Business as currently conducted."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If the Business's operations (inventory management, order processing, supplier coordination) run on the excluded ERP system, Stepair cannot conduct the Business "as currently conducted" without it. The § 4.6 rep would be inaccurate. Even if the ERP is truly "corporate-level" and shared across Seller's other lines (insulated apparel, footwear), there may be Business-specific configurations, data, or integrations that Stepair needs.

**Recommendation:**

- **Open question:** Confirm with the client (and through diligence) whether the ERP system is used in the day-to-day operations of the shell business. If it is, either (a) negotiate to include a license or transition services agreement for the ERP in the Purchased Assets, or (b) require the Seller to deliver all Business-specific data in a usable format and to cooperate with a transition, and add a specific covenant to that effect.
- If the ERP is genuinely not used in the Business, no action is needed, but we should document that conclusion.

## 9. Indemnification Cap and Survival – Thin Protection for an $18.4 Million Deal

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 8.4: Basket of $184,000; cap of $1,840,000 for breaches of representations and warranties. Exceptions from the cap: fraud and breaches of §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.5, and 4.14.
- APA § 8.1: General survival of 18 months post-closing.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

- The cap is 10% of the Purchase Price. For a deal of this size, 10% is on the low end (market for deals in this range is often 15–25%, or uncapped for fundamental reps). The most valuable rep for Stepair—§ 4.4 (Consents)—is not excepted from the cap.
- Eighteen months of survival is short. Product liability claims, latent IP infringement, or tax issues may not surface within that window. The fundamental reps (organization, authority, title, taxes) get longer survival (statute of limitations plus 60 days), but the operational reps (financial statements, inventory, material contracts, IP, compliance) all fall off at 18 months.
- The exclusive remedy provision (§ 8.6) means that for non-fraud claims, Stepair cannot sue for breach of contract outside the indemnification framework. If the cap is hit, Stepair is out of luck even if the actual loss is greater.

**Recommendation:**

- Negotiate to increase the cap to at least $3,680,000 (20% of the Purchase Price) or, preferably, make the consent rep (§ 4.4), the IP rep (§ 4.8), and the material contracts rep (§ 4.7) fundamental reps excepted from the cap.
- Negotiate to extend general survival to 24 months, with tax and environmental reps surviving for the full statute of limitations.
- If the Seller will not move on the cap, at minimum ensure that the Fjellbrand issue is resolved before closing (see Issue 1) so that the cap is not the binding constraint on Stepair's most likely loss scenario.

## 10. Non-Compete – Gap for Seller's Retained Lines

**Where it appears:**

- APA § 6.8: Five-year non-compete restricted to "technical outerwear shell products" in North America and Europe.
- APA § 2.2 and Schedule 2.2: Seller retains its "insulated apparel line" and "footwear line."

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

The non-compete does not prevent Seller from competing in insulated outerwear, which is a natural adjacent category and may overlap with shell products in the eyes of customers (e.g., a 3-layer system that combines a shell with an insulated mid-layer). If Seller's retained insulated apparel line markets itself as a system that includes shell components, or if it launches a new shell product under a different technical description, the non-compete may not reach it. The geographic scope (North America and Europe) is reasonable, but the product scope is narrow.

**Recommendation:**

- Expand the non-compete to cover "technical outerwear products, including shell and insulated shell products" or to reference the specific product categories in which Seller competes. At minimum, add a non-solicitation of Stepair's customers that is not limited to the shell product line.
- **Open question:** Confirm with the client whether Seller's insulated apparel line currently markets any products that could be characterized as "shell" products (e.g., insulated hardshells). If so, the gap is more acute.

## 11. Lease – Subordination Without SNDA Delivered at Closing

**Where it appears:**

- Lease § 12.3: The lease is subordinate to any mortgage on the Building, "provided the mortgagee delivers a commercially reasonable non-disturbance agreement."
- APA § 3.2: No requirement for the Seller or Landlord to deliver an SNDA at closing.

**Why it matters to Stepair:**

If the Building is encumbered by a mortgage and the Landlord defaults, Stepair's leasehold is subordinate to the mortgage. The lease provides for a non-disturbance agreement, but nothing in the deal documents requires that one be delivered before or at closing. If Stepair takes the lease without an SNDA in hand, it has no protection against loss of the premises in a foreclosure.

**Recommendation:**

- Require delivery of an executed SNDA from the mortgagee (or confirmation that no mortgage exists) as a condition to closing or as a post-closing covenant with a short outside date.
- At minimum, require the Landlord to represent in the Landlord Consent that the lease is not currently in default and that no foreclosure proceedings are pending.
