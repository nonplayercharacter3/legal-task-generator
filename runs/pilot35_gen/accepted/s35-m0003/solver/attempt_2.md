## I. Overview

Gliruth Event Rentals, LLC ("Gliruth") licenses the RentFlow inventory, booking, and dispatch platform from Breibeith Software, Inc. ("Breibeith") under a Software License Agreement dated July 1, 2023 (the "License Agreement") and a companion RentFlow Quality Agreement dated the same day (the "Quality Agreement"). The Initial Term ends June 30, 2026. Gliruth sent a notice of non-renewal on March 20, 2026, and has disclosed a pending acquisition by Ostrander Venue Group, Inc. ("Ostrander"). This memo identifies the key issues in the file and recommends next steps.

## II. Issues Identified

### A. Non-Renewal Notice Was Sent After the Contractual Deadline

**Where:** License Agreement § 12.2; Gliruth letter dated March 20, 2026, ¶ 1.

**The problem.** Section 12.2 provides that the Agreement renews automatically for successive two-year terms "unless either Party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 120 days before the end of the then-current term." The Initial Term ends June 30, 2026. Counting back 120 days, the deadline was **March 2, 2026**. Gliruth's letter is dated **March 20, 2026**—eighteen days late.

**Why it matters.** On its face, the automatic renewal in Section 12.2 has already been triggered. If Breibeith treats the Agreement as renewed for a two-year Renewal Term (July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2028), Gliruth would be locked in for another $768,000+ in License Fees (subject to up to a 5% increase under § 4.4) and, critically, the exclusivity obligation in § 3.3 would continue to bar Gliruth and its Affiliates from using any competing inventory, booking, logistics, or point-of-sale software "anywhere in the world."

Gliruth's letter disputes that any renewal has occurred and reserves its rights, which is helpful but does not cure the timing defect. Breibeith has a colorable argument that the notice was untimely.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Immediately open a dialogue with Breibeith to negotiate a **mutual waiver of the renewal** or an **early termination agreement** effective June 30, 2026. Frame it around Gliruth's pending sale to Ostrander (see Issue B below), which gives Breibeith a commercial reason to agree to a clean exit.
2. If Breibeith resists, assess whether any **material breach** by Breibeith (e.g., chronic service failures under Quality Agreement § 6.5) would support termination under License Agreement § 13.2, which would end the Agreement before the renewal date and moot the issue.
3. Document the timeline precisely. If there is any argument that the 120-day period should be measured differently (e.g., from the date of the last contract-year invoice), preserve it, but do not build the strategy on it.

### B. Pending Ostrander Merger Triggers Change-of-Control Consent Requirement

**Where:** License Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 14.2; Gliruth letter dated March 20, 2026, ¶ 3.

**The problem.** Section 14.2 provides that "Any Change of Control of Licensee, including by merger, is deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee and requires the prior written consent of Licensor." The Ostrander acquisition—under which Ostrander will acquire Control of Gliruth—squarely meets the definition in § 1.1(b). Gliruth has **not** obtained Breibeith's consent. The merger agreement was signed March 12, 2026, and the letter discloses the transaction "for information only."

**Why it matters.** If Gliruth closes the Ostrander merger without Breibeith's consent:

- Breibeith may **terminate** the Agreement on written notice (§ 14.2).
- **All License Fees for the remainder of the then-current term become immediately due and payable** (§ 14.2). If the Initial Term is still the operative term, this means fees through June 30, 2026. But if the Agreement has already auto-renewed (see Issue A), the "then-current term" is the two-year Renewal Term, and the acceleration would capture roughly **$768,000–$806,400** (with a potential 5% increase).
- Post-closing, Ostrander and its Affiliates would be bound by the exclusivity restriction in § 3.3 if the Agreement continues.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Before the Ostrander merger closes**, obtain Breibeith's written consent to the Change of Control under § 14.2. This is non-negotiable from a risk-management standpoint.
2. In the consent request, bundle it with the non-renewal/early-termination discussion (Issue A). A reasonable ask: Breibeith consents to the CoC, waives the fee-acceleration provision, and agrees that the Agreement will terminate June 30, 2026 with no Renewal Term.
3. If Breibeith conditions consent on continued performance (i.e., it wants the Agreement to survive the CoC and renew), negotiate a **limited consent** that (a) waives acceleration, (b) carves Ostrander out of the § 3.3 exclusivity, and (c) confirms the June 30, 2026 termination date.
4. Add the CoC consent as a **closing condition** in the Ostrander merger agreement if not already present.

### C. Exclusivity Clause Is Overly Broad and Operates During Wind-Down

**Where:** License Agreement § 3.3.

**The problem.** Section 3.3 prohibits Gliruth and its Affiliates from licensing, developing, or using "any inventory management, booking, logistics or point-of-sale software other than the Software, anywhere in the world" during the Term. The restriction also required migration of existing software within 90 days of the Effective Date (i.e., by September 29, 2023).

**Why it matters.** Even assuming the Agreement ends June 30, 2026 as Gliruth intends, Gliruth **cannot begin implementing a replacement platform** until that date. For an event rental business with a peak spring/summer season, this means Gliruth may need to run RentFlow through the busiest part of its year while simultaneously planning a transition it is not yet permitted to execute. If the Agreement renews (Issue A), the lock-in extends two more years.

**Recommended fix.**

1. As part of the negotiation with Breibeith (Issues A and B), seek a **mutual release from § 3.3** effective no later than April 30, 2026, to allow Gliruth to evaluate and begin implementing an alternative platform before the peak season.
2. If Breibeith will not agree to an early release, at minimum negotiate a **transition period** (e.g., 60–90 days post-termination) during which Gliruth may use the Software in read-only or limited mode while migrating data.

### D. Governing Law and Forum Conflict Between the Two Agreements

**Where:** License Agreement §§ 16.1–16.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington, DE courts) and 17.4 (order of precedence); Quality Agreement §§ 9.1–9.2 (New York law; New York County, NY courts).

**The problem.** The License Agreement designates Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington. The Quality Agreement designates New York law and exclusive jurisdiction in New York County for disputes relating to "the performance, availability or accuracy of the Software." Section 17.4 of the License Agreement provides that the Quality Agreement "controls on matters it addresses" in the event of a conflict.

**Why it matters.** This creates a **bifurcated dispute-resolution framework**. A claim for service credits, chronic failure, or data-accuracy breach would be brought in New York under New York law. A claim for wrongful termination, fee acceleration, or a CoC dispute would be brought in Delaware under Delaware law. If a single dispute implicates both (e.g., Gliruth terminates for chronic failure and Breibeith counters with fee acceleration), the parties could face parallel or sequential proceedings in two different forums. Neither forum is in Ohio, where Gliruth is based.

**Recommended fix.**

1. In any renegotiation (see Issues A–C), propose **harmonizing the governing law and forum** to a single jurisdiction. Delaware is the natural choice given the License Agreement's broader scope, but New York is also reasonable.
2. If the parties cannot agree on a single forum, at minimum add a **consolidation provision** requiring that related claims be heard in a single proceeding.
3. For the immediate term, be aware that any performance-related claims (e.g., asserting chronic failure under Quality Agreement § 6.5 to support termination) would need to be filed in New York.

### E. Data Export and Transition Timing

**Where:** License Agreement § 7.4; Gliruth letter dated March 20, 2026, ¶ 2.

**The problem.** Section 7.4 requires Breibeith to make Licensee Data available for export "within thirty (30) days after expiration or termination." If the Agreement ends June 30, 2026, the export deadline is July 30, 2026. Gliruth's letter asks Breibeith to confirm the export procedure within 15 business days (i.e., by approximately April 10, 2026) and to schedule a call in April to discuss file formats and timing.

**Why it matters.** Gliruth needs its inventory records, customer information, event bookings, delivery schedules, and dispatch instructions to stand up a replacement system. A July 30 export date is too late if Gliruth wants to transition before or during the peak season. The "standard, machine-readable format" requirement in § 7.4 is helpful but does not specify which format (CSV, JSON, API export, etc.), and there is a risk that Breibeith's export will be incomplete or difficult to map to a new platform.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Follow up with Breibeith if it has not responded to the letter within 15 business days. Escalate to a call with the named customer success manager (Quality Agreement § 2.3).
2. Negotiate an **early export date** (e.g., May 15, 2026) as part of the transition agreement. Gliruth should be entitled to a test export before the final one to validate data completeness.
3. Specify acceptable export formats in writing (e.g., CSV for tabular data, JSON for structured records) to avoid a dispute over whether Breibeith's output meets the "standard, machine-readable" requirement.
4. Confirm that the export will include **full dispatch history**, not just active bookings, since Gliruth may need historical data for customer relationships and reporting post-transition.

### F. Limited Remedies for Service Failures

**Where:** Quality Agreement §§ 6.1–6.6; License Agreement §§ 8.2, 10.1–10.3.

**The problem.** Quality Agreement § 6.6 states that Service Credits and the chronic-failure termination right in § 6.5 are "Licensee's remedies for Licensor's failure to meet the standards in this Quality Agreement." This is an **exclusive-remedy clause**. Combined with the License Agreement's consequential-damages waiver (§ 10.1) and the 12-month-fee liability cap (§ 10.2, approximately $384,000), Gliruth's recovery for underperformance is structurally limited.

The maximum monthly Service Credit is 25% of the Monthly Fee Amount (Quality Agreement § 6.3), which is approximately **$8,000 per month**.

**Why it matters.** If RentFlow has been underperforming—potentially a factor in Gliruth's decision to exit—Gliruth's financial recourse is capped at modest credits plus the right to terminate for chronic failure. Gliruth cannot recover lost profits or other consequential damages from a service outage, even if it loses a major event booking because the dispatch system was down.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Document all service failures** now. Request the quarterly reports required by Quality Agreement § 5.4 for each quarter of the Initial Term. If Breibeith has not delivered them, send a formal request. These reports will establish whether the chronic-failure thresholds (§ 6.5) have been met, which would give Gliruth a termination right under § 13.2 independent of the non-renewal issue.
2. If chronic failure has occurred, consider asserting termination under § 13.2 **before** June 30, 2026. This would (a) end the Agreement on Gliruth's terms, (b) avoid the auto-renewal problem, and (c) entitle Gliruth to a refund of prepaid fees under § 13.4.
3. For the remaining term, ensure Gliruth is tracking Availability and Incident data from the administrative dashboard (Quality Agreement § 3.2) to preserve evidence.

### G. Insurance: Compliant, But Monitor Renewal

**Where:** License Agreement § 11.1; Pellacott cover letter dated February 9, 2026.

**The problem.** This is not a deficiency, but a confirmation item. The Pellacott letter confirms that Gliruth's CGL policy carries $2,000,000 per occurrence (meeting the § 11.1 minimum), the insurer (Harbor Meridian) is rated A by A.M. Best (exceeding the A- minimum), and the insurer is authorized in Ohio and other states where Gliruth operates. The policy period runs January 1, 2026 to January 1, 2027.

**Why it matters.** If the Agreement auto-renews through June 30, 2028 (Issue A), the current policies expire January 1, 2027. Gliruth would need to maintain compliant coverage for the full Renewal Term. The Pellacott letter commits to sending updated certificates at renewal, which is adequate.

**Recommended fix.** No action needed now. If the Agreement renews, ensure the January 2027 policy renewal maintains the § 11.1 limits and that the updated certificate is delivered to Breibeith.
