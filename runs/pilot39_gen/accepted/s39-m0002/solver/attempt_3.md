## Issue 1 – Parent Guaranty Demand Window for Lot 2207 Has Likely Expired

**Where:** Parent Guaranty § 4.1; Order Form No. 3, § 5, Paragraph 2; MSA § 13.2.

**Explanation:** Guaranty § 4.1 provides that the Guarantor is liable "only if Beneficiary delivers a Demand to Guarantor within forty-five (45) days after Beneficiary first notifies Provider of the Default giving rise to that Guaranteed Obligation." Zaseil delivered its written Default Notice to Provider on August 3, 2026 (Order Form No. 3, § 5, Para. 2). Forty-five days from August 3, 2026 is September 17, 2026. Order Form No. 3, § 5, Para. 2 (dated October 20, 2026) expressly states: "As of the Order Form Date, no Demand has been made on the Guarantor under the Parent Guaranty for the matters described in the Default Notice." The 45-day window has therefore lapsed.

Unless Zaseil can persuasively argue that Provider's continued failure to remedy constitutes a new, separate default (triggering a new 45-day period from a new notice), Zaseil has lost its right to enforce the Parent Guaranty for the Lot 2207 claims. This is the single most time-sensitive issue in the file.

**Why it matters:** The Lot 2207 default involves failed FR performance (CPAI-84 and NFPA 701 Test Method 2 failures on Tarpaulin Line goods). If the defect is systemic or if the financial exposure exceeds what Prodous Coating Services LLC (a subsidiary) can absorb, the Guaranty is Zaseil's primary credit backstop. Losing it leaves Zaseil with an unsecured claim against the subsidiary only.

**Recommended fix:**
- Immediately assess whether a new Default Notice can be framed as relating to a distinct default (e.g., Provider's failure to cure within the 30-day period under MSA § 13.2, or Provider's failure to agree on a remedy under MSA § 6.2) and whether a demand tied to that new default is still timely. This is a close question; the Guaranty's "first notifies" language cuts against Zaseil.
- If a demand is still viable, make it now, well before any further deadline.
- Going forward, implement an internal protocol: any notice of default to Provider triggers a calendar entry to make a Guaranty demand within 40 days (leaving a 5-day buffer).
- **Requires Prodous/Guarantor agreement to fix prospectively:** Amend Guaranty § 4.1 to extend the demand window (e.g., to 90 days) or to tie the clock to Provider's failure to cure rather than Zaseil's initial notice.

**Flag – cannot resolve from documents alone:** We need to confirm from the client (a) the exact date the Default Notice was delivered (the Order Form states August 3, 2026, but we should verify against Zaseil's records), (b) whether any subsequent notices were sent to Provider regarding Lot 2207 that could restart or extend the clock, and (c) the current status of Provider's root-cause analysis and whether it proposed a specific remedy and when.

## Issue 2 – Provider's Unfettered Assignment Right Terminates the Parent Guaranty Automatically

**Where:** MSA § 14.2; Parent Guaranty § 6.2.

**Explanation:** MSA § 14.2 permits Provider to "assign or transfer the Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, to any person without Customer's consent, upon written notice to Customer." Parent Guaranty § 6.2 provides that the Guaranty "terminates automatically, without further action by any person, on any assignment or transfer by Provider of the Agreement or any SOW that is permitted under the MSA." Read together, Provider can assign the contract to any entity—regardless of creditworthiness—and the moment the assignment is made, the Guaranty is extinguished. Zaseil has no consent right, no right to require a replacement guaranty, and no notice period before the assignment takes effect (only after-the-fact written notice).

**Why it matters:** The entire purpose of the Parent Guaranty (MSA § 15) is to give Zaseil the credit support of Prodous Industrial Holdings, Inc. If Provider assigns to a newly formed LLC with no assets, Zaseil's recourse is against that empty entity, and the parent guaranty is gone. This is particularly concerning given that Lot 2207 has already demonstrated quality failures.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend MSA § 14.2 to require Customer's prior written consent for any assignment by Provider (mirroring § 14.1's restriction on Customer), or at minimum to require that any assignee assume the Guaranteed Obligations and that the Guaranty not terminate unless a replacement guaranty of equivalent creditworthiness is delivered.
- Alternatively, amend Guaranty § 6.2 to provide that the Guaranty survives any assignment so long as the assignee is an Affiliate of Provider or assumes all Provider obligations in writing.
- **Requires Prodous and Guarantor agreement.**

## Issue 3 – Pending Kestrel Transaction Triggers Change of Control; No Provider Consent in Place

**Where:** MSA §§ 1.4(b), 14.1, 14.3; Order Form No. 3, § 2.5, § 5, Paragraph 1.

**Explanation:** MSA § 1.4(b) defines "Change of Control" to include "the acquisition by any person or group of beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of that Party's voting securities." Order Form No. 3, § 5, Para. 1 discloses that Zaseil has signed a merger agreement with Kestrel Fiberworks Group, Inc. under which Zaseil "will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Kestrel." Closing is expected December 1, 2026. This is unambiguously a Change of Control of Customer.

MSA § 14.3 provides: "A Change of Control of Customer is deemed an assignment by Customer requiring Provider's prior written consent under Section 14.1. If Customer undergoes a Change of Control without that consent, Provider may terminate the Agreement and all SOWs by written notice to Customer, and Customer shall pay Provider the Early Termination Fee within thirty (30) days after the effective date of termination."

Order Form § 2.5 defines the Early Termination Fee as "the remaining Minimum Annual Commitment for the current Contract Year" (i.e., $2.4M minus amounts counted through the termination date). The current Contract Year runs March 2, 2026 to March 1, 2027. If the Kestrel closing occurs December 1, 2026 and Provider terminates, the ETF would be roughly the MAC for the remaining ~3 months of that Contract Year (approximately $600,000, less any fees already invoiced and counted).

Critically, Order Form § 5, Para. 1 states only that "Customer shall notify Provider in writing within five (5) business days after closing occurs." Notification is not consent. The MSA requires "prior written consent." There is no evidence in the file that Provider has given or will give consent.

**Why it matters:** If Zaseil closes the Kestrel transaction without Provider's consent, Provider can walk away from the entire contract package and collect the ETF. Zaseil would lose its coating supplier mid-stream, with 180,000 linear yards per month of capacity reservation gone, and would owe a seven-figure fee.

**Recommended fix:**
- Before the Kestrel closing (December 1, 2026), obtain Provider's prior written consent to the Change of Control. This should be a condition to closing or addressed in the merger agreement's regulatory/consent conditions.
- If Provider withholds consent, negotiate a side letter providing that (a) Provider will not terminate solely on account of the Kestrel transaction, (b) the ETF will be waived or reduced, and (c) the Minimum Annual Commitment will be equitably adjusted.
- **Requires Provider agreement.**
- **Flag – need from client:** Confirm whether the Kestrel merger agreement contains any condition precedent related to third-party consents, and whether Zaseil has already raised the MSA consent issue with Provider. Also confirm the expected closing date has not slipped.

## Issue 4 – SOW Caps Provider's Indemnification at the Liability Cap, Undermining MSA § 11.3(a)

**Where:** SOW No. 1 § 9.2; MSA §§ 10.1, 11.2, 11.3(a); Parent Guaranty § 2.3.

**Explanation:** MSA § 11.3(a) carves Provider's indemnification obligations (under § 10.1) out of the Liability Cap—meaning they are uncapped. However, SOW § 9.2 expressly modifies this: "for Claims arising from Coating Services performed under this SOW, Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 of the MSA shall be subject to the Liability Cap." Under the MSA's order of precedence (§ 1.3), a SOW provision that "expressly modif[ies] an identified MSA section" controls over the MSA. SOW § 9.2 identifies § 11.3(a) and modifies it. The cap therefore applies.

The Liability Cap (MSA § 11.2) is "the fees paid or payable under the applicable SOW in the 12 months preceding the claim." For a contract with a $2.4M annual MAC, the cap is roughly $2.4M.

Parent Guaranty § 2.3 further limits the Guarantor: "Guarantor's liability under this Guaranty shall not exceed the liability that Provider would have under the Agreement for the same Default." So the Guarantor's exposure for indemnity claims is also capped at ~$2.4M.

**Why it matters:** The core risk in FR coating is product liability—a fire or injury caused by a coating that failed its flame-retardant specification. A single catastrophic event (e.g., a curtain wall fire in a healthcare facility) could generate claims well in excess of $2.4M. Zaseil's indemnity recovery would be capped, and the Guarantor's backstop would be capped at the same amount. Zaseil's insurance (not in the file) would bear the excess.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate deletion of SOW § 9.2, restoring the MSA § 11.3(a) carve-out so that Provider's indemnity for bodily injury, property damage, and FR coating failure is uncapped.
- If Provider resists, seek a super-cap for indemnity claims (e.g., 3x or 5x the Liability Cap) or tie the cap to Provider's insurance limits (which are $5M CGL + $10M umbrella = $15M under MSA § 12.1).
- **Requires Provider (and potentially Guarantor) agreement.**

## Issue 5 – 24-Month Warranty Period May Not Cover the Full 36-Month Durability Period

**Where:** MSA § 7.2; SOW No. 1 § 3.3; MSA § 6.3.

**Explanation:** MSA § 7.2 provides: "Provider warrants that, for twenty-four (24) months after delivery: … (c) the FR coatings applied will meet the FR performance standards in the Specifications for the durability period stated in the applicable SOW." SOW § 3.3 sets the durability period at 36 months (Tarpaulin Line) and 36 months / 25 laundering cycles (Curtain Line).

The grammatical structure of § 7.2 subordinates all warranty items (a)–(d) to the 24-month period. Item (c) then references a 36-month durability period. The result is ambiguous: Does Provider warrant that, for 24 months, the coatings will meet 36-month durability standards? Or does item (c) carry its own 36-month warranty tail?

If the 24-month reading controls, an FR performance failure that manifests at month 30 (within the 36-month durability period but outside the 24-month warranty window) is not a warranty breach. Zaseil would need to rely on MSA § 6.3 (latent defects) or on the indemnity in § 10.1(a) (which covers "any failure of the FR coatings applied by Provider to meet the Specifications"), but both of those routes are narrower and harder to invoke than a straightforward warranty claim.

**Why it matters:** FR coatings can degrade in ways that are not apparent at delivery (e.g., UV-induced breakdown, hydrolysis). A 12-month gap between the warranty expiration and the end of the durability period leaves Zaseil exposed for exactly the kind of late-emerging failures that are most dangerous in the field.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend MSA § 7.2(c) to state expressly: "the FR coatings applied will meet the FR performance standards in the Specifications for the full durability period stated in the applicable SOW, and this warranty shall survive for the duration of such durability period."
- Alternatively, add to SOW § 3.3: "For purposes of MSA § 7.2, the warranty period for FR performance conformance is the durability period stated in this Section 3.3, not the general 24-month period."
- **Requires Provider agreement.**

## Issue 6 – Refunded or Credited Lots Still Count Toward the Minimum Annual Commitment

**Where:** Order Form No. 3, § 2.2(c); MSA § 6.2(c).

**Explanation:** Order Form § 2.2(c) provides that "processing fees for any Lot that Provider credits or refunds under Section 6.2(c) of the MSA" count toward the MAC "as though invoiced and paid." This means that if Provider must refund fees for a defective lot (such as Lot 2207), Zaseil still gets credit toward its $2.4M minimum commitment for those fees—even though Zaseil received no conforming goods and was refunded the money.

**Why it matters:** In a year with significant quality failures, Zaseil could find that a substantial portion of its MAC is "consumed" by lots it never received. This reduces the effective volume Zaseil must purchase to avoid a shortfall, but it also means Zaseil is paying (in the form of the MAC) for goods it did not get. More practically, it reduces Zaseil's leverage: if Zaseil is already near the MAC because of refunded lots, it has less flexibility to walk away or renegotiate.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend Order Form § 2.2(c) to exclude from the MAC count any fees that are refunded under MSA § 6.2(c) where the refund results from Provider's breach (as opposed to a mutual agreement to cancel).
- **Requires Provider agreement.**

## Issue 7 – NDA Confidentiality Duration Shorter Than MSA; Pre-Contract Disclosures Lose Protection Sooner

**Where:** NDA §§ 8.1, 8.2; MSA § 9.3.

**Explanation:** The NDA covers disclosures made between January 12, 2026 and January 12, 2028 (NDA § 8.1). Obligations continue for 3 years after that period ends (NDA § 8.2), so pre-MSA disclosures are protected until January 12, 2031 at the latest. The MSA's confidentiality provision (MSA § 9.3) runs for 5 years after expiration or termination of the Agreement. The MSA's initial term ends March 1, 2029, so MSA confidentiality runs to at least March 1, 2034.

Information Zaseil disclosed to Prodous during the pre-contract negotiation period (e.g., formulations, recipes, process parameters shared to enable Prodous to qualify the work) is protected only until January 12, 2031—roughly three years less than post-MSA disclosures.

**Why it matters:** Zaseil's competitive advantage in FR technical fabrics likely resides in its formulations and process parameters. If any of that was shared during the NDA period (January–March 2026), it enters the public domain (as far as contractual protection is concerned) in early 2031, while equivalent information shared under the MSA remains protected until 2034.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend NDA § 8.2 to extend the post-period obligation to 5 years (matching the MSA), or add a provision that any Confidential Information that also qualifies as "Customer Process Information" under the MSA shall be protected for the duration specified in MSA § 9.3.
- **Requires Provider agreement.**
- **Flag – need from client:** Confirm what specific information was disclosed during the NDA period (January 12 – March 2, 2026) and whether any of it constitutes trade secrets (which would be protected indefinitely under both the NDA and the MSA).

## Issue 8 – Late Delivery Remedy Caps at 20% with No Termination Right for Chronic Delay

**Where:** SOW No. 1 § 6.4; MSA § 13.2.

**Explanation:** SOW § 6.4 provides a credit of 2% of fees per business day of delay, capped at 20% (i.e., 10 business days). After the cap is reached, no further credit accrues for additional delay. There is no provision in the SOW for termination rights specific to chronic delivery failures. Zaseil's only route would be MSA § 13.2 (material breach + 30-day cure), which requires Zaseil to make the argument that the delivery failures rise to the level of a "material breach."

**Why it matters:** If Prodous systematically runs 15–20 business days late, Zaseil receives the maximum 20% credit but has no further financial remedy and no clear contractual right to terminate. For just-in-time production schedules, this could be operationally devastating.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add to SOW § 6.4: "If Provider fails to meet the lead times in Section 6.1 on more than three (3) occasions in any consecutive six (6) month period, Customer may terminate this SOW under Section 13.2 of the MSA without a cure period, and the Late Delivery Credit shall not cap Customer's rights."
- **Requires Provider agreement.**

## Issue 9 – 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice Creates a Long Lock-In

**Where:** MSA § 13.1.

**Explanation:** The MSA auto-renews for successive one-year periods unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. The initial three-year term ends March 1, 2029. To avoid the first auto-renewal, Zaseil must give notice by September 4, 2028.

**Why it matters:** 180 days is a long lead time. If Zaseil's business needs change (e.g., it develops in-house coating capability, or the Kestrel integration changes its sourcing strategy), it must make the decision to exit 180 days in advance. Combined with the MAC and ETF, the exit cost of the auto-renewal is significant.

**Recommended fix:**
- This is a business decision rather than a legal defect. If Zaseil wants more flexibility, negotiate to reduce the non-renewal notice to 90 or 120 days.
- **Requires Provider agreement.**
- **Action item for client:** Calendar the September 4, 2028 deadline (and subsequent 180-day deadlines for each renewal term) now.

## Issue 10 – Additional Insured Requirement Limited to CGL and Umbrella Policies

**Where:** MSA § 12.2.

**Explanation:** MSA § 12.2 requires that "policies in Section 12.1(a) and (b) shall name Customer as an additional insured"—that is, only the commercial general liability ($5M) and umbrella/excess ($10M) policies. The property/bailee's coverage (§ 12.1(c)), pollution legal liability (§ 12.1(e)), and workers' compensation (§ 12.1(d)) are not required to name Zaseil as additional insured.

**Why it matters:** If a pollution event at Prodous's facility contaminates Zaseil's fabric, Zaseil would need to rely on the bailee's property coverage (in which Zaseil is not an additional insured) or on its own insurance. The MSA § 11.3(d) carve-out (uncapped liability for loss/damage to Customer Fabric) provides a contractual remedy, but collecting from a financially distressed Provider is another matter.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend MSA § 12.2 to require that the property/bailee's policy (§ 12.1(c)) and the pollution legal liability policy (§ 12.1(e)) also name Customer as an additional insured, or at minimum require that the bailee's coverage expressly covers Customer's property to full replacement value with a direct-pay clause.
- **Requires Provider agreement.**
