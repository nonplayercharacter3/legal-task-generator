## Summary

I have reviewed the five documents in the matter folder: the Master Services Agreement (MSA), SOW No. 1, the Parent Guaranty, Order Form No. 3, and the Mutual NDA. I identify thirteen issues, ranked by significance. The four most critical are: (1) SOW No. 1 expressly caps Provider's indemnification obligation, overriding the MSA's uncapped indemnity carve-out; (2) the 45-day demand window under the Parent Guaranty has likely already expired with respect to the Lot 2207 default; (3) the combination of MSA § 14.2 and Guaranty § 6.2 allows Provider to assign the contract to any third party and thereby eliminate the parent guaranty without Zaseil's consent; and (4) the pending Kestrel merger constitutes a Change of Control that requires Provider's "prior written consent" under MSA § 14.3, which the Order Form does not appear to provide.

## Issue 1 – SOW No. 1 Caps Provider's Indemnification (CRITICAL)

**Where:** SOW No. 1, § 9.2, read with MSA §§ 10.1, 11.2, 11.3(a).

**Explanation:** MSA § 11.3(a) expressly carves Provider's indemnification obligations under § 10.1 out of both the consequential-damages exclusion (§ 11.1) and the liability cap (§ 11.2). This is a standard and important protection: it means that if Provider's FR coating fails and causes bodily injury, property damage, or a product recall, Zaseil's indemnity recovery is uncapped.

SOW No. 1 § 9.2 states: *"With respect to Section 11.3(a) of the MSA, for Claims arising from Coating Services performed under this SOW, Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 of the MSA shall be subject to the Liability Cap."*

This is an express modification of an identified MSA section, so under the order of precedence in MSA § 1.3, the SOW controls. The practical effect is that Provider's indemnity for fire-related injuries, property damage, or regulatory exposure arising from its coating work is capped at the fees paid or payable under SOW No. 1 in the 12 months preceding the claim. For a business whose entire value proposition is flame-retardant performance, a capped indemnity for FR coating failure is a severe risk. A single serious fire event could generate liabilities far exceeding 12 months of processing fees.

**Recommended Fix:** Delete SOW § 9.2 in its entirety, restoring MSA § 11.3(a) so that Provider's indemnification remains uncapped. If Prodous resists, negotiate a super-cap (e.g., 3× or 5× the Liability Cap) for indemnification claims, or at minimum exclude from the cap any claim arising from bodily injury or death. **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 2 – Guaranty Demand Window Has Likely Expired for Lot 2207 (CRITICAL)

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, § 4.1; Order Form No. 3, ¶ 2.

**Explanation:** Guaranty § 4.1 provides that *"Guarantor is liable for a Guaranteed Obligation only if Beneficiary delivers a Demand to Guarantor within forty-five (45) days after Beneficiary first notifies Provider of the Default giving rise to that Guaranteed Obligation."*

Order Form No. 3, ¶ 2 records that Zaseil delivered a written Default Notice to Provider on **August 3, 2026** concerning Lot 2207. The Order Form itself is dated **October 20, 2026** – 78 days later. ¶ 2 further states: *"As of the Order Form Date, no Demand has been made on the Guarantor under the Parent Guaranty for the matters described in the Default Notice."*

Unless a Demand was made between August 3 and September 17, 2026 (the 45th day), Zaseil has likely forfeited its right to demand performance from Prodous Industrial Holdings, Inc. for the Lot 2207 default. The Guaranty's language is unambiguous: the 45-day window is a condition to liability, not merely a procedural step.

**Recommended Fix:** First, confirm with the client whether a Demand was in fact made within the window (perhaps informally or by email). If no Demand was made, assess whether the language in Order Form ¶ 2 – *"Customer reserves all of its rights and remedies against Provider and the Guarantor relating to Lot 2207"* – could be construed as a waiver by Guarantor of the § 4.1 condition, given that Guarantor is not a party to the Order Form and did not sign it. This is uncertain. In the alternative, negotiate a side letter with Guarantor confirming that the § 4.1 condition is waived for the Lot 2207 default. Going forward, implement an internal protocol ensuring that any Default Notice to Provider is accompanied by a Demand on Guarantor within 45 days. **Requires Guarantor's (Prodous Industrial Holdings, Inc.) agreement for any waiver.**

## Issue 3 – Provider Can Assign the Contract and Eliminate the Guaranty (CRITICAL)

**Where:** MSA § 14.2; Parent Guaranty § 6.2.

**Explanation:** MSA § 14.2 permits Provider to *"assign or transfer the Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, to any person without Customer's consent, upon written notice to Customer."* There is no restriction on the assignee's creditworthiness, capability, or relationship to the Prodous group.

Guaranty § 6.2 provides: *"This Guaranty terminates automatically, without further action by any person, on any assignment or transfer by Provider of the Agreement or any SOW that is permitted under the MSA."* It further provides that even a *partial* assignment (of one or more SOWs only) terminates the Guaranty *"as to the Agreement as a whole."*

The combined effect: Provider can assign the entire contract – or even just SOW No. 1 – to an unrelated third party on written notice, and the parent guaranty vanishes automatically. Zaseil would then be looking to an unknown assignee with no parent credit support. This is particularly concerning given that the MSA is a three-year (potentially longer) commitment with a $2.4M annual minimum.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend MSA § 14.2 to require Customer's prior written consent for any assignment by Provider (or at minimum, consent for assignments to persons other than Provider's Affiliates). Conform Guaranty § 6.2 so that it terminates only on an assignment to a non-Affiliate, and require that any permitted assignee provide a substitute guaranty of equivalent credit before the assignment takes effect. **Requires Prodous's and Guarantor's agreement.**

## Issue 4 – Kestrel Merger Triggers Change-of-Control Termination Right (CRITICAL)

**Where:** MSA §§ 1.4(b), 14.1, 14.3; Order Form No. 3, ¶ 1.

**Explanation:** MSA § 1.4(b) defines "Change of Control" to include *"the acquisition by any person or group of beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of that Party's voting securities."* Order Form ¶ 1 discloses that Zaseil has entered into a merger agreement with Kestrel Fiberworks Group, Inc. under which Zaseil *"will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Kestrel."* Closing is expected December 1, 2026.

MSA § 14.3 provides: *"A Change of Control of Customer is deemed an assignment by Customer requiring Provider's prior written consent under Section 14.1. If Customer undergoes a Change of Control without that consent, Provider may terminate the Agreement and all SOWs by written notice to Customer, and Customer shall pay Provider the Early Termination Fee within thirty (30) days after the effective date of termination."*

Order Form ¶ 1 states only that *"Customer advises Provider"* of the transaction and that *"Customer expects to continue purchasing Coating Services under SOW No. 1 after closing."* It does not contain language granting Provider's "prior written consent" as required by § 14.3. The word "advises" is not "consents." By signing the Order Form, Provider may argue it merely acknowledged the information without consenting to the Change of Control.

If the merger closes on December 1, 2026 without Provider having given explicit prior written consent, Provider could terminate the entire Agreement and demand the Early Termination Fee (defined in Order Form § 2.5 as the remaining MAC for the current Contract Year – potentially up to $2.4M).

**Recommended Fix:** Before the Kestrel closing, obtain Provider's express written consent to the Change of Control, in a form that references MSA § 14.3 specifically. This could be a short side letter or an amendment to Order Form ¶ 1 stating: "Provider hereby consents to the Change of Control of Customer described in this Paragraph 1 for purposes of Section 14.3 of the MSA." **Requires Prodous's agreement.** If Provider refuses consent, Zaseil should assess whether to (a) negotiate a buyout of the ETF, (b) seek a court declaration that execution of the Order Form constitutes implied consent, or (c) restructure the transaction (e.g., asset purchase rather than stock merger) to avoid the CoC trigger – though this would be complex and may not be feasible given the merger agreement already in place.

## Issue 5 – Order of Precedence Favors MSA Over Order Form

**Where:** MSA § 1.3.

**Explanation:** The order of precedence is: (1) SOW (only provisions expressly modifying an identified MSA section); (2) MSA; (3) Order Form. The Order Form has the *lowest* priority. This means that if any commercial term in an Order Form (pricing, MAC, ETF, volume tiers) conflicts with a term in the MSA, the MSA controls.

While this may seem academic, it creates risk. For example, MSA § 4.4 states that MAC shortfalls are to be *"calculated and invoiced as set out in that Order Form,"* which is consistent. But if a future Order Form attempted to modify a payment term or a liability provision in a way that conflicted with the MSA, the MSA would prevail. The structure also means that the SOW can override the MSA only if it "expressly modif[ies] an identified MSA section" – a narrow standard that could be disputed.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend MSA § 1.3 to provide that, in the event of conflict, the most recently executed document controls, or at minimum that the Order Form controls over the MSA with respect to commercial terms (fees, MAC, ETF, volume pricing). **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 6 – Warranty Period Shorter Than Durability Period

**Where:** MSA § 7.2 (24-month warranty); SOW No. 1 § 3.3 (36-month durability period).

**Explanation:** MSA § 7.2 provides a warranty that lasts *"twenty-four (24) months after delivery."* Subsection (c) warrants that *"the FR coatings applied will meet the FR performance standards in the Specifications for the durability period stated in the applicable SOW."* SOW § 3.3 sets the durability period at 36 months (Tarpaulin Line) and 36 months / 25 laundering cycles (Curtain Line).

The result: Provider warrants the coating will last 36 months, but Zaseil's warranty claim expires at 24 months. If the FR coating degrades and fails at month 30, Zaseil is outside the warranty period. MSA § 6.3 preserves rights for "latent defects, including loss of FR performance that is not reasonably discoverable by inspection at delivery," but a gradual degradation that manifests at month 30 may not qualify as a "latent defect" in the traditional sense (i.e., a defect existing at delivery but not discoverable until later). The line between "latent defect" and "failure to meet durability warranty after the warranty period has expired" is uncertain.

**Recommended Fix:** Extend the warranty period in MSA § 7.2 to match the durability period in the SOW (i.e., 36 months for Tarpaulin, 36 months / 25 cycles for Curtain). Alternatively, add language to SOW § 3.3 stating that the warranty period in MSA § 7.2 is extended to the full durability period for purposes of § 7.2(c). **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 7 – Force Majeure Purchases Count Toward MAC

**Where:** MSA § 16.4 (second sentence); Order Form No. 3 § 2.2(b).

**Explanation:** MSA § 16.4 provides that if a force majeure event affecting Provider continues for more than 30 days, *"Customer may obtain substitute Coating Services from a third party during that period and any resulting purchases count toward the Minimum Annual Commitment."* Order Form § 2.2(b) confirms: *"purchases of substitute Coating Services under Section 16.4 of the MSA"* count toward the MAC.

This means that if Provider's plant is destroyed by fire or shut down by a regulatory action for 60 days, Zaseil must source coating services from a third party at potentially higher cost, and those purchases still count against the $2.4M MAC. Zaseil bears the cost of Provider's force majeure event in the form of lost capacity and potentially higher third-party pricing, while still being obligated to meet the minimum.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend MSA § 16.4 to provide that substitute purchases made due to Provider's force majeure do *not* count toward the MAC, or that the MAC is reduced pro rata for the period of Provider's force majeure. **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 8 – Refunded Lots Still Count Toward MAC

**Where:** Order Form No. 3, § 2.2(c).

**Explanation:** Order Form § 2.2(c) provides that *"processing fees for any Lot that Provider credits or refunds under Section 6.2(c) of the MSA, as though invoiced and paid"* count toward the MAC. This means that if a Lot is defective and Zaseil elects the refund remedy under MSA § 6.2(c), the fees for that Lot still count toward the $2.4M minimum. Zaseil gets its money back but has "used up" MAC allocation it cannot recover.

Combined with the Lot 2207 default already in the file, this provision has immediate practical relevance.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Order Form § 2.2(c) to exclude from the MAC any fees that Provider has credited or refunded under MSA § 6.2. **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 9 – One-Sided Assignment Rights

**Where:** MSA §§ 14.1, 14.2.

**Explanation:** MSA § 14.1 prohibits Customer from assigning without Provider's consent. MSA § 14.2 permits Provider to assign to *any person* without Customer's consent, on written notice only. This asymmetry is unusual and gives Provider the ability to transfer the contract (and the associated MAC revenue stream) to any entity – including a competitor of Zaseil or a financially weaker party – while Zaseil is locked in.

This issue is compounded by Issue 3 (Guaranty termination on assignment).

**Recommended Fix:** Make assignment rights mutual: neither party may assign without the other's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), or at minimum require that any assignee of Provider be an Affiliate of Provider or have a credit rating no lower than Provider's. **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 10 – One-Way Price Escalation

**Where:** SOW No. 1, § 7.3.

**Explanation:** SOW § 7.3 provides that after the first Contract Year, *"Provider may adjust fees once per Contract Year on ninety (90) days' written notice by no more than the percentage change in the U.S. Producer Price Index for Chemicals and Allied Products over the preceding twelve (12) months."* This is a one-way ratchet: Provider can increase fees (capped at PPI) but has no obligation to decrease them if PPI falls. Over a three-year term, this could result in cumulative fee increases with no corresponding relief to Zaseil.

**Recommended Fix:** Make the adjustment bilateral: if PPI decreases, fees decrease by the same percentage. Alternatively, cap the cumulative adjustment over the full MSA term (e.g., no more than 10% in aggregate). **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 11 – MAC Applies to Full First Contract Year Despite Late SOW and Order Form

**Where:** Order Form No. 3, § 2.1; SOW No. 1 (effective April 6, 2026); MSA (effective March 2, 2026).

**Explanation:** Order Form § 2.1 states: *"For the Contract Year beginning March 2, 2026, the Minimum Annual Commitment applies to that full Contract Year."* The MSA was effective March 2, 2026, but SOW No. 1 was not executed until April 6, 2026, and Order Form No. 3 was not executed until October 20, 2026. No purchase orders could have been issued (and no fees invoiced) before April 6, 2026, because the SOW defining the services did not yet exist.

This means Zaseil is committed to $2.4M for a Contract Year in which it had no ability to place orders for roughly the first 3.5 months (March 2 – April 6) and no Order Form in place until October 20. The effective window to generate MAC-qualifying invoices is roughly 5 months (October 20 – March 1, 2027) unless Zaseil ramps up quickly.

**Recommended Fix:** If this is still negotiable (i.e., before the first Contract Year ends on March 1, 2027), amend Order Form § 2.1 to prorate the MAC for the first Contract Year based on the period from the SOW Effective Date (April 6, 2026) or the Order Form Date (October 20, 2026), whichever is more appropriate. If the first Contract Year has already passed or is nearly complete, assess whether the MAC was met and whether a shortfall invoice is expected. **Requires Prodous's agreement** for any amendment. **Client input needed:** Confirm actual invoicing to date under SOW No. 1 and whether a MAC shortfall is anticipated for the first Contract Year.

## Issue 12 – Overlapping Confidentiality Regimes

**Where:** Mutual NDA (entire); MSA § 9.

**Explanation:** The NDA (effective January 12, 2026) and the MSA (effective March 2, 2026) each contain confidentiality provisions with different scopes, durations, and standards:

| Feature | NDA | MSA § 9 |
|---|---|---|
| Scope of protected info | Broad (includes "fact and content of discussions," plant layouts, capacity data) | "Confidential information disclosed in connection with the Agreement" |
| Disclosure period | Jan 12, 2026 – Jan 11, 2028 | Duration of Agreement |
| Post-termination duration | 3 years after disclosure period ends (≈ Jan 2031); trade secrets indefinite | 5 years after expiration/termination (≈ Mar 2034 if no renewal); trade secrets indefinite |
| Standard of care | "At least the degree of care it uses to protect its own confidential information of similar nature, and in no event less than reasonable care" | "At least reasonable care" |

The NDA's broader definition and the fact that it covers pre-MSA disclosures (during the evaluation phase) mean that some information may be protected under the NDA but not clearly under the MSA. Conversely, the MSA's longer tail (5 years post-termination vs. ~3 years post-disclosure-period) may protect post-MSA information longer than the NDA would.

This is not a major risk for Zaseil as the disclosing party (Zaseil's formulations and process parameters are protected under both), but it creates ambiguity about which regime governs in a dispute.

**Recommended Fix:** Add a sentence to MSA § 9 (or a side letter) clarifying that the MSA's confidentiality provisions supersede the NDA with respect to information disclosed on or after the MSA Effective Date, and that the NDA continues to govern pre-MSA disclosures. **Requires Prodous's agreement.**

## Issue 13 – 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice Period

**Where:** MSA § 13.1.

**Explanation:** The MSA auto-renews for successive one-year terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term. The initial three-year term ends March 1, 2029. To avoid the first auto-renewal, Zaseil must give notice by **September 3, 2028**. This is a long lead time and creates a risk that Zaseil's business needs (or the Kestrel integration) may not be clear that far in advance.

**Recommended Fix:** Reduce the non-renewal notice period to 90 or 120 days. **Requires Prodous's agreement.** In the interim, calendar a reminder for no later than July 2028 to make the decision.
