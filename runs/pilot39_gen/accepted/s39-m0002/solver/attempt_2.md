## Issue 1 – Parent Guaranty Demand Deadline Likely Missed for Lot 2207

**Where:** Parent Guaranty § 4.1; Order Form No. 3, § 5, Paragraph 2.

**Explanation.** Section 4.1 of the Parent Guaranty makes Guarantor's liability conditional on Beneficiary delivering a Demand "within forty-five (45) days after Beneficiary first notifies Provider of the Default giving rise to that Guaranteed Obligation." The Default Notice concerning Lot 2207 was delivered to Provider on August 3, 2026 (Order Form No. 3, § 5, ¶ 2). Forty-five days after August 3 is September 17, 2026. Order Form No. 3, dated October 20, 2026, expressly states: "As of the Order Form Date, no Demand has been made on the Guarantor under the Parent Guaranty for the matters described in the Default Notice." On the face of the documents, the 45-day window has closed without a Demand.

This matters because the Guaranty is Zaseil's primary credit enhancement. Without it, Zaseil's recovery for the Lot 2207 default (and any future defaults) is limited to the assets of Prodous Coating Services LLC, a subsidiary. Given that SOW § 9.2 also caps Provider's indemnification (see Issue 3), the practical value of Zaseil's claim against the subsidiary alone may be limited.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Confirm with the client whether any Demand (even informal) was delivered to Guarantor before September 17, 2026. If so, document it.
- If no Demand was made, assess whether the August 3 Default Notice can be distinguished from the "Default giving rise to that Guaranteed Obligation" (e.g., if the specific obligation—reprocess, replace, or credit—was not identified until later). This is a narrow argument and likely unsuccessful given the plain language, but worth preserving.
- For going-forward protection, negotiate an amendment to Parent Guaranty § 4.1 to extend or remove the 45-day deadline, or to tie the deadline to the resolution of the underlying dispute rather than the initial notice. **Requires Guarantor's agreement.**
- In the interim, ensure that any future Default Notice is accompanied by a Demand on the Guarantor within 45 days as a matter of internal protocol.

**Unresolved – need from client:** Confirmation of the exact date and content of the August 3, 2026 Default Notice; whether any communication (email, letter, phone call followed by written confirmation) was sent to Prodous Industrial Holdings, Inc. between August 3 and September 17, 2026 that could constitute a Demand under § 4.1(c) and § 9.1 of the Guaranty.

## Issue 2 – Kestrel Change of Control Triggers MSA § 14.3 Without Provider Consent

**Where:** MSA § 1.4(b) (definition of Change of Control), § 14.1, § 14.3; Order Form No. 3, § 5, Paragraph 1.

**Explanation.** MSA § 1.4(b) defines "Change of Control" to include "the acquisition by any person or group of beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent (50%) of that Party's voting securities." The Kestrel merger, under which Zaseil will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Kestrel Fiberworks Group, Inc., is squarely a Change of Control of Customer. MSA § 14.3 deems a Change of Control of Customer to be an assignment requiring Provider's prior written consent under § 14.1. If the Change of Control occurs without that consent, Provider may terminate the Agreement and all SOWs, and Customer must pay the Early Termination Fee (defined in Order Form No. 3, § 2.5 as the remaining MAC for the current Contract Year) within 30 days.

Order Form No. 3, Paragraph 1 states only that "Customer advises Provider" of the transaction and that "Customer shall notify Provider in writing within five (5) business days after closing occurs." An "advisement" is not a consent. Moreover, under MSA § 1.3, the Order Form has the *lowest* order of precedence, so even if Paragraph 1 were read as an attempt to modify § 14.3, it would be ineffective unless it "expressly modif[ies] an identified MSA section" (the standard for SOWs under § 1.3 does not apply to Order Forms at all—Order Forms cannot modify the MSA under the precedence hierarchy).

Closing is expected December 1, 2026. If it occurs without prior written consent from Provider, Zaseil faces termination of the entire contract package and a cash outlay equal to the unmet MAC for the Contract Year (potentially up to $2.4 million less amounts already invoiced).

**Recommended Fix.**

- Obtain Provider's prior written consent to the Kestrel transaction before December 1, 2026. This is the only clean solution. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- If Provider withholds consent or demands a price, negotiate a side letter or amendment to MSA § 14.3 that either (a) carves out the Kestrel transaction, or (b) provides that a Change of Control of Customer to a financially sound acquiror does not trigger the termination/ETF consequence. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- As a fallback, if consent cannot be obtained, assess whether Zaseil can restructure the transaction (e.g., Kestrel acquires assets rather than equity) to avoid the Change of Control definition—though this is likely impractical for a public-company merger.

**Unresolved – need from client:** (a) Whether any consent or no-objection letter from Provider has been sought or received; (b) the expected timing of closing and whether it can be delayed to allow negotiation; (c) Zaseil's current run-rate against the $2.4M MAC for the Contract Year beginning March 2, 2026, to quantify the potential ETF exposure.

## Issue 3 – SOW § 9.2 Caps Provider's Indemnification Obligations

**Where:** SOW No. 1, § 9.2; MSA § 11.3(a), § 11.2.

**Explanation.** MSA § 11.3(a) expressly excludes "Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1" from both the consequential-damages waiver (§ 11.1) and the Liability Cap (§ 11.2). This is a critical protection: it means that if a third party sues Zaseil because an FR-coated fabric failed to resist flame (e.g., a hospital curtain ignites and causes injury), Provider's duty to defend and indemnify under MSA § 10.1(a) is uncapped.

SOW § 9.2 directly overrides this: "With respect to Section 11.3(a) of the MSA, for Claims arising from Coating Services performed under this SOW, Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 10.1 of the MSA shall be subject to the Liability Cap." The Liability Cap (MSA § 11.2) is "the fees paid or payable under the applicable SOW in the 12 months preceding the claim." At a MAC of $2.4M, the cap is approximately $2.4M—woefully inadequate for a product-liability claim involving fire, injury, or death.

Because SOWs rank above the MSA in the order of precedence (MSA § 1.3), SOW § 9.2 controls. The Parent Guaranty is also affected: Guaranty § 2.3 limits Guarantor's liability to "the liability that Provider would have under the Agreement for the same Default," so the cap flows through to the Guarantor as well.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Negotiate deletion of SOW § 9.2, or a carve-out that preserves uncapped indemnification for Claims involving bodily injury, death, or property damage (i.e., MSA § 10.1(a)). At minimum, raise the cap for personal-injury Claims to a multiple of the standard Liability Cap or to the insurance limits in MSA § 12.1. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- Confirm that Provider's products-completed operations insurance (MSA § 12.1(a): $5M per occurrence / $5M aggregate; § 12.1(b): $10M umbrella) is intended to backstop the indemnity. Even so, the contractual cap means Zaseil cannot recover from Provider above the cap even if insurance pays the third-party claimant—Zaseil would bear the gap.
- If Provider resists, consider whether the insurance requirements in MSA § 12 and SOW § 9.3 (3-year tail on products-completed ops) provide adequate practical protection, and document the risk acceptance.

## Issue 4 – Warranty Period (24 Months) Shorter Than Required Durability Period (36 Months)

**Where:** MSA § 7.2 (24-month warranty); SOW No. 1, § 3.3 (36-month durability for Tarpaulin; 36 months or 25 launderings for Curtain).

**Explanation.** MSA § 7.2(c) warrants that "the FR coatings applied will meet the FR performance standards in the Specifications for the durability period stated in the applicable SOW." However, the warranty in § 7.2 is stated to run "for twenty-four (24) months after delivery." SOW § 3.3 sets the durability period at 36 months for the Tarpaulin Line. This creates a 12-month gap (months 25 through 36) during which the FR coating is contractually required to perform to specification but is not covered by the express warranty.

If FR performance degrades in month 30, Zaseil would need to prove breach of the Specifications (SOW § 3.2) rather than rely on the warranty, and would face the burden of establishing that the degradation was due to Provider's coating rather than normal wear, field conditions, or Zaseil's own handling. MSA § 6.3 (latent defects) preserves rights for defects "not reasonably discoverable by inspection at delivery," but this is a general principle, not a warranty, and its interaction with the 24-month warranty period is unclear.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Amend MSA § 7.2 to state that the warranty period for FR performance (clause (c)) runs for the full durability period specified in the applicable SOW, rather than a flat 24 months. Alternatively, add a sentence to SOW § 3.3: "For purposes of MSA § 7.2, the warranty period for FR performance under clause (c) shall be coextensive with the durability period set out in this Section 3.3." **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- In the interim, ensure that incoming inspection and field monitoring protocols are robust enough to detect FR performance degradation before month 24, so that any claim can be brought within the warranty window.

## Issue 5 – Provider's Unilateral Assignment Right Combined with Automatic Guaranty Termination

**Where:** MSA § 14.2; Parent Guaranty § 6.2.

**Explanation.** MSA § 14.2 permits Provider to "assign or transfer the Agreement or any SOW, in whole or in part, to any person without Customer's consent, upon written notice to Customer." Parent Guaranty § 6.2 provides that the Guaranty "terminates automatically, without further action by any person, on any assignment or transfer by Provider of the Agreement or any SOW that is permitted under the MSA."

Read together: Provider can assign the entire contract to any third party (including a newly formed entity with no assets) on mere notice, and the Parent Guaranty from Prodous Industrial Holdings, Inc. terminates automatically. Zaseil would then be looking to an unknown assignee with no parent credit support. There is no right of termination, no consent requirement, and no requirement that the assignee have equivalent creditworthiness.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Amend MSA § 14.2 to require Customer's prior written consent for any assignment by Provider, or at minimum to give Customer the right to terminate without penalty (and without the Early Termination Fee) if Provider assigns. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- Amend Parent Guaranty § 6.2 to provide that the Guaranty does not terminate on an assignment to an Affiliate of Provider, or that it terminates only if Customer consents to the assignment. **Requires Guarantor's agreement.**
- If Provider resists, negotiate a requirement that any assignee assume the obligations in writing and that the Guaranty continue for the benefit of Zaseil against the assignee's parent (if any).

## Issue 6 – Asymmetric Assignment and Change-of-Control Provisions

**Where:** MSA § 14.1 (Customer cannot assign without consent), § 14.2 (Provider can assign freely), § 14.3 (Customer CoC = deemed assignment).

**Explanation.** The assignment regime is heavily one-sided. Customer needs Provider's consent to assign (and a CoC is deemed an assignment), while Provider needs no consent at all. This asymmetry gives Provider leverage: it can exit the relationship (by assigning to a shell) and shed the Guaranty, while Customer is locked in unless it obtains Provider's permission to transfer.

This is particularly acute in light of Issue 2: the Kestrel merger triggers § 14.3, giving Provider a termination right and an ETF claim, while Provider itself could assign away tomorrow with no consequence to Zaseil beyond loss of the Guaranty.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Make § 14.1 and § 14.2 symmetric: neither Party may assign without the other's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), or at minimum require that any assignee by Provider be a similarly situated entity with equivalent or greater financial capacity. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- Soften § 14.3: a Change of Control of Customer should not automatically be a deemed assignment. Instead, it should give Provider the right to negotiate in good faith, with termination available only if the acquiror is a direct competitor or is in financial distress. **Requires Provider's agreement.**

## Issue 7 – Minimum Annual Commitment Applied Retroactively

**Where:** Order Form No. 3, § 2.1.

**Explanation.** The MAC of $2,400,000 per Contract Year applies to "the Contract Year beginning March 2, 2026"—the full year—even though the Order Form was not signed until October 20, 2026. Section 2.1 confirms: "Fees count toward it if they are invoiced under SOW No. 1 in that Contract Year, including fees invoiced before the Order Form Date." Zaseil is therefore committed to $2.4M for a year that was already approximately 7.5 months in progress at signing, with no documented negotiation of that commitment at the time the MSA and SOW were executed (March–April 2026).

If Zaseil's actual invoiced fees for the Contract Year (March 2, 2026 – March 1, 2027) fall below $2.4M, it will receive a shortfall invoice under § 2.3, payable within 45 days under MSA § 4.3. The shortfall could be substantial if volumes were lower than projected in the first half of the year.

**Recommended Fix.**

- This is a done deal (Order Form is signed). The practical step is to model Zaseil's expected invoiced fees through March 1, 2027 and determine whether a shortfall is likely. If so, consider whether additional purchase orders can be placed to close the gap (fees must be "invoiced" to count, per § 2.2(a)).
- For future Order Forms, negotiate that the MAC applies only from the Order Form Date forward, or that the first-year MAC is prorated.
- Note that § 2.3 makes the shortfall invoice Provider's "sole remedy" for MAC failure—this is favorable to Zaseil (no termination right for MAC shortfall).

**Unresolved – need from client:** Zaseil's actual invoiced fees under SOW No. 1 from March 2, 2026 through the present, and its forecast for the remainder of the Contract Year, to assess shortfall exposure.

## Issue 8 – Late-Delivery Remedy Is Modest and Lacks a Termination Trigger

**Where:** SOW No. 1, § 6.4; MSA § 13.2.

**Explanation.** SOW § 6.4 provides a credit of 2% of the late Lot's fees per business day, capped at 20% (i.e., the credit maxes out after 10 business days of delay). The Tarpaulin Line lead time is 10 business days (§ 6.1), so a Lot that is 20 business days late yields only a 20% credit. There is no right to terminate for chronic late delivery, no right to source from an alternate provider (except under force majeure, MSA § 16.4), and no liquidated-damages mechanism beyond the credit.

MSA § 13.2 allows termination for material breach with a 30-day cure period, but a pattern of 5–10 day delays may not rise to "material breach" depending on volume and impact.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Add a provision to SOW § 6.4 or a new section: if Provider misses the lead time in § 6.1 for more than [X] purchase orders in any [Y]-consecutive-month period, Customer may (a) source substitute capacity and charge the difference to Provider, or (b) terminate the affected SOW without ETF. **Requires Provider's agreement.**
- Consider raising the daily credit from 2% to 3% and the cap from 20% to 30%, to increase the economic incentive for on-time delivery. **Requires Provider's agreement.**

## Issue 9 – NDA Reverse-Engineering Prohibition vs. MSA Testing Obligations

**Where:** NDA § 3.4; MSA § 5.2; SOW No. 1, § 5.1.

**Explanation.** NDA § 3.4 prohibits the Receiving Party from "analyz[ing], reverse engineer[ing], decompil[ing] or chemically deconstruct[ing] any fabric, coating, chemical or other sample provided by the Disclosing Party, except to the extent the Disclosing Party authorizes in writing for the Purpose." The NDA's "Purpose" is defined narrowly as evaluating and negotiating a possible commercial relationship.

Under the MSA and SOW, Provider is required to test each Lot against FR performance standards (SOW § 5.1), which involves burning samples, chemical analysis, and physical testing. While one could argue the MSA constitutes "written authorization for the Purpose" (broadly construed), the NDA's Purpose definition does not encompass ongoing production and testing. The NDA's disclosure period runs until January 12, 2028 (§ 8.1), and obligations continue 3 years beyond that (§ 8.2).

In practice, this is unlikely to be asserted, but it creates a theoretical exposure: if a dispute arose and Provider alleged that Zaseil's specification of test methods or acceptance criteria constituted a breach of the NDA's reverse-engineering clause (from Provider's perspective as Receiving Party of Zaseil's fabric samples), Zaseil would need to point to the MSA as authorization. The MSA is a separate agreement from the NDA and is not referenced in the NDA.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Add a clarifying sentence to NDA § 3.4: "The obligations of this Section 3.4 are superseded with respect to any sample or material for which the Parties have entered into a definitive agreement authorizing testing, analysis or processing." Alternatively, execute a short side letter confirming that the MSA and SOW constitute written authorization under § 3.4. **Requires both Parties' agreement (though this is a low-friction ask).**
- Lower priority: consider whether the NDA should be formally terminated or superseded now that the commercial relationship is fully documented in the MSA package. The NDA's obligations would continue for their remaining term regardless.

## Issue 10 – Auto-Renewal with 180-Day Notice Period

**Where:** MSA § 13.1.

**Explanation.** The MSA has a 3-year initial term (March 2, 2026 – March 1, 2029) and then "renews automatically for successive one (1) year periods unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least one hundred eighty (180) days before the end of the then-current term." A 180-day notice period means Zaseil must decide by approximately September 3, 2028 whether to renew for the year beginning March 2, 2029. This is a long lead time that may not align with Zaseil's business planning cycles, and it creates a risk of inadvertent renewal if the notice date is missed.

**Recommended Fix.**

- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (180 days before each term anniversary) immediately. Set internal reminders at 270 days and 200 days before the deadline.
- If Zaseil wants more flexibility, negotiate to reduce the notice period to 90 or 120 days. **Requires Provider's agreement.** This is a low-priority ask and Provider may resist given that the long notice period also protects it.
