## Issue 1: Pellucid Change-of-Control Consent Not Listed on Schedule 4.4

**Where:** SPA, Schedule 4.9(a)(iii) (disclosing the Pellucid termination right) and Schedule 4.4 (listing "None" for consents); SPA § 4.4(c) (consents rep); SPA § 7.2(c) (closing condition).

**Explanation:** Schedule 4.9(a)(iii) discloses that the Co-Manufacturing and Supply Agreement with Pellucid Grocers, Inc. provides that "Pellucid may terminate the agreement on 30 days' notice if the Company undergoes a change of control without Pellucid's prior written consent." Pellucid accounted for approximately 38% of the Company's net sales for FY2025. Schedule 4.4, which lists consents required under § 4.4, states "None." The closing condition in § 7.2(c) required that "all consents set forth on Schedule 4.4 shall have been obtained." Because Schedule 4.4 is empty, the condition was technically satisfied at closing.

This creates a critical gap: if Pellucid's consent was in fact required (which the disclosed contract language strongly implies) but was neither obtained nor listed on Schedule 4.4, then (a) Pellucid holds a live 30-day termination right that was not addressed as a closing condition, and (b) Seller's representation in § 4.4(c) (that the transaction does not require any consent under any Material Contract) is inaccurate.

**Assumption:** I have assumed that Pellucid's prior written consent was *not* obtained before closing, because (i) it is not listed on Schedule 4.4, (ii) no payoff or consent letter for Pellucid appears among Seller's closing deliveries in § 3.2, and (iii) the parties' treatment of Schedule 4.4 as "None" suggests they did not view it as a required consent. If in fact consent was obtained, this issue is moot, but the failure to list it on Schedule 4.4 still weakens Laimul's position under § 4.4(c).

**Why it matters:** Loss of Pellucid would eliminate roughly 38% of revenue. The indemnity cap (Issue 4 below) is $3.6 million, which is a small fraction of the annual revenue at stake. Furthermore, SPA § 10.13 (Cross-Disclosure) provides that a matter disclosed in one schedule is "deemed disclosed for purposes of each other section of Article 4 to which the relevance of that matter is reasonably apparent on the face of the disclosure." Seller will likely argue that the Pellucid change-of-control provision, disclosed in Schedule 4.9(a), is cross-disclosed against § 4.4, thereby negating any breach of the consents rep. Even if the rep is breached, the 6-month survival period (Issue 3) means Laimul must commence an action before January 1, 2027 to preserve the claim.

**Recommended fix:**
- Immediately confirm whether Pellucid consent was obtained. If not, request it now while the 30-day termination right has not been exercised.
- Send a written notice to Seller under SPA § 9.5 (Claim Notice) asserting a breach of § 4.4(c), well before the January 1, 2027 survival deadline.
- If consent is not obtained and Pellucid threatens termination, evaluate whether the loss constitutes a Material Adverse Effect that would support a claim under § 4.6 (Absence of Changes), which also carries only the 6-month survival period.
- In any renegotiation or side letter, seek to have Seller confirm in writing that Pellucid consent was obtained or that Seller will use its influence to secure it.

## Issue 2: The Note's "Losses" Definition Is Materially Narrower Than the SPA's, and the Note Controls for Set-Off

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Losses"); Note § 1 (definition of "Losses"); SPA § 9.8 (set-off right); Note § 4.2 (set-off right); SPA § 11.9 (order of precedence).

**Explanation:** The SPA defines "Losses" broadly: "losses, damages, liabilities, deficiencies, judgments, interest, awards, penalties, fines, costs and expenses of whatever kind, including reasonable attorneys' fees, diminution in value and product withdrawal or recall costs."

The Note defines "Losses" far more narrowly: "out-of-pocket damages, liabilities, judgments, fines and penalties actually paid by Maker or the Company to a Person other than Maker, the Company or any of their Affiliates, **excluding attorneys' fees, diminution in value, consequential damages and costs of any product withdrawal or recall**."

SPA § 9.8 provides that "Buyer may set off any amount of Losses to which any Buyer Indemnitee is entitled under this Article 9 against any amount otherwise payable by Buyer to Seller under the Seller Note." Note § 4.2 provides that "Maker may set off against any payment of principal or interest due under this Note the amount of any Losses (as defined in this Note) for which a Buyer Indemnitee is entitled to indemnification under Article 9."

SPA § 11.9 states: "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall prevail; **provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control with respect to its own payment, set-off and acceleration terms.**"

Because the Note controls its own set-off terms, the Note's narrow "Losses" definition governs any set-off against the Note. This means Laimul can set off against the Note only amounts that are:
- Out-of-pocket (not accrued or estimated);
- Actually paid (not merely owed);
- Paid to a non-Affiliate third party;
- And do not include attorneys' fees, diminution in value, consequential damages, or product recall costs.

**Why it matters:** For a commercial bakery, product recall costs are among the most significant potential losses. The Note explicitly excludes them from set-off. Additionally, the "actually paid" requirement means Laimul must first pay out of pocket before it can reduce its Note payments—a cash-flow disadvantage. The exclusion of diminution in value means that if a representation breach causes the business to lose value (e.g., a key customer departs), Laimul cannot set off that loss against the Note; it can only seek recovery from the Indemnity Escrow or directly from Seller, subject to the cap.

**Recommended fix:**
- In any amendment or side agreement, seek to conform the Note's "Losses" definition to the SPA's, or at minimum to include product recall costs and attorneys' fees.
- If Seller will not agree, ensure that all potential claims are documented and paid (to the extent possible) before the survival period expires, so that the "actually paid" requirement is satisfied.
- As a practical matter, track all third-party payments that could qualify as Note "Losses" and preserve the right to give the 10-Business-Day set-off notice under Note § 4.2 before each payment date.
- Note that the SPA's broader "Losses" definition still applies to claims against the Indemnity Escrow (SPA § 9.7(a)) and direct claims against Seller (SPA § 9.7(c)), so the narrow Note definition only limits the set-off remedy, not the underlying indemnification right.

## Issue 3: Six-Month Survival Period for Most Representations

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(a)(iii).

**Explanation:** All representations and warranties other than Fundamental Representations (§§ 4.1–4.3, 4.20) and Tax representations (§ 4.17) survive only until six months after the Closing Date—i.e., January 1, 2027. SPA § 9.1(c) makes this a hard deadline: no indemnification claim may be brought unless an Action is commenced in the specified court before the survival period expires, and any claim not so commenced "shall be forever barred."

**Why it matters:** The following critical representations expire on January 1, 2027:
- § 4.5 (Financial Statements)
- § 4.6 (Absence of Changes / no MAE)
- § 4.7 (Title to Assets)
- § 4.9 (Material Contracts)
- § 4.10 (Customers and Suppliers)
- § 4.11 (Litigation)
- § 4.12 (Compliance with Laws; Permits)
- § 4.14 (Environmental)
- § 4.15 (Food Safety; Product Recalls)
- § 4.16 (Intellectual Property)
- § 4.18 (Insurance)
- § 4.19 (Related Party Transactions)

For a food manufacturing business, issues such as a latent food safety problem, a customer contract dispute, a permit deficiency, or a financial statement error may not manifest within six months. The FDA inspection cycle, customer audit schedules, and the lag between a product defect and a recall can easily extend beyond 180 days. Once January 1, 2027 passes, Laimul's only recourse for these matters is a fraud claim (which carries a much higher evidentiary burden) or a claim under the covenants (which survive until performed, per § 9.1(b)).

**Recommended fix:**
- Before January 1, 2027, conduct a thorough internal review of all areas covered by the expiring representations. If any potential breach is identified, file a Claim Notice under § 9.5 and, if necessary, commence an action in the Court of Chancery before the deadline.
- Consider whether any known issues (e.g., the Pellucid consent gap) should be the subject of a protective action or at minimum a detailed written reservation of rights to Seller before the survival period lapses.
- If the deal is still in a position to be amended (unlikely post-closing, but possible via side letter), seek to extend the survival period for food safety (§ 4.15), material contracts (§ 4.9), and financial statements (§ 4.5) to at least 12–18 months.

## Issue 4: Indemnity Cap Limited to the $3.6 Million Escrow Amount

**Where:** SPA § 9.4(b).

**Explanation:** "The aggregate liability of Seller under Section 9.2(a) (other than with respect to Fundamental Representations and Section 4.17) shall not exceed the Indemnity Escrow Amount" ($3,600,000). Combined with the $363,750 deductible in § 9.4(a), Laimul's effective maximum recovery for most representation breaches is $3,600,000, and it bears the first $363,750 of any loss.

**Why it matters:** Given the Pellucid concentration (38% of revenue ≈ $18.4 million on a $48.5 million implied revenue base), the loss of that single customer would dwarf the indemnity cap. Similarly, a significant product recall, a material environmental liability, or a financial statement restatement could easily exceed $3.6 million in losses. Above the cap, Laimul has no recourse against Seller except for fraud (§ 9.4(d)) or breach of a covenant (§ 9.2(b), which is not subject to the § 9.4(b) cap but is subject to the overall Purchase Price cap in § 9.4(c)).

**Recommended fix:**
- The cap is a negotiated business term and difficult to change post-closing. However, ensure that any claims that might exceed the cap are framed as covenant breaches (§ 9.2(b)) where possible, since the § 9.4(b) cap applies only to § 9.2(a) (representation breaches).
- For the Pellucid issue specifically, if the consent was not obtained, argue that this is a breach of the § 4.4(c) representation (subject to the cap) *and* potentially a breach of the § 6.4 covenant (Seller's obligation to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain consents), which would not be subject to the § 9.4(b) cap.
- Maintain robust insurance coverage (product liability, recall coverage) to mitigate losses that exceed the indemnity cap.

## Issue 5: Note Event of Default if Laimul Sells the Company to a Non-Affiliate

**Where:** Note § 7.1(f).

**Explanation:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells or otherwise transfers all or substantially all of the capital stock or assets of the Company to any Person other than an Affiliate of Maker." Upon such an Event of Default, Holder may accelerate the entire unpaid principal balance under Note § 7.2(b).

**Why it matters:** This provision effectively requires Laimul to hold Hearthline for the full four-year Note term (through July 1, 2030) unless it sells to an Affiliate. If Laimul's strategy includes a later sale of Hearthline to a strategic buyer or in connection with a portfolio restructuring, the Note would accelerate, requiring immediate repayment of the remaining balance. This constrains Laimul's exit flexibility and could force a distressed sale or refinancing.

**Recommended fix:**
- If Laimul anticipates a potential sale of Hearthline before 2030, seek an amendment to § 7.1(f) to (a) raise the threshold (e.g., "all or substantially all" to "100%"), (b) add a carve-out for sales to strategic buyers in the food industry, or (c) provide that a sale to a non-affiliate is not an Event of Default so long as the buyer assumes the Note or the Note is repaid from sale proceeds.
- Alternatively, ensure that any future sale transaction is structured to repay the Note from proceeds before or simultaneously with the transfer.

## Issue 6: Note Assignable by Seller Without Laimul's Consent

**Where:** Note § 9.2.

**Explanation:** "Holder may assign or transfer this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker, on written notice to Maker identifying the assignee."

**Why it matters:** Dekes can sell or pledge the Note to any third party—potentially a competitor, a distressed-debt fund, or an entity with adverse interests—without Laimul's approval. Laimul would then owe $6 million (plus interest) to an unknown party. While the subordination and payment terms would carry with the Note, the new holder could be more aggressive in enforcing covenants, monitoring financials, or declaring defaults. There is no restriction on the type of assignee.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek an amendment to § 9.2 requiring that any assignee (a) be an entity not engaged in the commercial baking industry, and (b) assume in writing the benefits and obligations of the Holder under the SPA (including the indemnity cap and survival limitations).
- At minimum, require that any assignment be subject to the assignee's acknowledgment that the set-off rights in Note § 4.2 and SPA § 9.8 are preserved.

## Issue 7: Cross-Default with Senior Debt

**Where:** Note § 7.1(e).

**Explanation:** An Event of Default occurs if "any Senior Lender accelerates the maturity of Senior Debt in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $5,000,000."

**Why it matters:** If Laimul incurs senior financing (as is common for an acquisition of this size) and that financing is accelerated (whether for a technical default or a payment default), the Note is simultaneously put into default. Dekes can then accelerate the full Note balance. This creates a cascading default scenario in which Laimul faces claims from both its senior lenders and Dekes at the same time, with no cure period for the Note default triggered by § 7.1(e) (the 10-business-day cure in § 7.1(a) applies only to payment defaults on the Note itself).

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek an amendment to add a cure period or grace period for the cross-default (e.g., the Note is not in default unless the Senior Debt acceleration remains uncured for 30 days).
- Ensure that any senior credit facility includes a provision that a technical default that is cured within the applicable grace period does not constitute an "acceleration" for purposes of the Note.

## Issue 8: Monthly Customer-Level Sales Reporting to Seller

**Where:** Note § 6.1(b).

**Explanation:** For so long as any principal remains outstanding (up to July 1, 2030), Laimul must deliver to Dekes, within 30 days after each month-end, "customer-level sales reports for the Company for that month, showing net sales, unit volumes and average net pricing by customer and by product category."

**Why it matters:** This gives Dekes granular, real-time visibility into the Company's customer relationships, pricing, and volumes—including the Pellucid relationship and any other key accounts. While Dekes is bound by the SPA non-compete (§ 6.2) for five years and the SPA confidentiality covenant (§ 6.1) indefinitely, the information still creates competitive risk. If the non-compete is ever challenged or found unenforceable (as broad non-competes increasingly are), Dekes would possess detailed pricing and customer data that could be used in a new venture. Additionally, the NDA expires on March 16, 2028, two years before the Note matures, creating a period where Dekes receives this data with only the SPA § 6.1 confidentiality obligation as protection.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek to amend Note § 6.1(b) to (a) provide quarterly rather than monthly reporting, (b) aggregate customer data above a de minimis threshold (e.g., customers representing less than 2% of revenue), or (c) exclude pricing data.
- Alternatively, require that all information delivered under § 6.1 be expressly subject to the NDA (as amended to extend its term) or a separate confidentiality agreement with a term matching the Note's maturity.

## Issue 9: Escrow Agreement Release Demand Mechanism Creates a Race Risk on the Adjustment Escrow

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 3.2(b); SPA § 2.4.

**Explanation:** Either party may deliver a "Release Demand" to the Escrow Agent for disbursement of all or part of the Adjustment Escrow Fund, certifying that "Closing Net Working Capital has been finally determined under Section 2.4 of the Purchase Agreement." If the other party does not deliver an "Objection Notice" within ten Business Days, the Escrow Agent disburses the demanded amount to the demanding party. The Escrow Agent's duties are "purely ministerial" (§ 4.1) and it has "no duty to inquire into the validity, accuracy or content" of any document (§ 4.2).

**Why it matters:** The working capital dispute is currently pending (Buyer asserts a $612,000 shortfall; no party has accepted the Closing Statement). If Seller delivers a Release Demand with a certification that NWC has been "finally determined" (for example, by arguing that the failure to proceed to the Independent Accountant within the 30-day negotiation period in SPA § 2.4(d) constitutes a final determination, or by asserting some other procedural basis), and if Laimul's team is slow to deliver an Objection Notice within 10 Business Days, the $750,000 Adjustment Escrow could be released to Seller. The Escrow Agent will not independently verify the certification.

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the 10-Business-Day objection window as a hard internal deadline. Assign specific responsibility to the deal team or outside counsel to monitor for any Release Demand from Seller.
- If a Release Demand is received, deliver an Objection Notice immediately (well within 10 Business Days) stating that NWC has not been finally determined and that the dispute is pending.
- Consider proactively delivering a written notice to the Escrow Agent (under § 4.5, which permits the agent to refuse to act on conflicting claims) stating that Buyer disputes the final determination of NWC and that no Release Demand should be honored without Joint Written Instructions.

## Issue 10: Tax Treatment of Escrow Earnings Imposes a Net Cost on Laimul

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 2.5.

**Explanation:** "For tax reporting purposes, Buyer is treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds, and all interest and earnings on them shall be reported as income of Buyer, until disbursed."

**Why it matters:** The $3.6 million Indemnity Escrow and $750,000 Adjustment Escrow are invested in a money market fund. Any interest earned is taxable to Laimul in the year earned, even though the principal (and potentially the earnings) will be disbursed to Seller. This creates a timing mismatch: Laimul pays tax now on income that economically belongs to Seller. If the Indemnity Escrow is released to Seller in January 2027 without any claims having been made, Laimul will have reported the 2026 interest as income with no corresponding economic benefit.

**Recommended fix:**
- Quantify the tax cost (money market yields × $4.35 million × applicable corporate tax rate for the holding period).
- In any settlement or release of the escrow funds, seek a corresponding gross-up or tax indemnity from Seller for the earnings tax Laimul incurred.
- If the Indemnity Escrow is released to Seller without claims, consider whether the earnings should be treated as a purchase price adjustment (consistent with SPA § 9.11) to mitigate the tax impact.

## Issue 11: NDA Expiration Creates a Confidentiality Gap for Note Reporting

**Where:** NDA § 8.1 (two-year term, expiring March 16, 2028); Note § 6.1 (reporting through July 1, 2030); SPA § 6.1 (Seller confidentiality).

**Explanation:** The NDA expires on March 16, 2028. The Note requires monthly financial and customer-level reporting through July 1, 2030. For the approximately 28-month period between NDA expiration and Note maturity, Dekes will receive Laimul's consolidated financial statements and Company customer data. SPA § 6.1 obligates Seller to "hold in confidence all confidential information concerning the Company and the Business," but this is limited to Company-level information and does not expressly cover Laimul's consolidated financials (which include non-Company subsidiaries) or the specific data formats required by Note § 6.1.

**Why it matters:** Laimul's consolidated financials may reveal information about other business units, financing arrangements, or strategic plans that are not "concerning the Company and the Business" within the meaning of SPA § 6.1. After March 2028, Dekes would have no clear contractual obligation to protect that information.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek an amendment to the NDA to extend its term to match the Note's maturity (July 1, 2030), or to provide that information delivered under the Note is expressly subject to the NDA for the duration of the Note.
- Alternatively, amend Note § 6.1 to include a specific confidentiality provision covering all information delivered thereunder, with a term matching the Note's life.

## Issue 12: Automatic Acceleration on Bankruptcy Without Cure Period

**Where:** Note § 7.2(a).

**Explanation:** Upon the occurrence of an Event of Default under § 7.1(c) (voluntary bankruptcy) or § 7.1(d) (involuntary bankruptcy not dismissed within 60 days), "the entire unpaid principal balance of this Note, together with accrued and unpaid interest, becomes immediately due and payable **without notice or other action by Holder**."

**Why it matters:** Unlike other Events of Default (which require written notice from Holder under § 7.2(b)), bankruptcy triggers automatic acceleration. If Laimul or the Company files for bankruptcy (or is involuntarily petitioned and the case is not dismissed within 60 days), the full Note balance becomes immediately due. In a bankruptcy proceeding, this claim would be subject to the subordination in Note § 5.4 (Senior Debt paid in full first), but it still crystallizes a $6 million+ unsecured (subordinated) claim that reduces the estate available for other creditors and could complicate any restructuring.

**Recommended fix:**
- This is a standard (if aggressive) provision in subordinated seller notes. The practical mitigation is to ensure that the Company's and Laimul's financial health is monitored closely and that any distress is addressed before it reaches the bankruptcy threshold.
- If Laimul is in a situation where a bankruptcy filing is being considered, the Note acceleration should be factored into the analysis.

## Issue 13: Clean Team Compliance Risk

If no formal designation was made, the "cooperate in good faith" language in § 3.5 may be difficult to enforce, but it also means there is no clear line between what was and was not restricted.

- Confirm internally whether a Clean Team protocol was established and whether any pricing/sales personnel accessed restricted information.
- If there is any question, document the steps taken to comply with § 3.5 and consider a written confirmation to Dekes that Laimul is in compliance.
- Ensure that going forward, any integration planning that involves customer-specific data from the deal process is routed through appropriate channels.

## Issue 16: SPA Section 9.6 Restriction on Seller Assuming Recall Defense

**Where:** SPA § 9.6.

**Explanation:** "The Indemnifying Party may not assume the defense of any claim involving a recall or market withdrawal demanded by a Governmental Authority or a customer of the Company."

**Why it matters:** In a food safety recall scenario, Seller (the Indemnifying Party) cannot take over the defense. Laimul (as Buyer Indemnitee) must manage the recall response, regulatory communications, and customer negotiations itself, then seek indemnification from Seller for the resulting Losses. This places the operational burden and reputational risk on Laimul during what would be a crisis, with recovery from Seller subject to the deductible, cap, and (for set-off against the Note) the narrow "Losses" definition.

**Recommended fix:**
- Ensure that the Company's recall response plan is updated and that insurance coverage (product recall/contamination) is in force and adequate.
- If a recall is threatened or initiated, document all costs meticulously to support an indemnification claim, and be prepared to manage the process independently.
- Note that recall costs are included in the SPA's "Losses" definition (so they are recoverable from the Indemnity Escrow and from Seller directly) but excluded from the Note's "Losses" definition (so they cannot be set off against the Note). This reinforces the importance of exhausting the escrow first.

## Issue 17: Order of Precedence Creates Ambiguity on Set-Off Scope

**Where:** SPA § 11.9; SPA § 9.8; Note § 4.2.

**Explanation:** SPA § 11.9 provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Agreements, "provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control with respect to its own payment, set-off and acceleration terms." SPA § 9.8 grants a broad set-off right using the SPA's "Losses" definition. Note § 4.2 grants a narrower set-off right using the Note's "Losses" definition and is "Subject to Section 9.7 of the Purchase Agreement" (the order of recovery).

The interaction is: the Note controls "set-off terms," which I read to mean the Note's § 4.2 governs the mechanics and scope of set-off against the Note. But SPA § 9.7 (order of recovery) requires that set-off be used only after the Indemnity Escrow is exhausted. So the full picture is:
1. First: recover from the Indemnity Escrow (SPA § 9.7(a));
2. Second: set off against the Note, but only for "Losses" as defined in the Note (narrow definition) (Note § 4.2, controlling per SPA § 11.9);
3. Third: recover directly from Seller (SPA § 9.7(c)).

**Why it matters:** This layered structure means that for any claim, Laimul must first use the escrow (which is capped at $3.6M and subject to the 6-month survival deadline), then can set off only narrow-category losses against the Note, and only then can pursue Seller directly. The practical effect is that the Note functions as a secondary, limited recovery source rather than a full backstop.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek clarification or amendment to conform the Note's "Losses" definition to the SPA's, at least for purposes of set-off.
- If amendment is not possible, ensure that all potential claims are asserted against the Indemnity Escrow before the survival period expires, preserving the Note set-off as a secondary remedy for claims that are "actually paid" to third parties.

## Issue 18: Note Section 6.1(a) – Monthly CFO-Certified Financials

**Where:** Note § 6.1(a).

**Explanation:** Laimul must deliver, within 30 days after each month-end, unaudited consolidated balance sheets and income/cash flow statements "certified by the chief financial officer of Maker as fairly presenting in all material respects the financial position of Maker and its subsidiaries."

**Why it matters:** A CFO certification is a personal attestation. If the certified financials are later found to be materially misleading, the CFO could face personal liability (and Laimul would be in breach of the Note's covenants, triggering a potential Event of Default under § 7.1(b) after a 30-day cure period). This is a significant ongoing obligation for the CFO and creates personal exposure.

**Recommended fix:**
- Ensure that Laimul's monthly close process is robust enough to support a CFO certification within 30 days.
- Consider whether the certification standard ("fairly presenting in all material respects") is consistent with Laimul's internal controls and whether the CFO is comfortable making this attestation monthly.
- If the financial reporting burden is excessive, seek to amend to quarterly reporting or to remove the certification requirement.
