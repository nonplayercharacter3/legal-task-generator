## Issue 1 – Untimely Notice of Disagreement on Closing Net Working Capital

**Where:** SPA §2.4(a)–(c); Escrow Agreement, Recitals C–E.

**Facts.** Seller delivered its Closing Statement on August 25, 2026 (Escrow Agreement, Recital C). SPA §2.4(b) gives Buyer 30 days after delivery to deliver a Notice of Disagreement. Applying SPA §10.3 (which excludes the reference date from the count), the 30-day period runs from August 26 through September 24, 2026. Buyer delivered its Notice of Disagreement on September 28, 2026 (Escrow Agreement, Recital D) — four days after the deadline.

**Why it matters.** SPA §2.4(c) provides that if Buyer does not deliver a timely Notice of Disagreement, "the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Net Working Capital shall be final and binding on the parties." On its face, this means Seller's calculation controls and Laimul's assertion of a $612,000 shortfall is barred. The entire $750,000 Adjustment Escrow Fund would be released to Seller.

**Mitigating factor.** Seller executed the Amended and Restated Escrow Agreement on October 6, 2026, which in Recital E states: "As of the date of this Agreement, no Party has accepted the Closing Statement. Buyer reserves all of its rights under the Purchase Agreement with respect to the Closing Statement, the Buyer Dispute Notice and Closing Net Working Capital." Seller's voluntary participation in a document that expressly acknowledges the dispute is ongoing could support an argument of waiver or estoppel as to the four-day delay. However, the SPA requires amendments to be in a "written instrument signed by Buyer and Seller" (§11.10), and the Escrow Agreement is a tripartite document with the Escrow Agent; it is not clearly an amendment of the SPA. There is no express waiver of the timeliness requirement in any document I have reviewed.

**Recommended fix.**
- Immediately send a letter to Seller acknowledging the four-day delay and asserting that Seller's execution of the Escrow Agreement (Recital E) constitutes a waiver of the timeliness objection, citing the doctrine of equitable estoppel and Seller's course of dealing.
- In the alternative, propose that the parties stipulate the Notice of Disagreement is timely, or that the dispute proceed to the Independent Accountant under §2.4(d) regardless.
- If Seller refuses, be prepared to litigate the timeliness issue in the Court of Chancery. The factual record (Seller signed a document three weeks after the late notice that expressly preserves the dispute) is favorable, but the plain language of §2.4(c) is against us.
- **Assumption:** I have assumed August 25, 2026 was the actual delivery date of the Closing Statement and that September 24, 2026 was a Business Day. If September 24 fell on a weekend or holiday, the deadline would extend to the next Business Day under §10.3, but it would still precede September 28. Please confirm the exact delivery date and method from the deal file.

## Issue 2 – Pellucid Grocers Change-of-Control Provision and Probable Breach of Section 4.4

**Where:** SPA §4.4; Schedule 4.4; Schedule 4.9(a)(iii)(1); SPA §7.2(c).

**Facts.** Schedule 4.9(a)(iii)(1) identifies the Co-Manufacturing and Supply Agreement with Pellucid Grocers, Inc. as a Material Contract. The schedule expressly states: "Pellucid accounted for approximately 38% of the Company's net sales for the year ended December 31, 2025. Pellucid may terminate the agreement on 30 days' notice if the Company undergoes a change of control without Pellucid's prior written consent." Schedule 4.4 (Consents; Non-Contravention) states: "None." Section 4.4 represents that, except as set forth on Schedule 4.4, the transaction does not require any consent under any Material Contract.

**Why it matters.** The acquisition closed on July 1, 2026 — a change of control. On the face of the disclosed contract, Pellucid's consent was required. Seller's representation that no consents were needed (§4.4, Schedule 4.4: "None") appears to be false. If Pellucid exercises its termination right, Laimul loses approximately 38% of Company revenue. This is an existential threat to the business case for the acquisition.

Laimul's remedy is an indemnification claim under §9.2(a) (breach of representation). However:
- Section 4.4 is **not** a Fundamental Representation (only §§4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.20 are), so the claim is subject to the $363,750 deductible (§9.4(a)) and the $3,600,000 cap (§9.4(b)).
- The survival period is six months: January 1, 2027 (§9.1(a)(iii)).
- Under §9.1(c), Laimul must **commence an Action** in the Court of Chancery before January 1, 2027, or the claim is "forever barred."
- The maximum recovery is $3,600,000 minus the deductible = $3,236,250. If Pellucid terminates and the lost revenue exceeds that amount (which is likely given 38% of a business acquired for $48.5M), Laimul absorbs the excess.

**Recommended fix.**
- **Immediately** assess whether Pellucid has given or threatened to give a termination notice. If not, consider whether to proactively contact Pellucid to seek ratification or a waiver (though this should be done carefully to avoid waiving the indemnification claim — coordinate with Seller).
- Prepare and file a complaint in the Court of Chancery well before January 1, 2027, asserting breach of §4.4. Even if the loss has not yet crystallized (i.e., Pellucid has not yet terminated), a declaratory judgment action or an action for indemnification based on the inaccuracy of the representation should be commenced to preserve the claim.
- Calculate the expected loss from Pellucid termination and confirm it exceeds the $363,750 deductible. If the lost revenue stream is, e.g., $15M+ annually, the deductible is easily cleared, but the $3.6M cap means Laimul recovers only a fraction.
- Consider whether the breach also implicates §4.9(b) (Material Contracts valid and in full force) or §4.10 (no customer has notified of intent to terminate), which would provide additional (though cumulative) grounds for the claim.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming the Pellucid contract language in Schedule 4.9(a)(iii)(1) accurately reflects the contract terms. The full contract should be pulled from the data room to confirm the change-of-control provision and its scope.

## Issue 3 – Note's Narrow "Losses" Definition Limits Set-Off Against the $6M Seller Note

**Where:** Note §1 ("Losses" definition); Note §4.2; SPA §1.1 ("Losses" definition); SPA §9.8; SPA §11.9.

**Facts.** The SPA defines "Losses" broadly: "losses, damages, liabilities, deficiencies, judgments, interest, awards, penalties, fines, costs and expenses of whatever kind, including reasonable attorneys' fees, diminution in value and product withdrawal or recall costs" (SPA §1.1). SPA §9.8 permits Buyer to set off "any amount of Losses to which any Buyer Indemnitee is entitled under this Article 9" against payments on the Seller Note.

The Note defines "Losses" narrowly: "out-of-pocket damages, liabilities, judgments, fines and penalties **actually paid** by Maker or the Company to a Person other than Maker, the Company or any of their Affiliates, **excluding attorneys' fees, diminution in value, consequential damages and costs of any product withdrawal or recall**" (Note §1). Note §4.2 expressly incorporates this narrow definition: "Maker may set off... the amount of any Losses (as defined in this Note)..."

SPA §11.9 provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Agreements in the event of conflict, "provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control with respect to its own payment, set-off and acceleration terms."

**Why it matters.** The proviso in §11.9 means the Note's set-off terms control. Therefore, the Note's narrow "Losses" definition governs what Laimul can set off against the $6,000,000 Note. Laimul **cannot** set off:
- Product recall or withdrawal costs (critical for a food business);
- Diminution in value (e.g., loss of enterprise value from a breached rep);
- Consequential damages;
- Attorneys' fees;
- Any loss not yet "actually paid" to a third party.

This creates a significant gap: Laimul may have a valid indemnification claim under the SPA (using the broad "Losses" definition) but be unable to exercise the set-off remedy against the Note for the most commercially important categories of loss in a bakery acquisition.

**Recommended fix.**
- Negotiate an amendment to Note §1 to conform the "Losses" definition to the SPA definition, or at minimum to include product recall costs and diminution in value. Given that the Note is already executed, this requires Seller's agreement.
- If Seller will not amend, ensure that any indemnification claims are structured to maximize the "actually paid to a third party" component (e.g., document all out-of-pocket payments to regulators, customers, or vendors).
- As a fallback, rely on the Indemnity Escrow Fund (§9.7(a)) as the primary source of recovery, with set-off under the Note as a secondary remedy for the subset of losses that fit the Note's narrow definition.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming the §11.9 proviso is enforceable as written and that a Delaware court would give the Note's set-off terms priority over SPA §9.8. The proviso is specific and was presumably negotiated, so it likely controls, but there is an argument that the SPA's exclusive remedy provision (§9.10) and the order of recovery (§9.7) were intended to operate as a package.

## Issue 4 – Six-Month Survival Period for General Representations and Warranties

**Where:** SPA §9.1(a)(iii); SPA §9.1(c).

**Facts.** All representations and warranties other than Fundamental Representations (§§4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.20) and the Tax representation (§4.17) survive for only **six months** after the Closing Date — i.e., until **January 1, 2027**. Furthermore, §9.1(c) requires that an Action be **commenced** in the Court of Chancery before the survival period expires, or the claim is "forever barred."

**Why it matters.** For a commercial bakery, the following risks are unlikely to fully manifest within six months:
- **Food safety / product recalls (§4.15):** Contamination events, allergen cross-contact, or labeling errors may not be detected until a customer complaint, regulatory inspection, or recall — potentially well after six months.
- **Environmental (§4.14):** Soil, water, or air contamination issues often have long detection lags.
- **Financial statements (§4.5):** The first post-closing annual audit (FY2026) will not be completed until at least March–April 2027, after the survival period expires. If the pre-closing financials (used to value the business) were materially misstated, Laimul may not discover the error in time.
- **Material contracts (§4.9):** Counterparty breaches or hidden termination rights may not surface for months.
- **Compliance with laws (§4.12):** Regulatory investigations can take years.

The six-month period is at the very short end of market (typically 12–24 months for general reps in middle-market deals). Combined with the requirement to file an Action (not merely give notice) before the deadline, Laimul is under extreme time pressure.

**Recommended fix.**
- Conduct an immediate internal audit of all areas covered by the general reps, prioritizing food safety records, environmental files, financial statement support, and material contract files.
- Calendar a hard internal deadline of **December 15, 2026** to identify and assess all potential claims, allowing two weeks to prepare and file any necessary Actions.
- For any claim that is identified but not yet quantifiable (e.g., a potential food safety issue that has not yet resulted in a recall), file a declaratory judgment or preliminary injunction Action in the Court of Chancery before January 1, 2027, to preserve the claim. The Action need not be fully pleaded; it must be "commenced."
- If Seller is amenable, negotiate an extension of the survival period for specific reps (e.g., food safety, environmental) to 18 or 24 months, possibly in exchange for releasing a portion of the Indemnity Escrow early.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming the parties did not negotiate a longer survival period and that six months reflects the final agreed term. If there is any side letter or email correspondence suggesting a different period, that should be reviewed.

## Issue 5 – Indemnification Cap Limited to the $3.6M Escrow Amount

**Where:** SPA §9.4(b).

**Facts.** "The aggregate liability of Seller under Section 9.2(a) (other than with respect to Fundamental Representations and Section 4.17) shall not exceed the Indemnity Escrow Amount" — i.e., $3,600,000, or approximately 7.4% of the $48.5M Purchase Price.

**Why it matters.** For a business with significant food safety, customer concentration, and regulatory risk, a 7.4% cap may be inadequate. If the Pellucid relationship is lost (Issue 2), the revenue impact could far exceed $3.6M. If a major food safety recall occurs, the costs (product destruction, customer credits, regulatory fines, brand damage) could also exceed the cap. Laimul's recovery for the most likely and most severe non-fundamental breaches is structurally limited.

**Recommended fix.**
- The cap is now baked into the executed SPA and cannot be unilaterally changed. The practical mitigation is to ensure that (a) the Indemnity Escrow Fund is preserved (not released prematurely) and (b) any claims are structured to fall within categories not subject to the cap (Fundamental Representations, Tax, fraud — per §9.4(d)).
- For the Pellucid issue (Issue 2), explore whether the breach could be characterized as fraud (which would take it outside the cap under §9.4(d)). If Seller knew of the change-of-control provision and deliberately omitted it from Schedule 4.4, there may be a fraud claim. This requires factual investigation.
- Going forward, in any renegotiation or amendment, seek to increase the cap or add a specific indemnity for the Pellucid consent issue.

## Issue 6 – True Deductible (Not a Tipping Basket)

**Where:** SPA §9.4(a).

**Facts.** "Seller shall not be liable... until the aggregate amount of all Losses under Section 9.2(a) exceeds $363,750 (the 'Deductible'), after which Seller shall be liable **only for Losses in excess of the Deductible**."

**Why it matters.** This is a "deductible" or "franchise" structure, not a "tipping basket" or "threshold." Once losses exceed $363,750, Laimul recovers only the amount above $363,750 — it does not recover from dollar one. For example, if total losses are $500,000, Laimul recovers $136,250, not $500,000. This reduces effective recovery by $363,750 on every claim. Combined with the $3.6M cap (Issue 5), Laimul's effective recovery range for general rep breaches is $1 to $3,236,250.

**Recommended fix.**
- No practical fix at this stage; the term is executed. However, when aggregating losses for a claim, ensure all related losses are bundled into a single claim to maximize the amount above the deductible.
- In any future amendment or side agreement, seek to convert the deductible to a tipping basket (where recovery from dollar one begins once the threshold is crossed).

## Issue 7 – Change-of-Control Event of Default in the Note

**Where:** Note §7.1(f).

**Facts.** "Maker sells or otherwise transfers all or substantially all of the capital stock or assets of the Company to any Person other than an Affiliate of Maker" is an Event of Default.

**Why it matters.** If Laimul ever wishes to sell Hearthline (the Company) to a strategic or financial buyer — a common exit for a holding company — it would trigger an Event of Default on the $6M Note, requiring immediate repayment of the full balance. This constrains Laimul's exit strategy and could force Laimul to either (a) refinance or repay the Note before any sale, or (b) negotiate a waiver with Dekes (as Holder or its assignee). Given that Dekes can assign the Note to any Person without consent (Note §9.2), the party Laimul would need to negotiate with may not be Dekes.

**Recommended fix.**
- If Laimul has a medium-term exit strategy (3–5 years) that contemplates selling Hearthline, negotiate a carve-out or consent right in the Note now, while the commercial relationship with Dekes is still cooperative.
- At minimum, ensure that the Note's maturity date (July 1, 2030) aligns with Laimul's intended holding period. If Laimul plans to sell before 2030, the Note must be repaid or assumed by the buyer.
- Monitor the Note §9.2 assignment right: if Dekes assigns the Note to a third party, Laimul's counterparty for any waiver or amendment changes.

## Issue 8 – Cross-Acceleration Trigger

**Where:** Note §7.1(e).

**Facts.** "Any Senior Lender accelerates the maturity of Senior Debt in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $5,000,000" is an Event of Default under the Note.

**Why it matters.** Laimul presumably financed part of the $48.5M purchase price with senior debt (SPA §5.4 represents "sufficient cash on hand or available committed financing"). If that senior lender accelerates more than $5M of debt (e.g., due to a covenant breach at the Laimul holdco level), the Seller Note is also in default. This creates a cascading default scenario: a senior debt issue at the parent level triggers acceleration of the $6M Note, which then triggers default interest (Note §2.3), restricted payments (Note §6.4), and potential set-off limitations.

**Recommended fix.**
- Review Laimul's senior credit agreement to identify covenants that could be breached and trigger acceleration. Ensure compliance monitoring is in place.
- The $5M threshold provides some cushion (acceleration of less than $5M of senior debt does not trigger the Note default), but in a meaningful leverage scenario, the entire senior facility could be accelerated.
- Consider negotiating a higher threshold or a cure period in the Note, if Seller is amenable.

## Issue 9 – Intrusive Information and Access Covenants in the Note

**Where:** Note §6.1.

**Facts.** For as long as any principal remains outstanding (up to July 1, 2030), Laimul must deliver to Dekes:
- (a) Monthly unaudited consolidated balance sheets, income statements, and cash flow statements for Laimul **and its subsidiaries** (including the Company), certified by Laimul's CFO;
- (b) Monthly **customer-level sales reports** for the Company, showing net sales, unit volumes, and average net pricing by customer and by product category;
- (c) Annual audited consolidated financial statements;
- (d) Access to books, records, and personnel of Laimul and the Company on five Business Days' notice.

**Why it matters.** This is unusually intrusive for a seller note. In particular:
- The customer-level sales reports (¶(b)) reveal Laimul's pricing, volumes, and margins by customer — competitively sensitive information that could be valuable to a competitor or to Dekes if it re-enters the market (though the non-compete in SPA §6.2 restricts Dekes for five years).
- The reports cover Laimul **and its subsidiaries**, not just the Company. If Laimul has or acquires other businesses, Dekes gets visibility into those as well.
- The access right (¶(d)) allows Dekes to inspect Laimul's (not just the Company's) books and personnel.
- Failure to deliver these reports is a covenant breach, and if it continues for 30 days after notice, it becomes an Event of Default (Note §7.1(b)).

**Recommended fix.**
- Implement a compliance calendar for all Note §6.1 deliverables. Assign responsibility internally (likely Finance/Controller's group) for monthly and annual reporting.
- Consider whether the customer-level sales reports can be provided in aggregate form (by product category) rather than by individual customer, if the Note language permits interpretation. The text says "by customer and by product category," which appears to require customer-level detail.
- Restrict internal access to the reports and ensure they are marked confidential. The NDA (still in effect until March 16, 2028) and SPA §6.1 provide some protection, but the NDA's clean team provision (§3.5) may further limit who at Laimul can see Dekes-provided information (though this cuts both ways).
- If the burden is excessive, negotiate an amendment to limit reporting to the Company only (not all subsidiaries) or to reduce customer-level detail.

## Issue 10 – NDA Employee Non-Solicitation Restricts Integration Hiring

**Where:** NDA §7.1.

**Facts.** For 12 months after the Effective Date (March 16, 2026 → March 16, 2027), Laimul shall not "directly or indirectly solicit for employment or hire any officer, plant manager or director-level employee of Hearthline with whom Laimul first had contact, or of whom Laimul first became aware, in connection with the Transaction."

**Why it matters.** Post-closing, Laimul owns Hearthline and presumably wants to retain (and possibly promote or reassign) key personnel. The restriction targets those Laimul "first had contact with" or "first became aware of" in connection with the Transaction. If Laimul's deal team met with Hearthline's plant manager, CFO, or food safety director during diligence, Laimul cannot solicit them for a new role (e.g., a holdco position) until March 16, 2027. The exceptions for "general solicitations" and persons whose employment ended six+ months prior are narrow.

This is particularly relevant if Laimul plans to integrate Hearthline into its existing operations and move personnel into new roles.

**Recommended fix.**
- Identify all Hearthline officers, plant managers, and director-level employees with whom Laimul personnel had contact during the deal process.
- Until March 16, 2027, avoid any affirmative solicitation of those individuals for new positions. Passive retention (i.e., they continue in their existing roles) should not be restricted, as the NDA targets "solicit[ing] for employment or hir[ing]" — not continuing an existing employment relationship.
- If Laimul needs to make integration-related personnel changes before March 16, 2027, structure them as internal reassignments of existing employees rather than new hires or solicitations.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming that "hire" in §7.1 refers to new employment relationships, not the continuation of existing ones post-closing. If Laimul terminates and re-hires a key employee in a different role, that could be argued to be a "hire" in violation of the NDA.

## Issue 11 – Ambiguity in NDA Customer/Supplier Contact Restriction

**Where:** NDA §7.2.

**Facts.** "Until the earlier of the termination of discussions regarding the Transaction and 12 months after the Effective Date, Laimul shall not... initiate contact with any customer, supplier or landlord of Hearthline concerning the Transaction or Hearthline's business without the prior written consent of Dekes."

**Why it matters.** The restriction ends on the "earlier of" (a) "termination of discussions regarding the Transaction" or (b) 12 months after the Effective Date (March 16, 2027). The Transaction was **consummated**, not terminated. It is ambiguous whether a successful closing constitutes "termination of discussions." If "termination" means only an unsuccessful ending (i.e., one party walks away), the restriction runs until March 16, 2027. If it means any ending of the discussion process (including by deal), the restriction ended at closing (July 1, 2026).

Post-closing, Laimul (as owner of Hearthline) needs to contact customers, suppliers, and landlords in the ordinary course. The exception for "contacts made in the ordinary course of Laimul's business unrelated to the Transaction" should cover most operational contacts, but there is a risk that Dekes could argue that early post-closing contacts (e.g., introducing new management, renegotiating terms) are "concerning... Hearthline's business" and thus restricted.

**Recommended fix.**
- Take the conservative position that the restriction runs until March 16, 2027.
- For any non-routine contact with Hearthline's customers, suppliers, or landlords before that date (e.g., strategic account reviews, lease negotiations), document that the contact is in the "ordinary course" and "unrelated to the Transaction" (i.e., it is now Laimul's business, not the deal).
- If a specific contact is needed that could be characterized as "concerning Hearthline's business," seek Dekes's written consent under §7.2 to avoid any dispute.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming the NDA was not amended or terminated at closing. The SPA does not contain an express termination of the NDA. If there is a closing deliverable or side letter that terminates the NDA, this issue is moot.

## Issue 12 – Recall Claims: Seller Cannot Assume Defense; Recall Costs Excluded from Note Set-Off

**Where:** SPA §9.6 (last sentence); Note §1 ("Losses" exclusion for "costs of any product withdrawal or recall"); Note §4.2.

**Facts.** SPA §9.6 provides that the Indemnifying Party (Seller) "may not assume the defense of any claim involving a recall or market withdrawal demanded by a Governmental Authority or a customer of the Company." Separately, the Note's "Losses" definition excludes "costs of any product withdrawal or recall," so Laimul cannot set off recall costs against the Note (see Issue 3).

**Why it matters.** If a pre-closing food safety issue triggers a post-closing recall:
1. Laimul must fund the recall itself (Seller cannot assume the defense).
2. Laimul can seek indemnification from Seller under §9.2(a) (breach of §4.15), recoverable from the Indemnity Escrow Fund (§9.7(a)).
3. Laimul **cannot** set off recall costs against the Note (excluded from Note's "Losses" definition).
4. The claim must be filed before January 1, 2027 (six-month survival).
5. Recovery is subject to the $363,750 deductible and $3.6M cap.

This creates a significant cash flow risk: Laimul pays the recall costs upfront (potentially millions of dollars for a major recall) and must then wait for escrow disbursement or litigation to recover.

**Recommended fix.**
- Ensure the Company's product liability and recall insurance (listed on Schedule 4.18) is in force and that Laimul has notified the insurer of the change of ownership. Confirm the policies cover pre-closing manufacturing defects.
- If a recall occurs, document all costs meticulously and file a Claim Notice under SPA §9.5 promptly. Commence an Action before the survival period expires.
- Consider whether the recall costs, if "actually paid" to a third party (e.g., a disposal vendor, a customer credit), might fit within the Note's "Losses" definition despite the express exclusion for "costs of any product withdrawal or recall." The exclusion appears broad, but the "actually paid to a Person other than Maker, the Company or any of their Affiliates" requirement means that payments to third-party vendors for recall execution might arguably be "out-of-pocket damages... actually paid... to a Person other than" the Company. This is a stretch, but worth preserving as an argument.
- **Assumption:** I am assuming the recall insurance policies are assignable to or cover the Company post-closing. The policies should be reviewed to confirm.

## Issue 13 – Dekes May Assign the Note Without Laimul's Consent

**Where:** Note §9.2.

**Facts.** "Holder may assign or transfer this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker, on written notice to Maker identifying the assignee."

**Why it matters.** Dekes can sell or assign the $6M Note to any third party — a fund, a competitor, or an unknown entity — without Laimul's approval. This has several implications:
- Laimul's set-off rights (already narrow, per Issue 3) would be exercised against an unknown party.
- The subordination provisions (Note §5) reference "Senior Lenders" — if the Note is assigned, the dynamics of subordination may shift.
- If the assignee is aggressive, it may be more likely to enforce covenants (e.g., the information rights in §6.1) or declare defaults.
- Laimul has no consent right over the identity of its creditor.

**Recommended fix.**
- Monitor for any assignment notice. If the Note is assigned, immediately assess the identity and motivations of the new holder.
- Consider whether to prepay the Note (permitted without penalty under Note §3.1) if assigned to an undesirable party. Prepayment requires only five Business Days' notice.
- In any future amendment, seek to add a "permitted transferee" limitation (e.g., assignment only to financial institutions or Dekes affiliates).

## Issue 14 – Tax on Escrow Earnings Accruing to Laimul

**Where:** Escrow Agreement §2.5.

The SPA §2.3 withholding provision and §9.11 (tax treatment of indemnification payments as purchase price adjustments) may provide some relief, but the escrow interest is not an "indemnification payment" — it is investment income.

**Recommended fix.**
- Quantify the expected tax cost based on current money market yields (the funds are invested in the Ashgrove Government Obligations Money Market Fund per Escrow Agreement §2.4).
- If the amount is material, consider whether the parties can agree to a gross-up or tax indemnity for escrow interest. Alternatively, structure the release of escrow funds to minimize the holding period.
- Ensure Laimul's tax department is aware of the 1099 reporting and can plan for the tax liability.

## Issue 15 – NDA Clean Team Provision May Impede Post-Closing Operations

**Where:** NDA §3.5; NDA §8.1.

**Facts.** The NDA requires the parties to "cooperate in good faith to designate any customer-specific pricing, margin, bid or capacity information as 'Clean Team Only.' Information so designated may be disclosed only to those Representatives of the Receiving Party who are not directly responsible for pricing, sales or customer account management, and to its outside counsel and advisors." The NDA remains in effect for two years from March 16, 2026 (i.e., until March 16, 2028).

**Why it matters.** Post-closing, Laimul operates Hearthline. Its pricing, sales, and customer account management personnel are now the ones who need access to customer-specific pricing and margin information to run the business. If any information was designated "Clean Team Only" during the deal process, those personnel may be restricted from accessing it until the NDA expires (March 16, 2028) — even though Laimul now owns the business and the information is its own.

In practice, this is likely to be a minor issue because (a) Laimul now owns the underlying business and its data, and (b) the NDA's use restriction (§3.1) is limited to "evaluating, negotiating, financing and, if applicable, consummating the Transaction." Post-closing operational use is arguably outside the NDA's scope. However, the Clean Team restriction in §3.5 is a standalone obligation that does not expressly expire at closing.

**Recommended fix.**
- Determine whether any information was actually designated "Clean Team Only" during the deal process. If not, this issue is theoretical.
