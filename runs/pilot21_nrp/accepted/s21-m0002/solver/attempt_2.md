## Issue 1 – "Covered Services" Definition Conflict (Critical)

**Where:**
- Order Form § 1.1: *"Covered Services means physical DCP duplication and hard-drive shipment only."*
- Order Form § 1.3: *"These include DCP mastering, KDM generation, hard-drive duplication and shipment, and related quality-control services…"*
- Supply Agreement § 1.4: *"Covered Services means DCP mastering, KDM generation, hard-drive duplication and shipment, and electronic delivery to exhibitors…"*
- Side Letter § 2.1 (rates for electronic delivery, referenced as a service "under Supply Agreement § 6.3")

**Why it matters:**
Order Form § 1.1 narrows "Covered Services" to two of the five service categories the parties actually use. Because the term "Covered Services" is the hook for nearly every operative protection in the Supply Agreement, this single sentence could silently exclude DCP mastering, KDM generation, and electronic delivery from:

| Protection | Provision | Effect if "Covered Services" is narrow |
|---|---|---|
| Service levels (99.5% / 99.8%) | Supply Agt § 6.4 | No SLA for mastering, KDMs, or electronic delivery |
| Service credits | Supply Agt § 6.5 | No credits for failures in those services |
| Re-performance at no charge | Supply Agt § 6.6 | No obligation to re-perform |
| MAC calculation | Order Form § 3.3 | Only duplication/shipment fees count toward the $850K |
| Supplier warranty of workmanlike performance | Supply Agt § 9.2(a) | No warranty for mastering or KDM work |
| Supplier warranty that DCPs conform to spec | Supply Agt § 9.2(b) | Ambiguous – DCPs are the output of mastering, not duplication |
| Content security obligations | Supply Agt § 7.1 | Arguably still applies (references "Content" generally), but § 7.2 facility restriction is tied to "Covered Services" |
| Indemnification for security breach | Supply Agt § 10.1(a) | Tied to "breach of Section 7" – may survive, but the scope of § 7 is uncertain |

The internal contradiction within the Order Form itself (§ 1.1 vs. § 1.3) makes it impossible to determine the parties' intent without external evidence.

Under Supply Agreement § 14.3 (Order of Precedence), the Supply Agreement prevails over the Order Form except for "pricing, the Initial Term and the Minimum Annual Commitment." A definitional conflict is not one of those three carve-outs, so § 1.4 of the Supply Agreement (the broader definition) should control. However, relying on a precedence clause to fix a drafting error is fragile, and the ambiguity itself creates dispute risk.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Order Form § 1.1 to conform to Supply Agreement § 1.4. The sentence should read: *"As used in this Order Form, Covered Services has the meaning given in Supply Agreement Section 1.4."* Alternatively, delete § 1.1 in its entirety and let the Supply Agreement definition operate. This should be done by a written amendment signed by both parties under Supply Agreement § 14.2. Until fixed, Breimourt should not assume that service levels, credits, or warranties apply to mastering, KDM generation, or electronic delivery.

## Issue 2 – MAC Reduction Notice Likely Untimely (High Financial Exposure)

**Where:**
- Order Form § 3.4: Notice must be "delivered no later than 45 days before the first day of the second Contract Year."
- Order Form § 2.1: First Contract Year begins March 1, 2025. Second Contract Year therefore begins **March 1, 2026**.
- 45 days before March 1, 2026 = **January 15, 2026**.
- Side Letter § 3.1: Customer delivered the Reduction Notice on **February 2, 2026** – 18 days after the deadline.
- Side Letter § 3.2: Supplier rejected the notice as untimely on February 9, 2026.
- Side Letter § 3.3: Customer disputes the rejection.

**Why it matters:**
On the face of the contract, Breimourt missed the deadline by 18 days. Supplier's rejection appears correct. If Breimourt loses (as the language suggests it likely will), the second-Contract-Year MAC remains $850,000 rather than the reduced $637,500 – a **$212,500 difference** that Breimourt must pay as a Shortfall Amount if actual fees fall below $850,000.

The Side Letter records the dispute but does not resolve it. Both parties have reserved rights (§ 3.4). Any dispute goes to NY courts under Supply Agreement § 15 (Side Letter § 3.5).

**Recommended fix:**
- **Immediate:** Assess whether there is any arguable basis for the notice to be timely (e.g., was it "delivered" under § 16.1 before January 15 even if Supplier received it later? Was there any prior oral or email communication that could support estoppel or waiver?). If no viable argument exists, consider whether to settle at a reduced figure or simply accept the $850,000 MAC and ensure actual spend is managed to avoid a Shortfall payment.
- **Going forward:** Calendar all notice deadlines under the Order Form and Supply Agreement with internal alerts at 60 and 30 days before each deadline. The 180-day non-renewal notice (Supply Agt § 2.2) and any future MAC-related deadlines should be tracked by the Business and Legal Affairs team.

## Issue 3 – Worldwide Exclusivity vs. US/Canada-Only Territory (Structural Gap)

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 4.1: *"Customer shall, and shall cause its Affiliates to, obtain all of their requirements for Exclusive Services **throughout the world** solely from Supplier."*
- Supply Agreement § 1.7: "Exclusive Services" means "mastering, encoding and delivery services for any Content, including the Covered Services."
- Order Form § 1.2: *"The Territory is the United States and Canada."*
- Order Form § 1.3: Supplier's performance obligation is limited to releases "in the Territory."

**Why it matters:**
Breimourt is contractually prohibited from using any other provider for Exclusive Services anywhere in the world, but Padisk is only obligated to perform services in the US and Canada. If Breimourt (or an Affiliate) has a release in, say, the UK, Australia, or Latin America, it must use Padisk for mastering, encoding, and delivery – but Padisk has no contractual duty to serve that market, no SLA for it, and no rate card for it. Breimourt is locked into an exclusive relationship with no corresponding service obligation outside two countries.

This is compounded by the fact that "Content" (Supply Agt § 1.5) is defined broadly to include "streaming, airline, and home-entertainment deliverables," suggesting the parties contemplated uses beyond theatrical in the US/Canada.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Supply Agreement § 4.1 to limit the exclusivity obligation to the Territory (US and Canada), or add a carve-out: *"provided that Supplier shall have no obligation to perform Exclusive Services outside the Territory, and Customer shall be released from the exclusivity requirement for any territory in which Supplier does not offer services."* Alternatively, negotiate a most-favored-territory clause or a right to terminate the exclusivity for any territory where Supplier declines to serve.

## Issue 4 – Parent Guaranty: Narrow Scope and Separate Forum (Enforcement Risk)

**Where:**
- Parent Guaranty § 2.1: Guaranty covers only (a) service credits/refunds under Supply Agt § 6.5, (b) termination refunds under § 13.3(b), (c) indemnification under § 10.1, and (d) enforcement costs.
- Parent Guaranty § 12.1: Governed by Ontario/Canadian law.
- Parent Guaranty § 12.2: Exclusive jurisdiction – Ontario Superior Court, Toronto.
- Supply Agreement § 15.1–15.2: Governed by NY law; exclusive jurisdiction – New York County.

**Why it matters:**
Two distinct problems:

*(a) Scope.* The Guaranty does not cover the Supplier's general performance obligations, re-performance under § 6.6, damages for breach (other than through the § 10.1 indemnity), or costs of cover. If Padisk Digital Cinema Services, Inc. (the Delaware subsidiary) becomes insolvent mid-term, Breimourt can claim against Padisk Holdings Ltd. only for service credits already accrued, indemnification amounts, and termination refunds. It cannot claim for lost profits, the cost of emergency re-sourcing, or general breach damages. For a distributor whose entire theatrical delivery pipeline runs through one vendor, this is a meaningful gap.

*(b) Forum and law mismatch.* To enforce the Guaranty, Breimourt must sue in Toronto under Ontario law. To establish the underlying obligation (e.g., that a service credit is owed), it likely needs a NY determination first. This creates a sequential-litigation problem: the Guarantor in Toronto will almost certainly argue that the underlying obligation is not established until a NY court says so, delaying recovery.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an expansion of the Guaranteed Obligations to include "all amounts owed by Supplier to Customer under the Agreement, including damages for breach and costs of cover."
- Add a provision that a final, non-appealable judgment in NY establishing the underlying obligation is conclusive for purposes of the Guaranty, or that the Ontario court will give it preclusive effect.
- Alternatively, seek a cross-guaranty structure where the Guarantor also submits to NY jurisdiction for Guaranty enforcement, or at minimum permit Breimourt to sue in NY if the underlying obligation has been adjudicated there.

## Issue 5 – Press Release: Probable Breach of § 16.7 and Overstated Scope

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 16.7: *"Neither Party may issue any press release or public statement about this Agreement without the other Party's prior written approval of its content."*
- Press Release, dated March 4, 2025 (three days after the Effective Date). No approval document is in the folder.
- Press Release § 3: Lists all four service categories (mastering, KDM, hard-drive, electronic delivery) as covered.
- Press Release § 4.2 (Padisk quote): References "teams in Los Angeles and Toronto."
- Supply Agreement § 7.2: Services performed "only at facilities that it has disclosed to Customer in writing." No facility disclosure is in the folder.

**Why it matters:**
- If neither party obtained the other's prior written approval, both are in breach of § 16.7. While this is a mutual breach (and unlikely to be litigated), it sets a poor tone and could be raised as evidence of the parties' actual course of performance if a dispute arises over other terms.
- The press release publicly represents that electronic delivery and mastering are part of the agreement. If the Order Form § 1.1 definition (narrow "Covered Services") is given effect, those services may not be contractually covered in the same way. A public statement that overstates the deal could create expectations with exhibitors and complicate any later dispute about scope.
- The reference to "Toronto" facilities without a written disclosure under § 7.2 is a minor compliance gap but could matter if a security incident occurs at an undisclosed location.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm whether written approval was exchanged (check email files). If not, document a retroactive mutual waiver of the § 16.7 requirement for this specific release.
- Obtain and file a written facility disclosure from Supplier identifying all locations where Covered Services will be performed, as required by § 7.2.
- If the Order Form § 1.1 issue (Issue 1) is resolved in favor of the broader definition, the press release is accurate. If not, consider a corrected public statement.

## Issue 6 – No Termination for Convenience; 180-Day Non-Renewal Notice (Lock-In)

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 13: Termination only for material breach (30-day cure) or insolvency. No convenience termination.
- Supply Agreement § 2.2: Auto-renewal for successive 3-year terms unless 180 days' written notice of non-renewal.
- Order Form § 2.1: Initial Term ends February 29, 2028.

**Why it matters:**
Breimourt has no right to walk away for business reasons. If the relationship underperforms (short of material breach), if Breimourt's slate shrinks, or if a better vendor emerges, Breimourt is locked in until the end of the then-current term. The first non-renewal notice deadline is **September 2, 2027** (180 days before February 29, 2028). Missing that date commits Breimourt to a third 3-year term (through ~2031) with a 7% rate increase (Order Form § 4.3).

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the September 2, 2027 non-renewal notice deadline immediately, with internal reminders at 270, 200, and 120 days out.
- In any renegotiation (e.g., to fix Issue 1), seek to add a termination-for-convenience right exercisable on 180 days' notice at the end of any Contract Year, with a breakage fee limited to the unamortized portion of the MAC.
- At minimum, negotiate a "sunset" on the auto-renewal (e.g., no more than two renewal terms) so the lock-in has a finite horizon.

## Issue 7 – 7% Automatic Rate Increase on Each Renewal (No Cap)

**Where:**
- Order Form § 4.3: *"All rates under this Order Form…increase by seven percent (7%) at the start of each Renewal Term."*
- Side Letter § 2.3: Confirms § 4.3 applies to electronic delivery rates.

**Why it matters:**
The increase is automatic, applies to all rates (including the separately-agreed electronic delivery rates), and has no cap or ceiling. Over two renewal terms (6 years beyond the Initial Term), rates would be ~14.5% above the Initial Term levels. Combined with the lock-in (Issue 6) and the worldwide exclusivity (Issue 3), Breimourt has no leverage to resist.

**Recommended fix:**
Negotiate a cap (e.g., "not to exceed 3% per Renewal Term" or "not to exceed the CPI increase for the preceding 12 months, in either case not to exceed 5%"). Alternatively, tie the increase to a volume threshold: no increase if Breimourt's annual spend exceeds a stated level.

## Issue 8 – KDM Reissue SLA Applies Only During "Release Weekends" (Coverage Gap)

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 3.5: *"Supplier shall…reissue any defective KDM within two (2) hours of notice **during release weekends**."*
- Supply Agreement § 3.6: Operations desk staffed 24/7 "during the period from the Thursday before through the Monday after each theatrical release."

**Why it matters:**
A KDM can fail at any point during a film's theatrical run – a Tuesday matinee, a Friday evening, a Sunday night. The 2-hour reissue SLA applies only on release weekends (Thursday–Monday per § 3.6). Outside that window, there is no contractual deadline for KDM reissue. If a KDM fails on a Wednesday and the playback window expires that night, Breimourt has no SLA-backed remedy and the $1,500-per-missed-screening credit (§ 6.5) may not apply if the failure is attributed to the KDM rather than a "missed screening" in the SLA sense.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend § 3.5 to extend the 2-hour reissue obligation to all days during the active playback window for a given KDM, not just release weekends. At minimum, add a next-business-day reissue SLA for non-weekend failures.

## Issue 10 – Force Majeure: 30-Day Gap Before Alternative Sourcing Permitted

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 16.4: *"If a force majeure event prevents Supplier from performing for more than thirty (30) consecutive days, Customer may obtain the affected services from a third party for the duration of the event."*
- Supply Agreement § 4.1: Exclusivity applies "during the Term."

**Why it matters:**
For a theatrical release, 30 days is an unacceptable gap. If a network outage, natural disaster, or other FM event hits during a wide release window, Breimourt cannot source alternative delivery for up to 30 days while the exclusivity obligation technically remains in effect. The FM clause excuses Supplier from liability but does not suspend the exclusivity requirement.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend § 16.4 to reduce the threshold to 5 business days (or 72 hours for services tied to a specific release date), and add express language that during any FM event, the exclusivity obligation in § 4.1 is suspended with respect to the affected services.

## Issue 11 – No Ingest Confirmation Timeline for Electronic Delivery

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 6.3: *"Supplier shall confirm the successful ingest of each electronic delivery to Customer."* No timeframe stated.
- Side Letter § 2.5: Supplier shall not invoice until ingest is confirmed. No timeframe stated.

**Why it matters:**
If Supplier transmits a DCP electronically but the ingest fails silently (a known risk with satellite/terrestrial delivery), and Supplier does not confirm promptly, Breimourt may discover the failure only when the exhibitor reports a missing title – potentially hours before a screening. The absence of a confirmation deadline means there is no SLA breach to trigger a service credit.

**Recommended fix:**
Add a requirement that Supplier confirm successful ingest (or notify of failure) within a specified time (e.g., 4 hours of transmission, or by 6:00 AM local time on the delivery date for next-day screenings). A failure to confirm within that window should be treated as a failed delivery for purposes of § 6.4–6.5.

## Issue 12 – Service Level Measured "Monthly Across All Titles" (Dilution Risk)

**Where:**
- Supply Agreement § 6.4: *"…measured monthly across all titles."*

**Why it matters:**
If Breimourt has one wide release (2,000 screens) and two smaller releases (100 screens each) in the same month, a 5% failure rate on the wide release could be diluted by perfect performance on the smaller ones, keeping the aggregate above 99.5%. Conversely, the $1,500-per-missed-screening credit (§ 6.5) provides some per-screen protection, but the percentage-point credit (5% of fees per point short) is calculated on the aggregate, not per title.

**Recommended fix:**
Add a per-title measurement: "The Service Level shall be measured both (a) monthly across all titles and (b) per title for any title with more than [X] scheduled deliveries in the month. Service credits shall be calculated on the worse of the two measurements."
