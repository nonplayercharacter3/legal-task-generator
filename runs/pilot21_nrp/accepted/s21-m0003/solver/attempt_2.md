## ISSUES REQUIRING INTERNAL DISCUSSION WITH NEGEL BEFORE ENGAGING ZAIGATH

### 1. Renewal Dispute – Non-Renewal Notice Was 14 Days Late

**Where:** MSA § 12.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital B and § 4.1–4.2.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA § 12.2 provides that the Agreement "shall automatically renew for a renewal term of five years" unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal "at least 120 days before expiration of the then-current term."
- The Initial Term ends February 28, 2026 (MSA § 12.1).
- 120 days before February 28, 2026 is **October 31, 2025**.
- Amendment No. 1, Recital B states that Negel delivered its notice of non-renewal on **November 14, 2025**—14 days after the deadline.
- Zaigath "considers the notice untimely and that the Agreement renewed through February 28, 2031."
- Amendment § 4.1 provides that nothing in the Amendment constitutes Negel's acceptance of the renewal, and § 4.2 preserves Zaigath's position.

**Why it matters:**

On the plain language, Zaigath appears to be correct. The notice was late. If the renewal is effective, Negel is locked in for **five additional years** (through February 28, 2031) with no right to walk away except for cause (MSA § 12.3) or insolvency (MSA § 12.4). This has enormous financial and strategic implications, particularly given that Negel is evaluating the sale of one or more Covered Buildings (Amendment, Recital C).

**Potential arguments for Negel (to be assessed with the client):**

- Whether "expiration of the then-current term" could be read as the day *after* February 28 (i.e., March 1), which would shift the 120-day deadline to November 1—still before November 14, so this likely does not help.
- Whether Zaigath's conduct (e.g., continued performance, the execution of Amendment No. 1 which references the SOW and makes operative changes to it) constitutes a waiver or estoppel. Amendment § 4.1 was drafted specifically to foreclose this argument, and a court may give it weight.
- Whether the 120-day notice period is a condition precedent or merely a default term that can be excused by substantial performance or laches. This is a weaker argument given the explicit contractual language.

**Recommended fix / next steps:**

- **Before raising anything with Zaigath**, we need to brief Negel on the strength of its position. In our assessment, the plain language favors Zaigath, and the reservation-of-rights clause in Amendment § 4.1, while helpful, is unlikely to overcome a clear 14-day miss on an unambiguous deadline.
- If Negel wishes to exit, the practical path is likely a **negotiated early termination** with Zaigath, using the assignment rights in Amendment § 3 (which permit Negel to assign the SOW to a building purchaser without consent) as leverage. Zaigath may be amenable to a buyout if it means keeping the remaining buildings.
- If Negel accepts the renewal, it should negotiate a **termination-for-convenience right** or a shortened renewal term as part of any future amendment.

## HIGH-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 2. Exclusivity Extends to All Customer Properties, Not Just the Five Covered Buildings

**Where:** MSA § 3.4 (Exclusivity); MSA § 1.6 (definition of "Customer Property"); SOW § 2.1.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA § 3.4: "During the Term, Customer shall engage Provider as its exclusive provider of Vertical Transportation Services at **every Customer Property**, and Customer shall not engage, or permit any Affiliate to engage, any other person to perform Vertical Transportation Services at any Customer Property, except as Provider may agree in writing."
- MSA § 1.6 defines "Customer Property" as "any building, structure or real property owned, leased, managed or hereafter acquired by Customer or any of its Affiliates, **wherever located**."
- SOW § 2.1 limits the actual service obligation to the five Covered Buildings in Schedule A.

**Why it matters:**

There is a significant gap between the scope of services (five buildings) and the scope of exclusivity (every property Negel or its Affiliates own, lease, manage, or *hereafter acquire*, anywhere). If Negel acquires a new building in Brooklyn, or if an Affiliate leases a property in New Jersey, Negel is contractually barred from using any other elevator contractor there without Zaigath's written consent. This could:

- Constrain Negel's acquisition strategy (a buyer may require the seller to switch elevator providers post-closing).
- Create a situation where Zaigath has exclusivity rights over properties for which it is not even providing service and for which no fee is being paid.
- Give Zaigath leverage in any fee negotiation, since Negel cannot shop the market at its other properties.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend MSA § 3.4 to limit exclusivity to the **Covered Buildings** identified in the applicable SOW, not "every Customer Property." This aligns the exclusivity obligation with the actual scope of engagement.
- If Zaigath resists, a fallback is to limit exclusivity to properties in Manhattan (or to properties where Zaigath is actually providing services under an executed SOW).

### 3. Provider's Indemnity Is Subject to the Liability Cap

**Where:** MSA § 10.1 (Provider's indemnity); MSA § 11.1 (cap); MSA § 11.3 (exclusions from cap).

**What the documents say:**

- MSA § 11.1 caps each Party's aggregate liability at "the fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event giving rise to the claim." At current fees ($38,500/month), that is approximately **$462,000**.
- MSA § 11.3 carves three categories out of the cap: (a) *Customer's* obligations under § 10.2; (b) Customer's obligation to pay Fees; and (c) fraud.
- Notably, **Provider's indemnity under § 10.1 is NOT carved out**. It remains subject to the $462,000 cap.

**Why it matters:**

Elevators carry people. A serious entrapment, a door malfunction causing a fall, or a car drop could result in catastrophic personal injury or death claims. If Zaigath's negligence causes such an event, Negel (as building owner) will face third-party claims. Negel's right to be indemnified by Zaigath under § 10.1 is capped at roughly $462,000—far below what a single serious injury or death claim could cost. Negel's own insurance (MSA § 13.3) will respond first, but the deductible and the excess above policy limits would fall on Negel, with recovery from Zaigath capped.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add Provider's indemnification obligations under § 10.1 to the list of exclusions in § 11.3. At minimum, bodily injury and death claims should be excluded from the cap.
- Alternatively, raise the cap for claims arising from Provider's negligence causing personal injury or death (e.g., to the limit of Provider's insurance coverage under MSA § 13.1, which is $2M per occurrence / $10M umbrella).

### 4. After-Hours Callbacks Are Excluded from the Monthly Fee Despite a 24/7 Response Obligation

**Where:** MSA § 5.4 (After-Hours Callbacks); MSA § 1.5 (Business Hours: 7 a.m.–6 p.m.); SOW § 4.1 (30-minute response, 24/7); SOW § 5.3 (overtime rates).

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 4.1 requires Provider to respond to every Service Call "within thirty (30) minutes, **24 hours a day, 7 days a week**."
- MSA § 5.4 defines an "After-Hours Callback" as any request received outside Business Hours (i.e., after 6 p.m., before 7 a.m., or on weekends). It states: "The monthly maintenance fee **does not include** After-Hours Callbacks." Customer pays for all labor, including travel time, at overtime rates.
- SOW § 5.3 sets overtime rates at $525/team hour (evenings/Saturdays) and $640/team hour (Sundays/holidays).

**Why it matters:**

Negel has five buildings with 82+ units. Elevator entrapments and breakdowns do not respect business hours. Every after-hours emergency triggers a billable callback at premium rates. For a portfolio this size, after-hours callbacks could represent a substantial and unpredictable cost on top of the $38,500 monthly fee. There is no cap, no minimum dispatch fee, and no inclusion of a set number of after-hours calls in the monthly fee.

The structural tension is this: the SOW *requires* 24/7/365 response, but the MSA *excludes* after-hours work from the base fee. Negel is contractually obligated to pay for every single after-hours response, no matter how minor.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a **set number of included after-hours callbacks** per month (e.g., 4–6 per building), with overtime rates applying only for calls beyond that threshold.
- Alternatively, negotiate a **flat monthly after-hours surcharge** that covers a reasonable volume of callbacks, with overage billed at the existing rates.
- At minimum, clarify whether the 30-minute response obligation in SOW § 4.1 applies to After-Hours Callbacks that Customer is not yet obligated to pay for (i.e., can Zaigath slow-walk a 2 a.m. call because it's a billable event?). The current language suggests the response obligation applies regardless, which is favorable to Negel but creates a cost exposure.

### 5. Customer's Indemnity to Provider Is Overly Broad

**Where:** MSA § 10.2.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA § 10.2: Customer indemnifies Provider for third-party claims "arising out of or relating to the condition of the Premises or the Equipment, **whether or not caused in part by Provider**."

**Why it matters:**

The "whether or not caused in part by Provider" language means that if a tenant is injured by an elevator malfunction that was *partly* caused by Zaigath's failure to maintain the equipment properly, Negel still has to indemnify Zaigath for that claim. This effectively shifts to Negel the risk of Zaigath's own contributory negligence as it relates to equipment condition. Combined with the fact that Customer's indemnity is *excluded* from the liability cap (MSA § 11.3(a)), Negel's exposure under this provision is unlimited.

**Recommended fix:**

- Delete "whether or not caused in part by Provider" or replace with "to the extent not caused by the negligent acts or omissions or willful misconduct of Provider or its personnel or subcontractors."
- If Zaigath resists, at minimum add a proportionate-fault limitation so that Customer's indemnity is reduced by the percentage of fault attributable to Provider.

## MEDIUM-PRIORITY ISSUES

### 6. Uptime Commitment Is Efforts-Based With No Remedy

**Where:** SOW § 4.4.

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 4.4: "Provider shall use **commercially reasonable efforts** to keep each passenger elevator Unit in service not less than 98% of the time in each calendar month."
- There is no service credit, no termination right, and no other remedy tied to the uptime target.

**Why it matters:**

The only enforceable service level with a monetary remedy is the 30-minute response time (SOW § 4.3). The 98% uptime figure is aspirational. If a building's elevators are down 15% of a month (roughly 36 hours for a single car, or proportionally less across multiple cars), Negel has no contractual recourse beyond a termination-for-cause claim (which requires a material breach and a 30-day cure period under MSA § 12.3).

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a service credit schedule for uptime below 98% (e.g., 1% of the Monthly Maintenance Fee for each 0.5% below target, capped at some reasonable maximum).
- Alternatively, provide that sustained uptime below 95% for two consecutive months constitutes a material breach, triggering the termination-for-cause right without the 30-day cure period.

### 7. Service Credits Are Capped and Do Not Cover Return-to-Service Failures

**Where:** SOW § 4.3 (credit schedule and cap); SOW § 4.2 (4-hour return-to-service).

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 4.3 caps monthly service credits at **15% of the Monthly Maintenance Fee** (i.e., $5,775/month at current fees).
- Credits apply only to Response Time failures (the 30-minute arrival standard).
- SOW § 4.2 requires "commercially reasonable efforts" to return a non-entrapped, out-of-service Unit within 4 hours after arrival, but there is **no credit or remedy** if this is not met.

**Why it matters:**

In a month with multiple late responses (e.g., 4 calls each exceeding 120 minutes = $6,000 in credits), the 15% cap ($5,775) means Negel recovers less than the full amount. More importantly, if a mechanic arrives on time but then takes 12 hours to fix a simple door issue (missing the 4-hour target), Negel has no remedy at all.

**Recommended fix:**

- Raise the monthly credit cap to 25–30% of the Monthly Maintenance Fee, or eliminate the cap.
- Add a service credit for failure to meet the 4-hour return-to-service target in SOW § 4.2 (e.g., $500 per Unit per occurrence).

### 8. "Normal Wear and Tear" vs. "Causes Outside Provider's Control" – Ambiguous Fee Boundary

**Where:** SOW § 3.2.

**What the documents say:**

- The Monthly Maintenance Fee includes labor and parts for components that "fail from normal wear and tear."
- It excludes repairs for "vandalism, misuse, flooding, fire, power surges **or other causes outside Provider's control**."
- Excluded repairs are billed time-and-material at the rates in SOW § 5.3.

**Why it matters:**

The phrase "other causes outside Provider's control" is a catch-all that Zaigath could invoke to reclassify what Negel considers a normal maintenance item as a billable repair. Examples:

- A rope that fails due to a manufacturing defect: is that "normal wear and tear" or a cause "outside Provider's control"?
- A controller board that fails after 10 years: normal wear or outside Provider's control?
- A door operator that fails because of a voltage fluctuation from the building's electrical system: is that a "power surge" (excluded) or normal operational variance (included)?

The burden of classification falls on Zaigath, which has an incentive to bill. The only check is that Zaigath must give a written estimate before performing the work (SOW § 3.2), but Negel has no contractual right to dispute the classification short of a full breach claim.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a non-exhaustive list of components whose failure is presumptively included in the Monthly Maintenance Fee (e.g., ropes, sheaves, door operators, controllers, brakes, governors).
- Require Zaigath to justify in writing any reclassification from "included" to "excluded," with supporting technical documentation, and give Negel 5 Business Days to accept or dispute before work proceeds.
- Clarify that "outside Provider's control" does not include ordinary component fatigue, manufacturing defects, or design limitations.

### 9. Annual Fee Adjustment Has No Cap and Applies to Both Fees and Labor Rates

**Where:** MSA § 5.5; SOW § 5.1 and § 5.3.

**What the documents say:**

- MSA § 5.5: Provider may adjust the Monthly Maintenance Fee once per contract year by "no more than the percentage increase in the wage rates payable to elevator mechanics under the collective bargaining agreement then in effect for the New York City area."
- SOW § 5.3: Labor rates "are subject to the same annual adjustment as the Monthly Maintenance Fee."

**Why it matters:**

- There is no absolute cap (e.g., "not to exceed 5%"). If the CBA produces a 12% wage increase, the fee and all labor rates go up 12%.
- The adjustment applies to *both* the Monthly Maintenance Fee *and* the time-and-material/overtime rates, so the compounding effect on after-hours and repair work is significant.
- If the renewal dispute resolves in Zaigath's favor (5-year term), Negel is exposed to up to five consecutive annual adjustments with no ceiling.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a cap (e.g., "not to exceed the lesser of the CBA wage increase or 5% per annum").
- Clarify that the adjustment applies to the Monthly Maintenance Fee only, and that labor rates are fixed for the term (or adjusted on a separate, lower schedule).
- Require Zaigath to provide documentation of the applicable CBA wage increase before the adjustment takes effect.

### 10. Takeover Inspection Report – Confirm Delivery and Completeness

**Where:** SOW § 2.3; SOW Schedule B (final paragraph).

**What the documents say:**

- SOW § 2.3 required Provider to complete a takeover inspection of each Unit within 45 days of the SOW Effective Date (i.e., by **April 15, 2023**) and deliver a written report.
- Schedule B states: "Unit-by-unit identification, including Department of Buildings device numbers, manufacturer, controller type and capacity, shall be set out in the takeover inspection report delivered under Section 2.3. That report is incorporated into this Schedule B on delivery."

**Why it matters:**

The entire equipment schedule is a placeholder until the takeover inspection report is delivered. If the report was never delivered, was incomplete, or did not identify all Units, there is a gap in the contractual definition of what Equipment is covered. This affects:

- The scope of the Monthly Maintenance Fee (which Units does it cover?).
- The § 2.3 protection (Zaigath cannot charge for pre-existing conditions it failed to identify—but only if a report was actually delivered).
- The service levels (response times, uptime, etc. apply to "each Unit"—but what is a "Unit" if the list was never finalized?).

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm with Negel's engineering team whether the takeover inspection report was delivered by April 15, 2023, and whether it identified all 82+ Units.
- If it was not delivered or is incomplete, send a demand letter requiring delivery within a set timeframe, and reserve rights under § 2.3.
- If it was delivered, obtain a copy for the file and confirm it matches the equipment actually in the buildings.

## LOWER-PRIORITY / OBSERVATIONAL ITEMS

### 11. Force Majeure – "Utility Failure" Carve-Out

**Where:** MSA § 15.2.

**What the documents say:**

Force majeure includes "utility failure." A strike by Provider's own personnel is *not* force majeure unless industry-wide (favorable to Negel).

**Why it matters:**

If a building loses electrical power (a "utility failure"), Zaigath could invoke force majeure to excuse delays in responding to or resolving the resulting elevator shutdowns. In a high-rise office building, a power outage that traps tenants in elevators is a serious safety event. The force majeure excuse could delay Zaigath's response without penalty.

**Recommended fix:**

- Clarify that force majeure does not excuse Provider's obligation to respond to Entrapments, or that the force majeure delay period is limited (e.g., 4 hours) after which the service level obligations resume.

### 13. Amendment No. 1 – Assignment Rights (Favorable to Negel)

**What the documents say:**

The amended § 15.4 permits Negel to assign the Agreement or SOW, in whole or in part, **without Provider's consent** to (a) any Affiliate or (b) any purchaser of a Covered Building. If Negel sells a building, it can assign the SOW to the buyer without needing Zaigath's approval, and the fee automatically reduces. This is a significant improvement over the original MSA § 15.4, which required mutual consent for any assignment.

**No fix needed.** This provision works as intended.
