## Issue 1 – "Facility" Definition Imports Wrong Property (Critical)

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Facility" and "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities"); TSA § 1.1 (definition of "Facility")

**Problem.** The SPA defines "Facility" by reference to the Transition Services Agreement: *"'Facility' has the meaning given to such term in the Transition Services Agreement."* In the TSA, "Facility" means *the real property owned by Provider (Stounaisk) at 2200 Sandusky Pike, Fremont, Ohio*—i.e., Seller's retained coating and warehousing plant, not the Company's operating locations.

The "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" definition (SPA § 1.1) limits the environmental indemnity to Losses "at or from any Facility" or "in connection with any Facility." As drafted, the cap-carve-out environmental indemnity in § 9.2(d) therefore covers only Stounaisk's Fremont, Ohio site. It does **not** cover the Erie, Pennsylvania or Rochester, New York plants—precisely the locations where the Company manufactures and where the real environmental exposure sits.

This is confirmed by the rest of the deal documents: the LOI § 7 contemplates Phase I/II ESAs of "the Erie and Rochester plants"; SPA § 6.2 grants access "for the purpose of conducting environmental assessments of the Erie and Rochester plants"; and SPA § 4.15 (the environmental rep) uses the broader phrase "any real property currently or formerly owned, leased or operated by the Company." The rep is broad; the indemnity is narrow and pointed at the wrong property.

**Why it matters.** If there is a pre-Closing environmental condition at Erie or Rochester (lead, solvents, sealant residues, PCBs in old curtain-wall materials), Votox's claim would fall under § 9.2(a) (breach of rep) and be subject to the $630,000 deductible and the $6.3 million cap. The intended uncapped (up to Purchase Price) environmental protection in § 9.5(c)(iii) would be unavailable.

**Recommended fix.** Delete the cross-reference in SPA § 1.1 and define "Facility" in the SPA to mean *the real property owned or leased by the Company at its Erie, Pennsylvania and Rochester, New York manufacturing plants, together with any other real property currently or formerly owned, leased or operated by the Company in connection with the Business.* Alternatively, replace "Facility" in the "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" definition with "any real property owned, leased or operated by the Company."

## Issue 2 – Survival Period Uses Wrong Defined Term (Critical)

**Where:** SPA § 9.1; Escrow Agreement § 1.1 (definitions of "Release Date" and "Indemnity Release Date")

**Problem.** SPA § 9.1 provides that representations and warranties "shall survive the Closing until the **Release Date** (as defined in the Escrow Agreement)." The Escrow Agreement defines "Release Date" as *ninety (90) days after the Closing Date.* A separate term, "Indemnity Release Date," means *eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date.*

The LOI § 4(b) expressly contemplated 18-month survival for general reps. As drafted, the SPA gives Seller's general reps (organization, financial statements, material contracts, real property, IP, litigation, compliance, employees, product warranty, customers/suppliers, insurance) only 90 days of survival. After 90 days, Votox would have no indemnity claim for a breached general rep, even though the Indemnity Escrow is still held for 18 months.

**Why it matters.** Ninety days is far too short to discover issues like undisclosed product warranty claims, material contract breaches, or financial-statement errors in a manufacturing business with multi-year installation contracts. The 18-month period in the LOI was negotiated and expected.

**Recommended fix.** Change SPA § 9.1 to read: *"...shall survive the Closing until the **Indemnity Release Date** (as defined in the Escrow Agreement)..."* This aligns with the LOI and with the Escrow Agreement's own 18-month release mechanism in § 5.4.

## Issue 3 – Pre-Closing Taxes Missing from Cap Carve-Outs (Significant)

**Where:** SPA § 9.5(c); LOI § 4(d)

**Problem.** LOI § 4(d) states: *"Neither the deductible nor the cap would apply to the following cap carve-outs: Fraud, fundamental representations, **pre-closing taxes**, and the special environmental indemnity."*

SPA § 9.5(c) carves out only: (i) Fraud, (ii) Fundamental Representations, and (iii) "the matters described in Section 9.2(d)" (Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities). Section 9.2(c) (Pre-Closing Taxes) is **not** listed. As a result, Pre-Closing Taxes are subject to both the $630,000 deductible and the $6.3 million cap.

**Why it matters.** The Company operates multi-state fabrication, supply, and installation contracts. Sales-and-use-tax exposure (nexus, exemption certificates, sourcing rules) can easily exceed $6.3 million for a business of this size. The LOI gave Votox uncapped (up to Purchase Price) protection for pre-Closing taxes; the SPA silently removes it.

**Recommended fix.** Add "the matters described in Section 9.2(c)" to the list in SPA § 9.5(c), so the provision reads: *"...(i) Fraud, (ii) any breach of any Fundamental Representation, (iii) the matters described in Section 9.2(c), or (iv) the matters described in Section 9.2(d)..."*

## Issue 4 – Adjustment Escrow Release Date Conflicts with Closing Statement Timeline (Significant)

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 5.1(b) and § 1.1 ("Release Date"); SPA § 2.3(b)

**Problem.** The Escrow Agreement provides that on the "Release Date" (90 days after Closing), the Escrow Agent shall disburse all remaining Adjustment Escrow Funds to Seller *unless* Buyer delivers a written notice before that date stating that amounts remain to be determined.

SPA § 2.3(b) gives Buyer **90 days after Closing** to deliver the Closing Statement. In other words, Buyer's deadline to deliver the statement is the same day the escrow releases. To preserve the escrow, Buyer must deliver a hold-back notice *before* day 90—i.e., by day 89—yet it may not have finished its working-capital calculation until day 90.

**Why it matters.** If Buyer misses the notice by even one day, the $750,000 Adjustment Escrow is released to Seller, and any subsequent working-capital shortfall must be collected from Seller directly (SPA § 2.3(c)), with no security.

**Recommended fix.** Either (a) change the Adjustment Escrow release trigger in Escrow Agreement § 5.1(b) to the later of the Release Date or the date 30 days after delivery of the Closing Statement (or any objection notice), or (b) extend the "Release Date" as it applies to the Adjustment Escrow Account to 120 days after Closing to accommodate the full objection and dispute-resolution cycle in SPA § 2.3(b).

## Issue 5 – TSA Liability Cap Inadequate for Company Materials (Moderate)

**Where:** TSA § 8.1 (Limitation of Liability); TSA § 5.2 (Risk of Loss; Insurance)

**Problem.** TSA § 5.2 places the risk of loss and damage to Company Materials on Provider and requires Provider to carry property insurance at replacement cost. However, TSA § 8.1 caps *each party's* aggregate liability at "the total Service Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." The estimated monthly fees in Schedule A are placeholders, but even if they total, say, $150,000–$250,000 over nine months, that cap would be far below the replacement value of a significant inventory of coated glass, interlayers, spacers, and finished units stored at the Facility.

**Why it matters.** If a fire, flood, or Provider negligence event destroys or damages Company Materials at the Fremont facility, Votox's recovery under the TSA would be limited to total Service Fees—potentially a fraction of the actual loss. The insurance requirement in § 5.2 helps, but insurance proceeds are not a substitute for contractual liability, and there is no requirement that Provider name the Company as loss payee on the property policy (only CGL additional insured is required).

**Recommended fix.** Carve out of the § 8.1 cap any liability arising from Provider's breach of § 5.2 (risk of loss / insurance obligations) or from Provider's negligence or willful misconduct causing loss of or damage to Company Materials. Alternatively, increase the cap to the greater of total Service Fees or the replacement value of Company Materials on hand at the Facility. Also add a requirement that Provider's property insurance name the Company as loss payee (not just additional insured) for Company Materials.

## Issue 6 – No Perfection of Company's Interest in Materials at Provider's Facility (Moderate)

**Where:** TSA § 2.5 (No Transfer of Title)

**Problem.** TSA § 2.5 establishes that title to Company Materials remains with the Company and that Provider holds them as bailee. It prohibits Provider from permitting liens to attach "arising by, through or under Provider." However, the TSA does not require the Company to file a UCC-1 financing statement against Provider to perfect its interest in the materials while they are in Provider's possession.

**Why it matters.** If Provider becomes insolvent during the transition period, the Company's unperfected interest in goods in the possession of a bailee/merchant may be subordinated to Provider's secured creditors or to a trustee in bankruptcy. The contractual "no lien" provision in § 2.5 is not enforceable against third-party creditors.

**Recommended fix.** Add to TSA § 2.5: *"The Company may file one or more UCC-1 financing statements against Provider describing the Company Materials, and Provider shall execute any documents reasonably necessary to evidence the Company's ownership interest. Provider shall not object to any such filing."*

## Issue 7 – Inconsistent Governing Law Between SPA and Escrow Agreement (Moderate)

**Where:** SPA § 11.9(a) (New York law); Escrow Agreement § 11.2 (Delaware law)

**Problem.** The SPA is governed by New York law. The Escrow Agreement is governed by Delaware law and subjects the parties to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Delaware Court of Chancery. While SPA § 11.4 provides that the SPA controls over any Ancillary Agreement in the event of a conflict, the different governing laws create ambiguity about which state's law interprets the cross-referenced defined terms (e.g., "Release Date," "Indemnity Release Date," "Pending Claim Amount") that flow from the Escrow Agreement into the SPA's survival and indemnity provisions.

**Why it matters.** In a dispute over whether a claim was timely (e.g., the survival-period issue in Issue 2 above), the choice of law could affect the outcome. Delaware and New York courts may interpret contractual terms, notice provisions, or the effect of a "conclusively deemed" admission differently.

**Recommended fix.** Conform the Escrow Agreement's governing law and jurisdiction to New York, matching the SPA. If the Escrow Agent (Pellucid Trust Company, N.A.) resists on the ground that it is a national bank accustomed to Delaware escrow documentation, at minimum add a provision stating that in the event of any conflict between the Escrow Agreement and the Purchase Agreement, the Purchase Agreement's governing-law clause controls the interpretation of shared defined terms.

## Issue 8 – No Deadlock Mechanism for Working-Capital Dispute (Minor)

**Where:** SPA § 2.3(b)

**Problem.** The provision states that an unresolved dispute "shall be submitted to an independent accounting firm mutually selected by Buyer and Seller." There is no mechanism if the parties cannot agree on the firm.

**Why it matters.** A deadlock on selecting the accounting firm would stall the purchase-price adjustment indefinitely, leaving the Adjustment Escrow in limbo (compounded by the timing issue in Issue 4).

**Recommended fix.** Add: *"If the parties are unable to mutually select an accounting firm within fifteen (15) days after either party's request, each party shall select one firm, and the two firms shall jointly select a third; the third firm shall serve as the independent accountant. If the two firms cannot agree on a third within ten (10) days, the third shall be [named firm / selected by lot / appointed by the President of the AICPA]."*

## Issue 10 – TSA Lacks Service-Level Commitments (Minor)

**Where:** TSA § 3 (Standard of Performance) generally; Schedule A

**Problem.** The TSA requires Provider to perform services in a "good and workmanlike manner" and with the same degree of care used in the prior 12 months (§ 3.1), and to follow coating specifications (§ 3.2). However, there are no quantitative service levels—no maximum response time for coating batches, no uptime percentage, no delivery-window commitment for warehousing or freight coordination.

**Why it matters.** The Company's manufacturing schedule depends on timely coating and material flow. If Provider's performance degrades gradually (e.g., coating throughput drops 20% but is still "workmanlike"), the Company has no objective trigger to demand cure or to invoke the force-majeure/alternative-source provision in TSA § 9.1 (which requires 15 consecutive days of non-performance).

**Recommended fix.** Add a Schedule B (or amend Schedule A) with minimum service levels for each Service—for example, coating throughput no less than X lites per shift, warehousing put-away within Y hours of delivery, freight scheduling confirmation within Z business days. Provide that failure to meet a service level for two consecutive months constitutes a material breach under § 7.3.
