## 1. Survival Period for General Representations Is 90 Days, Not 18 Months

**Where:** SPA § 9.1; Escrow Agreement § 1.1 (definitions of "Release Date" and "Indemnity Release Date"); LOI § 4(b)

**Problem.** SPA § 9.1 provides that Seller's general representations and warranties "shall survive the Closing until the Release Date (as defined in the Escrow Agreement)." The Escrow Agreement defines **"Release Date"** as 90 days after the Closing Date (§ 1.1). The 18-month date is defined separately as the **"Indemnity Release Date."** As drafted, Votox's general reps (financial statements, material contracts, product warranty, customers/suppliers, etc.) expire 90 days post-closing—less than half the period the LOI bargained for (LOI § 4(b): "18 months after closing").

**Why it matters.** Ninety days is barely enough time to discover most post-closing issues. A product warranty claim, an undisclosed liability, or a financial-statement error that surfaces in month four or five would be time-barred.

**Recommended fix.** Amend SPA § 9.1 to read: "…shall survive the Closing until the **Indemnity Release Date** (as defined in the Escrow Agreement)…" Confirm the Escrow Agreement's "Indemnity Release Date" (18 months) is the intended survival period for all non-fundamental, non-tax reps.

## 2. "Facility" Definition Limits Environmental Indemnity to the Fremont, Ohio Property

**Where:** SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Facility"); TSA § 1.1 (definition of "Facility"); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities"); SPA § 9.2(d)

**Problem.** The SPA defines "Facility" by incorporating the TSA's definition: "the real property owned by Provider located at 2200 Sandusky Pike, Fremont, Ohio, including the coating lines, warehouse buildings, loading docks, rail spur and related improvements." The SPA's "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" are defined as Losses arising from Hazardous Materials "at or from any **Facility**" or any Environmental Law violation "in connection with any **Facility**" (SPA § 1.1). Because "Facility" is limited to the Fremont, Ohio site, the environmental indemnity in § 9.2(d) arguably does **not** cover the Erie, Pennsylvania or Rochester, New York plants—where the actual glass fabrication, coating, and batch-house operations take place and where the greatest environmental exposure exists.

**Why it matters.** The Erie and Rochester plants use solvents, sealants, and coating chemicals (all listed in the "Hazardous Materials" definition). If a contamination issue emerges at either plant, Votox may find the § 9.2(d) indemnity inapplicable because those sites are not "Facilities" under the SPA's definition.

**Recommended fix.** Either (a) delete the cross-reference in SPA § 1.1 and define "Facility" in the SPA to mean "any real property owned, leased or operated by the Company, including the plants in Erie, Pennsylvania and Rochester, New York, and the Fremont, Ohio property," or (b) amend the "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" definition to use the broader phrase "any property owned, leased or operated by the Company" rather than "any Facility." Option (a) is cleaner and also fixes any other place in the SPA where "Facility" appears.

## 3. Pre-Closing Taxes Are Not Carved Out of the Cap and Deductible

**Where:** SPA § 9.5(c); LOI § 4(d)

**Problem.** LOI § 4(d) states that "Neither the deductible nor the cap would apply to the following cap carve-outs: Fraud, fundamental representations, **pre-closing taxes**, and the special environmental indemnity." SPA § 9.5(c) carves out only (i) Fraud, (ii) Fundamental Representations, and (iii) "the matters described in Section 9.2(d)" (Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities). **Section 9.2(c) (Pre-Closing Taxes) is missing from the carve-out list.** As a result, pre-closing tax claims are subject to the $630,000 deductible and the $6.3 million cap.

**Why it matters.** The LOI specifically flagged multistate sales-and-use-tax compliance on fabrication, supply, and installation contracts as a diligence item (LOI § 7). If the Company has under-collected or under-remitted sales tax across multiple states, the exposure could easily exceed $6.3 million. Under the current draft, Votox would absorb the first $630,000 and be capped at $6.3 million for the remainder.

**Recommended fix.** Add "(iv) the matters described in Section 9.2(c)" to SPA § 9.5(c), so that pre-closing taxes are excluded from both the cap and the deductible, consistent with the LOI.

## 4. Non-Compete May Prohibit Seller from Performing Its TSA Obligations

**Where:** SPA § 6.6; TSA §§ 2.1, 3.2; Schedule A (Services A-1 through A-4)

**Problem.** SPA § 6.6 prohibits Seller, for five years, from "directly or indirectly engag[ing] in the fabrication of architectural glass products in North America in competition with the Business." The TSA requires Seller to provide coating, warehousing, and freight services for the Company's glass products (Schedule A, Services A-1 through A-4). Coating is a fabrication step. A strict reading of § 6.6 could be argued to prohibit Seller from performing the very services it is contractually obligated to provide under the TSA.

**Why it matters.** If Seller claims the non-compete restricts its TSA performance (or if a court reads it that way), the Company could lose access to coating capacity during the transition period, disrupting production.

**Recommended fix.** Add a carve-out to SPA § 6.6: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Seller may provide services to the Company or Buyer under the Transition Services Agreement without violating this Section 6.6."

## 5. Governing Law and Forum Conflict Between SPA and Escrow Agreement

**Where:** SPA § 11.9(a)–(b) (New York law; New York County courts); Escrow Agreement § 11.2 (Delaware law; Court of Chancery / Delaware Superior Court / D. Del.)

**Problem.** The SPA is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. The Escrow Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Court of Chancery of Delaware. SPA § 11.4 provides that the SPA controls over Ancillary Agreements "in the event of any conflict," but the two agreements assign different governing laws and forums to what may be the same underlying dispute (e.g., a fight over whether an escrow disbursement was proper).

**Why it matters.** If Votox needs to compel a disbursement or challenge the Escrow Agent's inaction, it faces uncertainty about which law applies and where to file. The Escrow Agent has interpleader rights under § 6.4 in a "court specified in Section 11.2" (Delaware), but Votox's SPA claims would be in New York. Splitting a single factual dispute across two jurisdictions and two bodies of law increases cost and creates procedural friction.

**Recommended fix.** Conform the Escrow Agreement's governing law and forum to New York (matching the SPA), or at minimum add a provision in the Escrow Agreement stating that disputes between Buyer and Seller regarding the Escrow Funds are governed by the SPA's choice-of-law and forum provisions.

## 6. TSA Benefits-Administration Service Period May Exceed the Agreement Term

**Where:** TSA § 7.1 (Term = 9 months); TSA § 2.1 (Service Period per Schedule A); Schedule A, Service A-7 (Period: "Up to the earlier of the Term and December 31, 2027")

**Problem.** The TSA Term is 9 months from Closing. Service A-7 (benefits administration) runs "up to the earlier of the Term and December 31, 2027." If Closing occurs in Q4 2026, the 9-month Term ends in Q3 2027, but the benefits service is designed to run through December 31, 2027 (end of plan year). The TSA does not expressly provide that the Agreement remains in effect for a Service whose stated period extends beyond the 9-month Term. Section 7.1 says the Agreement "shall continue until the date that is 9 months after the Closing Date…unless terminated earlier."

**Why it matters.** If the Agreement terminates at month 9 but the benefits service is supposed to run through year-end, there is a gap during which employees may lose health coverage or the Company has no contractual right to continued administration.

**Recommended fix.** Add to TSA § 7.1: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, this Agreement shall remain in effect with respect to any Service whose Service Period in Schedule A extends beyond the date that is 9 months after the Closing Date, until the expiration of that Service Period." Alternatively, cap Service A-7 at "the earlier of the Term and December 31, 2027" and confirm the Term is long enough to cover the plan year for the expected Closing date.

## 7. No Third-Party Consent Condition to Closing

**Where:** LOI § 8(c) ("receipt of material third-party consents"); SPA Article VIII (no consent condition)

**Problem.** The LOI listed "receipt of material third-party consents" as a closing condition. The SPA's conditions to Buyer's obligation to close (§ 8.2) do not include a consent condition. The only protection is the no-conflicts rep (§ 4.5), which is subject to the Disclosure Schedule and the "material respects" bring-down standard.

**Why it matters.** If a key customer, landlord, or counterparty with a change-of-control clause refuses consent, Votox is still obligated to close (absent a material rep breach or MAE). The Kestrelline forklift lease (§ 9.1) requires Lessor consent to assignment, though a stock deal technically doesn't trigger assignment. Other contracts not visible in this folder may have more onerous consent requirements.

**Recommended fix.** Either (a) add a condition in § 8.2 that all consents identified in the Disclosure Schedule as required for the transaction have been obtained, or (b) confirm in the Disclosure Schedule that no material contract requires a change-of-control consent, and add a specific rep to that effect. If (a) is added, define a materiality threshold (e.g., contracts with annual value > $500,000) so a single small consent doesn't block closing.

## 8. Product Warranty Rep Survival May Be Inadequate for Latent Defects

**Where:** SPA § 4.17; SPA § 9.1 (survival); LOI § 4(b)

**Problem.** Section 4.17 represents that all products "have conformed in all material respects with applicable contractual commitments, express warranties and industry certification standards." The Disclosure Schedule lists only warranty claims > $50,000 since January 1, 2023. Once the survival period is corrected to 18 months (see Issue 1), Votox has 18 months to discover and assert claims for non-conforming products. However, insulated glass unit seal failures, laminated glass delamination, and curtain-wall water infiltration can take 2–5 years to manifest.

**Why it matters.** A building owner who discovers a seal failure in year three would look to the Company (now Votox's subsidiary) for warranty recourse. The Company's contractual warranty to the end customer may be 5–10 years. Votox would bear the cost of remediation with no indemnity from Seller because the § 4.17 rep has expired.

**Recommended fix.** Either (a) extend the survival of § 4.17 to match the Company's longest product warranty period (confirm in diligence), or (b) add a standalone indemnity in § 9.2 for pre-closing product warranty obligations that survives for the duration of those warranty periods, carved out from the cap and deductible (similar to the environmental and tax treatment).

## 9. Conduct-of-Business Covenant: Garbled Dividend Language

**Where:** SPA § 6.1

**Problem.** Section 6.1 provides that the Company shall not, without Buyer's consent, "declare any dividend other than cash dividends paid prior to the Closing." This is circular: a dividend "paid prior to the Closing" has already occurred, so the exception covers nothing going forward. The likely intent is to prohibit all dividends and distributions between signing and closing.

**Why it matters.** As drafted, the restriction is ambiguous. Seller could argue the Company may declare a dividend at or before Closing because it would be a "cash dividend paid prior to the Closing." This could drain working capital and affect the Closing Working Capital calculation.

**Recommended fix.** Replace with: "declare or pay any dividend or make any other distribution on or with respect to its capital stock."

## 10. No Fallback Mechanism for Selecting the Independent Accounting Firm

**Where:** SPA § 2.3(b)

**Problem.** Working capital disputes are submitted to "an independent accounting firm mutually selected by Buyer and Seller." There is no mechanism for what happens if the parties cannot agree on the firm.

**Why it matters.** If Seller stalls or refuses to name a firm, the post-closing adjustment (and release of the $750,000 Adjustment Escrow) is stuck indefinitely. The Escrow Agreement (§ 5.1(b)) releases the Adjustment Escrow on the Release Date (90 days) unless Buyer gives notice that amounts remain to be determined—but if the parties simply can't agree on the accountant, the process deadlocks.

**Recommended fix.** Add: "If the parties cannot agree on the independent accounting firm within fifteen (15) days after a dispute arises, either party may request the President of the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants to appoint a firm, whose determination shall be final and binding absent manifest error."

## 11. Escrow Agreement: Quarterly Tax Distributions May Create Administrative Burden

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 10.1, § 10.4

**Problem.** Section 10.1 treats Buyer as the owner of all Escrow Funds for income tax purposes, meaning Votox reports all escrow interest as income. Section 10.4 then requires the Escrow Agent to distribute 40% of quarterly earnings to Buyer within 30 days after quarter-end. The remaining 60% stays in escrow but is still Votox's taxable income.

**Why it matters.** Votox will owe tax on 100% of escrow earnings but only receive 40% in cash each quarter. The timing mismatch creates a cash tax cost with no corresponding cash inflow. Over 18 months (the Indemnity Escrow period), this compounds.

**Recommended fix.** This is largely a matter of tax structuring rather than a negotiation point with Seller (the 50/50 fee split in § 2.4(c) is the only shared-cost element). Votox's tax team should model the drag. If material, consider negotiating for 100% of earnings to be distributed quarterly to Buyer (with the principal remaining in escrow), or for the earnings to be allocated 50/50 between the parties for tax purposes.

## 12. TSA: Limited Audit Rights for Cost Verification

**Where:** TSA § 4.5

**Problem.** The Company may audit Provider's cost records "not more than once per calendar quarter." Over a 9-month Term, that is a maximum of three audits. Given that Service Fees are based on "actual cost" including "a reasonable allocation of indirect costs" (§ 4.1(d)), and the allocation basis is "consistent with Provider's allocation…before the Closing" (which may have been informal while the Company was a subsidiary), three audits may be insufficient to validate the numbers.

**Why it matters.** If indirect costs are over-allocated, Votox overpays for 9 months with limited ability to detect and recover the overcharge.

**Recommended fix.** (a) Increase audit frequency to monthly for the first 90 days, then quarterly; (b) require Provider to deliver a written description of its indirect-cost allocation methodology as an exhibit to the TSA at signing, so the Company can verify consistency from day one; (c) add a provision that if an audit reveals overbilling > 5%, Provider refunds the overcharge plus interest.

## 13. Kestrelline Forklift Lease: Operational Commitment Through ~March 2029

**Where:** Kestrelline Lease § 2.1; Schedule No. 1

**Problem.** The 48-month initial term began ~March 2025 and runs to ~March 2029. Aggregate Monthly Rent is $11,880 (~$142,560/year). The lease is governed by Pennsylvania law with venue in Erie County. The lessee may not assign without Lessor consent (§ 9.1).

**Why it matters.** This is a committed operating cost for the next ~2+ years post-closing. It is below the $250,000/year Material Contract threshold in SPA § 4.9, so it would not appear in the Disclosure Schedule under that section. Votox should confirm it was identified in financial diligence and that the monthly rent is reflected in the working capital model. If Votox plans to consolidate material handling post-closing, it will need Lessor consent to assign or must wait for the month-to-month period after March 2029.

**Recommended fix.** No change to the lease is needed for the transaction (stock deal = no assignment). But Votox should (a) confirm the lease is reflected in the Estimated Closing Statement's Indebtedness/Transaction Expenses if any amounts are accrued, and (b) calendar the March 2029 month-to-month transition as a decision point.

## 14. NDA Term May Expire If Closing Is Delayed

**Where:** NDA § 9 (2-year term from August 17, 2026 → expires August 17, 2028); SPA § 6.2 (access "subject to the Confidentiality Agreement")

**Problem.** SPA § 6.2 conditions pre-closing access on the NDA. If the transaction is not closed by August 17, 2028, the NDA expires and the access covenant loses its confidentiality backdrop. The SPA outside date (§ 10.1(b)) is left blank ([●], 2027), so this is unlikely but not impossible if there are regulatory or consent delays.

**Why it matters.** Low probability, but if it occurs, Seller could argue the access covenant is unenforceable because its condition (the NDA) has lapsed.

**Recommended fix.** Add to the SPA: "The Confidentiality Agreement shall remain in effect until the later of its stated expiration date and the Closing Date." Alternatively, confirm the outside date is well before August 2028.
