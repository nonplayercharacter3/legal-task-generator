**Re: Proposed Acquisition of Lumacrest Facade Systems, Inc.: Letter of Intent**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

Votox Glazing Holdings, Inc. ("**Buyer**") is pleased to submit this letter of intent (this "**Letter**"). It sets out the principal terms on which Buyer proposes to acquire all of the issued and outstanding capital stock (the "**Shares**") of Lumacrest Facade Systems, Inc., a Pennsylvania corporation (the "**Company**"), from Stounaisk Industries, Inc. ("**Seller**"). The proposed acquisition is referred to in this Letter as the "**Transaction**."

Except as expressly provided in Section 10, this Letter is a non-binding expression of the parties' present intentions. It is intended only as a basis for the negotiation of a definitive stock purchase agreement (the "**Definitive Agreement**") and related ancillary agreements.

## 1. Transaction Structure

Buyer would purchase from Seller all of the Shares, free and clear of all liens and encumbrances. The Company would be acquired as a going concern. The acquired business would include its tempered, laminated and insulated glass unit and curtain-wall glazing fabrication operations at its plants in Erie, Pennsylvania and Rochester, New York (the "**Business**").

## 2. Purchase Price

(a) The aggregate purchase price for the Shares would be $63,000,000 (the "**Purchase Price**"), payable in cash at closing (the "**Closing**").

(b) The Purchase Price assumes that the Company is delivered on a cash-free, debt-free basis with a normalized level of net working capital. The parties would agree a working capital target in the Definitive Agreement based on Buyer's financial due diligence.

(c) The Purchase Price would be subject to a customary post-Closing adjustment for closing working capital, indebtedness and unpaid transaction expenses.

## 3. Escrow

At Closing, Buyer would deposit a portion of the Purchase Price with a mutually acceptable third-party escrow agent, in two separate accounts:

(a) an indemnity escrow equal to ten percent (10%) of the Purchase Price, to secure Seller's indemnification obligations. This escrow would be released to Seller eighteen (18) months after Closing, less amounts held back for then-pending claims; and

(b) an adjustment escrow, in an amount to be agreed, to secure Seller's obligations under the post-Closing purchase price adjustment.

## 4. Representations, Warranties and Indemnification

(a) **Representations and Warranties.** The Definitive Agreement would contain representations and warranties of Seller customary for a transaction of this type. These would include organization, authority, capitalization, brokers, financial statements, material contracts, real property, taxes (including collection and remittance of sales and use taxes on the Company's fabrication, supply and installation contracts), environmental matters, product warranty, employee matters, and customers and suppliers.

(b) **Survival.** Seller's general representations and warranties would survive Closing for 18 months after closing. Fundamental representations (organization, authority, capitalization and brokers) and the tax representations would survive until sixty (60) days after expiration of the applicable statute of limitations.

(c) **Indemnification.** Seller would indemnify Buyer and its affiliates, including the Company after Closing, for:

- breaches of representations, warranties and covenants;
- all taxes of the Company attributable to periods (or portions of periods) ending on or before the Closing Date ("**pre-closing taxes**");
- environmental liabilities arising from conditions existing or activities conducted on or prior to the Closing Date at the Company's current and former facilities, through a special environmental indemnity; and
- unpaid indebtedness and transaction expenses.

(d) **Limitations.** Seller's liability for breaches of general representations and warranties would be subject to a deductible equal to one percent (1%) of the Purchase Price. Seller's aggregate indemnification liability would be capped at an amount equal to the indemnity escrow, which is ten percent (10%) of the Purchase Price. Neither the deductible nor the cap would apply to the following cap carve-outs: Fraud, fundamental representations, pre-closing taxes, and the special environmental indemnity. Seller's liability for these carve-out matters would be limited to the Purchase Price.

(e) **Exclusive Remedy.** Indemnification would be the parties' exclusive post-Closing remedy, except for fraud, equitable relief and the purchase price adjustment.

## 5. Transition Services

At Closing, Seller and the Company would enter into a transition services agreement. Under it, Seller would continue to provide the Company with coating, warehousing and certain shared administrative services currently furnished from Seller's retained operations. The services would be provided at cost, for an initial term of nine (9) months after Closing.

## 6. Restrictive Covenants

Seller would agree to customary non-competition and non-solicitation covenants for a period of five (5) years after Closing. These would cover the fabrication of architectural glass products in North America and the solicitation of Company employees.

## 7. Due Diligence

Buyer's proposal is subject to satisfactory completion of business, financial, legal, tax, environmental and insurance due diligence. This would include Phase I (and, where warranted, Phase II) environmental site assessments of the Erie and Rochester plants and a review of the Company's multistate sales and use tax compliance. Seller would cause the Company to give Buyer and its advisors reasonable access to the Company's personnel, properties, books and records.

## 8. Conditions

The Closing would be subject to:

(a) negotiation and execution of the Definitive Agreement and ancillary agreements satisfactory to each party;

(b) expiration or termination of any applicable waiting period under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust Improvements Act of 1976;

(c) receipt of material third-party consents;

(d) the absence of any material adverse change in the Business; and

(e) approval of the Transaction by Buyer's board of directors.

## 9. Timing

The parties would use good faith efforts to execute the Definitive Agreement within sixty (60) days after the date of this Letter, and to close promptly after the closing conditions are satisfied.

## 10. Binding Provisions

Only Sections 10 through 14 of this Letter (the "**Binding Provisions**") are legally binding on the parties. No other provision of this Letter creates any legal obligation. No binding agreement regarding the Transaction will exist unless and until the Definitive Agreement is executed and delivered.

## 11. Exclusivity

(a) For a period of sixty (60) days after the date of this Letter, Seller shall not, directly or indirectly, take any of the following actions with any person other than Buyer concerning any acquisition of the Company, the Shares or any material portion of the Company's assets:

- solicit, initiate, encourage or negotiate any proposal; or
- enter into any agreement.

(b) The parties may extend this exclusivity period by mutual written agreement.

## 12. Confidentiality

This Letter and its terms are subject to the mutual confidentiality agreement between Buyer and Seller, which remains in full force and effect in accordance with its terms.

## 13. Expenses

Each party shall bear its own costs and expenses in connection with this Letter and the Transaction, whether or not the Transaction is consummated.

## 14. Governing Law; Termination; Counterparts

(a) This Letter shall be governed by the laws of the State of New York, without regard to conflict of laws principles.

(b) This Letter shall terminate on the earliest of:

- execution of the Definitive Agreement;
- written notice of termination by either party; and
- expiration of the exclusivity period under Section 11.

Sections 12, 13 and 14 shall survive any termination.

(c) This Letter may be executed in counterparts, including by electronic signature.
