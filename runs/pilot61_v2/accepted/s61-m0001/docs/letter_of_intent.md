**Re: Proposed Acquisition of Ostbrook Kibble Company**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

This letter of intent (this "**Letter**") sets out the principal terms on which Peinol Pet Nutrition, Inc., a Delaware corporation ("**Buyer**"), proposes to acquire all of the issued and outstanding stock of Ostbrook Kibble Company, a Wisconsin corporation (the "**Company**"), from Laraisk Family Holdings, LLC, a Wisconsin limited liability company ("**Seller**") (the "**Proposed Transaction**").

Except as provided in Section 10, this Letter is a non-binding expression of the parties' current intentions. It does not create any legally binding obligation to complete the Proposed Transaction.

## 1. Structure

Buyer would purchase from Seller all of the issued and outstanding shares of capital stock of the Company (the "**Shares**"), free and clear of all liens and encumbrances. The Proposed Transaction would be documented in a definitive stock purchase agreement among Buyer, Seller and the Company (the "**Definitive Agreement**"), together with the related ancillary agreements described in this Letter.

## 2. Purchase Price and Payment

**2.1 Purchase Price.** The aggregate purchase price for the Shares would be $84,000,000 (the "**Purchase Price**"). The Purchase Price assumes a cash-free, debt-free basis and a normalized level of working capital. It would be subject to a customary working capital adjustment against a target to be agreed on the basis of Buyer's financial due diligence.

**2.2 Form of Payment.** Buyer would pay the Purchase Price at closing (the "**Closing**") as follows:

(a) cash, payable to Seller by wire transfer of immediately available funds, equal to the Purchase Price less the amounts in paragraphs (b) and (c), as adjusted;

(b) a subordinated promissory note issued by Buyer to Seller in the original principal amount of $12,000,000 (the "**Seller Note**"); and

(c) $4,200,000, deposited with a mutually acceptable escrow agent to secure Seller's indemnification obligations (the "**Indemnity Escrow**").

**2.3 Seller Note.** The Seller Note would bear interest at a fixed rate to be agreed and would have a term of approximately five years. It would be subordinated to Buyer's senior acquisition financing on terms reasonably acceptable to Buyer's senior lenders. Buyer would have the right to set off amounts finally determined to be owed to Buyer under the indemnification provisions of the Definitive Agreement against amounts payable under the Seller Note.

**2.4 Indemnity Escrow.** The Indemnity Escrow would be held and disbursed under a customary escrow agreement among Buyer, Seller and the escrow agent. The release mechanics would be set out in the Definitive Agreement and the escrow agreement.

## 3. Representations and Warranties

The Definitive Agreement would contain representations and warranties of Seller customary for a transaction of this type. These would include representations as to organization, authority, capitalization, title to the Shares, financial statements, material contracts, compliance with laws and permits, taxes, environmental matters, employees and benefit plans, real property, insurance, intellectual property and brokers.

Given the nature of the Company's business, the representations would also include specific representations concerning product safety, product quality and recalls. These would cover compliance with the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, the Food Safety Modernization Act and state feed laws; the absence of adulteration, misbranding and contamination; conformity to labeled nutrient profiles; and the absence of past or threatened recalls, market withdrawals and stock recoveries.

Buyer would give customary representations as to its organization, authority, financing and brokers.

## 4. Indemnification and Survival

**4.1 Survival.** The general representations and warranties of Seller would survive for 18 months after Closing; product safety and recall representations 24 months. Fundamental representations, including organization, authority, capitalization, title to the Shares, taxes and brokers, would survive until 60 days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations. Covenants would survive until fully performed.

**4.2 Indemnification.** Seller would indemnify Buyer and its affiliates, including the Company after Closing, for losses arising from breaches of Seller's representations, warranties and covenants, and for pre-Closing taxes and indebtedness of the Company. Losses would include the costs of product recalls, market withdrawals and stock recoveries, customer chargebacks, product destruction and regulatory remediation.

**4.3 Limitations.** Seller's liability for breaches of general representations would be subject to a basket equal to 0.5% of the Purchase Price and a cap equal to 10% of the Purchase Price. Neither limitation would apply to fundamental representations or fraud.

**4.4 Source of Recovery.** Indemnifiable losses would be recovered first from the Indemnity Escrow, then by set-off against the Seller Note at Buyer's election, and then directly from Seller.

## 5. Conditions to Closing

The Closing would be subject to customary conditions, including:

(a) completion of Buyer's business, financial, legal, regulatory and food safety due diligence to Buyer's satisfaction;

(b) negotiation and execution of the Definitive Agreement and related ancillary agreements;

(c) receipt of all required third-party and governmental consents, including consents under the Company's material customer and supply contracts;

(d) approval of the Proposed Transaction by Buyer's board of directors;

(e) Buyer's receipt of its senior acquisition financing on terms satisfactory to Buyer; and

(f) the absence of any material adverse change in the business, assets, financial condition or results of operations of the Company.

## 6. Due Diligence

Following execution of this Letter, Seller shall cause the Company to give Buyer and its representatives reasonable access to the Company's facilities, books, records, contracts and personnel. This includes the Company's manufacturing facility in Fond du Lac, Wisconsin, its quality assurance and supplier verification records, its regulatory inspection history, and its customer complaint and product return files.

## 7. Restrictive Covenants

The Definitive Agreement would include customary confidentiality covenants of Seller, and a covenant that Seller will not engage in the manufacture or distribution of pet food products in the United States or Canada for five years after Closing.

## 8. Personal Guaranties

Seller has informed Buyer that certain of its members have personally guaranteed obligations of the Company under equipment lease and ingredient supply arrangements. The parties would address the treatment of those guaranties in the Definitive Agreement or a related ancillary agreement, on terms to be negotiated.

## 9. Timing

The parties intend to use good-faith efforts to sign the Definitive Agreement by March 31, 2026, and to complete the Closing as promptly as practicable thereafter.

## 10. Binding Provisions

Only this Section 10 and Sections 6, 11 and 12 (the "**Binding Provisions**") are legally binding on the parties. No other provision of this Letter is binding, and no party will have any obligation regarding the Proposed Transaction unless and until the Definitive Agreement is executed and delivered. Either party may terminate negotiations at any time and for any reason by written notice to the other. The Binding Provisions survive any such termination, except that Section 11.1 ends as provided in it.

## 11. Exclusivity and Confidentiality

**11.1 Exclusivity.** From the date of Seller's countersignature until the earlier of (a) 90 days after that date and (b) the date either party gives written notice terminating negotiations, Seller shall not, and shall cause the Company and their respective representatives not to, directly or indirectly solicit, encourage, negotiate or enter into any agreement with any person other than Buyer concerning any sale of the Shares, any merger or recapitalization of the Company, or any sale of all or a material portion of the Company's assets outside the ordinary course of business. Seller shall promptly notify Buyer of any unsolicited proposal of that kind.

**11.2 Confidentiality.** The terms of this Letter and the existence of the parties' discussions are confidential. They are subject to the Confidentiality Agreement between Buyer and the Company dated October 6, 2025, which remains in full force and effect.

## 12. General

**12.1 Expenses.** Each party shall bear its own costs and expenses in connection with this Letter and the Proposed Transaction.

**12.2 Governing Law; Jurisdiction.** The Binding Provisions are governed by the laws of the State of New York, without regard to conflict-of-laws principles that would require the application of the laws of another jurisdiction. Each party submits to the jurisdiction of the state or federal courts sitting in New York County, New York, for any action arising out of the Binding Provisions.

**12.3 Counterparts.** This Letter may be executed in counterparts, including by electronic signature. Each counterpart is deemed an original, and all of them together constitute one instrument.

This Letter will expire if Seller does not countersign and return it by 5:00 p.m. Eastern Time on December 19, 2025.
