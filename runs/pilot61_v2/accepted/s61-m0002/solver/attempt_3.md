## Issue 1: Assumed Liabilities Broader Than Agreed in the Letter of Intent

**Where:**
- APA § 2.3(a): Buyer assumes "all Liabilities arising under the Assigned Contracts, **whether arising before, on or after the Closing Date**."
- LOI § 3: "Buyer will assume **only obligations under Assigned Contracts arising after the Closing**, together with obligations to ticket holders for events scheduled after the Closing to the extent of the deferred revenue credited to Buyer."
- LOI § 3 (second sentence): "Seller will retain all other liabilities of Seller and the Business, including **all liabilities under the Assigned Contracts arising before the Closing (such as artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements and refunds for events cancelled or rescheduled before the Closing)**."

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
The APA sweeps in pre-Closing liabilities under the Assigned Contracts—artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements, and refund obligations for cancelled or rescheduled shows. The LOI expressly excluded these from the Assumed Liabilities and assigned them to Seller. Under the APA as drafted, Vogert is on the hook for any pre-Closing artist guarantee or settlement that surfaces after Closing, even though the deal economics were negotiated on the assumption that Seller would retain them. Given that the business promotes live music events with artist guarantees (see APA § 4.7, referencing Schedule 4.7 for "all artist guarantees, unpaid settlements and outstanding deposit obligations"), this is a material economic exposure.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend APA § 2.3(a) to read: "all Liabilities arising under the Assigned Contracts, **whether arising on or after the Closing Date**." Add a clarifying sentence: "Liabilities arising under the Assigned Contracts before the Closing Date (including artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements, and refund obligations for events cancelled or rescheduled before the Closing) are Excluded Liabilities and shall be retained by Seller." Confirm that Schedule 4.7 items are reflected on Schedule 2.4 as Excluded Liabilities.

## Issue 2: Survival Period for General Representations Reduced from 18 Months to Approximately 6 Months

**Where:**
- APA § 9.1: "The representations and warranties of Seller in this Agreement, other than the Fundamental Representations, shall survive the Closing until the **Set-off Expiration Date** (as defined in the Seller Note)."
- Seller Note § 4(c): "Set-off Expiration Date" = "the **180th day after the Issue Date**." Issue Date is June 30, 2026, so the Set-off Expiration Date is approximately **December 27, 2026**—roughly 6 months post-Closing.
- LOI § 6(b): "Seller's general representations and warranties will survive for **18 months after Closing**."

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
The LOI contemplated 18 months of indemnification protection for Seller's general reps (financial statements, material contracts, ticketing/deferred revenue, IP, permits, compliance, employees, etc.). The APA as drafted cuts that to approximately 6 months. Any breach of a general rep discovered after December 27, 2026—such as a latent problem with the Ticketing Data (APA § 4.9), a permit that was not actually in full force (APA § 4.10), or a financial statement error (APA § 4.6)—will be time-barred. Given that the 2027 Ironfern Fest season is the primary asset being acquired, a 6-month survival window expires before the first major event of the post-Closing season.

**Recommended fix:**
Decouple survival from the Set-off Expiration Date. Amend APA § 9.1 to provide that non-Fundamental Representations survive for **eighteen (18) months** after the Closing Date, consistent with the LOI. The Set-off Expiration Date in the Seller Note should be a separate concept (limiting the window to *notice* set-off claims) and should not define the survival period. If the business rationale for tying the two together was to limit the period during which Vogert can reduce the note balance, that can be addressed by extending the Set-off Expiration Date in Seller Note § 4(c) to 18 months as well, or by leaving the set-off window at 180 days but providing that claims noticed within that window survive until finally resolved (which Seller Note § 4(c) already contemplates).

## Issue 3: Seller Note "Limited Recourse" Provision Conflicts with APA Cap Exceptions

**Where:**
- Seller Note § 5: "Holder's aggregate liability for all Indemnifiable Losses, whether recovered by set-off against this Note or otherwise, shall in no event exceed the **Indemnity Cap**."
- APA § 9.5(b): "The Deductible and the Indemnity Cap shall **not apply** to Losses arising out of Fraud, any inaccuracy in or breach of a Fundamental Representation or the representations in Section 4.13 (Taxes), any Excluded Liability, or claims under Section 9.3(b)."
- APA § 12.9: "In the event of any conflict between the terms of this Agreement and the terms of any Ancillary Agreement, the terms of this Agreement shall prevail; **provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control as to payment, set-off and recourse**."

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
The APA carves out Fraud, Fundamental Reps, Taxes, and Excluded Liabilities from the $2.4M cap. But the Seller Note—which controls as to "recourse" under APA § 12.9—states that *all* Indemnifiable Losses (defined in Seller Note § 4(a) by reference to Article IX) are subject to the Indemnity Cap without exception. If Dretusk argues that the Seller Note governs recourse, it could assert that even a Fraud claim or an Excluded Liability claim is capped at $2.4M. This gutters the uncapped protections that the APA (and the LOI) were designed to provide.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Seller Note § 5 to add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Indemnity Cap shall not apply to Indemnifiable Losses arising out of Fraud, any breach of a Fundamental Representation, the representations in Section 4.13 (Taxes), or any Excluded Liability, in each case consistent with Section 9.5(b) of the Purchase Agreement." Alternatively, amend APA § 12.9 to clarify that the Seller Note controls as to payment mechanics and set-off procedure but not as to the scope of recourse or the applicability of cap exceptions.

## Issue 4: Pending Parent Merger Triggers Change of Control Default Under the Seller Note

**Where:**
- Seller Note § 7(a)(v): A "Change of Control occurs without the prior written consent of Holder" is an Event of Default.
- Seller Note § 7(b): "Change of Control" includes "(i) any Person or group of Persons...acquiring...beneficial ownership of **more than fifty percent of the voting equity of Maker or of any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker**."
- Board Consent, Recital F: Vogert Entertainment Group, Inc. (sole member/Parent of Maker) entered into a Merger Agreement with Sablecrest Capital Partners dated August 28, 2026. A Sablecrest subsidiary will merge with and into the Parent; the Parent will survive as a wholly owned subsidiary of an entity controlled by Sablecrest funds. Expected to close Q1 2027.
- Seller Note § 7(c): Upon a Change of Control default, Holder may declare the entire unpaid principal balance ($4M) and all accrued interest immediately due and payable.

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
When the Sablecrest merger closes (expected Q1 2027), Sablecrest-controlled entities will own more than 50% of the voting equity of the Parent, which directly controls Maker (Vogert Live Holdings). This is a textbook Change of Control under Seller Note § 7(b)(i). Unless Dretusk consents in writing before the merger closes, the note becomes an Event of Default, and Dretusk can accelerate the $4M balance (plus interest and default interest at 8%). This could create a liquidity crisis for Vogert at the worst possible time—during merger integration.

**Recommended fix:**
Obtain Dretusk's written consent to the Change of Control *before* the Sablecrest merger closes. The consent should be documented as a waiver under Seller Note § 11(b) or as a side letter. If Dretusk resists, negotiate: (a) a carve-out from the Change of Control definition for transactions in which Maker remains a wholly owned subsidiary of a publicly traded or PE-backed parent; (b) a requirement that the acquirer assume or refinance the note; or (c) at minimum, an extension of the cure period. Flag this to the client immediately—the Board Consent (Resolution 3) authorizes participation in the Parent merger but does not address the Seller Note default risk.

## Issue 5: Objection Notice Deadline for Closing Statement Dispute Has Likely Passed

**Where:**
- APA § 2.7(b): Buyer may deliver an Objection Notice "within **thirty (30) days** after delivery of the Closing Statement."
- APA § 2.7(c): "If Buyer does not deliver an Objection Notice before the expiration of the Review Period, the Closing Statement shall be **final and binding** on the parties."
- Board Consent, Recital C: Seller delivered the Closing Statement by email on **August 3, 2026**.
- Board Consent, Recital D: Management estimates the Closing Deferred Revenue is understated by approximately **$1,150,000**.
- Board Consent, Resolution 1: Authorizes officers to "prepare, execute and deliver to the Seller an Objection Notice under Section 2.7(b)."
- Board Consent date: **September 8, 2026**.

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
The 30-day Review Period expired on **September 2, 2026** (30 days after August 3). The Board Consent authorizing the Objection Notice is dated September 8, 2026—six days after the deadline. If no Objection Notice was physically delivered to Dretusk on or before September 2, the Closing Statement is final and binding under APA § 2.7(c), and Vogert has lost its right to dispute the $1.15M understatement. The ratification clause in Resolution 4 ("all actions previously taken...are hereby ratified") helps only if an officer already delivered the notice before September 2; it cannot cure a missed deadline.

**Recommended fix:**
Immediately confirm with the client whether an Objection Notice was in fact delivered to Dretusk on or before September 2, 2026. If it was, file proof of delivery (email timestamp, courier receipt) and proceed with the dispute under § 2.7(d). If it was not, assess whether: (a) Dretusk can be persuaded to waive the deadline by agreement (APA § 12.3 permits amendment by written instrument); (b) there is a colorable argument that "delivery" under § 12.2 (email with confirmation of transmission) occurred within the period even if the Board formalized authorization later; or (c) the facts in Recital D (post-Effective Time refunds netted against receipts; omitted ticketing agent receipts) constitute a basis for a claim outside the § 2.7 process (e.g., breach of the § 4.8 representation regarding accurate recording of ticket sales). If the deadline is truly missed and no waiver is obtained, the $1.15M is likely unrecoverable through the adjustment mechanism, and the only remaining recourse would be an indemnification claim under Article IX (subject to the survival and cap issues in Issues 2 and 3 above).

## Issue 6: Seller Note Assignable Without Vogert's Consent

**Where:**
- Seller Note § 9: "Holder may **assign, transfer or pledge** this Note or any interest in it to **any Person without the consent of Maker**."

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
Dretusk can sell or pledge the $4M note to a third party—potentially a distressed-debt buyer or a fund with an aggressive collection posture—without Vogert's approval. In the context of the Sablecrest merger (Issue 4), if the note is accelerated or if Dretusk faces its own financial pressures post-sale, the note could end up in the hands of an unfamiliar holder with different expectations about set-off, information rights, or workout terms. The information rights in Seller Note § 6 (monthly ticket sales reports, quarterly financials, annual financials) would then run to a third party.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Seller Note § 9 to require Maker's consent for any assignment, or at minimum to restrict assignment to (a) Holder's Affiliates, (b) Holder's lenders for collateral purposes, or (c) a successor to all or substantially all of Holder's business. Add a provision that any assignee is bound by all modifications and waivers previously granted to Maker.

## Issue 7: Mutual Confidentiality Agreement Remains in Effect and Creates Compliance Tension

**Where:**
- MCA § 9: "This Agreement continues in effect for **two (2) years** after the Effective Date." Effective Date is January 12, 2026, so the MCA expires **January 12, 2028**.
- MCA § 2.2(c): Confidential Information includes "ticket sales data, advance sales reports, refund histories, pricing strategies and customer, purchaser and subscriber lists and data."
- APA § 2.1(c): "Ticketing Data" (defined in APA § 1.1 as "all customer lists, purchaser records, email and SMS subscriber lists, ticket sales histories and related data of the Business") is a Purchased Asset.
- MCA § 4.1: Receiving Party shall use Confidential Information "solely to evaluate, negotiate and, if applicable, complete the Proposed Transaction (the 'Permitted Purpose'), and for **no other purpose**."

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
Vogert now *owns* the Ticketing Data as a Purchased Asset. It needs to use that data to operate the business (sell tickets, market events, manage customer relationships). But the MCA restricts use of Dretusk's "Confidential Information" (which encompasses the same data) to the "Permitted Purpose" only. A strict reading could be used by Dretusk to argue that Vogert's post-Closing use of the ticketing data violates the MCA. While this argument is commercially unreasonable (Vogert bought the data), it creates a cloud over the asset and could be raised in a dispute.

**Recommended fix:**
Execute a short-form amendment or side letter to the MCA confirming that, effective as of the Closing Date (June 30, 2026), the Ticketing Data and all other information constituting a Purchased Asset is no longer subject to the MCA's use restrictions, and that Vogert's post-Closing use of such data in operating the Business does not constitute a breach. Alternatively, have Dretusk deliver a notice under MCA § 7 terminating its status as a "Disclosing Party" with respect to the Purchased Assets.

## Issue 8: Lighting and Rigging Agreement – Automatic Renewal and Consequential Damages Waiver

**Where:**
- Lighting Agreement § 10.1: Term runs to **December 31, 2027**, then "renews automatically for successive one-year periods unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least **sixty (60) days** before the end of the then-current period."
- Lighting Agreement § 9.1: Neither Party is liable for "indirect, incidental, consequential, special or punitive damages, or for **lost profits or lost ticket revenue**."
- Lighting Agreement § 9.2: Each Party's aggregate liability per Event Order is capped at "the total fees payable under that Event Order" (exceptions: indemnification under § 8, gross negligence/willful misconduct, and payment obligations).

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
(a) *Auto-renewal:* If Vogert wants to replace Lumenhoist after the initial term, it must deliver non-renewal notice by **November 1, 2027** (60 days before December 31, 2027). Missing that date locks Vogert in for another year. Given that the 2027 Ironfern Fest is the marquee event, Vogert will want flexibility to evaluate performance after the festival.

(b) *Lost ticket revenue waiver:* If a rigging or lighting failure causes a show to be cancelled or delayed, resulting in lost ticket revenue (potentially millions for a festival), § 9.1 bars recovery of that loss. The only recovery would be direct damages (equipment repair, crew costs) capped at the Event Order fees under § 9.2. For a festival-scale event, the Event Order fees may be a small fraction of the revenue at risk.

**Recommended fix:**
(a) Calendar the November 1, 2027 non-renewal notice deadline and set an internal reminder for October 1, 2027 to make the decision.
(b) Negotiate a carve-out from § 9.1 for lost ticket revenue where the cancellation is caused by Vendor's gross negligence or failure to comply with § 2.3 (Rigging Safety). At minimum, ensure the insurance requirements in § 7.1 (CGL $2M/$4M, umbrella $5M) are verified before each Ironfern Fest event and that certificates naming Vogert as additional insured are received in advance.

## Issue 9: Board Consent Does Not Address Seller Note Consent for Change of Control

**Where:**
- Board Consent, Resolution 3: Authorizes the Company to participate in the Parent merger "to the extent required of the Company as a subsidiary of the Parent." Sub-clauses cover furnishing information, cooperating on filings, operating in compliance with interim covenants, and executing closing documents.
- Board Consent, Recital F: Describes the Sablecrest merger but does not reference the Seller Note or the Change of Control provision.
- Seller Note § 7(a)(v): Change of Control without Holder's prior written consent = Event of Default.

**Why it matters for Vogert:**
The Board Consent authorizes the Company's *cooperation* with the Parent merger but does not authorize (or even acknowledge) the need to obtain Dretusk's consent to the Change of Control under the Seller Note. If the officers proceed under the general authority in Resolution 4 without addressing the note, the Company could find itself in default at the moment the merger closes. The Board should be aware of this exposure and should either (a) authorize officers to seek and negotiate Dretusk's consent, or (b) condition the merger participation on obtaining that consent.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend or supplement the Board Consent (or adopt a new consent) that: (a) expressly acknowledges the Change of Control risk under Seller Note § 7; (b) authorizes the Authorized Officers to negotiate and obtain Dretusk's written consent or waiver; and (c) if consent cannot be obtained on acceptable terms, provides that the Company shall not take any action that would trigger the default without further Board approval.
