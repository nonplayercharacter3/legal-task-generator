## II. ISSUES IN DETAIL

### Issue 1: Objection Notice Deadline Likely Expired (URGENT)

**Where:** APA § 2.7(b)–(c); Buyer Board Consent, Recitals C–D and Resolution 1.

**Facts:** Seller delivered the Closing Statement by email to Vogert's CFO on **August 3, 2026** (Board Consent, Recital C). APA § 2.7(b) gives Buyer **thirty (30) days** after delivery to deliver an Objection Notice. That deadline fell on or about **September 2, 2026**. The Board Consent is dated **September 8, 2026** and *authorizes* the officers to "prepare, execute and deliver" the Objection Notice, but does not state that one has already been delivered.

**Why it matters:** APA § 2.7(c) provides that if Buyer does not deliver an Objection Notice before the Review Period expires, "the Closing Statement shall be **final and binding** on the parties." Management estimates the Closing Statement understates Closing Deferred Revenue by approximately **$1,150,000** (Board Consent, Recital D). Under APA § 2.7(e)(i), if final Closing Deferred Revenue exceeds the estimate, Seller owes Buyer the difference. A final-and-binding Closing Statement that understates deferred revenue by $1.15M means Vogert loses that recovery permanently.

**Recommended fix:**
- Immediately confirm whether an Objection Notice was in fact delivered on or before September 2, 2026. If it was, the deadline issue is moot and the dispute proceeds under § 2.7(d).
- If it was **not** delivered, assess whether there is a basis to argue the deadline was tolled or waived (e.g., Seller's course of performance, equitable estoppel, or a mutual extension). This is a weak position given the plain language of § 2.7(c), but worth exploring before conceding.
- In any event, deliver the Objection Notice immediately and in writing, citing the two specific errors identified in Recital D (post-Effective Time refunds improperly netted; third-party ticketing agent receipts omitted). Even if the deadline has passed, putting Seller on notice preserves leverage and may prompt a commercial resolution.

### Issue 2: Pending Sablecrest Merger Triggers Change of Control Default (CRITICAL)

**Where:** Seller Note § 7(a)(v), § 7(b); Buyer Board Consent, Recital F.

**Facts:** The Board Consent (Recital F) discloses that Vogert's sole member, **Vogert Entertainment Group, Inc.**, entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger with **Sablecrest Capital Partners** dated August 28, 2026. A Sablecrest-controlled entity will merge with and into the Parent, with the Parent surviving as a wholly owned subsidiary of Sablecrest. The merger is expected to close in **Q1 2027**.

Seller Note § 7(b) defines "Change of Control" to include "(i) any Person or group… acquiring… beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent of the voting equity of Maker **or of any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker**." Once Sablecrest controls the Parent, it will indirectly control Vogert (Maker). This is a Change of Control.

Seller Note § 7(a)(v) makes a Change of Control "without the prior written consent of Holder" an **Event of Default**. Under § 7(c), upon an Event of Default (other than insolvency), Holder may declare the entire unpaid principal balance and accrued interest immediately due and payable.

**Why it matters:** The Seller Note has a **$4,000,000** principal balance maturing June 30, 2028. If the Sablecrest merger closes without Dretusk's prior written consent, Dretusk can accelerate the entire note. Given that Vogert's set-off rights expire 180 days post-closing (see Issue 5 below), by Q1 2027 Vogert will have no set-off leverage and will face a $4M+ cash obligation on demand.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Dretusk's **prior written consent** to the Change of Control before the Sablecrest merger closes. This should be a condition of the merger's internal approval process.
- In the consent negotiation, seek to (a) confirm that the consent is limited to the Sablecrest transaction and does not constitute a waiver of other defaults, and (b) if possible, negotiate a modest prepayment or restructuring of the note as consideration for the consent.
- Coordinate timing: the Board Consent (Resolution 3) authorizes the officers to "coordinate with the officers of the Parent and with counsel to the Parent regarding the timing of the closing of the Merger." Ensure the Dretusk consent is obtained **before** the merger closing date, not after.

### Issue 3: Assumed Liabilities Broader Than LOI – Pre-Closing Artist Guarantees and Settlements

**Where:** APA § 2.3(a); LOI § 3; APA § 4.7 (Schedule 4.7).

**Facts:** LOI § 3 stated that Buyer would assume "only obligations under Assigned Contracts **arising after the Closing**," and explicitly noted that Seller would retain "all liabilities under the Assigned Contracts arising before the Closing (such as artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements and refunds for events cancelled or rescheduled before the Closing)."

APA § 2.3(a), however, provides that Buyer assumes "all Liabilities arising under the Assigned Contracts, **whether arising before, on or after the Closing Date**." This is a material expansion from the LOI.

APA § 4.7 acknowledges the existence of pre-Closing exposures: "Schedule 4.7 lists all artist guarantees, unpaid settlements and outstanding deposit obligations under the Assigned Contracts as of the date hereof."

**Why it matters:** Vogert has assumed unlimited pre-Closing liabilities under the Assigned Contracts. Artist guarantees in the live music industry can be substantial (the APA's interim operating covenant in § 6.1 caps new guarantees at $250,000, suggesting individual guarantees can be that large or larger). Combined with unpaid venue settlements and pre-Closing refund obligations, this is an unquantified exposure that the LOI was designed to exclude.

**Recommended fix:**
- Review Schedule 4.7 to quantify the pre-Closing artist guarantees, unpaid settlements, and deposit obligations.
- If the amounts are material, negotiate an amendment to APA § 2.3(a) to conform to the LOI (limiting assumed liabilities to those arising after the Closing) or, alternatively, obtain a specific indemnity from Seller for pre-Closing liabilities under Assigned Contracts that is carved out from the Deductible and Indemnity Cap.
- If the deal has already closed (the Board Consent Recital A states "The Closing occurred on June 30, 2026"), this issue cannot be fixed by amendment. In that case, assess the exposure under Schedule 4.7 and evaluate whether any pre-Closing liabilities that have surfaced (or are likely to surface) can be characterized as **Excluded Liabilities** under APA § 2.4 (e.g., if they relate to Excluded Assets or pending litigation) or as breaches of Seller's representations under § 4.7, giving rise to an indemnification claim under § 9.2(a).

### Issue 4: Survival Period Drastically Shortened from LOI

**Where:** APA § 9.1; LOI § 6(b); Seller Note § 4(c).

**Facts:** LOI § 6(b) provided that "Seller's general representations and warranties will survive for **18 months** after Closing." APA § 9.1 provides that Seller's representations (other than Fundamental Reps and Tax reps) "shall survive the Closing until the **Set-off Expiration Date** (as defined in the Seller Note)." The Set-off Expiration Date is defined in Seller Note § 4(c) as "the **180th day** after the Issue Date." The Issue Date is June 30, 2026, making the Set-off Expiration Date approximately **December 27, 2026** – roughly **6 months** post-closing, not 18.

**Why it matters:** General representations (financial statements, material contracts, ticketing and deferred revenue, intellectual property and data, permits, compliance with laws, employees) survive for only ~6 months instead of the bargained-for 18 months. Many issues in a live music business (e.g., artist settlement disputes, permit renewals, data privacy claims) may not surface within 6 months. After December 27, 2026, Vogert has no indemnification remedy for breach of these representations (except to the extent a claim was "asserted in writing with reasonable specificity" before the deadline, per the last sentence of § 9.1).

**Recommended fix:**
- If the deal has not yet been finalized in all respects (the instruction states "before we finalize"), negotiate APA § 9.1 to restore the 18-month survival period from the LOI, decoupling it from the Set-off Expiration Date. The Set-off Expiration Date should limit the *set-off mechanism*, not the *underlying survival* of representations.
- If the deal has closed, ensure that all known or suspected representation breaches are asserted in writing with reasonable specificity **before December 27, 2026**, to preserve them under the last sentence of § 9.1.

### Issue 5: Seller Note Recourse Cap Overrides APA Carve-Outs

**Where:** Seller Note § 5; APA § 9.5(b); APA § 12.9.

**Facts:** APA § 9.5(b) provides that the Deductible and Indemnity Cap "shall **not** apply to Losses arising out of Fraud, any inaccuracy in or breach of a Fundamental Representation or the representations in Section 4.13 (Taxes), any Excluded Liability, or claims under Section 9.3(b)."

Seller Note § 5 provides: "Holder's aggregate liability for all Indemnifiable Losses, **whether recovered by set-off against this Note or otherwise**, shall in no event exceed the Indemnity Cap."

APA § 12.9 provides that in the event of a conflict between the APA and an Ancillary Agreement, the APA prevails, "**provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control as to payment, set-off and recourse**."

**Why it matters:** The phrase "whether recovered by set-off against this Note **or otherwise**" in Seller Note § 5 is broad enough to cap *all* recovery from Seller – including claims for Fraud, Fundamental Rep breaches, and Tax rep breaches – at the $2,400,000 Indemnity Cap. Combined with APA § 12.9's proviso that the Seller Note "shall control as to… recourse," a court could read these provisions together to eliminate the APA's carve-outs. This would mean that even in the case of actual fraud by Seller, Vogert's maximum recovery is $2.4M – a result that is almost certainly against public policy in Delaware and inconsistent with the parties' intent as expressed in the LOI.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend Seller Note § 5 to add a carve-out: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the Indemnity Cap shall not apply to Indemnifiable Losses arising out of Fraud, any inaccuracy in or breach of a Fundamental Representation, or the representations in Section 4.13 (Taxes) of the Purchase Agreement, in each case to the same extent as provided in Section 9.5(b) of the Purchase Agreement."
- Alternatively, amend APA § 12.9 to clarify that the Seller Note controls as to "payment, set-off and recourse" only with respect to the *mechanics* of set-off (timing, procedure, expiration) and does not override the substantive limitations (or lack thereof) in Article IX.

### Issue 6: Set-Off Expiration Date Limits Practical Remedy

**Where:** Seller Note § 4(c); APA § 9.7.

**Facts:** Seller Note § 4(c) limits Maker's right of set-off to claims "for which notice is delivered to Holder on or before the **180th day** after the Issue Date" (i.e., on or before ~December 27, 2026). APA § 9.7 confirms that set-off is exercised "in accordance with the terms of the Seller Note."

**Why it matters:** Even for claims that survive beyond 180 days (Fundamental Reps, Tax reps – which survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations), Vogert cannot use the set-off mechanism. It would need to pursue a separate indemnification action to recover. Given that the Seller Note is the primary credit instrument and the most efficient remedy, losing set-off for later-arising claims significantly weakens Vogert's practical enforcement position.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an extension of the Set-off Expiration Date to align with the survival periods in APA § 9.1. At minimum, the Set-off Expiration Date should run until the later of (a) 180 days after the Issue Date, or (b) the survival period for the applicable representation or covenant.
- If the deal has closed and this cannot be amended, ensure that all known claims are noticed in writing before December 27, 2026, to preserve set-off rights.

### Issue 7: Holder's Unrestricted Assignment Right

**Where:** Seller Note § 9.

**Facts:** "Holder may assign, transfer or pledge this Note or any interest in it to **any Person without the consent of Maker**."

**Why it matters:** Dretusk can sell or pledge the $4M note to any third party – a distressed debt fund, a competitor, or an unrelated entity – without Vogert's approval. The new holder would step into all of Dretusk's rights, including the right to declare an Event of Default (subject to the Change of Control consent issue in Issue 2), the information rights in § 6, and the collection remedies in § 7. This could complicate the commercial relationship and give an adverse third party leverage over Vogert's operations.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a limitation: Holder may not assign the Note to (a) a competitor of Maker's business, (b) any Person designated by Maker in writing (provided no more than [X] designations), or (c) any Person that is in default under its own obligations to Maker.
- At minimum, require that any assignee be a "financial institution" or "accredited investor" and that Maker receive notice of the assignment before it is effective (the current 10-Business-Day notice period in § 9 is after-the-fact and provides no opportunity to object).

### Issue 8: Mutual Confidentiality Agreement – Residual Obligations

**Where:** MCA § 9, § 6.2.

**Facts:** The MCA (effective January 12, 2026) continues in effect for **two years** after the Effective Date, i.e., until **January 12, 2028**. The employee non-solicitation in § 6.2 runs for **12 months** from the Effective Date, i.e., until **January 12, 2027**.

**Why it matters:** Vogert received substantial Confidential Information from Dretusk during due diligence (financials, artist contracts, ticketing data, festival plans). These obligations survive the closing. While not a deal issue per se, Vogert should ensure its personnel are reminded that Dretusk's pre-closing information remains confidential until January 2028 and that it cannot solicit Dretusk's former employees (with whom it first had contact through the transaction) until January 2027. This is particularly relevant if the Sablecrest merger integration team reviews Dretusk's data.

**Recommended fix:**
- Include a reminder in internal integration planning materials that MCA obligations persist.
- No amendment needed; this is an awareness item.
