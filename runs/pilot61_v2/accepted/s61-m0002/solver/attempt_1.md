## I. CRITICAL: Missed Objection Deadline on Closing Statement

**Where it appears:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 2.7(b)–(c); Buyer Board Consent, Recital C and Resolution 1.

**The problem.** The APA gives Buyer 30 days after delivery of the Closing Statement to deliver an Objection Notice (§ 2.7(b)). The Board Consent recites that Seller delivered the Closing Statement by email on **August 3, 2026** (Recital C). Thirty days from August 3 is **September 2, 2026**. The Board Consent itself is dated **September 8, 2026** and only then authorizes the Authorized Officers to prepare and deliver the Objection Notice (Resolution 1, second paragraph).

Under § 2.7(c): *"If Buyer does not deliver an Objection Notice before the expiration of the Review Period, the Closing Statement shall be final and binding on the parties."* Management estimates the Closing Statement understates Closing Deferred Revenue by approximately **$1,150,000** (Recital D). If the Objection Notice was not in fact delivered on or before September 2, the understatement is now locked in and Vogert has overpaid the Purchase Price by that amount with no contractual mechanism to recover it.

**Why it matters.** This is the single largest immediate financial exposure. The $1.15 million is money Vogert paid (or will be deemed to have paid) for deferred revenue that the Closing Statement attributes to Seller. Because the set-off right under Seller Note § 4(a) reaches only "Indemnifiable Losses" (defined to include amounts payable under APA § 2.7(e)), a final-and-binding Closing Statement eliminates the underlying claim entirely.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Confirm immediately** whether an Objection Notice was actually delivered to Seller on or before September 2, 2026, even if the Board authorization came later. Check the CFO's sent folder, email logs, and any courier records. The Board's ratification clause (Resolution 4, second paragraph) covers "actions previously taken by any manager or officer… to the extent consistent with them," which suggests someone may have acted before the formal consent.
2. **If no notice was timely delivered**, assess whether Seller's own conduct (e.g., continuing to provide access to working papers, engaging in informal discussions about the discrepancy) could support a waiver or estoppel argument under § 2.7(b) and general Delaware contract principles. This is a weaker position but worth preserving.
3. **If notice was timely delivered**, proceed with the Independent Accountant process under § 2.7(d) and ensure the set-off claim under Seller Note § 4 is noticed before the Set-off Expiration Date (see Issue II below).

## II. MAJOR: Survival Period for General Representations Is Six Months, Not Eighteen

**Where it appears:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 9.1; Seller Note § 4(c); Letter of Intent § 6(b).

**The problem.** The LOI states: *"Seller's general representations and warranties will survive for 18 months after Closing"* (LOI § 6(b)). Eighteen months from the June 30, 2026 Closing would be **December 30, 2027**.

The APA, however, provides: *"The representations and warranties of Seller in this Agreement, other than the Fundamental Representations, shall survive the Closing until the Set-off Expiration Date (as defined in the Seller Note)"* (APA § 9.1). The Seller Note defines the Set-off Expiration Date as *"the 180th day after the Issue Date"* (Seller Note § 4(c)). The Issue Date is June 30, 2026, making the Set-off Expiration Date approximately **December 27, 2026** — only six months after Closing.

**Why it matters.** Vogert's protection for breaches of general Seller representations (financial statements, material contracts, ticketing accuracy, IP/data, permits, compliance, employees) expires in roughly six months rather than the eighteen months contemplated in the LOI. Any claim not noticed by December 27, 2026 is time-barred. This also directly limits the window for exercising set-off against the Seller Note (Seller Note § 4(c): only claims noticed on or before the Set-off Expiration Date may be set off).

**Recommended fix.**

1. This is likely a drafting error in the APA that conflated the set-off expiration with the survival period. If the deal is still in a state where amendments are possible (e.g., as part of resolving the Closing Statement dispute or negotiating the Change of Control consent under Issue IV), seek an amendment to § 9.1 restoring the 18-month survival period from the LOI.
2. If the APA cannot be amended, **prioritize all potential claims and deliver written notice before December 27, 2026**. Under § 9.1, *"any claim asserted in writing with reasonable specificity before the end of the survival period shall survive until finally resolved."* This is the only saving grace.
3. Calendar a hard deadline of **December 15, 2026** (two weeks of buffer) for delivering all potential claim notices to Seller.

## III. MAJOR: Assumed Liabilities Are Broader Than the LOI Contemplated

**Where it appears:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 2.3(a); Letter of Intent § 3; APA § 4.7 and Schedule 4.7.

**The problem.** The LOI is explicit: *"Buyer will assume only obligations under Assigned Contracts arising after the Closing… Seller will retain all other liabilities of Seller and the Business, including all liabilities under the Assigned Contracts arising before the Closing (such as artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements and refunds for events cancelled or rescheduled before the Closing)"* (LOI § 3).

The APA departs materially: *"Buyer shall assume and agree to pay, perform and discharge when due only the following Liabilities of Seller… (a) all Liabilities arising under the Assigned Contracts, **whether arising before, on or after the Closing Date**"* (APA § 2.3(a), emphasis added).

**Why it matters.** Vogert has assumed pre-Closing artist guarantees, unpaid venue settlements, and refund obligations under Assigned Contracts that the LOI allocated to Seller. Schedule 4.7 to the APA lists *"all artist guarantees, unpaid settlements and outstanding deposit obligations under the Assigned Contracts as of the date hereof"* (APA § 4.7). These are now Vogert's problem. In a concert promotion business, artist guarantees can be substantial (the APA's conduct-of-business covenant caps new guarantees at $250,000 pre-Closing, suggesting existing ones may be of similar or greater magnitude).

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Pull Schedule 4.7** (not in the folder) and quantify the total pre-Closing artist guarantees, unpaid settlements, and deposit obligations now assumed by Vogert.
2. If the amount is material, raise this as a point in any negotiation with Seller (e.g., in connection with the Closing Statement dispute or the Change of Control consent request under Issue IV). Argue that the APA deviates from the agreed deal structure in the LOI and seek a credit or indemnity.
3. If the APA cannot be revisited, ensure these amounts are reflected in the business's reserves and that the 2027 Festival Budget (approved in Board Consent Resolution 2) accounts for them.
4. Confirm that the indemnification for Excluded Liabilities (APA § 9.2(c)) does not inadvertently cover these items — it does not, because they are now Assumed Liabilities, not Excluded Liabilities.

## IV. MAJOR: Parent Merger Triggers Change of Control Default on Seller Note

**Where it appears:** Seller Note § 7(a)(v), § 7(b); Buyer Board Consent, Recital F and Resolution 3.

**The problem.** Seller Note § 7(a)(v) provides that *"a Change of Control occurs without the prior written consent of Holder"* is an Event of Default. Section 7(b) defines Change of Control to include:

> *(i) any Person or group… acquiring… beneficial ownership of more than fifty percent of the voting equity of Maker or of any Person that directly or indirectly controls Maker.*

The Board Consent discloses that Vogert Entertainment Group, Inc. (Parent, sole member of Vogert Live Holdings / Maker) has signed a merger agreement with Sablecrest Capital Partners, expected to close in **Q1 2027** (Recital F). Upon closing, Sablecrest-controlled funds will own 100% of Parent, which directly controls Maker. This is squarely within § 7(b)(i).

**Why it matters.** Without Dretusk's prior written consent, the Sablecrest merger constitutes an Event of Default. Upon default, Dretusk may accelerate the entire $4,000,000 Note (plus accrued interest and 2% default interest) under § 7(c). The Note does not mature until June 30, 2028, so acceleration would force Vogert to pay $4 million roughly 15 months early. Additionally, the Board Consent (Resolution 3) authorizes Vogert to cooperate with the Parent merger but **does not address obtaining Dretusk's consent to the Change of Control**.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Request written consent from Dretusk (as Holder) to the Change of Control before the Sablecrest merger closes.** This should be a condition to the merger closing from Vogert's perspective.
2. Use the Closing Statement dispute (Issue I) and the survival-period issue (Issue II) as leverage in the consent negotiation. Dretusk needs Vogert's cooperation to resolve the post-closing adjustment; Vogert needs Dretusk's consent to avoid Note acceleration.
3. If Dretusk conditions its consent on a waiver of the survival-period reduction or a settlement of the Closing Statement dispute, evaluate the trade-off. A consent fee or partial prepayment of the Note (permitted without penalty under Seller Note § 10) may be a reasonable cost to avoid acceleration.
4. **Amend the Board Consent** to expressly require that the Authorized Officers obtain Holder's written consent under Seller Note § 7(a)(v) as a condition to the Merger closing, and to authorize negotiation of any consent fee.

## V. MODERATE: Seller Note Cap Conflicts with APA Carve-Outs for Excluded Liabilities

**Where it appears:** Seller Note § 5; APA § 9.5(b); APA § 12.9.

**The problem.** APA § 9.5(b) provides that the Deductible and Indemnity Cap *"shall not apply to Losses arising out of Fraud, any inaccuracy in or breach of a Fundamental Representation or the representations in Section 4.13 (Taxes), any Excluded Liability, or claims under Section 9.3(b)."*

Seller Note § 5 states: *"Holder's aggregate liability for all Indemnifiable Losses, whether recovered by set-off against this Note or otherwise, shall in no event exceed the Indemnity Cap."*

APA § 12.9 provides: *"In the event of any conflict between the terms of this Agreement and the terms of any Ancillary Agreement, the terms of this Agreement shall prevail; provided, however, that the Seller Note shall control as to payment, set-off and recourse."*

**Why it matters.** Because the Seller Note "controls as to… set-off and recourse," its $2,400,000 cap on set-off appears to override the APA's carve-out for Excluded Liabilities. If a significant Excluded Liability surfaces (e.g., a pre-Closing tax assessment under APA § 2.4(a), or a pending litigation liability under § 2.4(e)), Vogert's recovery via set-off against the Note would be capped at $2.4 million even though the APA says the cap does not apply to Excluded Liabilities. Any amount above $2.4 million would have to be pursued as a separate indemnification claim against Seller — but Seller is a selling corporation that may distribute its proceeds and become a shell, making collection difficult.

Additionally, the drafting in Seller Note § 5 ("Holder's aggregate liability") is imprecise. The Holder (Dretusk) does not have "liability" for Indemnifiable Losses; it is the Maker (Vogert) that has the right to set off. The provision should read something like: *"The aggregate amount that may be set off by Maker under Section 4 shall not exceed the Indemnity Cap."*

**Recommended fix.**

1. In any amendment or side letter negotiated with Dretusk (see Issues I–IV), clarify that Seller Note § 5 does not limit set-off for claims that are carved out from the Indemnity Cap under APA § 9.5(b) (i.e., Fraud, Fundamental Reps, Tax Reps, Excluded Liabilities).
2. If no amendment is achievable, ensure that any Excluded Liability claim is pursued as a direct indemnification action (not just set-off) within the survival period, and consider whether Dretusk will have sufficient assets to satisfy a judgment.

## VI. MODERATE: Board Consent Post-Dates Closing Deliverable Requirement

**Where it appears:** APA § 3.3(d); Buyer Board Consent (dated September 8, 2026); APA § 5.2.

**The problem.** APA § 3.3(d) required Buyer to deliver at Closing *"a certificate of an officer of Buyer certifying the resolutions of Buyer's Board of Managers approving this Agreement."* The Closing occurred June 30, 2026 (Board Consent, Recital A). The Board Consent is dated **September 8, 2026** — nearly three months after Closing. While the Consent includes a ratification clause (Resolution 4, ¶ 2), the original closing deliverable was either (a) based on a prior, undocumented board action, or (b) not actually delivered.

**Why it matters.** If no valid board approval existed at Closing, Seller could theoretically challenge the enforceability of Vogert's obligations under the APA by arguing the representation in § 5.2 (Authority) was inaccurate at Closing. In practice, the ratification in the September 8 Consent likely cures this under Delaware law, and Seller is unlikely to raise it given its own interests. However, it is a governance gap that could complicate the Change of Control consent negotiation (Issue IV) if Dretusk's counsel is aggressive.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Confirm whether an officer's certificate was actually delivered at Closing on June 30, 2026, and if so, what board action it certified. If a prior board meeting or consent existed but was not in the folder, file it.
2. If no prior approval existed, the September 8 ratification is the best available cure. Document the gap internally and ensure the ratification is referenced in any correspondence with Dretusk regarding the Change of Control consent.

## VIII. MINOR: Seller Note Information Rights Are Intrusive

**Where it appears:** Seller Note § 6(a)–(e).

**The problem.** The Note requires Vogert to deliver to Dretusk:
- Monthly ticket sales and artist settlement reports (within 20 days of month-end);
- Quarterly unaudited financials (within 45 days of quarter-end);
- Annual reviewed/audited financials (within 120 days of year-end);
- Prompt notice of any Event of Default or potential Event of Default (within 5 Business Days of an officer becoming aware).

These obligations were not mentioned in the LOI and are more extensive than typical for a $4 million seller note. The monthly operational reporting in particular creates an ongoing administrative burden and gives Dretusk visibility into Vogert's business that could be useful in the Change of Control negotiation (Issue IV) or in any post-closing dispute.

**Why it matters.** While not a legal defect, the information rights give Dretusk leverage and create compliance risk. A missed reporting deadline could be argued to be a covenant breach under § 7(a)(ii) (with a 30-day cure period), and the Event of Default notice obligation (§ 6(d)) could be triggered by the Sablecrest merger if an officer becomes aware of the Change of Control before consent is obtained.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Ensure the finance team has a calendar for all Seller Note reporting deadlines.
2. In the Change of Control consent negotiation, consider requesting that Dretusk waive the § 6(d) notice obligation with respect to the Sablecrest merger (i.e., agree that the merger itself will not constitute a "potential Event of Default" requiring notice).
3. If the Note is amended for any reason (Issues II, IV, or V), consider narrowing § 6(a) to quarterly reporting rather than monthly.

## IX. MINOR: Lighting and Rigging Agreement – Auto-Renewal and Merger Interaction

**Where it appears:** Lighting and Rigging Services Agreement §§ 10.1, 12.3.

**The problem.** The agreement runs through December 31, 2027 and **auto-renews** for successive one-year periods unless either party gives 60 days' non-renewal notice (§ 10.1). The Sablecrest merger is expected to close in Q1 2027. If Vogert (or its post-merger parent) wishes to exit the Lumenhoist relationship as part of integration, it must give notice by **November 1, 2027** (60 days before December 31, 2027) to prevent renewal into 2028.

Section 12.3 permits assignment to "a successor to all or substantially all of its business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice." A merger of Parent (not Vogert Live Holdings itself) likely does not constitute an assignment of the Lighting Agreement, but if post-merger integration involves restructuring Vogert into a different entity, the assignment provision would be triggered.

**Why it matters.** Low financial impact, but the auto-renewal could lock Vogert into a 90-day-notice termination regime (§ 10.2) for an additional year if not managed.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Calendar the **November 1, 2027** non-renewal notice deadline and decide in Q3 2027 whether to renew or terminate based on post-merger integration plans.
2. If a restructuring of Vogert Live Holdings is contemplated post-merger, provide written notice to Lumenhoist under § 12.3 to effect a permitted assignment.
