## 1. Bottom line

1. **The deal is a problem under these documents as written.** Any Corvane acquisition of Harrowgate is a "Change of Control" (MSA §1.1(c)). It is deemed an assignment needing Tessaline's prior written consent, with no reasonableness standard (MSA §15.3, §15.2). Without consent, Tessaline can terminate on 30 days' notice and accelerate the unpaid minimum purchase commitment for the rest of the current term (MSA §15.3). That clause appears to give Tessaline substantial leverage. We should fix it before or at signing, not after.
2. **The evergreen renewal makes the exposure larger.** The OF auto-renews for successive five-year terms. Non-renewal can be given only in a 30-day window (OF §2.2). If a Change of Control occurs in a Renewal Term, the acceleration could reach $12.0M (5 × $2.4M). Within the Initial Term, it is still a multiple of the OF's own 20% shortfall formula.
3. **Pre-signing diligence may already be in breach.** MSA §13.1 does not permit disclosure of Tessaline's pricing and Specifications to a prospective acquirer, and breach of §13 is uncapped (MSA §11.3(b)). This needs action now (Issue 3).
4. **Risk allocation favors Tessaline in ways Corvane's diligence will find.** Customer's indemnity is uncapped (MSA §11.4). Supplier's liability for defective film is capped at about 12 months of fees (MSA §11.2). Recall costs are allocated nowhere (QA §8.2 points to the MSA, which is silent).

## 4. Issues, ranked by significance

### Issue 1 (Critical): Change of Control deemed assignment, termination right and acceleration

**Where:** MSA §1.1(c), §15.2, §15.3, §15.4; OF §2.1, §2.2, §3.3, §3.2; MSA §14.3(a), §1.3.

**What the documents say**
- *Trigger.* Acquisition of more than 50% of voting equity "directly or indirectly," any merger after which the holders hold less than 50%, or an asset sale (MSA §1.1(c)). Any Corvane acquisition qualifies.
- *Consent.* The Change of Control is "deemed an assignment" needing Tessaline's "prior written consent," even in a merger where Harrowgate survives (MSA §15.3). There is no "not unreasonably withheld" standard and no deemed-consent mechanism.
- *Remedy.* Without consent, Tessaline may terminate on 30 days' notice at any time within 180 days after it "learns" of the Change of Control. The unpaid balance of the minimum annual purchase commitment for the remainder of the then-current term then becomes "immediately due and payable" (MSA §15.3).

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.*
  - **Supply cutoff.** A 30-day runway is far too short to qualify a replacement supplier of food-contact film. Compare the 90-day change-notice and 60-day approval cycle Harrowgate itself must run for even a resin change (MSA §6.3; QA §7.1–7.2).
  - **Windfall.** Tessaline is paid the accelerated minimum while released from supplying. No mitigation or credit applies. Harrowgate would also still owe finished goods and raw materials under MSA §14.3(a) and OF §5.3.
  - **Illustrative size.** The minimum is $2.4M per Contract Year (OF §3.1). If closing were around year-end 2026, about 26 months would remain in the Initial Term. That implies roughly $5M or more, before any netting for purchases already made ("unpaid balance" is undefined). In a Renewal Term, "then-current term" is five years, so up to $12.0M.
  - **Conflict with the OF.** The OF caps remedy for a purchase shortfall at 20% of the difference, and says that figure is "a reasonable estimate of Supplier's lost margin and is not a penalty" (OF §3.3). Acceleration at 100% is five times that. The OF outranks the MSA (MSA §1.3), so we have a credible argument that OF §3.3 governs Supplier's recovery for lost minimums. Tessaline will respond that §15.3 is a specific, separate trigger. I would not rely on the argument.
  - **Possible further defenses.** The acceleration is arguably not an "amount due for Products under Section 4," so it might fall under the cap in MSA §11.2 (§11.3(c) exempts only Product payments). It may also be excluded as lost profits (MSA §11.1). Both are arguable, not safe.
  - **Enforceability.** Whether the acceleration is an unenforceable penalty under Delaware law needs research. The folder does not address it.
- *As target.*
  - **Contingent liability and lost supply.** Corvane will treat this as a contingent liability of up to several million dollars and a supply-continuity risk. Expect a consent condition, a price adjustment or a special indemnity request.
  - **Leverage.** Tessaline can use the consent request to reprice or extract other concessions.
  - **Breadth.** "Indirectly" and "ultimate parent" in §1.1(c) could also capture later upstream changes at the sponsor level, including Corvane's own exit.

**Recommendation (negotiation ladder)**
1. **Preferred: amend §15.3 and §1.1(c) now.** Proposed language:
   > "15.3 *Change of Control of Customer.* Customer shall notify Supplier in writing within thirty (30) days after the closing of a Change of Control of Customer. A Change of Control of Customer is not an assignment of this Agreement and does not require Supplier's consent or give rise to any right of termination, acceleration or payment."
   >
   > Add to §1.1(c): "Change of Control excludes (x) any internal reorganization or transfer among Affiliates, (y) any public offering, and (z) any change in ownership of a direct or indirect holder of equity in Customer that does not result in a person other than Corvane or its Affiliates holding more than 50% of Customer's voting equity."
2. **Fallback:** a narrow termination right only if the acquirer is a named direct competitor of Tessaline or a sanctioned person. It would run on at least 12 months' notice, with no acceleration. Customer would owe only Shortfall Payments under OF §3.3, pro-rated to the termination date, plus MSA §14.3(a) amounts.
3. **Second fallback:** keep consent but add "not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed" and deemed consent after 15 business days. Delete the acceleration sentence and require at least 12 months' notice of termination. Conform OF §2.1's cross-reference.
4. **Minimum acceptable:** a signed transaction-specific consent and waiver. It should cover termination and acceleration, name the structure, and extend to post-closing integration steps we know of.
5. **Pair the ask with an estoppel** confirming the agreement is in force, no defaults, and the minimum-commitment status. Corvane will want this in any event.
6. **Possible trade items,** if the client agrees: portfolio volume potential, extended fixed pricing, or a modest volume bump. Do not offer a longer term without fixing Issue 2.

### Issue 2 (Critical): Five-year evergreen renewal with a 30-day non-renewal window

**Where:** OF §2.1, §2.2; MSA §2.1, §16.1; OF §3.1.

**What the documents say.** The Initial Term is three years. After that, the OF "shall automatically renew for successive five (5) year" terms. Non-renewal notice must be delivered under MSA §16 "no earlier than sixty (60) days and no later than thirty (30) days before the end" of the term. Any other notice "is of no effect." The $2.4M minimum and the pricing mechanism continue in each Renewal Term (OF §2.2). The MSA's term is whatever the OF says (MSA §2.1). Neither document gives Harrowgate a termination-for-convenience right. MSA §14 allows termination only for breach or insolvency.

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.*
  - A missed 30-day window locks in five more years of minimums at resin-indexed pricing.
  - The notice formalities are strict: hand, courier, or email plus a hard copy to the Chief Legal Officer in Lancaster (MSA §16.1). A post-closing change in legal personnel raises the risk of a miss.
  - The five-year renewal length also drives the acceleration amount in Issue 1.
- *As target.*
  - A buyer will see a long, inflexible, take-or-pay-style commitment. It limits Corvane's ability to consolidate or re-source packaging across the portfolio.
  - The minimum is modest ($2.4M per year), but the lock-in is the issue.

**Recommendation**
> "2.2 *Renewal.* At the end of the Initial Term, this Agreement shall renew for successive one (1) year Renewal Terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term. Supplier shall give Customer written notice of the upcoming renewal not more than 180 nor fewer than 120 days before the end of the term. If Supplier fails to do so, the then-current term shall continue until 90 days after Customer receives that notice."

Also seek a Customer termination right on 180 days' notice, or at minimum on a Change of Control or a Supplier supply-performance failure. Whatever happens, calendar the window (Action E).

### Issue 3 (High; immediate): Confidentiality and pre-closing diligence disclosure

**Where:** MSA §13.1, §13.4, §1.1(a), §11.3(b), §11.1–11.2; OF §4.1.

**What the documents say.** "Confidential Information" includes "pricing, Specifications, formulations and forecasts." The receiving party may disclose it only to "its and its Affiliates' employees, advisors and financing sources who need to know it and are bound by" equivalent confidentiality obligations (MSA §13.1). "Affiliate" requires control (MSA §1.1(a)). Breach of §13 sits outside both the consequential-damages exclusion and the cap (MSA §11.3(b)).

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.* Corvane is not yet an Affiliate. Posting the OF price table (OF §4.1), the QA/Specification Sheets or other Tessaline data to a data room may breach §13, with uncapped exposure and a possible §14.1 breach-termination argument. One counterargument: contract terms are mutually negotiated and arguably not "disclosed by the other Party." That argument is weak for Tessaline's specifications and formulations.
- *As target.* This creates a diligence-process risk and a pretext for Tessaline in any renegotiation. It also bears on any disclosure-schedule rep.

**Recommendation**
- Immediately audit the data room and any disclosures already made.
- Withhold or redact pricing and technical content, or use a clean-team arrangement, until Tessaline consents.
- Build a confidentiality waiver for the transaction into the Tessaline approach (Issue 1).
- For the long term, add to §13.1: "and, in connection with a bona fide financing or Change of Control transaction, prospective acquirers and investors and their advisors who are bound by written confidentiality obligations."

### Issue 4 (High): Asymmetric liability cap, uncapped Customer indemnity, and a recall-cost gap

**Where:** MSA §11.1, §11.2, §11.3, §11.4, §10.1, §10.2, §1.1(f), §7.4, §12.1; QA §5.4, §8.2.

**What the documents say**
- **Supplier's side.** Each party's liability is capped at "the fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months" (MSA §11.2). The cap applies to Supplier's §10.1 indemnity for product defects and its warranty liability.
- **Customer's side.** The Customer's §10.2 indemnity, which covers Artwork and labeling content, Filled Product liability including recalls, and Customer negligence, is "not … subject to any limitation of liability or exclusion of damages" (MSA §11.4).
- **Recall costs.** "Losses" means losses "arising from a Third-Party Claim" (MSA §1.1(f)), so the indemnities do not cover Harrowgate's own first-party costs. QA §8.2 says "Responsibility for the costs of a recall is determined under the Master Supply Agreement." The MSA has no recall-cost allocation. It mentions only a Customer indemnity for "recall claim[s]" (§10.2(c)) and a $2M recall insurance requirement (§12.1(b)).
- **Remedies for defective film.** Remedies are replace or credit under QA §5.4, plus MSA §10.1 indemnity. Consequential damages and lost profits are excluded (MSA §11.1), which would plausibly sweep in downtime and spoiled Filled Product.
- **Drafting anomaly.** The §11.2 cap wording "the fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months" is in lower case in the middle of capitalized text. "Fees" is undefined; the MSA elsewhere uses "prices." Early in the term "paid" is small. We should compare against the signed original.

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.* A contaminated or non-conforming film lot could produce a food-safety recall costing far more than a roughly $2.4M (or lower) cap. Harrowgate would bear the excess, while its own indemnity to Tessaline for Filled Product claims is uncapped. The §10.2(c) carve-out applies only where Product fails the "Specifications," not other warranty failures such as workmanship defects (§7.1(b)) or noncompliance with law.
- *As target.* This is the sort of uncapped, one-way exposure a buyer will price or ask the seller to backstop. The $2M recall insurance minimum (§12.1(b)) also appears small against that exposure.

**Recommendation**
- Replace §11.2 with: "…SHALL NOT EXCEED THE GREATER OF (A) [2×] THE AMOUNTS PAID AND PAYABLE FOR PRODUCTS IN THE PRIOR 12 MONTHS AND (B) $[10,000,000]."
- Add a mutual super-cap for each party's §10 indemnity obligations. At minimum, make §11.4 mutual by also exempting Supplier's §10.1 obligations. Fallback: cap §10.2 at the same super-cap.
- Add a new §10.5, "Recall and Product Failure Costs":
  > "To the extent caused by a Product's failure to conform to the Specifications, the warranties in Section 7.1 or applicable law, Supplier shall reimburse Customer's reasonable documented costs of any recall, market withdrawal or stock recovery, and of destroyed or unusable Filled Product, which costs are direct damages not excluded by Section 11.1."
- Amend §10.2(c) to read "…except to the extent caused by a Product's failure to conform to the Specifications, the warranties in Section 7.1 or applicable law, or by Supplier's negligence."
- Fix QA §8.2 to cross-refer to the new §10.5. Insist that the insurance limits in §12.1 be revisited to match.

### Issue 5 (High): Customer anti-assignment, including "by operation of law," with no affiliate or financing carve-out

**Where:** MSA §15.2, §15.4, §13.1, §1.1(a).

**What the documents say.** Customer may not assign or transfer "whether voluntarily, by operation of law or otherwise" without Supplier's prior written consent. A violating transfer is "void" (§15.4). There is no affiliate, successor or collateral-assignment exception.

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.* Post-closing restructurings (merger of Harrowgate into another portfolio company, a transfer to a newly formed holding entity or an add-on) would need Tessaline's consent. A purported transfer without it is void.
- *As target.* If Corvane's lenders take a security interest in Harrowgate's contract rights, §15.2 bars a collateral assignment on its face. A future Corvane exit is also caught by §15.3. Whether any statute overrides these restrictions is outside this folder and needs research.

**Recommendation.** Revise §15.2:
> "…without Supplier's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed, except that Customer may, without consent but on written notice, (a) assign to an Affiliate or to a successor to all or substantially all of the business to which this Agreement relates, and (b) collaterally assign to its financing sources."

Delete "by operation of law." Seek the lender-friendly language in the same amendment as Issue 1.

### Issue 6 (Medium-High): Supplier may assign and delegate freely

**Where:** MSA §15.1; compare §6.3, §6.4; QA §7.1, §4.1; MSA §1.1(c) (applies to "any Party" but §15.3 binds only Customer).

**What the documents say.** Supplier may assign "in whole or in part, without the consent of Customer, to any person," and delegate to "any Affiliate or subcontractor," with notice only within 30 days after the fact.

**Why it matters**
- *As customer.* Tessaline could assign to a Harrowgate competitor or to a weaker or unqualified manufacturer, or subcontract production. That sits uneasily with the change-control regime requiring 90 days' notice and Customer's written approval for a site, line or process change (MSA §6.3; QA §7.1(b)). The MSA does not say which provision prevails. Tessaline's own Change of Control is unrestricted, so a new owner could take over without notice or approval.
- *As target.* Supply-continuity and counterparty-quality risk, especially if the film is sole-sourced.

**Recommendation.** Make §15 mutual:
> "Supplier may not assign or delegate this Agreement without Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, except to a successor to substantially all of its relevant business that assumes this Agreement in writing and is not a direct competitor of Customer. Any delegation or subcontracting is subject to Section 6.3 and Quality Agreement Section 7.1, and Supplier remains fully liable."

Add a Tessaline Change-of-Control notice and, ideally, a Harrowgate termination right if Tessaline is acquired by a competitor. Rely on §1.1(c) already defining Change of Control for "any Party."

### Issue 8 (Medium): Minimum-commitment accounting interacts badly with rejections and credits

**Where:** OF §3.1, §3.2(c), §3.3; QA §5.4, §6.2.

**What the documents say.** The commitment is measured on invoiced value "net of … credits for rejected Products" (OF §3.1). OF §3.2(c) adds back rejected Products only if they are "not replaced by Supplier within the time required by the Quality Agreement." Under QA §5.4, Customer elects either replacement (within 10 business days) or credit or refund.

**Why it matters.** If Harrowgate elects a credit or refund for a bad lot, the credit reduces counted purchases. Because no replacement was "required," §3.2(c) may not restore the amount. That leaves Harrowgate exposed to a Shortfall Payment of 20% of the gap (OF §3.3) for Tessaline's own quality failure. The exposure is small per year (a maximum of $480,000 if no purchases at all), but it is easily avoided.

**Recommendation.** Amend OF §3.1 and §3.2(c):
> "…Products rejected or the subject of a warranty claim, whether replaced, credited or refunded, shall count toward the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment at their invoiced value."

### Issue 10 (Medium-Low): Inspection and warranty timing keyed to "delivery"

**Where:** QA §5.1, §5.2, §6.1, §6.2; MSA §5.1, §5.3; OF §5.4.

**What the documents say.** Delivery is FCA Supplier's facility to Customer's carrier, with title and risk passing then (MSA §5.1, §5.3; OF §5.4). The 10-business-day rejection window and the 18-month warranty both run from "delivery" (QA §6.2, §5.1). Incoming inspection occurs "upon receipt" (QA §6.1). Warranty claims are due within 15 business days of discovery (QA §5.2).

**Why it matters.** Under the MSA's meaning, "delivery" occurs when the carrier collects the goods. Transit time can use up part of the rejection window and warranty period. Deemed acceptance is limited to incoming inspection, and latent defects are preserved (QA §6.2), so this is a limited exposure. It is still a needless ambiguity.

**Recommendation.** Revise QA §6.2 and §5.1 to run from "receipt at Customer's (or its designee's) facility." Confirm in the same amendment that late warranty-claim notice does not forfeit the claim except to the extent of actual prejudice.

### Issue 11 (Medium-Low): Pricing mechanics and the MFN

**Where:** OF §1.2(d), §4.3, §4.4; MSA §4.1.

**What the documents say**
- Prices are fixed for the first Contract Year, which ends about Feb. 28, 2027. After that, either party may request an adjustment once per quarter if the Resin Index moves more than 5% (OF §4.3).
- The adjustment is the index change × a flat 55% resin share for every Product (OF §4.3).
- The index is the low-density polyethylene film-grade price from a service "the Parties designate in writing" (OF §1.2(d)). Nothing in the folder shows a designation.
- The MFN requires Supplier to "promptly offer" a lower price if it sells a "substantially similar product" to another customer "in comparable volumes and on comparable terms" at a lower price (OF §4.4).

**Why it matters.** A single LDPE index and uniform 55% resin share may not track the cost of the EVOH barrier film, PET/PE laminate or polyolefin shrink Products (OF §4.1). If no index was designated, the mechanism may be unworkable and invite disputes in 2027. The MFN has no verification, audit or retroactive credit, and "comparable terms" could be read to include Harrowgate's long, minimum-volume commitment. A buyer will care about price predictability.

**Recommendation.** Confirm or document the index designation now. Consider product-specific resin shares and indices. Add to §4.4 an annual officer's certificate, an independent-auditor verification right, and a credit retroactive to the date the lower price began. Any pricing renegotiation can ride the Issue 1 amendment.

### Issue 12 (Lower): Other gaps to note

- **No Customer remedy for chronic supply failure.** OF §6.2 requires on-time-in-full reporting but sets no standard or consequence. MSA §14.1 allows termination only for material breach after a 30-day cure period, and §5.2 gives only per-order remedies. Add an OTIF threshold with a cure plan and a termination right.
- **Force majeure.** Supplier's force majeure excuses delivery. Customer may cancel affected orders only after 60 days (MSA §17.3). Consider shortening this and adding an allocation obligation tied to OF §3.4's capacity reservation.
- **Single OF governs the term.** MSA §2.1 and §1.1(g) ("Order Form" includes later forms) leave unclear which OF sets the term if others are added. Add a clarifying sentence.
- **Audit frequency.** The MSA allows one audit per year absent cause (MSA §6.4). Corvane may want broader rights, or a cause-based audit following the deal.
