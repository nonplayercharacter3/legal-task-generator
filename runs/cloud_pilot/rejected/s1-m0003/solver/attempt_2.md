## 1. Bottom line

I reviewed the Master Supply Agreement ("MSA"), Order Form No. 1 ("OF") and Quality Agreement ("QA"), each dated March 1, 2026, together. All three are Delaware-law documents (MSA §17.1).

**The Corvane acquisition will, on the face of the documents, hand Tessaline a termination right and a large payment claim against Harrowgate.** MSA §15.3 treats any Change of Control of Harrowgate as an assignment requiring Tessaline's prior written consent. Without consent, Tessaline can terminate on 30 days' notice at any time within 180 days after it learns of the change. It can also accelerate the unpaid minimum purchase commitment for the rest of the then-current term. Order Form §2.2 can make that term five years. No deal structure avoids the clause, because §15.3 expressly reaches a merger in which Harrowgate survives.

Other significant problems:

- A five-year auto-renewal with a 30-day opt-out window.
- A one-sided liability structure: Harrowgate's indemnity is uncapped, Tessaline's is capped, and there is a recall-cost gap.
- A confidentiality clause that may already have been breached by data-room disclosure.
- Free assignment rights for Tessaline.

I recommend that Tessaline's consent and waiver be dealt with as a signing/closing workstream, and that we use that approach to Tessaline to fix the second and third problems at the same time.

### Issue 1 (Critical, immediate): Change of Control triggers consent, termination and acceleration

**Where it appears**
- MSA §1.1(c) defines "Change of Control" as any acquisition of more than 50% of the voting equity, any merger after which the old holders hold less than 50% (whether or not the Party survives), or a sale of substantially all assets.
- MSA §15.2 bars assignment "by operation of law or otherwise" without Supplier's prior written consent. It states no reasonableness standard.
- MSA §15.3 says:
  - Any Change of Control of Customer, "including by merger in which Customer is the surviving entity," is deemed an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent.
  - Without consent, Supplier may terminate "at any time within 180 days after it learns" of the change, on 30 days' notice.
  - On that termination, "the unpaid balance of the minimum annual purchase commitment under the Order Form for the remainder of the then-current term shall become immediately due and payable."
- MSA §15.4 voids transfers made in violation of §15.
- OF §2.1 preserves termination under §15.3 during the Initial Term.

**Why it matters to Harrowgate as customer**
- Corvane's purchase of more than 50% of the voting equity falls squarely within §1.1(c)(i). A reverse triangular structure does not help, because §15.3 names the surviving-entity merger.
- The trigger is "prior" consent. After closing, the only fix is a waiver, and Tessaline will know it has leverage.
- The termination notice period is only 30 days. Lead times are 15–30 business days (OF §5.1). Replacing food-contact film would require supplier qualification, specification work and QA §7 change-control-type validation. A 30-day cutoff is operationally severe. Accepted POs would survive under MSA §2.2, but no new orders would follow.
- **Exposure.**
  - The OF §3.1 minimum is $2.4M per Contract Year. Over the three-year Initial Term, that is $7.2M. If the change occurs in a five-year Renewal Term, it is up to $12M (OF §2.2).
  - The acceleration clause is ambiguous on whether purchases already made in the current term are credited, and on whether it covers the full remaining term or only the current Contract Year. Tessaline will argue the former. My figures are approximate and need confirming against actual purchases.
  - MSA §14.3(a) also makes Harrowgate pay for finished goods and custom raw materials on termination (limited by OF §5.3). The consequence is that Harrowgate could pay the unused minimums and lose the supply.
- **Conflict with the Order Form.** OF §3.3 limits the shortfall remedy to 20% of the shortfall. It also recites that this is "a reasonable estimate of Supplier's lost margin and is not a penalty." Under MSA §1.3, the Order Form controls over the MSA. We can argue that the 20% rule governs and §15.3's 100% acceleration is inconsistent with it. We can also argue that 100% acceleration alongside termination is an unenforceable penalty, because the parties themselves valued the loss at 20%. I have not researched Delaware authority on this, and we should not rely on that argument in negotiation.

**Why it matters as an acquisition target**
- This is a consent and termination right in a supply contract. It belongs on the disclosure schedules, and any rep that no consents are required needs a carve-out.
- Corvane will see a contingent liability of millions of dollars and a supply risk. It may ask for a closing condition or a purchase price adjustment, so the client's leverage is best used early.
- The 180-day tail after closing leaves the risk open post-closing. Tessaline could use the window to extract price or term concessions.
- If acquisition financing includes a collateral assignment of material contracts, §15.2 would also restrict it. We should check this with the finance team.

**Recommended fix**

*Preferred: a pre-closing amendment, or a consent and waiver.* Replace §15.3 with:

> "15.3 Change of Control of Customer. A Change of Control of Customer, and any merger, equity transfer or financing of Customer or its direct or indirect parent, shall not constitute an assignment of this Agreement, shall not require the consent of Supplier, and shall not give rise to any right of termination, acceleration or other remedy. Customer shall notify Supplier within thirty (30) days after a Change of Control."

*Consent-and-waiver letter, if Tessaline will not amend.* The letter should:
1. Consent to the Corvane transaction under MSA §§15.2–15.3.
2. Irrevocably waive all termination and acceleration rights arising from it.
3. Confirm the agreement is in full force, with no defaults.
4. Include an estoppel confirming purchases to date, the Contract Year status, the current prices, the Resin Index designation and the Schedule A revisions.

*Fallbacks if Tessaline insists on keeping some right.*
- Limit termination to an acquirer that is a Tessaline competitor or a sanctioned person.
- Lengthen the termination notice to 180 days.
- Replace acceleration with the OF §3.3 Shortfall Payment for the then-current Contract Year only, or delete it.
- Add a reasonableness standard to consent: "not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed, and deemed given if Supplier does not object within 15 business days."
- Reduce the 180-day window to 60 days from written notice of closing, and say "learns" means receipt of that notice.

*Negotiating position.*
- Tessaline gains nothing from a termination except to extract a concession, and the contract is worth a minimum of $2.4M per year to it.
- We can offer a prompt notice of the deal, introductions to Corvane, and confirmation that quality contacts and ordering continue unchanged.
- Any trade involving a longer term needs Corvane's agreement and should come with the Issue 2 fix. A longer term is not free to Harrowgate.
- Timing and sequencing should be agreed with Corvane first, subject to the NDA question (Question 2).

### Issue 2 (High): Five-year automatic renewal with a 30-day opt-out window

**Where it appears**
- OF §2.1: three-year Initial Term from March 1, 2026, so it ends on or about February 28 / March 1, 2029.
- OF §2.2: automatic renewal for successive **five-year** Renewal Terms. Non-renewal notice is effective only if delivered under MSA §16 "no earlier than sixty (60) days and no later than thirty (30) days before the end" of the term. A notice outside that window "is of no effect." The $2.4M minimum and the pricing mechanism continue during renewal.
- MSA §16.1 requires hand delivery, overnight courier, or email plus one of the others. Notice goes to Tessaline's General Counsel in Wilmington.
- MSA §14 has no termination for convenience.

**Why it matters**
- The notice window is about December 30, 2028 to January 29, 2029 (or December 31 to January 30, if the term ends March 1). A missed or defective notice locks Harrowgate in for five years. That is up to $12M of minimums, with no exit except breach or insolvency (MSA §14.1, §14.2).
- Because the same "then-current term" feeds the Issue 1 acceleration formula, a renewal multiplies the exposure.
- A buyer will see a long-dated, auto-renewing minimum commitment and will want it flexible. Corvane may also have views on consolidating packaging purchasing.
- Tessaline can also decline to renew with the same 30-day window, which is bad for supply continuity.

**Recommended fix**
Replace OF §2.2:

> "At the end of the Initial Term, this Agreement shall renew for successive one (1) year Renewal Terms unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least one hundred eighty (180) days before the end of the then-current term. Supplier shall send Customer a written reminder not more than two hundred seventy (270) days and not less than two hundred ten (210) days before the end of each term; if Supplier fails to do so, the notice deadline is extended to thirty (30) days after the date Supplier's reminder is delivered. The Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment during a Renewal Term shall be [seventy-five percent (75%)] of the commitment in the prior Contract Year, and Customer may reduce it further on ninety (90) days' notice if [...]."

*Fallback:* keep the five-year renewal but widen the window to 180–90 days and add the reminder mechanism. *Alternative:* add a Customer termination-for-convenience right on 180 days' notice with a fee equal to the 20% Shortfall Payment on the remaining current-year commitment.

*Tactics.* Tessaline will value a longer lock. If we trade a firm Initial Term for a clean consent, the renewal provision is where we should protect the buyer. Either way, calendar the window now: **December 2028 – January 2029**.

### Issue 3 (High): One-sided liability allocation and the recall-cost gap

**Where it appears**
- MSA §10.1: Supplier indemnifies Customer for Third-Party Claims "to the extent arising from" Product defects or non-conformity, IP infringement, or Supplier's negligence.
- MSA §10.2(c): Customer indemnifies Supplier for third-party claims arising from "the contents of any Filled Product, including any product liability, personal injury, illness or recall claim relating to a Filled Product, except to the extent caused by a Product's failure to conform to the Specifications."
- MSA §11.4 makes Customer's §10.2 obligations "not … subject to any limitation of liability or exclusion of damages."
- MSA §11.1 excludes consequential damages and lost profits, and §11.2 caps each Party's liability at "the fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months … measured from the date of the event." Neither carve-out in §11.3 covers Supplier's §10.1 indemnity.
- MSA §1.1(f) defines "Losses" only as arising from a Third-Party Claim.
- MSA §7.4 and QA §5.4: Customer's warranty remedies are "set out in the Quality Agreement" (replace, or credit or refund the price, with return freight and disposal), "together with" §10.1.
- QA §8.2: Customer decides on any recall, and "[r]esponsibility for the costs of a recall is determined under the Master Supply Agreement."
- MSA §12.1: each Party carries $5M CGL and only $2M product recall insurance.

**Why it matters to Harrowgate as customer**
- A film contamination or migration event is the largest risk in this relationship.
  - Harrowgate's indemnity to Tessaline is uncapped.
  - Tessaline's liability to Harrowgate, including its §10.1 indemnity, is capped at roughly the prior 12 months' fees. That is about $2.4M or less, and lower still early in the term because the base is "fees paid or payable" measured at the event date.
  - Consequential damages and lost profits are excluded.
- **The recall-cost gap.** QA §8.2 sends recall-cost responsibility to the MSA, but the MSA contains no recall-cost clause. Harrowgate's own recall costs, including product destruction, retrieval, notifications, lost inventory and line downtime, are not "Losses" (third-party claims only). They would be ordinary damages claims subject to the §11.1 exclusions and the §11.2 cap. MSA §7.4 can also be read to limit Harrowgate to the QA's replace-or-refund remedies for the warranty breach, which cover only the price of the film.
- **Overlap.** §10.2(c) excepts only non-conformity to Specifications. §10.1(a) covers any "defect" and non-compliance with law. A defect that does not breach a Specification (for example, an unspecified migrant, or a latent defect) falls into both indemnities. Harrowgate's uncapped indemnity then competes with Tessaline's capped one.
- The $2M recall coverage in §12.1(b) is small against the exposure.

**Why it matters as a target**
- Corvane will see an uncapped, one-way indemnity in a food-contact supply contract, and a product-recall gap, and may ask for price protection or insurance. R&W underwriters may exclude it.
- Any fix needs Tessaline's agreement, so it belongs in the same conversation as Issue 1.

**Recommended fix**
1. Narrow §10.2(c):

   > "…except to the extent arising from (i) any defect in, or failure of, a Product to conform to the Specifications, the warranties in Section 7.1 or applicable law, (ii) Supplier's breach of this Agreement or the Quality Agreement, or (iii) the negligence or willful misconduct of Supplier or its personnel."

2. Replace §11.4 with a mutual carve-out: "Sections 11.1 and 11.2 do not apply to either Party's obligations under Section 10." Fallback is a super-cap on each side's indemnity of the greater of $[10]M and the insurance required under §12.1.
3. Add an express recall-cost clause (new MSA §10.5):

   > "To the extent a recall, market withdrawal or stock recovery of Filled Product is caused by Nonconforming Product or Supplier's breach, Supplier shall reimburse Customer's reasonable costs of retrieval, notification, destruction, replacement product, testing, and regulatory compliance. Such amounts are direct damages and are not excluded by Section 11.1."

4. Amend §7.4 so that QA §5.4 remedies are "cumulative with, and not in lieu of, all other remedies at law, in equity or under this Agreement."
5. Fix the cap base. Use "the greater of (a) the fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event and (b) the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment." This also corrects the formatting (lowercase text in an otherwise all-caps clause), which suggests the clause was patched.
6. Raise the recall coverage in §12.1(b) to match the new allocation, and add Harrowgate as additional insured and a waiver of subrogation.

*Negotiating position.* A mutual cap structure is market-standard and defensible. If Tessaline resists, we can trade a modest increase in the Customer's recall insurance obligations for the recall-cost clause.

### Issue 4 (High, immediate): Data-room disclosure may breach the confidentiality clause

**Where it appears**
- MSA §13.1: Confidential Information (expressly "pricing, Specifications, formulations and forecasts") may be disclosed only to the recipient's and "its Affiliates'" employees, advisors and financing sources who need to know it. It may be used only to perform the Agreement.
- MSA §13.4: obligations run for the Term plus five years.
- MSA §11.3(b): a breach of §13 is excluded from the cap and the consequential-damages waiver.

**Why it matters**
- A prospective acquirer is not an "Affiliate" until closing, and Corvane is not Harrowgate's "advisor" or "financing source." Putting the Order Form pricing, Specifications or Tessaline-originated forecasts in a data room accessible to Corvane risks a §13 breach. The damages would be uncapped, and the breach could be material under MSA §14.1.
- Harrowgate's own advisors, including this firm, are within the permitted class.
- After closing, sharing Tessaline's information with Corvane and portfolio companies (then Affiliates) is permitted, but use is still limited to performing the Agreement. Benchmarking Tessaline's pricing across the portfolio is not clearly permitted.

**Recommended fix**
- **Now:** find out what has been posted and who has access. Restrict or redact Tessaline pricing and specification content, or move it to a clean-team folder limited to named individuals under NDA, until consent is obtained.
- Fold a limited waiver into the Issue 1 consent request: Tessaline consents to disclosure of this agreement to Corvane and its advisors and financing sources under customary confidentiality undertakings, and to post-closing use by Affiliates.
- **Amend §13.1** to add "actual or bona fide prospective acquirers, investors, lenders and their advisors who are bound by written confidentiality obligations."

### Issue 5 (Medium-High): Tessaline may freely assign; Harrowgate has no exit

**Where it appears**
- MSA §15.1: Supplier may assign the Agreement "in whole or in part, without the consent of Customer, to any person," delegate to any Affiliate or subcontractor, and notify only within 30 days afterward.
- MSA §1.1(c) defines Change of Control for "any Party," but only Customer's is used.
- MSA §14 gives termination only for uncured material breach (30-day cure) and insolvency. §5.2 and §17.3 allow cancellation of individual POs only. There is no right to terminate for chronic late delivery, quality failure or Tessaline's loss of GFSI certification (QA §4.1).

**Why it matters**
- Under a minimum commitment running three to eight years, Harrowgate could find itself bound to a competitor of Harrowgate (or of Corvane's other holdings), or to a weaker operator. Section 6.3 change control protects against changes to site and process but not against a change of counterparty.
- The asymmetry strengthens our position on Issue 1: if Tessaline can assign freely, Harrowgate should not need consent for its own change of control.
- For a buyer, an inability to exit a failing supplier while still owing minimums is a diligence finding.

**Recommended fix**
- Make §15.1 mutual, or require Customer's consent (not unreasonably withheld) except for a transfer to a successor to substantially all of Supplier's business that assumes the Agreement in writing, holds the QA §4.1 certification, and is not a competitor of Customer.
- Add a Customer termination right (without payment of minimums) on 30 days' notice for: Supplier Change of Control to a competitor; loss of certification not cured in 60 days; on-time-in-full performance below an agreed threshold for [3] consecutive months (OF §6.2 already requires monthly reporting); or a Force Majeure Event lasting more than 60 days (MSA §17.3, which currently allows only PO cancellation).
- Extend the §15.1 notice to prior notice.

### Issue 6 (Medium): Price adjustment mechanism is incomplete; MFN is weak

**Where it appears**
- OF §1.2(d) defines the Resin Index by reference to a service "the Parties designate in writing." Nothing in the folder shows that designation.
- OF §4.3: prices are fixed through the first Contract Year (to about February 28, 2027). After that, either Party may request a quarterly adjustment if the Resin Index average moved more than 5% from "the quarter on which current prices were based," applying a 55% resin share. The baseline quarter is not stated.
- OF §4.4: Most Favored Pricing applies only if Tessaline sells a "substantially similar product" to another customer "in comparable volumes and on comparable terms" at a lower price, and then only "promptly offer[s]" the lower price. There is no verification, audit or retroactivity.

**Why it matters**
- Without a designated index and baseline, the first adjustment (as early as spring 2027) will be disputed. It may fall in the middle of the closing and integration period.
- The mechanism is symmetric, but a resin spike passes through at 55% with a 5% trigger and no cap.
- The MFN is largely unenforceable in practice because Harrowgate cannot see Tessaline's other contracts.

**Recommended fix**
- Immediately agree and sign a letter designating the Resin Index service and fixing the baseline quarter and index value used for the initial prices.
- Add a collar, for example a cap on any single adjustment or an annual adjustment of no more than [8]%.
- Strengthen the MFN: an annual officer's certificate of compliance, a third-party audit right, and a credit back to the date of the lower price.
- If Corvane plans to aggregate purchasing, ask for Affiliates to be able to buy under the MSA at the same prices. Today the contract benefits only "Customer."

### Issue 8 (Medium): Warranty and quality mechanics

**Where it appears and why it matters**
1. **Warranty period.** QA §5.1 sets 18 months from delivery. Harrowgate's finished goods may stay in the market longer than that. The MSA §10.1 indemnity is not time-limited, but first-party recovery ends at 18 months. Consider 24 months or the Filled Product's shelf life.
2. **Claim deadline.** QA §5.2 requires claims within 15 business days after discovery. The clause does not say a late claim is barred, but Tessaline would argue it. Add "except to the extent Supplier is not materially prejudiced."
3. **Remedy limits.** QA §5.4 gives replacement or refund of price only. Combined with MSA §7.4 (see Issue 3), it may exclude the cost of ruined Filled Product, labor and downtime. Add reimbursement of those direct costs.
4. **Change control standard.** QA §7.2 says Harrowgate "shall not unreasonably withhold approval" and must respond in 60 days. MSA §6.3 requires approval with no reasonableness qualifier. Under MSA §1.3, the MSA outranks the QA, so the conflict is unclear. A buyer in the food industry will want approval at Harrowgate's discretion on food-safety grounds. Amend QA §7.2 accordingly.
5. **Specification amendments.** QA §3.3 lets the quality representatives amend the Specifications, but QA §11.2 and MSA §17.5 refer to signed instruments. After closing, confirm who holds the Harrowgate quality-representative role (QA §10.1), and who has authority.

### Issue 9 (Low): Housekeeping and drafting points

- **Survival.** MSA §14.4 does not list §12 (Insurance), though §12.1 runs "for two (2) years afterward." The "nature" catch-all probably saves it, but add §12 expressly.
- **Payment term.** MSA §4.2 refers to "receipt of undisputed invoice," which is ambiguous. Use "within 45 days after receipt of an invoice, subject to Section 4.3."
- **Forecast liability.** OF §5.3 ties Harrowgate's raw-material liability to forecasts the MSA calls non-binding (MSA §3.1). This is a limited exposure, but keep Harrowgate's forecasts consistent and documented.
- **Precedence.** MSA §1.3 ranks OF > MSA > QA. The QA's operational detail therefore yields to the MSA wherever they conflict (Issues 3 and 8). Consider a clause that the QA controls on technical quality matters.
- **Regulatory covenant.** MSA §6.2 and QA §3.2(a) refer to federal food-contact law (21 C.F.R. Parts 174–186). They do not mention state-law packaging restrictions. I would ask regulatory counsel whether a state-law compliance covenant is advisable. This point is outside the documents.
- **Notice details.** After closing, update MSA §16.1 (Attention: Chief Legal Officer) and QA §10.1 if personnel change.
- **Dispute resolution.** MSA §17.2 gives exclusive New Castle County, Delaware jurisdiction. There is no escalation step or jury waiver. Optional.
