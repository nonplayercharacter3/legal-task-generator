## 1. Bottom line and immediate actions

1. **Tessaline holds a veto and a termination-plus-payment right over the Corvane deal** (MSA §15.3). Any Change of Control of Harrowgate is deemed an assignment requiring Tessaline's prior written consent. Without consent, Tessaline can terminate and accelerate the unpaid minimum purchase commitment. We should plan a consent-and-amendment package now, and consent should be a pre-closing deliverable.
2. **The five-year evergreen renewal (OF §2.2) compounds the exposure.** After the three-year Initial Term, the contract renews for five-year terms unless notice lands in a 30-day window. That multiplies both the acceleration exposure and the value of the commitment a buyer will diligence.
3. **Harrowgate may already be in breach of confidentiality** (MSA §13.1) if the MSA, pricing or forecasts went into the Corvane data room. That breach is uncapped (MSA §11.3(b)). This is the most time-sensitive item.
4. **Risk allocation is lopsided.** Harrowgate's indemnity is uncapped (MSA §11.4). Tessaline's liability is capped at roughly 12 months of fees. Neither document allocates first-party recall costs.

**Immediate (before signing or closing):** Issues 1, 4 and 10 below. **Same negotiation, no hard date:** Issues 2, 3, 5 and 6.

## 2. Ranked issues

### Issue 1 (Critical, immediate): Change of Control consent, termination right and payment acceleration

**Where:**
- MSA §1.1(c) defines "Change of Control".
- MSA §15.2 bars assignment, including by operation of law.
- MSA §15.3 is the operative clause.
- OF §2.1 preserves the §15.3 termination right.
- OF §3.3 sets the contractual shortfall remedy.

**What the documents say:**
- Change of Control covers acquisition of more than 50% of voting equity, any merger whether or not Harrowgate survives, and a sale of substantially all assets (MSA §1.1(c)(i)–(iii)). Every plausible Corvane structure falls within it.
- Section 15.3 expressly deems any Change of Control of Customer an assignment, "including by merger in which Customer is the surviving entity". It therefore requires Tessaline's prior written consent under §15.2. Harrowgate cannot argue that a stock purchase or reverse triangular merger is not an assignment.
- There is no reasonableness standard, no deemed consent and no timeline for Tessaline to respond.
- If Harrowgate closes without consent, Tessaline may terminate on 30 days' notice at any time within 180 days after it "learns of" the Change of Control (§15.3). The "unpaid balance of the minimum annual purchase commitment … for the remainder of the then-current term" then becomes immediately due (§15.3).

**Why it matters to Harrowgate as customer:**
- The minimum is $2.4M per Contract Year (OF §3.1).
  - The Initial Term runs to March 1, 2029 (OF §2.1).
  - Illustratively, a closing around year-end 2026 leaves about 26 months. That implies up to roughly $5M of accelerated minimum, less whatever has already been purchased.
  - If the closing falls in a Renewal Term, the "then-current term" is five years, so the figure could approach $12M (OF §2.2).
- Tessaline gets the money and also the right to stop supplying. Nothing requires credit for purchases already made, discounting or mitigation.
- The remedy conflicts with the parties' own pricing of a shortfall. OF §3.3 sets Tessaline's remedy for missing the minimum at 20% of the difference, "a reasonable estimate of Supplier's lost margin." Section 15.3 demands 100% of the unpaid balance.
  - OF §3.3 supports a penalty and unenforceability argument, and an argument that the Order Form's measure controls under MSA §1.3.
  - Delaware law on this point needs confirmation. We should treat the argument as leverage, not a plan.
- "Learns of" is ambiguous. It is unclear whether it is triggered by announcement or signing, before any Change of Control has occurred. The 180-day tail also leaves a post-closing overhang.

**Why it matters as a target:**
- Corvane will almost certainly treat this as a required consent or a disclosure-schedule item. Tessaline can use the consent as leverage, for example for price, term or volume concessions.
- The clause is perpetual. It would be triggered again on Corvane's own exit (a sale of Harrowgate), on a merger of Harrowgate into another portfolio company, and possibly on internal reorganizations.
- Section 15.2 also bars any transfer "of rights", including a collateral assignment to acquisition lenders. That could conflict with Corvane's financing.
- Tessaline's own change of control and assignments are unrestricted (Issue 5).

**Recommended fix:**

*Sequencing.*
- Confirm with Corvane's counsel how the merger agreement treats this consent (closing condition, covenant or schedule only).
- Approach Tessaline only under a short NDA and with Corvane's agreement, so the approach does not breach the deal's confidentiality terms.
- Aim to have a signed consent and amendment before closing. A written waiver from Tessaline can still cure after the fact (MSA §17.5), but it should not be left to that.

*Primary ask:* amend §15.3.

> **15.3 Change of Control of Customer.** A Change of Control of Customer is not an assignment of this Agreement, does not require Supplier's consent, and does not give rise to any right of termination, acceleration or payment, provided that Customer notifies Supplier in writing within thirty (30) days after the Change of Control. Supplier consents to, and waives all rights under this Agreement with respect to, the acquisition of Customer by Corvane Equity Partners IV, L.P. or its Affiliates, any financing of that acquisition (including collateral assignments to financing sources), and any subsequent Change of Control or transfer among Customer's Affiliates.

*Fallback 1 (consent right with guardrails):*
- Consent is required only if the acquirer is a Supplier Competitor.
- Consent may not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed.
- Consent is deemed given if Supplier does not object within 10 business days.

*Fallback 2 (termination right retained):*
- Termination is available only on at least 180 days' notice.
- The acceleration sentence is deleted.
- Customer's purchase obligations end on the termination date.
- Supplier must honor accepted Purchase Orders and offer a last-time buy.

*Trade options.* Tessaline will likely worry about the leverage of a sponsor-owned buyer. Harrowgate can offer:
- confirmation of the Net-45 payment record;
- a modest volume uplift reflecting Corvane's portfolio (the capacity reservation tops out at 125% of the minimum, OF §3.4);
- a limited credit assurance.

These are priced against the fixes in Issues 2 and 3.

Also conform OF §2.1, which cross-refers to §15.3, and amend §15.2 to permit assignment to Affiliates and to a successor to the business.

### Issue 2 (High): Five-year evergreen renewal with a 30-day opt-out window

**Where:** OF §2.2; OF §2.1; MSA §2.1; MSA §16.1 (notice mechanics).

**What the documents say:**
- The Initial Term is three years from March 1, 2026.
- The agreement then renews "automatically" for successive five-year terms.
- A non-renewal notice is effective only if delivered under MSA §16 no earlier than 60 and no later than 30 days before the end of the term. A notice outside that window "is of no effect."
- The $2.4M minimum and the pricing mechanism continue to apply during each Renewal Term (OF §2.2).
- There is no termination for convenience anywhere in the MSA (§14 allows only breach and insolvency).

**Why it matters:**
- A missed 30-day window locks Harrowgate into $12M of minimum commitment over five years, and into a further five-year term at the next cycle.
- The same default applies to Tessaline, which can walk at the end of the Initial Term on equal notice.
- The window is easy to miss after a deal closes and personnel change. Notice must also go by hand or courier, with email only as a supplementary copy (MSA §16.1).
- Corvane will treat a long evergreen as an encumbrance on exit value. Combined with Issue 1, the accelerated sum grows in a Renewal Term.

**Key dates** (assuming the Initial Term ends March 1, 2029; if it ends February 28, the window shifts one day earlier, so use the overlapping dates):

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Non-renewal window opens | about Dec. 31, 2028 |
| Non-renewal window closes | about Jan. 29–30, 2029 |
| End of Initial Term | Mar. 1, 2029 |
| Contract Year 1 ends / price fix ends | about Mar. 1, 2027 |

**Recommended fix:**

*Primary ask:* replace OF §2.2.

> **2.2 Renewal.** At the end of the Initial Term, this Agreement renews for one (1) successive term of one (1) year unless either Party gives written notice of non-renewal at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term. Any further renewal requires a written agreement signed by both Parties. The Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment applies during any renewal term only as the Parties agree in writing.

*Fallback:*
- Keep the automatic renewal but shorten each Renewal Term to one or two years.
- Widen the window to 180 to 60 days.
- Require Supplier to send a reminder notice 120 to 90 days before expiry. If it does not, Customer may terminate on 90 days' notice.
- Add a Customer termination-for-convenience right during any Renewal Term.

Also calendar the window now and record it in the post-closing compliance calendar.

### Issue 3 (High): Asymmetric liability, a thin cap and no recall-cost allocation

**Where:** MSA §§10.1, 10.2, 11.1–11.4, 12.1, 1.1(f); QA §§5.4, 8.2; MSA §7.4.

**What the documents say:**
- Harrowgate indemnifies Tessaline for labeling, Artwork and "the contents of any Filled Product," including product liability, personal injury, illness or recall claims. The only exception is to the extent caused by a Product's failure to conform to the Specifications (MSA §10.2(b)–(c)).
- That indemnity is expressly unlimited and outside the consequential-damages exclusion (MSA §11.4).
- Tessaline's indemnity (MSA §10.1) is subject to the §11.2 cap and §11.1 exclusion. The §11.3 exceptions cover fraud, gross negligence, willful misconduct, confidentiality and Customer's payment obligations. They do not cover Tessaline's indemnity.
- The cap is "fees paid or payable in the prior 12 months … measured from the date of the event." The wording is lowercase and unconformed to the surrounding text (MSA §11.2).
  - In the first year the cap could be a few hundred thousand dollars.
  - Even at full run-rate it is about $2.4M, below the $5M CGL and $2M recall insurance each party must carry (MSA §12.1).
- "Losses" covers only Third-Party Claims (MSA §1.1(f)). Harrowgate's own recall costs, lost Filled Product, line downtime and sorting costs fall outside Tessaline's indemnity.
- Harrowgate's remedy for defective film is replacement or credit of the price (QA §5.4). Consequential damages and lost profits are excluded (MSA §11.1). Remedies are "together with" the §10.1 indemnity (MSA §7.4).
- QA §8.2 says recall cost responsibility "is determined under the Master Supply Agreement." The MSA has no recall-cost clause, so the cross-reference leads nowhere.
- The §10.2(c) carve-out applies only where the Product fails the Specifications. A defect that breaches a warranty without breaching a written Specification (for example workmanship under MSA §7.1(b)) is not clearly excepted. Sections 10.1(a) and 10.2(c) can also overlap on the same claim.

**Why it matters:**
- *As customer:* a contamination event traced to Tessaline's film leaves Harrowgate with the film price back, little else, and possibly uncapped exposure to Tessaline for the resulting third-party claims.
- *As target:* an uncapped contingent liability on a food business is a diligence flag and may feed price, escrow or special-indemnity negotiations. Corvane's insurers will look at the gap.

**Recommended fix:**

*Primary ask:*
- Delete MSA §11.4.
- Add to §11.3 a carve-out for each party's Third-Party Claim indemnity, subject to a super-cap that is mutual.

> The liability of each Party under Section 10 shall not exceed the greater of (i) $[10,000,000] and (ii) the insurance limits required by Section 12.1, and insurance proceeds are in addition to the cap.

- Revise §11.2 so the cap is the greater of (a) amounts paid or payable in the prior 12 months (or, in the first Contract Year, the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment) and (b) $[5,000,000].
- Add a new §10.5.

> **10.5 Recall Costs.** If a recall, market withdrawal or stock recovery of Filled Product results from Nonconforming Product or Supplier's breach, Supplier shall reimburse Customer's reasonable documented costs, including notification, retrieval, destruction, replacement product, sorting, testing and disposal. Those costs are direct damages and are not excluded by Section 11.1.

- Amend QA §5.4 to cover Filled Product and handling costs.
- Conform QA §8.2 to the new §10.5.
- Expand the §10.2(c) exception to "any Product that is defective, fails the Specifications or breaches Section 7.1."

*Fallback:* accept a cap on both indemnities at a higher multiple, in exchange for a recall-cost reimbursement sub-limit tied to the $2M recall insurance. Whatever the outcome, confirm Harrowgate's own coverage meets MSA §12.1 and survives the change of ownership. Section 12.2 requires 30 days' notice before cancellation or material reduction.

### Issue 4 (High, immediate): Confidentiality blocks disclosure to Corvane

**Where:** MSA §13.1; MSA §13.4; MSA §11.3(b); MSA §16.1.

**What the documents say:**
- Confidential Information includes pricing, Specifications, formulations and forecasts. It may be used "only to perform this Agreement."
- It may be disclosed only to a party's and its Affiliates' employees, advisors and financing sources who need to know it (§13.1).
- A prospective acquirer is not on that list. Corvane is not an Affiliate before closing.
- A breach of §13 is excluded from both the cap and the consequential-damages bar (§11.3(b)).
- The Order Form pricing is arguably Tessaline's Confidential Information, and Harrowgate's forecasts are its own.

**Why it matters:**
- Diligence by Corvane necessarily involves the contract terms. If the MSA, Order Form or QA, or Tessaline's pricing or specifications, is already in the data room, Harrowgate may be exposed to an uncapped claim. Tessaline could also use it to strengthen its position on consent.
- The Corvane data room request list will likely include this contract.

**Recommended fix:**
- *Immediately:* confirm what has been uploaded. If necessary, limit access to a clean-team arrangement or redact pricing and specifications until consent is obtained.
- Include in the consent letter a retroactive acknowledgment and waiver covering disclosures to Corvane, its financing sources and advisors.
- Amend §13.1 to add:

> actual or prospective acquirers, investors, lenders and their advisors who are bound by written confidentiality obligations.

### Issue 5 (Medium-High): Supplier may assign and delegate freely; Customer may not

**Where:** MSA §15.1 vs §§15.2–15.3; MSA §§6.3, 6.4; QA §§4.1, 7.1.

**What the documents say:**
- Supplier may assign to "any person" without consent and may delegate to any Affiliate or subcontractor, with notice only within 30 days after the fact (§15.1).
- No release is stated, and no assumption is required.
- Customer has no equivalent freedom (§§15.2–15.3).
- Tessaline's own change of control triggers nothing.
- Section 15.1 conflicts with the change-control requirement for new manufacturing sites, subcontractors and raw-material suppliers (MSA §6.3; QA §7.1) and with the QMS certification covenant (QA §4.1). These are all in the same documents, and nothing says which prevails.

**Why it matters:**
- As customer: the film could end up supplied by a competitor of Harrowgate, a less qualified manufacturer or a distressed owner. Harrowgate could lose audit and quality rights in practice.
- As target: Corvane will weigh supply-chain continuity. A one-way clause is also a bargaining point against Tessaline's own demand for consent in Issue 1.

**Recommended fix:**

> 15.1 Supplier may not assign or delegate this Agreement, in whole or in part, without Customer's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld, except to a successor to substantially all of its business that assumes this Agreement in writing, is not a competitor of Customer, and maintains the certifications required by the Quality Agreement. No delegation or assignment relieves Supplier of its obligations, and Sections 6.3 and 6.4 and the Quality Agreement apply to any Affiliate, subcontractor or assignee.

*Fallback:* mutualize. Whatever change-of-control standard Tessaline keeps for Harrowgate applies equally to Tessaline.

### Issue 6 (Medium): Minimum commitment is inflexible and its remedy is not exclusive

**Where:** OF §§3.1–3.4; MSA §14.1; MSA §15.2; MSA §1.1(a).

**What the documents say:**
- There is a fixed $2.4M minimum with credits for undelivered, cancelled and unreplaced rejected Products (OF §§3.1–3.2).
- Shortfall remedy: Supplier "may invoice" 20% of the difference (OF §3.3). This is favorable and should be preserved.
- The Order Form does not say the Shortfall Payment is Tessaline's *exclusive* remedy. Tessaline could argue a breach and invoke MSA §14.1 termination.
- No adjustment applies for divestitures, plant closures, product discontinuation or integration with Corvane portfolio purchasing.
- Only Harrowgate may buy. There are no Affiliate purchasing rights, and an internal reorganization is an assignment (MSA §15.2).

**Why it matters:**
- As customer: post-closing integration could reduce Harrowgate's film demand, or Corvane may want to pool volume. Neither is contemplated.
- As target: the commitment is a fixed liability against revenue forecasts.

**Recommended fix:**

> The Shortfall Payment is Supplier's sole and exclusive remedy for any failure to meet the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment, and no such failure is a breach of this Agreement.

Also request:
- an equitable reduction in the minimum for divested or discontinued lines;
- Affiliate purchasing rights, with Affiliate purchases counting toward the minimum;
- permitted assignment to a successor to the business.

These asks are the lowest priority in the Tessaline negotiation and can be traded.

### Issue 7 (Medium): Pricing mechanism has gaps

**Where:** OF §§4.3, 4.4, 1.2(d); MSA §4.1.

**What the documents say:**
- Prices are fixed for Contract Year 1 (to about March 1, 2027). After that, either party may request a quarterly adjustment if the Resin Index moves more than 5%, scaled by a 55% resin share (OF §4.3).
- The "Resin Index" is defined as the LDPE film-grade contract price, from a service "the Parties designate in writing" (OF §1.2(d)). I saw no designation in the folder.
- The base quarter for the first comparison is not stated ("the quarter on which current prices were based").
- One LDPE index and one 55% share apply to all four Products, including EVOH barrier film (TP-BF-3325) and PET/PE laminate (TP-PR-1875) (OF §4.1).
- The most-favored pricing clause has no audit or verification right and is limited to "comparable volumes and terms" (OF §4.4).

**Why it matters:**
- Absent a designated service, the adjustment mechanism may not operate, which invites disputes. The index mismatch may favor either side.
- A sponsor-owned Harrowgate may want volume-based pricing, and the MFN is hard to enforce.
- The first price-adjustment right opens in about March 2027, near the expected closing.

**Recommended fix:**
- Sign a short side letter naming the index service, the base quarter and publication date, and a fallback index.
- Consider product-specific indices for non-LDPE products.
- Add an annual officer's certificate on MFN compliance and a verification right.
- Add a collar or annual cap on adjustments.

### Issue 8 (Medium): Warranty and quality mechanics

**Where:** QA §§5.1, 5.2, 5.4, 3.1, 3.3, 7.2, Schedule A; MSA §§6.3, 7.1, 7.4; MSA §17.5.

**What the documents say:**
- The warranty period is 18 months from delivery (QA §5.1).
  - Filled Product shelf life may run longer, but the Filled Product indemnity in MSA §10.2(c) has no time limit.
  - Harrowgate must submit a claim within 15 business days of discovery (QA §5.2). The consequence of a late claim is not stated.
- Remedies are replacement or credit of price (QA §5.4); see Issue 3.
- The Specifications consist of Product Specification Sheets "attached as Schedule A" (QA §3.1). In the folder Schedule A is only an index of sheet references and revision numbers (all Rev. 01), not the sheets themselves. If the sheets were never signed by both parties, the warranty baseline is uncertain (QA §2(f)).
- Change approval: MSA §6.3 requires Customer's "written approval." QA §7.2 adds that approval shall not be unreasonably withheld. The MSA ranks above the QA, so the standard is ambiguous.
- Amendments: Specification revisions need only the quality representatives' signatures (QA §3.3), while MSA §17.5 requires both parties' written signed instruments.

**Why it matters:** the specifications are the basis for every quality claim and for the §10.2(c) carve-out. A buyer's quality diligence will ask for them.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain and attach the signed Specification Sheets (Rev. 01) now.
- Amend QA §5.2 to state that late notice bars a claim only to the extent of material prejudice.
- Extend the warranty to the labeled shelf life of the Filled Product, or the longer of 18 months and the Filled Product shelf life.
- Align the change-control approval standard in MSA §6.3 and QA §7.2.

### Issue 9 (Low-Medium): Continuity and supply protections

**Where:** MSA §17.3; MSA §14.2; OF §§3.4, 5.5; MSA §2.2.

**What the documents say:**
- Customer may cancel affected orders and source elsewhere only after a force majeure event affecting Supplier lasts more than 60 days (MSA §17.3).
- There is no second-source, step-in or last-time-buy right on Supplier insolvency or exit.
- Capacity is reserved only up to 125% of the minimum (OF §3.4).
- Safety stock covers only two products and only two weeks (OF §5.5).

**Why it matters:** these are modest relative to Issues 1–5, but a buyer in the food sector will ask about supply security. They give Harrowgate additional currency in the negotiation.

**Recommended fix:** add a short supply-continuity rider covering a last-time buy, a technology-transfer or second-source right on Supplier exit, and extended safety stock for the other two products.

## 3. Document interplay: key points

| Interaction | Effect |
|---|---|
| OF §2.1 cross-refers to MSA §15.3 | Any fix to Change of Control must be made in both documents. |
| OF §2.2 extends the minimum and pricing into Renewal Terms | The MSA §15.3 acceleration scales with the term (Issues 1 and 2). |
| OF §3.3 (20% shortfall) vs MSA §15.3 (100% acceleration) | The Order Form ranks above the MSA (MSA §1.3). This supports an argument that the Order Form's measure of Supplier's loss controls, but it is not a certain conflict. |
| MSA §7.4 and QA §5.4 vs MSA §§10.1 and 11 | First-party losses are limited to price. The indemnity reaches only Third-Party Claims, and consequential damages are excluded. |
| QA §8.2 cross-refers to the MSA on recall costs | The MSA has no recall-cost provision. |
| MSA §15.1 vs MSA §6.3 and QA §§4.1 and 7.1 | Free delegation conflicts with site and supplier change control. |
| QA §7.2 (approval not unreasonably withheld) vs MSA §6.3 | The MSA controls but is silent on the standard. |

**Strong asks:**

**Tradeable:**

- Issue 7 pricing clean-up.

I would not open the whole contract. Each additional ask gives Tessaline more room to price its consent, so I recommend a short, targeted amendment with a consent letter.
