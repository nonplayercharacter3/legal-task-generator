## 2. ISSUES

### Issue 1: The renewal notice is facially late

**Where:** DA §2.2; DA §2.1; DA §17.1–17.2; DA §19.1; Notice §§1, 3.

**What the documents say:**
- Renewal requires written notice "at least 90 days before Initial Term expiry" (DA §2.2). The Initial Term ends Dec. 31, 2026 (DA §2.1). Counting back, the last day was Oct. 2 (Oct. 1 on a stricter count).
- If notice is not timely, the DA "will expire at the end of the Initial Term" (DA §2.2).
- Courier notice is effective one Business Day after dispatch (DA §17.1). A letter dispatched Oct. 15 (Thursday) is effective Oct. 16, about 76 days before expiry, so roughly 14 days late.
- Email does not count (DA §17.2). The Notice acknowledges this (Notice §3), so the courtesy email cannot cure anything.
- There is no cure or grace language in §2.2. Waivers must be in a signed writing, and delay does not waive rights (DA §19.1). Brightwater's silence or a bare "receipt acknowledged" countersignature (Notice §5) should not be relied on as a waiver.

**Why it matters:**
- Absent a waiver, the DA expires Dec. 31, 2026. Exclusivity (DA §3.1–3.2) ends, and Brightwater may appoint others or sell directly.
- Damages are a poor remedy. DA §13.1 bars lost profits and consequential damages, and the only exceptions (§§11, 12) do not cover wrongful non-renewal.
- Courts generally enforce option deadlines strictly. I have not researched NY authority on equitable relief for late exercise and expect it to be a long shot.
- The Corvane price and earn-out depend on this contract (see Issue 5).

**Recommended course:**
1. Gather proof of dispatch and delivery (courier tracking, date and time) and any earlier written or oral renewal communications with Brightwater.
2. Do not argue timeliness in writing yet, and avoid admissions. Do not let a "receipt acknowledged" countersignature stand in for a waiver.
3. Pursue a negotiated fix: a short written amendment or waiver signed by both parties (DA §19.1) deeming the Notice timely, or a new three-year extension. Brightwater has reasons to agree, since the Am. gives it a $2.4M annual minimum from an exclusive three-state distributor. Whether to package this with the consent request (Issue 3) is a strategy call for you (see Issue 3).
4. Prepare the fallback in Issue 2 in parallel.
5. Have NY counsel or research confirm the strict-compliance rule and any waiver or estoppel arguments, including whether Brightwater has accepted performance or made assurances.

### Issue 3: Corvane transaction requires Brightwater's prior written consent

**Where:** DA §14.2 (change of control), §14.3 (void transfers), §15.3 (immediate termination), §16.2 (no repurchase on a §15.3 termination); LOI §§1.1–1.2, 4.1, 6.

**What the documents say:**
- A transfer of more than 50% of the membership interests is "deemed an assignment" requiring Brightwater's prior written consent. The request must be made at least 30 days before the transfer (DA §14.2). The LOI is a 100% equity purchase (LOI §1.1).
- A transfer without consent is "void" (DA §14.3), and Brightwater may terminate immediately (DA §15.3).
- A §15.3 termination carries no inventory repurchase (DA §16.2).
- There is no "not unreasonably withheld" qualifier. Compare WSA §16.1, which has one.
- LOI §1.2 says contracts "remain with the Company." That is true for a stock deal generally but does not solve DA §14.2, which treats the equity sale itself as an assignment. Converting to a merger or asset sale does not help either (DA §14.1–14.2).
- The LOI's closing conditions (§6) omit Brightwater's consent.

**Why it matters:**
- Brightwater holds two levers at once: consent and the renewal. It can condition consent on concessions.
- Closing without consent risks termination with no repurchase right.
- Corvane has not conditioned closing on consent, which is both a risk and a negotiating gap.

**Recommended course:**
1. Send the written consent request by Oct. 31 (earlier if possible), by courier under DA §17.1.
2. Get Corvane's written consent first (see Issue 4).
3. Require consent as a closing condition in the Definitive Agreement, with a closing date that respects the 30-day lead time. Avoid a simultaneous sign-and-close under LOI §4.2 until consent is in hand.
4. Decide with the client whether to address the renewal and the consent in one package.

### Issue 4: Diligence and disclosure create a confidentiality exposure

**Where:** DA §§11.1–11.2, 13.1, 15.1, 18.4; LOI §§5.2, 5.3, 10; WSA §11.1.

**What the documents say:**
- DA §11.2 permits disclosure of Confidential Information (including pricing, e.g., Exhibit B) only to employees, advisors and financing sources. It does not permit disclosure to prospective acquirers. Contrast WSA §11.1, which expressly allows it.
- LOI §5.2 puts the DA, with its pricing, in scope for Corvane's diligence.
- DA §13.1 excludes §11 from the consequential-damages waiver, so a breach exposes Halvorsen to broader damages. Brightwater can also seek an injunction (DA §18.4) and could assert material breach (DA §15.1).
- LOI §10 (binding) bars disclosing the LOI's existence or terms without the other parties' consent. Approaching Brightwater for consent therefore needs Corvane's sign-off. Under LOI §5.3, Corvane may not contact Brightwater without the Company's consent.
- The Aug. 12, 2026 Confidentiality Agreement is not in the folder.

**Why it matters:** Halvorsen may already be in technical breach, and the consent request itself needs Corvane's permission.

**Recommended course:**
- Find out what Brightwater information Corvane has received. Pause or redact Exhibit B pricing and Brightwater technical information until Brightwater consents.
- Obtain Corvane's written consent to approach Brightwater.
- Include a confidentiality consent in the Brightwater request.
- Review the Aug. 12 NDA. It should be at least as protective as WSA §11.1 and DA §11.2.

### Issue 7: Annual Minimum compliance is unknown; the Amendment has retroactive and cumulative consequences

**Where:** Am. §§1.2, 1.3, 3.1–3.4, 4.1–4.2, 5.1–5.5; DA §19.2.

**What the documents say:**
- The Annual Minimum is $2.4M in Net Purchases per Contract Year (Am. §3.1).
- Contract Year 2025 began Jan. 1, 2025, before the Mar. 1, 2025 Amendment, with no proration (Am. §§1.2, 3.2). The full 2025 minimum therefore applied.
- Brightwater must deliver a reconciliation within 45 days after year-end, which for 2025 was by about Feb. 14, 2026. The distributor has 30 days to dispute (Am. §4.2).
- A Shortfall can be cured within 90 days after final determination by extra purchases that do not count toward the minimum for the year made (Am. §5.1).
- If not cured, Brightwater may convert to non-exclusive on notice within 60 days after the cure period (Am. §5.2).
- A Shortfall above 25%, or uncured Shortfalls in two consecutive years, entitles Brightwater to terminate without further cure (Am. §5.3). Such a termination is treated as a §15.1 Supplier termination, so there is no §16.2 repurchase (Am. §5.4).
- Brightwater's remedies for a miss are exclusive (Am. §5.5).
- Net Purchases exclude freight, taxes, credits and warranty reimbursements (Am. §1.3).
- The Annual Minimum is reduced for Brightwater's rejections and 60-day delivery failures (Am. §3.3).
- The Amendment controls over the DA body (DA §19.2).
- The Renewal Term is on the terms then in effect (DA §2.3), so the Annual Minimum continues.

**Why it matters:**
- A 2025 or 2026 shortfall would give Brightwater leverage in the renewal and consent discussions.
- Corvane's diligence will test this.
- Am. §5.5 helps Halvorsen: a shortfall is not a ground for denying the renewal right. But a termination right in two consecutive years is a real risk.

**Recommended course:**
- Obtain 2025 and year-to-date 2026 purchase data and Brightwater's 2025 reconciliation.
- Compute Net Purchases under Am. §1.3, and assemble any §3.3 reductions (rejected or late orders).
- Prepare the Q3 statement due Oct. 30 carefully; it will frame the discussion.
- If a Shortfall looks likely, plan the cure purchases and their cash and inventory cost.

### Issue 8: Drafting points in the Amendment to note

**Where:** Am. §§2.1, 3.1, 5.1, 5.4.

- Am. §2.1 says exclusivity continues "subject to" satisfying the Annual Minimum, while §5 provides a conversion mechanism with notice. The remedy provisions should control, with §5.5 supporting that reading, but Brightwater could argue otherwise.
- "Products" in Am. §§3–5 includes all Supplier goods, spare parts and accessories, which is broader than DA Exhibit A. That helps Halvorsen meet the minimum, but exclusivity protects only Exhibit A products.
- Cure purchases do not count toward the minimum of the year in which they are made (Am. §5.1). Halvorsen could therefore carry both the cure and the following year's minimum.
- Am. §5.4 removes the repurchase right on a shortfall termination.

**Recommendation:** If the Amendment is reopened in a renewal package, consider clarifying §2.1 and tying any termination right to a notice-and-cure process.

### Issue 10: Tidemark liability limits and insurance versus inventory value

**Where:** WSA §§9.2, 9.4, 10.1–10.2, Ex. B; DA §6.2, §16.2.

**What the documents say:**
- Tidemark's liability is capped at documented landed cost, up to $250,000 per occurrence and $1,000,000 per year (WSA §9.2).
- Tidemark carries only $1M per occurrence of warehouse legal liability insurance (WSA §10.1(b)).
- Halvorsen must insure Goods at full value and waive subrogation except to the extent of Tidemark's §9 liability (WSA §10.2).
- Limits can be increased at $0.10 per $100 of declared value per month (WSA §9.2, Ex. B). For example, $2M of declared value would cost about $2,000/month.
- Claim notice is due within 60 days of discovery and in any event within 9 months (WSA §9.4).
- Title and risk pass to Halvorsen at delivery in Allentown (DA §6.2), and Brightwater repurchases only new, unused, resalable goods (DA §16.2).

**Why it matters:**
- At unit prices of $9,850 to $17,900 (DA Ex. B) and a $2.4M annual purchase level, inventory could far exceed the cap. A single large loss would fall on Halvorsen's own insurance, and damaged goods are not repurchasable.
- Corvane will review this in diligence.

**Recommended course:** Get current inventory values and Halvorsen's property policy. Confirm coverage is adequate and consider raising declared values.

### Issue 11: Tidemark change-of-control, lender and lien points

**Where:** WSA §§7.5, 8.2, 16.1; LOI §6(h).

**What the documents say:**
- A change of control is not an assignment under the WSA, so Tidemark's consent is not needed for the Corvane deal (WSA §16.1).
- Tidemark must sign a customary bailee waiver for Halvorsen's lenders on request (WSA §8.2). LOI §6(h) requires repayment of the revolver at closing and release of liens.
- Tidemark's warehouse lien covers undisputed charges more than 30 days overdue, with 15 days' notice before enforcement (WSA §7.5).

**Why it matters:** The WSA presents no consent obstacle. Unpaid Tidemark charges, though, could encumber goods that Halvorsen needs to return to Brightwater in a repurchase.

**Recommended course:** Keep Tidemark invoices current. Coordinate lien releases and any bailee waiver changes with the closing checklist.
