## II. BOTTOM LINE

1. **The renewal appears untimely, so the DA will likely expire Dec 31, 2026 unless Brightwater agrees otherwise.** This is the central problem.
2. **The Corvane sale needs Brightwater's prior written consent, and the DA gives Brightwater sole discretion.** Without consent, Brightwater can terminate immediately and Halvorsen loses its repurchase right.

## III. ISSUES

### Issue 1: Renewal notice was late (CRITICAL)

**Where:** DA §2.2 (90 days before Initial Term expiry); §2.1 (expiry Dec 31, 2026); §17.1 (courier notice effective one Business Day after dispatch); Renewal Letter (dated Oct 15, sent by overnight courier).

**Analysis:**
- Ninety days before Dec 31, 2026 is Friday, Oct 2, 2026. Counting conservatively, it is Oct 1.
- A letter dispatched Thursday, Oct 15 is effective Friday, Oct 16. That is 76 days before expiry, roughly 14 days short.
- Section 2.2 states the consequence: absent timely notice, "this Agreement will expire at the end of the Initial Term."
- The email courtesy copy does not count (DA §17.2; Renewal Letter §3).
- New York courts generally enforce option-exercise deadlines strictly, and relief for a tardy exercise is narrow. Please have this confirmed.

**Why it matters:**
- Halvorsen's exclusivity, trademark license (§10.1) and supply rights end Dec 31.
- After that, Brightwater may sell directly or appoint others in the Territory. The §3.2 restriction applies only "during the Term."
- Lost-profits damages are waived (DA §13.1), so a damages claim is not a realistic fallback.

**Weaknesses in any argument that notice was valid:**
- Section 19.1 requires waivers to be in a signed writing, and it says delay does not operate as a waiver.
- Section 2.2 gives the option only to the Distributor. Brightwater has no obligation to accept a late exercise.

**Recommendation:**
- (a) Confirm the actual dispatch date and courier tracking. Confirm whether any earlier notice or communication exists.
- (b) Approach Brightwater promptly for a signed instrument under §19.1. It should state that the Oct 15 notice is accepted as timely, or amend §2.1/§2.2 to extend the Initial Term or grant a renewal. Package it with the change-of-control consent (Issue 3).
- (c) Do not rely on Brightwater countersigning the Renewal Letter's "Receipt Acknowledged" block. It acknowledges receipt only. Revise the block to include an express waiver and agreement that the notice is timely, or use a separate short letter agreement.
- (d) Prepare the downside plan. Expiry triggers the §16.2 repurchase, which is not excluded for expiration. It also triggers the §16.3 180-day sell-off, which runs to about June 29, 2027. The plan should cover coordinating that inventory with Tidemark (Issue 6).
- (e) Do not send further "we have renewed" communications to Brightwater without reserving rights.

**Smaller points in the Renewal Letter:**
- §1 says the Renewal Term "begins upon the expiration of the Initial Term on December 31, 2026." That is slightly imprecise, since it starts Jan 1, 2027.
- §2 describes exclusivity as continuing "throughout the Renewal Term." It omits that exclusivity is subject to the Annual Minimum (Am. §2.1).
- The letter does not reserve rights.

### Issue 2: The Annual Minimum, Shortfall mechanics and exclusivity risk (HIGH)

**Where:** Am. §§1.3, 2.1, 3.1–3.4, 4.2, 5.1–5.5; DA §19.2 (the Amendment prevails over the DA body).

**Analysis:**
- Halvorsen must make $2.4M of Net Purchases per calendar year.
- Net Purchases count goods *shipped* in the year (Am. §1.3). Cure purchases do not count toward the year in which they are made (Am. §5.1).
- Brightwater's annual reconciliation is due 45 days after year-end, and Halvorsen has 30 days to dispute (Am. §4.2).
- An uncured Shortfall allows conversion to non-exclusive (Am. §5.2).
- A Shortfall above 25% that is uncured, or uncured Shortfalls in two consecutive years, allows termination with no further cure (Am. §5.3).
- Those remedies are Brightwater's "sole and exclusive" remedies for missing the minimum (Am. §5.5). That limits any damages claim.
- The minimum carries into any Renewal Term, because renewal is on the terms then in effect (DA §2.3).

**Why it matters:**
- The folder has no purchase data, so I cannot say whether 2025 or 2026 is at risk.
- If Brightwater can show an uncured Shortfall, it gains leverage in the renewal and consent negotiations. Corvane will also find the problem in diligence (LOI §5.2).

**Drafting ambiguity:** Am. §3.1 defines "Products" for the minimum as all Supplier goods, including parts and accessories, while the DA defines "Products" by Exhibit A (DA §1.1). This broader definition helps Halvorsen, but the inconsistency should be cleaned up in any amendment.

**Recommendation:**
- Obtain the 2025 reconciliation (due about Feb 14, 2026) and 2026 year-to-date Net Purchases.
- If 2026 is short, consider timing shipments before Dec 31.
- Use any amendment with Brightwater to confirm there is no existing Shortfall.

### Issue 3: Corvane sale requires Brightwater's prior written consent (CRITICAL)

**Where:** LOI §§1.1–1.3 (purchase of 100% of the membership interests; Halvorsen "will continue as a separate legal entity"); DA §14.2 (transfer of more than 50% of equity is a deemed assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent, requested at least 30 days before the transfer); §14.3 (void transfers); §15.3 (immediate termination right); §16.2 (no repurchase after a §15.3 termination).

**Analysis:**
- LOI §1.2 reassures that contracts stay with the Company. That is correct as a matter of entity law but irrelevant under DA §14.2. A 100% sale is squarely within it.
- Section 14.2 has no "not unreasonably withheld" standard. Brightwater can refuse or extract concessions.
- Section 14.2 also covers mergers, conversions and series of related transactions, so restructuring will not avoid it.

**Why it matters:**
- Closing without consent lets Brightwater terminate immediately (§15.3). Halvorsen would then lose its repurchase right for unsold inventory (§16.2).
- The LOI's closing conditions (§6) do not mention Brightwater's consent, although they include "customary" conditions.

**Recommendation:**
- (a) Obtain Corvane's written consent to disclose the deal (see Issue 4).
- (b) Send the §14.2 consent request, by courier to the §17.1 address, no later than Thursday, Oct 29 for a Nov 30 closing. If the closing date moves, recalculate.
- (c) Make Brightwater's written consent an express closing condition in the Definitive Agreement. Do not sign and close simultaneously under LOI §4.2 until consent is in hand.
- (d) Combine the consent with the renewal fix (Issue 1) in one negotiated instrument.
- (e) Expect Brightwater to ask about Corvane's affiliates and any competing equipment lines (cf. DA §3.3). Ask Corvane about this in advance.

### Issue 4: Confidentiality conflicts in both directions (HIGH)

**Where:** LOI §10 (binding; no public disclosure of the LOI's existence or terms without consent); LOI §5.2 (diligence will include the Distribution Agreement); DA §11.2 (disclosure permitted only to "employees, advisors and financing sources").

**Analysis:**
1. **Disclosure to Brightwater.** Halvorsen cannot tell Brightwater about the LOI without Corvane's written consent, which is a binding obligation.
2. **Disclosure to Corvane.** DA §11.2 does not list prospective acquirers as permitted recipients. By contrast, the Tidemark WSA §11.1 does expressly allow disclosure to "actual or prospective investors or acquirers." If the DA terms or Brightwater pricing and customer data count as Brightwater Confidential Information (§11.1), sharing them with Corvane may breach §11.2. That breach would hand Brightwater another lever.

**Recommendation:**
- Get Corvane's consent to the Brightwater disclosure now.
- Pause or limit data-room sharing of the DA, Exhibit B pricing and Brightwater data until Brightwater consents, or share them only with Corvane's counsel under a clean-team arrangement. Include a confidentiality consent in the Brightwater request.

### Issue 5: Interaction of the lapse risk with the Corvane deal (HIGH)

**Where:** LOI §§2.1, 2.3(b), 3.3, 6(a), 6(e), 9, 12; DA §2.2.

**Analysis:**
- The $18.5M price (§2.1) and the 2027 Gross Profit earn-out (§2.3(b)) assume Brightwater continues (LOI §1.3).
- Corvane's closing conditions include diligence results satisfactory in its "sole discretion" (§6(a)) and no material adverse change (§6(e)). The late renewal is likely to surface in diligence.
- General rep caps do not apply to fraud (§3.3). Sellers who fail to disclose the renewal problem face uncapped exposure.
- Binding exclusivity runs to Dec 15 (§9), but Buyer may walk "at any time" (§12). The Sellers are locked up while Buyer holds options. Section 12 also refers to "either Party" although the LOI has three types of Party, which is minor but sloppy.

**Recommendation:**
- Plan disclosure to Corvane in a controlled way, ideally alongside the Brightwater fix.
- Negotiate in the Definitive Agreement: a specific disclosure schedule for the renewal issue, a Brightwater consent condition, and a price or earn-out mechanism that accounts for the risk.
- Seek a shorter exclusivity period if Brightwater's consent is not received by a set date.

### Issue 6: Tidemark liability cap, space commitment and exit mechanics (MEDIUM)

**Where:** WSA §§2.1, 3.2, 9.2, 10.1–10.2, 13.3, 14.1, Exhibit B; DA §§6.2, 16.2.

**Analysis:**
1. **Liability cap.** Tidemark's liability for Goods is limited to landed cost, up to $250,000 per occurrence and $1M per year (WSA §9.2). Halvorsen bears risk of loss from delivery at Allentown (DA §6.2). With machines priced from $9,850 to $17,900 and an annual purchase minimum of $2.4M, inventory could exceed the per-occurrence cap in one event.
2. **Property insurance.** Customer must insure Goods at full value (WSA §10.2). Confirm Halvorsen has such coverage. The valuation increase (§9.2, $0.10 per $100 per month) is an option.
3. **Space commitment.** Halvorsen pays for 12,000 sq ft (WSA §3.2) at $1.15 per sq ft per month, about $13,800 per month, through May 31, 2028 (§2.1). A convenience exit is available on 120 days' notice, paying the Dedicated Space charges through the effective date (§13.3). A termination notice would cost about $55,000 in remaining charges, a bounded exposure if the DA lapses.
4. **Exit logistics.** On termination Halvorsen must remove all Goods within 30 days (§14.1). That timing must be coordinated with any Brightwater repurchase (DA §16.2) and the 180-day sell-off (DA §16.3).
5. **Warehouse lien.** Tidemark has a lien for undisputed charges unpaid more than 30 days past due (WSA §7.5). Keep Tidemark current, since a lien would complicate a Brightwater repurchase or Corvane's lien-release requirement (LOI §6(h)).
6. **Lender waiver.** Tidemark must provide a bailee waiver on request (WSA §8.2), which Corvane's lender may want.

**Corvane deal:** no Tidemark consent is needed. A change in control is expressly not an assignment (WSA §16.1). This is a point to confirm to Corvane.

**Recommendation:** review insurance and consider a valuation increase. Calendar the Tidemark non-renewal deadline (90 days before May 31, 2028, about March 2, 2028; WSA §2.2). Decide whether to give a §13.3 notice after the Brightwater outcome is clear.

### Issue 7: Termination without repurchase under the Amendment (MEDIUM)

**Where:** Am. §5.4; DA §§16.1, 16.2.

**Analysis:** A termination under Am. §5.3 is deemed a Supplier termination under DA §15.1 "for purposes of Sections 16.1 and 16.2." DA §16.2 excludes Supplier §15.1 terminations from the repurchase right, and the §16.1 exception reaches §15.1 terminations for non-payment. This is drafted unclearly, but it likely means that a minimum-purchase termination strips Halvorsen of inventory repurchase. Brightwater could also argue that it need not fill accepted orders.

**Recommendation:** clarify the repurchase right and the fill-accepted-orders obligation if an amendment is negotiated.
