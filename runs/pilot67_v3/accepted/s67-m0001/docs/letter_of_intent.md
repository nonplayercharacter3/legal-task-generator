**Re: Proposed Acquisition of Copperwake Brewing Company**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

This letter of intent (this "**Letter**") sets out the principal terms on which Tinav Beverage Partners, Inc., a Delaware corporation ("**Buyer**"), proposes to acquire all of the issued and outstanding shares of capital stock (the "**Shares**") of Copperwake Brewing Company, an Oregon corporation (the "**Company**"), from Braikal Beverage Group, Inc., a Delaware corporation ("**Seller**"). The acquisition of the Shares and the related transactions described in this Letter are together the "**Proposed Transaction**."

This Letter follows our management meetings in Marlow Bend, Oregon and our review of the confidential information memorandum and preliminary data room materials Seller has provided. Except as provided in Section 15, this Letter is an expression of intent only and does not create any binding obligation on either party.

## 1. Proposed Transaction

1.1 **Structure.** Buyer would purchase the Shares from Seller, free and clear of all liens, encumbrances, options and other third-party rights, under a definitive stock purchase agreement between Buyer and Seller (the "**Definitive Agreement**"). At the closing of the Proposed Transaction (the "**Closing**"), the Company would become a wholly owned subsidiary of Buyer.

1.2 **The Business.** Our proposal is based on our understanding, from the materials provided, that the Company's business consists of:

(a) the brewing, packaging and sale of craft beer under the COPPERWAKE brand and related brands, including its year-round, seasonal and limited-release lines, in draft, can and bottle formats;

(b) the production and sale of hard seltzer and other flavored malt beverages under the COPPERWAKE brand, which the Company launched in 2024 and which we understand represents a growing share of revenue;

(c) contract brewing, co-packing and alternating proprietorship services for third-party brewers and beverage brands, using the Company's brewhouse, fermentation and packaging capacity; and

(d) the operation of the Company's taproom and on-premises retail operations, including food service and merchandise sales,

in each case together with the related wholesale distribution relationships, self-distribution activities permitted under Oregon law, and associated intellectual property, recipes, yeast strains and know-how (collectively, the "**Business**"). The valuation in Section 2 assumes that the Company will own or have the right to use, at Closing, all assets necessary to conduct each of these lines of business as currently conducted.

1.3 **Cash-Free, Debt-Free.** The Proposed Transaction would be on a cash-free, debt-free basis. Seller would cause all indebtedness of the Company for borrowed money and all transaction expenses (including any change-of-control, sale or retention bonuses and the employer portion of related payroll taxes) to be paid at or before Closing. We currently do not propose a working capital adjustment, on the basis that Seller will cause the Company to operate in the ordinary course and maintain working capital at normal levels through Closing; we reserve the right to revisit this point if diligence shows material seasonal swings in inventory or receivables.

## 2. Purchase Price and Consideration

2.1 **Purchase Price.** The aggregate purchase price for the Shares would be $48,000,000 (the "**Purchase Price**"), payable as follows:

(a) $40,000,000 in cash at Closing, by wire transfer of immediately available funds, less the Indemnity Escrow Amount described in Section 4; and

(b) $8,000,000 by the issuance by Buyer to Seller at Closing of a subordinated promissory note on the terms described in Section 3 (the "**Seller Note**").

2.2 **Basis of Valuation.** The Purchase Price reflects our valuation of the Business as described in Section 1.2, based on the Company's audited financial statements for fiscal years 2024 and 2025, the unaudited interim financial statements made available to us, and management's projections for fiscal years 2026 through 2028. The Purchase Price assumes, among other things, (a) no material deterioration in the Company's distributor depletions or contract brewing volumes before Closing, (b) the continued availability of the Company's alcohol permits without interruption following the change in ownership, and (c) the transfer to the Company before Closing of the Seller-owned assets described in Section 8.

2.3 **Financing.** Buyer intends to fund the cash portion of the Purchase Price from cash on hand and borrowings under its existing senior credit facility. The Proposed Transaction would not be subject to a financing condition.

## 3. Seller Note

3.1 **Principal Terms.** The Seller Note would have the following principal terms, to be set out in full in the form of note attached to the Definitive Agreement:

(a) *Principal:* $8,000,000.

(b) *Interest:* a fixed rate of 6.00% per annum, payable quarterly in arrears.

(c) *Amortization:* interest only for the first two years, followed by quarterly principal installments of $500,000, with the remaining balance due on the fifth anniversary of the Closing.

(d) *Prepayment:* prepayable at Buyer's option at any time without premium or penalty, and mandatorily prepayable on a sale of the Company by Buyer to an unaffiliated third party.

(e) *Subordination:* subordinated in right of payment to Buyer's senior institutional indebtedness, on terms customary for seller financing and acceptable to Buyer's senior lenders, including customary payment blockage and turnover provisions.

(f) *Reporting:* Buyer would provide Seller with periodic financial information concerning the Company while the Seller Note is outstanding, on terms to be agreed in the Definitive Agreement documentation.

(g) *Events of Default:* limited to customary events, including non-payment, insolvency and acceleration of material senior indebtedness, with customary notice and cure periods.

3.2 **Set-Off.** Buyer would have the right to set off amounts owing to it under the indemnification provisions of the Definitive Agreement against amounts payable under the Seller Note, including the right to withhold amounts claimed in good faith pending resolution of the claim. A good-faith withholding would not constitute a default under the Seller Note.

## 4. Indemnity Escrow

4.1 **Escrow Amount.** At Closing, Buyer would deposit $2,400,000 of the cash portion of the Purchase Price (the "**Indemnity Escrow Amount**") with a mutually acceptable national bank or trust company acting as escrow agent, to secure Seller's indemnification obligations under the Definitive Agreement.

4.2 **Release.** The balance of the escrow, less any amounts subject to pending claims, would be released to Seller at the end of the general survival period described in Section 5.2(a). Amounts withheld for pending claims would be released as and when those claims are resolved.

4.3 **Escrow Costs.** Buyer and Seller would each bear one-half of the fees and expenses of the escrow agent.

4.4 **Not Exclusive.** The escrow would be a source of recovery for Seller's indemnification obligations, and Buyer could also recover directly from Seller and by set-off under Section 3.2, subject to the limitations described in Section 5.

## 5. Representations, Warranties, Survival and Indemnification

5.1 **Representations and Warranties.** The Definitive Agreement would contain representations and warranties of Seller customary for a transaction of this type in the craft brewing industry, including as to organization, authority, capitalization and title to the Shares, absence of conflicts and required consents, brokers, financial statements, absence of undisclosed liabilities, material contracts (including distribution, contract brewing and supply agreements), real property and leases, sufficiency of assets, intellectual property (including recipes and yeast strains), environmental matters (including wastewater discharge and pretreatment compliance), permits, taxes (including federal and state beer excise taxes and brewer's reports of operations), employment and benefits, litigation, compliance with alcohol beverage laws, insurance, customers and suppliers, product liability and recalls, and affiliate transactions. Buyer would make customary representations as to organization, authority, absence of conflicts, brokers and sufficiency of funds.

5.2 **Survival.** The representations and warranties would survive the Closing as follows:

(a) *General representations:* eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date.

(b) *Fundamental representations* (organization, authority, capitalization and title to the Shares, and brokers): until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations.

(c) *Tax representations:* until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations. Given the assessment periods applicable to federal excise taxes on beer administered by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau and to Oregon beer privilege taxes, and the possibility of extended assessment periods in the case of underreporting, Buyer regards this survival period as essential.

Claims notified in writing before the end of the applicable survival period would survive until finally resolved.

5.3 **Indemnification by Seller.** Seller would indemnify Buyer, the Company and their respective affiliates and representatives for losses arising from (a) any inaccuracy in or breach of Seller's representations and warranties, (b) any breach of Seller's covenants, (c) any pre-Closing indebtedness or transaction expenses not paid at Closing, and (d) any specific matters identified in Buyer's due diligence and agreed by the parties to be indemnified on a line-item basis ("**Specified Matters**").

5.4 **Limitations.** Seller's indemnification obligations would be subject to the following:

(a) a deductible basket of $360,000 for claims for breaches of general representations, after which Seller would be liable only for losses in excess of the basket;

(b) an aggregate cap of 10% of the Purchase Price ($4,800,000) for claims for breaches of general representations and other claims under the indemnity, other than as provided in clause (c);

(c) claims for fraud, for breaches of fundamental representations and for Specified Matters would not be subject to the basket or the cap described in clause (b), but claims other than for fraud would be capped at the Purchase Price; and

(d) customary provisions for the reduction of losses by insurance proceeds actually received, mitigation and the avoidance of double recovery.

5.5 **Materiality Scrape; No Sandbagging Defense.** For purposes of determining both breach and the amount of losses, materiality and material adverse effect qualifiers would be disregarded. Buyer's rights to indemnification would not be affected by any investigation by or knowledge of Buyer.

5.6 **Buyer Indemnity.** Buyer would provide a reciprocal indemnity for breaches of its representations and covenants, subject to the same basket and cap as apply to Seller's indemnity for general representations.

5.7 **Exclusive Remedy.** Except for fraud, equitable relief and claims under the ancillary agreements, the indemnification provisions and the set-off right would be the parties' exclusive post-Closing remedy for breaches of the Definitive Agreement.

## 6. Restrictive Covenants

6.1 **Non-Competition.** For a period of five (5) years after the Closing, Seller would not, and would cause its affiliates not to, directly or indirectly, engage in, or own or hold an interest in any person that engages in, any business that competes with any part of the Business anywhere in the United States, subject to a customary exception for passive ownership of less than 2% of the publicly traded securities of any person.

6.2 **Non-Solicitation.** For the same period, Seller would not, and would cause its affiliates not to, (a) solicit or hire any employee of the Company, subject to customary exceptions for general solicitations and for persons whose employment the Company terminated at least six months earlier, or (b) induce any distributor, customer (including any contract brewing customer), supplier or licensor of the Company to terminate or adversely modify its relationship with the Company.

6.3 **Confidentiality.** After the Closing, Seller would keep confidential all confidential information concerning the Company and the Business, including recipes, formulations, brewing processes, pricing, distributor and customer information and financial information.

6.4 **Remedies.** Seller would acknowledge that the restrictive covenants are a material inducement to Buyer, are reasonable in scope and duration, and may be enforced by injunctive relief without the posting of a bond.

## 7. Key Employee; Employees

7.1 **Head Brewer.** Buyer regards the continued involvement of Imelda Sorrentino, the Company's head brewer, as important to the value of the Business. It would be a condition to Buyer's obligation to close that Ms. Sorrentino enter into an employment agreement with the Company, effective as of the Closing, on terms acceptable to Buyer and Ms. Sorrentino, including customary confidentiality, invention assignment and restrictive covenant provisions. Buyer would expect to discuss the terms of that agreement directly with Ms. Sorrentino, with Seller's consent, following the signing of this Letter.

7.2 **Other Employees.** Buyer currently intends that the Company will continue to employ its existing workforce following the Closing on substantially comparable terms of base compensation. Buyer would expect to work with Company management on employee communications at the appropriate time. Seller would retain any liability under its own benefit plans, and the Company's participation in any Seller-sponsored plans would terminate at Closing, with appropriate transition arrangements to be agreed.

## 8. Seller-Owned Assets

8.1 **Intellectual Property.** We understand that the COPPERWAKE trademarks and related registrations, the Company's beer and hard seltzer recipes and certain related intellectual property used in the Business are owned by Seller rather than by the Company. At or before Closing, Seller would assign all of that intellectual property, together with the associated goodwill, to the Company, under an intellectual property assignment in a form acceptable to Buyer, free and clear of all encumbrances and without additional consideration.

8.2 **Canning Line.** We understand that the canning line installed at the Company's brewery is owned by Seller. At or before Closing, Seller would transfer the canning line to the Company under a bill of sale in a form acceptable to Buyer, free and clear of all encumbrances and without additional consideration.

8.3 **Affiliate Arrangements.** Except as Buyer may agree, all contracts and arrangements between the Company and Seller or its other affiliates, including any shared services, tax sharing and intercompany payable arrangements, would be terminated at or before Closing without liability to the Company.

8.4 **Transfer Taxes.** Seller would bear any transfer, sales, use and similar taxes arising from the Proposed Transaction, including from the transfers described in Sections 8.1 and 8.2.

## 9. Due Diligence

9.1 **Scope.** Buyer's proposal is subject to the satisfactory completion of Buyer's business, financial, accounting, tax, legal, regulatory, environmental, technical and quality assurance due diligence. Among the areas Buyer will want to examine in particular are:

(a) the Company's distributor agreements and depletion trends by distributor, brand and package type;

(b) the Company's contract brewing and co-packing agreements, including volumes, pricing, term, termination rights and any provisions triggered by a change of control;

(c) the Company's taproom operations and related retail and on-premises licenses;

(d) the Company's TTB brewer's notice, OLCC licenses, label and formula approvals, and excise tax filing and payment history;

(e) the Company's compliance with industrial wastewater discharge limits and pretreatment requirements of the local publicly owned treatment works, and any related notices or correspondence;

(f) the condition and remaining useful life of the brewhouse, fermentation and packaging equipment, including the canning line; and

(g) the brewery lease, including any landlord consent required in connection with the Proposed Transaction.

9.2 **Access.** Seller would cause the Company to provide Buyer and its representatives with reasonable access, during normal business hours, to the Company's facilities, books, records, contracts, personnel and advisors, and would continue to populate the virtual data room promptly in response to Buyer's diligence requests. Buyer would not contact the Company's distributors, contract brewing customers, suppliers, landlord or regulators without Seller's prior consent, which Seller would not unreasonably withhold.

9.3 **Environmental Assessment.** Buyer may engage an environmental consultant to conduct a Phase I environmental site assessment of the brewery premises and to review the Company's wastewater discharge data. Any further investigation involving sampling would require Seller's consent.

## 10. Conditions to Closing

The Closing would be subject to customary conditions for a transaction of this type, including:

(a) the execution of the Definitive Agreement and related ancillary agreements satisfactory to both parties;

(b) the accuracy of Seller's representations and warranties (in all respects for fundamental representations and in all material respects for other representations) and the performance of Seller's covenants in all material respects;

(c) the absence of any material adverse effect on the Company since the date of the Definitive Agreement;

(d) the making of all required filings under federal and state alcohol beverage laws, and the receipt of any regulatory approval required for the Company to continue operating under its alcohol permits following the change in ownership;

(e) the receipt of third-party consents to be identified in the Definitive Agreement, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer;

(f) the execution of the employment agreement with Ms. Sorrentino described in Section 7.1;

(g) the completion of the transfers described in Sections 8.1 and 8.2; and

(h) the absence of any law or order restraining or prohibiting the Proposed Transaction.

Buyer does not expect that any filing under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Antitrust Improvements Act of 1976 will be required, but the parties would confirm this with counsel before signing the Definitive Agreement.

## 11. Definitive Agreement; Timing

11.1 **Preparation.** Buyer's counsel would prepare the initial draft of the Definitive Agreement and the related ancillary agreements, including the Seller Note, the escrow agreement, the intellectual property assignment, the bill of sale and the employment agreement with Ms. Sorrentino. Seller's counsel would prepare the initial draft of the disclosure schedule.

11.2 **Timing.** The parties would use good-faith efforts to negotiate and sign the Definitive Agreement by the end of September 2026, with the Closing to occur as promptly as practicable after the satisfaction of the closing conditions, which we expect will be driven principally by the timing of the alcohol beverage regulatory filings.

11.3 **Terms of the Definitive Agreement.** The Definitive Agreement would contain the terms set out in this Letter and such other representations, warranties, covenants, conditions, indemnities and other terms as are customary for transactions of this type and are mutually acceptable to the parties, including customary interim operating covenants requiring Seller to cause the Company to operate in the ordinary course between signing and Closing.

## 12. Exclusivity

12.1 **Exclusivity Period.** In consideration of the time and expense Buyer will devote to due diligence and to the negotiation of the Definitive Agreement, from the date of Seller's countersignature of this Letter until the earlier of (a) 11:59 p.m. Pacific Time on the ninetieth (90th) day after that date and (b) the date on which Buyer notifies Seller in writing that it has abandoned the Proposed Transaction (the "**Exclusivity Period**"), Seller shall not, and shall cause the Company and their respective affiliates, directors, officers, employees, advisors and other representatives not to, directly or indirectly:

(a) solicit, initiate, knowingly encourage or facilitate any inquiry, proposal or offer from any person other than Buyer concerning any acquisition of any equity interest in the Company, any merger, consolidation or other business combination involving the Company, or any sale of any material portion of the assets of the Company outside the ordinary course of business (an "**Alternative Transaction**");

(b) participate in any discussions or negotiations with, or furnish any non-public information concerning the Company to, any person in connection with an Alternative Transaction; or

(c) enter into any letter of intent, agreement or understanding concerning an Alternative Transaction.

12.2 **Notice.** Seller shall promptly, and in any event within two (2) business days, notify Buyer of the receipt of any inquiry, proposal or offer concerning an Alternative Transaction, including the identity of the person making it and its material terms, and shall immediately terminate any existing discussions with any person other than Buyer concerning an Alternative Transaction.

12.3 **Extension.** The Exclusivity Period may be extended by mutual written agreement, including by e-mail exchange between the parties' respective chief executive officers or counsel.

## 13. Confidentiality

The existence and terms of this Letter, and the fact that the parties are in discussions concerning the Proposed Transaction, are confidential and are subject to the Mutual Confidentiality Agreement between Buyer and Seller dated May 28, 2026 (the "**Confidentiality Agreement**"), which remains in full force and effect in accordance with its terms. Neither party shall make any public announcement concerning the Proposed Transaction without the prior written consent of the other party, except as required by law, in which case the disclosing party shall, to the extent legally permitted, consult with the other party in advance as to the form and content of the disclosure.

## 14. Expenses

Each party shall bear its own costs and expenses, including the fees and expenses of its legal counsel, accountants, financial advisors and other representatives, incurred in connection with this Letter, the Proposed Transaction and the negotiation of the Definitive Agreement, whether or not the Proposed Transaction is consummated; provided that, if the Proposed Transaction is consummated, transaction expenses incurred by or on behalf of the Company would be borne by Seller as provided in Section 1.3.

## 15. Binding and Non-Binding Provisions

15.1 **Non-Binding Provisions.** Except for this Section 15 and Sections 9.2 (second sentence), 12, 13, 14, 16 and 17 (the "**Binding Provisions**"), this Letter is a non-binding expression of the parties' current intentions only. It does not constitute, and shall not be construed as, a binding agreement or offer to enter into a binding agreement, and no party shall have any obligation with respect to the Proposed Transaction unless and until the Definitive Agreement has been executed and delivered by both parties, and then only on its terms. Without limiting the foregoing, neither party shall have any liability to the other for failing to negotiate or enter into the Definitive Agreement for any reason or for no reason.

15.2 **Binding Provisions.** The Binding Provisions are legally binding on and enforceable against the parties. The Binding Provisions shall survive any termination of this Letter, except that Section 12 shall terminate at the end of the Exclusivity Period. Termination shall not relieve either party of liability for any breach of a Binding Provision occurring before termination.

15.3 **No Reliance.** Each party acknowledges that it has not relied on any statement, representation or warranty of the other party in connection with this Letter, other than the Binding Provisions, and that any representations and warranties concerning the Company or the Proposed Transaction will be made only in the Definitive Agreement.

## 16. Governing Law; Jurisdiction

This Letter, and all claims arising out of or relating to it, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the internal laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law provision or rule that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction. Any action arising out of or relating to the Binding Provisions shall be brought exclusively in the Delaware Court of Chancery (or, if that court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware or the United States District Court for the District of Delaware), and each party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of those courts. Each party acknowledges that a breach of Section 12 or 13 may cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages would not be an adequate remedy, and that the non-breaching party shall be entitled to seek injunctive relief and specific performance in addition to any other remedy available to it.

## 17. Termination; Miscellaneous

17.1 **Termination.** This Letter shall terminate on the earliest of (a) the execution of the Definitive Agreement, (b) the written agreement of the parties, and (c) the expiry of the Exclusivity Period, unless extended under Section 12.3, subject in each case to Section 15.2.

17.2 **Amendment.** This Letter may be amended only by a written instrument signed by both parties.

17.3 **Assignment.** Neither party may assign its rights or obligations under this Letter without the prior written consent of the other party, except that Buyer may assign its rights under this Letter to an affiliate of Buyer that would be the purchaser under the Definitive Agreement.

17.4 **Counterparts; Electronic Signature.** This Letter may be executed in counterparts, each of which shall be deemed an original and all of which together shall constitute one instrument. Signatures delivered by e-mail in PDF format or by electronic signature service shall be effective as delivery of an original signature.

17.5 **Expiration of Proposal.** This proposal will expire if Seller has not countersigned and returned this Letter to Buyer by 5:00 p.m. Pacific Time on July 24, 2026.
