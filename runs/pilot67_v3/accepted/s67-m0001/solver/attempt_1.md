## URGENT ITEMS REQUIRING ATTENTION BEFORE CLIENT ADVISORY

### 1. Tax Representation Survival Period Deviates from LOI

**Location:** SPA § 9.1(a) (General Survival Period); LOI § 5.2(c)

The SPA provides that all representations other than Fundamental Representations survive only 18 months post-Closing (§ 9.1(a)). The Fundamental Representations are defined in § 1.1 as only §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.5 (organization, authority, capitalization/title, and brokers). Tax representations (§ 4.12) therefore fall within the 18-month general survival period.

The LOI, at § 5.2(c), specifically provided that tax representations would survive "until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations" and stated that, "[g]iven the assessment periods applicable to federal excise taxes on beer administered by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau and to Oregon beer privilege taxes, and the possibility of extended assessment periods in the case of underreporting, Buyer regards this survival period as essential."

The Disclosure Schedule at § 4.12 contains no disclosures ("None"), meaning the tax representations are unqualified. TTB excise tax assessment periods can extend well beyond 18 months, particularly in cases of underreporting or fraud. If a TTB or OLCC tax issue surfaces after month 18 but within the applicable limitations period, Tinav would have no indemnification recourse under the SPA.

**Why it matters:** This is a material economic risk. The Company's tax compliance is central to its operations (brewer's reports, federal excise taxes, Oregon privilege taxes), and the LOI identified this survival period as "essential" to Buyer. The SPA silently drops it to 18 months.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 9.1 to add a new subsection (or expand § 9.1(b)) providing that the representations in § 4.12 (Taxes) survive until 60 days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations, consistent with the LOI. Alternatively, add § 4.12 to the definition of "Fundamental Representations" in § 1.1, though this would also remove it from the basket and cap (which may not be desired). The cleaner approach is a separate survival provision for tax reps.

### 2. Specified Matter 1 (Wastewater NOV) Recovery Capped Below Estimated Cost

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 9.2(d), Specified Matter 1, ¶ (d); SPA § 2.4; Disclosure Schedule § 9.2(d), ¶ (c)

The Disclosure Schedule provides that "[l]osses arising out of Specified Matter 1 shall be recoverable by the Buyer Indemnitees **solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund**" (Specified Matter 1, ¶ (d)). The Indemnity Escrow Amount is $2,400,000 (SPA § 2.4).

The estimated cost of the required on-site wastewater pretreatment system is $3,100,000 per the Larkspur Water Engineering report (Disclosure Schedule § 9.2(d), ¶ (c)). That figure excludes interim high-strength surcharges and any administrative penalty.

**Why it matters:** Even if the full estimated cost materializes, Tinav's maximum recovery is $2,400,000—the escrow balance. Tinav would bear at least $700,000 of the pretreatment cost out of pocket, plus all interim surcharges and any penalty. The SPA's general indemnity cap (§ 9.5(a)) does not apply to Specified Matters (§ 9.5(b)), but the Disclosure Schedule's "solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund" language overrides that by limiting the source of recovery. This effectively caps the Specified Matter 1 indemnity at $2.4M regardless of actual losses.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate deletion of the "solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund" language in Specified Matter 1, ¶ (d), or increase the Indemnity Escrow Amount to at least $3,100,000 (the engineering estimate) plus a cushion for surcharges and penalties. If Seller resists increasing the escrow, consider (a) a purchase price reduction of $700,000+ to reflect the known cost shortfall, or (b) a specific indemnity from Seller for the excess over the escrow, backed by a parent guarantee or letter of credit.

### 3. Feldspar Hollow Change-of-Control Consent Not Listed as a Required Consent

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 4.7(b)(4); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Required Consents"); SPA § 7.2(d); Disclosure Schedule § 4.4, Part A

The Feldspar Hollow Contract Brewing Agreement (a Material Contract) provides that "[a]ny assignment of the agreement by the Company, and any change of control of Copperwake, requires the prior written consent of Feldspar Hollow. Feldspar Hollow may terminate the agreement on sixty (60) days' written notice if that consent is not obtained" (Disclosure Schedule § 4.7(b)(4)). Feldspar Hollow accounted for approximately 28% of the Company's production volume for FY 2025 and H1 2026, and is the Company's second-largest customer (Disclosure Schedule § 4.17).

The definition of "Required Consents" in SPA § 1.1 is "the consents, approvals and notices listed in Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule." Section 4.4, Part A of the Disclosure Schedule lists only two items: (1) the landlord consent and (2) the City Wastewater Division approval. The Feldspar Hollow consent is **not** listed there.

Consequently, the Feldspar Hollow consent is not a "Required Consent" and is not a condition to Buyer's obligation to close under SPA § 7.2(d). While the Feldspar Hollow consent requirement is disclosed in the description of the contract at § 4.7(b)(4), and SPA § 11.13 deems disclosures relevant on their face, the operative closing condition (§ 7.2(d)) specifically references "Required Consents" as a defined term.

**Why it matters:** Tinav could close the transaction without having obtained Feldspar Hollow's consent. Post-closing, Feldspar Hollow could terminate on 60 days' notice, eliminating 28% of production volume and a major revenue stream. The SPA provides no walk-right or indemnity trigger for this specific risk because it is not a failed closing condition.

**Recommended fix:** Add the Feldspar Hollow consent to Section 4.4, Part A of the Disclosure Schedule as a Required Consent. This makes it a condition to Closing under § 7.2(d) and gives Tinav the right to walk if it is not obtained. Additionally, consider adding a specific representation that Feldspar Hollow has not indicated any objection, and a Specified Matter indemnity if the consent is conditioned on terms unacceptable to Tinav.

### 4. "Business" Definition Is Narrower Than the LOI and May Undermine the Non-Compete

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 1.1(a); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Business"); SPA § 6.6; LOI § 1.2

The SPA defines "Business" as "the business of the Company as conducted on the Closing Date, **as described in Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule**" (SPA § 1.1). Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule states: "the business of the Company is the brewing, packaging and sale of malt beverages at the Company's brewery located at 1180 Furnace Street, Marlow Bend, Oregon 97389."

The LOI's description (§ 1.2) was materially broader, encompassing: (a) craft beer under the COPPERWAKE brand; (b) hard seltzer and flavored malt beverages; (c) contract brewing, co-packing and alternating proprietorship services; and (d) taproom and on-premises retail operations.

The non-compete in SPA § 6.6(a) prohibits Seller from "engaging in the Business anywhere in the United States." Read with the narrow Disclosure Schedule definition, "the Business" may be limited to brewing, packaging, and sale of malt beverages at the Marlow Bend facility. It may not clearly encompass:
- Contract brewing or co-packing services for third parties (a significant revenue line—Feldspar Hollow alone is 28% of volume);
- Taproom and on-premises retail operations;
- Hard seltzer / flavored malt beverage production (though "malt beverages" could be argued to include these);
- Operations at the warehouse (77 Kiln Road).

**Why it matters:** If Seller (Braikal) launches or acquires a contract brewing operation or a taproom post-closing, Tinav may struggle to enforce the non-compete because those activities may fall outside the defined "Business." The LOI's broader formulation ("any business that competes with any part of the Business") was more protective.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule to track the LOI's four-part description of the Business (craft beer, hard seltzer/FMB, contract brewing/co-packing/alternating proprietorship, and taproom/retail operations). Alternatively, amend the "Business" definition in SPA § 1.1 to include "all lines of business conducted by the Company as of the Closing Date, including without limitation [the four LOI categories]."

### 7. Seller Note: Company-Level Insolvency Triggers Automatic Acceleration

**Location:** Seller Note § 6.1(d)–(e), § 7.2

The Events of Default include voluntary or involuntary insolvency of "Maker **or the Company**" (§ 6.1(d)–(e)). Upon such an Event of Default, the entire note **automatically** accelerates (§ 7.2)—no notice or declaration required.

**Why it matters:** If Copperwake Brewing encounters financial distress and files for (or is subject to) a bankruptcy proceeding, the full $8,000,000 note becomes immediately due against Tinav. This is more aggressive than typical seller note provisions, which trigger on the maker's (not the subsidiary's) insolvency. Because the Company is the primary operating asset and cash-flow source, a Company-level distress event is plausible in a downside scenario—and it would simultaneously eliminate the cash flow needed to service the note while accelerating the full balance.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate to limit the insolvency trigger to Maker-only (Tinav), or at minimum require that Company-level insolvency trigger acceleration only if it also constitutes a material adverse effect on Maker's ability to pay. If Seller resists, ensure the subordination provisions (§ 9) and payment blockage mechanics provide adequate protection, and model the liquidity impact of an $8M acceleration on Tinav's senior credit facility.

### 8. Seller Note: Onerous Monthly Reporting Covenants

**Location:** Seller Note §§ 5.1, 5.2

Maker must deliver, **within 30 days after each calendar month-end**:
- Unaudited monthly balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement of the Company, certified by CFO (§ 5.1); and
- Distributor depletion reports by distributor, brand, and package type, plus shipment data (§ 5.2).

Failure to comply is an Event of Default after a 30-day cure period (§ 6.1(b)).

**Why it matters:** Monthly certified financials plus detailed depletion reporting is a significant administrative burden for a company of this size (7 employees listed in the Disclosure Schedule with compensation over $75K). The certification requirement (CFO sign-off) adds personal liability exposure. The depletion reports give Seller (Braikal) detailed visibility into the Company's commercial performance, which is sensitive given the 5-year non-compete—Seller could use this data to identify market opportunities.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Negotiate to quarterly reporting (consistent with the interest payment frequency) or at minimum remove the certification requirement from monthly statements. (b) Add a confidentiality obligation on Seller's use of the reporting data, or limit the depletion report to aggregate volumes rather than by-distributor detail. (c) Confirm that the reporting data is subject to the SPA § 6.5 confidentiality covenant.

### 9. Intercompany Payable: "Settled or Capitalized" Is Ambiguous

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 4.19, item 4; Bill of Sale § 6.2; SPA § 2.3

The Company owes Seller an intercompany payable of $412,600 as of June 30, 2026. The Disclosure Schedule states it is "to be settled or capitalized at or before the Closing." The Bill of Sale (§ 6.2) repeats this language.

"Capitalized" is undefined. It could mean: (a) added to the purchase price (increasing what Tinav pays); (b) converted into a credit against the cash payment at Closing (reducing what Tinav pays to Seller); or (c) left as a payable of the Company to Seller post-closing (which would contradict SPA § 2.3 requiring all Indebtedness to be repaid at Closing).

**Why it matters:** If "capitalized" means the amount is added to the purchase price, Tinav pays $412,600 more than the stated $48M. If it means a credit at Closing, the net cash to Seller is reduced. The ambiguity could create a dispute at Closing.

**Recommended fix:** Clarify in the SPA or a side letter that the $412,600 intercompany payable will be (a) paid by the Company to Seller before Closing (reducing the Company's cash), or (b) credited against the cash purchase price at Closing (reducing the wire to Seller by $412,600). Option (b) is cash-neutral for Tinav and consistent with the "cash-free, debt-free" structure in LOI § 1.3.

### 10. Split Governing Law and Jurisdiction: SPA (Delaware) vs. Seller Note (New York)

**Location:** SPA § 11.8, § 11.9; Seller Note § 10.1, § 10.2; SPA § 11.12

The SPA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery. The Seller Note is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York State or federal courts in New York County. SPA § 11.12 provides that the Seller Note "shall control with respect to its own payment and enforcement terms."

**Why it matters:** A dispute involving the intersection of the SPA and the Note—for example, Tinav's exercise of the set-off right under SPA § 9.8 against a Note payment, or Seller's claim that a set-off constitutes a default under the Note—could implicate both Delaware and New York law and require litigation in two forums. The Note's § 10.3 ("Maker acknowledges that this Note is an instrument for the payment of money only") may limit equitable defenses that would be available in a Delaware court.

**Recommended fix:** If possible, negotiate to conform the Note's governing law and jurisdiction to Delaware to match the SPA. If Seller insists on New York (perhaps for its own reasons), add a provision to the SPA (or Note) addressing the interplay: specifically, that the set-off right under SPA § 9.8 is not subject to the Note's New York governing law, and that any dispute about the validity or amount of a set-off is governed by Delaware law and heard in Delaware.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 11. Key Employee Agreement: Post-Employment Non-Compete Inapplicable on Without-Cause Termination

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement § 10.2, § 10.3

The 12-month post-employment non-compete (Oregon and Washington) applies after the Termination Date **only if**: (a) the Company terminates for Cause, (b) Executive resigns without Good Reason, or (c) the Company elects to pay 50% of annual gross base salary during the Restricted Period (§ 10.3).

If Tinav terminates Sorrentino without Cause (e.g., a post-closing restructuring), the non-compete does **not** apply unless Tinav affirmatively elects to pay the 50% amount.

**Why it matters:** Sorrentino is the head brewer with access to all recipes, the CW-1 yeast strain, and brewing processes. If Tinav lets her go without Cause, she is free to join a competitor in Oregon or Washington immediately (subject only to the confidentiality and non-solicitation covenants, which do not restrict her employment).

**Recommended fix:** This is largely a function of Oregon law (ORS 653.295), which requires payment or specific termination circumstances for post-employment non-competes. The structure is legally sound. However, Tinav should: (a) be aware of this limitation in any workforce planning; (b) ensure the confidentiality (§ 8) and non-solicitation (§§ 10.4–10.5) covenants are robust (they are, and they apply regardless of termination reason); and (c) consider whether the "Good Reason" definition (§ 6.4) is appropriately narrow so that a constructive resignation doesn't inadvertently trigger the non-compete.

### 12. Key Employee Agreement: Non-Renewal Treated as Without-Cause Termination

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement § 3.2

After the 3-year Initial Term, the agreement auto-renews for successive one-year periods. "A notice of non-renewal by the Company shall be treated as a termination without Cause under Section 6.5, effective at the end of the Term then in effect."

**Why it matters:** If Tinav decides not to renew at the end of the Initial Term (or any Renewal Term), it must pay 12 months of base salary ($185,000) plus pro-rated bonus plus COBRA premiums as severance. The 90-day advance notice requirement means the decision must be made well in advance.

**Recommended fix:** This is a business decision rather than a drafting error. Tinav should factor the $185K+ severance cost into its 3-year planning horizon. If the auto-renewal is unacceptable, negotiate to make non-renewal a mutual right without severance (though this may be difficult given Sorrentino's leverage as a closing condition).

The Optiscan software license is particularly important—it's the fill-level inspection system, a quality-critical component.

(b) If Seller cannot obtain all consents pre-Closing, add a specific covenant with a defined timeline (e.g., 30 days) and a remedy if consent is wrongfully withheld by the counterparty. (c) Confirm that the Optiscan license is transferable and that the license key will be re-issued to the Company.

### 14. Business Day Definition Inconsistency Between SPA and Seller Note

**Location:** SPA § 1.1; Seller Note § 1.2

The SPA defines "Business Day" by reference to banks in **Wilmington, Delaware or Portland, Oregon**. The Seller Note defines "Business Day" by reference to banks in **New York, New York**.

**Why it matters:** In practice, the differences are minimal (all three locations observe the same federal bank holidays). However, if a payment date under the Note falls on a day that is a Business Day in New York but not in Delaware/Oregon (or vice versa), the SPA and Note could produce different results. This is unlikely but theoretically possible if a local observance differs.

**Recommended fix:** Conform the Seller Note's "Business Day" definition to the SPA's (Wilmington/Portland), or add a proviso that for purposes of payment dates under the Note, "Business Day" means a day that is a Business Day under both the SPA and the Note definitions. Low priority.

### 15. Key Employee Agreement: Salary Increase vs. Interim Operating Covenants

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement § 4.1; Disclosure Schedule § 4.13(a); SPA § 6.1(e)

The Disclosure Schedule lists Sorrentino's current annual base compensation as **$168,000**. The Key Employee Agreement provides for a base salary of **$185,000**—a $17,000 (approximately 10%) increase effective at Closing.

SPA § 6.1(e) prohibits the Company from "increas[ing] the compensation or benefits of any employee other than in the ordinary course consistent with past practice" between signing and Closing.

**Why it matters:** While the Key Employee Agreement is an Ancillary Agreement required by the SPA (making the increase arguably "contemplated" by the transaction), a strict reading of § 6.1(e) could be invoked by Seller to argue the increase violates the interim covenants. This is unlikely to be a practical issue (Seller drafted the requirement), but it creates a technical inconsistency.

**Recommended fix:** Add an express carve-out in SPA § 6.1(e) for compensation changes made under the Key Employee Agreement or other Ancillary Agreements. Alternatively, note in the Disclosure Schedule that the salary increase is a transaction-related adjustment.

### 17. Domain Name Transfer: 5-Day Post-Closing Gap

**Location:** IP Assignment § 3.3

The copperwake.com and copperwakebrewing.com domain names are transferred within **5 Business Days after** the Closing Date. During that period, Seller retains registrar control.

**Why it matters:** In a 5-day window, Seller could theoretically (though contractually prohibited) alter DNS records, redirect traffic, or allow the domains to lapse. If the registrar process requires more time, have Seller provide the authorization/EPP codes at Closing so the Company can initiate the transfer immediately.
