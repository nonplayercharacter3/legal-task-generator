## URGENT ITEMS – REQUIRES ACTION BEFORE CLIENT ADVICE

### 1. Tax Representation Survival Period Does Not Match the LOI

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 9.1(a) (General Survival Period); Letter of Intent § 5.2(c)

**Issue:** The LOI expressly provided that tax representations would survive "until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations" and stated that "Buyer regards this survival period as essential" given TTB excise tax assessment periods and Oregon beer privilege tax exposure. In the SPA, however, tax representations (Section 4.12) are not Fundamental Representations (defined in § 1.1 as only §§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.5). They therefore fall under the General Survival Period of 18 months (§ 9.1(a)). Section 9.1(c) extends survival to 60 days after the statute of limitations only for claims under §§ 9.2(c) (Indebtedness/Transaction Expenses) and 9.2(d) (Specified Matters), not for tax representation breaches under § 9.2(a).

**Why it matters:** For a brewery, federal excise tax assessment periods can extend beyond 18 months, particularly in cases of underreporting on brewer's reports of operations. If a TTB or Oregon DOR assessment arises after month 18, Tinav would have no indemnity claim against Seller despite the LOI treating this protection as essential.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 9.1(a) or add a new § 9.1(e) to provide that representations in Section 4.12 (Taxes) survive until 60 days after expiration of the applicable statute of limitations, consistent with the LOI. Alternatively, add tax matters to the list in § 9.1(c).

### 2. Specified Matter 1 (Wastewater NOV) Recovery Capped at Escrow – Conflicts with SPA Indemnity Structure

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 9.2(d), Specified Matter 1, paragraph (d); Stock Purchase Agreement § 9.5(b); Letter of Intent § 5.4(c)

**Issue:** Specified Matter 1(d) in the Disclosure Schedule states: "Losses arising out of Specified Matter 1 shall be recoverable by the Buyer Indemnitees solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund." The Indemnity Escrow Amount is $2,400,000 (SPA § 2.4). The Company's own engineering consultant estimates the pretreatment system cost at $3,100,000 (Disclosure Schedule § 9.2(d)(c)), excluding interim surcharges and any administrative penalty.

This "solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund" language directly conflicts with SPA § 9.5(b), which provides that the 10% cap "shall not apply to Losses arising out of the Specified Matters," and with the LOI § 5.4(c), which contemplated that Specified Matter claims would be capped at the full Purchase Price ($48,000,000), not at the escrow amount. The "solely" language also eliminates Tinav's right to pursue Seller directly or exercise the set-off right under SPA § 9.8 against the Seller Note.

**Why it matters:** Tinav faces a known $3.1M+ cost (plus surcharges and potential penalties) for a disclosed environmental violation, but its contractual recovery is limited to $2.4M. The $700K+ gap is borne entirely by Tinav. If interim surcharges or an administrative penalty are assessed, the shortfall grows.

**Recommended fix:** Delete or revise Specified Matter 1(d) to remove the "solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund" limitation. The escrow should remain as a source of payment (per SPA § 9.6(a)), but Seller should remain directly liable for amounts exceeding the escrow, subject to the Purchase Price cap in § 9.5(b). If Seller resists, consider increasing the Indemnity Escrow Amount to at least $3,100,000 or negotiating a specific price reduction.

### 3. Feldspar Hollow Change-of-Control Consent Is Not a Required Consent or Closing Condition

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 4.7(b)(4); Stock Purchase Agreement §§ 1.1 ("Required Consents"), 4.4, 6.3, 7.2(d); Disclosure Schedule § 4.4 (Part A)

**Issue:** The Feldspar Hollow Contract Brewing Agreement (approximately 28% of the Company's production volume for FY2025 and H1 2026) requires Feldspar Hollow's prior written consent for any change of control of Copperwake, and Feldspar Hollow may terminate on 60 days' notice if consent is not obtained (Disclosure Schedule § 4.7(b)(4)). However, "Required Consents" is defined in SPA § 1.1 as "the consents, approvals and notices listed in Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule." Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule lists only (1) the Furnace Street Holdings landlord consent and (2) the City Wastewater Division approval. Feldspar Hollow's consent is not listed there.

Consequently:
- Feldspar Hollow's consent is not a condition to Buyer's closing obligation (SPA § 7.2(d));
- Seller has no affirmative covenant to obtain it (SPA § 6.3 applies only to "Required Consents"); and
- Because the consent requirement is disclosed in § 4.7(b)(4) and cross-referenced to § 4.4 via SPA § 11.13 (deemed disclosure), Seller's representation in SPA § 4.4(c) is effectively qualified, so failure to obtain the consent is not a representation breach giving rise to indemnity.

**Why it matters:** If Feldspar Hollow withholds consent and terminates, Tinav loses 28% of production volume post-closing with no contractual remedy against Seller. The LOI § 10(e) contemplated "the receipt of third-party consents to be identified in the Definitive Agreement, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer," suggesting this consent was meant to be a closing condition.

**Recommended fix:** Add Feldspar Hollow's consent to Part A of Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule so it becomes a Required Consent and a closing condition under SPA § 7.2(d). Alternatively, add a specific condition to closing in SPA § 7.2 requiring Feldspar Hollow's consent, or a covenant in § 6.3 requiring Seller to use best efforts to obtain it. If consent is not obtained before closing, consider a specific indemnity or price adjustment.

## SIGNIFICANT ITEMS

### 4. Business Day Definition Mismatch Between SPA and Seller Note

**Location:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 1.1 ("Business Day" – Wilmington, DE and Portland, OR); Seller Note § 1.2 ("Business Day" – New York, NY)

**Issue:** The SPA defines Business Day by reference to banks in Wilmington, Delaware or Portland, Oregon. The Seller Note defines Business Day by reference to banks in New York, New York. While SPA § 11.12 provides that the Seller Note "shall control with respect to its own payment and enforcement terms," the mismatch creates practical confusion: a payment date that is a Business Day under the SPA (e.g., for calculating the Outside Date, closing timing, or notice periods) may not be a Business Day under the Note (e.g., for calculating interest payment dates, prepayment notice periods, or default cure periods), or vice versa.

**Why it matters:** In a dispute over whether a payment was timely, a notice was given within the required period, or a default has occurred, the parties could argue different Business Day definitions apply. The 5-Business-Day prepayment notice (Note § 4.1), the 5-Business-Day interest payment grace period (Note § 6.1(a)(ii)), and the 30-day reporting cure period (Note § 6.1(b)) all turn on the Note's Business Day definition, while the SPA's set-off and escrow release mechanics (SPA §§ 9.6, 9.8) use the SPA's definition.

**Recommended fix:** Conform the Business Day definition in Seller Note § 1.2 to match the SPA (Wilmington, DE and Portland, OR), or at minimum add Portland, OR to the Note's definition. Given that the Company operates in Oregon and the SPA is governed by Delaware law, the SPA definition is more appropriate.

### 5. D&O, EPLI, and Cyber Insurance Coverage Ceases at Closing with No Tail or Replacement Arranged

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 4.16 (last paragraph); Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 9.2; Key Employee Agreement § 14

**Issue:** The Company is an additional named insured under Braikal's master D&O, EPLI, and cyber liability policies. Coverage under those policies ceases at Closing (Disclosure Schedule § 4.16). The insurance broker's letter states that "coverage for a subsidiary that ceases to be a subsidiary generally continues only for wrongful acts or incidents occurring before the date the entity ceases to be a subsidiary, subject to each policy's run-off provisions" and advises that "Tinav should confirm the run-off terms directly with Braikal's broker" (Insurance Letter § 9.2). No transaction document addresses:
- Whether Seller will purchase a "tail" (extended reporting period) under the D&O policy;
- What the actual run-off terms are for EPLI and cyber; or
- Whether Tinav will arrange replacement coverage before or at closing.

The Key Employee Agreement § 14 requires the Company to maintain D&O insurance covering the Executive, creating an immediate post-closing obligation with no policy in place.

**Why it matters:** Without confirming run-off terms, Tinav may discover post-closing that pre-closing acts (e.g., employment decisions, data security incidents, or director conduct) are not covered. The EPLI gap is particularly concerning given the Key Employee Agreement's severance and restrictive covenant provisions, which create employment-related litigation risk. The cyber gap is material for a company with proprietary recipes, customer data, and financial systems.

**Recommended fix:** Before closing: (a) obtain and review the run-off provisions of Braikal's master D&O, EPLI, and cyber policies; (b) require Seller to purchase a 6-year D&O tail (matching the SPA § 6.13 indemnification period) at Seller's expense, or negotiate a specific indemnity for pre-closing acts not covered; (c) arrange replacement EPLI and cyber coverage for the Company effective at closing (the broker has already gathered the necessary information per Insurance Letter § 9.3 and needs 30 days' lead time).

### 6. Workers' Compensation Policy Expires January 1, 2027 – Potential Coverage Gap

**Location:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter §§ 2.2, 7.3; Stock Purchase Agreement § 10.1(d) (Outside Date: January 29, 2027)

**Issue:** The SAIF workers' compensation policy expires January 1, 2027. The broker states it will "bind the renewal on the Company's instructions before expiration" (Insurance Letter § 7.3). However, the SPA Outside Date is January 29, 2027. If closing has not occurred by January 1, 2027, the Company's workers' compensation coverage lapses. The broker notes that "binding the renewal before the closing will not affect any coverage after the closing," but this requires someone to give instructions before January 1.

**Why it matters:** A gap in workers' compensation coverage would expose the Company (and thus Tinav post-closing) to uninsured employer liability for any workplace injury occurring between January 1 and the actual closing date. It could also violate the SPA representation in § 4.16 that all policies are "in full force and effect."

**Recommended fix:** Confirm that the SAIF renewal is bound before January 1, 2027. If closing is expected to occur after that date, ensure Seller (or the Company's management, acting at Seller's direction under SPA § 6.1) gives the broker instructions to bind the renewal. Add a closing condition or covenant requiring the Company to maintain all insurance policies in force through the closing date.

### 7. Key Employee Non-Compete: Tight 10-Day Election Window and Without-Cause Gap

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement §§ 10.2, 10.3; Letter of Intent § 7.1

**Issue:** The non-compete in § 10.2 (12 months, Oregon and Washington, brewing leadership roles) applies after a without-Cause termination only if the Company elects, "by written notice to Executive within ten (10) days after the Termination Date," to pay 50% of her annual gross base salary (or 50% of the ORS 653.295 threshold, if greater) during the Restricted Period (§ 10.3(c)). At her $185,000 base salary, this costs approximately $92,500.

The LOI identifies Imelda Sorrentino's continued involvement as important to the value of the Business, and her departure (voluntary or involuntary) would be a significant operational risk. If Tinav terminates her without Cause and misses the 10-day election window, she is free to join a competitor immediately.

**Why it matters:** The 10-day window is short and creates a trap: Tinav may not immediately recognize the competitive risk of a without-Cause termination (e.g., a cost-cutting decision) and could lose the non-compete protection by missing the deadline. Additionally, if she resigns for Good Reason (e.g., a "material diminution in duties" under § 6.4(b)), the non-compete does not apply at all because § 10.3 requires either Cause termination, resignation without Good Reason, or the payment election.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Calendar the 10-day election deadline as a critical post-termination action item; (b) consider whether the Good Reason definition in § 6.4 is too broad (particularly clause (b), which could be triggered by a reorganization that shifts her reporting line or reduces her involvement in recipe development); (c) ensure that any post-closing reorganization of brewing operations is done with awareness of the Good Reason trigger.

## MODERATE ITEMS

### 8. Intercompany Payable – Confirm Treatment at Closing

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule § 4.19(4); Stock Purchase Agreement § 2.3; SPA § 1.1 ("Indebtedness")

**Issue:** The Company owes Seller an intercompany payable of $412,600 as of June 30, 2026. The Disclosure Schedule states it is "to be settled or capitalized at or before the Closing." SPA § 2.3 requires Seller to cause "all Indebtedness of the Company" to be repaid at or before Closing. Whether this intercompany payable constitutes "Indebtedness" under the SPA definition depends on its characterization: if it represents amounts owed for services under the Shared Services Agreement or use of the canning line, it may be an "obligation for the deferred purchase price of property or services" (within the definition); if it is a trade payable in the ordinary course, it is excluded.

**Why it matters:** If the payable is not "Indebtedness," § 2.3 does not require it to be paid at closing, and the Company (now owned by Tinav) would owe $412,600 to Seller post-closing. The word "capitalized" in the Disclosure Schedule is ambiguous – it could mean converted to equity (which would be unusual and potentially problematic) or simply settled.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm with Seller that the $412,600 intercompany payable will be paid in full at or before closing as part of the cash-free, debt-free structure. If there is any dispute, add it to the Specified Matters or require a specific payoff letter as a closing deliverable under § 3.2(e).

### 9. IP Assignment – PLM System Export Certification Is 30 Days Post-Closing

**Location:** IP Assignment Agreement § 3.1

**Issue:** Seller must export all records from the Braikal PLM System and "certify in writing to Assignee, within thirty (30) days after the Closing Date, that the export is complete." Until that certification is delivered, Tinav has no contractual confirmation that all recipes, formulations, batch records, lab results, and change logs have been transferred.

**Why it matters:** The recipes and know-how are core to the Business value (LOI § 1.2). If the export is incomplete or corrupted, Tinav may not discover the gap until after the 30-day certification period. While the further assurances provision (§ 8.1) and the SPA representations (§ 4.10) provide theoretical remedies, the practical difficulty of proving what was in the PLM System versus what was exported could be significant.

**Recommended fix:** Consider requiring the export to be completed and verified before the Effective Time (i.e., at closing), with the 30-day certification serving as a backstop rather than the primary delivery mechanism. Alternatively, require a pre-closing data migration test or a third-party verification of the export completeness.

## MINOR / HOUSEKEEPING ITEMS

### 11. Seller Note Amortization Timing – Slight Deviation from LOI

**Location:** Seller Note § 3.1(a); Letter of Intent § 3.1(c)

**Issue:** The LOI states "interest only for the first two years, followed by quarterly principal installments." The Note provides that the first principal installment is due "on the Interest Payment Date occurring in the last calendar quarter in which the second (2nd) anniversary of the Issue Date falls." Depending on when in the quarter the Issue Date falls, the first principal payment could be up to approximately 3 months after the second anniversary. For example, if the Issue Date is October 2, 2026, the second anniversary is October 2, 2028 (Q4), and the first principal payment is December 31, 2028 – about 14 weeks after the second anniversary.

**Why it matters:** Minimal. The LOI is non-binding, and the deviation slightly favors Tinav (more interest-only period). No action required, but be aware for cash flow modeling.
