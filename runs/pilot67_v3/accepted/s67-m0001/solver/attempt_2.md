## I. ISSUES REQUIRING URGENT ATTENTION BEFORE CLIENT ADVISE

### 1. Specified Matter 1 (Wastewater NOV): Recovery Capped at Escrow, Which Is $700,000+ Short of Estimated Cost

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 9.2(d), Specified Matter 1, paragraph (d); SPA Section 2.4; SPA Section 9.5(b).

**Issue.** The Disclosure Schedule provides that "Losses arising out of Specified Matter 1 shall be recoverable by the Buyer Indemnitees **solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund**." The estimated cost of the required pretreatment system is $3,100,000 (Larkspur Water Engineering report, July 28, 2026, referenced in Specified Matter 1(c)), excluding interim surcharges and any administrative penalty. The Indemnity Escrow Fund is $2,400,000 (SPA Section 2.4). Even a full recovery from the escrow leaves Tinav with a shortfall of at least $700,000, plus unquantified surcharges and penalties, with no recourse against Seller.

Although SPA Section 9.5(b) carves Specified Matters out of the general $4,800,000 cap, the Disclosure Schedule's "solely from" language overrides that benefit by restricting the *source* of recovery to the escrow. Because the Disclosure Schedule is incorporated into the SPA by Section 11.13, this limitation is contractually binding.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** Tinav would bear the economic risk of a known, quantified $3.1M+ environmental compliance obligation with only $2.4M of security. The BOD exceedances (up to 3,850 mg/L against a 1,200 mg/L limit) are severe, and the compliance schedule is not yet finalized (see Issue 7 below), so the actual cost could exceed the current estimate.

**Recommended fix.** Before advising the client to proceed to Closing, negotiate one or more of the following:
- Delete "solely from the Indemnity Escrow Fund" and replace with language permitting recovery from Seller generally (subject to the super-cap in Section 9.5(b) for Fundamental Reps, or uncapped for Specified Matters as the LOI contemplated);
- Increase the Indemnity Escrow Amount to at least $3,500,000 to cover the estimate plus a cushion for surcharges; or
- Add a specific covenant requiring Seller to fund any amount by which the actual cost of the pretreatment system exceeds $2,400,000.

### 2. Feldspar Hollow Change-of-Control Consent Not Listed as a Required Consent

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.7(b)(4); SPA Section 1.1 (definition of "Required Consents"); SPA Section 7.2(d).

**Issue.** The Disclosure Schedule discloses that Feldspar Hollow Brewing Co. accounted for approximately **28% of the Company's production volume** for fiscal 2025 and the six months ended June 30, 2026. Section 14.3 of the Contract Brewing Agreement requires Feldspar Hollow's prior written consent for any change of control of Copperwake, and Feldspar Hollow **may terminate on 60 days' notice** if consent is not obtained.

However, the Feldspar Hollow consent is **not listed in Section 4.4, Part A** of the Disclosure Schedule. The SPA defines "Required Consents" as "the consents, approvals and notices listed in Section 4.4 of the Disclosure Schedule" (SPA Section 1.1). SPA Section 7.2(d) makes receipt of all Required Consents a condition to Buyer's closing obligation. Because the Feldspar Hollow consent is not in Section 4.4, it is not a Required Consent, and Tinav **cannot walk away from the deal** if Feldspar Hollow withholds consent or terminates.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** Losing Feldspar Hollow would eliminate roughly 28% of production volume overnight. The Business definition in the LOI (Section 1.2(c)) expressly includes contract brewing as a core line, and the valuation (LOI Section 2.2) assumes continued operations. A post-closing termination would devastate revenue and could trigger a Material Adverse Effect, but by then Tinav would have paid $48M and have limited remedies (the 18-month survival period and the $4.8M cap would apply, and the loss might be characterized as a business risk rather than a rep breach).

**Recommended fix.** Add the Feldspar Hollow consent to Section 4.4, Part A of the Disclosure Schedule as a Required Consent. This makes it a closing condition under Section 7.2(d) and gives Tinav the right to terminate if it is not obtained. Begin the consent request immediately, as Feldspar Hollow's cooperation is not guaranteed and the 60-day termination notice period means a post-closing loss could occur before Tinav can react.

### 3. Tax Representations Have Only 18-Month Survival, Contrary to the LOI

**Location:** SPA Section 9.1(a); LOI Section 5.2(c).

**Issue.** The LOI expressly provides that tax representations survive "until 60 days after expiry of the applicable statute of limitations" and states that "Buyer regards this survival period as **essential**" given TTB excise tax assessment periods and Oregon beer privilege tax rules. In the SPA, however, the tax representations (Section 4.12) are not Fundamental Representations (which are limited to Sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, and 4.5 per SPA Section 1.1). They therefore fall under the General Survival Period of **18 months** (SPA Section 9.1(a)).

SPA Section 9.1(c) extends survival to 60 days after the statute of limitations only for claims under Sections 9.2(c) (Indebtedness/Transaction Expenses) and 9.2(d) (Specified Matters). Tax rep breaches fall under Section 9.2(a) and are subject to the 18-month period.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** TTB excise tax assessment periods can extend beyond 18 months, particularly in cases of underreporting or fraud (where the period is indefinite). Oregon beer privilege tax may have its own assessment window. If a tax deficiency for a pre-closing period is assessed 19 months after Closing, Tinav would have no indemnification recourse against Seller despite the LOI's explicit treatment of this as essential. The 18-month window also means Tinav must identify and assert any tax claim before the expiration of the General Survival Period.

**Recommended fix.** Amend SPA Section 9.1 to add a new subsection providing that the representations in Section 4.12 (Taxes) survive until 60 days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations, consistent with LOI Section 5.2(c). This should be raised as a priority drafting correction.

### 4. Definition of "Business" in the Disclosure Schedule Is Materially Narrower Than the LOI

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 1.1(a); SPA Section 1.1 (definition of "Business"); LOI Section 1.2.

**Issue.** The SPA defines "Business" as "the business of the Company as conducted on the Closing Date, **as described in Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule**." Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule states: "the brewing, packaging and sale of malt beverages at the Company's brewery located at 1180 Furnace Street, Marlow Bend, Oregon 97389."

The LOI (Section 1.2) describes the Business as including four distinct lines:
- (a) craft beer under the COPPERWAKE brand (draft, can, bottle);
- (b) hard seltzer and flavored malt beverages;
- (c) **contract brewing, co-packing and alternating proprietorship services**; and
- (d) **taproom and on-premises retail operations**, including food service and merchandise.

The Disclosure Schedule's description omits hard seltzer/FMB, contract brewing, co-packing, alternating proprietorship, and taproom/retail operations. It also limits the Business to a single physical location.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** The definition of "Business" feeds into:
- The non-compete (SPA Section 6.6): Seller is restricted from engaging in "the Business" in the United States for 5 years. If "the Business" is only "brewing, packaging and sale of malt beverages at 1180 Furnace Street," Seller could argue it is free to operate a contract brewing facility elsewhere, open a taproom in another Oregon city, or launch a hard seltzer brand.
- The non-solicitation (SPA Section 6.7): restricted to persons with "a material business relationship with the Company" in connection with "the Business."
- The sufficiency of assets rep (SPA Section 4.9(c)): assets must be sufficient to conduct "the Business."
- The MAE definition (SPA Section 1.1): events materially adverse to "the business... of the Company."

A narrow definition undermines the protective covenants and key representations.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Section 1.1(a) of the Disclosure Schedule to describe the Business with the same breadth as LOI Section 1.2, expressly including all four lines of business (craft beer, hard seltzer/FMB, contract brewing/co-packing/alternating proprietorship, and taproom/retail) and the related distribution relationships. This is a critical correction that should be made before the SPA is finalized.

### 5. D&O, EPLI, and Cyber Coverage Ceases at Closing with No Confirmed Replacement

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.16 (final paragraph); Insurance Certificate, Sections 9.1–9.3.

**Issue.** The Company is currently an additional named insured under Braikal's master D&O, EPLI, and cyber liability policies. The Disclosure Schedule states that "Coverage for the Company under those policies will cease at the Closing." The insurance broker (Whitcombe & Lark) confirms this and notes that coverage for a subsidiary that ceases to be a subsidiary "generally continues only for wrongful acts or incidents occurring before the date the entity ceases to be a subsidiary, subject to each policy's run-off provisions." The broker recommends that "Tinav should confirm the run-off terms directly with Braikal's broker" and states that it would need "at least **thirty days** before the closing to obtain terms from the markets" for replacement coverage.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** At Closing, the Company will have no D&O, EPLI, or cyber coverage unless Tinav has arranged a replacement. The 30-day lead time means this work must begin immediately. Additionally, the run-off terms for pre-closing acts are unconfirmed—if they are narrow or nonexistent, former officers and employees (including Sorrentino, whose agreement contemplates D&O indemnification under Section 14) could be exposed, creating retention and litigation risk.

**Recommended fix.**
- (a) Instruct Whitcombe & Lark to begin quoting standalone D&O, EPLI, and cyber coverage for the Company immediately (the broker has already gathered the census, revenue, and security questionnaire data per Section 9.3).
- (b) Request that Braikal's broker provide the specific run-off terms for the master policies so Tinav can assess the gap for pre-closing acts.
- (c) Consider requiring, as a condition to Closing or a post-closing covenant, that Seller purchase a "tail" policy for D&O coverage for pre-closing acts at its own expense (customary in M&A).

### 6. Shared Services Agreement Terminates at Closing Without a Transition Services Agreement in Place

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.19, item 2; SPA Section 6.11.

**Issue.** The Shared Services Agreement (dated March 4, 2019) covers "payroll, human resources, information technology, treasury and insurance procurement services." It terminates at Closing per SPA Section 6.11 and the Disclosure Schedule. The Disclosure Schedule notes: "Transition support, if any, is to be provided under a separate agreement **to be negotiated** by the parties." No transition services agreement is listed as a closing deliverable in SPA Section 3.2 or 3.3, and none is included in the folder.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** On the Closing Date, the Company loses its payroll processing, HR administration, IT infrastructure, and treasury management overnight. If no transition arrangement is in place, the Company could face immediate operational disruption—payroll failures, IT outages, inability to process payments or receive deposits. This is particularly acute because the Company has 7+ employees with compensation above $75,000 (Disclosure Schedule Section 4.13(a)) and complex brewing operations dependent on IT systems (PLM, Ekos, POS).

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate a transition services agreement (TSA) with Seller before Closing, covering at minimum payroll, IT, and treasury functions for a period of 60–90 days post-closing. Make execution of the TSA a closing deliverable under SPA Section 3.2. If Seller resists, ensure Tinav has internal capacity or a third-party provider ready to assume these functions on Day 1.

### 7. Wastewater Compliance Schedule Not Finalized; Tinav Would Assume Unfinalized Obligations

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 9.2(d), Specified Matter 1(b); Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.4, Part A, item 2.

**Issue.** The City Wastewater Division's proposed compliance schedule requires the Company to "design, permit, install and commission an on-site wastewater pretreatment system capable of consistently meeting the BOD local limit by June 30, 2027." The schedule "has not yet been finalized." Separately, the Discharge Permit (Section 7.3) requires the incoming owner to "deliver a written agreement to assume the permit conditions, **including the compliance schedule** described in Section 9.2(d)."

**Why it matters for Tinav.** Tinav would be required to assume a compliance obligation whose final terms (timeline, technical specifications, surcharge amounts, penalty exposure) are not yet agreed. The $3.1M estimate is based on a preliminary design narrative (June 19, 2026 response). If the finalized schedule imposes a tighter deadline, higher specifications, or additional penalties, the cost could increase materially. The June 30, 2027 commissioning deadline is less than 5 months after the earliest realistic Closing date, creating significant execution risk.

**Recommended fix.**
- (a) Seek to finalize the compliance schedule with the City Wastewater Division before Closing, or at minimum obtain a written confirmation of the key parameters (deadline, BOD limit, surcharge rates).
- (b) If finalization is not possible before Closing, negotiate a provision in the Specified Matter 1 indemnity that covers any cost increase resulting from the finalization of the schedule (i.e., the indemnity should be based on actual cost, not the current estimate, and should not be limited to the escrow—see Issue 1).
- (c) Assess whether the June 30, 2027 deadline is achievable given permitting timelines, and if not, request an extension from the City before assuming the obligation.

### 9. Seller Note Governed by New York Law; SPA Governed by Delaware Law

**Location:** Seller Note, Section 10.1–10.2; SPA, Sections 11.8–11.9.

**Issue.** The Seller Note is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York state or federal courts (Southern District of New York). The SPA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery. SPA Section 11.12 provides that the Seller Note "shall control with respect to its own payment and enforcement terms," but this does not resolve the practical issue of split jurisdiction.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** The SPA's set-off provision (Section 9.8) allows Tinav to withhold or set off indemnification claims against Seller Note payments. If Seller disputes a set-off and files a payment demand in New York, while Tinav asserts the set-off as a defense in a Delaware indemnification action, the parties could be litigating in two forums under two bodies of law. The New York forum also means Tinav (a Delaware corporation with operations in Oregon) would need to defend in New York.

**Recommended fix.** If possible, amend the Seller Note to align governing law and jurisdiction with the SPA (Delaware). If Seller resists (it may prefer New York as a neutral commercial law jurisdiction), ensure that SPA Section 9.8 explicitly provides that any dispute over the validity or amount of a set-off shall be resolved in the Delaware courts as part of the underlying indemnification claim, and that Seller's right to demand payment in New York is subject to the pending set-off.

### 10. Intercompany Payable of $412,600: Ambiguous Treatment at Closing

**Location:** Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.19, item 4; SPA Section 2.3.

**Issue.** The Company owes Seller an intercompany payable of $412,600 (as of June 30, 2026). The Disclosure Schedule states it is "to be settled or capitalized at or before the Closing." The SPA Section 2.3 requires Seller to cause all "Indebtedness" to be repaid at or before Closing. Whether this intercompany payable constitutes "Indebtedness" under the SPA definition is unclear—the definition includes "obligations for the deferred purchase price of property or services (other than trade payables incurred in the ordinary course of business)," which could encompass a shared services payable.

"Capitalized" is not defined in the SPA and is ambiguous in this context (Seller is selling 100% of the equity, so there is no equity to capitalize into).

**Why it matters for Tinav.** If the payable is not "Indebtedness" under Section 2.3, Seller has no affirmative obligation to cause it to be paid before Closing. If it remains on the Company's balance sheet post-closing, the Company (now Tinav's subsidiary) would owe $412,600 to Seller—an affiliate of the Seller Note holder—creating an ongoing financial entanglement and potential set-off complications.

**Recommended fix.** Add an express provision (in the SPA or a side letter) requiring Seller to cause the Company to pay the $412,600 intercompany payable in full before Closing, or requiring Seller to forgive it. The cleanest approach is payment before Closing, consistent with the cash-free, debt-free basis contemplated by the LOI (Section 1.3).

### 12. Key Employee Agreement: Non-Compete Requires Affirmative Election and Payment

**Location:** Key Employee Agreement, Section 10.3.

**Issue.** The post-termination non-compete (Section 10.2) applies only if the Company "otherwise elects, by written notice to Executive within ten (10) days after the Termination Date, to pay Executive during the Restricted Period... the greater of fifty percent (50%) of her annual gross base salary... and fifty percent (50%) of the threshold amount then in effect under ORS 653.295." This is a requirement of Oregon law (ORS 653.295(1)(e)) for non-competes against employees.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** If the Company terminates Sorrentino without Cause (triggering 12 months of severance under Section 7.2), the non-compete will **not** apply unless the Company makes a separate, affirmative written election within 10 days and pays the required amount during the 12-month restriction period. This is an easy step to miss in the heat of a termination. At $185,000 base salary, the payment obligation would be approximately $92,500 (50% of base) for 12 months, on top of the 12 months of severance already owed.

**Recommended fix.** Implement an internal protocol requiring that any termination of Sorrentino trigger an immediate review of whether the Section 10.3(c) election should be made, with a hard deadline 5 days after the Termination Date to allow for the written notice within the 10-day window. Budget for the additional ~$92,500 cost if the election is made.

### 13. Outside Date May Be Insufficient for Regulatory Timelines

**Location:** SPA Section 10.1(d); LOI Section 11.2; Disclosure Schedule, Section 4.4, Part B.

**Issue.** The Outside Date is January 29, 2027—approximately 119 days (3.5 months) from the SPA signing date of October 2, 2026. The LOI anticipated that Closing would be "driven principally by the timing of the alcohol beverage regulatory filings." Required filings include:
- TTB amended Brewer's Notice (27 C.F.R. Part 25);
- OLCC change-of-ownership application with personal history materials;
- Washington State Liquor and Cannabis Board notice;
- Idaho State Police ABC notice.

TTB change-of-ownership processing can take 60–90 days or longer if additional information is requested. OLCC processing times vary. If any filing is incomplete or requires supplementation, the timeline could exceed 3.5 months.

**Why it matters for Tinav.** If the Outside Date passes before all regulatory approvals are obtained, either party can terminate (SPA Section 10.1(d)). While Tinav would not want to terminate, the risk is that Seller could use a regulatory delay as leverage to renegotiate terms, or that the deal simply breaks.

**Recommended fix.** Monitor the regulatory filing status weekly. If TTB or OLCC processing is not complete by mid-December 2026, begin discussions with Seller about a mutual extension of the Outside Date (e.g., to March 31, 2027). The landlord consent remains valid through April 30, 2027 (Landlord Consent Section 2.4), so an extension to March 31 would not require a new landlord consent.

### 14. Bill of Sale: Line Contracts Requiring Third-Party Consent

**Location:** Bill of Sale, Section 5.2; Bill of Sale, Schedule 1, Part C (not reproduced in folder).

**Issue.** Section 5.2 of the Bill of Sale provides that if any Line Contract (warranty, service, support, or software license for the canning line) cannot be assigned without third-party consent, and consent has not been obtained by the Effective Time, the assignment is ineffective. Seller must then use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain consent post-Closing, and in the interim holds the contract for the Company's benefit.

If a key warranty or service contract (e.g., with the line's manufacturer or integrator) cannot be assigned, the Company could lose warranty coverage or service support for a major asset. The interim "hold for benefit" arrangement is less protective than a direct assignment because the Company cannot enforce the contract in its own name.

**Recommended fix.** Review Schedule 1, Part C of the Bill of Sale (not in the folder—request it) to identify which Line Contracts require third-party consent. Seek those consents before Closing where possible. For any that cannot be obtained pre-Closing, negotiate a specific post-closing covenant with a defined timeline (e.g., 30 days) and a remedy if consent is refused.

### 15. Seller Note: Event of Default Triggered by Senior Indebtedness Acceleration Above $5M

**Location:** Seller Note, Section 6.1(f); LOI Section 2.3.

**Issue.** The Seller Note provides an Event of Default if "Any Senior Indebtedness in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $5,000,000 is accelerated and declared due and payable before its stated maturity, and the acceleration is not rescinded within thirty (30) days." The LOI states that Buyer intends to fund the cash portion "from cash on hand and borrowings under its existing senior credit facility."

**Why it matters for Tinav.** If Tinav's existing senior credit facility exceeds $5M (likely, given it is funding $37.6M of cash consideration), a technical default under that facility (e.g., a financial covenant breach) that triggers acceleration would simultaneously constitute an Event of Default under the Seller Note, allowing Seller to accelerate the $8M note. This creates a cross-default cascade: a problem with Tinav's bank could trigger immediate repayment of the full Purchase Price.

**Recommended fix.** Confirm the principal amount of Tinav's senior credit facility. If it exceeds $5M, consider negotiating a higher threshold (e.g., $10M or $15M) or a carve-out for accelerations that are subsequently cured or rescinded within the 30-day period (which is already in the provision, but the initial acceleration still triggers the Event of Default). Also confirm that the senior facility does not contain a restriction on subsidiary-level debt that could be violated by the Seller Note.
