## Issue 1 – Missed Working Capital Dispute Deadline (CRITICAL)

**Documents:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 2.4(b); Buyer Board Consent (entire)

**What it says:** Section 2.4(b) provides that Buyer must deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement," otherwise "the Closing Statement shall be final and binding on the Parties." The Board Consent (¶ 1.3) states that Seller delivered the Closing Statement on **September 2, 2026**. Thirty days later is **October 2, 2026**. The Board Consent is dated **October 20, 2026**—eighteen days after the deadline expired.

**Why it matters:** If no Notice of Disagreement was delivered to Seller on or before October 2, 2026, the Closing Statement is final and binding. Sasos would be locked into Seller's (apparently overstated) working capital figure and could owe Seller an additional cash payment under § 2.4(d). Management's own review (Board Consent ¶ 1.4) identifies specific overstatements in inventory valuation, refrigerant accounting, and AR reserves. The financial exposure could be material given that the Target Working Capital is $6.2 million and the Purchase Price is $48.5 million.

**Complicating factor:** Resolution 7 of the Board Consent ratifies "all actions previously taken by any officer of the Company that are consistent with the purposes of the foregoing resolutions." This language implies an officer may have already delivered the Notice before October 20. However, the Consent does not state the date the Notice was actually delivered, and ratification of a past act does not cure a missed contractual deadline if the act was never performed.

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately confirm with the CFO and CEO whether a Notice of Disagreement was in fact delivered to Seller on or before October 2, 2026, and obtain a copy with proof of delivery (courier receipt, email transmission confirmation).
- If the Notice was **not** timely delivered, assess whether Seller has waived the deadline by conduct (e.g., by engaging in substantive negotiation on disputed items) or whether equitable tolling or estoppel arguments are available under Delaware law. This would require prompt factual development.
- If the Notice **was** timely delivered, document the delivery date and method in the file and confirm that the 30-day negotiation period under § 2.4(c) is running.

## Issue 2 – Side Letter Shifts Pre-Closing Cargo Claims to Sasos (CRITICAL)

**Documents:** Side Letter ¶¶ 2.2–2.3; Asset Purchase Agreement § 1.1 (definitions of "Assumed Liabilities" and "Excluded Liabilities"), § 9.3(b), § 9.5(c)

**What it says:** The original APA defined "Assumed Liabilities" as post-Closing liabilities under Assigned Contracts that do not relate to pre-Closing Seller breach. It defined "Excluded Liabilities" to include "all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims relating to goods received, stored or transported on or before the Closing" (clause (ii)). The Side Letter **deletes and replaces** both definitions so that:

- "Assumed Liabilities" now includes "all Liabilities under the Customer Contracts, **whether arising before, at or after the Closing**, including all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims relating to goods received, stored, handled or transported under the Customer Contracts" (¶ 2.2(b)).
- "Excluded Liabilities" clause (ii) is narrowed to exclude only claims "other than claims under the Customer Contracts" (¶ 2.3).

**Why it matters:** In a refrigerated warehousing and trucking business, cargo loss, product spoilage, and temperature-excursion claims are the **core operational risk**. By making all such claims under Customer Contracts "Assumed Liabilities":

1. **Sasos bears the loss.** These are no longer Excluded Liabilities that Seller must indemnify under § 9.2(c).
2. **Sasos indemnifies Seller.** Under § 9.3(b), Buyer indemnifies Seller for "any Assumed Liability." So if a customer sues Sasos for a pre-Closing temperature excursion, Sasos pays—and if Seller is dragged into the suit, Sasos must indemnify Seller as well.
3. **No cap or basket protection for Sasos on these indemnities.** Section 9.5(c) expressly states that "The Cap and the Basket shall not apply to any claim under Section 9.3(b)." Sasos's indemnity obligation to Seller for Assumed Liabilities is unlimited.
4. **The escrow does not help.** The Escrow Agreement defines "Indemnity Claim" narrowly as claims under § 9.2(a) (rep/warranty breaches). Claims for Assumed Liabilities are not secured by the escrow.
5. **The rep in § 4.10 provides limited comfort.** Seller represents that no customer has asserted a claim "exceeding $50,000" in the past two years, but this is (a) knowledge-qualified, (b) has a $50,000 threshold that excludes smaller claims, and (c) only survives until the Escrow Release Date (90 days post-Closing) under § 9.1(a).

**Recommendation:**
- Quantify the open and potential pre-Closing cargo claims. Use the claims files that Seller is obligated to deliver under Side Letter ¶ 3.1 (due 10 Business Days after Closing, i.e., by approximately July 14, 2026) and the 12-month employee cooperation period in ¶ 3.2.
- Assess whether Sasos's own insurance program (cargo legal liability, warehouse legal liability) covers these claims. Side Letter ¶ 3.3 requires Seller to provide pre-Closing policy information—confirm Sasos has received it and that coverage is in place for the transition period.
- If the exposure is material, consider negotiating a specific indemnity or escrow holdback for pre-Closing Customer Contract cargo claims, or a shared-loss mechanism. At minimum, document the business rationale for accepting this risk so the file reflects informed consent.
- Review the Customer Contracts on Schedule 1.1(b) to identify which have the highest historical claim frequency and severity.

## Issue 3 – 90-Day Escrow Release and Survival Period (CRITICAL)

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement § 1 ("Escrow Release Date"); Asset Purchase Agreement § 9.1(a)

**What it says:** The "Escrow Release Date" is defined as **ninety (90) days after the Closing Date** (Escrow Agreement § 1). Closing is June 30, 2026, so the Escrow Release Date is **September 28, 2026**. Under APA § 9.1(a), all non-fundamental representations and warranties survive only "until the Escrow Release Date." After that date, the $4.85 million escrow is released to Seller (minus pending claims), and Sasos can no longer bring claims for breach of those reps.

**Why it matters:** Ninety days is an extremely short period to discover problems in a cold-chain business. Specific risks that may not manifest within 90 days include:

- **Equipment condition (§ 4.8):** Refrigeration compressors, evaporators, and transport refrigeration units can fail in a delayed manner. A compressor that is near end-of-life may not fail until the first hot stretch in summer or the first heavy-load cycle.
- **Environmental / refrigerant compliance (§ 4.9):** Leak-rate issues, ammonia process-safety non-compliance, or unreported refrigerant releases may not be discovered until a routine inspection or a regulatory audit that occurs after day 90.
- **Customer contract issues (§ 4.10):** Termination rights, volume-commitment disputes, or latent performance issues may not surface until the first renewal cycle or the first billing dispute.
- **Permits (§ 4.11):** Transferability issues with food-facility registrations or motor carrier authority may only become apparent when Sasos attempts to operate under them.

The only reps that survive longer are the "Fundamental Representations" (§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.13, 5.1, 5.2)—organization, authority, title, and brokers. These are unlikely to be the source of material economic loss.

**Recommendation:**
- Conduct an accelerated post-Closing audit of all key reps within the 90-day window. Prioritize: (a) physical inspection of all refrigeration equipment and transport units; (b) review of environmental and refrigerant compliance records; (c) review of all Customer Contracts for termination rights, change-of-control provisions, and performance issues; (d) confirmation that all permits have been properly transferred.
- If any potential claim is identified before September 28, 2026, deliver a Claim Notice under Escrow Agreement § 4.1 immediately to preserve the claim against the escrow.
- For the future: in any subsequent transaction, negotiate a survival period of at least 18 months for non-fundamental reps (matching the tax and equipment-warranty cycles), or at minimum 12 months.

## Issue 4 – Escrow Secures Only Rep/Warranty Breaches, Not Covenants or Excluded Liabilities (SIGNIFICANT)

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement § 1 ("Indemnity Claim"); Asset Purchase Agreement § 2.5, § 9.2, § 11.4

**What it says:** APA § 2.5 states that the Escrow Amount is "security for all Indemnity Claims." However, the Escrow Agreement defines "Indemnity Claim" as "a claim by Buyer for indemnification for Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty under **Section 9.2(a)** of the Purchase Agreement." This excludes claims under § 9.2(b) (breach of Seller's covenants) and § 9.2(c) (Excluded Liabilities). APA § 11.4 provides that "the Seller Note and the Escrow Agreement shall control as to their own subject matter," so the narrower Escrow Agreement definition likely governs.

**Why it matters:** If Seller breaches a post-Closing covenant (e.g., fails to deliver claims files under Side Letter ¶ 3.1, fails to cooperate on customer notifications under APA § 6.2, or fails to remit AR collections under § 6.5), or if an Excluded Liability materializes (e.g., a pre-Closing environmental fine, a pre-Closing employment claim, a pre-Closing tax assessment), Sasos cannot reach the escrow for those amounts. It must rely on:
- Setoff against the Seller Note (§ 9.7), which is limited to the remaining note balance; or
- Direct payment from Seller, which depends on Seller's creditworthiness post-Closing.

**Recommendation:**
- Note that the Seller Note balance will amortize from September 30, 2027, reducing the setoff collateral over time. If a covenant breach or Excluded Liability claim arises after the Note is paid off, Sasos has no setoff and must pursue Seller directly.
- Ensure that all Excluded Liabilities are identified and quantified as soon as possible. The 90-day window is the practical limit for rep claims, but Excluded Liability claims under § 9.2(c) are not subject to the survival period in § 9.1(a)—they are governed by the applicable statute of limitations. However, without escrow security, collection risk is higher.
- In future deals, negotiate for the escrow to secure all indemnification obligations under § 9.2, not just § 9.2(a).

## Issue 5 – TSA Allows Seller to Delegate Services Without Sasos Consent (SIGNIFICANT)

**Documents:** Transition Services Agreement § 11.3

**What it says:** "Service Provider may, **without the consent of Recipient**, assign this Agreement or delegate any Services to any Person."

**Why it matters:** The Services include 24/7 alarm monitoring for temperature excursions, door-open events, power loss, and refrigerant detection (TSA § 2.1(c)). In a cold-chain operation, a failure in alarm monitoring can result in total loss of a refrigerated load (potentially hundreds of thousands of dollars) and regulatory violations. If Seller delegates the alarm monitoring function to a subcontractor of unknown quality, Sasos has no contractual right to object or to require a specific standard of sub-performance. The Service Levels in Schedule A (5-minute escalation) would technically still apply, but enforcement against an unknown subcontractor is difficult.

Additionally, the WMS hosting (which includes the customer-facing inventory portal) could be migrated to a different data center or provider without Sasos's approval, creating potential data-security and continuity risks.

**Recommendation:**
- Negotiate an amendment to § 11.3 requiring Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any delegation of the Alarm Monitoring or WMS Hosting services, or at minimum requiring that any delegate meet the same Service Levels and data-security standards.
- If the amendment cannot be obtained before the end of the Term (March 31, 2027), ensure that the Migration Plan (TSA § 3.1, due within 30 days of the Effective Date, i.e., by July 30, 2026) prioritizes early migration off the Alarm Monitoring and WMS services to reduce the window of delegation risk.
- Confirm that Sasos's own insurance program includes coverage for losses resulting from third-party monitoring failures during the transition period.

## Issue 6 – Tax Mismatch on Escrow Earnings (SIGNIFICANT)

**Documents:** Escrow Agreement §§ 9.1, 9.3

**What it says:** Section 9.1 provides that "Buyer shall be treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds until they are disbursed. Buyer shall report **all** income earned on the Escrow Funds, whether or not distributed." Section 9.3 provides that the Escrow Agent shall pay to Buyer only "thirty percent (30%) of the earnings for that quarter."

**Why it matters:** Sasos is taxed on 100% of the escrow earnings but receives only 30% in cash. The remaining 70% of earnings stays in the escrow and, under § 5.4, "shall follow the principal"—meaning when the escrow is released to Seller, the associated earnings go to Seller as well. Sasos bears the tax on income that economically belongs to Seller.

On a $4.85 million escrow invested in a Treasury money market fund, quarterly earnings might be approximately $25,000–$35,000 (depending on rates). Sasos would owe tax on the full amount but receive only $7,500–$10,500. Over the 90-day escrow period (roughly one quarter), the immediate cash impact is modest, but the principle matters and could be more significant if the escrow is extended due to pending claims.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm with Sasos's tax advisors whether the 70% of earnings allocated to Seller should be treated as a constructive distribution to Seller (who would then owe tax) or whether the current structure is the intended allocation. If Seller should be bearing the tax on its share, negotiate a tax gross-up or a reallocation of the earnings.
- If the escrow is extended beyond one quarter due to pending claims, the cumulative tax mismatch will grow. Monitor this.

## Issue 7 – Seller Note Governed by New York Law (SIGNIFICANT)

**Documents:** Seller Note § 9.1; Asset Purchase Agreement §§ 11.9, 11.4

**What it says:** The Seller Note is governed by **New York law** (§ 9.1) and subject to exclusive jurisdiction of New York courts (§ 9.2). The APA is governed by **Delaware law** (§ 11.9) and subject to exclusive jurisdiction of Delaware courts (§ 11.10). APA § 11.4 provides that the Seller Note "shall control as to its own subject matter."

**Why it matters:** The Note's setoff provision (§ 5) incorporates by reference Article IX of the APA (a Delaware-governed agreement). New York and Delaware may interpret setoff rights, subordination, and the interaction between the Note and the APA differently. For example:

- New York law may have different requirements for the validity or enforceability of contractual setoff.
- If a dispute arises over whether a particular claim is properly subject to setoff, Sasos could face a threshold choice-of-law fight before reaching the merits.
- The subordination provisions (§ 6) interact with any senior debt Sasos may have. New York's approach to subordination in the context of inter-creditor arrangements may differ from Delaware's.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm with New York counsel that the setoff right in Note § 5 is enforceable under New York law as drafted, particularly in the context of a good-faith withholding under APA § 9.7 (which provides that withholding "shall not constitute a default under the Seller Note").
- If Sasos obtains senior debt financing, ensure the lender's documentation is consistent with the subordination terms in Note § 6 and that New York law will give effect to the turnover provision (§ 6.4).
- Consider whether the governing-law mismatch was intentional (e.g., Seller's preference) or a drafting oversight. If it was an oversight, it may be worth raising in any post-Closing negotiation.

## Issue 10 – Weak Service Credit Remedy in TSA (MODERATE)

**Documents:** Transition Services Agreement, Schedule A ("Service Credits")

**What it says:** For each month a Service fails to meet its Service Level, Recipient receives a credit of **10%** of the monthly fee for that Service. If the failure continues for two consecutive months, the credit increases to **25%**.

**Why it matters:** The maximum monthly fees total $70,750. A 10% credit on the WMS ($38,500) is $3,850; on Alarm Monitoring ($11,200) it is $1,120. If the alarm monitoring system fails to escalate a temperature excursion within 5 minutes and the result is a spoiled load worth $200,000, Sasos's remedy is a $1,120 credit (or $2,800 if the failure persists for two months). The consequential damages exclusion in § 8.1 prevents recovery of the actual loss (unless it rises to gross negligence or willful misconduct, which is a high bar).

**Recommendation:**
- Ensure that the Migration Plan prioritizes early independence from the Alarm Monitoring service, given the asymmetric risk.
- Confirm that Sasos's own insurance (including technology E&O or cyber policies) covers losses from monitoring system failures during the transition period.
- If the TSA is still being negotiated or amended, seek to increase service credits for the Alarm Monitoring service specifically, or to carve temperature-excursion losses caused by monitoring failures out of the consequential damages exclusion.

## Issue 11 – Deductible (Not Tipping) Basket (MODERATE)

**Documents:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 9.5(a)

**What it says:** "Seller shall not be liable under Section 9.2(a) until the aggregate Losses under that Section exceed $485,000 (the 'Basket'). **Once that amount is exceeded, Seller shall be liable only for Losses in excess of the Basket.**"

**Why it matters:** This is a **deductible** basket, not a **tipping** (or "first-dollar") basket. Under a tipping basket, once losses exceed the threshold, the indemnifying party is liable for **all** losses from the first dollar. Here, Sasos always absorbs the first $485,000 of losses. In a cold-chain business where individual cargo claims can be $50,000–$200,000, Sasos could have two or three moderate claims and still be below the basket threshold with no recovery.

**Recommendation:**
- This is a negotiated term and likely not changeable post-Closing. However, it should be factored into the post-Closing claims strategy: Sasos needs to aggregate all rep/warranty breaches and present them as a single claim to cross the $485,000 threshold. Individual small claims should not be made separately.
- Note that the Basket does not apply to Fundamental Reps, fraud, covenant breaches, or Excluded Liabilities (§ 9.5(c)), so the deductible only affects ordinary rep/warranty claims.

## Issue 13 – Side Letter Not Listed as an Ancillary Agreement (MODERATE)

**Documents:** Side Letter (entire); Asset Purchase Agreement § 1.1 ("Ancillary Agreements")

**What it says:** The APA defines "Ancillary Agreements" as "the Bill of Sale, the Assignment and Assumption Agreement, the Lease Assignments, the Transition Services Agreement, the Escrow Agreement and the Seller Note." The Side Letter is **not** included in this definition.

**Why it matters:** The Side Letter amends the APA under § 11.6, so it is technically an amendment to the APA itself, not a separate ancillary agreement. This should mean it has the same precedence as the APA under § 11.4. However, the omission creates a technical ambiguity: if a conflict arises between the Side Letter and, say, the Transition Services Agreement (which *is* an Ancillary Agreement), the order of precedence in § 11.4 (APA prevails over Ancillary Agreements) may not clearly resolve it because the Side Letter is neither the APA nor an Ancillary Agreement in the defined sense.

In practice, this is unlikely to be a significant issue because the Side Letter's amendments are specific and do not appear to conflict with any Ancillary Agreement. But it is a drafting gap.

**Recommendation:**
- No immediate action required, but note the issue in the file. If a conflict arises, the Side Letter should prevail as a specific amendment to the APA, and the APA (as amended) should prevail over the Ancillary Agreements under § 11.4.

## Issue 14 – TSA Standard of Performance Is Relative to Seller's Prior Performance (MODERATE)

**Documents:** Transition Services Agreement § 2.2(b)

**What it says:** Service Provider shall perform the Services "at a level of quality, priority and care **no less than that at which it performed them for the Business during the twelve (12) months before the Closing Date**."

**Why it matters:** If Seller's performance in the 12 months pre-Closing was substandard (e.g., slow alarm response, frequent WMS outages, poor help desk service), the contractual floor is that substandard level. Sasos cannot require Seller to perform better than it historically did, even if the historical performance was inadequate for a cold-chain operation.

**Recommendation:**
- Review any pre-Closing performance data (system uptime logs, alarm response times, help desk ticket volumes) to establish what the baseline actually is.
- If the baseline is inadequate, the Service Levels in Schedule A (99.5% WMS availability, 5-minute alarm escalation, etc.) provide an absolute standard that supplements the relative standard in § 2.2(b). Ensure that the Service Levels are being monitored and that credits are being claimed for any failures.
- In the Migration Plan, set a target to migrate off the lowest-performing services first.

## Issue 15 – Press Release Creates Public Commitments (LOW)

**Documents:** Press Release §§ 1.3, 2.1, 3.1

**What it says:** The press release (issued July 1, 2026) states that "Most Teivil employees who supported the business have joined Sasos" (§ 1.3), that customers will continue to receive services "without interruption" (§ 2.1), and that Sasos's CEO described the priority as "a seamless transition for every customer. That means the same people, the same facilities and the same attention to product integrity" (§ 3.1).

**Why it matters:** While a press release is not a contract and does not create direct legal liability to customers or employees, these public statements:
- Set expectations that may be difficult to manage if the transition is not seamless (e.g., if key employees depart, if there are service interruptions during system migration, or if alarm monitoring gaps occur).
- Could be cited by a customer in a dispute as evidence of an implied representation or as a factor in a bad-faith claim.
- May create internal pressure to avoid acknowledging transition difficulties, potentially delaying escalation of TSA service failures.

**Recommendation:**
- No legal action required, but ensure that the communications team is aligned with operations on what "seamless" means in practice and that any customer-facing issues are handled through the contractual channels (TSA service credits, claims process) rather than being publicly minimized.
- If any material transition difficulties arise, assess whether updated communications are appropriate to manage expectations.
