## Issue 1 – Side Letter Shifts All Pre-Closing Cargo Claims Under Customer Contracts to Sasos

**Document / Section:** Side Letter, ¶¶ 2.2–2.3 (amending APA § 1.1 definitions of "Assumed Liabilities" and "Excluded Liabilities").

**Explanation:**
Under the original APA, "Assumed Liabilities" covered only post-Closing liabilities under Assigned Contracts, and "Excluded Liabilities" expressly included "all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims relating to goods received, stored or transported on or before the Closing" (APA § 1.1, Excluded Liabilities cl. (ii)). Seller indemnified Buyer for Excluded Liabilities under APA § 9.2(c), and that indemnity was uncapped (subject only to the Purchase Price ceiling under § 9.5(c)).

The Side Letter deletes and replaces both definitions. As amended:

- "Assumed Liabilities" now include "(b) all Liabilities under the Customer Contracts, whether arising before, at or after the Closing, including all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims relating to goods received, stored, handled or transported under the Customer Contracts" (Side Letter ¶ 2.2).
- "Excluded Liabilities" cl. (ii) is narrowed to pre-Closing cargo claims "other than claims under the Customer Contracts" (Side Letter ¶ 2.3).

The practical effect: Sasos has assumed 100% of the cargo-claim risk for the Customer Contracts, including claims that arose before the Closing. Because these are now Assumed Liabilities (not Excluded Liabilities), APA § 9.2(c) does not apply, and Sasos has no indemnity from Seller for them. Worse, under APA § 9.3(b), Sasos affirmatively indemnifies Seller for Assumed Liabilities—meaning if a customer sues both parties, Sasos could be on the hook even for Seller's pre-Closing handling of the goods.

The representation in APA § 4.10 states that no customer has asserted a cargo/spoilage/temperature claim exceeding $50,000 in the past two years (except as disclosed on Schedule 4.10). This means there could be numerous sub-$50,000 claims that are now Sasos's problem with no recourse.

**Why it matters:** For a cold-chain business, cargo loss and temperature-excursion claims are the most common and most financially significant category of customer claims. By assuming all of them—including pre-Closing events—Sasos has taken on an unquantified, potentially large liability with no indemnity backstop and no escrow security. The Escrow Agreement defines "Indemnity Claim" narrowly as covering only APA § 9.2(a) (breach of representations), so the escrow provides zero protection for these assumed cargo claims.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a carve-out: Sasos should assume only post-Closing cargo claims under Customer Contracts, or at minimum claims relating to goods in Sasos's possession or control after the Closing.
- If the Side Letter is already signed and the deal has closed (as the press release and Board Consent indicate), assess the magnitude of open and potential claims using the claim files Seller was required to deliver under Side Letter ¶ 3.1 (due 10 Business Days post-Closing, i.e., by approximately July 14, 2026). Confirm whether those files were delivered and whether they reveal significant exposure.
- Consider whether the APA § 4.10 representation (no claims >$50,000 in two years) was breached by the Side Letter's expansion of assumed liabilities—i.e., whether there are undisclosed sub-$50,000 claims that, in aggregate, are material. If so, a rep-breach claim against the escrow may be available before the Escrow Release Date (September 28, 2026). **Note: that date has likely already passed.** See Issue 2.

## Issue 2 – Closing Statement Dispute Deadline Appears to Have Lapsed

**Document / Section:** APA § 2.4(b); Buyer Board Consent, Recital 1.3 and date line.

**Explanation:**
APA § 2.4(a) requires Seller to deliver the Closing Statement within 90 days after Closing. The Board Consent recites that Seller delivered the Closing Statement on **September 2, 2026**. APA § 2.4(b) gives Buyer **30 days** after delivery to serve a Notice of Disagreement; otherwise "the Closing Statement shall be final and binding on the Parties."

Thirty days after September 2, 2026 is **October 2, 2026**. The Board Consent is dated **October 20, 2026**—18 days after the deadline. Resolution 1 authorizes the officers to "prepare, execute and deliver to Seller a Notice of Disagreement," and Resolution 7 ratifies "all actions previously taken by any officer." The ratification language suggests a notice may have been delivered before the Board met, but the document does not confirm the actual delivery date.

**Why it matters:** If the Notice of Disagreement was not delivered to Seller on or before October 2, 2026, the Closing Statement is final and binding. The Board Consent's findings (Recital 1.4) identify specific overstatements in inventory and receivables (obsolete packaging, refrigerant valued at replacement cost rather than per Schedule 2.3, inadequate AR reserves). If the deadline has passed, Sasos cannot recover the purchase price adjustment it is arguably owed, and the overstatement becomes a permanent increase in effective cost.

**Recommended fix:**
- **Immediately** confirm with the CFO whether and when the Notice of Disagreement was actually delivered to Seller. Obtain the delivery confirmation (courier receipt, email timestamp, etc.).
- If it was delivered by October 2, file the proof in the matter record and proceed to the 30-day good-faith negotiation period under § 2.4(c).
- If it was **not** delivered by October 2, assess whether Seller's conduct (e.g., failure to provide adequate supporting detail under § 2.4(a), or a late delivery that is arguable) provides a basis to challenge the finality. This is a weak position under the plain text, but worth evaluating.
- In any event, calendar the Independent Accountant deadline: if a timely Notice was given, the 30-day negotiation period under § 2.4(c) should have expired by approximately November 1, 2026. Confirm whether the parties have agreed on an Independent Accountant or whether that step has also lapsed.

## Issue 3 – TSA Permits Service Provider to Assign or Delegate Without Sasos's Consent

**Document / Section:** Transition Services Agreement § 11.3.

**Explanation:**
TSA § 11.3 provides: "Service Provider may, without the consent of Recipient, assign this Agreement or delegate any Services to any Person." This is one-directional: Sasos cannot assign without Teivil's consent, but Teivil can freely delegate.

The Services include 24/7 alarm monitoring with a 5-minute escalation requirement (Schedule A), continuous temperature telemetry with 5-minute reading intervals, and WMS hosting at 99.5% availability. These are operationally critical for a cold-chain business. A temperature excursion that goes undetected because a delegated sub-provider fails to monitor could result in cargo spoilage—and under the Side Letter (Issue 1), Sasos would bear the cost of any resulting customer claim.

**Why it matters:** Sasos has no control over who actually performs the monitoring and data services during the 9-month transition. If Teivil delegates to a low-cost or inexperienced provider, the quality of temperature monitoring could degrade, increasing the risk of undetected excursions and cargo loss. Sasos's recourse would be limited by the TSA's liability cap (see Issue 6).

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment requiring Teivil to obtain Sasos's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) before assigning or delegating any Service, or at minimum before delegating the Alarm Monitoring and Temperature Telemetry services.
- If the TSA is already in effect and the amendment is not feasible, require Teivil to identify in writing any sub-providers it intends to use, and obtain their qualifications and insurance certificates.
- Confirm whether Teivil has already delegated any Services.

## Issue 4 – Escrow Secures Only Rep & Warranty Breaches, Not Covenant Breaches or Excluded Liabilities

**Document / Section:** Escrow Agreement § 1 (definition of "Indemnity Claim"); APA §§ 9.2, 9.6, 11.4.

**Explanation:**
The APA defines "Indemnity Claim" broadly as "any claim for indemnification under Section 9.2" (APA § 1.1). Section 9.2 covers three categories: (a) breach of representations, (b) breach of covenants, and (c) Excluded Liabilities.

The Escrow Agreement, however, redefines "Indemnity Claim" narrowly: "a claim by Buyer for indemnification for Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty under Section 9.2(a) of the Purchase Agreement" (Escrow Agreement § 1). Only this narrow category triggers the escrow claim procedure (Escrow Agreement § 4.1).

APA § 11.4 provides that the Escrow Agreement "shall control as to its own subject matter." The subject matter of the Escrow Agreement is the holding and disbursing of escrow funds. Therefore, the Escrow Agreement's narrower definition governs what can be claimed against the escrow.

**Why it matters:**
- If Seller breaches a covenant (e.g., fails to deliver claim files under Side Letter ¶ 3.1, fails to cooperate on customer transition under APA § 6.2, or breaches the non-compete under APA § 10.1), Sasos cannot reach the escrow. It must rely on setoff against the Seller Note (APA § 9.7) or direct payment from Seller.
- If an Excluded Liability surfaces (e.g., a pre-Closing environmental claim, a pre-Closing tax assessment, or a pre-Closing cargo claim not under a Customer Contract), the same limitation applies.
- Given that the Escrow Release Date was September 28, 2026 (90 days post-Closing), the escrow has likely already been released to Seller less any pending claims. Sasos's security may already be gone.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the Escrow Agent whether the escrow has been released and, if so, the net amount released and whether any Pending Claim Amount was retained.
- For future covenant or Excluded Liability claims, Sasos's primary security is the right of setoff against the Seller Note (APA § 9.7; Seller Note § 5). Ensure the finance team is aware of this mechanism and that any indemnity claim notice is also delivered to Seller with a copy to the Escrow Agent (per APA § 9.4(a)) while the note is still outstanding.
- Note that the Seller Note matures June 30, 2029, so setoff is available for approximately three more years.

## Issue 5 – Seller Note Governed by New York Law with New York Forum

**Document / Section:** Seller Note §§ 9.1–9.2; APA § 11.9–11.10.

**Explanation:**
The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (APA §§ 11.9–11.10). The Seller Note is governed by **New York** law with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York** state or federal courts in New York County (Seller Note §§ 9.1–9.2).

The setoff right is cross-referenced in both documents: APA § 9.7 permits Sasos to set off indemnity amounts against the Note, and Seller Note § 5.1 incorporates APA § 9.7. But the Note is a New York instrument. If Teivil argues that a setoff is improper, the dispute could be litigated in New York under New York law, while the underlying indemnity claim arises under the Delaware-governed APA.

**Why it matters:**
- Dual forums and dual governing laws create the risk of inconsistent interpretations, increased litigation cost, and potential anti-suit or forum-non conveniens motions.
- New York law on setoff and on the interpretation of "good faith" withholding (APA § 9.7) may differ from Delaware law.
- If Sasos withholds a Note payment in good faith pending resolution of an indemnity claim (as APA § 9.7 permits), Teivil could argue in New York court that the withholding constitutes a payment default under Seller Note § 7.1(a), triggering the 2% default rate and potential acceleration.

**Recommended fix:**
- Before exercising any setoff or withholding, obtain New York counsel's confirmation that the withholding is protected under both the APA and the Note as a matter of New York law.
- Document the good-faith basis for any withholding meticulously, including the underlying indemnity claim notice delivered under APA § 9.4.
- If possible in any future amendment, conform the Note's governing law and forum to Delaware to eliminate the split.

## Issue 6 – TSA Liability Cap Is Inadequate Relative to Operational Risk

**Document / Section:** Transition Services Agreement § 8.2; Schedule A (fees).

**Explanation:**
TSA § 8.2 caps each party's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." The monthly fees are $38,500 + $14,750 + $11,200 + $6,300 = $70,750. Over the 9-month Term (June 30, 2026 through March 30, 2027), total fees are approximately **$636,750**.

The cap does not apply to breaches of Section 5 (Business Data and Confidentiality), payment obligations, or gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud (§ 8.2). But for an ordinary breach—such as a failure to meet service levels that results in a temperature excursion and cargo loss—the maximum recovery is ~$637,000.

**Why it matters:**
A single significant temperature excursion in a pharmaceutical or food client's cargo could result in a seven- or eight-figure loss. If the excursion was caused by Teivil's failure to monitor or escalate alarms (an ordinary breach of the Service Levels, not gross negligence), Sasos's recovery under the TSA is capped at ~$637,000. And under the Side Letter, the customer claim itself is an Assumed Liability that Sasos must pay.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a super-cap for service failures that directly cause cargo loss (e.g., 3× total fees or a fixed dollar amount tied to the value of cargo in storage).
- Ensure Sasos's own cargo legal liability and warehouse legal liability insurance policies are in place and adequate to cover the gap between the TSA cap and actual losses.
- Confirm that the service credits in Schedule A (10% of monthly fee for a failed month, 25% for two consecutive failed months) are being tracked and applied.

## Issue 8 – Escrow Tax Treatment Creates Cash-Flow Mismatch for Sasos

**Document / Section:** Escrow Agreement §§ 9.1, 9.3.

**Explanation:**
Escrow Agreement § 9.1 provides that "Buyer shall be treated as the owner of the Escrow Funds until they are disbursed. Buyer shall report all income earned on the Escrow Funds, whether or not distributed." Section 9.3 provides that the Escrow Agent shall distribute to Buyer only **30%** of quarterly earnings.

**Why it matters:**
Sasos has a tax liability on 100% of the escrow earnings (interest on $4.85M in a money market fund) but receives only 30% in cash distributions. For the quarter ending September 30, 2026 (the first full quarter), if the fund earned, say, 4.5% annualized, quarterly earnings would be approximately $54,500. Sasos would owe tax on $54,500 but receive only ~$16,400 in cash. This creates a cash tax cost with no corresponding economic benefit, particularly acute because the escrow was Sasos's own money (part of the Purchase Price) held as security.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm the actual earnings and distributions received for Q3 2026 and Q4 2026 (if the escrow was still funded).
- Ensure the tax return reflects the full income and that the 70% undistributed portion is tracked for any future distribution or release.
- In future transactions, negotiate for 100% pass-through of earnings to the tax-owner, or for the seller to be the tax owner of the escrow.

## Issue 9 – Side Letter Is Not an "Ancillary Agreement," Creating Precedence Ambiguity

**Document / Section:** APA § 1.1 (definition of "Ancillary Agreements"); APA § 11.4 (Order of Precedence); Side Letter ¶ 4.1.

**Explanation:**
APA § 1.1 defines "Ancillary Agreements" as "the Bill of Sale, the Assignment and Assumption Agreement, the Lease Assignments, the Transition Services Agreement, the Escrow Agreement and the Seller Note." The Side Letter is not included.

APA § 11.4 provides: "If there is any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall prevail, except that the Seller Note and the Escrow Agreement shall control as to their own subject matter."

The Side Letter amends the APA under § 11.6 (Side Letter ¶ 2.1). As an amendment to the APA, it should have the same force as the APA itself. But because it is not an "Ancillary Agreement," the § 11.4 precedence rule does not expressly address conflicts between the Side Letter and the Ancillary Agreements.

**Why it matters:**
The most concrete example is the definition of "Indemnity Claim." The APA (as amended) uses the broad definition (§ 1.1: "any claim for indemnification under Section 9.2"). The Escrow Agreement uses the narrow definition (§ 1: only § 9.2(a) claims). If the Side Letter's amendments to "Assumed Liabilities" and "Excluded Liabilities" were intended to change which claims are indemnifiable under § 9.2(c), the interaction with the Escrow Agreement's definition is unclear. Does the Escrow Agreement "control as to its own subject matter" and thus maintain the narrow definition regardless of the Side Letter's amendments? Almost certainly yes—but the ambiguity could be exploited in a dispute.

More broadly, if any term of the Side Letter conflicts with a term of the TSA, Seller Note, or Escrow Agreement, there is no clear precedence rule.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a clarifying provision (by amendment or side agreement) stating that the Side Letter, as an amendment to the APA, prevails over any Ancillary Agreement to the extent of any conflict, subject to the Escrow Agreement's and Seller Note's control over their own subject matter as provided in APA § 11.4.
- In the interim, when asserting any right under the Side Letter that interacts with an Ancillary Agreement, cite both the Side Letter and the APA § 11.4 precedence rule to preempt a counter-argument.

## Issue 10 – Seller Note Event of Default Triggers on Sale of Purchased Assets

**Document / Section:** Seller Note § 7.1(e).

**Explanation:**
Seller Note § 7.1(e) provides that an Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells all or substantially all of the Purchased Assets to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is paid in full at or before that sale."

**Why it matters:**
This restricts Sasos's ability to restructure, divest, or sell the acquired business (or a substantial part of it) within the three-year Note term (maturing June 30, 2029) without first repaying the $7M Note. If Sasos wants to sell one of the three Distribution Centers or a portion of the fleet, it may need to refinance or pay off the Note. The "substantially all" standard is not defined, creating uncertainty about what scale of asset sale would trigger the default.

**Recommended fix:**
- In any future divestiture or restructuring, analyze whether the assets to be sold constitute "all or substantially all of the Purchased Assets" and, if there is any doubt, seek a waiver from Teivil or arrange to pay down the Note.
- Consider whether the Affiliate carve-out is sufficient for Sasos's corporate structure (i.e., whether a sale to a parent or sister entity would qualify).

## Issue 11 – Migration Plan Has No Dispute Resolution Mechanism for Failure to Agree

**Document / Section:** Transition Services Agreement § 3.1.

**Explanation:**
TSA § 3.1 requires the parties to "agree in good faith on a written plan" for migrating Business Data within 30 days after the Effective Date (i.e., by approximately July 30, 2026). If the parties cannot agree, the TSA provides no escalation path, no default plan, and no consequence for failure to agree. The general dispute resolution provision (§ 10.1) requires escalation from service managers to senior executives, but it does not impose a deadline for reaching agreement or provide for a third-party determination.

**Why it matters:**
If Teivil stonewalls or proposes an unworkable migration plan, Sasos has no contractual lever to compel agreement. The Business Data (including temperature logs, customer records, and lane data) remains in Teivil's systems for up to 9 months. Without a migration plan, Sasos cannot complete its transition to its own systems, prolonging its dependence on Teivil (and the risks in Issues 3 and 6).

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm whether a Migration Plan was agreed by July 30, 2026. If not, escalate under TSA § 10.1 and document the impasse.
- Negotiate a provision that if the parties cannot agree within 30 days, the plan proposed by Sasos (or a plan developed by a mutually agreed third-party systems integrator) shall govern, with costs shared.
- Track the § 3.3 deadline: no later than 10 Business Days before the end of the Term (i.e., by approximately March 15, 2027), Teivil must deliver a complete final copy of all Business Data. Calendar this date.

## Issue 12 – Press Release Overstates Employee Retention and Operational Continuity

**Document / Section:** Press Release §§ 1.3, 3.1; APA § 7.1.

**Explanation:**
The press release (dated July 1, 2026) states that "[m]ost Teivil employees who supported the business have joined Sasos" (§ 1.3) and quotes the CEO saying "the same people, the same facilities and the same attention to product integrity" (§ 3.1).

APA § 7.1 provides that Buyer offered employment to employees "listed on Schedule 7.1, on terms substantially comparable **in the aggregate** to their current terms," and that Seller terminates employment of those who accept. The "in the aggregate" qualifier means individual employees may have received less favorable terms. Additionally, only employees on Schedule 7.1 were offered positions—other Business employees may not have been.

**Why it matters:**
- If a significant number of employees did not accept or were not offered positions, the press release statements are misleading. Customers who rely on the "same people" representation may be disappointed if key personnel (e.g., claims handlers, refrigeration technicians) depart.
- In a cargo-claim dispute, Teivil or a customer could argue that Sasos publicly represented continuity of operations and personnel, and that any disruption caused by personnel changes was a breach of that representation (though the press release is not a contract, it could be admissible as evidence of course of dealing or to rebut a claim of surprise).
- The "in the aggregate" language in § 7.1, combined with the press release's "same people" language, creates a gap between what was promised publicly and what was contractually committed.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm the actual employee retention rate against Schedule 7.1. If retention is below "most," consider a corrective communication.
- Ensure that key personnel (claims handlers, refrigeration engineers, dispatchers) are among those who accepted, given the Side Letter's expansion of cargo-claim liability to Sasos.
- In any future customer dispute, be aware that the press release may be cited as evidence of Sasos's representations about operational continuity.
