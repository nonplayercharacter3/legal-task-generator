## ISSUE 1 – CRITICAL: Working Capital Dispute Deadline Has Likely Expired

**Document/Section:** Asset Purchase Agreement § 2.4(b); Buyer Board Consent (entire document, dated October 20, 2026)

**Explanation:**
Section 2.4(b) of the APA provides that Buyer must deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement," and that "[i]f Buyer does not deliver a Notice of Disagreement within that period, the Closing Statement shall be final and binding on the Parties."

Per Section 1.3 of the Board Consent, Seller delivered the Closing Statement on **September 2, 2026**. Thirty days later is **October 2, 2026**. The Board Consent is dated **October 20, 2026**—eighteen days after the deadline. Resolution 1 of the Board Consent *authorizes* the officers to "prepare, execute and deliver" the Notice of Disagreement; it does not recite that the Notice was already delivered. Resolution 7 ratifies "all actions previously taken by any officer," but only if such actions were in fact taken before the deadline.

Management's review (Board Consent § 1.4) identifies specific overstatements: obsolete packaging and spare parts in Closing Inventory, refrigerant stock valued at replacement cost rather than per Schedule 2.3, and inadequate AR reserves for accounts >90 days past due or subject to temperature-excursion credits. If the Notice was not delivered by October 2, these points are no longer actionable and the Closing Statement is final.

**Why it matters:** The Purchase Price is $48.5M. The Target Working Capital is $6.2M. Any overstatement in Closing Working Capital means Sasos overpaid dollar-for-dollar (APA § 2.3). Given the specific items management identified, the overstatement could be material. If the deadline has passed, Sasos has lost this adjustment.

**Recommendation:**
1. Immediately confirm with the CEO and CFO whether a Notice of Disagreement was in fact delivered to Seller on or before October 2, 2026. Obtain a copy and the delivery confirmation.
2. If no Notice was delivered, assess whether Seller has waived the deadline by conduct (e.g., if Seller engaged in informal discussions about the disputed items before October 2). Document any such communications.
3. If the deadline has been missed and no waiver can be established, evaluate whether a claim for breach of the representation in APA § 4.6 (Inventory and Receivables) is still available. Note, however, that non-fundamental representations survive only until the Escrow Release Date (see Issue 2), which was September 28, 2026—also passed.
4. If the Notice was timely delivered, ensure the file contains proof of delivery (certified mail receipt, email with confirmation under APA § 11.1(c), or courier tracking).

## ISSUE 2 – CRITICAL: Side Letter Transfers Pre-Closing Customer Claims to Sasos with No Indemnity Backstop

**Document/Section:** Side Letter §§ 2.2–2.3 (amending APA § 1.1 definitions of "Assumed Liabilities" and "Excluded Liabilities"); APA §§ 9.2(c), 9.3(b), 9.5

**Explanation:**
The original APA defined "Assumed Liabilities" as post-Closing liabilities under Assigned Contracts that do not relate to pre-Closing Seller breaches, and defined "Excluded Liabilities" to include "all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims relating to goods received, stored or transported on or before the Closing" (APA § 1.1). Under that structure, Seller indemnified Buyer for Excluded Liabilities (APA § 9.2(c)), and the Cap/Basket did not apply to those claims (APA § 9.5(c)).

The Side Letter deletes and replaces both definitions. The amended "Assumed Liabilities" now include "(b) all Liabilities under the Customer Contracts, whether arising before, at or after the Closing, **including all cargo loss, product spoilage and temperature-excursion claims** relating to goods received, stored, handled or transported under the Customer Contracts." The amended "Excluded Liabilities" carve out "claims under the Customer Contracts" from the pre-Closing exclusion.

The net effect: **Sasos bears all pre-Closing and post-Closing customer contract claims, including temperature-excursion and product-spoilage claims, with no indemnity protection from Seller.** Under APA § 9.3(b), Sasos must indemnify *Seller* for Assumed Liabilities. Under APA § 9.2(c), Seller's indemnity covers only Excluded Liabilities—customer contract claims are no longer Excluded.

**Why it matters:** For a cold chain business, temperature-excursion and product-spoilage claims are a core operational risk. A single excursion affecting a high-value pharmaceutical or perishable food load can generate six- or seven-figure claims. The APA representation in § 4.10 states no customer has asserted a claim exceeding $50,000 in the past two years (except as disclosed on Schedule 4.10, which we do not have). But the Side Letter means that even if that representation is accurate, any *future* assertion of a pre-Closing excursion (which may not surface for months) is Sasos's problem with no recourse against Seller.

Compounding this, the survival period for non-fundamental representations is only 90 days (see Issue 3), so even a breach of the § 4.10 representation must be claimed before September 28, 2026.

**Recommendation:**
1. Obtain Schedule 4.10 (Customer Contracts disclosure) and identify any claims already disclosed. Cross-reference with the claims files Seller was required to deliver under Side Letter § 3.1 (due 10 Business Days after Closing, i.e., by approximately July 14, 2026). Confirm receipt and completeness.
2. Review Sasos's cargo legal liability and warehouse legal liability insurance policies to confirm coverage for pre-Closing excursions. Side Letter § 3.3 entitles Sasos to Seller's pre-Closing policy information—confirm this was provided.
3. Assess the historical frequency and severity of temperature-excursion claims in the Business using the temperature logs and alarm records (Purchased Assets under APA § 2.2(a)(viii)).
4. If any open or recently resolved claims exist, quantify the exposure and determine whether it is reflected in the Closing Statement's working capital calculation (see Issue 5).
5. Consider whether the Side Letter's risk transfer was adequately priced into the $48.5M Purchase Price. If not, this is a business-level concern for the client.

## ISSUE 3 – HIGH: 90-Day Survival Period Leaves No Security After Escrow Release

**Document/Section:** APA § 9.1(a); Escrow Agreement § 1 (definition of "Escrow Release Date"), § 5.1

**Explanation:**
Non-fundamental representations and warranties survive only until the "Escrow Release Date" (APA § 9.1(a)). The Escrow Release Date is defined in the Escrow Agreement as **90 days after the Closing Date**, i.e., **September 28, 2026**. On that date, the Escrow Agent releases the $4.85M to Seller (less any Pending Claim Amount) (Escrow Agreement § 5.1).

After September 28, 2026:
- No new Indemnity Claims can be made for non-fundamental representation breaches (APA § 9.1(a)).
- The escrow security is gone.
- Sasos's only remaining recourse for representation breaches is against Seller's general assets, with no collateral.

For a business involving three distribution centers, a reefer fleet, and complex cold-chain operations, 90 days is a very short window to discover representation breaches (e.g., equipment condition issues under § 4.8, environmental compliance under § 4.9, or AR collectibility under § 4.6).

**Why it matters:** If a transport refrigeration unit fails in November 2026 due to pre-Closing maintenance deficiencies (a breach of § 4.8), or if an environmental violation is discovered in Q4 2026 (a breach of § 4.9), Sasos cannot make an indemnity claim because the survival period has expired. The exclusive remedy provision (APA § 9.8) further limits Sasos to the indemnification framework.

**Recommendation:**
1. Conduct an accelerated post-Closing audit of all represented matters (equipment condition, environmental compliance, permits, customer contracts, AR quality) to identify potential breaches before the survival period expired. If the period has already passed (it has, as of this memo), document any issues discovered and assess whether they constitute Fundamental Representations (which survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations expires under APA § 9.1(b)) or fraud (which is not subject to the survival limit under APA § 9.5(c) and § 9.8(a)).
2. For any issue discovered that falls outside the survival period, evaluate whether it rises to the level of fraud or a Fundamental Representation breach.
3. In future transactions, negotiate a longer survival period (12–18 months is more typical for non-fundamental reps in asset purchases of this complexity) or a longer escrow holdback.

## ISSUE 4 – HIGH: Escrow Agreement Narrows "Indemnity Claim" Definition, Reducing Security Coverage

**Document/Section:** Escrow Agreement § 1 (definition of "Indemnity Claim"); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Indemnity Claim"), § 9.2

**Explanation:**
The APA defines "Indemnity Claim" broadly: "any claim for indemnification under Section 9.2" (APA § 1.1). Section 9.2 covers three categories: (a) representation breaches, (b) covenant breaches, and (c) Excluded Liabilities.

The Escrow Agreement defines "Indemnity Claim" narrowly: "a claim by Buyer for indemnification for Losses arising out of any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty under **Section 9.2(a)** of the Purchase Agreement" (Escrow Agreement § 1).

Under Escrow Agreement § 4.5, the Escrow Agent withholds funds "only against an Indemnity Claim set out in a Claim Notice." Because the Escrow Agreement's definition is limited to § 9.2(a) (rep breaches), **claims for covenant breaches (§ 9.2(b)) and Excluded Liabilities (§ 9.2(c)) cannot be secured against the escrow.** Sasos must pursue those claims against Seller's general assets directly.

**Why it matters:** If Seller breaches a post-Closing covenant (e.g., fails to cooperate on customer transition under APA § 6.2, fails to deliver claims files under Side Letter § 3.1, or fails to maintain confidentiality under APA § 6.4), or if an Excluded Liability claim arises (e.g., a pre-Closing environmental fine, a pre-Closing employee claim, a pre-Closing tax assessment), Sasos has no escrow security. It must litigate or negotiate with Seller directly, and Seller's willingness or ability to pay is unsecured.

**Recommendation:**
1. For any covenant breach or Excluded Liability claim, do not rely on the escrow. Pursue Seller directly and consider whether the setoff right under APA § 9.7 / Seller Note § 5.1 provides an alternative security mechanism (Sasos can set off against the $7M Note).
2. Note that the setoff right under APA § 9.7 is available for "any amount to which [Sasos] is entitled under this Article IX"—which includes § 9.2(b) and § 9.2(c) claims. This partially mitigates the escrow gap, as long as the Note remains outstanding (through June 30, 2029).
3. In any renegotiation or amendment, seek to conform the Escrow Agreement's "Indemnity Claim" definition to the APA's broader definition.

## ISSUE 5 – HIGH: Side Letter Amendment Affects Working Capital Calculation

**Document/Section:** Side Letter § 2.2 (amended "Assumed Liabilities"); APA § 2.3 (Working Capital formula); Buyer Board Consent § 1.4

**Explanation:**
APA § 2.3 defines Closing Working Capital as "the Closing Inventory plus the Accounts Receivable, net of reserves, **minus the current liabilities included in the Assumed Liabilities**."

The Side Letter's amended "Assumed Liabilities" definition includes all liabilities under Customer Contracts, whether arising before, at, or after the Closing. If there were accrued but unpaid customer claims (e.g., temperature-excursion credits agreed with a customer but not yet paid) as of the Closing Date, those are current liabilities included in the Assumed Liabilities and should have been **deducted** in the Closing Working Capital calculation.

The Board Consent (§ 1.4(c)) notes that management identified "the absence of adequate reserves against Accounts Receivable that are more than ninety (90) days past due, or that are subject to customer offsets for temperature-excursion credits." This suggests that some AR should have been reserved against because customers had asserted offsets for temperature-excursion credits—i.e., the net realizable value of the AR was lower than Seller stated.

**Why it matters:** If Seller's Closing Statement failed to (a) deduct accrued customer claims from working capital, or (b) adequately reserve against AR subject to temperature-excursion offsets, the Closing Working Capital is overstated and Sasos overpaid the Purchase Price. This is a separate and additional component of the working capital dispute beyond the inventory and AR valuation issues identified in the Board Consent.

**Recommendation:**
1. In the Notice of Disagreement (if timely) or in any negotiation with Seller, specifically itemize:
   - All open customer claims as of the Closing Date that represent current liabilities under the amended Assumed Liabilities definition;
   - All AR subject to customer offsets for temperature-excursion credits, with the amount of the offset.
2. Request from Seller the detail supporting its Closing Working Capital calculation, particularly the AR aging and any customer credit memos issued before the Closing.
3. If the working capital dispute proceeds to the Independent Accountant (APA § 2.4(c)), ensure the submission addresses the Side Letter's effect on the "current liabilities included in the Assumed Liabilities" component of the formula.

## ISSUE 6 – MODERATE: TSA Liability Cap Is Inadequate for Cold Chain Risk

**Document/Section:** Transition Services Agreement § 8.2 (Cap), Schedule A (Service Credits); APA § 1.1 (Side Letter amendment to Assumed Liabilities)

**Explanation:**
The TSA caps each party's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement" (TSA § 8.2). The monthly fees total $70,750 ($38,500 + $14,750 + $11,200 + $6,300). Over the 9-month Term, total fees are approximately **$636,750**.

The TSA covers temperature telemetry and 24/7 alarm monitoring for three distribution centers and the entire reefer fleet. A failure of the alarm monitoring service (e.g., a sensor goes down, an alarm is not escalated) could result in product spoilage. Under the Side Letter, any resulting customer claim under a Customer Contract is an Assumed Liability—Sasos's problem.

The service credit for missing a Service Level is only 10% of the monthly fee for that service (TSA Schedule A). For alarm monitoring ($11,200/month), a 10% credit is **$1,120**. If a monitoring failure causes a $500,000 product spoilage claim, Sasos's recovery under the TSA is capped at $1,120 in service credits (or $636,750 in total liability, and only if the failure constitutes a breach that isn't excused).

The cap does not apply to "a breach of Section 5" (data/confidentiality), "Recipient's payment obligations," or "gross negligence, willful misconduct or fraud" (TSA § 8.2). A simple failure to monitor alarms is unlikely to rise to gross negligence.

**Why it matters:** Sasos is dependent on Seller's systems for the first 9 months to monitor its most critical operational risk (temperature excursions). If those systems fail, the financial exposure from product spoilage vastly exceeds the TSA liability cap. And because the Side Letter makes customer contract claims Assumed Liabilities, Sasos has no indemnity backstop from Seller for the underlying customer claim.

**Recommendation:**
1. Ensure Sasos has its own backup temperature monitoring capability (even if rudimentary) that does not depend on Seller's platform, particularly for high-value or temperature-sensitive loads.
2. Confirm that Sasos's cargo legal liability and warehouse legal liability insurance policies are in place and cover spoilage resulting from monitoring failures during the transition period.
3. If the TSA is still in its Term, consider negotiating an amendment to (a) increase the liability cap for the alarm monitoring and temperature telemetry services specifically, or (b) add a super-cap for service failures that result in product loss.
4. Track all alarm events and service level performance meticulously during the TSA Term to preserve evidence of any breach.

## ISSUE 7 – MODERATE: TSA Allows Seller to Delegate Services Without Sasos's Consent

**Document/Section:** Transition Services Agreement § 11.3 (Assignment)

**Explanation:**
TSA § 11.3 provides: "Service Provider may, **without the consent of Recipient**, assign this Agreement or delegate any Services to any Person. Recipient may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of Service Provider."

This is entirely one-sided. Seller can hand off the warehouse management system, temperature telemetry, or alarm monitoring to a third party—potentially a less experienced or less reliable provider—without Sasos's approval. Sasos, by contrast, cannot assign its rights.

**Why it matters:** For a cold chain business, the identity and competence of the party monitoring temperature alarms is operationally critical. If Seller delegates the alarm monitoring to an under-resourced third party, service quality could degrade, increasing the risk of undetected excursions. Sasos has no contractual right to object.

**Recommendation:**
1. Require that any delegation or assignment under the TSA be to a party with qualifications no less than Seller's, and that Sasos be given prior written notice (even if consent is not required).
2. If the TSA is still in its Term, send a notice to Seller identifying Sasos's key personnel contacts and service expectations, making clear that any degradation in service will be documented as a potential breach of the Standard of Performance (TSA § 2.2).
3. In the Migration Plan (TSA § 3.1), build in milestones that reduce dependence on Seller's systems as quickly as practicable.

## ISSUE 8 – MODERATE: Governing Law and Forum Conflict Between APA and Seller Note

**Document/Section:** Seller Note §§ 9.1–9.2 (New York law; New York forum); APA §§ 11.9–11.10 (Delaware law; Delaware forum)

**Explanation:**
The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (APA §§ 11.9–11.10). The Seller Note is governed by **New York** law with exclusive jurisdiction in **New York** state or federal courts in New York County (Seller Note §§ 9.1–9.2).

The setoff right (APA § 9.7; Seller Note § 5.1) allows Sasos to offset indemnity amounts against Note payments. But the indemnity claim arises under a Delaware-law agreement, while the Note is a New York-law instrument. If Seller disputes the setoff, Sasos could face a threshold choice-of-law and forum battle: Does Delaware or New York law govern the validity and effect of the setoff? Must Sasos assert the setoff in a Delaware action (on the APA) or a New York action (on the Note)?

**Why it matters:** If Sasos needs to exercise the setoff right (e.g., to withhold a Note payment while an indemnity claim is pending), the governing law conflict could delay enforcement, increase legal costs, and create uncertainty about whether the setoff is effective. Seller Note § 7.1(a) carves out from the payment default any amount "withheld or set off under Section 5," but a New York court might interpret the setoff right differently than a Delaware court would.

**Recommendation:**
1. Before exercising any setoff, obtain advice on whether to assert it in the Delaware action (if one is pending) or in a New York action, and whether the setoff is effective as a matter of New York law.
2. Ensure that any setoff notice to Seller (required by Seller Note § 5.2) is drafted to comply with both the APA's notice provisions (Delaware) and the Note's notice provisions (which incorporate APA § 11.1).
3. In any future amendment or refinancing of the Note, seek to conform the governing law and forum to Delaware to eliminate the conflict.

## ISSUE 9 – MODERATE: Seller Note Acceleration Trigger on Asset Sale Limits Strategic Flexibility

**Document/Section:** Seller Note § 7.1(e) (Event of Default)

**Explanation:**
Seller Note § 7.1(e) provides that an Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells all or substantially all of the Purchased Assets to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is paid in full at or before that sale."

**Why it matters:** If Sasos wishes to sell the acquired business (or a substantial portion of it, such as one or two of the three Distribution Centers) to a strategic buyer within the 3-year Note term (through June 30, 2029), it must first pay off the entire $7M Note. This could constrain Sasos's ability to execute a portfolio optimization or exit strategy. The "Affiliate" carve-out provides some flexibility for internal reorganizations but not for third-party sales.

**Recommendation:**
1. Note the constraint in Sasos's strategic planning. If a sale of the business or a significant division is being considered, model the cost of paying off the Note early (no prepayment penalty under Seller Note § 4, so the cost is purely the loss of the 8% financing benefit).
2. Confirm that any internal reorganization (e.g., transferring assets to an operating subsidiary) qualifies as a sale to an "Affiliate" and thus does not trigger the default.

## ISSUE 11 – MODERATE: No Address for Employees Who Decline Buyer's Offer

**Document/Section:** APA § 7.1 (Offers of Employment), § 7.2 (Seller Obligations); Press Release § 1.3

**Explanation:**
APA § 7.1 provides that Buyer offered employment to employees on Schedule 7.1 and that "Seller shall terminate the employment of each employee who accepts Buyer's offer, effective as of the Closing." The APA does not address what happens to employees who **decline** the offer. Seller presumably retains those employees, but the APA is silent on transition obligations, non-solicitation (beyond the 3-year covenant in § 10.2), or knowledge transfer.

The Press Release states that "[m]ost Teivil employees who supported the business have joined Sasos"—confirming that some did not.

The non-solicitation covenant (APA § 10.2) prevents Seller from poaching Sasos's employees for 3 years, but it does not prevent Seller from hiring back the employees who stayed with it and using their knowledge of the Business (which they now operate for Sasos under the TSA) to Sasos's detriment.

**Recommendation:**
1. Identify which employees from Schedule 7.1 declined the offer and assess whether any hold critical operational knowledge.
2. Confirm that the TSA's personnel provision (TSA § 2.3) requires Seller to assign qualified personnel, and document the identities of those assigned.
3. Ensure that the confidentiality covenant (APA § 6.4) is being enforced—Seller must not use Business information (customer lists, pricing, lane data, temperature records) except as required to perform the TSA.

## ISSUE 12 – MODERATE: Non-Assignable Contracts Create Ongoing Seller Dependency

**Document/Section:** APA § 6.3 (Non-Assignable Contracts)

**Explanation:**
If any Assigned Contract cannot be assigned without a consent that has not been obtained, "Seller shall continue to hold that Assigned Contract for Buyer's benefit." Seller must use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain the consent. Buyer performs post-Closing obligations "to the extent it receives the benefits of it."

**Why it matters:** This creates a triangular arrangement in which Seller is the nominal party to the contract but Sasos performs and receives the benefit. If Seller becomes uncooperative, dissolves, or is acquired by a competitor, the contract could be disrupted. Additionally, if a dispute arises under the non-assigned contract, it is unclear whether Sasos or Seller is the proper party to litigate. The "commercially reasonable efforts" standard is soft and hard to enforce.

**Recommendation:**
1. Identify which Assigned Contracts (if any) remain non-assigned. Review Schedule 3.2(e) (third-party consents) to see which consents were obtained at Closing.
2. For any contract still held by Seller, request a written status update on consent efforts and set a deadline for resolution.
3. If a material contract remains non-assigned beyond a reasonable period, consider whether this constitutes a breach of Seller's covenant and whether the setoff right (APA § 9.7) can be exercised.

## ISSUE 14 – LOW: Escrow Agent Annual Fee Is Not Prorated for a 90-Day Hold

**Document/Section:** Escrow Agreement Schedule 3 (Fees); APA § 2.5

**Explanation:**
Schedule 3 of the Escrow Agreement sets an "Annual administration fee (payable at Closing and on each anniversary, not prorated)" of $5,000. The escrow is held for only 90 days (until the Escrow Release Date). Sasos and Seller each pay one-half (APA § 2.5; Escrow Agreement § 7.1), so Sasos's share is $2,500 for a 90-day hold.

**Recommendation:** No action required. Note for future transactions that escrow fees should be prorated if the holding period is less than 12 months.
