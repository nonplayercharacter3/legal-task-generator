## Issue 1 – Film Library Definition Mismatch Between SPA and Bill of Sale

**Documents and Sections:**
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Film Library"): *"all released and unreleased motion picture titles that Seller or any of its Affiliates distribute, including the 2026 Release Slate."*
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "2026 Release Slate"): *"the motion pictures scheduled by Seller or the Company for initial theatrical release in calendar year 2026, as listed in Section 1.1 of the Disclosure Schedule."*
- Bill of Sale § 1.1 (definition of "Film Library"): *"all motion picture titles, whether feature-length or short-form, that Seller or any of its Affiliates owns or holds distribution rights in and that were theatrically released on or before December 31, 2024, together with all prequels, sequels, remakes and derivative rights in those titles."*

**Why it matters:**
The Bill of Sale's definition is materially narrower than the SPA's. It excludes:
1. **All titles theatrically released in 2025** (the cutoff is December 31, 2024);
2. **All unreleased titles**, including the entire 2026 Release Slate that the SPA specifically incorporates into the Film Library definition; and
3. **Titles not theatrically released** (e.g., direct-to-digital or streaming-only titles).

SPA § 4.10 represents that, upon completion of the contribution under § 6.8, the Company will own rights in "each title in the Film Library" (using the SPA's broader definition). But the Bill of Sale only contributes a subset. Semeith is paying $46 million for a company whose value is substantially in its Film Library, yet the contribution document does not deliver the full asset the SPA represents. If the 2025 releases and 2026 Release Slate are material to the deal's economics, Semeith is not receiving what it bargained for.

Additionally, SPA § 6.8 requires Seller to contribute "all of their right, title and interest in and to the Film Library" (SPA definition) by the Bill of Sale. The Bill of Sale as drafted fails to satisfy this covenant because it uses a different, narrower definition.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Bill of Sale § 1.1 to conform to the SPA's definition of "Film Library," or add a separate schedule (or expand Schedule A) that expressly includes (a) all titles theatrically released in 2025, (b) all unreleased titles, and (c) the 2026 Release Slate. Alternatively, if Seller's position is that only titles released through 2024 are being contributed, the SPA's definition and the § 4.10 representation must be revised to match, and the Purchase Price should be re-evaluated. Given that the Bill of Sale is scheduled to be signed on March 30 (deal call notes § 3.2), this must be resolved before then.

## Issue 2 – Ventrice Change of Control Consent Not Obtained

**Documents and Sections:**
- Ventrice Employment Agreement § 10.1(a): "Change of Control" includes "any sale, transfer or other disposition, in one transaction or a series of related transactions, of more than 50% of the outstanding voting stock of the Company."
- Ventrice Employment Agreement § 10.2: *"The Company shall not undergo a Change of Control without the Executive's prior written consent."* If a CoC occurs without consent: (a) it constitutes Good Reason; (b) she receives 24 months' Base Salary ($850,000) plus pro-rated bonus and COBRA; and **(c) all Executive Titles revert to her**, including "the Company's distribution rights, its interest in Elements and marketing materials, and its rights to receive revenue from those titles after that date."
- SPA § 4.6 (Consents): Only the Kestrelwave consent is identified. Ventrice's consent is not mentioned.
- SPA § 7.2(d) (Closing Condition): Only requires the Kestrelwave consent.
- Seller Board Consent: Makes no reference to Ventrice's consent or her employment agreement.

**Why it matters:**
The stock purchase is unambiguously a Change of Control under § 10.1(a). Without Ventrice's prior written consent, § 10.2 is triggered. The most dangerous consequence is § 10.2(c): all Executive Titles revert to her. Exhibit A (which lists the Executive Titles) is not in the folder, so we cannot yet quantify the exposure, but these are titles she "originated, sourced or brought to the Company" (§ 1.1). If she resigned for Good Reason within 90 days post-Closing, Semeith could lose a potentially significant subset of the Film Library it just paid $46 million to acquire.

The SPA does not treat her consent as a closing condition, and no document in the folder indicates it has been sought or obtained. This appears to be an oversight by both sides.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Immediately request a copy of Exhibit A to the Ventrice agreement to assess the scope of Executive Titles.
2. Seek Ventrice's written consent to the Change of Control before Closing. Add it as a condition in SPA § 7.2 (or obtain a waiver of § 10.2 in writing).
3. If consent is not forthcoming, negotiate an amendment to § 10.2 that (a) removes the reversion right in exchange for enhanced severance, or (b) limits the reversion to titles that are not yet released.
4. If the deal closes without her consent, be prepared for a Good Reason resignation and reversion claim within 90 days.

## Issue 3 – Escrow Agreement Makes Escrow the "Sole and Exclusive Source of Recovery," Undermining the Fundamental Rep Carve-Out

**Documents and Sections:**
- SPA § 9.5(a): Cap on Seller's indemnity for General Rep breaches = $4,600,000 (10% of Purchase Price).
- SPA § 9.5(d): Cap and Basket do **not** apply to Losses arising from Fraud, breaches of Fundamental Representations, or Taxes.
- SPA § 9.6: Indemnification amounts "shall first be satisfied from the Escrow Funds."
- Escrow Agreement § 7.4: *"The Escrow Funds shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery of the Buyer Indemnified Parties for all Losses under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, whether or not subject to the Cap."*
- SPA § 11.9: In the event of a conflict between the SPA and an Ancillary Agreement, "the Ancillary Agreement governs with respect to its own subject matter."

**Why it matters:**
The Escrow Amount is $3,450,000. The stated Cap is $4,600,000. For General Rep breaches, the effective recovery is limited to $3.45 million (the Escrow), not $4.6 million, because of § 7.4.

More critically, SPA § 9.5(d) carves Fundamental Representations (Organization, Authority, Capitalization, Taxes, Brokers) out of the Cap, meaning Seller's exposure for those should be unlimited. But Escrow Agreement § 7.4 states the Escrow is the "sole and exclusive source of recovery... whether or not subject to the Cap." Read together with SPA § 11.9 (Ancillary Agreement governs), this means that even for a breach of a Fundamental Representation—say, a capitalization defect or a tax liability—Semeith's recovery is capped at $3.45 million. This nullifies the purpose of the Fundamental Rep carve-out.

The only claims that escape this limitation are Fraud claims, which SPA § 9.7 expressly preserves outside the exclusive remedy framework. But Fraud requires proof of "actual and intentional fraud" (SPA § 1.1), a very high bar.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend Escrow Agreement § 7.4 to provide that the Escrow is the sole and exclusive source of recovery only for Losses subject to the Cap (i.e., General Rep breaches and covenant breaches under § 9.2(a)–(b)). For Losses arising from Fundamental Representation breaches, Taxes, or Fraud, Buyer should retain the right to seek recovery from Seller directly beyond the Escrow. Alternatively, increase the Escrow Amount to at least the Cap ($4.6 million) and clarify that the Escrow is the exclusive remedy only up to the Cap, with direct recourse above it.

## Issue 4 – Survival Period for General Representations Is Only 120 Days Post-Closing

**Documents and Sections:**
- SPA § 9.1: "The General Representations shall survive the Closing until the Initial Release Date."
- Escrow Agreement § 5.1: "Initial Release Date" = 120 days after the Closing Date (approximately July 29, 2026).
- Escrow Agreement § 7.1: Buyer may deliver a Claim Notice only "at any time before the Initial Release Date."
- Escrow Agreement § 5.2: On the Initial Release Date, the Escrow Agent disburses all Escrow Funds to Seller (less Pending Claim Amounts).

**Why it matters:**
Semeith has only 120 days after Closing to discover and assert claims based on General Representations. For a film distribution business, this is an extremely short window. Key risks that may not manifest within 120 days include:
- **Participation and residual disputes** (SPA § 4.12): Producer participation statements are rendered within 90 days after June 30 and December 31. The December 31, 2025 statements would be due approximately March 31, 2026—the Closing Date itself. Disputes arising from those statements would likely surface well after the Initial Release Date.
- **Copyright and chain-of-title defects** (SPA § 4.10): These often come to light only when a title is licensed or distributed in a new territory or medium.
- **Exhibitor receivables** (SPA § 4.12, last sentence): The representation that receivables are "bona fide and arise from settlements in the ordinary course" may not be testable until settlements are actually collected.

After the Initial Release Date, the Escrow is released to Seller, and under Escrow Agreement § 7.4, the Escrow is the "sole and exclusive source of recovery." So if a General Rep claim is discovered on day 121, Semeith has no recourse.

**Recommended fix:**
Extend the survival period for General Representations to at least 18 months post-Closing (to cover two full participation statement cycles and at least one release season). Correspondingly, extend the Initial Release Date in the Escrow Agreement. If Seller resists, consider a tiered release: 50% of the Escrow at 120 days, the remainder at 18 months.

## Issue 5 – Buyer's Indemnity for Seller Guarantees Is Uncapped

**Documents and Sections:**
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Seller Guarantees"): Includes "Seller's guarantees of the Company's minimum guarantee payment obligations to producers and licensors of motion pictures."
- SPA § 9.3(c): Buyer indemnifies Seller for "any Seller Guarantee, including any payment made or obligation incurred by Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Seller Guarantee."
- SPA § 9.5(e): "The Cap, the Basket and the other limitations in this Section 9.5 shall not apply to claims under Section 9.3(c)."
- SPA § 6.7: Post-Closing, "Buyer shall use commercially reasonable efforts to cause Seller and its Affiliates to be released from the Seller Guarantees."

**Why it matters:**
Semeith has an **uncapped** indemnification obligation to Seller for any payments Seller makes under its guarantees of the Company's minimum guarantee obligations to producers and licensors. In the film distribution business, minimum guarantees can be substantial. If a title underperforms and the producer calls the minimum guarantee, Seller pays under its guarantee, then turns to Semeith for reimbursement—without limit.

This is compounded by § 6.7, which obligates Semeith to use commercially reasonable efforts to get Seller released from those guarantees. Once Seller is released, the underlying minimum guarantee obligations fall directly to the Company (now Semeith's subsidiary). The indemnity in § 9.3(c) covers the transition period, but the uncapped nature means Semeith bears full economic risk for any minimum guarantee calls during that period.

**Recommended fix:**
Negotiate a cap on the § 9.3(c) indemnity (e.g., limited to the aggregate remaining minimum guarantee obligations identified in the Disclosure Schedule as of Closing). Alternatively, require Seller to deliver a schedule of all outstanding minimum guarantee obligations and their remaining payment schedules so Semeith can quantify the exposure before Closing. Ensure the Disclosure Schedule identifies all titles with uncalled minimum guarantees.

## Issue 6 – Transition Services Agreement Allows Provider to Delegate Without Consent

**Documents and Sections:**
- TSA § 12.3: *"Provider may assign this Agreement, or delegate the performance of any Service, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Recipient."*
- TSA § 3.3: Provider shall "use commercially reasonable efforts to keep assigned the personnel who performed the corresponding functions before the Closing."

**Why it matters:**
Section 12.3 effectively nullifies Section 3.3. Seller can delegate the theatrical booking, participation accounting, and digital cinema delivery functions to any third party—potentially a competitor or an inexperienced vendor—without the Company's approval. The Company is dependent on these services for nine months post-Closing, and the quality of participation accounting in particular directly affects producer relationships and the Company's reputation.

The TSA liability cap (§ 10.2) is limited to "total Service Fees paid and payable," which at cost (no markup, per § 5.1) may be modest. If a delegated provider botches the participation accounting, the Company's recovery is limited.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend TSA § 12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment or delegation of the Services. At minimum, require that any delegatee have comparable experience and that Provider remains fully liable for the delegatee's performance.

## Issue 7 – Bill of Sale Delivery Timing Creates Post-Closing Gap for Elements and Laboratory Access

**Documents and Sections:**
- Bill of Sale § 1 (preamble): Effective Date = March 30, 2026 (the day before Closing).
- Bill of Sale § 4.1: Laboratory access notices to be delivered "within ten Business Days after the Effective Date" (approximately April 13, 2026).
- Bill of Sale § 4.2: Digital Elements and Marketing Materials to be transferred "within ten Business Days after the Effective Date."
- SPA § 3.1: Closing is effective as of 12:01 a.m. on March 31, 2026.

**Why it matters:**
At Closing (March 31), Semeith owns the Company, but the Company does not yet have laboratory access or possession of digital Elements for approximately two weeks. During this gap:
- The Company cannot deliver digital cinema packages for any titles in the 2026 Release Slate or for re-releases.
- If a title is scheduled for release in early April, the Company may miss its window.
- The Company's right to the Elements is contractual (via the Bill of Sale) but not yet operationalized.

**Recommended fix:**
Shorten the delivery period in Bill of Sale §§ 4.1 and 4.2 to three Business Days after the Effective Date (i.e., by April 3, 2026). Alternatively, require Seller to deliver the laboratory access notices and digital Elements **before** the Closing Date as a condition to Closing (add to SPA § 7.2). At minimum, add a specific indemnity or liquidated damages provision for failure to deliver within the stated period.

## Issue 8 – Participation Statement Gap at Closing

**Documents and Sections:**
- SPA § 4.12: "Producer participation statements are rendered within 90 days after each June 30 and December 31."
- SPA § 4.7: Financial statements are as of December 31, 2025.
- Deal Call Notes § 9.1: No further tax items raised; Seller to file pre-Closing returns.

**Why it matters:**
The December 31, 2025 participation statements are due within 90 days—i.e., by approximately March 31, 2026, the Closing Date. It is unlikely these statements will be finalized and rendered before Closing. The representation in § 4.12 covers only statements "rendered since January 1, 2023," meaning the most recent rendered statements at signing (February 17) would be for the June 30, 2025 period. There is a six-month gap (July–December 2025) for which participation accuracy is not represented, and the December 2025 statements may not exist at Closing.

If participation payments for H2 2025 were miscalculated, Semeith would discover this only after the Initial Release Date (see Issue 4), at which point the Escrow would have been released.

**Recommended fix:**
Require Seller to render (or at least prepare in draft form) the December 31, 2025 participation statements before Closing and deliver them to Buyer as a closing deliverable. Add a specific representation that those statements are accurate in all material respects. If this is not feasible, extend the survival period (see Issue 4) to cover at least the next statement cycle.

## Issue 10 – TSA Service Fees Not Yet Finalized

**Documents and Sections:**
- TSA § 5.1: Service Fees are "equal to Provider's fully loaded cost of providing the Services, without mark-up," as set forth on Schedule A.
- Deal Call Notes § 7.3: Seller to provide fully loaded cost figures by March 17, 2026.
- TSA Schedule A: Not yet populated (referenced but not in the folder).

**Why it matters:**
The Service Fees are the basis for the TSA liability cap (§ 10.2: "total Service Fees paid and payable"). If the fees are low (because they are at cost), the cap on Provider's liability is correspondingly low. Semeith needs to understand the fee structure to (a) budget for the nine-month transition period and (b) assess the adequacy of the liability cap.

Additionally, "fully loaded cost" is not defined in the TSA. Without a definition or supporting schedule, there is a risk of dispute over what constitutes a pass-through cost versus a Service Fee.

**Recommended fix:**
Review the cost figures when delivered (March 17). Ensure Schedule A itemizes the components of "fully loaded cost" (personnel, overhead allocation, technology, etc.) so that the fees are transparent and auditable. Consider adding a definition of "fully loaded cost" to TSA § 1.1. Confirm that the audit right in TSA § 7.4 (twice during the Term, once post-Term) is sufficient to verify the fees.
