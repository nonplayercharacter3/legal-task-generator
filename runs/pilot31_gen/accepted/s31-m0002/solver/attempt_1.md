## Issue 1: Uncapped Indemnity for Grower Claims "Whatever the Cause"

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 1.6 (definition of "Grower Claims"), § 11.2 (Customer Indemnification), § 12.3(a) (exclusion of § 11 from liability cap).

**Why It Matters:**

Section 11.2 requires Sovex to defend and indemnify Ludis against any claim "arising out of or relating to Customer's use, handling, storage, resale or planting of Products, including any Grower Claims." The definition of "Grower Claims" in § 1.6 is extraordinarily broad: it covers "any claim, demand, suit or proceeding brought by a Grower relating to the crop stand, emergence, yield or performance of Products, **whatever the cause**."

In practical terms, this means that if a grower's crop fails due to drought, hail, a pest outbreak the seed treatment didn't cover, or the grower's own management decisions, and that grower brings a claim against Sovex (the local, familiar face), Sovex must indemnify Ludis for the full amount. Because § 12.3(a) carves § 11 obligations out of the liability cap in § 12.1, this indemnity is **uncapped**. Sovex's only protection would be if the claim actually relates to a defect in the Products themselves, in which case § 11.1 (Supplier's IP indemnity) or § 8 (warranty) might shift the risk back to Ludis—but § 11.2 does not contain any carve-out for claims caused by product nonconformity.

This is the single largest exposure in the agreement. A single large grower claim (e.g., a 5,000-acre field with a total crop failure) could generate a seven- or eight-figure judgment against Sovex, and Sovex would have to indemnify Ludis for any portion of it that is not attributable to product defect.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add a carve-out in § 11.2 so that Sovex's indemnity does not extend to Grower Claims to the extent caused by the Products' failure to conform to the Specifications or by Ludis's breach of § 8.1. Proposed language:

> "Customer's obligations under this Section 11.2 do not extend to any claim, demand, suit or proceeding to the extent arising from or relating to (a) the Products' failure to conform to the Specifications, (b) Supplier's breach of Section 8.1, or (c) any defect in the Products' germplasm, genetics or seed treatment."

Additionally, consider negotiating a sub-cap or super-cap for the indemnity (e.g., 2× the 12-month fee cap) to provide some financial predictability.

**Open Question for Client:** Does Sovex currently carry product liability insurance with a limit sufficient to absorb a large grower claim (e.g., $10M+)? The certificate shows $5M per occurrence CGL, but grower claims in agriculture can easily exceed that. If not, the indemnity exposure is unmitigated by insurance.

## Issue 2: Overly Broad Exclusivity Obligation (Nationwide, Three Crops)

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 4.1 (Exclusive Purchasing).

**Why It Matters:**

Section 4.1 provides that during the Term, Sovex "shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute any corn, soybean or sorghum seed from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier **anywhere in the United States and Canada**."

This is not limited to the Territory (Iowa and Nebraska, per Order Form No. 2 § 2.2). It is not limited to hybrid corn (the product actually being supplied). It covers soybean and sorghum seed as well, and it applies nationwide. If Sovex currently distributes any other seed brand in any crop in any market, this is a present breach. Even if Sovex does not currently do so, the restriction eliminates any ability to diversify supply, hedge against Ludis's performance failures, or serve customer requests for alternative products.

Combined with the Minimum Purchase Commitment (§ 5) and the fact that Ludis's allocation obligation in § 16.2 is only "fair and non-discriminatory" (no priority for the exclusive distributor), Sovex is locked into a single supplier for a large portion of its revenue with no safety valve.

The Term runs to December 31, 2028, with automatic three-year renewals (§ 2.2), so this restriction could persist well into the 2030s.

**Recommended Fix:**

Narrow § 4.1 to (a) the Territory only, (b) hybrid corn seed only (matching the actual product), and (c) add a material supply failure exception. Proposed language:

> "During the Term, Customer shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute hybrid corn seed from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier in the Territory. This Section 4.1 does not apply to (a) soybean or sorghum seed, (b) areas outside the Territory, or (c) any period during which Supplier has failed to deliver ordered Products in accordance with Section 7 for more than thirty (30) consecutive days, in which case Customer may source substitute Products from third parties for the affected varieties and quantities, provided that Customer gives Supplier prompt written notice and an opportunity to cure."

**Open Question for Client:** Does Sovex currently purchase, sell, or distribute any corn, soybean, or sorghum seed from any source other than Ludis, anywhere in the United States or Canada? If yes, Sovex is in present breach of § 4.1 and needs to address this immediately.

## Issue 3: March 30 Germination Claim – Likely Time-Barred Under § 8.4

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 8.4 (Germination Claims – 21-day notice); Order Form No. 2, Recital B (delivery February 20, 2026; notice March 30, 2026; Ludis rejected as untimely on April 3, 2026); Order Form No. 2 § 7.1–7.2 (reservation of rights).

**Why It Matters:**

The Products were delivered on February 20, 2026. Section 8.4 requires written notice of a germination failure "within 21 days after the Delivery Date." Twenty-one days after February 20 is March 13, 2026. Sovex's notice was dated March 30, 2026—38 days after delivery, 17 days late. Section 8.4 provides that failure to give timely notice means the Products are "deemed accepted as to germination."

Ludis rejected the claim on April 3, 2026. Sovex has reserved its rights in Order Form No. 2 §§ 7.1–7.2, which is procedurally correct, but a reservation of rights does not cure a contractual time bar. The practical question is whether the 21-day period is enforceable as written or whether it can be equitably tolled or construed differently.

Arguments in Sovex's favor:
- The 21-day period may be unreasonably short for a distributor that must receive the seed, select lots, send samples to an accredited laboratory (§ 8.3), receive results, and then give notice. If the lab turnaround alone exceeds 21 days, the clause may be effectively impossible to perform.
- Under New York law (governing per § 19.1), a contractual waiver of claims that operates as an exculpatory clause for known defects may be scrutinized, though this is a weaker argument for a notice period.
- If Ludis knew or should have known of the germination failure (e.g., from its own testing under § 8.2), equitable estoppel or waiver might apply.

Arguments in Ludis's favor:
- The clause is clear and unambiguous. Sovex is a sophisticated commercial party with its own legal department.
- Sovex had the ability to inspect and test "after delivery" (§ 8.3) and presumably knew or should have known of the issue well before 21 days.
- The reservation of rights in Order Form No. 2 was negotiated after Ludis's rejection, suggesting Sovex conceded the timeliness issue was at least contested.

**Recommended Fix:**

This is a live dispute. The immediate recommendation is:

1. **Preserve evidence.** Confirm that the laboratory reports supporting the March 30 Claim are complete, dated, and show when samples were submitted and when results were received. If the lab was not engaged until after day 21, that undermines the claim. If the lab was engaged promptly but results took longer, that supports an argument that the 21-day period was impracticable.

2. **Assess settlement value.** The claim involves certain lots from Order Form No. 1. The financial exposure depends on the value of those lots and the degree of germination failure.

3. **For going forward,** negotiate an amendment to § 8.4 to extend the notice period or tie it to lab results. Proposed language:

> "Customer must give Supplier written notice of any claim that Products fail to meet the germination rate in the Specifications within thirty (30) days after Customer receives the results of a warm germination test conducted at an accredited seed laboratory, provided that Customer has submitted the sample for testing no later than fourteen (14) days after the Delivery Date. If Customer does not comply with this Section 8.4, the Products are deemed accepted as to germination."

**Open Question for Client:** (a) What is the dollar value of the lots at issue in the March 30 Claim? (b) When exactly were the samples submitted to the lab, and when were results received? (c) Does Sovex want to pursue this claim aggressively or is it amenable to a commercial resolution as part of a broader negotiation on the other issues?

## Issue 4: Minimum Purchase Commitment with Uncapped Shortfall Fee

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 5.1–5.3; Order Form No. 2 § 3.2–3.3 (40,000 unit minimum; $42.00 per-unit shortfall rate); Supply Agreement § 12.3(d) (shortfall fee excluded from liability cap).

**Why It Matters:**

Sovex has committed to purchasing at least 40,000 units for the 2026–2027 Season. If actual purchases fall below that, Sovex owes $42.00 per unit of shortfall, payable within 30 days of invoice. The shortfall fee is excluded from the liability cap (§ 12.3(d)), so it is a firm, uncapped obligation.

Maximum exposure: If Sovex purchases zero units (an extreme case), the shortfall is 40,000 × $42 = **$1,680,000**. More realistically, if the market softens and Sovex sells only 32,000 units, the shortfall is 8,000 × $42 = **$336,000**.

The only reduction mechanism is § 5.3 (adjustment for supply failure—reduction unit for unit if Ludis fails to deliver or Products are rejected under § 8). Given the March 30 Claim dispute, if that claim is ultimately sustained, the minimum would be reduced. But if it is time-barred (as in Issue 3), no reduction applies.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Negotiate a lower shortfall rate or a cap on the total shortfall fee (e.g., not to exceed 25% of the total Order Form value).
2. Add a market-condition adjustment: if overall seed demand in the Territory declines by more than a stated percentage (e.g., 15%) due to factors beyond Sovex's control (drought, economic downturn, regulatory change), the minimum is reduced proportionally.
3. Clarify that § 5.3 applies not only to rejected Products but also to Products for which a valid germination claim is pending.

Proposed language for a market-condition adjustment:

> "If, due to conditions beyond Customer's reasonable control (including but not limited to drought, flood, significant reduction in planted acreage in the Territory, or adverse regulatory action), total hybrid corn seed demand in the Territory for the applicable Season declines by more than fifteen percent (15%) compared to the prior Season, the Minimum Purchase Commitment shall be reduced by the same percentage, upon Customer's written notice to Supplier with reasonable supporting data."

**Open Question for Client:** What is Sovex's current sales forecast for the 2026–2027 Season? Is the 40,000-unit minimum achievable based on current grower commitments and market conditions?

## Issue 5: Data Breach Liability Capped at 12 Months of Fees

**Documents and Sections:** DPA § 13.2 (liability subject to Agreement § 12); Supply Agreement § 12.1 (cap = 12 months of fees); Supply Agreement § 12.3 (exclusions—data breach not listed).

**Why It Matters:**

DPA § 13.2 states that "Each Party's liability under this Addendum is subject to Section 12 of the Agreement." Section 12.1 caps liability at fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event. Section 12.3 lists exclusions from the cap: indemnification (§ 11), confidentiality breach (§ 14), gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and payment obligations. A data breach by Ludis that is merely negligent (not grossly negligent) would be **subject to the cap**.

If Ludis suffers a Security Incident exposing Sovex's growers' personal data, field data, and yield data, Sovex could face:
- Regulatory penalties under the Iowa Consumer Data Protection Act and Nebraska Data Privacy Act (both listed in DPA § 1.2);
- Class-action or individual lawsuits from growers;
- Reputational damage and loss of grower relationships;
- Costs of notification, credit monitoring, and remediation (which DPA § 8.4 puts on Ludis only "to the extent a Security Incident results from Supplier's or its Subprocessor's breach of this Addendum").

The 12-month fee cap (roughly $14M based on Order Form No. 2 values) may be insufficient to cover a serious data breach involving thousands of growers' personal and operational data.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add data protection obligations to the list of exclusions from the cap in § 12.3, or create a separate super-cap in the DPA. Proposed language for DPA § 13.2:

> "Each Party's liability under this Addendum is subject to Section 12 of the Agreement, except that the cap in Section 12.1 shall not apply to (a) Supplier's obligations under Sections 3, 6, 7, and 8 of this Addendum, or (b) any regulatory fines, penalties or assessments imposed on Customer as a result of a Security Incident caused by Supplier's or its Subprocessor's breach of this Addendum. In lieu of an uncapped obligation, the Parties agree that Supplier's aggregate liability for data protection claims under this Addendum shall not exceed [dollar amount or multiple of the cap, e.g., three times the Section 12.1 cap]."

## Issue 6: Auto-Renewal with Narrow 30-Day Notice Window

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 2.2 (Renewal).

**Why It Matters:**

The Initial Term ends December 31, 2028. The Agreement auto-renews for successive three-year terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal "not more than 150 days and not less than 120 days before the end of the then-current Term." For the Initial Term, that window is **July 4, 2028 through August 3, 2028**—a 30-day window. If Sovex misses it, it is locked in for another three years (through December 31, 2031) on the same terms, including the exclusivity, minimum purchase, and indemnity obligations identified above.

Given the breadth of the current terms, auto-renewal is a significant trap.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Extend the notice window (e.g., 180–120 days) to reduce the risk of missing it.
2. Add a requirement that the renewing party confirm the renewal in writing, so that silence does not operate as renewal.
3. Alternatively, convert to a non-renewal model: the Agreement does not renew unless both parties affirmatively agree in writing.

Proposed language:

> "At the end of the Initial Term, this Agreement will renew for successive three (3) year terms only if both Parties deliver written notice of intent to renew to the other Party not less than one hundred twenty (120) days before the end of the then-current Term. If either Party fails to deliver such notice, this Agreement will expire at the end of the then-current Term."

**Action Item:** Calendar the non-renewal notice window (July 4 – August 3, 2028) immediately, with internal reminders starting no later than May 2028.

## Issue 7: Ambiguous Risk of Loss – FCA vs. Delivery Location

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 1.3 (Delivery Date defined as delivery to Delivery Location), § 7.1 (FCA Supplier's designated facility), § 7.3 (title and risk pass on Delivery Date).

**Why It Matters:**

Section 7.1 states that Supplier will deliver "FCA (Incoterms 2020) Supplier's designated facility, for carriage to the delivery location." Under FCA, the seller's delivery obligation is complete when the goods are placed at the carrier's disposal at the named place (Supplier's facility). Risk of loss passes to the buyer at that point under standard Incoterms.

However, § 7.3 states that "Title to and risk of loss of Products pass to Customer on the Delivery Date," and § 1.3 defines Delivery Date as "the date on which the Products are delivered to the Delivery Location stated in the applicable Order Form" (i.e., Sovex's warehouse in Ames, Iowa).

These two provisions are in tension. If a shipment is damaged in transit between Ludis's facility and Sovex's warehouse, Ludis could argue that risk passed at its facility under FCA, while Sovex would argue risk passed at its warehouse under § 7.3. The Agreement's order of precedence (§ 19.2) does not resolve this because both provisions are in the same document.

In practice, the specific terms (§§ 1.3 and 7.3) should likely control over the general Incoterms reference in § 7.1, but the ambiguity creates litigation risk in a total-loss scenario.

**Recommended Fix:**

Delete the FCA reference and state the delivery term clearly. Proposed language for § 7.1:

> "Supplier will deliver Products to the Delivery Location stated in the applicable Order Form. Supplier is responsible for arranging and paying for carriage to the Delivery Location. Title to and risk of loss of Products pass to Customer upon delivery to the Delivery Location, as provided in Section 7.3."

## Issue 8: Conflicting Governing Law and Forum (New York vs. Iowa)

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 19.1 (New York law; New York County courts); DPA § 12.1–12.2 (Iowa law; Polk County, Iowa courts); Supply Agreement § 19.2 (DPA prevails on data protection matters).

**Why It Matters:**

The relationship is governed by two different bodies of law in two different forums. For a dispute that implicates both the commercial terms (e.g., a germination claim that also involves grower data used in the analysis) and data protection, the parties could end up in parallel or sequential proceedings in New York and Iowa. This increases cost, creates the risk of inconsistent rulings, and complicates settlement strategy.

While § 19.2 attempts to allocate (DPA prevails on "data protection matters"), the line between a data protection issue and a commercial issue is often blurry in practice.

**Recommended Fix:**

Conform the DPA to the Agreement's governing law and forum, or vice versa. Given that Sovex is an Iowa company and the DPA involves Iowa and Nebraska data protection laws, Iowa law and forum may be the more practical choice for both documents. Alternatively, keep New York for the commercial agreement but add a provision in the DPA that any data protection dispute arising from the same transaction as a commercial dispute will be consolidated in the commercial forum.

Proposed language (if conforming DPA to Agreement):

> "Section 12.1: This Addendum, and any dispute arising out of or relating to it, is governed by the laws of the State of New York, without regard to its conflict of laws rules. Section 12.2: Each Party submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in New York County, New York, for any such dispute."

## Issue 9: No Priority Allocation in Supply Shortage

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 16.2 (allocation "fairly and on a non-discriminatory basis").

**Why It Matters:**

If Ludis experiences a crop shortfall or force majeure event that limits supply, § 16.2 requires only that Ludis allocate "fairly and on a non-discriminatory basis" among "its customers." As the exclusive distributor for Iowa and Nebraska, Sovex is not given any priority. Ludis could allocate a larger share to its direct national account customers or to distributors in other territories, leaving Sovex short.

This is particularly acute because of the exclusivity obligation (§ 4.1): if Sovex cannot get enough seed from Ludis, it cannot buy from anyone else.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add a priority allocation provision. Proposed language:

> "If a force majeure event or crop shortfall limits Supplier's available supply of any Product, Supplier will first allocate available supply to fulfill Customer's Minimum Purchase Commitment for the affected Season, and will then allocate remaining supply among its other customers fairly and on a non-discriminatory basis. If supply is insufficient to meet Customer's Minimum Purchase Commitment in full, the Minimum Purchase Commitment shall be reduced in accordance with Section 5.3."

## Issue 10: DPA Aggregated Data – Broad Use Rights with Weak De-Identification Standard

**Documents and Sections:** DPA § 1.1 (definition of Aggregated Data), § 4.2 (Supplier's right to use Aggregated Data for "internal research and product development").

**Why It Matters:**

Ludis can create Aggregated Data from Sovex's growers' data and use it for "internal research and product development." The only restriction is that it cannot be disclosed "in a form that identifies Customer" (§ 4.2, second sentence). There is no restriction on:
- Using the data to develop products for sale to other distributors in other territories (potentially at lower prices, undercutting Sovex's margins);
- Using the data to improve agronomic recommendations that Ludis provides directly to its national account customers;
- Sharing the Aggregated Data with third parties (e.g., equipment manufacturers, input companies) as long as Sovex is not identified.

The de-identification standard ("cannot reasonably be used to identify any Grower, any individual or any specific field") may be weak in a small market. In Iowa and Nebraska, a combination of field location, hybrid, seeding rate, and yield data could be re-identified by someone with local knowledge, even if it is "de-identified" in the abstract.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Narrow the Permitted Purposes for Aggregated Data to exclude product development that results in products sold in competition with the Products in the Territory.
2. Add a re-identification prohibition that survives de-identification: "Supplier will not attempt to re-identify any Grower, individual, or field from Aggregated Data, and will not use Aggregated Data in combination with other data sources in a manner that would reasonably permit such re-identification."
3. Require that Aggregated Data used for product development be subject to a minimum sample size threshold (e.g., data from no fewer than 500 fields) to reduce re-identification risk.

## Issue 11: Insurance Certificate – Coverage Period and Renewal Tracking

**Documents and Sections:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter §§ 2.2, 3.2 (policy period January 1, 2026 – January 1, 2027); Supply Agreement § 13.1 (coverage required "during the Term and for two (2) years afterward"), § 13.3 (certificates on each renewal).

**Why It Matters:**

The certificate evidences coverage only through January 1, 2027. The Agreement requires coverage through at least December 31, 2030 (Initial Term ending December 31, 2028, plus two years). The broker states it will issue renewal certificates "promptly after each renewal binds" (§ 5.1), but there is no penalty or consequence stated if a renewal certificate is late. If coverage lapses or a renewal certificate is not delivered within 30 days of the Effective Date or each renewal (§ 13.3), Sovex would be in breach.

Additionally, the workers' compensation policy (Heartland Casualty, A- rated) meets the "required by law" standard, but the A.M. Best rating requirement in § 13.1 applies to "each Party" maintaining CGL at A- VII or better. The CGL insurer (Prairie Mutual) is rated A (Excellent), FSC XII, which satisfies the requirement.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Establish an internal tracking system for insurance renewals (January 1 annually) with a 60-day advance reminder to the broker.
2. Confirm with the broker that the additional insured endorsement (CG 20 15 equivalent) will be renewed with each policy renewal and that the 30-day cancellation notice endorsement will remain in place.
3. Consider whether the $5M per occurrence limit is adequate given the uncapped indemnity in Issue 1. If Sovex expects large grower claims, a higher limit or an umbrella/excess policy may be warranted.

**Open Question for Client:** Does Sovex carry any excess/umbrella liability insurance above the $5M CGL limit? What is the total liability tower?

## Issue 12: Variety Allocation Deadline Gives Ludis Control

**Documents and Sections:** Order Form No. 2 § 5.3 (Customer to provide variety allocation by November 1, 2026, or Supplier allocates pro rata).

**Why It Matters:**

If Sovex does not provide its variety allocation among the three delivery tranches by November 1, 2026, Ludis will allocate each variety pro rata across the tranches. This may not match Sovex's actual sales pipeline. For example, if Sovex has strong demand for the earlier-maturing varieties (LG-1048, LG-1082) for its western Nebraska growers who plant earlier, a pro rata allocation could front-load the later-maturing varieties (LG-1127, LG-1146) in Tranche 1, creating a mismatch with grower needs and potentially forcing Sovex to hold inventory longer than optimal.

**Recommended Fix:**

Extend the deadline to December 1, 2026 (giving Sovex more time to firm up grower orders), or allow Sovex to provide a revised allocation up to 30 days before each tranche's Delivery Date. Proposed language:

> "Customer will give Supplier its allocation of varieties among tranches by written notice no later than December 1, 2026, and may revise the allocation for any tranche by written notice no later than thirty (30) days before that tranche's Scheduled Delivery Date. If Customer does not provide an allocation by the applicable deadline, Supplier will allocate each variety pro rata across the tranches."

## Issue 13: Return Policy – Restocking Charge and Shelf-Life Limitation on Treated Seed

**Documents and Sections:** Order Form No. 2 § 6.2 (Return Policy); Supply Agreement § 6.7 (Returns).

**Why It Matters:**

Sovex may return up to 10% of total units delivered (4,800 units), by June 30, 2027, at the net price paid less a 5% restocking charge. Treated Products are returnable only if within the treatment's labeled shelf life on the date of return.

The 5% restocking charge reduces the effective credit. On the average price of ~$305/unit (before the 4% early order discount, so ~$293 net), the restocking charge is ~$14.65/unit. For 4,800 units, the total restocking cost is ~$70,000.

The shelf-life limitation on treated seed is a practical concern: if the seed treatment has a 12-month shelf life from date of treatment, and the seed is treated in late 2026 for spring 2027 planting, the shelf life may expire before the June 30, 2027 return deadline. Sovex could be left with treated seed it cannot return.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Negotiate to eliminate or reduce the restocking charge (e.g., 0% for returns made within 60 days of delivery).
2. Clarify the shelf-life issue: confirm the labeled shelf life of the seed treatments in Exhibit A and ensure the return deadline falls within it. If not, extend the return deadline for treated Products to the later of June 30, 2027 or 90 days before the expiration of the treatment's labeled shelf life.

**Open Question for Client:** What is the labeled shelf life of the seed treatments used on the 2026–2027 Season Products? Is it likely to extend past June 30, 2027?

## Issue 14: Supplier's Price Change Right

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 6.1 (Supplier may change price list for a future Season with 90 days' notice before the Order Form deadline).

**Why It Matters:**

Ludis can unilaterally increase prices for future seasons with 90 days' notice. The only constraint is that the Order Form must be "consistent with Customer's most recent forecast and the then-current price list" for Ludis to be obligated to sign (§ 3.3). If Ludis raises prices significantly, Sovex is in a bind: it must either accept the higher prices (eroding margins) or not place an order (triggering the shortfall fee under § 5.2, since the Minimum Purchase Commitment is stated in units, not dollars).

There is no price cap, escalation formula, or most-favored-customer provision.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add a price protection mechanism. Proposed language:

> "Supplier may increase prices for a future Season by no more than five percent (5%) over the prices in the immediately preceding Season's Order Form, unless the increase is necessitated by a documented increase in raw material or input costs exceeding five percent (5%), in which case Supplier may pass through the excess cost with supporting documentation. Any price increase requires written notice at least one hundred twenty (120) days before the Order Form deadline for the affected Season."

## Issue 15: DPA Subprocessor – FieldLine Analytics

**Documents and Sections:** DPA Annex 1 (FieldLine Analytics, LLC – "Yield data normalization and agronomic modeling").

**Why It Matters:**

FieldLine Analytics is a third-party subprocessor that handles yield data normalization and agronomic modeling. This means Sovex's growers' yield data (one of the most sensitive categories in DPA § 1.3(f)) is being processed by a third party. While DPA § 7.3 requires Supplier to impose "no less protective" obligations on Subprocessors and § 7.4 restricts processing to the United States, Sovex has no direct contractual relationship with FieldLine Analytics and limited visibility into its security practices.

The audit right in DPA § 10.2 is limited to once per 12 months and requires 30 days' notice, which may not be sufficient if a concern arises about FieldLine specifically.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Request a copy of the data processing agreement between Ludis and FieldLine Analytics (DPA § 10.1 entitles Sovex to "information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance," which should include subprocessor agreements).
2. Confirm that FieldLine Analytics' security certifications (e.g., SOC 2) are current.
3. Consider adding FieldLine Analytics to the notice chain for Security Incidents, or at minimum confirming that Ludis's 72-hour notice obligation (DPA § 8.1) covers incidents at the subprocessor level.

**Open Question for Client:** Is Sovex comfortable with a third party (FieldLine Analytics) having access to its growers' yield data? Are there any grower contracts or commitments that restrict sharing data with third parties?

## Issue 16: Confidentiality – Grower Data Excluded from Agreement's Confidentiality Regime

**Documents and Sections:** Supply Agreement § 14.1 (last sentence: "Grower Data is governed by the Data Processing Addendum"); DPA § 2.3 (ownership).

**Why It Matters:**

Grower Data is carved out of the Agreement's confidentiality provisions (§ 14) and governed solely by the DPA. This means that the DPA's protections apply, but the Agreement's broader confidentiality obligations (e.g., the three-year post-term survival in § 14.4, the trade secret protection) do not extend to Grower Data. The DPA's survival provision (§ 13.3) covers §§ 2.3, 3.3, 3.4, 8, 9.3, 11, and 12—but not § 3.1 (instructions), § 3.2 (compliance), § 5 (personnel), or § 6 (security). So after termination, the security and personnel obligations in the DPA do not explicitly survive, even though § 11 (return/deletion) does.

In practice, once the data is returned or deleted (§ 11.2), the security obligations become moot. But if any data is retained under § 11.3 (to the extent required by law), the question is what obligations apply to that retained data. § 11.3 states that retained data "remains subject to this Addendum," which should preserve the security obligations, but the survival clause (§ 13.3) does not list § 6. This is an internal inconsistency in the DPA.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add § 6 (Security) and § 5 (Personnel) to the DPA's survival clause (§ 13.3), at least to the extent data is retained under § 11.3. Proposed amendment to DPA § 13.3:

> "Sections 2.3, 3.3, 3.4, 5, 6, 8, 9.3, 11 and 12 survive expiration or termination of the Agreement, with Sections 5 and 6 applying for so long as any Grower Data is retained under Section 11.3."
