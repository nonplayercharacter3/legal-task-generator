## Tier 1 – Highest Exposure

### 1. One-Way Indemnification for Grower Claims "Whatever the Cause"

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 11.2 (indemnification by Customer), read with § 1.6 (definition of "Grower Claims").

**Issue:** Section 11.2 requires Sovex to defend and indemnify Ludis against "any claim, demand, suit or proceeding arising out of or relating to Customer's use, handling, storage, resale or planting of Products, **including any Grower Claims**." The definition of "Grower Claims" in § 1.6 is: "any claim, demand, suit or proceeding brought by a Grower relating to the crop stand, emergence, yield or performance of Products, **whatever the cause**."

This means Sovex indemnifies Ludis for grower claims even where the claim is caused by a defect in the seed itself. If a lot of seed fails to germinate because of a manufacturing or genetic problem at Ludis, and the affected grower sues, Sovex must defend and indemnify Ludis for that claim. The only scenario in which Sovex would *not* owe this indemnity is if the grower's claim falls outside the "crop stand, emergence, yield or performance" language, which is difficult to imagine for a seed product.

**Why it matters:** This inverts the normal risk allocation. The party that manufactures the product (Ludis) is shielded from product-performance claims by the party that merely distributes it (Sovex). Given that Sovex is the entity that interacts with growers and is the natural first target for grower complaints, this clause exposes Sovex to potentially significant liability for Ludis's own product failures. The March 30 Claim (see Issue 3 below) illustrates the practical risk: if those germination failures cause grower losses and growers bring claims, § 11.2 could require Sovex to indemnify Ludis for them.

**Recommendation:** Amend § 11.2 to carve out claims to the extent caused by Supplier's breach of warranty under § 8.1, Supplier's negligence, or a defect in the Products. Proposed language:

> "Customer will defend Supplier … against any claim … arising out of or relating to Customer's use, handling, storage, resale or planting of Products, including any Grower Claims; **provided, however, that Customer shall have no obligation under this Section 11.2 to the extent any such claim is caused by or arises from (i) Supplier's breach of the warranty in Section 8.1, (ii) a defect in the Products, or (iii) Supplier's negligence or willful misconduct.**"

Alternatively, at minimum, add a mutual offset: if Sovex's indemnity obligation to Ludis under § 11.2 is triggered by a product defect, Sovex should be entitled to recover from Ludis under § 8.6 (remedies) and/or § 11.1 (Supplier's IP indemnity, by analogy).

### 2. Nationwide Exclusivity Across Three Crop Species

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 4.1.

**Issue:** Section 4.1 provides: "During the Term, Customer shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute any **corn, soybean or sorghum seed** from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier **anywhere in the United States and Canada**."

This is not limited to the Territory (Iowa and Nebraska, per Order Form No. 2 § 2.2). It is not limited to the Products (Ludis hybrid corn). It covers three crop species and the entire North American market. The Term runs to December 31, 2028, and auto-renews for successive three-year periods (§ 2.2).

**Why it matters:** If Sovex currently distributes soybean or sorghum seed from other suppliers, or corn seed from other suppliers, this clause prohibits it. Even if Sovex does not currently carry those lines, the clause prevents Sovex from adding them during the Term. For a regional distributor whose business model depends on offering growers a full line of seed, this could be existential. The clause also restricts Sovex's Affiliates, which could affect group-wide strategy.

There is no materiality qualifier, no "to the extent Supplier does not offer a comparable product" exception, and no right to terminate for loss of exclusivity value if Ludis's product line narrows.

**Recommendation:** This is the single most commercially significant clause in the Agreement. Sovex should:

- Narrow the scope to **corn seed only** (the product Ludis actually makes), or at minimum to "Products" as defined.
- Limit the geographic scope to the **Territory** stated in the applicable Order Form, not all of the US and Canada.
- Add a **materiality or availability carve-out**: "provided that Customer may purchase, sell or distribute seed from other suppliers to the extent Supplier does not offer a comparable product in the same crop species and relative maturity range."
- Add a **termination right** for Sovex if Ludis fails to maintain a product line sufficient to meet Sovex's customers' needs.

Proposed replacement for § 4.1:

> "During the Term, Customer shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute any hybrid corn seed from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier **within the Territory**, provided that Customer may continue to sell soybean and sorghum seed from other suppliers and may sell corn seed from other suppliers to the extent Supplier does not offer a comparable product."

**Open question:** Does Sovex currently sell soybean or sorghum seed, or corn seed from other suppliers? If so, this clause is already in breach or requires an immediate carve-out. This needs client input before we can assess the urgency of renegotiation.

### 3. The March 30 Claim – Untimely Notice Under § 8.4

**Document/Section:** Order Form No. 2, Recital B; Supply Agreement § 8.4.

**Issue:** The Delivery Date for Order Form No. 1 was February 20, 2026. Sovex gave written notice of germination failure on March 30, 2026 — **38 days** after delivery. Section 8.4 requires notice "within **21 days** after the Delivery Date." Ludis rejected the notice as untimely on April 3, 2026.

Order Form No. 2 § 7 includes a reservation of rights, which preserves the claim procedurally but does not cure the timing problem.

**Why it matters:** If the 21-day deadline is enforced, Sovex loses its remedy under § 8.6 (replacement or refund) for the affected lots. The seed has presumably been planted by now (spring 2026 planting season), so the practical question is whether Sovex can recover for grower losses caused by the germination failure. If § 8.4 bars the warranty claim, Sovex may be left holding the bag on grower complaints — and under § 11.2 (see Issue 1), it may even have to indemnify Ludis for those grower claims.

**Potential arguments for Sovex:**

- **Impracticability / reasonable time:** Warm germination testing at an accredited laboratory (§ 8.3) may not be completable within 21 days of delivery, particularly if the seed has already been planted and field emergence data must be collected. The clause deems products "accepted as to germination" if no notice is given, but a court might read this as requiring notice within a reasonable time after the failure becomes *known or knowable*, not within 21 days of delivery regardless of when the problem manifests.
- **Course of dealing / waiver:** If this is not the first time Sovex has raised a post-21-day issue and Ludis has accepted it, there may be an argument of waiver or estoppel.
- **The deemed-acceptance language is a condition precedent, not a limitation period:** Some jurisdictions treat such clauses as waivable or subject to unconscionability analysis, particularly in adhesion contexts. However, this is a negotiated B2B agreement, so that argument is weaker.

**Recommendation:**

- **Immediately:** Assess the strength of the timeliness defense. If the lab results show a germination rate well below 95% and the failure was not discoverable within 21 days (e.g., because the seed was planted before lab results came back), prepare a supplemental letter arguing that the 21-day period should run from when the failure was or should have been known.
- **Going forward:** Amend § 8.4 to extend the notice period to **60 days** after the Delivery Date, or alternatively to **30 days after Customer has reasonable cause to believe the Products fail to meet the Specifications** (whichever is later). For seed products, 21 days is operationally unrealistic.
- **Negotiating leverage:** The reservation of rights in Order Form No. 2 § 7 shows Sovex has not conceded the point. If the relationship is to continue, this dispute should be resolved as part of any amendment package.

**Open question:** What do the laboratory results actually show? What was the germination rate versus the 95% specification? Was the seed planted before the lab results were available? These facts are critical to assessing the strength of Sovex's position and need client input.

### 4. Asymmetric Liability Cap

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 12.1 (cap), § 12.3(d) (exclusion of Customer's payment obligations from the cap).

**Issue:** Section 12.1 caps each party's total liability at "Fees paid or payable by Customer under this Agreement in the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the liability." However, § 12.3(d) excludes "Customer's obligation to pay Fees and any shortfall fee under Section 5.2" from both the cap and the consequential damages exclusion.

This creates an asymmetry: Sovex's obligation to pay Ludis (including shortfall fees) is **uncapped**, while Sovex's ability to recover from Ludis for any breach (including delivery of nonconforming seed, IP infringement, or data breach) is **capped** at 12 months of fees.

**Why it matters:** For the 2026–2027 Season, total fees are approximately 48,000 units × ~$309 average (before the 4% discount) ≈ $14.8 million. The 12-month cap would therefore be roughly $14.8 million. If Ludis delivers defective seed that causes Sovex to lose grower relationships and face grower claims (which, under § 11.2, Sovex must indemnify Ludis for), Sovex's recovery from Ludis is capped at ~$14.8 million, while its exposure to growers and its indemnity obligation to Ludis are uncapped.

**Recommendation:**

- Delete § 12.3(d) or narrow it to exclude only "undisputed Fees for conforming Products delivered."
- Add a **super-cap** for Supplier's breach of warranty (§ 8.1) and Supplier's indemnity obligation (§ 11.1): e.g., "Notwithstanding Section 12.1, Supplier's liability under Sections 8 and 11.1 shall not be less than two times (2×) the cap stated in Section 12.1."
- At minimum, ensure that the cap in § 12.1 is calculated on a **rolling 24-month** basis rather than 12 months, to better reflect the multi-season nature of the relationship.

## Tier 2 – Significant Exposure

### 5. Governing Law and Venue – New York

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 19.1.

**Issue:** The Agreement is governed by New York law and disputes must be brought in New York County courts. Neither party is headquartered in New York. Ludis is in Champaign, Illinois; Sovex is in Ames, Iowa.

**Why it matters:** Litigating in New York is expensive and inconvenient for Sovex. New York law is generally pro-contract and may not interpret the 21-day germination notice period (§ 8.4) or the "whatever the cause" indemnity (§ 11.2) more favorably to Sovex than Iowa or Illinois law would. Additionally, the DPA is governed by Iowa law with Polk County venue (DPA § 12.1–12.2), creating a split that complicates any integrated dispute.

**Recommendation:** Propose Illinois law and venue (Ludis's home state) or Iowa law and venue (Sovex's home state). If Ludis insists on a neutral forum, propose the state where the products are delivered (Iowa) or arbitration under AAA commercial rules. At minimum, harmonize the governing law between the Supply Agreement and the DPA to avoid the current split.

### 6. No Evidence of Ludis's Insurance Compliance

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 13.1–13.3; Insurance Certificate Cover Letter (Sovex's certificate only).

**Issue:** Section 13.3 requires **each** Party to deliver a certificate of insurance within 30 days of the Effective Date and on each renewal. The matter folder contains only Sovex's certificate (delivered February 10, 2026, within the 30-day window). There is no certificate from Ludis.

Additionally, § 13.2 requires Ludis to name Sovex as an additional insured under its products liability coverage "as respects the Products." We have no evidence this has been done.

**Why it matters:** If a grower is injured by a product defect and brings a claim, Sovex wants the comfort that Ludis carries adequate products liability insurance and that Sovex is named as an additional insured so it can look to Ludis's policy for coverage. Without confirmation, Sovex may be relying on Ludis's indemnity (§ 11.1) and its own CGL policy, which may be insufficient or may have exclusions for product defects originating with the manufacturer.

**Recommendation:**

- Request Ludis's certificate of insurance and the additional insured endorsement naming Sovex, immediately.
- Confirm that Ludis's products liability limits meet the $5 million per occurrence minimum in § 13.1(a).
- If Ludis has not complied, put it on notice in writing under § 18.1 and reserve rights.

**Open question:** Has Sovex received Ludis's certificate? If not, why not, and has Sovex raised the issue with Ludis?

### 7. DPA – Aggregated Data Use for Product Development

**Document/Section:** Data Processing Addendum § 4.2.

**Issue:** Section 4.2 permits Ludis to "create Aggregated Data from Grower Data and … use Aggregated Data for its **internal research and product development**." The only restriction is that Ludis will not disclose Aggregated Data "in a form that identifies Customer."

**Why it matters:** Over time, as Sovex shares planting data, yield data, soil data, and hybrid placement data for its Iowa and Nebraska growers, Ludis will build a rich dataset on exactly which hybrids perform where, at what populations, on what soil types. Ludis can use this to develop new products targeted at Sovex's specific market, potentially reducing Sovex's differentiation as a distributor. The "does not identify Customer" restriction is weak — if Ludis develops a new hybrid specifically for the Iowa/Nebraska market based on Sovex's data, it can sell that hybrid to other distributors in the same region without identifying Sovex.

**Recommendation:**

- Add a restriction that Aggregated Data derived from Sovex's Grower Data will not be used to develop products for sale in the **Territory** during the Term.
- Alternatively, require that any product developed using Aggregated Data from Sovex's Grower Data be offered to Sovex on the same terms as to other distributors (no exclusivity advantage to competing distributors).
- At minimum, require annual reporting to Sovex on whether Aggregated Data has been used in product development.

### 8. Auto-Renewal with Narrow Notice Window

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 2.2.

**Issue:** The Agreement auto-renews for successive three-year terms unless either party gives notice of non-renewal "not more than 150 days and not less than 120 days before the end of the then-current Term." This is a **30-day window** (days 120–150 before term end).

**Why it matters:** Missing this window locks Sovex into another three-year term, including the exclusivity restriction (§ 4.1), the indemnity (§ 11.2), and all other terms. Given the issues identified above, Sovex may not want to renew on the current terms, but a missed deadline would bind it for three more years. The Initial Term ends December 31, 2028, so the notice window would be approximately July 3 – August 2, 2028.

**Recommendation:**

- Calendar the notice deadline immediately (July 3, 2028 at the latest; target June 1, 2028 to be safe).
- Negotiate to extend the window to "not more than 180 days and not less than 90 days" to reduce the risk of missing it.
- Add a requirement that the renewing party send a reminder notice 200 days before the window opens.

## Tier 3 – Moderate / Operational Issues

### 9. Shortfall Fee Exposure

**Document/Section:** Order Form No. 2 § 3.2–3.3; Supply Agreement § 5.2.

**Issue:** The Minimum Purchase Commitment is 40,000 units; the ordered quantity is 48,000 units. The shortfall rate is $42.00 per unit. Maximum shortfall exposure: (48,000 − 40,000) × $42 = **$336,000**.

**Why it matters:** This is a meaningful sum, but it is capped and predictable. The more significant issue is that § 5.3 reduces the minimum only for supply failures or rejections under § 8. If the March 30 Claim is successful and those lots are "rejected," the minimum for the 2026 Season (under Order Form No. 1) would be reduced. But for the 2026–2027 Season, the $336,000 exposure stands unless Ludis fails to deliver.

**Recommendation:** Acceptable as drafted, but ensure that any resolution of the March 30 Claim (Issue 3) accounts for whether those rejected units reduce the 2026 Season minimum under Order Form No. 1. Confirm with the client whether Order Form No. 1 had its own minimum and shortfall rate.

### 10. Force Majeure – No Priority Allocation

**Document/Section:** Supply Agreement § 16.2.

**Issue:** If a force majeure event or crop shortfall limits Ludis's supply, Ludis will allocate "among its customers fairly and on a non-discriminatory basis." There is no priority for Sovex despite the exclusivity arrangement.

**Why it matters:** In a supply-constrained year (drought, disease, production failure), Sovex could receive less seed than it needs to serve its growers, while Ludis's other customers (in other territories, or direct national accounts) receive proportional allocations. Given that Sovex has committed to exclusivity and a minimum purchase, it bears the downside of a supply shortfall without receiving the upside of priority.

**Recommendation:** Add: "In the event of a supply limitation, Supplier will allocate available Products to Customer on a priority basis sufficient to meet Customer's Minimum Purchase Commitment for the affected Season, before allocating to other customers."

---

### 11. DPA Governing Law Split

**Document/Section:** DPA § 12.1–12.2 (Iowa law, Polk County venue) vs. Supply Agreement § 19.1 (New York law, New York County venue).

**Issue:** The Supply Agreement § 19.2 provides that the DPA "prevails on data protection matters," but the two documents have different governing laws and venues. A dispute that involves both data protection and commercial terms (e.g., a data breach that also constitutes a breach of the confidentiality clause) could raise threshold questions about which law and forum applies.

**Recommendation:** Harmonize to a single governing law and venue for all documents in the arrangement. Iowa is the natural choice given Sovex's location and the data protection context.

---

### 12. Return Policy – 10% Cap and Restocking Charge

**Document/Section:** Order Form No. 2 § 6.2.

**Issue:** Sovex may return up to 10% of total units delivered (4,800 units), by June 30, 2027, subject to a 5% restocking charge. Treated products must be within labeled shelf life.

**Why it matters:** The 10% cap means Sovex can return at most 4,800 of 48,000 units. If Sovex over-ordered or if market demand shifts, it is stuck with the remainder. The 5% restocking charge reduces the credit. The June 30, 2027 deadline is after the planting season, which is reasonable, but if Sovex needs to return product early (e.g., due to a variety being pulled from the market), the clause does not address that.

**Recommendation:**

- Increase the return cap to 15% or tie it to the difference between ordered and minimum units (8,000 units in this case).
- Add: "Customer may return any Product in its entirety if Supplier withdraws that variety from its product line or if a regulatory action prohibits its sale in the Territory, without restocking charge."

---

### 13. Notice of Cancellation – 30 Days

**Document/Section:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 4.1; Supply Agreement § 13.3.

**Issue:** The certificate provides 30 days' notice of cancellation, which meets the § 13.3 requirement. However, the notice goes to Ludis (the certificate holder), not to Sovex. Sovex is the policyholder, so it will know of cancellation directly, but the contractual notice runs to Ludis.

**Why it matters:** Low risk in practice, but if Sovex's policy is cancelled and Sovex fails to notify Ludis (because the notice went to Ludis's broker, not to Sovex's management), there could be a technical breach of § 13.3.

**Recommendation:** Confirm internally that Sovex's insurance team has a process to notify Ludis within the 30-day window if a policy is cancelled or materially reduced, independent of the broker's notice.
