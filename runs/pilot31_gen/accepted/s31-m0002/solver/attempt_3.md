## Summary

## Issue 1: Uncapped Indemnity for All Grower Claims, "Whatever the Cause"

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 1.6 (definition of "Grower Claims"), § 11.2 (Customer indemnity), § 12.3(a) (exclusion from liability cap).

**Why it matters:** "Grower Claims" is defined as *any* claim, demand, suit or proceeding by a Grower "relating to the crop stand, emergence, yield or performance of Products, **whatever the cause**." Section 11.2 requires Sovex to defend and indemnify Ludis against all such claims arising from Sovex's "use, handling, storage, resale or planting" of the Products. Section 12.3(a) carves indemnification obligations out of the liability cap in § 12.1, making this exposure **uncapped**.

In practical terms: if a drought, hailstorm, or grower error causes a crop failure and the grower sues Sovex (or Ludis), Sovex must indemnify Ludis for all resulting losses, damages, and attorneys' fees—even though the seed performed to specification and the failure had nothing to do with the product. In a region like Iowa/Nebraska, a single bad weather year could generate multiple grower claims simultaneously. Because the definition sweeps in claims "whatever the cause," Ludis could potentially assert that any grower complaint about yield triggers Sovex's indemnity.

This is the single largest legal exposure in the paperwork.

**Recommended fix:**

- Narrow the definition of "Grower Claims" to exclude claims caused by weather, acts of God, grower negligence, or other causes outside the Products' performance. Proposed language for § 1.6:

  > "Grower Claims" means any claim, demand, suit or proceeding brought by a Grower alleging that the Products failed to conform to the Specifications or were defective in manner or quality, **excluding any claim to the extent arising from weather, acts of God, grower error or negligence, or other causes not attributable to the Products.**

- Limit § 11.2 to claims arising from Sovex's **negligence or willful misconduct** in handling, storage, or resale, rather than the current broad "arising out of or relating to" standard. Proposed language:

  > Customer will defend Supplier … against any claim arising out of Customer's **negligence or willful misconduct** in the handling, storage or resale of Products …

- Remove § 11.2 from the § 12.3(a) carve-out so that the liability cap applies to the Customer indemnity, or at minimum add a separate sub-cap for Grower Claims (e.g., two times the annual Fees).

## Issue 2: Absolute Exclusivity Across All Corn, Soybean, and Sorghum Seed in the US and Canada

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 4.1; § 2.1–2.2 (Term and Renewal).

**Why it matters:** During the Term (initial: January 15, 2026 – December 31, 2028; then automatic three-year renewals), Sovex "shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute any corn, soybean or sorghum seed from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier **anywhere in the United States and Canada**."

This is not a territory-limited exclusivity—it is a **national and international** exclusive-dealing obligation covering three seed categories. If Ludis fails to supply, goes out of business, or the relationship deteriorates, Sovex cannot legally purchase seed from any other supplier until the Agreement is terminated. The only termination rights are for material breach (§ 15.1), insolvency (§ 15.2), or regulatory prohibition (§ 15.3). A supply shortage or quality dispute that doesn't rise to "material breach" would leave Sovex locked in.

Combined with the automatic renewal (§ 2.2) and the narrow non-renewal notice window (150–120 days before term end), Sovex could be bound for nine or more years before it can walk away.

**Open question for client:** Does Sovex currently sell or intend to sell seed from other suppliers (e.g., soybean or sorghum from non-Ludis sources)? If so, this provision may already be in breach or will be immediately upon signing. We need to understand Sovex's current product line to assess whether the exclusivity is operationally feasible.

**Recommended fix:**

- Limit the exclusivity to the **Territory** stated in the applicable Order Form (Iowa and Nebraska for Order Form No. 2), not the entire US and Canada.
- Limit it to **Products** (Ludis's proprietary hybrid corn seed), not all corn, soybean, and sorghum seed generally.
- Add a **supply-failure escape**: if Ludis fails to deliver more than X% of ordered units in a Season (beyond the § 5.3 adjustment), Sovex may purchase the shortfall from alternative suppliers without breaching § 4.1.
- Shorten the non-renewal notice window or add a right to terminate for convenience on 180 days' notice after the first Season.

Proposed language for § 4.1:

> During the Term, Customer shall not, directly or through any Affiliate, purchase, sell or distribute any corn seed from or on behalf of any person other than Supplier **in the Territory**. This Section 4.1 does not restrict Customer from purchasing soybean or sorghum seed from other suppliers.

## Issue 3: March 30 Claim – Germination Notice Likely Time-Barred

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 8.4 (21-day notice requirement); Order Form No. 2, Recital B (delivery date and claim date); Order Form No. 2 § 7.1–7.2 (reservation of rights).

**Why it matters:** Products under Order Form No. 1 were delivered on **February 20, 2026**. Section 8.4 requires written notice of a germination failure within **21 days** after the Delivery Date—i.e., by **March 13, 2026**. Sovex gave notice on **March 30, 2026**, which is 38 days after delivery and 17 days past the deadline. Ludis rejected the notice as untimely on April 3, 2026.

The reservation-of-rights clauses in Order Form No. 2 (§§ 7.1–7.2) state that Sovex's execution of the new Order Form does not waive the claim. However, a reservation of rights does not cure a missed contractual deadline. If § 8.4 operates as a condition precedent (which the language "the Products are deemed accepted as to germination" strongly suggests), Sovex has lost its contractual remedy for the germination failure.

**Practical impact:** If the seed from Order Form No. 1 genuinely failed to germinate at the specified rate, Sovex may have already sold that seed to growers and will face grower complaints or claims—with no recourse against Ludis under the Agreement. This also weakens Sovex's negotiating position on the broader relationship.

**Open question for client:**

- What were the actual germination results from the lab? Did the seed fail the 95% minimum?
- Why was the notice 17 days late? Was there a delay in lab results, internal processing, or another reason? If the lab results were not available until after March 13, there may be an argument that the 21-day period should run from when the failure was *discoverable* rather than from delivery.
- Has Sovex already sold the affected seed to growers? If so, what is the volume and what grower complaints, if any, have been received?

**Recommended fix:**

- **Short term:** Assess whether there is a viable argument that the 21-day period should be tolled or extended (e.g., if lab results were delayed through no fault of Sovex, or if Ludis's own conduct—such as a delay in providing the certificate of analysis under § 8.2—contributed to the late discovery). If the argument is weak, consider whether a commercial resolution (e.g., a credit on Order Form No. 2 pricing) is preferable to litigation.
- **Going forward:** Amend § 8.4 to extend the germination claim period to **60 days** after the Delivery Date, or alternatively to 30 days after Sovex receives the certificate of analysis (whichever is later). For seed products, 21 days is unreasonably short given that germination testing takes time and field emergence may not be apparent until after planting.

Proposed language:

> Customer must give Supplier written notice of any claim that Products fail to meet the germination rate in the Specifications within **sixty (60) days** after the Delivery Date, or within **thirty (30) days** after Customer receives the certificate of analysis under Section 8.2, whichever is later.

## Issue 4: Shortfall Fee – Up to $1.68 Million, Uncapped

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 5.1–5.2; § 12.3(d); Order Form No. 2 § 3.2–3.3.

**Why it matters:** The Minimum Purchase Commitment for the 2026–2027 Season is **40,000 units** at a shortfall rate of **$42.00 per unit**. If Sovex purchases zero units, the maximum shortfall fee is **$1,680,000**. Section 12.3(d) explicitly excludes this obligation from the liability cap, so it is uncapped.

Section 5.3 reduces the minimum only to the extent Ludis "fails to deliver ordered Products or delivers Products that are rejected under Section 8." This does not account for:
- Market downturns (e.g., if corn prices collapse and growers switch to soybeans);
- Sovex's inability to sell due to factors outside its control;
- The March 30 Claim (if seed from Order Form No. 1 was rejected, the § 5.3 reduction applies, but only if the rejection is valid—see Issue 3).

**Open question for client:** What is Sovex's realistic sales forecast for the 2026–2027 Season? Is 40,000 units (out of 48,000 ordered) achievable given current grower commitments? Are there market conditions (input costs, commodity prices, weather outlook) that could reduce demand?

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a **market-adjustment mechanism**: if the seasonal corn price falls below a stated threshold (e.g., the USDA December forecast by a certain percentage), the Minimum Purchase Commitment is reduced proportionally.
- Limit the shortfall fee to a percentage of the per-unit price (e.g., 15–20%) rather than a fixed dollar amount, so it reflects actual lost margin rather than full price.
- Add a **cap on aggregate shortfall fees** across all Order Forms in a Season (e.g., not to exceed 10% of total Fees for that Season).
- Clarify that the § 5.3 reduction applies not only to rejected Products but also to Products for which Sovex has a valid, pending quality claim.

## Issue 5: National Accounts Carve-Out Undermines Territory Exclusivity

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 4.2; Order Form No. 2 § 2.3.

**Why it matters:** Section 4.2 promises that Ludis will not appoint another distributor in the Territory, "except for direct sales by Supplier to national accounts identified in the applicable Order Form." Order Form No. 2 currently lists "none," but each future Order Form can designate national accounts. If Ludis designates a large Iowa or Nebraska grower operation as a "national account," it can sell directly to that grower, bypassing Sovex and eroding Sovex's customer base.

There is no definition of "national account" in the Agreement, no size threshold, and no requirement that the account be truly "national" (i.e., multi-state). A single large farm in Iowa could qualify.

**Recommended fix:**

- Define "national account" to mean a grower operation that purchases seed for planting in **at least three (3) states** and has a total acreage under management exceeding a stated threshold (e.g., 10,000 acres).
- Require that national accounts be identified in the Order Form **at least 60 days before the Season** and that Sovex have the right to object if the account is within the Territory and Sovex has an existing relationship with it.
- Add a most-favored-customer provision: if Ludis sells directly to a national account in the Territory, Sovex is entitled to the same per-unit price.

Proposed language:

> "National Account" means a grower operation that (a) plants seed in at least three (3) states, and (b) has total acreage under management of at least ten thousand (10,000) acres. Supplier will identify all National Accounts in the applicable Order Form no later than sixty (60) days before the first Delivery Date for that Season.

## Issue 6: Aggregated Data – Ludis Can Use Sovex's Grower Data for Product Development

**Document / Section:** DPA § 4.2; § 1.1 (definition of "Aggregated Data"); § 3.4 (No Competitive Use).

**Why it matters:** Section 4.2 permits Ludis to create Aggregated Data from Grower Data and use it for "internal research and product development." The only restriction is that Ludis "will not disclose Aggregated Data to any third party in a form that identifies Customer."

This means Ludis can use the yield data, soil data, planting data, and field-level information from Sovex's growers to develop new hybrids or refine existing ones. Those improved products could then be sold to other distributors or to Ludis's direct national accounts, potentially outperforming the products Sovex is selling. The "No Competitive Use" provision (§ 3.4) prohibits soliciting growers for direct sales and setting grower-specific prices, but it does **not** prohibit using the data to improve products that are then sold through competing channels.

The de-identification standard ("cannot reasonably be used to identify any Grower, any individual or any specific field") may be insufficient. With detailed field boundary data, yield maps, and soil information, re-identification of specific farms may be possible, especially when combined with publicly available land records.

**Open question for client:** Does Sovex consider its growers' data (yield maps, soil data, field boundaries) to be a competitive asset that Sovex uses to provide agronomic services to those growers? If so, the risk of Ludis using that data to build competing offerings is more acute.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a restriction that Aggregated Data derived from Sovex's Territory growers will not be used to develop products that are marketed or sold in the Territory during the Term.
- Strengthen the de-identification standard to require that Aggregated Data be derived from a minimum number of growers (e.g., data from fewer than 10 growers in a single county cannot be aggregated).
- Add a right for Sovex to audit the Aggregated Data creation process (beyond the general audit right in § 10.2).

## Issue 7: DPA Liability Cap Subject to New York Law – Conflict with Iowa Governing Law

**Document / Section:** DPA § 13.2 ("Each Party's liability under this Addendum is subject to Section 12 of the Agreement"); DPA § 12.1 (Iowa governing law); Supply Agreement § 12 (liability cap and consequential damages exclusion); § 19.1 (New York governing law); § 19.2 (order of precedence).

**Why it matters:** The DPA is governed by Iowa law (§ 12.1), but § 13.2 subjects DPA liability to Section 12 of the Agreement, which is governed by New York law. Section 19.2 provides that the DPA "prevails on data protection matters," but § 13.2 explicitly incorporates the Agreement's liability framework. This creates an ambiguity: does the New York-law consequential damages exclusion (§ 12.2) apply to data protection claims under the DPA? If a Security Incident causes Sovex to lose grower relationships (lost profits), can Sovex recover those damages under the DPA, or are they excluded by § 12.2?

Additionally, the liability cap in § 12.1 (Fees paid in the preceding 12 months) may be inadequate to cover the costs of a major data breach (notification, credit monitoring, regulatory fines, class action defense).

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a carve-out in DPA § 13.2: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the exclusions in Sections 12.1 and 12.2 of the Agreement do not apply to Supplier's obligations under Sections 6, 7, 8, or 11 of this Addendum, or to Supplier's liability resulting from a Security Incident caused by Supplier's or its Subprocessor's breach of this Addendum."
- Alternatively, set a separate, higher liability cap for data protection claims (e.g., $5,000,000 or the limit of Supplier's cyber liability insurance, if any).

## Issue 8: Narrow Non-Renewal Notice Window (30 Days)

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 2.2.

**Why it matters:** To prevent automatic renewal at the end of the Initial Term (December 31, 2028), either party must deliver notice "not more than 150 days and not less than 120 days before the end of the then-current Term." For the Initial Term, that window is **July 3, 2028 through August 2, 2028**—a 30-day window. Missing it by even one day locks Sovex into another three-year term (through December 31, 2031), with the same exclusivity and minimum purchase obligations.

Given the breadth of the exclusivity (Issue 2) and the uncapped indemnity (Issue 1), being locked in for an additional three years without the ability to renegotiate is a significant risk.

**Recommended fix:**

- Widen the notice window to **180–90 days** before term end, giving Sovex a broader period to make the decision.
- Add a right to terminate for convenience on **180 days' notice** after the first Season, at least during the Initial Term.
- Require that any Renewal Term be subject to renegotiation of key economic terms (pricing, minimums, shortfall rate) before the renewal takes effect.

## Issue 9: No Remedy for Short Delivery Delays (1–10 Days)

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 7.2; Order Form No. 2 § 5.2.

**Why it matters:** Section 7.2 gives Sovex the right to cancel only if delivery is more than **10 days** late. If a tranche is 1–10 days late, Sovex has no contractual remedy—no price reduction, no liquidated damages, no right to source from an alternative supplier. For seed, timely delivery is critical: if the first tranche (20,000 units, due January 15, 2027) is delivered 8 days late, Sovex may miss the optimal planting window for those units, resulting in lost sales to growers with no recourse against Ludis.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a liquidated damages provision for late delivery: e.g., "For each day of delay beyond the scheduled Delivery Date, Supplier will credit Customer 0.5% of the Fees for the affected tranche, up to a maximum of 5%."
- Alternatively, give Sovex the right to source the delayed quantity from an alternative supplier at Ludis's expense if delivery is more than 5 days late.

## Issue 10: Insurance – No Primary and Non-Contributory Language; Renewal Gap Risk

**Document / Section:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter §§ 2.5, 5.1; Supply Agreement § 13.1–13.3.

**Why it matters:**

(a) **Primary and non-contributory:** The cover letter states that Ludis is named as an additional insured "as respects the Products" under a "CG 20 15 equivalent" endorsement. While CG 20 15 typically provides primary and non-contributory coverage, the letter does not explicitly confirm this. If Ludis carries its own CGL policy, a subrogation or contribution dispute could arise in the event of a products liability claim.

(b) **Renewal gap:** Both policies expire January 1, 2027. The broker commits to issuing renewal certificates "promptly after each renewal binds" (§ 5.1). Section 13.3 requires certificates "on each policy renewal." If there is any lapse between January 1 and the binding of the renewal, Sovex would be in breach of § 13.1, which requires coverage "during the Term." The Agreement runs through December 31, 2028, so Sovex needs continuous coverage for at least two more renewal cycles.

**Recommended fix:**

- Request that the broker confirm in writing that the additional insured endorsement provides **primary and non-contributory** coverage, and that it includes a waiver of subrogation in favor of Ludis.
- Add a requirement that renewal policies be bound **no later than December 15** of each year (15 days before the expiration date) to eliminate gap risk.
- Consider requiring Ludis to maintain its own products liability insurance at the same limits, so that both parties' coverage is in place regardless of renewal timing.

## Issue 11: New York Governing Law and Venue

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 19.1.

**Why it matters:** The Agreement is governed by New York law with exclusive venue in New York County. Sovex is in Ames, Iowa; Ludis is in Champaign, Illinois. New York is inconvenient for both parties and adds cost and complexity to any dispute. The DPA, by contrast, uses Iowa law and Polk County venue (§ 12), which is favorable to Sovex but creates a split-law situation (see Issue 7).

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate to change governing law and venue to **Iowa** (Sovex's home state) or at minimum **Illinois** (Ludis's home state), both of which are more convenient than New York. Alternatively, use the location of the Delivery Location (Ames, Iowa) as the venue.
- If New York law is a non-negotiable point for Ludis, at least consider arbitration as a less costly dispute resolution mechanism.

## Issue 12: 21-Day Germination Claim Window Is Unreasonably Short

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 8.4.

**Why it matters:** As demonstrated by the March 30 Claim (Issue 3), 21 days is insufficient for seed germination testing in practice. Warm germination tests typically take 7–10 days in a lab. If Sovex receives the seed, sends a sample to an accredited lab, receives results, and then prepares and sends a claim with supporting detail, 21 days from delivery is tight—especially if there are multiple lots or if the lab has a backlog. The "deemed accepted" consequence for missing the deadline is severe.

**Recommended fix:** See Issue 3 for proposed language extending the window to 60 days or 30 days from receipt of the certificate of analysis, whichever is later.

## Issue 13: Section 5.3 Adjustment Does Not Cover Valid Pending Claims

**Document / Section:** Supply Agreement § 5.3.

**Why it matters:** The Minimum Purchase Commitment is reduced only for Products that are "rejected under Section 8." If a claim is pending (as with the March 30 Claim) but not yet "rejected" by Ludis, the minimum is not reduced. This means Sovex could owe a shortfall fee on units that were the subject of a legitimate quality dispute.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 5.3 to read:

> The Minimum Purchase Commitment for a Season will be reduced unit for unit to the extent Supplier fails to deliver ordered Products or delivers Products that are **rejected or subject to a pending and good-faith claim under Section 8** (with the reduction to be finalized upon resolution of the claim).

## Issue 14: DPA Subprocessor List – FieldLine Analytics

**Document / Section:** DPA Annex 1.

**Why it matters:** FieldLine Analytics, LLC is listed as a subprocessor for "yield data normalization and agronomic modeling." This is a third party that will have access to detailed yield and agronomic data from Sovex's growers. While § 7.3 requires Supplier to impose "no less protective" obligations on subprocessors, Sovex has no direct contractual relationship with FieldLine and limited visibility into its security practices. The audit right (§ 10.2) extends to Supplier but is not explicitly stated to extend to subprocessors.

**Open question for client:** Is FieldLine Analytics a well-known entity in the agronomy space? Does Sovex have any independent knowledge of its security posture? Would Sovex's growers object to their data being shared with a third-party analytics firm?

**Recommended fix:**

- Clarify in § 10.2 that the audit right extends to subprocessors (or at minimum that Supplier must provide subprocessor audit reports).
- Require that subprocessor agreements include the same security incident notification obligations as § 8, flowing down to the subprocessor level.
- Consider requiring Sovex's prior written consent (not just the right to object) for new subprocessors that will access field-level or yield data specifically.

## Issue 15: Return Policy – 5% Restocking Charge and 10% Cap

**Document / Section:** Order Form No. 2 § 6.2; Supply Agreement § 6.7.

**Why it matters:** Sovex may return up to 10% of total units delivered (4,800 units out of 48,000) by June 30, 2027, at a credit of net price less a 5% restocking charge. For treated products, they must be within labeled shelf life. The 10% cap and 5% charge limit Sovex's ability to manage inventory if market conditions shift or if Sovex over-ordered. The June 30, 2027 deadline is after the planting season, which is reasonable, but if Sovex has unsold seed at the end of the season, it has limited flexibility.

**Recommended fix:** This is a lower-priority item, but consider negotiating:
- An increase in the returnable percentage to 15%;
- Elimination of the restocking charge for returns made within 30 days of delivery;
- A right to return seed that is within specification but for which Sovex has documented over-ordering (e.g., based on a forecast provided under § 3.2).
