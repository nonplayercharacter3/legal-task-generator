## Executive Summary

Stabind has two urgent problems. First, the September 15, 2026 Side Letter (the "Side Letter") quietly replaced Stabind's uncapped right to recover Product Contamination Losses with a right that applies only to Kogein's *willful misconduct*—a far narrower standard. Second, Stabind's Chronic Failure Election Notice of August 28, 2026 appears to have been delivered 21 days after the contractual deadline, potentially forfeiting Stabind's right to reduce its Minimum Annual Volume or terminate for Chronic Failure. Both issues are detailed below with recommended actions.

## Issue 1 – Side Letter Gutted the Contamination Liability Protection

**Where:** Side Letter § 3.1 (amending Agreement § 11.2(d)); Agreement § 1.1(l) (definition of "Product Contamination Losses"); Agreement § 11.1 (liability cap).

**What happened.** Original Agreement § 11.2(d) excluded all Product Contamination Losses from the 12-month-fee liability cap. The Side Letter deleted that provision and replaced it with:

> "(d) Product Contamination Losses, to the extent arising from Kogein's willful misconduct."

The Side Letter was countersigned by Stabind on September 15, 2026 (Side Letter, signature block). Under Agreement § 17.4, the Side Letter controls over the Agreement in the event of conflict.

**Why it matters.** Product Contamination Losses (Agreement § 1.1(l)) include recall costs, destroyed beer/wort/packaging, and equipment remediation—costs that in a serious contamination event can easily exceed 12 months of hop fees. Under the amended provision, if Kogein's negligence (e.g., a cold-chain break, a processing error, a storage failure) causes contamination, Stabind's recovery is capped at the fees paid in the prior 12 months. Only if Stabind can prove Kogein *intentionally* caused the contamination does the cap fall away. Willful misconduct is an extremely high evidentiary bar.

**Recommendation.**

- **Immediate:** Determine whether Stabind can negotiate a revert or partial restoration. A reasonable compromise: exclude Product Contamination Losses from the cap to the extent arising from Kogein's *gross negligence* or willful misconduct (aligning with the existing § 11.2(c) exclusion). Propose language:

  > "(d) Product Contamination Losses, to the extent arising from Kogein's gross negligence or willful misconduct."

- **If Kogein resists:** At minimum, seek to raise the insurance requirement in Agreement § 12.1(b) (currently $3,000,000 per occurrence for product contamination and recall) to a level that covers a realistic worst-case contamination event for a brewery of Stabind's size. The insurance is not subject to the liability cap and would provide a funding source.

- **Process note:** Flag internally that the Side Letter was negotiated in the context of the Chronic Failure dispute and remediation plan. The § 3 amendment appears to have been a Kogein concession-extraction that Stabind's signatory may not have fully evaluated.

## Issue 2 – Chronic Failure Election Notice Likely Untimely

**Where:** Service Level Schedule § 4.2 (30-day election window); Side Letter § 1.2(b)–(d) (recital of the dispute); Side Letter § 2.1 (positions reserved).

**What happened.** A Chronic Failure requires OTD below 92% in two consecutive calendar quarters (Service Level Schedule § 4.1). The Q2 2026 Performance Report, delivered July 8, 2026, showed OTD of 88% (Q1 2026) and 89% (Q2 2026)—both below 92%. Section 4.2 requires Stabind to elect a remedy "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Performance Report for the second of the two consecutive calendar quarters." The second quarter is Q2 2026; its Performance Report was delivered July 8, 2026. The 30-day deadline fell on **August 7, 2026**. Stabind's Election Notice was delivered **August 28, 2026**—21 days late.

Kogein has asserted the notice is untimely (Side Letter § 1.2(d)). The Side Letter preserves both parties' positions but does not resolve the dispute (Side Letter § 2.1).

**Why it matters.** If the notice was untimely and the election is ineffective, Stabind has lost:
- The right to reduce the Minimum Annual Volume by up to 50% (Service Level Schedule § 4.2(a)); and
- The right to terminate for Chronic Failure (Service Level Schedule § 4.2(b); Agreement § 13.3).

Stabind remains locked into the 180,000-pound Minimum Annual Volume (Agreement § 3.2) for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026, and potentially beyond, even though Kogein is chronically underperforming on delivery.

**Possible arguments for Stabind (flag – not resolvable from the documents alone):**

- The Performance Report is required to show OTD for "the Measurement Period just ended *and* the immediately preceding Measurement Period" (Service Level Schedule § 3.1(a)). One could argue that the "Performance Report for the second of the two consecutive calendar quarters" is not the Q2 report per se but the first report that *establishes* the Chronic Failure by showing both quarters. However, the Q2 report is the only report that shows both Q1 and Q2, and it was delivered July 8. This argument is weak.
- If there was any dispute over the Q1 calculation (e.g., exclusions under § 2.2(c)), Stabind could argue the clock should not run until the dispute is resolved. But § 3.3 states that "a dispute over a calculation does not suspend any time period in this Schedule." This argument also appears weak.
- Practical/equitable arguments (laches, waiver by Kogein's continued performance, course of dealing) may be available but are fact-intensive and uncertain.

**Recommendation.**

- **Short-term:** The Side Letter § 2.3 holds Kogein to a no-invoice covenant for the 2026 Contract Year shortfall until the dispute is resolved (plus 60 days). Use this leverage to negotiate a settlement: Stabind concedes the notice was late in exchange for a contractual reduction of the MAV (e.g., to 120,000 pounds) or a one-time MAV waiver for the 2026 Contract Year.
- **Medium-term:** If the dispute escalates, Stabind should preserve its position that the Chronic Failure remedy is available for *future* Chronic Failures (Service Level Schedule § 4.5: "Each Chronic Failure is a separate event"). Calendar the 30-day window for any future Performance Report showing sub-92% OTD.
- **Process fix:** Implement an internal alert so that when a Performance Report arrives, the 30-day election clock is immediately flagged to the General Counsel with a hard deadline 5 days before expiry.

## Issue 3 – Five-Year Auto-Renewal with Unilateral Price Reset

**Where:** Agreement § 2.2.

**What the provision says.** On expiration of the Initial Term (January 31, 2029), the Agreement automatically renews for successive **five-year** terms. Non-renewal notice must be given no more than 210 days and no less than 180 days before the end of the then-current term. Pricing for each Renewal Term is "Kogein's then-current list price" as of the first day of that Renewal Term. The Minimum Annual Volume continues to apply.

**Why it matters.**

- The non-renewal window for the Initial Term is approximately **July 15 – August 4, 2028** (a ~20-day window). Missing it locks Stabind in for five more years.
- On renewal, Stabind loses all price protection. Kogein can set list prices at any level, and Stabind must either accept or walk (but walking is only possible if it hits the narrow notice window).
- Combined with the MAV, Stabind would be committed to buying 180,000 pounds (or whatever the MAV is at that time) at Kogein's then-current price for five years.

**Recommendation.**

- **Now:** Calendar the non-renewal notice window (July 15 – August 4, 2028) with multiple reminders starting no later than May 2028.
- **At renewal negotiation (if Stabind wishes to renew rather than exit):** Push for (a) a price cap or escalation formula (e.g., CPI-linked, not-to-exceed 5% per year), (b) a shorter renewal term (one or two years, not five), and (c) a mutual most-favored-customer clause so Stabind's renewal price is no higher than Kogein's price to comparable-volume customers.
- **If Stabind wishes to exit:** Begin qualifying an alternative hop supplier in 2027 so that a non-renewal decision in mid-2028 is not made under time pressure.

## Issue 4 – Asymmetric Assignment Rights and Overly Broad "Kogein Group Member" Definition

**Where:** Agreement § 15.2; DPA § 1.1(b) (definition of "Kogein Group Member"); DPA § 1.1(g) (definition of "Subprocessor" excludes Kogein Group Members); DPA § 3.4 (disclosure to Kogein Group Members).

**What the provisions say.**

- Stabind may not assign without Kogein's consent (Agreement § 15.2).
- Kogein may assign to any "Kogein Group Member" on **written notice only**—no consent required (Agreement § 15.2).
- "Kogein Group Member" includes "any entity with which Kogein has a strategic supply or processing arrangement" (DPA § 1.1(b)). This is not limited to Affiliates or entities under common control.
- Because Kogein Group Members are excluded from the definition of "Subprocessor" (DPA § 1.1(g)), Kogein can share Stabind Personal Data with these entities without triggering the 30-day notice and objection rights in DPA § 4.2.

**Why it matters.** Kogein could enter a "strategic supply or processing arrangement" with a competitor of Stabind or with an entity Stabind has not vetted, then assign the Agreement to that entity (or share Stabind's data with it) on notice alone. Stabind's Blend Specifications (Agreement § 4.2) are Stabind's exclusive property, and the non-compete in § 4.2 binds "Kogein"—but if the Agreement is assigned to a new entity, the enforceability of § 4.2 against the assignee depends on whether the assignment carries the obligation (it should, under § 15.1, but the practical risk is that a less sophisticated assignee may not honor it as strictly).

**Recommendation.**

- **Negotiating position:** Amend Agreement § 15.2 to limit Kogein's no-consent assignment right to **Affiliates** (as defined in Agreement § 1.1(a)) only. Any other assignment, including to entities with "strategic supply or processing arrangements," should require Stabind's prior written consent.
- **DPA fix:** Narrow DPA § 1.1(b) to "Kogein and its Affiliates." Entities outside that group should be treated as Subprocessors subject to DPA § 4.
- **Interim step:** Request that Kogein identify in writing all current "Kogein Group Members" (other than Kogein itself) that have access to Stabind Personal Data or to which the Agreement could be assigned, so Stabind can assess the risk.

## Issue 5 – DPA Liability Subject to the (Now Reduced) Cap

**Where:** DPA § 10.1; Agreement § 11.1–11.2 (as amended by Side Letter § 3.1).

**What the provision says.** DPA § 10.1 provides that "Each Party's liability under this DPA is subject to Section 11 of the Agreement." Section 11.2 excludes from the cap: indemnification obligations, breach of confidentiality (§ 9), gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and (post-Side Letter) Product Contamination Losses from willful misconduct only.

**Why it matters.** If Kogein suffers a data breach caused by its ordinary negligence (e.g., failure to patch a system, misconfigured access controls), Stabind's recovery for notification costs, credit monitoring, regulatory fines, and third-party claims is capped at 12 months of hop fees. The DPA's security incident cost-reimbursement provision (DPA § 6.3) is subject to this cap.

**Recommendation.**

- Add DPA § 6 (Security Incidents) to the list of exclusions from the liability cap in Agreement § 11.2. Proposed new clause:

  > "(e) Kogein's obligations under Section 6 of the Data Processing Addendum."

- Alternatively, negotiate a super-cap for data protection liabilities (e.g., 3× the general cap).

## Issue 6 – Force Majeure: 60-Day Wait Before Stabind Can Source Alternatives

**Where:** Agreement § 14.3.

**What the provision says.** If a Force Majeure Event prevents Kogein from supplying Products for more than 60 days, Stabind may buy substitutes from third parties, and those quantities count toward the MAV. If the FM event continues more than 120 days, either party may terminate.

**Why it matters.** A brewery cannot easily pause production for 60 days. If a Yakima Valley wildfire or pest event knocks out Kogein's supply, Stabind must either (a) brew without hops for 60 days, (b) buy hops on the open market at whatever price is available and *not* count them toward the MAV (reducing its effective commitment but incurring spot-market risk), or (c) wait out the 60 days. The 120-day termination right is helpful but means Stabind is stuck for four months minimum.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate to reduce the 60-day threshold to **30 days**. A brewery's production schedule and retail commitments (especially now that Ridgeline Haze is a year-round product—see Issue 7) cannot absorb a two-month supply gap.
- Add a requirement that Kogein provide a supply continuity plan (e.g., pre-positioned inventory at a third-party cold storage facility) as a condition of invoking FM for crop failure.

## Issue 7 – Press Release Creates Public Commitment to Year-Round Product Dependent on Underperforming Supply Chain

**Where:** Press Release §§ 1.1–1.2, 2.3; Agreement § 3.2 (MAV); Service Level Schedule § 2.1 (95% OTD target).

**What the documents show.** On November 3, 2026, Stabind publicly announced Ridgeline Haze as a **year-round** hazy IPA using Yakima Valley hop pellets that are "cold-stored and handled under refrigeration from the farm to Stabind's brewhouse." This was announced while Kogein's OTD performance was at 88–89% (below the 92% Chronic Failure threshold) and the Chronic Failure dispute was unresolved.

**Why it matters.**

- A year-round product increases volume demand, making the 180,000-pound MAV more likely to be a binding constraint (and increasing Stabind's exposure to shortfall invoices if the MAV is not reduced).
- The public cold-chain commitment heightens the consequences of any Kogein delivery failure: if hops arrive warm or late, the product Stabind has advertised to consumers is at risk.
- If Kogein's performance does not improve, Stabind faces a choice between (a) publicly sourcing from another supplier (undercutting the "Yakima Valley from the farm" narrative) or (b) absorbing the quality/reliability risk.

**Recommendation.**

- Use the Ridgeline Haze launch as leverage in negotiations with Kogein: Stabind is publicly committing to Kogein's hops in a year-round product, which benefits Kogein's marketing. In exchange, Stabind should seek (a) resolution of the Chronic Failure dispute in Stabind's favor (see Issue 2), (b) restoration of the contamination liability protection (see Issue 1), and (c) a service-level improvement commitment with meaningful consequences for continued underperformance in 2027.
- Operationally, begin qualifying a secondary hop source for the specific varieties used in Ridgeline Haze so that Stabind has a fallback without publicly contradicting its press release.

## Issue 8 – Service Credit Ceiling Is Low Relative to the Harm

**Where:** Service Level Schedule § 2.3.

**What the provision says.** The maximum Service Credit is 10% of Fees invoiced for the relevant quarter (for OTD below 92%). At Stabind's scale, this is a modest amount relative to the operational disruption of late or short deliveries (brew schedule changes, expedited freight, potential product quality impact).

**Why it matters.** Service Credits are the *only* automatic financial remedy for delivery shortfalls that don't rise to a Chronic Failure. If OTD hovers at 93–94%, the credit is only 2–4%. Given that Stabind's production is scheduled around hop deliveries, even a 5–7% late rate can force costly schedule changes.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a steeper credit curve or a higher maximum (e.g., 15% for OTD below 90%).
- Add a provision for "expedited freight pass-through": if Stabind must arrange alternative or expedited delivery because of a Kogein late delivery, Kogein reimburses the incremental cost. This could be added as a new clause in the Service Level Schedule.
