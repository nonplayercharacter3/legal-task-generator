## Executive Summary

Stabind faces three critical issues: (1) the September 2026 Side Letter narrowed the uncapped-contamination-liability protection in a way that exposes Stabind to significant financial risk from any non-willful contamination event; (2) Stabind's Chronic Failure election appears to be time-barred, which means Stabind has likely lost its contractual right to reduce the Minimum Annual Volume or terminate for Kogein's delivery failures; and (3) the Agreement's automatic renewal structure locks Stand into five-year terms at Kogein's then-current list price with a narrow opt-out window. Each is addressed below with recommended actions.

## Issue 1 (Critical): Side Letter Narrows Uncapped Contamination Liability to Willful Misconduct Only

**Where:** Side Letter § 3.1 (amending Supply Agreement § 11.2(d)); Supply Agreement § 1.1(l) (defining "Product Contamination Losses"); Supply Agreement § 11.1 (cap).

**What happened.** Original Agreement § 11.2(d) excluded *all* Product Contamination Losses from the 12-month-fees liability cap. The Side Letter, signed by both parties on September 15, 2026, replaced that provision with:

> "(d) Product Contamination Losses, to the extent arising from Kogein's willful misconduct."

**Why it matters.** Product Contamination Losses (Agreement § 1.1(l)) encompass recall costs, market withdrawal, customer notification, destroyed beer/wort/packaging, and equipment remediation. Under the amended provision, these losses are uncapped *only* if Stabind can prove Kogein acted with willful misconduct. Negligence—the far more common cause of contamination (e.g., cold-chain failure, equipment cross-contamination, testing error)—now falls within the 12-month-fees cap. Given that Stabind is launching Ridgeline Haze (a high-profile, generously hopped IPA) in late November 2026, a single contamination event could generate losses well in excess of 12 months of hop-pellet fees, and Stabind would bear the excess.

The indemnification in Agreement § 10.1(a) (third-party claims from contaminated product) remains uncapped under § 11.2(a), so this gap is specifically about *Stabind's own* first-party losses: the cost of the recall, the destroyed inventory, the equipment shutdown.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Immediate:** Assess whether the Side Letter can be renegotiated. The Side Letter is a signed amendment (Side Letter § 5.1; Agreement § 17.4 gives side letters top precedence). If Kogein will agree, restore the original language: "Product Contamination Losses" without the willful-misconduct qualifier.
- **Fallback position:** If Kogein resists full restoration, negotiate (a) a super-cap for contamination losses (e.g., 3× or 5× the 12-month-fees cap) and (b) a lower threshold than willful misconduct—e.g., "gross negligence or willful misconduct."
- **Practical step:** Confirm that Kogein's $3,000,000 product contamination and recall insurance (Agreement § 12.1(b)) names Stabind as an additional insured or provides direct recovery. Request a certificate of insurance now. If the policy does not cover Stabind's first-party losses (recall, destroyed product, equipment remediation), Stabind should consider purchasing its own product recall insurance to bridge the gap created by the Side Letter amendment.

**Flag:** The Side Letter is already signed by Stabind's CEO. If the client did not appreciate the significance of § 3.1 at the time of signing, there may be an internal governance question. I cannot determine from the documents whether Stabind had leverage to refuse this term (e.g., in exchange for the Chronic Failure concessions in Side Letter §§ 2 and 4).

## Issue 2 (High): Chronic Failure Election Notice Likely Time-Barred

**Where:** Side Letter §§ 1.2(b)–(d), 2.1; Service Level Schedule §§ 4.1–4.2; Supply Agreement § 3.2.

**What happened.** The SLS defines a Chronic Failure as On-Time Delivery below 92% in two consecutive calendar quarters (SLS § 4.1). Kogein's Q2 2026 Performance Report, delivered July 8, 2026, showed OTD of 88% (Q1 2026) and 89% (Q2 2026) (Side Letter § 1.2(b)). Both quarters are below 92%, so a Chronic Failure occurred.

SLS § 4.2 requires Stabind to deliver its election notice "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Performance Report for the second of the two consecutive calendar quarters." The second quarter is Q2 2026. The Performance Report was delivered July 8, 2026. The 30-day deadline expired **August 7, 2026**. Stabind's Election Notice was delivered **August 28, 2026**—21 days late (Side Letter § 1.2(c)).

Kogein has asserted the notice is untimely (Side Letter § 1.2(d)). The parties have not resolved the dispute (Side Letter § 2.1).

**Why it matters.** If the Election Notice is ineffective, Stabind has lost:
- The right to reduce the Minimum Annual Volume by up to 50% (SLS § 4.2(a)); and
- The right to terminate for Chronic Failure (SLS § 4.2(b); Agreement § 13.3).

Without the reduction, Stabind remains contractually obligated to purchase 180,000 pounds per Contract Year (Agreement § 3.2). If actual purchases fall below that threshold (plausible if Kogein's poor delivery performance disrupts Stabind's brewing schedule), Kogein can invoice the shortfall at the weighted average price. Side Letter § 2.3 temporarily blocks that invoice until 60 days after resolution, but it does not extinguish the obligation.

**Assessment of Stabind's position.** The SLS is unambiguous: the clock runs from "delivery of the Performance Report." There is no provision for extension, no "excusable delay" language, and no requirement that Stabind first verify the data before the clock starts. Stabind's position is weak on the plain text.

Possible (but unlikely to succeed) arguments:
- Kogein's own breach (the delivery failures) should excuse strict compliance with the election deadline. The SLS does not provide for this, and Agreement § 17.3 requires amendments in writing.
- The Performance Report was defective or incomplete, tolling the deadline. SLS § 3.3 provides a dispute mechanism for calculation errors but explicitly states that "a dispute over a calculation does not suspend any time period in this Schedule."

**Recommended fix.**

- **Practical step (immediate):** Accept that the timeliness argument is weak. Pivot to a commercial resolution: negotiate a written agreement (which would operate as a side letter under Agreement § 17.4) that (a) reduces the MAV for the current Contract Year (February 1, 2026 – January 31, 2027) by a specified percentage, and (b) confirms Stabind's right to elect a remedy for any *future* Chronic Failure. Leverage the remediation plan obligations (Side Letter § 4) and the ongoing monthly meetings (Side Letter § 4.2) as the forum.
- **Protective step:** Ensure that the Side Letter § 2.3 invoicing hold is not allowed to lapse without a resolution in place. If the 60-day post-resolution window opens and Kogein invoices the shortfall, Stabind should dispute under Agreement § 6.3 (good-faith dispute, 30-day resolution period).
- **Forward-looking:** Calendar the SLS § 4.2 deadline for any future Chronic Failure. The Performance Report for Q3 2026 (quarter ending September 30, 2026) is due within 10 business days after September 30, i.e., by approximately October 14, 2026. If Q3 OTD is also below 92%, the next Chronic Failure election deadline would be 30 days after that Q3 Performance Report delivery. Set an internal reminder to act within 10 days of receiving any Performance Report showing sub-92% performance.

**Flag:** I cannot determine from the documents why Stabind's Election Notice was 21 days late. If there was an internal delay (e.g., the COO was unavailable, or legal review took too long), that is an operational lesson. If Kogein delayed delivery of the Performance Report or provided incomplete data, that may support an equitable argument, though the SLS text does not obviously accommodate it.

## Issue 3 (High): Automatic Renewal for Five-Year Terms at Kogein's List Price

**Where:** Supply Agreement § 2.2; § 3.2 (MAV continues in Renewal Terms).

**What the provision says.** On expiration of the Initial Term (January 31, 2029), the Agreement automatically renews for successive **five-year** terms. Pricing during each Renewal Term is "Kogein's then-current list price for each Product in effect on the first day of that Renewal Term." The MAV continues to apply. Non-renewal requires written notice given **not more than 210 days and not less than 180 days** before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters.**

- **Pricing risk:** "Kogein's then-current list price" is a unilateral pricing mechanism. Stabind has no cap, no formula, no most-favored-customer provision, and no negotiation right for Renewal Term pricing. If hop prices rise or Kogein restructures its price list, Stabind absorbs the increase for five years.
- **Duration risk:** A five-year renewal is an extraordinarily long commitment for a commodity input. Market conditions, Stabind's product portfolio (e.g., Ridgeline Haze may change the blend mix), and Kogein's performance could all shift materially over that period.
- **Opt-out window:** For the Initial Term ending January 31, 2029, the non-renewal notice window is approximately **July 4, 2028 through August 4, 2028** (210 to 180 days before January 31, 2029). Missing this 30-day window by even one day triggers a five-year lock-in.
- **MAV trap:** The 180,000-pound MAV continues in each Renewal Term. If Stabind's volume needs have changed (up or down), it is locked into the original commitment.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Negotiating position (at renewal or in a future amendment):**
  - Reduce Renewal Term length from five years to one or two years.
  - Replace "Kogein's then-current list price" with a pricing formula (e.g., indexed to a published hop price benchmark, or list price subject to a cap on annual increase of X%).
  - Widen the non-renewal notice window (e.g., 270 to 150 days before term end) to reduce the risk of missing the deadline.
  - Add a right to adjust the MAV at each renewal based on actual trailing volume.
- **Practical step (immediate):** Calendar the non-renewal notice window for the Initial Term (approximately July–August 2028) with multiple internal reminders. Assign ownership to the COO or General Counsel. If Stabind wishes to remain with Kogein but on better terms, begin renewal negotiations no later than Q1 2028 to have leverage before the notice window opens.

**Flag:** We do not have Exhibit B (Initial Term pricing), so I cannot assess whether the current prices are market or whether the "list price" mechanism would represent a significant increase.

## Issue 4 (Moderate): Asymmetric Assignment Rights and Overly Broad "Kogein Group Member" Definition

**Where:** Supply Agreement § 15.2; Agreement § 1.3 (incorporating DPA § 1.1(b) definition); DPA § 1.1(b); DPA § 3.4.

**What the provisions say.**

- Stabind may not assign without Kogein's prior written consent (Agreement § 15.2).
- Kogein may assign to any "Kogein Group Member" on **written notice only**—no consent required.
- "Kogein Group Member" (DPA § 1.1(b)) means: Kogein; any Affiliate of Kogein; **and any entity with which Kogein has a strategic supply or processing arrangement.**

**Why it matters.** The phrase "strategic supply or processing arrangement" is undefined and potentially very broad. It could encompass a contract grower, a co-packer, a logistics partner, or a joint-venture entity. Kogein could assign the entire supply agreement—along with Stabind's Blend Specifications, Forecasts, and ordering history—to such an entity with only notice, no consent. The receiving entity would step into Kogein's shoes with respect to the confidentiality obligations (Agreement § 9), the quality warranties (Agreement § 8), and the service levels (SLS), but Stabind would have no say in whether it wants to do business with that entity.

Additionally, DPA § 3.4 permits Kogein to disclose Stabind Personal Data to any Kogein Group Member, meaning the same broad category of entities could receive Stabind's personnel data.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Negotiating position:** Amend the "Kogein Group Member" definition (or the assignment provision) to limit assignable entities to Kogein and its Affiliates (as defined in Agreement § 1.1(a), requiring >50% control). If Kogein insists on including strategic partners, require Stabind's prior written consent (not merely notice) for assignment to any entity that is not an Affiliate.
- **Practical step:** Request that Kogein identify in writing all entities it currently considers "Kogein Group Members" under the DPA definition, so Stabind knows the universe of entities to which its data and contract could be transferred.

## Issue 5 (Moderate): DPA Liability Subject to the Agreement's General Cap

**Where:** DPA § 10.1; Supply Agreement § 11.1.

**What the provision says.** DPA § 10.1 states: "Each Party's liability under this DPA is subject to Section 11 of the Agreement." Section 11.1 caps aggregate liability at fees paid in the prior 12 months. The exclusions from the cap (Agreement § 11.2) cover indemnification, confidentiality breaches, gross negligence/fraud/willful misconduct, and (as amended) Product Contamination Losses from willful misconduct. A data breach caused by ordinary negligence would fall within the cap.

**Why it matters.** If Kogein suffers a data breach involving Stabind Personal Data, Stabind's recoverable costs (investigation, notification, credit monitoring under DPA § 6.3) are capped at 12 months of hop-pellet fees. For a brewery, that amount may be modest relative to the cost of a breach affecting customer or employee data. However, the data in scope (DPA Annex 1) is limited to business contact information of Stabind personnel (names, titles, business emails, phone numbers, delivery-dock sign-in records). No sensitive data is in scope. This limits the practical exposure.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Negotiating position:** Add DPA obligations (or at least Security Incident costs under DPA § 6.3) to the list of exclusions from the cap in Agreement § 11.2. Alternatively, negotiate a separate, higher cap for data protection liability.
- **Practical step:** Given the limited data in scope, this is lower priority. Confirm that Kogein's security program (DPA Annex 3) is being maintained and that the annual review (DPA § 5.2) is occurring.

## Issue 6 (Lower): Press Release Publicly Identifies the Supply Relationship

**Where:** Press Release §§ 1.2, 1.3; Supply Agreement § 9.1 (Confidential Information).

**What the press release says.** "Ridgeline Haze uses hop pellets grown and processed in Washington's Yakima Valley. The pellets are cold-stored and handled under refrigeration from the farm to Stabind's brewhouse" (§ 1.2). "The recipe was developed over eighteen months of small-batch pilot brewing" (§ 1.3).

**Why it matters.** While the press release does not name Kogein, it publicly confirms that Stabind sources hop pellets from a Yakima Valley farm and describes the cold-chain handling. Given that Kogein is Stabind's contracted hop supplier under a dedicated agreement with custom Blend Specifications, a reasonable reader in the craft beer industry would identify Kogein. This:
- Publicly confirms the supply relationship, reducing Stabind's ability to source from alternatives without market signal.
- Discloses general processing parameters (whirlpool hop, dry-hop twice, cold-stored from farm) that, while not the full Blend Specifications, provide competitive insight.
- Was issued November 3, 2026—after the Side Letter (September 15, 2026)—so it does not implicate the Side Letter's confidentiality clause (Side Letter § 5.2).

**Assessment.** The Blend Specifications (defined in Agreement § 1.1(b) as "the proprietary hop variety, blend ratio, alpha-acid, oil-content and processing parameters") are Stabind's Confidential Information. The press release does not disclose the specific variety, ratio, or analytical parameters. It discloses only the general brewing technique. This is unlikely to constitute a breach of § 9, but it is a competitive information leak.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Practical step:** Before issuing future press releases or marketing materials that reference hop sourcing, run them through legal to confirm they do not disclose Blend Specifications or identify Kogein by description. Consider adding a line to the Agreement's confidentiality section (or a side letter) that the identity of the supply relationship is Confidential Information of both parties.

## Issue 7 (Lower): Audit Frequency May Be Insufficient Given Current Performance

**Where:** Supply Agreement § 7.3; Side Letter § 4.

**What the provision says.** Stabind may audit Kogein's facilities, records, and quality systems no more than twice per Contract Year, on 10 business days' notice, unless a contamination event has occurred.

**Why it matters.** Kogein's OTD has been below the 95% service level for at least two consecutive quarters (88%, 89%). The Side Letter requires a remediation plan (due ~September 30, 2026) and monthly progress meetings, but does not increase audit rights. If the remediation plan identifies issues at Kogein's Moxee facility (cold-storage capacity, carrier allocation, pelletizing-line staffing per Side Letter § 4.1(a)–(c)), Stabind may want to verify remediation through an on-site audit. The twice-per-year limit may be insufficient if one audit is used for routine quality and another is needed to verify the remediation plan.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Practical step:** Use one of the two available audits this Contract Year to verify the remediation plan's implementation, timed after the first or second monthly progress meeting. If additional audit access is needed, negotiate an amendment to § 7.3 that permits an additional audit specifically to verify compliance with a remediation plan or corrective action plan.
