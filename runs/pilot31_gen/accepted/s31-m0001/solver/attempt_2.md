## Executive Summary

Stabind's supply relationship with Kogein is under significant stress. Kogein's on-time delivery has been well below the contractual 95% target (88% in Q1 2026, 89% in Q2 2026), triggering a Chronic Failure. Stabind attempted to elect a remedy but likely missed the contractual deadline. Compounding this, the September 2026 Side Letter contains an amendment that substantially weakens Stabind's protection against contamination losses. With the Ridgeline Haze launch now publicly announced for late November 2026, the operational and financial stakes are elevated. The issues below are ordered by importance to the client.

## Issue 1: Side Letter Amendment Eliminates Uncapped Contamination Recovery (CRITICAL)

**Where:** Side Letter § 3.1 (amending Agreement § 11.2(d)); Agreement §§ 1.1(l), 11.1, 11.2(d), 17.4.

**What happened:** The original Agreement § 11.2(d) excluded Product Contamination Losses from the liability cap entirely, meaning Stabind could recover unlimited first-party contamination costs (recall, destroyed inventory, equipment remediation) from Kogein. The Side Letter, effective September 15, 2026, replaced that provision with:

> "(d) Product Contamination Losses, to the extent arising from Kogein's willful misconduct."

Under the order of precedence (Agreement § 17.4), the Side Letter controls. Product Contamination Losses are now subject to the § 11.1 cap (fees paid in the prior 12 months) unless Stabind can prove Kogein acted with *willful misconduct*—a standard that requires intentional wrongdoing, far higher than gross negligence.

**Why it matters:** Product Contamination Losses (Agreement § 1.1(l)) include recall costs, destroyed beer/wort/packaging, and equipment remediation. A single contamination event during the Ridgeline Haze launch window could produce losses well in excess of 12 months of hop fees. The practical effect is that for any contamination caused by negligence (the most common scenario), Stabind's recovery is capped. Note that third-party claims remain uncapped via the § 10.1(a) indemnification (excluded from the cap by § 11.2(a)), but Stabind's own first-party losses are not protected.

**Recommendation:**
- **Negotiating position:** Seek to restore the original § 11.2(d) language or, at minimum, lower the threshold from "willful misconduct" to "gross negligence." Proposed language: *"(d) Product Contamination Losses, to the extent arising from Kogein's gross negligence or willful misconduct."*
- **Practical step:** If Kogein resists, negotiate a higher dedicated contamination insurance requirement (currently $3M per occurrence under Agreement § 12.1(b)) to close the gap between the cap and realistic loss exposure. Confirm the actual 12-month fee cap amount by reviewing Exhibit B pricing (not in the folder—see Open Items).
- **Flag:** This amendment was signed by Stabind's CEO on September 15, 2026. If the client believes the amendment was obtained without adequate understanding of its effect, there may be arguments around知情 consent or unconscionability, but these are fact-intensive and uncertain under New York law.

## Issue 2: Chronic Failure Election Notice Likely Untimely (HIGH)

**Where:** Service Level Schedule §§ 2.1, 4.1, 4.2, 4.3; Side Letter §§ 1.2(b)–(d), 2.1.

**What happened:** A Chronic Failure occurs when on-time delivery (OTD) is below 92% in two consecutive calendar quarters (SLS § 4.1). Q1 2026 OTD was 88%; Q2 2026 OTD was 89%. The Q2 2026 Performance Report was delivered July 8, 2026. SLS § 4.2 requires Stabind's election notice within 30 days after delivery of that report—i.e., by **August 7, 2026**. Stabind's Election Notice was delivered **August 28, 2026**—21 days late.

Kogein has disputed timeliness (Side Letter § 1.2(d)). The Side Letter preserves both parties' positions (Side Letter § 2.1) but does not resolve the dispute.

**Why it matters:** If the notice was untimely, Stabind lost its contractual right to (a) reduce the Minimum Annual Volume by up to 50% or (b) terminate the Agreement under § 13.3. Stabind is then locked into the full 180,000-lb MAU despite Kogein's chronic underperformance, with no contractual mechanism to adjust it short of a future Chronic Failure or material breach termination.

**Recommendation:**
- **Assess the material breach alternative.** SLS § 4.6 states that Chronic Failure remedies are "in addition to... Stabind's other rights and remedies under the Agreement." A sustained two-quarter failure to meet a core delivery obligation (95% OTD) at 88–89% may constitute a material breach under Agreement § 13.1, for which Stabind could give a 30-day cure notice and terminate if uncured. This is a stronger but more aggressive path; Kogein may argue the specific SLS remedies are the intended exclusive route for OTD failures.
- **Negotiate a commercial resolution.** Use the pending dispute and the remediation plan (Side Letter § 4) as leverage to agree to a MAU reduction for the current and future Contract Years, documented in a further side letter.
- **Calendar going forward.** If another Chronic Failure occurs, the 30-day election window is strict. Implement an internal protocol: the moment a Performance Report arrives, the clock starts. Assign ownership to the COO's office with a hard internal deadline of 20 days from receipt.

## Issue 3: Minimum Annual Volume Shortfall Exposure (HIGH)

**Where:** Agreement §§ 3.2, 13.4; Side Letter § 2.3.

**What happened:** Stabind must purchase at least 180,000 lbs per Contract Year. If actual purchases fall below that, Kogein may invoice the shortfall at the weighted average price (Agreement § 3.2). The Side Letter temporarily blocks Kogein from issuing a shortfall invoice for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026, pending resolution of the Chronic Failure dispute (Side Letter § 2.3), but Kogein's right to invoice within 60 days after resolution is preserved.

**Why it matters:** If Kogein's delivery failures (88–89% OTD) caused Stabind to receive fewer pounds than it ordered, Stabind's actual *purchases* may be below 180,000 lbs through no fault of its own. The Agreement does not contain a provision reducing the MAU for Kogein-caused delivery shortfalls outside the Chronic Failure mechanism. If the Chronic Failure election is deemed untimely (Issue 2), Stabind has no contractual shield against a shortfall invoice for pounds Kogein failed to deliver.

**Recommendation:**
- **Quantify the exposure.** Determine actual pounds purchased vs. 180,000 for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026. Multiply the shortfall by the weighted average price. This is the worst-case invoice Stabind could receive.
- **Negotiating position:** In any resolution of the Chronic Failure dispute, insist that the MAU be reduced to reflect pounds Kogein failed to deliver due to its own OTD failures. Propose language: *"The Minimum Annual Volume for the Contract Year beginning February 1, 2026 shall be reduced by the number of pounds that Kogein failed to deliver On Time and In Full during that Contract Year, as reflected in the Performance Reports."*
- **Practical step:** If Kogein issues a shortfall invoice after the dispute is resolved, dispute it in writing under Agreement § 6.3 (good faith dispute, 30-day resolution window) and assert that the shortfall was caused by Kogein's breach of its delivery obligations.

## Issue 4: Automatic Renewal for Five Years at Kogein's List Price (MEDIUM-HIGH)

**Where:** Agreement § 2.2.

**What happened:** The Initial Term ends January 31, 2029. The Agreement then auto-renews for successive **five-year** terms. Pricing during each Renewal Term is "Kogein's then-current list price" as of the first day of that term. Non-renewal notice must be given no more than 210 days and no less than 180 days before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters:** If Stabind misses the notice window (approximately July 4 – August 3, 2028), it is locked in for five additional years at whatever price Kogein publishes—no negotiation, no cap, no reference to the Initial Term pricing in Exhibit B. For a commodity input like hops, list prices can shift materially. A five-year lock-in at an uncontrolled price is a significant commercial risk, particularly for a regional brewery with limited bargaining leverage.

**Recommendation:**
- **Calendar the deadline immediately.** Non-renewal notice must be delivered between **July 4, 2028** (210 days before January 31, 2029) and **August 3, 2028** (180 days before). Set internal reminders for May 2028 to begin the decision process.
- **Negotiating position for renewal:** If Stabind intends to renew, seek to amend § 2.2 to replace "Kogein's then-current list price" with a formula tied to a published hop index (e.g., the USDA or Hop Commission average price for the relevant varieties) plus/minus a fixed margin, or to require good-faith price negotiation with a dispute mechanism.
- **Practical step:** Even if Stabind plans to renew, the threat of non-renewal is leverage. Consider raising the renewal pricing issue in the next quarterly review (SLS § 5.1) or in connection with the Chronic Failure resolution.

## Issue 5: Asymmetric Assignment and Overbroad "Kogein Group Member" Definition (MEDIUM)

**Where:** Agreement § 15.2; DPA § 1.1(b); Agreement § 4.2; DPA § 3.4.

**What happened:** Stabind cannot assign the Agreement without Kogein's consent (Agreement § 15.2). Kogein, however, may assign to any "Kogein Group Member" on mere written notice. "Kogein Group Member" is defined in DPA § 1.1(b) as Kogein, any Affiliate, **and any entity with which Kogein has a strategic supply or processing arrangement.** This is not limited to entities Kogein controls.

**Why it matters:**
- **Blend Specifications risk.** The Blend Specifications are Stabind's exclusive property (Agreement § 4.2). Kogein is barred from producing for others using them. But if Kogein assigns the Agreement to a "strategic supply" partner—an entity Stabind has never vetted—that partner steps into Kogein's shoes with access to the specs. While the assignee would be contractually bound by § 4.2, the practical risk of proprietary recipe leakage increases with each unknown entity in the chain.
- **Data sharing risk.** Under DPA § 3.4, Kogein may disclose Stabind Personal Data (personnel names, emails, phone numbers, delivery dock records, visitor logs) to any Kogein Group Member. The overbroad definition means this could include entities with no relationship to Stabind other than a "strategic supply arrangement" with Kogein.
- **Accountability risk.** If Kogein assigns to a financially weaker or less established entity, Stabind's credit and performance security may be diminished.

**Recommendation:**
- **Negotiating position:** Amend the "Kogein Group Member" definition to limit it to Affiliates (entities under common control per Agreement § 1.1(a)) and delete the "strategic supply or processing arrangement" prong. Alternatively, require Stabind's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any assignment to a non-Affiliate.
- **Practical step:** Request that Kogein identify all current and anticipated Kogein Group Members to whom the Agreement or Stabind Personal Data may be assigned or disclosed. If any are competitors' suppliers or entities with competing brewing interests, raise specific concerns.

## Issue 6: Ridgeline Haze Launch Timing vs. Kogein Delivery Performance (MEDIUM)

**Where:** Press Release (Nov. 3, 2026); Side Letter §§ 1.2(b), 4.1–4.2; SLS §§ 2.1, 4.1.

**What happened:** Stabind publicly announced Ridgeline Haze on November 3, 2026, with retail availability "beginning in late November 2026" and a launch event on November 21, 2026. The beer uses Washington Yakima Valley hop pellets, cold-stored from farm to brewhouse (Press Release § 1.2). Kogein's OTD has been 88–89% for the last two quarters. The Side Letter required Kogein to deliver a remediation plan within 15 business days of September 15, 2026 (i.e., by approximately October 2, 2026).

**Why it matters:** The public announcement creates consumer and trade expectations. If Kogein's delivery problems persist into the November–December launch window, Stabind risks:
- Delayed or incomplete product availability at retail
- Quality degradation if hops are held beyond optimal storage or arrive outside the temperature specification (Agreement § 5.2: ≤ 40°F in transit)
- Reputational harm from a high-profile launch failure

The contract does not give Stabind a specific right to expedite or prioritize Ridgeline Haze orders, though Purchase Orders consistent with the Forecast "may not be rejected" (Agreement § 5.1).

**Recommendation:**
- **Confirm remediation plan delivery.** As of the documents in the folder, there is no evidence Kogein delivered the remediation plan by the October 2 deadline. If it has not been delivered, send a written demand referencing Side Letter § 4.1 and note that failure to deliver constitutes a breach of the Side Letter.
- **Practical step:** Ensure the most recent Forecast (due the first business day of each quarter, Agreement § 3.3) specifically identifies Ridgeline Haze volume requirements and variety/blend specs so that Kogein's crop allocation obligation (Agreement § 3.4) covers the launch.
- **Contingency:** Identify an alternative hop supplier or source for the specific varieties used in Ridgeline Haze as a backup, even if only for the launch period. Agreement § 14.3 permits substitute purchases during a Force Majeure event, but there is no equivalent right for ordinary delivery failures.

## Issue 7: DPA Data Sharing to Overbroad Group (MEDIUM-LOW)

**Where:** DPA §§ 1.1(b), 3.4; Annex 1.

**What happened:** As noted in Issue 5, the "Kogein Group Member" definition is overbroad. DPA § 3.4 permits Kogein to share Stabind Personal Data with any Kogein Group Member "to the extent necessary" for Agreement performance. Annex 1 identifies the data as: names, job titles, business emails, business phone numbers, signatures, delivery-dock sign-in records, and facility visitor records.

**Why it matters:** While the data categories are not highly sensitive (no consumer data, no sensitive data per Annex 1), facility visitor records and delivery-dock sign-in records could reveal operational patterns (e.g., which carriers visit, when, how frequently) that are commercially sensitive. Sharing this with Kogein's "strategic supply" partners could inadvertently disclose Stabind's logistics footprint.

**Recommendation:**
- Fold into the negotiating position in Issue 5: narrow the Kogein Group Member definition.
- **Practical step:** In the next quarterly review, ask Kogein to confirm in writing which Kogein Group Members (if any) have actually received Stabind Personal Data and for what purpose.

## Issue 8: DPA Liability Subject to General Cap with No Data-Breach Exclusion (LOW)

**Where:** DPA § 10.1; Agreement §§ 11.1, 11.2.

**What happened:** DPA § 10.1 subjects all DPA liability to Agreement § 11 (the general cap and exclusions). None of the § 11.2 exclusions specifically addresses data protection breaches. A data breach caused by Kogein's ordinary negligence would be capped at 12 months of fees.

**Why it matters:** Given the limited scope of Stabind Personal Data (business contacts, delivery records—per Annex 1), the realistic exposure from a data breach is modest compared to contamination or delivery risks. However, if a breach exposed facility security information (visitor logs, delivery schedules), the operational risk could exceed the cap.

**Recommendation:**
- **Negotiating position:** Add a DPA-specific exclusion from the cap for Security Incidents caused by Kogein's breach of DPA § 5 (Security). Proposed language for DPA § 10.1: *"Each Party's liability under this DPA is subject to Section 11 of the Agreement, except that the limitation in Section 11.1 shall not apply to Kogein's liability arising from a Security Incident caused by Kogein's breach of Section 5."*
- **Practical step:** Lower priority given the data scope. Address in the next DPA amendment cycle if at all.
