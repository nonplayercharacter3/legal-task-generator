## 1. Exclusivity Clause Dramatically Expanded from Term Sheet to Order Form

**Where:** Term Sheet §5.1; Order Form No. 1 §6.1; License Agreement §17.2 (order of precedence).

The Term Sheet committed Trouvar to "no third-party batch-record software at the Raleigh, North Carolina facility during the Initial Term." The executed Order Form §6.1 expands this to: (a) all facilities worldwide; (b) all Affiliates; (c) "manufacturing execution, quality-management or batch-record software" (not just batch-record); (d) the full Term (including any auto-renewals) plus 24 months; and (e) prohibits Trouvar from even *developing* alternative software. Under License Agreement §17.2, the Order Form prevails over the License Agreement, so §6.1 is the binding term.

**Why it matters:** Trouvar is a sterile injectables manufacturer that may operate or acquire additional sites. The worldwide, all-Affiliate scope means Trouvar cannot deploy a competing QMS or MES at any entity it controls, even if LairalQMS is not licensed there. The "develop" language could restrict internal R&D. The 24-month tail beyond the Term means Trouvar remains locked in even after non-renewal.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to Order Form §6.1 restoring the Term Sheet scope (Raleigh facility only, batch-record software only, Initial Term only, no "develop" restriction). If Lairal resists, seek at minimum: (a) limitation to facilities where LairalQMS is actually licensed; (b) deletion of "develop"; and (c) reduction of the post-Term tail to 12 months.

## 2. Phase 1 Deliverables Likely Deemed Accepted – Rejection Was Time-Barred

**Where:** License Agreement §6.3; SOW No. 2, Recital C.

Phase 1 Deliverables were deployed on September 2, 2026 (SOW No. 2, Recital B). Section 6.3 of the License Agreement gives 15 Business Days to reject. Counting from September 3, 2026, the deadline fell on or about September 23, 2026. Trouvar's written rejection was not issued until October 6, 2026 (Recital C) – approximately nine Business Days late. Under §6.3, the Deliverables are deemed accepted.

**Why it matters:** Trouvar has lost its contractual right to reject and force rework under §6.4. Its remaining remedies are: (a) the 90-day Deliverable warranty under §6.6 (running from deemed acceptance, i.e., expiring around late December 2026), which allows correction of nonconformities but not termination or refund; and (b) a general material-breach claim under §14.3 if the nonconformities are sufficiently severe. SOW No. 2 §2.9(e) expressly excludes Phase 1 rework from the Phase 2 scope, so Lairal has no contractual obligation to fix these issues as part of Phase 2.

Additionally, §6.3 provides a second deemed-acceptance trigger: use in the production environment for commercial release. Once go-live occurs, any unresolved Phase 1 issues will be contractually deemed accepted regardless of the warranty period.

**Recommended fix:** Immediately (a) assess whether the identified nonconformities (review-by-exception workflow, audit-trail configuration, equipment master data) can be raised under the §6.6 warranty before it expires; (b) document the nonconformities in writing as a warranty claim, not a rejection; and (c) negotiate a side letter or change order with Lairal addressing the Phase 1 issues as a condition of executing SOW No. 2. Do not allow production go-live to occur until the Phase 1 issues are resolved or expressly documented as accepted with a fee credit.

## 3. SOW No. 2 Is Unexecuted – No Binding Phase 2 Agreement

**Where:** SOW No. 2, header ("DRAFT: October 20, 2026. For discussion purposes only. Not binding until executed by both Parties."); signature blocks are blank.

**Why it matters:** Trouvar has no enforceable right to Phase 2 deliverables, schedule commitments, or the fixed milestone fee structure ($380,000 total). If Trouvar's personnel begin validation activities or provide Licensee Materials in reliance on the draft, Lairal could argue no binding scope exists. Conversely, if Trouvar wants to walk away or renegotiate, it has no contractual commitment – but also no protection.

**Recommended fix:** Execute SOW No. 2 promptly, but only after resolving the Phase 1 issue (Item 2 above). Before signing, confirm: (a) the key personnel in §9.1 are actually assigned; (b) the assumptions in §11.1 are realistic (particularly the 180 OQ test script cap and 40 person-day limit); and (c) add a provision that execution of SOW No. 2 does not constitute a waiver of Trouvar's rights regarding the Phase 1 Deliverables.

## 4. Quality Agreement Not Yet Executed

**Where:** License Agreement §8.3 (60-day deadline from Effective Date); Term Sheet §8.3.

The Effective Date is March 16, 2026. The 60-day deadline for executing the quality agreement expired on or about May 15, 2026. No quality agreement appears in the folder.

**Why it matters:** The quality agreement is the operational document that allocates GxP responsibilities for change control, deviation management, incident management, and supplier audits. Without it, Trouvar's quality unit lacks a contractual basis for requiring Lairal to follow Trouvar's change-control procedures when patches or workarounds are applied (see SLS §6.8, which references the quality agreement). In a regulatory inspection, the absence of a quality agreement for a hosted GxP system is a significant finding.

**Recommended fix:** Escalate to Lairal immediately. The obligation is to "negotiate in good faith and enter into" the agreement within 60 days. Trouvar should put Lairal on written notice that the deadline has passed and demand a execution date. If Lairal resists, document the gap as a potential material breach under §14.3 (30-day cure period).

## 5. Subscription Fees Run Regardless of Go-Live Date

**Where:** Order Form No. 1 §4.4; §5.1 (billing schedule).

Section 4.4 states that "the Subscription Start Date, the Initial Term and Licensee's obligation to pay the Annual Subscription Fee are not affected by the date of production go-live." The first $1,240,000 invoice was due March 16, 2026. Phase 2 validation is not yet contracted (Item 3), and the Phase 1 dispute (Item 2) could delay go-live by months.

**Why it matters:** Trouvar is paying full subscription fees for a system it cannot use for GxP batch release. If go-live slips to, say, Q2 2027, Trouvar pays nearly two years of subscription fees before the system is in production. There is no abatement, credit, or termination right tied to go-live delay.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a go-live milestone provision: if production go-live has not occurred by a longstop date (e.g., 18 months from the Subscription Start Date) for reasons attributable to Licensor (including unresolved Phase 1 nonconformities or failure to deliver Phase 2 services), Trouvar may terminate the Order Form with a pro-rata refund of prepaid subscription fees. Alternatively, seek a fee holiday or reduction for the period between the Subscription Start Date and actual go-live.

## 6. SOW No. 2 Licensee Indemnity Is Broad

**Where:** SOW No. 2 §8.1(b).

Trouvar indemnifies Lairal for claims arising from "Licensee-supplied validation protocols, test scripts, user requirement specifications and acceptance criteria, including any deficiency in them or in Licensee's validation of the Licensed Software for its intended use."

**Why it matters:** The phrase "any deficiency in them or in Licensee's validation" is open-ended. If a validation protocol has a gap that contributes to a data-integrity failure leading to a product recall or regulatory action, Trouvar would owe Lairal an indemnity for Lairal's resulting liability – even if Lairal's own configuration or software contributed to the problem. The exclusion in §8.2 (Licensor's breach or negligence) helps but places the burden on Trouvar to prove Licensor fault.

**Recommended fix:** Before executing SOW No. 2, narrow §8.1(b) to cover deficiencies in Licensee-supplied documents only to the extent the claim would not have arisen but for those deficiencies (a "but-for" causation standard), and add that the indemnity does not apply where the deficiency was caused or contributed to by Licensor's software, configuration, or failure to follow the documented specifications.

## 7. Version Lock Assumption Creates Schedule Risk

**Where:** SOW No. 2 §11.1(d); License Agreement §3.4.

SOW No. 2 assumes "the version of the Licensed Software deployed at go-live will be the version deployed to the validation environment at the start of OQ execution, with only patch-level Updates during execution." If Lairal releases a major version during the validation window, the assumption breaks, triggering a change request under §11.2. Under License Agreement §3.4, Lairal must give 90 days' notice of a major release and deploy to the validation environment first, but it is not prohibited from making the release.

**Why it matters:** A mid-validation major release would require re-testing, potentially re-execution of OQ/PQ protocols, and delay to go-live. The fixed milestone fees in SOW No. 2 §6.1 do not account for this rework, and any additional work would be billed at time-and-materials rates (§6.2).

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision to SOW No. 2 (or a side letter) that if a major version release occurs during the validation execution window, Licensor will absorb the cost of re-validation support up to a stated cap (e.g., 10% of the Phase 2 fee), and the schedule will be extended day-for-day without additional charge to Licensee.

## 8. Auto-Renewal Compounds the Exclusivity Lock-In

**Where:** License Agreement §14.1; Order Form No. 1 §4.3; §6.1.

Order Form No. 1 renews automatically for successive one-year terms unless either party gives 90 days' non-renewal notice. The Initial Term ends March 15, 2029, so the non-renewal notice deadline is December 15, 2028. The exclusivity in §6.1 runs for "the Term" (which includes renewals) plus 24 months.

**Why it matters:** If Trouvar misses the December 2028 deadline, it is locked into another year of subscription fees *and* another year of worldwide exclusivity. Combined with Item 1, this creates a compounding lock-in effect.

**Recommended fix:** Calendar the December 15, 2028 non-renewal notice deadline now. If the exclusivity is not narrowed per Item 1, consider whether the auto-renewal is acceptable given the exclusivity tail. At minimum, ensure the business team (not just legal) is aware of the deadline.

## 9. Deemed Acceptance by Production Use – A Go-Live Trap

**Where:** License Agreement §6.3 (second sentence).

"A Deliverable will also be deemed accepted if Licensee uses it in the production environment for the release of commercial product, other than for testing or validation purposes."

**Why it matters:** Once Trouvar releases its first commercial batch using LairalQMS, every deliverable under every SOW is deemed accepted. This eliminates any residual right to claim nonconformity, even for issues discovered after go-live (subject only to the software warranty in §13.2, which covers performance against Documentation, not deliverable conformance).

**Recommended fix:** Before go-live, ensure all open deliverable acceptance/rejection decisions are resolved in writing. Consider a go-live checklist that includes sign-off from the project manager confirming no open deliverables remain. If any Phase 1 issues are still unresolved at go-live, document them as a known issue with an agreed remediation plan and fee adjustment, so they are not silently deemed accepted.
