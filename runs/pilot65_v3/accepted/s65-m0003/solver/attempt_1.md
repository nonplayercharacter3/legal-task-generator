## I. Executive Summary

Trouvar entered a three-year hosted software license with Lairal Systems, LLC (effective March 16, 2026) for the LairalQMS platform at its Raleigh, North Carolina sterile injectables facility. The arrangement is documented across a master License Agreement, Order Form No. 1, a Service Level Schedule, a Kessmont parent guaranty, a prior mutual NDA, a non-binding January 2026 term sheet, and a draft SOW No. 2. Phase 1 implementation deliverables were rejected by Trouvar on October 6, 2026, and the dispute is unresolved. SOW No. 2 (Phase 2 validation) remains in draft.

The most pressing issues are: (1) an exclusivity clause in the Order Form that is materially broader than what the term sheet contemplated; (2) the active Phase 1 dispute and its interaction with the Phase 2 schedule; (3) subscription fees running from the Effective Date regardless of go-live; and (4) a liability cap that limits the practical value of the Kessmont guaranty.

## II. Issues and Recommendations

### Issue 1: Overly Broad Exclusivity Commitment

**Where:** Order Form No. 1, § 6.1; compare Term Sheet, § 5.1.

**The problem.** The term sheet (non-binding) contemplated exclusivity limited to "no third-party batch-record software at the Raleigh, North Carolina facility during the Initial Term." The executed Order Form, however, provides that "during the Term and for twenty-four (24) months thereafter, Licensee shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, license, procure, develop or use, **at any facility worldwide**, any manufacturing execution, quality-management or batch-record software other than the Licensed Software." This is a five-year, worldwide, all-facility lock-in covering not just batch-record software but all QMS and MES platforms, and it extends to Affiliates. Under License Agreement § 17.2, the Order Form prevails over the License Agreement in the event of conflict, so this is the operative term.

**Why it matters.** If Trouvar opens a second manufacturing site, acquires another company, or wants to pilot a different quality-management tool at any location, it would breach the Order Form. The 24-month tail means the restriction outlives the subscription by two years. Given the current Phase 1 dispute, Trouvar may want to evaluate alternative vendors as leverage or as a fallback, but this clause forecloses that option.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate an amendment to § 6.1 that (a) limits the geographic scope to the Raleigh Facility (or facilities where the Licensed Software is actually deployed under an Order Form); (b) narrows the product scope to electronic batch-record software only; (c) deletes the 24-month tail or reduces it to the remainder of the then-current Subscription Year; and (d) carves out Affiliates that do not use the Licensed Software. Use the Phase 1 dispute as the commercial lever.

### Issue 2: Unresolved Phase 1 Dispute and Its Impact on Phase 2

**Where:** SOW No. 2, Recital C; SOW No. 2, § 2.9(e); SOW No. 2, § 6.3; License Agreement, §§ 6.3–6.4.

**The problem.** Trouvar rejected the Phase 1 Deliverables on October 6, 2026, citing nonconformities in the electronic batch-record review-by-exception workflow, audit-trail configuration, and migrated equipment master data. Lairal disputes the rejection. SOW No. 2, § 2.9(e) expressly excludes "any rework of the Phase 1 Deliverables" from Phase 2 scope, and § 6.3 confirms the $310,000 Phase 1 milestone fee remains governed by SOW No. 1. The draft SOW No. 2 was prepared on the assumption (Recital E) that the parties would "proceed with the next phase of the project" in parallel with the Phase 1 discussions.

**Why it matters.** If the Phase 1 nonconformities are not resolved before or during Phase 2 validation, Trouvar risks validating a system that does not meet its user requirements. The SOW No. 2 assumptions in § 11.1(d) (same software version at go-live as at OQ start, only patch-level updates) may be invalidated if Phase 1 rework requires a configuration change or version update. Additionally, under License Agreement § 6.3, the 15-Business-Day rejection window has long passed, so the question is whether Trouvar's October 6 rejection was timely under SOW No. 1's terms. If Lairal can argue the rejection was untimely, the Phase 1 Deliverables may be deemed accepted, which would affect Trouvar's leverage and its right to withhold the $310,000 milestone fee.

**Recommended fix.** Before executing SOW No. 2: (a) confirm the rejection was timely under SOW No. 1's acceptance procedure and document the basis; (b) require Lairal to either cure the Phase 1 nonconformities or agree in writing to a specific remediation plan with dates as a condition of Phase 2 proceeding; (c) add a provision to SOW No. 2 (or a side letter) stating that if Phase 1 rework is required, it will be performed at no additional charge and will not delay the Phase 2 schedule; and (d) consider whether to invoke the License Agreement § 17.3 dispute escalation before signing SOW No. 2.

### Issue 3: Subscription Fees Running Before Go-Live

**Where:** Order Form No. 1, §§ 4.1, 4.4, 5.1; License Agreement, § 9.1.

**The problem.** The Annual Subscription Fee of $1,240,000 is invoiced annually in advance starting March 16, 2026. Order Form § 4.4 states explicitly that "the Subscription Start Date, the Initial Term and Licensee's obligation to pay the Annual Subscription Fee are not affected by the date of production go-live." Trouvar has now been paying for over seven months (since March 16, 2026) without the system in production use, and the Phase 1 dispute makes a near-term go-live uncertain.

**Why it matters.** Trouvar is funding a $1.24M/year subscription for a system it cannot yet use for GxP manufacturing. If the Phase 1 dispute drags on into 2027, Trouvar will have paid a second annual fee (due March 16, 2027) while still not in production. This weakens Trouvar's negotiating position and creates cash-flow pressure.

**Recommended fix.** Negotiate one or more of the following: (a) a fee abatement or credit for the period between the Subscription Start Date and actual production go-live (capped at a reasonable number of months); (b) a milestone-based deferral of the Year 2 invoice until go-live occurs; or (c) if go-live does not occur by a longstop date (e.g., 12 months from the Effective Date), a right to terminate Order Form No. 1 with a full refund of prepaid fees. Tie this to resolution of Issue 2.

### Issue 4: Parent Guaranty Subject to Liability Cap

**Where:** Kessmont Parent Guaranty, § 2.5; License Agreement, § 12.2.

**The problem.** Guaranty § 2.5 provides that "any limitation or exclusion of Licensor's liability under Section 12 of the License Agreement applies to the Guarantor's liability for the corresponding Guaranteed Obligation to the same extent, and in the same way, as it applies to Licensor." The License Agreement caps aggregate liability at fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim (§ 12.2). This means Kessmont's maximum exposure on most claims is approximately $1,240,000 (plus any Professional Services fees in the trailing 12 months), which is the same as what Trouvar could recover from Lairal directly. The guaranty adds credit-enhancement value only if Lairal itself is unable to pay (e.g., insolvency), but it does not expand the quantum of recovery.

**Why it matters.** The guaranty was a material inducement for Trouvar to enter the License Agreement (Guaranty Recital C; Term Sheet § 13). If Trouvar suffers a significant loss—e.g., a data integrity failure requiring product recall, or a prolonged service outage—the $1.24M cap may be far short of actual damages. The exceptions in License Agreement § 12.3 (breach of confidentiality/data security, gross negligence, fraud, willful misconduct, indemnification obligations) are not capped, but proving gross negligence or willful misconduct in a software hosting context is difficult.

**Recommended fix.** Seek an amendment to Guaranty § 2.5 to carve out from the cap passthrough at least: (a) Licensor's data restoration obligations under License Agreement § 12.4; (b) obligations arising from a Security Incident under § 10.8; and (c) Licensor's indemnification obligations under § 11.1. If Lairal resists, accept the current structure but ensure Trouvar's own cyber and product liability insurance is calibrated to the gap.

### Issue 5: Deemed Acceptance Risk for Phase 2 Deliverables

**Where:** License Agreement, § 6.3; SOW No. 2, §§ 5.1–5.2.

**The problem.** Under § 6.3, a Deliverable is deemed accepted if Trouvar does not issue a written rejection within 15 Business Days of delivery, or if Trouvar uses the Deliverable in the production environment for commercial release. SOW No. 2, § 5.2 adds that approval of protocols (D2.3, D2.4, D2.7) by the Licensee Validation Lead in the document management system "constitutes acceptance." The Phase 2 schedule (SOW No. 2, § 4.1) compresses multiple deliverables into tight windows (e.g., D2.1 and D2.2 due 15 Business Days from the SOW Effective Date; IQ and OQ protocols due 35 Business Days).

**Why it matters.** Given the volume of validation documentation and the GxP context, a 15-Business-Day review window is aggressive. If Trouvar's quality unit misses the window, the Deliverable is deemed accepted and the associated milestone fee becomes payable (§ 6.5). The Phase 1 dispute demonstrates that Lairal will assert deemed acceptance if it believes the window has passed.

**Recommended fix.** Before executing SOW No. 2: (a) negotiate an extension of the review period for protocol Deliverables (D2.3, D2.4) to 20 or 25 Business Days, given their regulatory significance; (b) add a provision that the 15-Business-Day clock does not start until Trouvar's designated project manager confirms receipt in writing; and (c) ensure internal workflows (document management system alerts, validation lead calendar) are set up to track delivery dates.

### Issue 6: NDA Disclosure Period Expiring

**Where:** Mutual NDA, § 10.1; License Agreement, § 10.12.

**The problem.** The NDA's Disclosure Period ends on the second anniversary of the NDA Effective Date: October 14, 2027. After that date, the NDA no longer covers newly disclosed information. However, License Agreement § 10.12 provides that Confidential Information exchanged under the NDA before the License Agreement Effective Date (March 16, 2026) "will, from the Effective Date, be treated as Confidential Information under this [License] Agreement." So information disclosed before March 16, 2026 is covered by the License Agreement's confidentiality regime (which runs for 5 years post-termination, or indefinitely for trade secrets and Licensee Data). Information disclosed between March 16, 2026 and October 14, 2027 is covered by both. Information disclosed after October 14, 2027 is covered only by the License Agreement.

**Why it matters.** In practice, this is a low-risk gap because the License Agreement's confidentiality provisions (§ 10) are at least as protective as the NDA. However, the NDA contains a few provisions not replicated in the License Agreement: the combination rule (§ 2.2), the no-reverse-engineering covenant (§ 3.6), and the prevailing-party fee-shifting (§ 11.3). If a dispute arises over information disclosed after October 14, 2027, Trouvar would lose the benefit of those NDA-specific protections.

**Recommended fix.** Low priority. If the relationship continues beyond 2027, consider either (a) extending the NDA Disclosure Period by mutual amendment, or (b) confirming that the License Agreement's confidentiality section adequately covers the gap. No action needed before the next renewal cycle.

### Issue 7: Auto-Renewal and Non-Renewal Deadline

**Where:** License Agreement, § 14.1; Order Form No. 1, § 4.3.

**The problem.** Order Form No. 1 renews automatically for successive one-year terms unless either party gives written notice of non-renewal at least 90 days before the end of the then-current term. The Initial Term ends March 15, 2029. The non-renewal notice deadline for the first renewal is therefore December 15, 2028.

**Why it matters.** If Trouvar is dissatisfied with the relationship (e.g., due to the exclusivity lock-in, service performance, or pricing at renewal), it must act by mid-December 2028. Missing that deadline commits Trouvar to another year at up to 5% (or CPI-U) higher fees.

**Recommended fix.** Calendar the December 15, 2028 deadline now. Build the renewal decision into the 2028 Q3 business planning cycle. If the exclusivity issue (Issue 1) is not resolved, make non-renewal a realistic option.
