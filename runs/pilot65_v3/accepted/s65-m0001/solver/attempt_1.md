## I. CRITICAL: Bill of Sale Conveys Narrower Rights Than SPA Represents

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 1.2 ("Distribution Rights" definition) vs. SPA § 1.1 ("Distribution Rights" definition) and SPA Recital B.

**The issue:** The SPA defines "Distribution Rights" to include rights in "all media, formats and methods of delivery now known or later devised, including theatrical, non-theatrical, physical home video, television (free, pay and basic), and all forms of on-demand and internet-delivered exhibition, including subscription, advertising-supported and transactional video-on-demand" (SPA § 1.1). Recital B states that Rights Holdings "conveyed to the Company the Distribution Rights in the Library Titles under the Rights Bill of Sale," using the SPA's broad defined term.

However, the Bill of Sale defines "Distribution Rights" in § 1.2 as only "the theatrical, non-theatrical and physical home-video rights in and to that Picture." Section 2.1 conveys only those rights. Television, streaming (SVOD, AVOD, TVOD), and all other digital/internet-delivered rights are **not** conveyed by the Bill of Sale.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt is paying $28.5 million for a company whose library presumably generates significant digital/streaming revenue. If the Company does not hold title to those digital rights, (a) the SPA representation in § 4.9(b) that the Company "owns or holds" the Distribution Rights (as broadly defined in the SPA) is false; (b) the Company's existing digital licenses may be unenforceable or subject to challenge by Rights Holdings; and (c) Subirt may be paying for rights it does not actually acquire. Because § 4.9 is a General Representation (not a Fundamental Representation), Subirt's indemnification recovery would be subject to the $213,750 deductible and the 12% Seller Cap ($3,420,000) under SPA § 9.5(a)–(b), and the survival period is only until the Note Maturity Date (January 1, 2027) under SPA § 9.1(a).

**Recommended fix:** Before closing, require Seller to either (a) deliver a supplemental assignment from Rights Holdings to the Company covering all digital, television, and other rights not included in the Bill of Sale's narrower definition; or (b) confirm in writing, with supporting chain-of-title documentation, that the Company acquired those rights directly from producers or other licensors under separate agreements (which would appear as Material Contracts under SPA § 4.7(a)(ii)). If neither is possible, the purchase price should be adjusted or the deal should be re-evaluated. At minimum, the SPA Recital B and § 4.9(b) should be conformed to accurately describe what was conveyed.

## II. Bill of Sale Consent Gap Creates Chain-of-Title Risk

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 2.6; SPA § 4.9(d).

**The issue:** Bill of Sale § 2.6 provides that if assignment of an "Assigned Contract" requires third-party consent that has not been obtained, the Bill of Sale "does not constitute an assignment of that Contract or right." Until consent is obtained, Transferor holds the contract for Transferee's benefit. Schedule E (Required Consents) is not attached to the execution copy in our file.

SPA § 4.9(d) represents that "the chain of title for each Library Title is complete and unbroken."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If consents for material distribution or acquisition agreements have not been obtained, the Company may not have validly assumed those contracts. This undermines the chain-of-title representation and could expose the Company to claims from counterparties that their contracts were wrongfully assigned.

**Recommended fix:** Before closing, obtain and review the final Schedule E. Confirm that all consents for Material Contracts (as defined in SPA § 4.7(a)) have been obtained. For any consent not yet obtained, require Seller to deliver it as a closing condition (it is already listed under SPA § 8.2(e) as a condition to Buyer's obligation to close, but the specific consents should be verified).

## III. General Representation Survival Period Is Only Nine Months

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(a); SPA § 1.1 ("Note Maturity Date"); Seller Note § 2.

**The issue:** General Representations survive until "the earlier of (i) eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date and (ii) the Note Maturity Date" (SPA § 9.1(a)). The Note Maturity Date is nine months after the Issue Date (Seller Note § 2), i.e., January 1, 2027. Therefore, General Representations—including the Library Titles representation in § 4.9, the Financial Statements representation in § 4.5, the Material Contracts representation in § 4.7, and the Tax representation cross-reference—survive only until January 1, 2027.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt has only a nine-month window to discover and assert claims for breaches of General Representations. Given the digital rights gap identified in Issue I above, if the problem is not discovered and a Claim Notice delivered before January 1, 2027, Subirt loses its indemnification remedy for that breach (SPA § 9.1(d)). This is a short period for a company with 41 titles, multiple revenue streams, and ongoing transition services.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate to extend the survival period for General Representations to the full 18 months (deleting the "Note Maturity Date" alternative), or at minimum extend it to 12 months. If Seller resists, ensure that the digital rights issue (Issue I) is resolved before closing so it does not become a post-closing indemnification claim subject to the short survival period.

## IV. Seller Note Change-of-Control Default Restricts Subirt's Flexibility

**Where:** Seller Note § 8.1(f).

**The issue:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells, transfers or otherwise disposes of a majority of the outstanding capital stock of the Company, or the Company sells all or substantially all of its assets, in either case to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** During the nine-month life of the Note (April 1, 2026 – January 1, 2027), Subirt cannot sell Nightjar or substantially all of its assets to a non-Affiliate without triggering acceleration of the entire $6 million Note plus default interest (2% above the 8% rate, per Seller Note § 3.3). This constrains Subirt's strategic flexibility in the near term, including any bolt-on acquisitions that might involve a reverse merger or asset purchase structure.

**Recommended fix:** If Subirt anticipates any near-term restructuring of Nightjar, negotiate a carve-out in Seller Note § 8.1(f) for transactions to Affiliates of Maker or for transactions in which Maker receives consideration no less favorable than the then-outstanding Obligations. Alternatively, plan to prepay the Note (permitted without penalty under Seller Note § 5.3) before executing any such transaction.

## V. Seller Note Cross-Default with Senior Debt

**Where:** Seller Note § 8.1(e); Funds Flow § 4.1.

**The issue:** An Event of Default on the Seller Note occurs if "Senior Debt in an aggregate principal amount exceeding $2,500,000 is accelerated as a result of a default by Maker or the Company, and the acceleration is not rescinded within thirty (30) days." The Company currently has a $2.3 million revolving credit facility with First Meridian Bank (Funds Flow § 4.1). If that facility is increased or if additional Senior Debt is incurred post-closing, a default on that debt above $2.5 million would cross-default the Seller Note.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt may want to leverage the Company's balance sheet post-closing (e.g., to fund integration costs or new acquisitions). If it incurs Senior Debt above $2.5 million and then experiences a technical default, Seller could accelerate the Note. This creates a compounding liquidity risk.

**Recommended fix:** Be mindful of the $2.5 million threshold when planning post-closing financing. If Subirt expects to incur Senior Debt above that level, negotiate to raise the threshold or delete § 8.1(e). At minimum, calendar the 30-day cure period so that any technical default on Senior Debt is cured before it cross-defaults the Note.

## VI. Non-Recourse Limitation on Indemnification Recovery

**Where:** SPA § 11.13.

**The issue:** "All claims arising out of this Agreement may be made only against the Persons expressly named as Parties. No past, present or future partner, member, manager, director, officer, employee or Affiliate of any Party has any liability for any obligation of that Party under this Agreement, except in the case of fraud."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt's indemnification claims can be made only against Fural Media Partners, L.P. as an entity. Subirt cannot pursue Daniel Okafor or any GP personally (absent fraud). If the LP has distributed its assets to limited partners before Subirt's claim is resolved, recovery may be limited to whatever remains in the entity. This is particularly relevant given the short survival period (Issue III) and the potential size of a digital rights claim (Issue I).

**Recommended fix:** This is a standard provision and difficult to change. Mitigate by (a) resolving the digital rights issue pre-closing (Issue I); (b) considering whether to require a parent guarantee or escrow for a portion of the purchase price; and (c) ensuring the Claim Notice process is executed promptly if any issue arises within the survival period.

## VII. TSA Service Dependencies Limit Migration Flexibility

**Where:** TSA Schedule 1 ("Dependencies"); TSA § 6.2.

**The issue:** The Service Schedule states: "Accounting Services depend on Collection Services and Systems Access. Recipient's termination of Collection Services or Systems Access before the termination of Accounting Services may limit the scope of Accounting Services that Provider can perform."

While Recipient may terminate any Service for convenience on 30 days' notice (TSA § 6.2), terminating Collection Services or Systems Access first would degrade Accounting Services.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt's integration plan (Board Consent § 9.1) calls for migrating all Services before the end of the six-month initial term. If Subirt builds out its own collection infrastructure first but still needs participation/residual statement preparation, it may find the Accounting Services degraded. The migration sequence matters.

**Recommended fix:** Plan the migration in the correct dependency order: maintain Collection Services and Systems Access until Accounting Services are fully internalized, then terminate Collection Services and Systems Access. Alternatively, negotiate with Seller to decouple the dependency or to provide a transition period where Accounting Services continue at full scope even after Collection Services are terminated.

## VIII. Governing Law Inconsistency Across Documents

**Where:** SPA § 11.8 (New York); Seller Note § 10.1 (Delaware); Bill of Sale § 9.6 (New York); TSA § 11.6 (New York).

**The issue:** The Seller Note is governed by Delaware law and subject to exclusive jurisdiction of the Delaware Court of Chancery, while the SPA, Bill of Sale, and TSA are all governed by New York law with New York County jurisdiction. SPA § 11.12 acknowledges this: "the governing law provision of each Ancillary Agreement governs that Ancillary Agreement."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If a dispute arises that implicates both the SPA (e.g., an indemnification claim) and the Seller Note (e.g., a set-off under SPA § 9.8 / Note § 4), Subirt may face proceedings in two different jurisdictions under two different bodies of law. Delaware law may interpret the Note's subordination, default, or set-off provisions differently than New York law would interpret the SPA's indemnification provisions.

**Recommended fix:** This is a structural issue that is difficult to change at this stage. Be aware of the split when advising on dispute strategy. Ensure that the set-off mechanics in SPA § 9.8 and Note § 4 are drafted to operate consistently regardless of which court interprets them (they currently track each other closely, which helps).

## X. Press Release "More Than a Decade" Statement

**Where:** Press Release § 3.2.

**The issue:** The release states: "Over more than a decade, Nightjar has built a reputation for identifying distinctive, filmmaker-driven projects..."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If Nightjar Releasing, Inc. was not incorporated or did not begin operations more than ten years before April 1, 2026 (i.e., before 2016), this statement is inaccurate. While a press release is not a contractual representation, an inaccurate public statement about the target could create issues with counterparties, in any subsequent securities filing, or in a dispute where the release is introduced as evidence of the parties' understanding.

**Recommended fix:** Verify Nightjar's formation date and first release. If the company is younger than ten years, revise the language to "Over several years" or state the actual period. This should be corrected before the release is finalized for the Closing Date.

## XI. Funds Flow: Working Capital Shortfall Treatment

**Where:** Funds Flow § 3.1 (Line E), § 6.4; SPA § 2.2(a)(v).

**The issue:** The Estimated Closing Working Capital is $1,097,400, which is $52,600 below the $1,150,000 Target. This shortfall reduces the Cash Purchase Price by $52,600. The Funds Flow correctly reflects this (Line E: ($52,600.00)), and § 6.4 confirms it "is not separately paid to any person."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** This is not an error, but it is worth confirming that the $52,600 shortfall is driven by items Subirt can verify in the Closing Statement. If the shortfall is due to Seller's pre-closing operations (e.g., unpaid payables that should have been accrued), Subirt should ensure those items are properly captured in the Closing Statement within the 90-day window (SPA § 2.3(b)). The post-closing true-up under SPA § 2.3(d) could result in additional payment by Seller to Buyer if the final Working Capital is even lower than estimated.

**Recommended fix:** No immediate fix required. Ensure the finance team is prepared to build the Closing Statement within 90 days and to scrutinize the working capital components, particularly current liabilities that may have been understated in the estimate.

## XII. SPA § 6.9 Post-Closing Receipts: Trust Obligation on Seller

**Where:** SPA § 6.9(b); TSA § 2.4; Funds Flow § 10.4.

**The issue:** Seller is obligated to hold Post-Closing Receipts in trust and remit them within five Business Days. The TSA assigns the operational mechanics to Provider (Seller) under § 2.4. The Funds Flow confirms that on the Closing Date, the Company will designate the receiving account (the Nightjar operating account at Westbrook Federal Bank, per Schedule 1, Wire 6).

**Why it matters for Subirt:** During the six-month TSA period, Seller continues to receive payments into Collection Accounts it controls. If Seller fails to remit, Subirt's remedies are (a) indemnification under SPA § 9.2(d) (which is not subject to the cap or deductible per SPA § 9.5(c)(ii)), (b) specific performance under TSA § 11.8, and (c) set-off against the Seller Note under SPA § 9.8. The trust obligation and the set-off right are important protections, but they are only as good as Seller's solvency.

**Recommended fix:** Monitor the monthly reconciliations required under TSA § 2.4(c) closely. If any discrepancy arises, deliver a Claim Notice promptly (to preserve the set-off right under Note § 4.2) and consider accelerating payor redirection under TSA § 2.5 to reduce the volume of funds flowing through Seller's accounts.

## XIII. SPA § 4.10(d): No Collective Bargaining Agreement, but Assumption Agreements Exist

**Where:** SPA § 4.10(d); Bill of Sale § 4.1(b) and § 4.2(b).

**The issue:** SPA § 4.10(d) states: "The Company is not a signatory to any collective bargaining agreement. Section 4.10 of the Disclosure Schedule identifies each assumption agreement under which the Company has assumed Residuals obligations in respect of any Library Title."

The Bill of Sale § 4.1(b) has the Company assume "Participations and Residuals payable in respect of revenue derived from the exercise of the Distribution Rights after the Effective Date." Section 4.2(b) excludes "all Participations and Residuals payable in respect of revenue received by or credited to Transferor before the Effective Date."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** The Company has assumed post-Effective Date Residuals obligations via the Bill of Sale, and has separate assumption agreements (per the Disclosure Schedule). If any of those assumption agreements reference rights broader than what the Bill of Sale conveyed (e.g., digital residuals), there could be a mismatch. Additionally, SPA § 9.2(e) provides that Seller indemnifies for "any Participations or Residuals relating to the exploitation of any Library Title before the Closing, to the extent not accrued in Closing Working Capital." Subirt should confirm that all pre-closing Residuals are properly accrued in the working capital calculation.

**Recommended fix:** Review the assumption agreements identified in Section 4.10 of the Disclosure Schedule to confirm they are consistent with the Bill of Sale's scope. Verify that pre-closing Residuals are accrued in the Estimated Closing Statement. If any assumption agreement covers digital rights not conveyed by the Bill of Sale, flag as part of Issue I.
