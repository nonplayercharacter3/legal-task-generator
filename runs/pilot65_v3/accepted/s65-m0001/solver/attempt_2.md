## 1. CRITICAL: Distribution Rights Definition Mismatch Between SPA and Bill of Sale

**Where:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Distribution Rights") vs. Bill of Sale § 1.2 (definition of "Distribution Rights") and § 2.1 (conveyance).

**Issue:** The SPA defines "Distribution Rights" broadly to include "all media, formats and methods of delivery now known or later devised, including theatrical, non-theatrical, physical home video, television (free, pay and basic), and all forms of on-demand and internet-delivered exhibition, including subscription, advertising-supported and transactional video-on-demand" (SPA § 1.1). The SPA further represents in § 4.9(b) that "Under the Rights Bill of Sale, Rights Holdings conveyed to the Company all of the Distribution Rights in each Library Title held by Rights Holdings" and that the Company "owns or holds... the Distribution Rights in each Library Title for the territories and terms set forth on Schedule 4.9, free and clear of all Encumbrances."

However, the Bill of Sale defines "Distribution Rights" in § 1.2 as only "the theatrical, non-theatrical and physical home-video rights in and to that Picture, throughout the Territory and for the Term." Section 2.1 conveys only "the theatrical, non-theatrical and physical home-video rights." The Bill of Sale does not convey any digital, streaming, VOD, or other non-theatrical/non-physical-home-video rights.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt is paying $28.5 million for a library whose value in 2026 is predominantly in digital and streaming exploitation. If the Company's rights derive solely from the Bill of Sale, the Company may not own the digital/streaming rights that the SPA represents it owns. This would mean:
- The SPA § 4.9(b) representation is inaccurate;
- A substantial portion of the library's revenue-generating rights was never conveyed to the Company;
- Subirt's acquisition of those rights is defective.

**Recommended fix:** Before Closing, require Seller to confirm in writing the source of the Company's digital/streaming rights. If they derive from the Bill of Sale, the Bill of Sale must be amended to include the broader definition, or a supplemental assignment must be executed. If they derive from other agreements (e.g., the Acquisition Agreements listed on Bill of Sale Schedule A), require production and review of those agreements to confirm they grant digital rights to the Company. Add a specific representation in the SPA (or a bring-down certificate) that the Company owns all rights described in the SPA's definition of "Distribution Rights," not just those conveyed by the Bill of Sale.

## 2. HIGH: General Representation Survival Period Limited to Nine Months

**Where:** SPA § 9.1(a) (survival of General Representations); SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Note Maturity Date"); Seller Note § 2 (Maturity Date = 9 months after Issue Date).

**Issue:** Section 9.1(a) provides that General Representations survive "until the earlier of (i) eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date and (ii) the Note Maturity Date." The Note Maturity Date is 9 months after the Closing Date (Seller Note § 2). Therefore, General Representations survive for only **9 months** after Closing (i.e., until approximately January 1, 2027).

All representations other than the Fundamental Representations (§§ 4.1–4.3, 4.15, 5.1–5.2) and Tax Representations (§ 4.12) are General Representations. This includes, among others:
- § 4.4 (No Conflicts; Consents)
- § 4.5 (Financial Statements)
- § 4.7 (Material Contracts)
- § 4.8 (Litigation)
- § 4.9 (Library Titles; Distribution Rights)
- § 4.10 (Participations and Residuals)
- § 4.11 (Intellectual Property)
- § 4.13 (Employees and Benefit Plans)
- § 4.14 (Compliance with Laws)
- § 4.16 (Affiliate Arrangements)
- § 4.17 (Bank Accounts; Collection Accounts)

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Nine months is an extremely short window. The Closing Statement is due 90 days after Closing (§ 2.3(b)), and the dispute process (§ 2.3(c)) can consume an additional 90–120 days. If the working capital adjustment is not finalized until September or October 2026, Subirt would have only 3–4 months remaining to investigate and assert claims for other General Representation breaches. Many issues (contract defaults, IP infringements, undisclosed liabilities, participation/residual shortfalls) may not surface within 9 months.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate to decouple the survival period from the Note Maturity Date. A standard 18-month survival for General Representations is market. If Seller insists on the shorter period as a condition of the note structure, consider: (a) extending the Note Maturity Date to 18 months; (b) carving out specific representations (e.g., § 4.9 Distribution Rights, § 4.10 Participations and Residuals, § 4.11 IP) from the shortened survival period; or (c) adding a "discovery period" that tolls the survival deadline for claims not reasonably discoverable within the initial period.

## 3. HIGH: Non-Recourse Provision Limits Recovery to Seller Entity

**Where:** SPA § 11.13 (Non-Recourse).

**Issue:** Section 11.13 provides that "All claims arising out of this Agreement may be made only against the Persons expressly named as Parties. No past, present or future partner, member, manager, director, officer, employee or Affiliate of any Party has any liability for any obligation of that Party under this Agreement, except in the case of fraud."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt's indemnification rights under Article IX can be enforced only against Fural Media Partners, L.P. as an entity. Subirt cannot pursue:
- Daniel Okafor (Managing Partner) or any other individual;
- Fural Media GP, LLC (the general partner);
- Rights Holdings or any other Fural affiliate;
- Any limited partner of Fural.

If Fural Media Partners, L.P. distributes the sale proceeds to its partners or otherwise becomes asset-light post-Closing, Subirt's indemnification rights may be economically worthless. The Seller Cap (§ 9.5(a)) is $3,420,000 (12% of Base Purchase Price), and the non-recourse provision means that cap can only be collected from the LP entity.

**Recommended fix:** This is a structural issue that is difficult to change at this stage (the SPA is signed). However:
- Confirm that Fural Media Partners, L.P. will not distribute the sale proceeds (including Note payments) to its partners before the survival period expires. Consider requesting a covenant or side letter to that effect.
- Ensure the set-off right (SPA § 9.8; Note § 4) is preserved as a practical mechanism to secure indemnification claims against the Note balance.
- For future transactions, negotiate to carve out the general partner and/or key individuals from the non-recourse provision, or require a parent guarantee.

## 4. HIGH: Buyer's Uncapped Indemnification for Post-Closing Claims Against Seller

**Where:** SPA § 9.3(b) (Buyer's indemnification); § 9.5(e) (exclusion of limitations for Buyer).

**Issue:** Section 9.3(b) requires Buyer to indemnify Seller Indemnitees for "the ownership or operation of the Company or the exploitation of the Library Titles from and after the Closing, including any claim made against any Seller Indemnitee by any licensor, licensee, participant, guild, union or other third party in respect of any Library Title." Section 9.5(e) provides that "None of the limitations set forth in this Section 9.5 shall apply to Buyer's obligations under Section 9.3."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Subirt has **uncapped** indemnification exposure for any post-Closing third-party claim against Seller relating to any Library Title. This includes claims by filmmakers, guilds (e.g., SAG-AFTRA, DGA), licensors, and licensees. The breadth of "any claim... in respect of any Library Title" is not limited to claims arising from Subirt's own operations or negligence. If a guild audit identifies underpaid residuals from the post-Closing period and the guild asserts a claim against Fural (as former owner/affiliate), Subirt would be obligated to indemnify without limit.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate to:
- Limit § 9.3(b) to claims "arising from or related to" Subirt's post-Closing ownership or operation (i.e., claims caused by Subirt's actions or inactions);
- Exclude claims that are in substance pre-Closing in nature (e.g., residual underpayments attributable to the pre-Closing period, which should be covered by § 9.2(e));
- Apply at least the Seller Cap to Buyer's obligations under § 9.3(b), or a separate reasonable cap.

## 5. MEDIUM: Governing Law Conflict Between SPA (New York) and Seller Note (Delaware)

**Where:** SPA § 11.8 (New York law; New York courts); Seller Note § 10.1 (Delaware law; Delaware Court of Chancery).

**Issue:** The SPA and the Seller Note are governed by different state laws and subject to different exclusive jurisdictions. Both documents address set-off rights (SPA § 9.8 and Note § 4), and both are relevant to Subirt's ability to withhold or offset Note payments for indemnification claims.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If Subirt exercises a set-off under SPA § 9.8 and Seller challenges it under the Note, the dispute could involve:
- A New York action under the SPA (where Subirt would assert the set-off right);
- A Delaware action under the Note (where Seller would assert that the set-off constitutes a payment default).

While SPA § 11.12 provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Agreements in case of conflict, this does not resolve the choice-of-law or jurisdictional question. The practical effect is increased litigation cost and complexity for Subirt.

**Recommended fix:** Amend the Note to conform its governing law and jurisdiction to the SPA (New York). If Seller resists, at minimum add a provision to the Note stating that any dispute regarding the validity or effect of a set-off under § 4 shall be governed by and determined under the SPA.

## 6. MEDIUM: Note Reporting Obligations Exceed TSA Systems Access Term

**Where:** Seller Note § 6.1 (monthly reports for full Note term); TSA § 6.1 (initial Service Term of 6 months) and Schedule 1 (Systems Access: 6 months); TSA § 6.3 (one extension of up to 3 months).

**Issue:** The Note requires Maker (Subirt) to deliver monthly reports—including title-by-title performance, box-office reports, and streaming/digital revenue reports—within 30 days after each month-end for the full 9-month Note term (Note § 6.1). The TSA provides Systems Access (to Provider's rights-management, royalty-accounting and avails systems) for only 6 months, extendable once by up to 3 months (TSA § 6.3, Schedule 1).

If Subirt relies on Provider's systems to generate the Note reports (likely during the transition period), and the TSA is not extended, Subirt may lack the data infrastructure to comply with Note § 6.1 for months 7–9. Failure to deliver reports for 30 days after notice constitutes an Event of Default (Note § 8.1(c)), which could trigger acceleration of the $6 million Note.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** An inadvertent technical default on the Note would give Seller the right to accelerate the full $6 million principal plus interest, creating a significant liquidity event for Subirt.

**Recommended fix:**
- Calendar the TSA extension deadline (notice required at least 30 days before the 6-month term ends, i.e., by September 1, 2026) and ensure the extension is exercised if systems access is still needed for Note reporting.
- Alternatively, negotiate with Seller to confirm that Note § 6.1 reporting obligations are satisfied by good-faith efforts during the transition period, or that the 30-day cure period in § 8.1(c) is sufficient to address any gap.
- Ensure Subirt's own systems are capable of generating the required reports by month 7 at the latest.

## 7. MEDIUM: Note Event of Default for Change of Control / Asset Sale

**Where:** Seller Note § 8.1(f).

**Issue:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells, transfers or otherwise disposes of a majority of the outstanding capital stock of the Company [Nightjar], or the Company sells all or substantially all of its assets, in either case to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker."

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If Subirt later wishes to:
- Merge Nightjar into Subirt or another affiliate (potentially triggering the "majority of stock" prong if structured as a sale);
- Sell Nightjar as part of a portfolio restructuring;
- Have Nightjar sell its library or substantially all assets to a third party;

...it would trigger an Event of Default on the $6 million Note, giving Seller the right to accelerate. The "Affiliate of Maker" carve-out provides some flexibility (Subirt can move Nightjar within its own corporate structure), but any sale to an outside party would be problematic.

**Recommended fix:** Before Closing, confirm with Seller that the Affiliate carve-out is sufficient for Subirt's integration plans. If Subirt anticipates any restructuring within the 9-month Note term, negotiate a waiver or amendment to § 8.1(f) at this time. At minimum, ensure the Board is aware of this constraint when planning post-Closing integration.

## 8. MEDIUM: TSA Liability Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** TSA § 9.1 (liability cap = total Service Fees paid in preceding 6 months = $255,000).

**Issue:** Provider's (Seller's) aggregate liability under the TSA is capped at $255,000. If Provider mismanages Post-Closing Receipts, makes material errors in distribution or participation statements, or fails to perform Delivery Services, Subirt's recovery under the TSA is limited to this amount.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** The library generates revenue from multiple platforms and territories. A single month's collection errors could involve amounts well in excess of $255,000. However, SPA § 9.2(d) provides a separate indemnification for "any failure by Seller or any of its Affiliates to hold in trust or remit any Post-Closing Receipts in accordance with Section 6.9," which is not subject to the Seller Cap (SPA § 9.5(c)(ii)). This provides a backstop for Post-Closing Receipts specifically, but not for other TSA service failures (e.g., erroneous participation statements, delivery failures).

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm that the SPA § 9.2(d) indemnification covers all Post-Closing Receipts failures regardless of whether they arise under the TSA or the SPA.
- For non-Post-Closing-Receipts service failures, consider negotiating a higher TSA cap (e.g., 2x or 3x annual fees) or a super-cap for gross negligence/willful misconduct (which is already carved out of the cap under § 9.1).
- Ensure the specific performance remedy in TSA § 11.8 is understood as the primary protection for service failures.

## 9. LOW: Board Consent – General Counsel Name Missing

**Where:** Buyer Board Consent, Annex 1 (Authorized Officers table).

**Issue:** The table identifying Authorized Officers lists the General Counsel and Secretary as "[Name]" – a blank. The Consent authorizes the General Counsel to perform numerous functions (confirm Seller deliverables, deliver Claim Notices, exercise set-off rights, record copyright assignments, etc.).

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If the General Counsel's identity is not recorded in the board consent, there is a potential challenge to whether that individual was properly authorized to act. This could affect the validity of closing certificates, indemnification claims, or other actions taken in the General Counsel's name.

**Recommended fix:** Complete Annex 1 with the General Counsel's name before the Consent is relied upon for Closing deliverables. If the General Counsel has not yet been hired or appointed, either (a) appoint and record the name, or (b) amend the Consent to authorize a specific individual by name and title.

## 10. LOW: Funds Flow Memorandum – Confusing Account Reference for Post-Closing Receipts

**Where:** Funds Flow Memorandum § 10.4; Schedule 1, Wire 6.

**Issue:** Section 10.4 designates the account for Post-Closing Receipts as "the Company operating account identified as Wire 6's receiving bank in Schedule 1, under the account name 'Nightjar Releasing, Inc. – Operating.'" Wire 6 is the **payroll** account at Westbrook Federal Bank (account ending -1173). The section appears to be using Wire 6 only to identify the bank (Westbrook Federal Bank), but the drafting is ambiguous. A reader could reasonably conclude that Post-Closing Receipts should be sent to the payroll account (Wire 6) rather than a separate operating account at the same bank.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If Post-Closing Receipts are inadvertently directed to the payroll account, there could be operational confusion, commingling issues, or delays in identifying funds. More importantly, if Seller relies on this designation and remits to the wrong account, disputes could arise about whether the remittance obligation under SPA § 6.9 was satisfied.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 10.4 to explicitly state the full account details for the Post-Closing Receipts account (bank name, ABA, account number, account name) rather than cross-referencing Wire 6. Confirm with the Company's controller that the designated operating account is correct and distinct from the payroll account.

## 11. LOW: Seller Note Interest Payment Timing – Minimal Final Period

**Where:** Seller Note § 3.2 (quarterly interest on last Business Day of June, September, December); § 2 (Maturity Date = 9 months after Issue Date = January 1, 2027).

**Issue:** The Note matures on January 1, 2027. The last quarterly interest payment date is December 31, 2026 (last Business Day of December). This leaves only one day (December 31 to January 1) between the final interest payment and maturity. While not a substantive problem, it creates an administrative oddity: the final wire would need to carry only 1 day of interest (or none, if December 31 is the last Business Day and January 1 is a holiday).

**Why it matters for Subirt:** Minimal. The Treasurer should be aware that the final payment on January 1, 2027 will be essentially principal-only (plus at most 1 day of interest). If January 1, 2027 is not a Business Day, the Maturity Date shifts to the next Business Day (Note § 2), and interest continues to accrue.

**Recommended fix:** No action required. Calendar the final payment date and confirm whether January 1, 2027 is a Business Day. If not, the Maturity Date shifts and the final payment includes additional interest.

## 12. LOW: Press Release – Public Commitment to Contact Rights Holders

**Where:** Press Release § 3.6; § 7.5(a).

**Issue:** The approved press release states: "Subirt and Nightjar intend to contact the producers, sales agents and other rights partners behind each Library Title in the coming weeks. They will introduce the combined team and discuss plans for each film." The spokesperson guidance (§ 7.5(a)) repeats this commitment.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** This creates a public expectation that Subirt will contact all rights holders "in the coming weeks." If Subirt does not do so promptly (e.g., due to internal resource constraints or strategic decisions about which titles to prioritize), it could:
- Damage relationships with filmmakers and sales agents;
- Create the appearance of bad faith;
- Potentially trigger change-of-control notification obligations under some agreements (though the release states existing agreements are "not affected").

**Recommended fix:** Ensure the communications team and business development team have a plan and timeline for contacting rights holders before the release is issued. If the "coming weeks" timeline is not realistic, consider modifying the language to "in the coming weeks or months" before finalizing the release.

## 13. LOW: SPA § 4.4 Representation – No Governmental Consents Required

**Where:** SPA § 4.4 (second sentence): "No consent, approval or filing with any Governmental Authority is required in connection with the transactions contemplated by this Agreement."

**Issue:** This is a flat representation by Seller that no governmental consent or filing is required. For a $28.5 million stock purchase, this could implicate:
- HSR Act (if the transaction meets the size-of-transaction and size-of-person thresholds);
- State-level filings or approvals;
- Foreign investment review (if any foreign element exists).

The representation is a General Representation (not Fundamental), so it is subject to the 9-month survival period, the $213,750 deductible, and the $3,420,000 cap.

**Why it matters for Subirt:** If a required filing was not made, the transaction could be subject to challenge or penalty. However, given the 9-month survival period, Subirt would need to discover and assert the claim quickly. The cap of $3.42 million may not cover potential HSR penalties (which can be significant).

**Recommended fix:** Confirm through independent regulatory analysis (not relying solely on Seller's representation) that no HSR or other filing is required. If there is any doubt, consider making the closing condition in § 8.2(e) (consents) explicitly cover regulatory filings, or add a specific representation about HSR compliance.
