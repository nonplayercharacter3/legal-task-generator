## I. Issues That Could Affect Closing or the Value of the Purchased Assets

### Issue 1: The Wickline Toolkit License Cannot Be Assigned Without Okoro's Consent, and Schedule 4.4 Falsely States No Consents Are Required

**Where:**
- Asset Purchase Agreement (APA), Schedule 2.1(c) (lists the Key Employee Agreement as an Assigned Contract); APA § 6.4 (requires assignment of the Key Employee Agreement at Closing); APA Schedule 4.4 (states "None. No third-party consents are required"); APA § 4.4 (Consents representation); APA § 7.2(c) (closing condition tied to Schedule 4.4).
- Key Employee Agreement (KEA) § 12.4 (anti-assignment clause); KEA § 7.2(a)–(b) (Okoro retains ownership of the Wickline Toolkit; grants Glesar a non-exclusive license); KEA § 1.1(d) (definition of "Wickline Toolkit").

**Why it matters:**
The Wickline Toolkit is a proprietary engine toolkit that Okoro developed before her employment and retains ownership of. Glesar's right to use it is a non-exclusive license under KEA § 7.2(b). KEA § 12.4 provides that Glesar "may not assign or transfer this Agreement, or any of its rights under it, including the license granted in Section 7.2, without the prior written consent of Employee," and that "[a]ny purported assignment or transfer in violation of this Section 12.4 is void, and upon any such purported assignment or transfer the license granted in Section 7.2 terminates."

The Emberfall Game Code (a core Purchased Asset) almost certainly incorporates the Wickline Toolkit or Company Modifications built on it. If the assignment of the KEA at Closing occurs without Okoro's consent:
1. The assignment is void.
2. The Wickline license terminates.
3. Dilur may lose the right to use, modify, or distribute the Wickline Toolkit as embodied in the Emberfall Game Code.

This would materially impair the value of the Game Code and potentially the entire franchise. Additionally, Schedule 4.4's statement that no consents are required makes Seller's representation in APA § 4.4 inaccurate as of the Signing Date, and it means the closing condition in § 7.2(c) is vacuously satisfied (no consents listed = no consents to obtain), providing no protection to Dilur.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Okoro's written consent to the assignment of the KEA and the Wickline license before Closing. Make this a condition to Dilur's obligation to close (amend Schedule 4.4 to list the consent and add it to § 7.2(c)).
- Consider negotiating a direct, irrevocable license from Okoro to Dilur (or an assignment of the Wickline Toolkit itself) as a separate deliverable at Closing, so that Dilur's right to use Wickline does not depend on the continued employment relationship.
- Amend Schedule 4.4 to identify Okoro's consent as a required third-party consent.

**Proposed language for amended Schedule 4.4:**
> 1. Consent of Tamsin Okoro to the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement and the license granted therein under Section 12.4 of that Agreement, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

**Proposed additional closing condition (APA § 7.2(f)):**
> (f) Buyer shall have received the written consent of Tamsin Okoro to the assignment of the Key Employee Agreement and the transfer of the license under Section 7.2 thereof, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

### Issue 2: IP Assignment Agreement § 5.2 Imposes an Uncapped Indemnity on Dilur for All IP-Related Losses, Including Pre-Closing Claims

**Where:**
- IP Assignment Agreement (IPAA) § 5.2.
- APA § 2.4(d) (Excluded Liabilities: pre-Closing IP infringement retained by Seller); APA § 2.4(e) (Excluded Liabilities: pre-Closing facts/circumstances); APA § 9.2(c) (Seller indemnifies for Excluded Liabilities); APA § 11.12 (order of precedence: IPAA controls on Assigned IP matters).

**Why it matters:**
IPAA § 5.2 provides: "Assignee shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless the Assignor Indemnitees from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to the Assigned IP or the ownership, use, licensing, distribution or other exploitation of the Assigned IP, **whether arising before or after the Effective Date**. Assignee's obligations under this Section 5.2 are **not subject to any limitation in the Purchase Agreement**."

This means Dilur would indemnify Glesar for the Composer Claim (which relates to the Emberfall Soundtrack, an item of Assigned IP), even though the claim arises from pre-Closing exploitation. It directly contradicts APA § 2.4(d), which assigns pre-Closing IP infringement liabilities to Seller as Excluded Liabilities. Because APA § 11.12 gives the IPAA precedence on matters concerning Assigned IP, the IPAA's broad indemnity would control over the APA's Excluded Liabilities framework.

The practical effect: Glesar could pass the Composer Claim (and any other pre-Closing IP claim) to Dilur, and Dilur would have no cap on its exposure.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend IPAA § 5.2 to carve out pre-Closing matters and to conform to the APA's Excluded Liabilities structure.

**Proposed language:**
> **5.2 Indemnification by Assignee.** From and after the Effective Date, Assignee shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless the Assignor Indemnitees from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to the ownership, use, licensing, distribution or other exploitation of the Assigned IP **after the Effective Date**, to the extent such Losses are not Excluded Liabilities under the Purchase Agreement. Assignee's obligations under this Section 5.2 shall be subject to the limitations set forth in Article IX of the Purchase Agreement.

### Issue 3: IP Assignment Agreement § 6.3 Reduces the Cap on IP Representation Breaches from the Full Purchase Price to the General Cap

**Where:**
- IPAA § 6.3.
- APA § 9.5(c) (Fundamental Representations, including § 4.9, excluded from General Cap; capped at full Purchase Price of $48,500,000); APA § 9.5(a) (General Cap = 15% of Purchase Price = $7,275,000); APA § 11.12 (IPAA controls on Assigned IP matters).

**Why it matters:**
APA § 9.5(c) designates Section 4.9 (Intellectual Property) as a Fundamental Representation, excluding it from the General Cap and the basket, with a cap equal to the full Purchase Price ($48.5M). This is a critical protection for Dilur given that the IP is the primary asset being acquired.

IPAA § 6.3 provides: "Assignor's liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement or Section 3 of this Agreement **shall be subject to the General Cap**." The General Cap is $7,275,000 (15% of Purchase Price).

Because APA § 11.12 states that the IPAA controls on matters concerning Assigned IP, and Section 4.9 is specifically about the Assigned IP, the IPAA's lower cap would govern. This reduces Dilur's maximum recovery for IP representation breaches by a factor of roughly 6.7×.

The Board Consent (Recital D) correctly identifies Section 4.9 as a Fundamental Representation under the APA, but does not appear to address the IPAA override.

**Recommended fix:**
Amend IPAA § 6.3 to conform to the APA's Fundamental Representation treatment, or amend APA § 11.12 to clarify that the APA's cap structure controls.

**Proposed language for IPAA § 6.3:**
> **6.3 Liability for Intellectual Property Matters.** Assignor's liability for any inaccuracy in or breach of Section 4.9 of the Purchase Agreement or Section 3 of this Agreement shall be subject to the limitations set forth in Section 9.5 of the Purchase Agreement, including the treatment of such representations as Fundamental Representations under Section 9.5(c) thereof.

### Issue 4: The Composer Claim – Section 6.7 Deemed Cure May Have Already Operated

**Where:**
- APA § 6.7 (Schedule Update procedure; 10-Business-Day termination window; deemed cure); APA § 10.1(d) (Buyer termination right tied to § 6.7); APA § 7.2(a) (closing condition: reps true and correct); APA § 9.2(a) (indemnification for rep breaches); APA § 9.2(c) (indemnification for Excluded Liabilities).
- Board Consent, Recitals C–F; Resolutions 2.1–2.2.

**Why it matters:**
The September Schedule Update (received September 14, 2026) discloses the Composer Claim – a pending copyright infringement and royalty claim against the Emberfall Soundtrack. This claim directly impugns:
- APA § 4.7 (Litigation – no pending or threatened claims);
- APA § 4.9(c) (Non-infringement);
- APA § 4.9(d) (No royalty obligations – Schedule 4.9(d) states "None").

Section 6.7 provides that if Buyer does not terminate within ten (10) Business Days after receipt of the Schedule Update, "the Schedule Update shall be deemed to have cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty disclosed in it, **for purposes of Article IX** and **for purposes of determining whether the conditions in Article VII have been satisfied**."

Counting 10 Business Days from September 14, 2026 (a Monday): September 15, 16, 17, 18, 22, 23, 24, 25, 29, 30. The deadline was **September 30, 2026**. The Board Consent is dated **October 6, 2026** – six calendar days (four Business Days) after the deadline.

If the deemed-cure provision has operated:
1. Dilur has lost its right to terminate under § 10.1(d).
2. For purposes of Article IX, the inaccuracy in the representations is deemed cured – potentially eliminating Dilur's indemnification claim under § 9.2(a) for the Composer Claim.
3. For purposes of Article VII, the Composer Claim does not prevent satisfaction of the closing conditions.

**However**, the Composer Claim should still be an Excluded Liability under APA § 2.4(d) (pre-Closing IP infringement) and § 2.4(e) (liabilities arising from pre-Closing facts). Indemnification for Excluded Liabilities is under § 9.2(c), which is a separate ground from § 9.2(a) (rep breaches). The § 6.7 deemed-cure language refers to "any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty" – it does not expressly address Excluded Liabilities, which are not representations.

**The critical risk** is that IPAA § 5.2 (see Issue 2) could override even the Excluded Liabilities protection by making Dilur indemnify Glesar for all IP-related losses.

**Recommended fix:**
- Assert in the Objection Notice (authorized by Board Resolution 2.1) that (a) the Composer Claim constitutes an Excluded Liability under §§ 2.4(d) and 2.4(e), for which Seller retains responsibility and indemnifies under § 9.2(c); and (b) the § 6.7 deemed-cure provision applies only to representation and warranty inaccuracies, not to the allocation of Excluded Liabilities.
- Negotiate a special indemnity or escrow/holdback specifically covering the Composer Claim, as authorized by Board Resolution 2.3. Given the timing issue, a special indemnity is the most practical protection.
- If possible, seek an amendment to the APA that clarifies § 6.7 does not affect the Excluded Liabilities allocation or § 9.2(c).

**Proposed special indemnity language (for amendment or side letter):**
> **Special Indemnity – Composer Claim.** Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in this Agreement or the IP Assignment Agreement, Seller shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless the Buyer Indemnitees from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to the Composer Claim disclosed in the September 14, 2026 Schedule Update, including any royalty, infringement or other liability of Seller in respect of the Emberfall Soundtrack. Seller's obligations under this Special Indemnity shall not be subject to any cap, basket or other limitation in Article IX.

### Issue 5: The Wickline Toolkit Is Not a Purchased Asset, Yet the Game Code Depends on It

**Where:**
- APA § 1.1(c) (definition of "Assigned IP" – limited to IP "owned or purported to be owned by Seller"); APA § 2.1(a) (Purchased Assets include Assigned IP); APA § 4.9(b) (sufficiency of IP representation).
- KEA § 7.2(a) (Okoro retains ownership of Wickline); KEA § 7.2(d) (Company owns only "Company Modifications"); KEA Exhibit A (description of Wickline Toolkit).

**Why it matters:**
The Assigned IP definition captures only IP "owned or purported to be owned by Seller." The Wickline Toolkit is owned by Okoro, not Glesar. It is therefore not a Purchased Asset. Dilur acquires only:
1. The Company Modifications to Wickline (owned by Glesar as Work Product under KEA § 7.1 and § 7.2(d)); and
2. The benefit of the non-exclusive license under KEA § 7.2(b) – which, as noted in Issue 1, terminates if the KEA is assigned without Okoro's consent.

APA § 4.9(b) represents that "the Assigned IP, together with the rights licensed to Seller under the Assigned Contracts, constitutes all Intellectual Property necessary to develop, publish and exploit the Emberfall franchise." If the Wickline license is lost, this representation is false, and the Assigned IP alone is insufficient.

**Recommended fix:**
- In addition to obtaining Okoro's consent (Issue 1), negotiate for one of the following:
  - (a) Assignment of the Wickline Toolkit from Okoro to Dilur (or to Glesar for inclusion in the Purchased Assets), with a new IP assignment as a Closing deliverable; or
  - (b) A direct, irrevocable, perpetual, royalty-free license from Okoro to Dilur (and its Affiliates and successors) covering the Wickline Toolkit, in a separate agreement delivered at Closing.
- If neither is achievable, assess whether the Company Modifications alone are sufficient to operate the Game Code without the underlying Wickline Toolkit. If not, the Purchase Price should be adjusted.

## II. Other Issues Requiring Attention

### Issue 6: APA § 2.7 (Non-Assignable Assets) Provides Inadequate Protection if Okoro Consent Is Not Obtained

**Where:** APA § 2.7.

**Why it matters:**
If the KEA cannot be assigned at Closing (because Okoro has not consented), § 2.7 provides that "Seller shall, until the consent is obtained, provide Buyer the benefits of that Assigned Contract under any reasonable arrangement Buyer requests." This is vague and gives Dilur no enforceable right to the Wickline license or to Okoro's continued service. It also has no time limit.

**Recommended fix:**
If Okoro's consent is obtained before Closing (as recommended in Issue 1), this section becomes moot. If it is not, amend § 2.7 to require Seller to use best efforts to obtain consent within a specified period (e.g., 30 days post-Closing) and to indemnify Dilur for any loss resulting from the failure to obtain it.

### Issue 7: KEA "Good Reason" Trigger Could Be Activated by Post-Closing Integration

**Where:** KEA § 1.1(c) (definition of "Good Reason"); KEA § 5.5 (severance upon Good Reason resignation).

**Why it matters:**
"Good Reason" includes "a material diminution in Employee's title, authority or creative responsibilities for the Emberfall franchise." If Dilur reorganizes the studio post-closing and reduces Okoro's role, she could resign for Good Reason and trigger 12 months' severance ($285,000 + COBRA). More importantly, her departure could destabilize the creative team and the franchise.

**Recommended fix:**
This is less a document issue and more a post-closing integration planning matter. Ensure that Dilur's onboarding plan preserves Okoro's title, authority, and creative responsibilities for at least 12–24 months post-Closing. Consider a side letter with Okoro confirming her post-Closing role as a condition of her consent to the assignment (Issue 1).

---

### Issue 8: APA § 4.9(e) and Schedule 4.9(e) – Open-Source Software Representation May Be Incomplete

**Where:** APA § 4.9(e); Schedule 4.9(e) (states "None").

**Why it matters:**
The representation states that the Emberfall Game Code does not incorporate any open-source software under a copyleft or source-disclosure license. Schedule 4.9(e) confirms "None." However, the Wickline Toolkit (Exhibit A to the KEA) includes "build and asset-cooking utilities" and "editor plug-ins" that were developed by Okoro independently. If any component of Wickline incorporates open-source code, and Wickline is embedded in the Game Code, the representation could be inaccurate. This was not addressed in the Composer Claim Schedule Update.

**Recommended fix:**
Confirm through technical due diligence (code scanning) that neither the Emberfall Game Code nor the Wickline Toolkit components incorporate any open-source software subject to copyleft or source-disclosure obligations. If any is found, require an amended Schedule 4.9(e) and assess the impact.

### Issue 9: APA § 3.2(g) – Delivery of Code Repositories and Credentials

**Where:** APA § 3.2(g); IPAA § 2.4.

**Why it matters:**
Both the APA and IPAA require delivery of "complete copies of the Emberfall Game Code repositories, together with all access credentials" at Closing. If the Wickline Toolkit is embedded in the repositories and Okoro retains the ability to revoke access (e.g., through version control permissions she set up as the original author), there is a risk that the delivered code is incomplete or that Okoro can disrupt access post-Closing.

**Recommended fix:**
As part of obtaining Okoro's consent (Issue 1), require her to confirm in writing that she will (a) grant Dilur full administrative access to all repositories containing the Emberfall Game Code and any Wickline components, and (b) not take any action to restrict or revoke such access.

## III. The Nightjar Localization Services Agreement

**Assessment:** The Nightjar agreement (dated February 2, 2026) is a pre-existing services contract between Dilur and Nightjar Localization Ltd. for translation, localization, and QA services on "certain Dilur titles." It does not involve Glesar, the Emberfall franchise, or any of the Purchased Assets. It is not listed as an Assigned Contract on Schedule 2.1(c) of the APA.

The only tangential connection is operational: post-Closing, Dilur may engage Nightjar to localize the Emberfall titles. In that case, the agreement's IP and indemnity provisions would apply (Dilur owns Deliverables; Dilur indemnifies Nightjar for IP claims arising from Dilur Materials). If Dilur provides Emberfall Soundtrack materials to Nightjar for localization while the Composer Claim is unresolved, Nightjar could theoretically assert its indemnity right under § 8.2. This is a post-Closing operational risk, not a deal defect.

**Conclusion:** The Nightjar agreement does not bear on the issues identified above and requires no modification in connection with this transaction.
