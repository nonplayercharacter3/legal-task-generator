**Re: Letter of Intent: Proposed Acquisition of the Spent Lithium-Ion Battery Recycling Business of Goulound Lithium Reclamation, Inc.**

Ladies and Gentlemen:

This letter of intent (this "**Letter**") sets out the principal terms on which Givand Battery Recovery, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company ("**Buyer**"), proposes to acquire substantially all of the assets of the spent lithium-ion battery recycling business of Goulound Lithium Reclamation, Inc., a Nevada corporation ("**Seller**"). The proposed acquisition is the "**Transaction**."

Except as stated in Section 12, this Letter is a non-binding expression of the Parties' present intentions. It does not create any legally binding obligation to consummate the Transaction. Buyer and Seller are each a "**Party**" and together the "**Parties**."

## 1. The Business

The "**Business**" means the business of collection, discharge and shredding of spent lithium-ion batteries and hydrometallurgical refining of black mass, as conducted by Seller at its leased facility at 1450 Glendale Avenue, Sparks, Nevada 89431 (the "**Facility**").

## 2. Structure of the Transaction

2.1 The Transaction will be structured as a purchase of assets. Buyer would acquire all of Seller's right, title and interest in all assets, properties and rights used in the Business (the "**Purchased Assets**"). These would include:

(a) all machinery, equipment and processing lines at the Facility, including the receiving and sorting station, the discharge systems, the shredding and separation lines, and the hydrometallurgical refining line, with its leach reactors, solvent-extraction circuits and lithium carbonate crystallizer;

(b) all inventory, including spent batteries, black mass, work in process and refined products;

(c) Seller's leasehold interest in the Facility;

(d) the supply, offtake, tolling and service contracts of the Business, as agreed by the Parties;

(e) all transferable permits, including environmental permits;

(f) all intellectual property, process know-how and trade secrets of the Business, including Seller's patented selective leaching and solvent extraction process; and

(g) all books, records and goodwill of the Business.

2.2 Buyer would not acquire cash, Seller's corporate records, insurance policies, tax refunds for pre-closing periods, or any other assets the Parties agree to exclude in the Definitive Agreement.

2.3 Buyer would assume only the obligations under the assigned contracts that arise after closing, and ordinary-course trade payables of the Business reflected in the closing working capital. Seller would retain all other liabilities. Retained liabilities would include all environmental liabilities arising from conditions at, or operations of, the Facility on or before closing.

## 3. Strategic Rationale

Buyer's interest in the Transaction rests on acquiring an integrated operation that takes spent batteries from intake through to battery-grade refined products. A key reason for the Transaction is Seller's Cathode Feedstock Supply Agreement with Pellancourt Cathode Materials, Inc. (the "**Pellancourt Agreement**"). Under that agreement Pellancourt takes a substantial majority of the Facility's refined lithium carbonate, cobalt sulfate and nickel sulfate on a take-or-pay basis. Buyer's valuation assumes that the Pellancourt Agreement, and the refining capacity that supplies it, will continue in full force after closing.

## 4. Purchase Price

4.1 The purchase price for the Purchased Assets would be $48,500,000 (the "**Purchase Price**"). The Purchase Price would be paid in cash at closing, plus the assumption of the assumed liabilities.

4.2 The Purchase Price assumes delivery of the Business with a normalized level of working capital. The Purchase Price would be subject to a customary post-closing working capital adjustment against a target agreed in the Definitive Agreement.

## 5. Indemnity Escrow

At closing, Buyer would deposit 10% of the Purchase Price ($4,850,000) with a mutually acceptable national escrow agent. The amount would secure Seller's indemnification obligations. The escrow would be released 18 months after closing, less amounts subject to pending claims.

## 6. Indemnification

The Definitive Agreement would contain customary representations, warranties and indemnities for a transaction of this kind. The Parties contemplate a cap on Seller's indemnity for breaches of general representations equal to 10% of the Purchase Price. The cap would not apply to fraud, breaches of fundamental representations, or retained liabilities, including pre-closing environmental liabilities.

## 7. Restrictive Covenants

Seller and its affiliates would agree not to engage in the Business, or in any business competitive with it, in the United States, Canada or Mexico for five years after closing. They would also agree not to solicit customers, suppliers or transferred employees of the Business during that period.

## 8. Transition Services

Seller would provide Buyer with accounting, IT, logistics and regulatory reporting support for up to nine months after closing under a transition services agreement.

## 9. Conditions

Closing would be subject to customary conditions, including:

(a) completion of Buyer's due diligence, including financial, legal, operational and environmental diligence (which may include Phase II sampling at the Facility), to Buyer's satisfaction;

(b) negotiation and execution of a definitive asset purchase agreement and related documents satisfactory to both Parties (the "**Definitive Agreement**");

(c) receipt of all third-party consents required to assign the material contracts of the Business, including the consent of Pellancourt Cathode Materials, Inc. under the Pellancourt Agreement and the consent of the Facility landlord;

(d) approval by the Nevada Division of Environmental Protection of the transfer to Buyer, or reissuance to Buyer, of the environmental permits for the Facility; and

(e) the absence of any material adverse change in the Business.

## 10. Due Diligence; Access

From the date of countersignature, Seller will give Buyer and its advisors reasonable access to the Facility, personnel, books, records and contracts of the Business. This includes access for environmental assessments. Seller will populate an electronic data room for this purpose.

## 11. Timing

The Parties will use good-faith efforts to sign the Definitive Agreement by September 30, 2026 and to close by December 31, 2026.

## 12. Binding Provisions

Only Sections 12 through 16 of this Letter are legally binding on the Parties. They survive any termination of this Letter. No other provision of this Letter creates any binding obligation.

## 13. Exclusivity

For 60 days after Seller's countersignature of this Letter, Seller will not, and will cause its affiliates and representatives not to, solicit, encourage, negotiate or enter into any agreement with any person other than Buyer about any sale of the Business or any material part of its assets. Seller will promptly notify Buyer of any unsolicited proposal of that kind.

## 14. Confidentiality

The existence and terms of this Letter, and all information exchanged in connection with the Transaction, are subject to the Mutual Confidentiality Agreement between the Parties dated May 12, 2026.

## 15. Expenses

Each Party will bear its own costs and expenses in connection with this Letter and the Transaction.

## 16. Governing Law; Termination

16.1 This Letter is governed by the laws of the State of Delaware.

16.2 This Letter terminates on the earliest of:

(a) execution of the Definitive Agreement;

(b) written agreement of the Parties; and

(c) the expiration of the exclusivity period in Section 13.

16.3 Termination does not affect the survival of Sections 12 through 16.
