## Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement ("SPA"), the Transition Services Agreement ("TSA"), the Brilal Board Consent, the Bill of Sale for the Lagoon Assets, and the February 2026 prior draft of SPA Article IX. I identify **seventeen issues** that leave Ratel exposed or fall short of what it needs to close and operate the business. The five most significant are: (1) the Kettle Ridge raw milk supply consent is not a closing condition; (2) Ratel's indemnity for the Company Guaranties was broadened materially from the prior draft and is uncapped; (3) the Bill of Sale subjects lagoon-related environmental claims to the Cap, contradicting the SPA; (4) there is no escrow or holdback securing Seller's indemnification obligations; and (5) the TSA's confidentiality provision is narrower than the SPA's, leaving unmarked Company information exposed during the transition period.

## Issue 1 – Kettle Ridge Raw Milk Supply Consent Is Not a Closing Condition

**Location:** SPA Schedule 4.5; SPA § 4.5; SPA § 7.2(d); Brilal Board Consent, Recital 1.6 and Resolution 4.

**Explanation:** The Brilal Board Consent (Recital 1.6) discloses that the Company is party to a Raw Milk Supply Agreement with Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative, under which Kettle Ridge supplies approximately 40% of the Company's raw milk. A change in control requires Kettle Ridge's prior written consent; absent consent, Kettle Ridge may terminate on 30 days' notice. Despite this, Kettle Ridge's consent does not appear on SPA Schedule 4.5 (which lists only the landlord consent, the organic certifying agent notice, and the VANT notice). Consequently:

- SPA § 4.5 (Consents) represents that no consent is required except as listed on Schedule 4.5. If Kettle Ridge's consent is in fact required, this representation is inaccurate as of signing.
- SPA § 7.2(d) makes it a condition to Buyer's closing obligation only that Seller obtain the consents listed on Schedule 4.5. Kettle Ridge's consent is not a condition to closing.
- Board Consent Resolution 4 authorizes Brilal to request Kettle Ridge's consent **following the Closing**, not before.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Ratel could close the transaction and, within 30 days, lose 40% of its raw milk supply. For a dairy processor, raw milk is the critical input; losing 40% of supply would threaten the Company's ability to operate at scale, meet customer orders, and maintain organic certification (which depends on a documented supply chain). The indemnity for an inaccurate § 4.5 representation would be subject to the Deductible ($648,750) and the Cap ($8.65 million) and would survive only 18 months—far less than the operational damage a supply disruption would cause.

**Assumption:** I assume Kettle Ridge is not an Affiliate of Brilal (the Board Consent treats it as an independent third party requiring consent). If Kettle Ridge were an Affiliate, SPA § 6.6 would terminate the Raw Milk Supply Agreement at Closing (it is not one of the three exceptions), which would be an even more acute problem.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add Kettle Ridge's consent to Schedule 4.5 as a new item (e.g., item 4: "Consent of Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative to the change in control of the Company under the Raw Milk Supply Agreement dated [●]").
2. Add a closing condition in § 7.2 requiring delivery of Kettle Ridge's written consent (or, if consent is conditioned on the Company entering a new supply agreement with Kettle Ridge on specified terms, delivery of that agreement).
3. Add a specific representation in § 4.6 or a new § 4.15 that the Raw Milk Supply Agreement is in full force and effect, that no default exists, and that the term extends at least [●] months beyond the Closing Date.
4. If Kettle Ridge will not consent pre-closing, negotiate a specific indemnity (outside the Deductible/Cap) for loss of the Raw Milk Supply Agreement, with a survival period of at least the remaining term of that agreement.

**Proposed drafting (Schedule 4.5, new item 4):**

> 4. Consent of Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative to the change in control of the Company pursuant to Section [●] of the Raw Milk Supply Agreement between the Company and Kettle Ridge dated [●], in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

**Proposed drafting (§ 7.2, new clause (g)):**

> (g) Seller shall have obtained, and delivered to Buyer a copy of, the written consent of Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative to the transactions contemplated by this Agreement pursuant to the Raw Milk Supply Agreement, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

## Issue 2 – Ratel's Indemnity for Company Guaranties Was Broadened and Is Uncapped

**Location:** SPA § 9.3(b); compare Prior Draft Article IX § 9.3(b); SPA § 9.5; Schedule 1.1(a).

**Explanation:** The prior draft (February 20, 2026) provided that Buyer indemnifies Seller for Company Guaranty payments only "to the extent the payment or performance relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises and is attributable to the period after the Closing." The current SPA § 9.3(b) reads:

> "the Company Guaranties, including any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, **whether arising before or after the Closing**, and **notwithstanding Section 9.5**."

Two changes are adverse to Ratel:

1. **Temporal scope expanded:** The indemnity now covers obligations "whether arising before or after the Closing." If the Company was in default under its lease or supply contracts before Closing (triggering Brilal's guaranty), Ratel would indemnify Brilal for that pre-Closing default.
2. **Expressly carved out from the Deductible and Cap:** The phrase "notwithstanding Section 9.5" removes both the 0.75% Deductible and the 10% Cap. Ratel's exposure is unlimited.

Schedule 1.1(a) identifies two guaranties: (1) the St. Albans Plant lease guaranty and (2) the packaging/ingredient supply contract guaranties. The lease is for the Company's primary operating facility; a default could trigger acceleration of rent or a claim for the full remaining lease term. The supply contracts are Material Contracts (SPA § 4.6(e)) and could involve significant payment obligations.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Ratel has assumed uncapped, unlimited liability for Brilal's guaranty exposure. If the Company's lease is, say, 10 years with $5 million annual rent, and the Company defaults post-Closing, Brilal could be called for $50 million, and Ratel would owe that amount to Brilal with no cap. Even pre-Closing defaults (e.g., unpaid rent from 2025) would trigger Ratel's indemnity.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Restore the temporal limitation from the prior draft: cover only obligations that "first arise and are attributable to the period after the Closing."
2. Remove "notwithstanding Section 9.5" and subject the guaranty indemnity to the Cap (or, at minimum, a separate cap equal to the maximum exposure under the guaranties).
3. Add a representation that no default or event of default exists under the St. Albans Plant lease or the supply contracts as of the Closing Date (so that pre-Closing triggers are disclosed and indemnified under § 9.2(a) with the standard Deductible/Cap).
4. Require delivery of estoppel certificates or landlord/counterparty confirmations at Closing confirming no default.

**Proposed drafting (§ 9.3(b)):**

> (b) the Company Guaranties, including any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, to the extent such payment, performance or obligation relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises and is attributable to the period after the Closing Date;

**Proposed new representation (§ 4.15 or addition to § 4.6):**

> As of the Closing Date, neither the Company nor, to Seller's knowledge, any counterparty is in default or in breach of any provision of the Lease of the St. Albans Plant or any supply contract listed on Schedule 4.6, and no event has occurred that, with notice or the passage of time, would constitute such a default or breach.

## Issue 3 – Bill of Sale Subjects Lagoon-Related Environmental Claims to the Cap, Contradicting the SPA

**Location:** Bill of Sale § 4.1; SPA § 9.5(b)(iii); SPA § 12.8.

**Explanation:** Bill of Sale § 4.1 provides:

> "All Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, **including any Environmental Liability**, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, **and subject to the Cap**."

SPA § 9.5(b)(iii) provides that the Deductible and the Cap do **not** apply to "the Specified Environmental Matter, whether claimed under Section 9.2(a) or Section 9.2(c)." The Specified Environmental Matter is defined as "contamination originating from the St. Albans Plant wastewater lagoon system" (SPA § 1.1).

These provisions are in direct conflict. SPA § 12.8 states: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, the Ancillary Agreement shall control as to its subject matter." The Bill of Sale is an Ancillary Agreement (SPA § 1.1). Its subject matter includes the Lagoon Assets and related Environmental Liabilities. Under § 12.8, the Bill of Sale would control, meaning the Cap **would** apply to the Specified Environmental Matter when the claim is framed as relating to the Conveyed Assets.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** The Specified Environmental Matter is the known, identified environmental risk at the St. Albans Plant. Lagoon contamination (nitrogen, phosphorus, BOD, pathogens—see the "Hazardous Material" definition in SPA § 1.1) could require multi-year remediation costing tens of millions of dollars. If the Cap ($8.65 million) applies, Ratel's recovery is limited to that amount. If the lagoon fails and groundwater contamination is discovered, the remediation cost could far exceed $8.65 million. The SPA's intent (as reflected in § 9.5(b)(iii) and the 7-year survival in § 9.1) was to give Ratel full, uncapped recovery for this known issue.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Amend Bill of Sale § 4.1 to carve out the Specified Environmental Matter from the Cap, consistent with SPA § 9.5(b)(iii).
2. Alternatively, add a clarifying sentence to SPA § 12.8 stating that the SPA controls over Ancillary Agreements with respect to indemnification and limitation-of-liability provisions.

**Proposed drafting (Bill of Sale § 4.1, revised):**

> 4.1 Recovery. All Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, including any Environmental Liability, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, and subject to the Cap; **provided that** the foregoing reference to the Cap shall not apply to Losses arising out of or relating to the Specified Environmental Matter, which shall be recoverable in accordance with Section 9.5(b)(iii) of the Purchase Agreement.

## Issue 4 – No Escrow or Holdback to Secure Seller's Indemnification Obligations

**Location:** SPA Article IX (as a whole); SPA § 2.2.

**Explanation:** The Purchase Price ($86.5 million, less debt payoff and working capital adjustment) is paid in full at Closing by wire transfer (SPA § 2.2). There is no escrow, holdback, letter of credit, or other security for Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX. Seller's indemnities survive for 18 months (general reps), 5 years (environmental reps), 7 years (Specified Environmental Matter), and 60 days beyond the statute of limitations (Fundamental Reps and Tax Reps).

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Brilal is a Delaware corporation selling its subsidiary. Post-Closing, Brilal will have received the full Purchase Price and may distribute it to its shareholders, invest it in other businesses, or face its own financial difficulties. If Ratel needs to make an indemnity claim 3–7 years post-Closing (e.g., for lagoon contamination or a tax audit), it will be an unsecured creditor of Brilal. Collecting a multi-million-dollar judgment from a corporate shell that has distributed its assets is difficult and expensive.

**Assumption:** I assume Brilal is a holding company or has other operations beyond Sunfold. If Brilal is liquidating or merging post-Closing, the collection risk is even greater.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Negotiate an escrow of a portion of the Purchase Price (e.g., 10–15%, or $8.65–$12.975 million) for 18 months to secure general indemnity claims, with a separate environmental escrow (e.g., $5–$10 million) for 5–7 years to secure the Specified Environmental Matter and environmental rep claims.
2. Alternatively, require Brilal to post a letter of credit or surety bond in an amount equal to the Cap ($8.65 million) for the general indemnity period and a separate amount for the environmental period.
3. If Seller resists an escrow, at minimum require a representation that Brilal will not distribute the Purchase Price to its shareholders for [●] months post-Closing without Buyer's consent, or that Brilal will maintain net worth of at least $[●] during the indemnity survival period.

**Proposed drafting (new § 2.5):**

> **2.5 Indemnity Escrow.** At the Closing, $[●] of the Purchase Price otherwise payable to Seller shall be deposited with [●] as escrow agent (the "Indemnity Escrow") pursuant to an escrow agreement in form reasonably satisfactory to the Parties. The Indemnity Escrow shall be held for the benefit of the Buyer Indemnitees to secure Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX and shall be released as follows: (a) $[●] shall be released to Seller 18 months after the Closing Date, less any amounts then subject to pending claims; (b) $[●] shall be released to Seller five years after the Closing Date, less any amounts then subject to pending claims; and (c) the remainder shall be released to Seller seven years after the Closing Date.

## Issue 5 – TSA Confidentiality Definition Is Narrower Than the SPA, Leaving Unmarked Information Exposed

**Location:** TSA § 7.1; TSA § 7.3; SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Company Confidential Information"); SPA § 6.7; SPA § 12.8.

**Explanation:** The SPA defines "Company Confidential Information" broadly: "all non-public information concerning the Company or its business, in any form and **whether or not marked or identified as confidential**, including customer lists and customer order data, farm supplier identities and pricing, herd records, product formulas and recipes, organic certification files, laboratory testing data, production and logistics data, and financial information" (SPA § 1.1). SPA § 6.7 obligates Seller to hold all Company Confidential Information in strict confidence post-Closing.

The TSA defines "Confidential Information" narrowly: information "that is **marked as confidential at the time of disclosure**" (TSA § 7.1). TSA § 7.3 then provides: "Provider's obligations with respect to information of the Company received by Provider or Provider Personnel in performing the Services are governed **exclusively** by this Section 7."

Under SPA § 12.8, the TSA (as an Ancillary Agreement) controls as to its subject matter in case of conflict. The subject matter of TSA § 7 is the protection of information exchanged during the transition services. Therefore, for information Provider receives in performing the Services, the TSA's narrower (marking-required) definition governs, not the SPA's broader definition.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** During the 12-month TSA period, Provider will handle raw milk samples, production schedules, customer order data, delivery routes, supplier identities, and organic certification files. If the Company's personnel share this information with Provider without marking it "confidential" (which is operationally impractical in the course of daily lab testing and logistics coordination), Provider has no obligation under the TSA to keep it secret. A competitor could obtain the Company's customer lists, farm supplier pricing, or product formulas through Provider with no breach of the TSA.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Amend TSA § 7.1 to adopt the SPA's broader definition, or at minimum to provide that information "reasonably understood to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure" is covered regardless of marking.
2. Amend TSA § 7.3 to provide that SPA § 6.7 applies in addition to (not exclusively replaced by) TSA § 7 for Company information.
3. Require Provider to implement internal protocols at the start of the Service Period designating all Company data received in performing the Services as confidential.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 7.1, revised first sentence):**

> "**Confidential Information**" means any non-public business, technical or financial information disclosed by one Party (the "Disclosing Party") to the other Party (the "Receiving Party") in connection with this Agreement, in written, electronic or other tangible form, that is marked as confidential at the time of disclosure **or that a reasonable Person in the Receiving Party's position would understand to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure**. With respect to information of the Company received by Provider in performing the Services, Confidential Information includes all Company Confidential Information as defined in the Purchase Agreement.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 7.3, revised):**

> 7.3 Information Received in Performing the Services. Provider's obligations with respect to information of the Company received by Provider or Provider Personnel in performing the Services are governed by this Section 7 **and Section 6.7 of the Purchase Agreement**. In the event of a conflict, the provision providing greater protection to the Company shall control.

## Issue 6 – TSA Performance Levels Have No Consequence for Non-Compliance

**Location:** TSA Schedule A (Performance Level column); TSA § 5.1; TSA § 9.2.

**Explanation:** Schedule A specifies performance levels for each service (e.g., "Results reported within 4 hours of sample receipt; 99% on-time" for raw milk testing; "97% on-time delivery; product at or below 41°F" for cold-chain transport). However, the TSA provides no service credit, fee reduction, or specific remedy if Provider fails to meet these levels. The only remedies are:

- Termination for material breach (§ 9.2), which requires a 30-day cure period and is a blunt instrument for a single late test result.
- Indemnification for third-party claims (§ 8.2), which doesn't address first-party operational losses.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** For a dairy processor, a 4-hour turnaround on antibiotic residue testing is operationally critical. If raw milk tests late, the milk may need to be held in refrigeration beyond its usable window, or the Company may be forced to accept milk without test results (a food safety risk). A 97% on-time delivery standard means 3% of shipments can be late without consequence—potentially missing customer delivery windows and triggering customer penalties. The absence of a service credit mechanism means Provider has no financial incentive to meet the stated performance levels beyond the threat of termination.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add a service credit provision: for each month in which Provider fails to meet a performance level, the Company receives a credit against Fees equal to [●]% of the monthly fee for the affected service.
2. Add a termination right for chronic underperformance: if Provider fails to meet a performance level in [3] consecutive months, the Company may terminate that Service on 15 days' notice without penalty.

**Proposed drafting (new TSA § 5.5):**

> **5.5 Service Credits.** If Provider fails to meet a performance level set forth in Schedule A for any calendar month, the Company shall be entitled to a credit against the Fees for that month equal to five percent (5%) of the monthly Fee for the affected Service for each percentage point by which the actual performance falls below the stated performance level, up to a maximum credit of twenty-five percent (25%) of the monthly Fee for that Service. If Provider fails to meet a performance level in three (3) consecutive months, the Company may terminate the affected Service on fifteen (15) days' written notice without penalty, and the Service shall be deemed terminated for purposes of Section 6.4 (Migration Assistance).

## Issue 7 – TSA Cold-Chain Per-Load Rates Are Not Fixed in the Document

**Location:** TSA Schedule A, Annex A-4.

**Explanation:** Annex A-4 states: "Per-load rates for each delivery lane are as agreed between the Service Coordinators on the Closing Date. They equal the rates charged to the Company for the same lanes in the month of March 2026." The actual rates are not in the document. They are to be "agreed" by the Service Coordinators on the Closing Date, with no fallback mechanism if they cannot agree.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Cold-chain logistics is a significant ongoing cost (the only service without a fixed monthly fee). If the Service Coordinators cannot agree on rates at Closing, the Company has no contractual basis to require Provider to transport product, and no basis to calculate the Fees owed. This creates uncertainty about one of the three core services and a potential dispute at the most inopportune time (Closing Day).

**Recommended fix:**

1. Populate Annex A-4 with the actual per-load rates for each delivery lane before execution, based on March 2026 invoices.
2. If the rates are voluminous, attach them as a schedule with a mechanism for annual adjustment (e.g., CPI or fuel index) rather than leaving them to be "agreed" at Closing.

**Proposed drafting (Annex A-4, revised):**

> **Annex A-4 (Per-Load Rates).** The following per-load rates apply to each delivery lane and are fixed for the initial Service Period:
>
> | Lane | Origin | Destination | Rate per Load |
> |------|--------|-------------|---------------|
> | 1 | St. Albans, VT | [Customer DC], MA | $[●] |
> | 2 | St. Albans, VT | [Customer DC], NH | $[●] |
> | ... | ... | ... | ... |
>
> Rates shall be adjusted annually on each anniversary of the Closing Date by the percentage change in the BLS Freight Transportation Price Index (or, if unavailable, the U.S. DOE diesel fuel price) from the prior anniversary, provided that no adjustment shall exceed [5]% in either direction.

## Issue 8 – No Transition Arrangements for Back-Office Functions (Accounting, IT, HR)

**Location:** TSA (scope limited to lab testing, organic compliance, cold chain); SPA § 6.6; SPA § 4.7.

**Explanation:** The TSA covers only three service categories: laboratory testing, organic certification compliance support, and cold-chain logistics. SPA § 6.6 provides that "all Contracts between the Company, on the one hand, and Seller or any of its Affiliates, on the other, shall be terminated at or before the Closing without further liability to the Company" (except for the TSA, Bill of Sale, and Company Guaranties).

If Brilal or its affiliates were providing the Company with back-office services—accounting, payroll, IT infrastructure, HR administration, procurement—those arrangements terminate at Closing with no replacement. The TSA does not cover them. SPA § 4.7 represents that the Company's assets "together with the Lagoon Assets and the services to be provided under the Transition Services Agreement, are sufficient for the conduct of the Company's business as currently conducted," but if back-office services were provided by Brilal, this representation is inaccurate (and the remedy is subject to the Deductible/Cap and 18-month survival).

**Assumption:** I assume Brilal or its affiliates provided at least some back-office functions to the Company pre-Closing, given that the Company is a wholly owned subsidiary and the TSA was negotiated to cover only operational (not administrative) services. If the Company had fully standalone back-office functions, this issue does not arise.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Without accounting, IT, and HR systems in place at Closing, the Company could face: inability to process payroll on time; loss of access to financial systems needed for the working capital true-up (SPA § 2.3(b) requires Buyer to deliver a working capital statement within 90 days post-Closing); disruption to customer ordering and invoicing; and compliance gaps (e.g., tax filings, benefit plan administration).

**Recommended fix:**

1. Confirm during diligence whether Brilal or its affiliates provided any back-office services to the Company. If so, either (a) expand the TSA to include those services for a transition period, or (b) require Brilal to deliver all systems, data, and access credentials at Closing with a knowledge-transfer period.
2. Add a specific representation in the SPA that the Company has independent access to all systems, records, and vendor relationships necessary to operate its business without reliance on Seller or its Affiliates post-Closing.
3. If back-office services will continue under the TSA, add them to Schedule A with fees and performance levels.

**Proposed drafting (new SPA § 4.15 or addition to § 4.7):**

> The Company maintains, or will maintain as of the Closing Date, independent and fully functional accounting, information technology, human resources and payroll systems, and has independent relationships with all service providers (including its accounting firm, IT hosting provider, payroll provider and benefits administrator) necessary for the conduct of its business, in each case without reliance on Seller or any of its Affiliates.

## Issue 9 – Bill of Sale Contains No Representation as to Condition or Compliance of Lagoon Assets

**Location:** Bill of Sale § 3.3; Bill of Sale Schedule 1; SPA § 4.7.

**Explanation:** Bill of Sale § 3.3 disclaims all representations and warranties except for title (§ 3.1) and the SPA Article IV reps. There is no representation that the Lagoon Assets are in good working order, free from defect, in compliance with Environmental Laws, or capable of treating the St. Albans Plant's wastewater within permit limits. The only asset-condition representation is SPA § 4.7 (sufficiency of assets), which is a broad, forward-looking statement subject to the Deductible/Cap and 18-month survival.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** The lagoon system includes liners, aerators, pumps, and monitoring wells (Bill of Sale Schedule 1). If the liners are degraded (a common failure mode in wastewater lagoons), the aerators are at end-of-life, or the system cannot meet permit limits without capital upgrades, Ratel inherits a potentially multi-million-dollar capital project with no recourse against Seller. The 18-month survival for the § 4.7 representation may expire before latent defects (e.g., liner leaks) manifest.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add a specific representation in the Bill of Sale that the Lagoon Assets are in good working order and condition, have been maintained in accordance with applicable Environmental Laws, and are capable of treating the St. Albans Plant's process wastewater within the limits of the Company's wastewater discharge permit.
2. Require delivery of a pre-Closing environmental assessment or lagoon inspection report as a closing deliverable.
3. If Seller resists a condition representation, negotiate a specific indemnity for capital expenditures required to bring the Lagoon Assets into compliance within [12] months post-Closing, outside the Deductible/Cap.

**Proposed drafting (new Bill of Sale § 3.4):**

> **3.4 Condition; Compliance.** Seller represents and warrants to the Company that, as of the Closing Date: (a) the Conveyed Assets are in good working order and condition, ordinary wear and tear excepted; (b) the Conveyed Assets have been operated and maintained in material compliance with all Environmental Laws; and (c) the Conveyed Assets are capable of treating the process wastewater generated by the St. Albans Plant within the limits set forth in the Company's wastewater discharge permit held from the Vermont Agency of Natural Resources. This Section 3.4 shall survive the Closing for five (5) years and shall not be subject to the Deductible or the Cap.

## Issue 10 – TSA Liability Cap May Be Inadequate for First-Party Losses

**Location:** TSA § 8.1; TSA Schedule A (Fees).

**Explanation:** TSA § 8.1 caps Provider's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." The fixed monthly Fees total $91,000 ($38,500 + $27,000 + $14,500 + $11,000), or approximately $1,092,000 over the 12-month Service Period, plus per-load cold-chain rates (amount unknown per Issue 7). The cap does not apply to third-party claim indemnities (§§ 8.2, 8.3), confidentiality breaches, or gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud.

However, first-party losses—such as the cost of destroyed or spoiled product due to a cold-chain failure, lost production due to a lab outage, or the cost of emergency replacement services if Provider terminates—are subject to the cap.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** A single day of lab testing outage could force the Company to reject or hold incoming raw milk (valued at potentially $50,000–$100,000 per day for a facility of this scale). A cold-chain failure during summer could spoil an entire shipment of yogurt or cheese. The $1.1 million cap may not cover the cumulative first-party losses from a series of service failures, and the Company would have no remedy beyond the cap except in cases of gross negligence or willful misconduct (a high bar).

**Recommended fix:**

1. Increase the cap to at least 2x the annual Fees (i.e., ~$2.2 million) or a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $3 million).
2. Carve out from the cap any losses resulting from Provider's failure to maintain the laboratory accreditations required by § 5.2 (since loss of accreditation would be a fundamental failure of the service).
3. Require Provider to maintain professional liability and product liability insurance with limits of not less than $[5] million, naming the Company and Buyer as additional insureds, and to deliver certificates of insurance at Closing.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 8.1, revised first sentence):**

> The aggregate liability of Provider under this Agreement shall not exceed the greater of (a) two times (2x) the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement during the twelve (12) months preceding the event giving rise to the claim, or (b) $[3,000,000].

**Proposed new TSA § 5.6:**

> **5.6 Insurance.** Provider shall maintain, at its own expense, throughout the Service Period: (a) commercial general liability insurance with limits of not less than $[2,000,000] per occurrence; (b) professional liability (errors and omissions) insurance with limits of not less than $[5,000,000] per claim; (c) automobile liability insurance covering Provider's fleet with limits of not less than $[5,000,000] per occurrence; and (d) product liability insurance with limits of not less than $[5,000,000] per occurrence. Provider shall name the Company and Ratel Dairy Holdings, LLC as additional insureds on policies (a), (c) and (d) and shall deliver certificates of insurance evidencing the foregoing to the Company at the Closing and upon each renewal.

## Issue 11 – Asymmetric Assignment Provision Favors Seller

**Location:** SPA § 12.4.

**Explanation:** SPA § 12.4 provides: "Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of Seller, and any purported assignment without that consent shall be void. Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the consent of Buyer."

**Why it matters to Ratel:** Ratel cannot assign its rights under the SPA (including indemnity claims) without Brilal's consent. This is problematic if: (a) Ratel seeks acquisition financing and the lender requires an assignment of the SPA as collateral; (b) Ratel restructures (e.g., merges into a new holding company) and needs to assign the agreement; or (c) Ratel wants to sell its indemnity claims to a third-party funder. Meanwhile, Brilal can freely assign its obligations (including the TSA service obligations and the Bill of Sale conveyance) to any Affiliate or third party without Ratel's consent, potentially degrading the quality of performance.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Make the assignment provision mutual: neither Party may assign without the other's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), except that either Party may assign to an Affiliate or in connection with a merger or sale of all or substantially all of its assets.
2. Carve out an exception allowing Buyer to assign to a lender as security for acquisition financing.

**Proposed drafting (SPA § 12.4, revised):**

> **12.4 Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of the other Party, such consent not to be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed; provided that (a) either Party may assign this Agreement to an Affiliate without consent, and (b) Buyer may assign this Agreement or any of its rights hereunder to a lender or other financing source as security for acquisition financing without Seller's consent, so long as Buyer remains primarily liable for its obligations hereunder. Any purported assignment in violation of this Section 12.4 shall be void.

## Issue 12 – TSA Migration Assistance Available Only During Last 90 Days of Service Period

**Location:** TSA § 6.4; TSA § 9.2; TSA § 9.3.

**Explanation:** TSA § 6.4 provides: "During the last 90 days of the Service Period, Provider shall provide the Company with reasonable assistance in transitioning each Service to the Company or to a replacement provider." This obligation is tied to the "last 90 days of the Service Period"—i.e., the final quarter of the 12-month term.

If Provider terminates early for the Company's convenience (§ 9.1, 30 days' notice), for Provider's own breach by the Company (§ 9.2), or due to Provider's insolvency (§ 9.3), the Company may not be in the "last 90 days of the Service Period" and therefore may not be entitled to migration assistance. In the insolvency scenario, Provider may be unable to provide any assistance regardless.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** If Provider becomes insolvent in month 6 (a realistic risk for a company that has just sold a major subsidiary and may be restructuring), the Company would lose lab testing, organic compliance support, and cold-chain logistics with only 30 days' notice (or immediately, in the case of insolvency) and no contractual right to transition assistance. For organic certification, a gap in compliance support could jeopardize the Company's NOP certification, which is existential for an organic dairy brand.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Amend § 6.4 to provide migration assistance upon any termination or expiration, not just during the last 90 days.
2. Add a data/records escrow requirement: Provider shall maintain a current copy of all organic system plan documentation, test methods, carrier contracts, and customer delivery specifications in a shared repository accessible to the Company at all times during the Service Period.
3. Require Provider to maintain a business continuity plan and to notify the Company immediately of any event that could impair its ability to perform.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 6.4, revised):**

> **6.4 Migration Assistance.** Upon any expiration or termination of this Agreement or any Service (whether by notice, by operation of law, or by insolvency), Provider shall provide the Company with reasonable assistance in transitioning the affected Service to the Company or to a replacement provider, including the transfer of test methods, organic system plan documentation, carrier contact information, and all records required by Section 6.3. Provider shall begin such assistance within five (5) Business Days after the effective date of termination and shall continue for up to ninety (90) days thereafter, or such shorter period as the Company specifies.

**Proposed new TSA § 6.5:**

> **6.5 Shared Repository.** Throughout the Service Period, Provider shall maintain a current and complete copy of (a) the Company's organic system plan, (b) all test methods and protocols used in the Laboratory Testing Services, (c) carrier contracts and delivery specifications used in the Cold-Chain Logistics Services, and (d) all records required by Section 6.3, in a shared electronic repository to which the Company and its designees have continuous access.

## Issue 13 – Seller Controls Environmental Remediation with Only a "Reasonable Interference" Standard

**Location:** SPA § 9.4(c).

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.4(c) provides: "Seller shall control the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter, in consultation with Buyer, using qualified environmental consultants and in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with operations at the St. Albans Plant."

Seller has the right to control the remediation. Buyer's protection is limited to the "unreasonably interfere" standard. There is no requirement that remediation be completed by a specific date, no approval right for Buyer over the remediation plan, and no specific remedy if Seller's chosen approach causes operational disruption.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** The lagoon is at the St. Albans Plant where Ratel's business operates. Remediation could involve dewatering lagoon cells, replacing liners, installing new treatment equipment, or restricting discharge—all of which could disrupt production. If Seller chooses a slow, low-cost approach that keeps the lagoon partially out of service for years, Ratel's production is impaired but its remedy is limited to arguing that the interference was "unreasonable."

**Recommended fix:**

1. Require Seller to deliver a remediation plan to Buyer for approval (not to be unreasonably withheld) before commencing work, with a target completion date.
2. Provide that if Seller's remediation causes the St. Albans Plant to reduce production by more than [●]% for more than [●] consecutive days, Seller shall reimburse the Company for lost profits during the disruption period (outside the Cap).
3. Give Buyer the right to take over remediation control if Seller fails to make material progress within [●] months.

**Proposed drafting (SPA § 9.4(c), revised):**

> (c) Seller shall control the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter, in consultation with Buyer, using qualified environmental consultants. Before commencing any remediation activity, Seller shall deliver to Buyer a written remediation plan, including a schedule and budget, which Buyer may comment on but which shall not be unreasonably withheld. Seller shall complete the remediation no later than [●] months after the Closing Date, unless extended by written agreement of the Parties. If Seller fails to make material progress consistent with the remediation plan for [60] consecutive days, Buyer may, by written notice, assume control of the remediation, and Seller shall cooperate and provide access. Remediation shall be conducted in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with operations at the St. Albans Plant; provided that if remediation activity causes the Company to reduce production by more than [10]% for more than [5] consecutive Business Days, Seller shall reimburse the Company for documented lost profits during the period of reduction, and such reimbursement shall not be subject to the Deductible or the Cap.

## Issue 14 – TSA Does Not Require Key Personnel

**Location:** TSA § 2.4; TSA § 11.2.

**Explanation:** The TSA permits Provider to use subcontractors (§ 2.4) and does not require any specific named individuals to perform the Services. For organic certification compliance support (Service A-3), the quality of service depends heavily on the specific individual's familiarity with the Company's organic system plan, its certifying agent's expectations, and its supplier base. For lab testing (Services A-1 and A-2), the lab director and key technicians' competence directly affect result accuracy and turnaround time.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** If Provider replaces its organic compliance officer or key lab personnel with less experienced staff (or subcontractors) during the Service Period, the quality of services could degrade without triggering a technical breach of the Service Standard (which is measured by output metrics, not personnel identity). A new organic compliance officer might miss a certifying agent deadline or fail to properly document a supplier change, risking the Company's NOP certification.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Identify key personnel in Schedule A and require Provider to use them (or equally qualified replacements approved by the Company) during the Service Period.
2. Require 30 days' advance notice of any personnel change.

**Proposed drafting (new TSA § 2.6):**

> **2.6 Key Personnel.** Provider shall perform the Services using the individuals identified as "Key Personnel" on Schedule A, or replacements of equivalent or greater qualifications approved in advance by the Company (such approval not to be unreasonably withheld). Provider shall give the Company at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice of any proposed change in Key Personnel.

## Issue 15 – Force Majeure Includes "Animal Disease Outbreak" with a 30-Day Service Gap

**Location:** TSA § 11.3.

**Explanation:** TSA § 11.3 lists "animal disease outbreak" as a force majeure event. If a force majeure event prevents performance of any Service for more than 30 days, the Company may terminate that Service. During the 30-day period, Provider is excused from performance and the Company has no remedy.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** An animal disease outbreak (e.g., highly pathogenic avian influenza affecting a region, or a bovine disease affecting the raw milk supply chain) could simultaneously (a) reduce raw milk supply and (b) trigger Provider's force majeure excuse for cold-chain logistics (if the outbreak affects Provider's carriers or routes). The Company would be without transport for up to 30 days with no fee abatement specified and no service credit. For a perishable dairy product, 30 days without cold-chain logistics is catastrophic.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Clarify that force majeure does not excuse Provider's obligation to use commercially reasonable alternative means of performance (e.g., alternative carriers, alternative routes).
2. Provide that Fees abate proportionally during any force majeure delay.
3. Shorten the 30-day termination trigger to 15 days for the Cold-Chain Logistics Services.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 11.3, add at end):**

> During any force majeure event, the Fees for the affected Service shall abate on a daily basis for the duration of the delay. Provider shall use commercially reasonable efforts to perform the Services through alternative means (including alternative carriers, routes or facilities) and shall not invoke force majeure to the extent performance is achievable through such alternatives. If a force majeure event prevents performance of the Cold-Chain Logistics Services (Services A-4 or A-5) for more than fifteen (15) consecutive days, the Company may terminate the affected Service on written notice.

## Issue 16 – Non-Compete Does Not Explicitly Bind Seller's Successors in Interest

**Location:** SPA § 6.8.

**Explanation:** SPA § 6.8 binds "Seller" and "its Affiliates" for three years post-Closing. However, it does not explicitly bind a successor to Seller in the event of Seller's merger, acquisition, or asset sale. If Brilal is acquired by a competitor (or sells its remaining dairy assets to a competitor) within the three-year period, the new owner might argue it is not bound by the non-compete (particularly if it is not an "Affiliate" of Brilal at the time of the competitive activity).

**Why it matters to Ratel:** The non-compete is Ratel's primary protection against Brilal (or its successors) re-entering the organic dairy market in New England and New York. If Brilal sells its other operations to a competitor post-Closing, that competitor could process and sell organic fluid milk, yogurt, or cheese in the restricted states without violating § 6.8, if it is not an Affiliate of Brilal.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add language binding successors and assigns of Seller.
2. Add a provision that any merger, acquisition, or sale of all or substantially all of Seller's assets by Seller during the non-compete period does not relieve the successor of the non-compete obligations.

**Proposed drafting (SPA § 6.8, add at end):**

> This Section 6.8 shall be binding upon Seller's successors and assigns, including any Person that acquires all or substantially all of Seller's assets or into which Seller merges. Any such successor or assignee shall be deemed to have assumed the obligations of Seller under this Section 6.8.

## Issue 17 – TSA Effective Date Is Fixed at April 30, 2026, But SPA Closing Date Is Conditional

**Location:** TSA § 1.1 (definition of "Closing Date"); SPA § 3.1; SPA § 8.1(b).

**Explanation:** The TSA defines "Closing Date" as "April 30, 2026" (TSA § 1.1). The SPA provides that Closing occurs on "the third Business Day after satisfaction or waiver of the conditions in Article VII" (SPA § 3.1) and may be terminated if Closing has not occurred by June 30, 2026 (SPA § 8.1(b)). If the conditions are not satisfied by April 27 (three Business Days before April 30), the actual Closing Date will be later than April 30, creating a mismatch with the TSA's fixed date.

**Why it matters to Ratel:** If the TSA's Effective Date passes before the actual Closing, there is a gap during which Provider is not contractually obligated to perform the Services, but the Company may still need them (e.g., if the Closing is delayed by a few weeks due to a consent issue). Conversely, if the TSA is effective before the Closing, Provider could argue it is obligated to perform before it has the benefit of the SPA's closing conditions.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Change the TSA's "Closing Date" definition to track the SPA: "The 'Closing Date' means the date on which the Closing occurs under the Purchase Agreement."
2. Make the TSA's Effective Date the actual Closing Date rather than a fixed calendar date.

**Proposed drafting (TSA § 1.1, revised):**

> "**Closing**" and "**Closing Date**" have the meanings given to them in the Purchase Agreement. The Effective Date of this Agreement shall be the Closing Date.

## Additional Observations (Lower Priority)

**A. SPA § 6.11 (Books and Records) – Access Runs Both Ways.** This section requires Buyer to afford Seller access to pre-Closing books and records for 7 years. This is standard but worth noting as an ongoing administrative burden on Ratel.

**B. SPA § 10.3 (Transfer Taxes) – Split Equally.** Transfer, documentary, and similar taxes are borne equally by Buyer and Seller. For a stock purchase, transfer taxes are typically minimal, but if any state imposes a tax on the transaction, Ratel bears half.

**C. TSA § 4.1 (Fees at Cost Without Markup).** The Fees reflect Provider's "fully loaded cost...without markup." This is favorable to Ratel. However, "fully loaded cost" is not defined and could be disputed. Consider adding a definition or requiring Provider to provide cost documentation upon request.

**D. SPA § 9.5(e) (Materiality Scrape).** The materiality scrape applies to Loss calculation but not to determining whether a breach occurred. This is standard and favorable to Ratel (it means the full amount of loss is recoverable once a breach is established, even if the representation contained a materiality qualifier).

**E. Brilal Board Consent – Three Directors.** The consent is signed by three directors (three signature blocks). Confirm that three constitutes the full board and that all have signed. If the board has more than three members, the consent is not "unanimous" as required by the heading.
