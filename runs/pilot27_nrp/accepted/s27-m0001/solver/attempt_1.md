## Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (the "SPA"), the Transition Services Agreement (the "TSA"), the Brilal board consent (the "Board Consent"), the Bill of Sale for the Lagoon Assets (the "Bill of Sale"), and the February 20, 2026 prior draft of SPA Article IX (the "Prior Draft"). I identify fourteen issues below, ordered by significance to Ratel. The three most critical are: (1) a direct conflict between the Bill of Sale and the SPA that likely re-imposes the indemnification cap on lagoon environmental losses; (2) the absence of the Kettle Ridge raw milk consent as a closing condition, leaving 40% of the Company's raw milk supply at risk immediately post-Closing; and (3) an expansion of Ratel's guaranty indemnity from the Prior Draft that is now uncapped and covers pre-Closing obligations.

## Issue 1: Bill of Sale Re-Imposes the Cap on Lagoon Environmental Losses

**Location:** Bill of Sale § 4.1; SPA § 9.5(b)(iii); SPA § 12.8

**Explanation:** SPA § 9.5(b)(iii) expressly excludes the Specified Environmental Matter from both the Deductible and the Cap. SPA § 9.2(c) provides a standalone indemnity for the Specified Environmental Matter with a seven-year survival period (SPA § 9.1). The clear intent is that Ratel has uncapped, long-tail protection for lagoon contamination.

However, Bill of Sale § 4.1 provides that "[a]ll Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, including any Environmental Liability, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, **and subject to the Cap**."

SPA § 12.8 provides that "[i]n the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, the Ancillary Agreement shall control as to its subject matter." The Bill of Sale is an Ancillary Agreement (SPA § 1.1), and its subject matter is the Lagoon Assets. A claim for lagoon contamination is squarely within the Bill of Sale's subject matter. On a plain reading, the Bill of Sale's "subject to the Cap" language controls, overriding the SPA's exclusion in § 9.5(b)(iii).

The practical effect: Ratel's recovery for lagoon-related environmental losses—potentially the single largest post-Closing exposure given the known Specified Environmental Matter—is capped at 10% of the Purchase Price, or approximately $8,650,000.

**Assumption:** I assume the parties did not intend this result. The SPA's structure (separate indemnity in § 9.2(c), seven-year survival, express exclusion from the Cap) indicates the negotiated deal was for uncapped environmental protection. The Bill of Sale language appears to be a drafting error or an overlooked carry-over from a form.

**Recommended Fix:** Amend Bill of Sale § 4.1 to delete "and subject to the Cap" and to add a clarifying sentence:

> "All Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, including any Environmental Liability, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement. For the avoidance of doubt, Losses relating to the Specified Environmental Matter shall not be subject to the Deductible or the Cap, consistent with Section 9.5(b)(iii) of the Purchase Agreement."

Alternatively, if Seller resists, add to SPA § 12.8 a carve-out: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Article IX of this Agreement shall control over any Ancillary Agreement with respect to the scope, limitations and survival of indemnification obligations."

## Issue 2: Kettle Ridge Raw Milk Consent Not a Closing Condition

**Location:** SPA Schedule 4.5; SPA § 7.2(d); Board Consent Recital 1.6 and Resolution 4

**Explanation:** Board Consent Recital 1.6 discloses that the Company's Raw Milk Supply Agreement with Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative provides that a change in control requires Kettle Ridge's prior written consent, "failing which Kettle Ridge may terminate the Raw Milk Supply Agreement on 30 days' notice." Kettle Ridge supplies approximately 40% of the Company's raw milk.

Despite this, Schedule 4.5 to the SPA lists only three items: (1) landlord consent, (2) notice to the organic certifying agent, and (3) notice to Vermont ANR. The Kettle Ridge consent is not listed. Consequently, it is not a condition to Closing under SPA § 7.2(d), which requires only "each consent listed on Schedule 4.5."

Board Consent Resolution 4 confirms the parties' intent to defer: it authorizes the Seller's officers to "request such consent **following the Closing**, in cooperation with the Buyer and the Company."

This creates a severe post-Closing risk: Ratel closes, takes title to the Company, and then Kettle Ridge has 30 days' notice to walk away with 40% of the raw milk supply. The SPA provides no specific protection for this scenario. While the Kettle Ridge agreement is presumably a Material Contract under SPA § 4.6(a) (each Contract for the purchase of raw milk), and Seller's representation in § 4.3(c) that no consent is required "except as set forth on Schedule 4.5" would be inaccurate if the Kettle Ridge consent is in fact required, Ratel's remedy would be a breach-of-representation claim subject to the 18-month survival period, the Deductible, and the Cap.

**Assumption:** I assume the Kettle Ridge consent was deliberately excluded from Schedule 4.5 as a business concession to keep the deal moving, rather than an oversight. The Board Consent language ("following the Closing") supports this.

**Recommended Fix (in order of preference):**

1. **Add Kettle Ridge consent to Schedule 4.5** and make it a condition to Closing under § 7.2(d). This is the cleanest solution but may be commercially difficult if Kettle Ridge is reluctant to consent pre-Closing.

2. **If Seller resists adding it to Schedule 4.5**, add a specific post-Closing covenant in Article VI:

> "**6.12 Raw Milk Supply; Kettle Ridge.** Seller shall use its best efforts to obtain Kettle Ridge's consent to the change in control under the Raw Milk Supply Agreement within 30 days after the Closing Date. If Kettle Ridge delivers notice of termination of the Raw Milk Supply Agreement, Seller shall indemnify Buyer and the Company for all Losses resulting therefrom, including the cost of replacing the affected raw milk supply at a higher price for a period of twelve (12) months following the Closing Date. This indemnification shall not be subject to the Deductible or the Cap."

3. **At minimum**, add a specific representation in Article IV:

> "**4.15 Raw Milk Supply.** The Raw Milk Supply Agreement between the Company and Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative is in full force and effect. Neither Kettle Ridge nor, to Seller's knowledge, the Company is in breach thereof. Seller has not received any notice of termination or intent to terminate from Kettle Ridge."

And ensure this representation is included in the "all other representations" that must be true and correct in all material respects at Closing (§ 7.2(a)).

## Issue 3: Ratel's Guaranty Indemnity Expanded and Made Uncapped

**Location:** SPA § 9.3(b); Prior Draft § 9.3(b); SPA Schedule 1.1(a)

**Explanation:** The Prior Draft limited Ratel's indemnification for Company Guaranties to "any payment or performance by Seller or any of its Affiliates under a Company Guaranty, **to the extent the payment or performance relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises and is attributable to the period after the Closing**."

The current SPA § 9.3(b) reads: "any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, **whether arising before or after the Closing, and notwithstanding Section 9.5**."

Two adverse changes:
- The temporal limitation ("first arises and is attributable to the period after the Closing") has been deleted. Ratel now indemnifies Seller for guaranty obligations that arose *before* Closing.
- The phrase "notwithstanding Section 9.5" makes this indemnity expressly subject to neither the Deductible nor the Cap.

The Company Guaranties (Schedule 1.1(a)) cover: (1) the lease of the St. Albans Plant, and (2) payment obligations under packaging and ingredient supply contracts. If the Company (now Ratel's subsidiary) defaults on rent or supplier payments after Closing, Seller as guarantor could be called upon, and Ratel would owe Seller the full amount with no cap. If a pre-Closing default triggers the guaranty (e.g., a supplier invoice that was disputed pre-Closing but paid post-Closing), Ratel is still on the hook.

**Recommended Fix:**

Revert to the Prior Draft formulation with a narrow expansion if commercially necessary:

> "**9.3(b)** any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, to the extent the payment or performance relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises or becomes due after the Closing Date. This indemnification shall be subject to Section 9.5."

If Seller insists on pre-Closing coverage, limit it to a specific dollar amount or to obligations that were disclosed on the Disclosure Schedule as being in default or at risk of default as of the signing date.

## Issue 4: TSA Liability Cap Inadequate for Service-Failure Risk

**Location:** TSA § 8.1; TSA Schedule A

**Explanation:** TSA § 8.1 caps Provider's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." Based on Schedule A, the fixed monthly fees total $91,000 ($38,500 + $27,000 + $14,500 + $11,000), plus per-load cold-chain rates. Over the 12-month Service Period, total fees are approximately $1.1 million to $1.5 million (assuming per-load rates comparable to the fixed fees).

The Services include laboratory testing of raw milk (antibiotic residue, somatic cell, bacteria) and cold-chain transport at or below 41°F. A failure in either service could result in:
- A contaminated product lot reaching the market (food safety liability, recall costs, regulatory action);
- Loss of organic certification (existential for the Company's business model);
- Product spoilage in transit (direct loss of inventory plus lost revenue).

The § 8.1 cap does not apply to (a) confidentiality breaches, (b) gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud, or (c) third-party claims under § 8.2 or § 8.3. However, a simple negligence-level failure to meet the Service Standard (e.g., a lab technician processes samples incorrectly but not grossly negligently) resulting in first-party losses (cost of spoiled product, lost revenue from a delayed shipment) would be subject to the ~$1.1M cap and the exclusion of consequential damages and lost profits in § 8.1.

**Assumption:** I assume the Company's monthly revenue is in the range of $5M–$10M based on the $86.5M Purchase Price (roughly 8.7x–17x revenue, typical for food processing). A single-day cold-chain failure could destroy hundreds of thousands of dollars of product.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add a super-cap or carve-out in TSA § 8.1:

> "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Provider's aggregate liability for Losses resulting from (a) any product spoilage, contamination or recall caused by Provider's failure to meet the Service Standard, or (b) any loss, suspension or revocation of the Company's organic certification resulting from Provider's breach of this Agreement, shall not be subject to the limitation set forth in the first sentence of this Section 8.1, provided that Provider's aggregate liability under this sentence shall not exceed five (5) times the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement."

Also consider adding service credits in Schedule A:

> "If Provider fails to meet the on-time performance level set forth in this Schedule A for any calendar month, Provider shall credit the Company an amount equal to 5% of the monthly Fee for the affected Service for each full percentage point by which the actual performance falls below the stated level, up to a maximum credit of 25% of the monthly Fee."

## Issue 5: No Representation as to Condition or Adequacy of Lagoon System

**Location:** SPA § 4.7; SPA § 4.8; Bill of Sale § 3.3

**Explanation:** SPA § 4.7 represents that the Company's assets "together with the Lagoon Assets and the services to be provided under the Transition Services Agreement, are sufficient for the conduct of the Company's business as currently conducted." This is a sufficiency representation, not a condition representation. It does not address:
- Whether the lagoon system is in good working order;
- Its remaining useful life;
- Whether it has adequate capacity to treat the Company's wastewater in compliance with its discharge permit;
- Whether any component is near end-of-life and will require near-term capital expenditure.

SPA § 4.8 carves out the Specified Environmental Matter and otherwise represents compliance with Environmental Laws. But if the lagoon is *currently* in compliance yet is undersized for the Company's production volumes (e.g., the Company has been scaling back production to stay within lagoon capacity), Ratel inherits a latent constraint.

Bill of Sale § 3.3 disclaims all representations about the Conveyed Assets except title (Bill of Sale § 3.1) and the SPA Article IV reps. There is no "as-is" or "working order" representation.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add to SPA Article IV:

> "**4.16 Lagoon System.** To Seller's knowledge, the Lagoon Assets are in good working order and repair, are adequate in capacity to treat the Company's process wastewater in compliance with all applicable permits and Environmental Laws, and have a remaining useful life of not less than [X] years. Schedule 4.16 identifies any Lagoon Asset that is (a) past its recommended service life, (b) the subject of a pending or recommended capital replacement, or (c) not in compliance with any applicable standard."

Alternatively, if Seller will not give a condition representation, require a pre-Closing engineering assessment of the lagoon system at Seller's cost, with the results delivered to Buyer as a condition to Closing.

## Issue 6: TSA Cold-Chain Rates Not Fixed

**Location:** TSA Schedule A, Annex A-4

**Explanation:** Annex A-4 states: "Per-load rates for each delivery lane are as agreed between the Service Coordinators on the Closing Date. They equal the rates charged to the Company for the same lanes in the month of March 2026."

This creates three problems:
1. **No fixed schedule.** If the Service Coordinators cannot agree on the Closing Date (or if one party's coordinator is unavailable), there is no fallback rate. The TSA is silent on what happens in that event.
2. **Reference to March 2026 rates.** These rates are not attached to the TSA. If a dispute arises about what the March 2026 rates were, the Company would need to produce its own records, and Provider could assert different numbers.
3. **No cap on rate increases during the Service Period.** The TSA does not provide that per-load rates are fixed for the duration of the Service Period. Provider could argue that rates should be adjusted for fuel, inflation, or other factors.

**Recommended Fix:**

Replace Annex A-4 with a fixed rate schedule:

> "Annex A-4 (Per-Load Rates). The per-load rates for each delivery lane are set forth in the table below and shall remain fixed for the duration of the Service Period (including any extension under Section 3.2):
>
> | Lane | Origin | Destination | Per-Load Rate |
> |------|--------|-------------|---------------|
> | 1 | St. Albans Plant | [DC 1] | $[X] |
> | 2 | St. Albans Plant | [DC 2] | $[X] |
> | ... | ... | ... | ... |
>
> Fuel surcharges shall be calculated at the Provider's actual cost, documented with carrier invoices, and shall not exceed [X]% of the base per-load rate in any month."

If the parties cannot agree on specific rates before signing, add: "If the Service Coordinators are unable to agree on per-load rates on the Closing Date, the rates set forth in Provider's invoice to the Company for the month of March 2026 (a copy of which Provider shall deliver to the Company at least five (5) Business Days before the Closing Date) shall apply."

## Issue 7: No Security for Seller's Indemnification Obligations

**Location:** SPA Article IX generally; SPA § 2.2

**Explanation:** The Purchase Price is paid in full at Closing (less debt payoff and working capital adjustment). There is no escrow, holdback, letter of credit, or other security for Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX. The Specified Environmental Matter indemnity (§ 9.2(c)) has a seven-year tail. The Company Guaranties could be triggered at any time. If Brilal Foods Group experiences financial deterioration, merges into another entity, or becomes insolvent within the survival period, Ratel's indemnification claims become unsecured general claims against a potentially empty shell.

**Assumption:** I have not seen Brilal's financial statements or credit information. I assume Brilal is a mid-cap food company that could experience financial stress in the 5–7 year environmental tail period.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add an escrow provision to Article IX:

> "**9.8 Escrow.** At the Closing, [X]% of the Purchase Price (the "Escrow Amount") shall be deposited with [Escrow Agent] pursuant to an escrow agreement in the form attached as Exhibit C. The Escrow Amount shall be held for the benefit of the Buyer Indemnitees as security for Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX. The Escrow Amount shall be released to Seller as follows: (a) [X]% on the first anniversary of the Closing Date; (b) [X]% on the third anniversary of the Closing Date; and (c) the remainder on the fifth anniversary of the Closing Date, in each case less any amounts then subject to pending claims. The Escrow Amount shall not be available to satisfy claims under Section 9.2(c) (Specified Environmental Matter) until the seventh anniversary of the Closing Date."

A typical escrow for a deal of this size might be 10%–15% of the Purchase Price ($8.65M–$13M), released over 3–5 years, with the environmental portion held for 7 years.

## Issue 8: Asymmetric Assignment Rights

**Location:** SPA § 12.4

**Explanation:** "Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of Seller, and any purported assignment without that consent shall be void. Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the consent of Buyer."

This is problematic for Ratel in two scenarios:
1. **Ratel restructuring.** If Ratel wishes to move the Sunfold shares to a newly formed subsidiary (e.g., for tax, ring-fencing, or financing purposes), it needs Seller's consent. Seller could extract a fee or refuse.
2. **Seller assignment.** Seller could assign its indemnification obligations (or the benefit of the Purchase Price) to an affiliate or a special purpose vehicle with less substance, making Ratel's post-Closing claims harder to collect.

**Recommended Fix:**

Amend § 12.4:

> "Buyer may assign this Agreement to an Affiliate of Buyer without Seller's consent, provided that Buyer remains secondarily liable for its obligations hereunder. Buyer may not otherwise assign this Agreement without Seller's prior written consent. Seller may assign this Agreement only to an Affiliate of Seller that is a corporation or limited liability company with total assets of not less than $[X] and only with prior written notice to Buyer. Any assignment by Seller shall not relieve Seller of its obligations under this Agreement."

## Issue 9: TSA Force Majeure Includes "Animal Disease Outbreak"

**Location:** TSA § 11.3

**Explanation:** The force majeure clause lists "animal disease outbreak" as an excusing event. For a dairy processing company, an animal disease outbreak (e.g., H5N1 avian influenza affecting feed costs, or a bovine disease affecting raw milk supply) is a realistic and recurring risk in New England. If such an event occurs, Provider could suspend laboratory testing, cold-chain transport, or organic compliance support for up to 30 days before the Company can terminate.

During that 30-day window, the Company could be operating without:
- Raw milk testing (shipping untested milk, risking contamination);
- Cold-chain transport (product quality degradation);
- Organic compliance support (missing certifying agent deadlines).

**Recommended Fix:**

Narrow the force majeure clause:

> "Neither Party shall be liable for any delay or failure to perform under this Agreement (other than payment obligations) caused by events beyond its reasonable control, including acts of God, fire, flood, severe weather, epidemic affecting human populations, labor disputes not involving its own employees, or acts of a Governmental Authority. 'Animal disease outbreak' shall constitute a force majeure event only to the extent it results in a Governmental Authority order that specifically prohibits Provider from performing the affected Service. Provider shall use commercially reasonable efforts to provide the affected Service through alternative means (including subcontractors) during any force majeure event."

Also add: "If a force majeure event prevents performance of any Service for more than ten (10) consecutive days, the Company may terminate that Service on written notice without penalty."

## Issue 10: No Service Credits or Remedies for Chronic Underperformance

**Location:** TSA Schedule A (Performance Level column); TSA § 9.2

**Explanation:** Schedule A specifies performance levels (e.g., "99% on-time" for raw milk testing, "97% on-time delivery" for cold chain), but the TSA provides no consequence for missing them. The only termination right for underperformance is the general material breach provision in § 9.2, which requires a 30-day cure period. A service that consistently delivers at 95% instead of 99% may not rise to the level of "material breach" in Provider's view, and the Company would be stuck for the remainder of the Service Period.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add to TSA Section 5:

> "**5.5 Service Credits.** If Provider fails to meet the performance levels set forth in Schedule A for any calendar month, Provider shall issue the Company a credit equal to [5]% of the monthly Fee for the affected Service for each full percentage point by which actual performance falls below the stated level, up to a maximum of [25]% of the monthly Fee for that month. If Provider fails to meet the performance level for three (3) consecutive months with respect to any Service, the Company may terminate that Service on ten (10) days' written notice without penalty, and Provider shall refund any prepaids for the terminated Service."

## Issue 11: Seller Controls Environmental Remediation for Seven Years

**Location:** SPA § 9.4(c); SPA § 9.1 (7-year survival for § 9.2(c) claims)

**Explanation:** "Seller shall control the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter, in consultation with Buyer, using qualified environmental consultants and in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with operations at the St. Albans Plant."

For up to seven years post-Closing, Seller (a party that no longer owns the Company, no longer operates the plant, and whose economic interest is minimizing its indemnification exposure) controls the scope, timing, and method of environmental remediation at Ratel's facility. The "in consultation with Buyer" language is weak—it does not give Buyer a veto. The "does not unreasonably interfere" standard is a high bar for Ratel to meet if Seller chooses a cheaper, slower remediation approach.

**Recommended Fix:**

Amend § 9.4(c):

> "Seller shall control the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter; **provided that** (a) Buyer shall have the right to approve or disapprove (such disapproval not to be unreasonably withheld) the remediation plan, the selection of environmental consultants, and the schedule for implementation; (b) if Buyer disapproves of any element, the Parties shall attempt in good faith to resolve the disagreement, and if unresolved, Buyer may perform or cause to be performed the disputed element and charge the cost to Seller under this Section 9.4(c); and (c) Seller shall complete all remediation within [X] months after the Closing Date unless extended by mutual agreement or as required by a Governmental Authority."

## Issue 12: TSA Extension Can Be Unreasonably Refused

**Location:** TSA § 3.2

**Explanation:** The Company may request a three-month extension, and Provider "shall not unreasonably withhold its agreement." But what constitutes "unreasonable" is undefined. If the Company is in the middle of qualifying a new lab or negotiating a new logistics contract and needs six more weeks, Provider could argue that a 90-day extension is unreasonable (too short to be useful, or too long to commit). There is no automatic right.

**Recommended Fix:**

Amend § 3.2:

> "The Company may, by written notice delivered at least 60 days before the end of the Service Period, extend the Service Period for any Service for up to three (3) additional months. Provider shall agree to such extension unless Provider demonstrates that it is unable to provide the Service during the extension period due to circumstances beyond its reasonable control. During any extension, the Fees for the extended Service shall increase by 5%."

This shifts the burden to Provider to justify refusal.

## Issue 13: Non-Compete Limited to "Organic" Products

**Location:** SPA § 6.8(a)

**Explanation:** The non-compete prohibits Seller from owning, operating, or managing "any business that processes or sells **organic** fluid milk, yogurt or cheese" in seven states. If Brilal (or an Affiliate) launches a conventional (non-organic) dairy brand in Vermont or New Hampshire, it is not restricted. Given that the Company's competitive advantage is its organic certification and its relationships with organic dairy farmers, a conventional product line from Seller could:
- Undercut the Company on price;
- Solicit the same retail customers with a cheaper alternative;
- Poach the Company's non-organic supplier relationships (the non-solicit in § 6.8(b) covers "any supplier of raw milk," which helps, but only for three years).

**Assumption:** I assume Brilal has the capability and potential interest to compete in conventional dairy, given that it is a "Foods Group" with presumably multiple product lines.

**Recommended Fix:**

Broaden § 6.8(a):

> "For three years after the Closing Date, Seller shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, directly or indirectly (a) own, operate or manage any business that processes or sells fluid milk, yogurt or cheese (whether organic or conventional) in the States of Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island or New York, or (b) solicit any employee of the Company or any supplier of raw milk to the Company to terminate or adversely modify its relationship with the Company."

If Seller resists, compromise by adding: "or any business that processes or sells dairy products using raw milk sourced from farms located in the States of Vermont, New Hampshire or Maine."

## Issue 14: TSA Does Not Provide for Data Migration or IP Transfer at End of Service Period

**Location:** TSA § 6.4; TSA § 6.3

**Explanation:** TSA § 6.4 requires Provider to give "reasonable assistance in transitioning each Service to the Company or to a replacement provider, including the transfer of test methods, organic system plan documentation and carrier contact information" during the last 90 days. TSA § 6.3 requires Provider to deliver records "at the end of the Service Period."

However, there is no obligation to:
- Transfer or reproduce the Company's data in Provider's systems (e.g., laboratory information management system data, cold-chain tracking data) in a usable format;
- Provide training to the Company's personnel or replacement provider;
- Cooperate with the Company's new service provider during a transition overlap period.

If the Company's new lab needs historical test data to establish baselines, or if the new logistics provider needs route optimization data, Provider has no affirmative obligation to provide these in a usable format.

**Recommended Fix:**

Add to TSA § 6.4:

> "During the last 90 days of the Service Period, Provider shall (a) make available to the Company or its designee all data, records and information in Provider's possession relating to the Services, in a format reasonably requested by the Company (including electronic, machine-readable formats); (b) provide up to [40] hours of training to the Company's personnel or its replacement provider's personnel on the systems, methods and procedures used in performing the Services; and (c) cooperate with the Company's replacement provider during an overlap period of up to [30] days following the end of the Service Period, for which the Company shall pay Provider at Provider's then-current hourly professional rates."
