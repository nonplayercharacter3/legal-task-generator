## Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA), Transition Services Agreement (TSA), Brilal Board Consent, Bill of Sale for the Lagoon Assets, and the February 2026 prior draft of SPA Article IX. Below are the issues I have identified, ranked by significance to Ratel. ## Issue 1: Kettle Ridge Raw Milk Supply Agreement – Consent Sought Only Post-Closing

**Location:** Brilal Board Consent, Recital 1.6 and Resolution 4; SPA Schedule 4.5 (omission); SPA §4.5.

**Facts.** Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative supplies approximately 40% of the Company's raw milk. The Raw Milk Supply Agreement requires Kettle Ridge's prior written consent to a change in control; absent consent, Kettle Ridge may terminate on 30 days' notice. The Brilal Board Consent (Recital 1.6) acknowledges this, and Resolution 4 authorizes Brilal's officers to "request such consent following the Closing, in cooperation with the Buyer and the Company." Critically, the Kettle Ridge consent is **not** listed on Schedule 4.5 to the SPA, is not a condition to Closing under §7.2(d), and is not addressed in the SPA's consent representation (§4.5).

**Why it matters to Ratel.** Losing 40% of raw milk supply on 30 days' notice would be existential for a dairy processor. At an $86.5M purchase price, the business case almost certainly depends on continued access to Kettle Ridge's organic raw milk. If Kettle Ridge withholds consent or terminates post-closing, Ratel would own a plant without sufficient feedstock, with no contractual recourse against Brilal because the SPA does not treat this as a closing condition, a represented consent, or an indemnified matter. The Board Consent's language—"request such consent following the Closing"—makes clear that Brilal does not consider this a pre-closing obligation.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Add the Kettle Ridge consent to Schedule 4.5** and make it a condition to Buyer's closing obligation under §7.2(d). Proposed amendment to Schedule 4.5:

   > 4. Consent of Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative to the change in control of the Company under the Raw Milk Supply Agreement dated [●].

2. **Add a specific representation** in Article IV. Proposed new §4.15:

   > **4.15 Raw Milk Supply.** Schedule 4.15 sets forth the Raw Milk Supply Agreement between the Company and Kettle Ridge Organic Dairy Cooperative. The Company is not in material breach of the Raw Milk Supply Agreement, and no event has occurred that, with notice or the passage of time, would constitute a breach or give Kettle Ridge the right to terminate. To Seller's knowledge, no dispute exists between the Company and Kettle Ridge that could reasonably be expected to affect Kettle Ridge's willingness to consent to the change in control.

3. **Add a post-closing covenant** requiring Brilal to use best efforts (not merely "commercially reasonable efforts") to obtain Kettle Ridge's consent, with a specific timeline. Proposed new §6.12:

   > **6.12 Kettle Ridge Consent.** Seller shall use best efforts to obtain Kettle Ridge's written consent to the change in control under the Raw Milk Supply Agreement within 30 days after the Closing Date. Seller shall cooperate fully with Buyer and the Company in any discussions with Kettle Ridge, including providing Kettle Ridge with financial information about Buyer reasonably requested. If Kettle Ridge withholds consent or terminates the Raw Milk Supply Agreement as a result of the change in control, Seller shall indemnify Buyer and the Company for all resulting Losses, without limitation under Section 9.5.

4. **If the seller resists a condition to closing**, at minimum negotiate a specific indemnity (carved out from the Cap and Deductible) for loss of the Kettle Ridge supply relationship attributable to the change in control.

**Assumption:** I am assuming the Raw Milk Supply Agreement is a material contract that should appear on Schedule 4.6. If it does not, that is a separate gap. I am also assuming that 40% of raw milk supply is correct as stated in the Board Consent and that no alternative supply arrangements are in place.

## Issue 2: Bill of Sale Section 4.1 Caps Environmental Liability – Directly Contradicts SPA

**Location:** Bill of Sale §4.1; SPA §9.5(b)(iii); SPA §12.8.

**Facts.** Bill of Sale §4.1 provides:

> "All Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, **including any Environmental Liability**, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, **and subject to the Cap**."

The SPA, by contrast, provides in §9.5(b)(iii) that the Cap does **not** apply to "the Specified Environmental Matter, whether claimed under Section 9.2(a) or Section 9.2(c)." The Specified Environmental Matter is defined as "contamination originating from the St. Albans Plant wastewater lagoon system" (SPA §1.1). The Lagoon Assets (the "Conveyed Assets") are the wastewater lagoon system itself.

SPA §12.8 provides: "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, the Ancillary Agreement shall control as to its subject matter." The Bill of Sale is an Ancillary Agreement (SPA §1.1). Its subject matter is the Lagoon Assets. Section 4.1 is squarely within that subject matter.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** As drafted, a court could hold that the Bill of Sale's cap provision controls over the SPA's uncapped treatment for all environmental losses related to the lagoon. This would limit Ratel's recovery for the Specified Environmental Matter to the Cap ($8,650,000 = 10% of the Purchase Price), despite the parties' apparent intent (as reflected in both the SPA and the February draft) to leave environmental liability uncapped. Given that the lagoon is a known contamination source (the entire reason for the "Specified Environmental Matter" carve-out from the environmental representation in §4.8), remediation costs could substantially exceed $8.65M.

**Recommendation.** Delete the phrase "and subject to the Cap" from Bill of Sale §4.1. Revised §4.1:

> "All Losses arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or the underlying real property on which the Conveyed Assets are located, including any Environmental Liability, shall be recoverable by the Company, Buyer and the other Buyer Indemnitees solely under, and in accordance with the procedures of, Article IX of the Purchase Agreement. For the avoidance of doubt, Losses arising from the Specified Environmental Matter shall not be subject to the Deductible or the Cap pursuant to Section 9.5(b)(iii) of the Purchase Agreement."

Alternatively, add a clarifying sentence to SPA §9.5(b)(iii): "For the avoidance of doubt, the foregoing exclusion from the Cap applies regardless of any provision in any Ancillary Agreement to the contrary."

## Issue 3: Unlimited Buyer Indemnity for Company Guaranties – Material Deterioration from February Draft

**Location:** SPA §9.3(b) and §9.5; compare spa_article_ix_prior_draft_feb2026.md §9.3(b) and §9.5(a)(iii).

**Facts.** The final SPA §9.3(b) provides that Buyer indemnifies Seller for:

> "the Company Guaranties, including any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, **whether arising before or after the Closing, and notwithstanding Section 9.5**."

The February 2026 prior draft §9.3(b) was materially narrower:

> "any payment or performance by Seller or any of its Affiliates under a Company Guaranty, **to the extent the payment or performance relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises and is attributable to the period after the Closing**."

The final version (a) eliminates the "post-Closing only" limitation, (b) adds "other obligation" (broader than "payment or performance"), and (c) adds "notwithstanding Section 9.5," which expressly removes the Cap ($8.65M) and the Deductible. The Company Guaranties (Schedule 1.1(a)) cover the St. Albans Plant lease and packaging/ingredient supply contracts.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** The St. Albans Plant lease is the Company's primary facility. If the lease is long-term (e.g., 10+ years) and the Company underperforms or defaults, Brilal's guaranty could be called for a substantial amount. Because the indemnity is "notwithstanding Section 9.5," Ratel's exposure is theoretically unlimited. The pre-closing obligations being swept in means that even if the Company was already in technical default on a supply contract before closing (and Brilal's guaranty was called), Ratel would indemnify Brilal for it.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Restore the temporal limitation** from the February draft. Revise §9.3(b) to:

   > "the Company Guaranties, including any payment, performance or other obligation of Seller or any of its Affiliates under any Company Guaranty, **solely to the extent such obligation relates to an obligation of the Company that first arises after the Closing Date**."

2. **Remove "notwithstanding Section 9.5"** so that the Cap applies. If the seller insists on an uncapped guaranty indemnity, limit it to post-closing obligations only and cap it at a stated amount (e.g., the remaining lease obligations or a fixed dollar figure).

3. **Add a release of pre-closing guaranty obligations.** New sentence in §9.3(b): "Seller's obligations under the Company Guaranties in respect of any default or obligation of the Company arising before the Closing Date shall be released and of no further effect as of the Closing Date, and Buyer shall have no indemnification obligation in respect thereof."

4. **Require Brilal to seek release of the Company Guaranties at or before Closing.** Add to §3.2(a) a new deliverable: "written releases of the Company Guaranties from the landlord and the applicable supply contract counterparties, or evidence that such guaranties have been terminated." If releases are not obtained, negotiate a reduction in the Purchase Price or an escrow to cover the guaranty exposure.

## Issue 4: TSA Confidentiality Provision Is Materially Weaker Than SPA

**Location:** TSA §7.1; compare SPA §1.1 (definition of "Company Confidential Information") and SPA §6.7.

**Facts.** TSA §7.1 defines "Confidential Information" as information "that is **marked as confidential at the time of disclosure**." The SPA's definition of "Company Confidential Information" (§1.1) covers "all non-public information concerning the Company or its business, in any form and **whether or not marked or identified as confidential**," and expressly includes "customer lists and customer order data, farm supplier identities and pricing, herd records, product formulas and recipes, organic certification files, laboratory testing data, production and logistics data, and financial information."

During the 12-month (potentially 15-month) TSA period, Brilal's personnel will have ongoing access to all of this information in performing lab testing, organic compliance, and logistics services. TSA §7.3 states that "Provider's obligations with respect to information of the Company received by Provider or Provider Personnel in performing the Services are governed exclusively by this Section 7."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** If Brilal's personnel receive customer order data, supplier pricing, or product formulas during the TSA period and that information is not marked "confidential" (which is unlikely in the course of routine service delivery—test results are delivered on standard lab forms, logistics data is in shipping manifests, etc.), it falls outside the TSA's confidentiality protection. Brilal could potentially use or disclose this information without breach. The SPA's §6.7 confidentiality covenant survives closing and is broader, but TSA §7.3's "governed exclusively" language could be read to displace §6.7 for information received in the course of the Services.

**Recommendation.** Revise TSA §7.1 to align with the SPA:

> "**Confidential Information**" means any non-public business, technical, financial or operational information of the Disclosing Party disclosed to the Receiving Party in connection with this Agreement or received by the Receiving Party or its Personnel in performing the Services, in any form and whether or not marked or identified as confidential, including customer lists, customer order data, supplier identities and pricing, product formulas and recipes, organic certification files, laboratory testing data, production and logistics data, and financial information.

And add to TSA §7.3:

> "Provider's obligations with respect to Company Confidential Information (as defined in the Purchase Agreement) received by Provider or Provider Personnel in performing the Services are governed by this Section 7 **and Section 6.7 of the Purchase Agreement**, whichever provides greater protection to the Company."

## Issue 5: Seller Controls Environmental Remediation Post-Closing

**Location:** SPA §9.4(c).

**Facts.** Section 9.4(c) provides: "Seller shall control the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter, in consultation with Buyer, using qualified environmental consultants and in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with operations at the St. Albans Plant."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** After closing, Ratel owns the Company, the St. Albans Plant, and (via the Bill of Sale) the Lagoon Assets. Brilal no longer has any ownership interest in the business. Yet Brilal "controls" the remediation of the lagoon contamination. "In consultation with Buyer" is not the same as "subject to Buyer's approval" or "in a manner directed by Buyer." Brilal's economic incentive is to minimize its own remediation costs (even though the Cap doesn't apply, Brilal still bears the direct cost of the work). Brilal might: (a) select a less aggressive remediation approach to reduce cost; (b) delay remediation to spread out expenses; or (c) select consultants who are less thorough. Meanwhile, Ratel bears the operational risk—if the lagoon fails or a regulatory agency orders accelerated action, it is Ratel's plant that is disrupted.

**Recommendation.** Revise §9.4(c) to give Ratel meaningful control:

> "Seller shall conduct the investigation and remediation of the Specified Environmental Matter **under the direction of Buyer**, using environmental consultants reasonably acceptable to Buyer. Seller shall (a) provide Buyer with a remediation plan within 60 days after the Closing Date, (b) not commence any remediation activity without Buyer's prior written approval of the specific work plan and timeline, (c) provide Buyer with monthly progress reports, and (d) complete all remediation in a manner that does not unreasonably interfere with operations at the St. Albans Plant. Buyer shall have the right, upon 30 days' written notice, to assume control of the investigation and remediation if Seller fails to make material progress consistent with the approved remediation plan."

Alternatively, if Brilal resists ceding control, at minimum add: "Buyer's consultation rights include the right to approve or disapprove (not to be unreasonably withheld) the selection of environmental consultants, the remediation approach, and the timeline."

## Issue 6: TSA Cold-Chain Per-Load Rates Are Not Fixed

**Location:** TSA Schedule A, Annex A-4.

**Facts.** Annex A-4 provides: "Per-load rates for each delivery lane are as agreed between the Service Coordinators on the Closing Date. They equal the rates charged to the Company for the same lanes in the month of March 2026."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** The cold-chain logistics service (A-4) is one of the three core TSA services. Unlike the other services, which have fixed monthly fees, the cold-chain rates are to be "agreed" on the Closing Date with no stated fallback. If the Service Coordinators cannot agree on rates (e.g., if Brilal's coordinator demands higher rates than were charged in March 2026, or if there is a dispute about which lanes are included), there is no mechanism to resolve the gap. The Company could be left without a defined cost basis for a critical service on day one.

**Recommendation.** Replace Annex A-4 with a fixed schedule:

> "Per-load rates for each delivery lane are set forth in the table below and shall remain fixed for the initial Service Period. During any extension under Section 3.2, rates shall increase by 5%."

Then attach a table with lane-by-lane rates based on the actual March 2026 invoices (which Ratel should obtain in diligence). Add a fallback: "If the Service Coordinators are unable to agree on rates for a lane not listed in the table by the Closing Date, the rate for that lane shall be the rate charged to the Company for the same lane in the most recent month before the Closing Date, as evidenced by Provider's invoices."

## Issue 7: TSA Liability Cap May Be Inadequate for Product Recall Scenarios

**Location:** TSA §8.1; §8.2(c).

**Facts.** TSA §8.1 caps Provider's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." Based on Schedule A, fixed monthly fees total approximately $91,000/month ($38,500 + $27,000 + $14,500 + $11,000), or roughly $1,092,000 over 12 months, plus per-load cold-chain rates (unknown, but likely several hundred thousand dollars annually). Total Fees are therefore in the range of $1.3M–$1.5M.

Section 8.2(c) indemnifies the Company for "any product spoilage, contamination or recall caused by Provider's failure to meet the Service Standard." However, §8.1's cap applies to "the aggregate liability of Provider under this Agreement" and the carve-outs in §8.1 are limited to (a) confidentiality breach, (b) gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud, and (c) "indemnification obligations under Section 8.2 or Section 8.3 for third-party claims."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** If the Burlington lab fails to detect a pathogen (e.g., Listeria, Salmonella) in finished product, and the Company must recall product, the Company's **first-party** losses (recall logistics, destroyed product, customer credits, brand damage, lost revenue during the recall) may not be "Losses arising out of third-party claims" and thus may not fall within the §8.1(c) carve-out. They would be subject to the ~$1.3M–$1.5M cap. A single dairy product recall in the organic segment can easily exceed $5M–$10M in direct costs.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Clarify that §8.2(c) covers first-party losses, not just third-party claims.** Revise §8.2(c):

   > "(c) any product spoilage, contamination or recall caused by Provider's failure to meet the Service Standard, **including all first-party costs of the recall (including logistics, disposal, customer notifications, customer credits and lost revenue directly attributable to the recall period), whether or not such costs give rise to a third-party claim**."

2. **Carve out recall-related losses from the §8.1 cap.** Add to §8.1:

   > "The limitations in this Section 8.1 do not apply to ... (d) Provider's indemnification obligations under Section 8.2(c) for product spoilage, contamination or recall."

3. **Require Brilal to maintain product liability and recall insurance** with the Company named as an additional insured, with a minimum limit (e.g., $10M per occurrence). Add as new TSA §5.5:

   > "Provider shall maintain, at its own expense, commercial general liability and product recall insurance with limits of not less than $10,000,000 per occurrence, naming the Company and its Affiliates as additional insureds. Provider shall deliver certificates of insurance to the Company before the Closing Date and upon each renewal."

## Issue 8: Asymmetric Assignment Rights

**Location:** SPA §12.4.

**Facts.** "Buyer may not assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of Seller, and any purported assignment without that consent shall be void. **Seller may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the consent of Buyer.**"

**Why it matters to Ratel.** Ratel is a holding company (an LLC). It is common and often necessary for a buyer to restructure post-signing—for example, to transfer the acquired stock to an operating subsidiary, to effect a tax-driven reorganization, or to bring in co-investors. The current provision requires Brilal's consent for any such transfer. Meanwhile, Brilal could assign its indemnification obligations (or its rights under the TSA) to an affiliate or even a third party without Ratel's consent, potentially leaving Ratel looking to an entity with less creditworthiness for post-closing indemnity payments.

**Recommendation.** Revise §12.4:

> "Buyer may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it to an Affiliate of Buyer without the consent of Seller, provided that Buyer remains liable for the performance of all obligations under this Agreement. Buyer may not otherwise assign this Agreement without Seller's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld). Seller may not assign this Agreement or any of its obligations under it (including its indemnification obligations under Article IX) without the prior written consent of Buyer."

## Issue 9: TSA Force Majeure Includes "Animal Disease Outbreak"

**Location:** TSA §11.3.

**Facts.** Force majeure events include "animal disease outbreak." If a force majeure event prevents performance of any Service for more than 30 days, the Company may terminate that Service.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** In the dairy industry, animal disease outbreaks (e.g., highly pathogenic avian influenza affecting the region, bovine tuberculosis, or a feed-contamination event) are not hypothetical—they are periodic and can disrupt supply chains for months. If an outbreak occurs, Brilal could invoke force majeure to suspend lab testing or cold-chain logistics. The Company's only remedy is to terminate the affected Service after 30 days—but termination does not provide the service; it merely ends the contractual relationship. Ratel would then need to find a replacement provider on an emergency basis.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Narrow the force majeure provision** to exclude events that do not physically prevent performance. Add: "Force majeure shall not apply to the extent the affected Service can be performed by alternative means (including use of alternative laboratories, carriers or routes) without material additional cost to Provider."

2. **Add a continuity obligation.** New sentence: "During any force majeure event, Provider shall use commercially reasonable efforts to maintain the minimum level of Service necessary to prevent product spoilage, regulatory non-compliance or loss of organic certification, and shall provide the Company with a recovery plan within five Business Days."

3. **Shorten the termination trigger** from 30 days to 14 days for the Laboratory Testing Services and Organic Certification Compliance Support, given the regulatory urgency of those services.

## Issue 10: General Survival Period of 18 Months May Be Insufficient

**Location:** SPA §9.1.

**Facts.** General representations and warranties survive 18 months after the Closing Date. The Closing Date is April 30, 2026, so general reps expire approximately October 30, 2027.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** In a dairy processing business, certain issues may not manifest within 18 months:
- **Customer contract disputes:** A material customer might claim a quality issue or seek to terminate based on a pre-closing event that only becomes apparent after a full seasonal cycle (e.g., a summer production issue that affects fall deliveries).
- **Employee benefit plan liabilities:** ERISA or wage-and-hour claims can have long detection periods.
- **Permit compliance:** A regulatory agency might issue a notice of violation for a pre-closing period more than 18 months after closing.

The 18-month period is shorter than the 24-month period that is common in mid-market deals of this size.

**Recommendation.** Extend general survival to 24 months. Revise §9.1:

> "The representations and warranties in this Agreement shall survive the Closing until **24 months** after the Closing Date; provided that ..."

If the seller resists, at minimum extend survival for §4.6 (Material Contracts) and §4.10 (Compliance with Laws; Permits) to 24 months, as these are the reps most likely to have delayed manifestation.

## Issue 11: TSA Migration Assistance Period May Be Too Short

**Location:** TSA §6.4.

**Facts.** "During the last 90 days of the Service Period, Provider shall provide the Company with reasonable assistance in transitioning each Service to the Company or to a replacement provider, including the transfer of test methods, organic system plan documentation and carrier contact information."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** Transitioning a USDA-accredited organic compliance function requires notifying the certifying agent, potentially undergoing a new inspection, and establishing new recordkeeping systems. Transitioning a dairy lab requires the replacement lab to be accredited by the Vermont Agency of Agriculture, Food and Markets (or equivalent) and validated for the specific test methods used. Transitioning cold-chain logistics requires qualifying new carriers for food-grade transport and establishing new route schedules. Ninety days of "reasonable assistance" from the incumbent may not be sufficient if Ratel is starting the search late in the TSA period.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Extend the migration assistance period to the last 120 days** of the Service Period.
2. **Add specific deliverables.** Revise §6.4:

   > "During the last 120 days of the Service Period, Provider shall: (a) provide the Company with a complete organic system plan file, including all supplier certificates, transaction records and inspection reports; (b) provide all test methods, validation data and quality control records used in the Laboratory Testing Services; (c) provide all carrier contracts, route schedules, temperature monitoring protocols and customer delivery specifications used in the Cold-Chain Logistics Services; (d) introduce the Company to its certifying agent, laboratory accreditation body and key carriers; and (e) make Provider Personnel available for up to ten (10) hours per week to answer questions and facilitate the transition."

3. **Add a covenant in the SPA** requiring Ratel to begin the transition process no later than 6 months after Closing. (This protects Brilal and makes the migration assistance provision more enforceable.)

## Issue 12: TSA "Same Degree of Care" Standard Is Relative and Potentially Weak

**Location:** TSA §5.1(a).

**Facts.** Provider shall perform the Services "with at least the same degree of care, skill, diligence and priority as it exercised in providing the same services to the Company during the 12 months before the Closing Date."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** This is a relative standard benchmarked to Brilal's own prior performance. If Brilal's lab was already missing the 4-hour turnaround on some raw milk tests, or if its cold-chain delivery was already below 97% on-time, the "same degree of care" standard would not require improvement. The Company would be locked into sub-par performance for 12 months with no contractual basis to demand better.

**Recommendation.** Add an absolute floor to §5.1:

> "In no event shall Provider's performance fall below the performance levels set forth in Schedule A, regardless of Provider's historical performance to the Company."

This ensures the Schedule A metrics (99% on-time for raw milk testing, 98% for finished product, 97% on-time delivery, etc.) are the minimum standard, not just a target.

## Issue 13: SPA Section 4.7 "Sufficiency of Assets" Representation Is Forward-Looking and Limited

**Location:** SPA §4.7.

**Facts.** "The assets of the Company, together with the Lagoon Assets and the services to be provided under the Transition Services Agreement, are sufficient for the conduct of the Company's business as currently conducted."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** This representation is true only for the duration of the TSA (12–15 months). After the TSA expires, the Company must have its own lab, organic compliance function, and cold-chain logistics. The representation gives Ratel no protection if, after the TSA ends, it discovers that the Company's own assets are insufficient (e.g., the plant lacks in-house lab capability, or the organic certification requires a compliance officer that the Company does not employ). More importantly, this is a representation by Seller, but the practical risk falls on Ratel: if the business cannot operate without the TSA services and Ratel has not secured replacements by month 12–15, it faces a business disruption with no indemnity hook (the TSA services were "provided" as represented).

**Recommendation.** This is more of a business/diligence issue than a drafting fix, but I would add a sentence to §4.7:

> "Seller has disclosed to Buyer all assets, personnel, systems and third-party relationships that the Company requires to conduct its business independently of Seller and its Affiliates after the expiration of the Transition Services Agreement, and Buyer has had the opportunity to evaluate the Company's ability to operate without the services described in the Transition Services Agreement."

This creates a record that Seller disclosed the transition requirements and gives Ratel a potential rep breach claim if Seller knew of a critical dependency it failed to disclose.

## Issue 14: TSA Section 2.3 "Additional Services" Provision Is Too Weak

**Location:** TSA §2.3.

**Facts.** If the Company identifies a service provided pre-closing that is not in Schedule A, "Provider shall consider the request in good faith." Agreement requires a "written amendment to Schedule A signed by both Parties."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** "Consider in good faith" is not an obligation to provide the service. If Ratel discovers post-closing that Brilal was providing an additional service (e.g., a specific type of environmental monitoring, a regulatory reporting function, or an IT support service) that is not in Schedule A, Brilal can decline to provide it and Ratel has no remedy. Given that the SPA §4.7 represents that the Company's assets plus TSA services are "sufficient for the conduct of the Company's business as currently conducted," any undisclosed service that is actually necessary creates a gap.

**Recommendation.** Revise §2.3:

> "If the Company identifies a service that Provider or its Affiliates provided to the Company during the 12 months before the Closing Date and that is not included in Schedule A, Provider **shall provide such service on the same terms and at the same cost as provided before the Closing Date** for the remainder of the Service Period, unless Provider demonstrates in writing that providing the service would require material additional cost or resources not available to Provider. Any agreed additional service shall be set out in a written amendment to Schedule A."

## Issue 15: No Escrow or Holdback for Indemnification Obligations

**Location:** SPA Article IX (general); SPA §2.2 (Payment).

**Facts.** The Purchase Price is paid in full at Closing (less debt payoff and working capital adjustment). There is no escrow, holdback or security for Seller's indemnification obligations under Article IX.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** Seller's indemnity obligations include the Specified Environmental Matter (uncapped, 7-year survival), general rep breaches (capped at $8.65M, 18-month survival), and covenant breaches. If Brilal experiences financial deterioration post-closing (it is selling a business unit, which could affect its own liquidity), Ratel's indemnity recovery could be impaired. An $86.5M purchase price with no security for indemnity is a significant credit risk.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate an escrow or holdback of a portion of the Purchase Price (e.g., 10% = $8.65M, matching the Cap) to be held for 18 months (the general survival period) and released pro rata as the survival period expires. For the Specified Environmental Matter, consider requiring Brilal to maintain a pollution legal liability insurance policy or an environmental remediation trust for the 7-year survival period.

If the seller resists an escrow, at minimum require:
- A representation that Seller will maintain net worth of not less than $[●] through the survival period; or
- A parent guaranty of Seller's indemnification obligations (if Brilal has a creditworthy parent); or
- A right of setoff against any amounts payable by Buyer to Seller under the TSA.

## Issue 16: Working Capital Adjustment – Estimated Shortfall Reduces Closing Payment But Final True-Up Is Asymmetric

**Location:** SPA §2.3.

**Facts.** Under §2.3(a), if Estimated Working Capital is below the $9.2M target, the shortfall reduces the amount payable at Closing. Under §2.3(c), if final working capital is below the estimate, Seller pays the deficiency to Buyer "within five Business Days after final determination." The final determination could occur up to 90 days (Buyer's statement) + 45 days (Seller's objection period) + 30 days (dispute resolution) = 165 days post-closing, plus the accounting firm's determination time.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** The asymmetry is that Ratel gets the benefit of the Estimated Working Capital Shortfall at Closing (reducing its cash outlay), but if the final working capital is higher than estimated, Ratel must pay the excess to Seller. The risk is that Ratel's post-closing working capital management decisions (e.g., building inventory for a new product line, extending receivables to a new customer) could artificially inflate the "actual" working capital as of the Closing Date if the measurement is done carelessly. More practically, the 165-day+ timeline for final determination means Ratel is in a state of uncertainty about its final purchase price for over five months.

**Recommendation.** This is a lower-priority item, but I would:
1. Confirm that Schedule 2.3 (the working capital calculation principles) is attached and that Ratel's finance team has reviewed it.
2. Add a provision that the 90-day period for Buyer to deliver the statement runs from the later of the Closing Date or the date Buyer receives all books and records necessary to prepare the statement (to protect against Seller withholding information).
3. Consider shortening the objection period from 45 days to 30 days.

## Issue 17: SPA Section 6.11 – Buyer Must Retain Pre-Closing Records for 7 Years and Provide Access to Seller

**Location:** SPA §6.11.

**Facts.** "For seven years after the Closing, Buyer shall cause the Company to retain the books and records of the Company relating to periods before the Closing and to afford Seller reasonable access to them for legitimate business purposes."

**Why it matters to Ratel.** This is a long-term operational burden. "Legitimate business purposes" is undefined and could be broadly interpreted by Brilal. For a company handling organic certification records, lab data, and customer information, giving a former owner (and now competitor, subject to the non-compete) access to seven years of records creates confidentiality and competitive risk. The non-compete in §6.8 only covers organic dairy in seven states for three years; after that, Brilal could be a competitor with access to Ratel's historical records.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Limit the retention period to the extent required by Law** (e.g., tax records for 7 years, but operational records for 3 years).
2. **Narrow "legitimate business purposes"** to: "for purposes of defending or enforcing indemnification claims under Article IX, complying with Tax Law, or as required by Governmental Authority."
3. **Add a confidentiality condition:** "Any access by Seller to such records shall be subject to the confidentiality obligations in Section 6.7, and Seller shall not use any pre-Closing records for any competitive purpose."

## Issue 18: TSA Does Not Address Data/IT Transition

**Location:** TSA Schedule A (omission); TSA §6.4 (partial).

**Facts.** The TSA covers laboratory testing, organic compliance support, and cold-chain logistics. There is no service for IT systems, data migration, ERP transition, or digital infrastructure. The migration assistance in §6.4 covers "test methods, organic system plan documentation and carrier contact information" but not IT systems.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** If the Company's operations (production scheduling, quality management, financial systems, customer ordering) run on IT infrastructure that Brilal currently hosts or manages, and this is not addressed in the TSA, Ratel could face an IT gap at the end of the TSA period. I am making the assumption here that some IT dependency on Brilal exists, given that Brilal was providing the operational services. If the Company has fully independent IT, this issue is moot.

**Recommendation.** Confirm in diligence whether the Company has independent IT infrastructure. If not, either (a) add an IT transition service to the TSA, or (b) add a covenant in the SPA requiring Brilal to cooperate with IT migration at no additional cost during the TSA period.

## Issue 19: Non-Compete Does Not Cover Non-Organic Dairy or Adjacent Products

**Location:** SPA §6.8.

**Facts.** The non-compete prohibits Brilal from "own[ing], operat[ing] or manag[ing] any business that processes or sells **organic** fluid milk, yogurt or cheese" in seven states for three years.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** The restriction is limited to *organic* products. If Brilal (or an affiliate) launches a conventional (non-organic) dairy brand in the same seven states, it could compete directly with the Company for the same retail shelf space, distribution channels and customer relationships. The Company's customers (grocery chains, distributors) likely carry both organic and conventional products. A Brilal conventional brand could undercut the Company on price and divert customer attention.

Additionally, the non-compete does not cover: (a) dairy ingredients (e.g., whey, casein, cream); (b) non-dairy plant-based alternatives; or (c) the sale of raw milk to other processors (which could divert the Company's supplier base, though the non-solicit in §6.8(b) partially addresses this).

**Recommendation.** Broaden §6.8(a) to:

> "own, operate or manage any business that processes or sells **dairy products (whether organic or conventional), including fluid milk, yogurt, cheese, cream, butter, whey or casein products**, in the States of Vermont, New Hampshire, Maine, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Rhode Island or New York."

If the seller resists, at minimum add: "or any business that markets dairy products under a brand name that is confusingly similar to any brand name used by the Company."

## Issue 20: No Specific Protection for Organic Certification Continuity

**Location:** SPA §4.9; TSA §2.1(b); Schedule 4.5, item 2.

**Facts.** The SPA represents that the Company holds current organic handler certification (§4.9). Schedule 4.5 requires "notice to the Company's USDA-accredited organic certifying agent of the change in ownership." The TSA provides organic compliance support for 12 months. However, there is no specific covenant requiring Brilal to take affirmative steps to ensure the certification is not interrupted, no representation that the certifying agent has confirmed it will continue the certification post-closing, and no indemnity specifically tied to loss of organic certification.

**Why it matters to Ratel.** Organic certification is the core value proposition of the business. If the certification is suspended or revoked (even temporarily) due to a transition issue—e.g., the certifying agent requires a new on-site inspection, or there is a gap in the organic system plan during the handoff from Brilal's compliance team to Ratel's—the Company could not sell product as organic. This would be a material adverse effect on the business. The MAE condition (§7.2(e)) only protects pre-closing; post-closing, Ratel would need to rely on the §4.9 rep (18-month survival) or the TSA service standard.

**Recommendation.**

1. **Add a specific post-closing covenant** in the SPA:

   > "**6.13 Organic Certification.** Seller shall cooperate with the Company and its organic certifying agent to ensure the uninterrupted continuation of the Company's organic handler certification following the Closing. Seller shall (a) provide the certifying agent with all information reasonably requested in connection with the change in ownership, (b) make available any pre-Closing organic compliance records requested by the certifying agent, and (c) if requested by the certifying agent, make Seller personnel available for transition interviews or site visits during the first 90 days after the Closing Date."

2. **Add a specific indemnity** (carved out from the Cap):

   > "Seller shall indemnify the Buyer Indemnitees for all Losses arising from any suspension, revocation or failure to renew the Company's organic handler certification that is attributable to any act or omission of Seller or its Affiliates before or within 90 days after the Closing Date."
