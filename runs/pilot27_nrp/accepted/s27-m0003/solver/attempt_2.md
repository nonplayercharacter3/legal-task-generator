## 1. Survival Period for Non-Fundamental Representations Is Only ~5 Months Post-Closing

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 9.1(a)

**Issue.** Section 9.1(a) provides that non-Fundamental Representations survive "until the date that is twelve (12) months after the date of this Agreement." The SPA is dated March 2, 2026; the Scheduled Closing Date is September 30, 2026 (§ 2.4(a)). The survival period therefore expires on March 2, 2027—only approximately five months after Closing.

**Why it matters.** Ascendra is an elevator and escalator maintenance company. The most valuable non-fundamental reps for Mouren are § 4.12 (elevator code compliance, inspection certificates, maintenance control programs), § 4.9 (legal proceedings and claims), § 4.6 (material contracts / Service Contracts), and § 4.11 (employees and labor). Latent code violations, uncured inspection citations, pending personal-injury claims from entrapment or mechanical failure, and Service Contract disputes routinely surface 6–18 months after a transaction. With only five months of post-closing coverage, Mouren would bear essentially all of that risk. The 10% cap (§ 9.5(a)) and the $242,500 deductible (§ 9.5(b)) further compound the exposure.

**Recommendation.** Amend § 9.1(a) to tie survival to the Closing Date: "twelve (12) months after the Closing Date" (i.e., through September 30, 2027). For § 4.12 and § 4.9 specifically, argue for 18–24 months post-closing, consistent with the inspection cycle and the statute of limitations for personal-injury claims in the relevant states.

**Client decision required.** Yes. The survival period is a core economic term. The client should decide whether to accept 12 months from Closing as a floor and push for longer on the code-compliance and litigation reps, or whether the five-month period is a walk-away issue.

## 2. IP Assignment Permits Seller to Transfer the LiftSense License to Any Third Party, Including Competitors

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement § 9.2(a)

**Issue.** Section 9.2(a) allows Seller to assign the Agreement "in whole or in part, together with the License and its other rights and obligations under this Agreement, without the consent of any other party. Any such assignment may be made to any Person, including to any successor by merger, consolidation or other business combination, or to any purchaser of all or any portion of Seller's assets or equity."

**Why it matters.** The License (§ 4.2) is perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, and covers the right to "use, reproduce, modify, create derivative works of, and distribute in object-code form the LiftSense Software" in the Retained Business. If Stamox sells its modernization and new-installation business to a company that also provides elevator maintenance services in the Mid-Atlantic, that acquirer steps into the License with no consent requirement from Mouren or the Company. The SPA non-compete (§ 6.6(a)) binds "Seller" and its "Affiliates," but a third-party purchaser of Stamox's assets is neither. Mouren would have paid $48.5 million for a business whose core technology platform can be handed to a competitor by operation of contract.

**Recommendation.** Amend § 9.2(a) to require the Company's (or Buyer's) prior written consent for any assignment of the License, or at minimum to limit permissible assignees to entities that are not engaged in elevator or escalator maintenance, repair, or inspection services in the states covered by the non-compete. Alternatively, tie the License to Stamox's continued ownership of the Retained Business and provide for automatic termination of the License upon a change of control of Seller that is not to an Affiliate.

**Client decision required.** Yes. This goes to the strategic value of the LiftSense acquisition. The client should confirm whether the IP is a primary driver of deal value and whether it is willing to accept a broad license as the cost of the non-compete carve-out in SPA § 6.6(c).

## 3. Conflicting Governing Law Between the Seller Note (New York) and the SPA (Delaware)

**Document / Section:** Seller Promissory Note § 12.1; Stock Purchase Agreement §§ 11.8(a), 11.12

**Issue.** The Note is governed by New York law (§ 12.1) and subject to exclusive jurisdiction in New York courts (§ 12.2). The SPA is governed by Delaware law (§ 11.8(a)) and subject to exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (§ 11.8(b)). SPA § 11.12 provides that the SPA prevails over Ancillary Documents "except that the Seller Note shall control with respect to matters arising under or relating to the Seller Note."

**Why it matters.** The right of setoff appears in both the SPA (§ 9.8) and the Note (§ 6). The subordination provisions (§ 7 of the Note) interact with the setoff right: if Mouren exercises setoff to withhold a payment, Stamox could argue the withholding constitutes a "payment received in violation of" the subordination (§ 7.3 turnover), or that the setoff is governed by NY law under the Note while the indemnification claim giving rise to the setoff is governed by DE law under the SPA. A court in New York and a court in Delaware could reach different conclusions on the same factual dispute. This creates litigation risk and expense for Mouren, which is the party most likely to exercise setoff.

**Recommendation.** Conform the Note's governing law and jurisdiction to Delaware to match the SPA. If Stamox insists on New York law for the Note (perhaps for its own treasury or tax reasons), add an express provision in § 11.12 of the SPA stating that disputes concerning the interplay of setoff (§ 9.8) and subordination (Note § 7) shall be governed by Delaware law and resolved in the Delaware Court of Chancery.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a drafting fix, though the client should be informed that the change may be resisted by Stamox.

## 4. License Back Is Overly Broad; Seller Owns All Improvements with No Obligation to Share

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement §§ 4.2, 4.3, 4.4

**Issue.** The License permits Seller to "use, reproduce, modify, create derivative works of, and distribute in object-code form the LiftSense Software" (§ 4.2). Seller may sublicense to "Affiliates, contractors and customers" (§ 4.3). Seller owns all Improvements and "shall have no obligation to deliver Improvements to the Company" (§ 4.4).

**Why it matters.** LiftSense is described in Schedule A as a platform comprising edge-device firmware, a cloud analytics service, and a technician mobile application, plus fault-code libraries and controller-integration modules. If Seller (or its sublicensees) develops improvements to the diagnostic algorithms or sensor-integration protocols and retains them exclusively, the Company's version of the software will stagnate while Seller's version advances. Over the 36-month Note term and beyond, this could erode the competitive advantage Mouren is paying for. The sublicense right to "customers" is particularly concerning: a building-owner customer of Stamox's modernization business could receive object-code distributions of LiftSense and potentially use them for their own monitoring purposes outside the Retained Business.

**Recommendation.**
- Narrow § 4.2 to "use" and "reproduce" only, deleting "modify, create derivative works of, and distribute in object-code form." If Seller needs to modify the software for integration with its modernization controllers, limit that right to internal modifications that do not create standalone distributable products.
- In § 4.3, delete "customers" from the list of permissible sublicensees, or limit sublicensing to contractors actually performing work on a specific modernization or installation project.
- In § 4.4, require Seller to offer Improvements to the Company on a non-exclusive, royalty-free basis before commercializing them independently (a "right of first refusal" or "most-favored-nation" provision).

**Client decision required.** Partially. The scope of the license back is a commercial negotiation point. The client should indicate how important it is that LiftSense remain a differentiated asset versus accepting a broader license as the quid pro quo for the non-compete carve-out.

## 5. Seller Note Change-of-Control Trigger Constrains Mouren's Future Exit

**Document / Section:** Seller Promissory Note § 8.1(d)

**Issue.** Section 8.1(d) makes it an Event of Default if "Maker sells all or substantially all of the Shares, or the Company sells all or substantially all of its assets, to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is repaid in full at the closing of that sale."

**Why it matters.** Mouren is a "Building Systems Holdings" company. If its strategy includes consolidating the Mid-Atlantic elevator portfolio and later selling Ascendra (or its assets) to a strategic buyer, the $7.5 million Note (plus accrued interest) becomes a mandatory pay-off at closing. This reduces the net proceeds of any future sale and could complicate financing for the buyer of Ascendra, who would need to underwrite the Note payoff. The 36-month maturity means the constraint is in effect for the entire initial holding period.

**Recommendation.** Amend § 8.1(d) to permit a sale to a strategic buyer so long as the Note is either (a) repaid in full, (b) assumed by the purchaser under a novation acceptable to Holder, or (c) subordinated to the purchaser's acquisition financing on terms no less protective than the existing subordination. At minimum, add a cure period of 30 days after notice before the Event of Default becomes effective.

**Client decision required.** Yes. The client should confirm its medium-term strategy for Ascendra (hold vs. potential sale) so we can calibrate the ask.

## 6. Five-Year Non-Compete May Be Unenforceable, Voiding Mouren's Primary Protection Against Stamox

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 6.6(a)

**Issue.** The non-compete restricts Seller and its Affiliates from "elevator or escalator maintenance, repair or inspection services" in six jurisdictions for five years post-closing.

**Why it matters.** Delaware courts (which govern the SPA under § 11.8) will enforce non-competes in the M&A context but scrutinize duration and geographic scope. Five years is at the aggressive end; Delaware courts have upheld three-to-five-year restrictions in connection with the sale of a going concern, but the analysis is fact-specific. The greater risk is that if a court finds the restriction overbroad, it may refuse to blue-pencil and void the entire provision. The non-compete is Mouren's primary contractual protection against Stamox re-entering the maintenance market; the IP license back (Issue 4) and the unrestricted assignment right (Issue 2) mean that without the non-compete, Stamox (or its assignees) could compete directly using LiftSense-derived technology.

**Recommendation.** Reduce the duration to three years, which is more defensible and still provides meaningful protection given the 36-month Note term. Alternatively, keep five years but add a severability/savings clause specific to § 6.6 providing that if any portion is held unenforceable, the court shall modify the restriction to the maximum enforceable duration and geography (a "reformation" clause).

**Client decision required.** Yes. The client should weigh the value of five years of protection against the risk that an overbroad provision is voided entirely.

## 7. IP Assignment Representation Carve-Out for "Customers in the Ordinary Course" Is Vague

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement § 6.1(b)

**Issue.** Seller represents that it "has not granted any license, option or other right in the Assigned IP that remains in effect, other than non-exclusive licenses to the Company and to customers in the ordinary course."

**Why it matters.** The phrase "customers in the ordinary course" is undefined. If Stamox has granted building owners or property managers access to LiftSense dashboards, diagnostic reports, or API integrations as part of its modernization service, those third parties may retain rights in the software post-closing. The Company's ownership of the Assigned IP (§ 2.1) is "free and clear of all liens, security interests and other encumbrances, other than the License" (§ 2.1)—but existing customer licenses are not the "License" (which is the license back to Seller). They are a separate encumbrance not addressed by the assignment.

**Recommendation.** Require Seller to identify all existing customer licenses in a schedule to the IP Assignment (or in the Disclosure Schedule), specifying the scope, duration, and termination rights of each. Add a rep that no such license permits the customer to use LiftSense for purposes other than monitoring equipment installed by Seller, and that all such licenses terminate or convert to Company-controlled licenses at Closing.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a diligence and drafting item.

## 8. Bill of Sale Does Not Individually Identify Vehicles; No Condition Reps

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, Schedule A (Part 1) and § 5.1

**Issue.** The 32 vehicles are listed by range (e.g., "1–14: 2022–2024 Ford Transit 250") with VINs noted as "Per title certificates." Section 5.1 incorporates by reference only SPA § 4.7 (title and sufficiency of assets). There is no representation regarding the mechanical condition, emissions compliance, or accident history of the vehicles.

**Why it matters.** Mouren is acquiring a field-service fleet. If vehicles have undisclosed mechanical issues, open recalls, or title defects (e.g., branded titles from prior accidents), the Company inherits those problems. The "sufficiency" rep in § 4.7 is forward-looking and does not address condition. With 32 vehicles across five states, the risk of at least one title or condition issue is non-trivial.

**Recommendation.**
- Require a vehicle-by-vehicle schedule with full VINs, title status (clean, salvage, rebuilt), odometer readings, and any known defects, to be delivered as a condition to Closing.
- Add a specific rep in the Bill of Sale (or in the Disclosure Schedule) that the Vehicles are free of mechanical defects material to their use in field service and that no Vehicle has a branded or reconstructed title.
- Include a walk-and-verify protocol: Buyer's representatives may inspect the Vehicles during the pre-Closing access period (SPA § 6.2) and any Vehicle that fails inspection may be excluded from the Fleet Assets with a corresponding Purchase Price adjustment.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a diligence and drafting item.

## 9. Transition Cooperation for LiftSense Is Limited to 90 Days and Two Personnel

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement § 3.4

**Issue.** Seller must make available "up to two of its personnel familiar with the LiftSense Software" for "ninety (90) days after the Effective Date."

**Why it matters.** Schedule A describes a multi-component platform: edge-device firmware, cloud analytics service, technician mobile application, fault-code libraries, and controller-integration modules for "major traction and hydraulic controller families." Two people for 90 days is likely insufficient to transfer institutional knowledge about the full stack, particularly the controller-integration modules which require domain-specific elevator engineering knowledge. If key personnel depart Stamox before or during the transition window, the Company may be left with source code it cannot fully maintain.

**Recommendation.** Extend the transition period to 180 days. Increase the personnel commitment to "such number of personnel as are reasonably necessary, not fewer than three," including at least one individual with firmware/edge-device expertise and one with cloud-platform expertise. Add a requirement that Seller identify the specific individuals by name in a schedule at least 30 days before Closing and covenant not to terminate their employment during the transition period without replacement.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a drafting item, though the client's internal technical team should confirm the adequacy of the proposed resource level.

## 10. Contractor License Transfer Is a Condition to Both Parties' Closing Obligation

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 7.1(b)

**Issue.** Section 7.1(b) makes the transfer or reissuance of each Contractor License a condition to the obligations of *all parties* to close. If any single license cannot be transferred (e.g., a state elevator board denies the change-of-control filing or requires a new qualifying individual), neither party is obligated to close.

**Why it matters.** The Company operates in at least five states (per the non-compete geography and the vehicle title states in the Bill of Sale). Each state may have different change-of-control requirements for elevator contractor licenses. If one state's process is delayed or denied, the entire $48.5 million deal can be blocked. For Mouren, which is paying a premium for the licensed workforce and the Service Contract portfolio, this creates deal-certainty risk that is disproportionate to the underlying issue (a single license in a single state).

**Recommendation.** Amend § 7.1(b) to provide that the failure to transfer a Contractor License in a single jurisdiction shall not prevent Closing so long as (a) the Company retains licenses in all other jurisdictions, and (b) Seller uses commercially reasonable efforts to cure the deficiency within 90 days after Closing. Alternatively, make it a condition only to the extent the failure affects the Company's ability to perform more than a de minimis percentage of its Service Contracts.

**Client decision required.** Yes. The client should confirm how many Service Contracts are in each jurisdiction so we can calibrate the materiality threshold.

## 11. SPA § 4.12(c) Code-Compliance Rep Uses "Material Respects" Qualifier

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 4.12(c)

**Issue.** The rep states that each serviced unit "has been maintained and repaired by the Company in compliance in all material respects with" ASME A17.1, A17.3, and state/local elevator safety laws.

**Why it matters.** In the elevator industry, a single code violation (e.g., a defective governor, a misaligned door interlock, or an expired inspection certificate) can result in a unit being taken out of service by a state elevator board. "Material respects" gives Seller a significant defense: it can argue that a handful of violations across hundreds of units is not "material." Combined with the short survival period (Issue 1), Mouren could discover post-closing that a non-trivial number of units are out of compliance and have no recourse.

**Recommendation.** Delete "in all material respects" from § 4.12(c), making the rep an absolute compliance representation. If Seller resists, accept "material" but define it in the context of this section to mean any violation that results in (or could reasonably be expected to result in) a suspension, revocation, or conditioning of a certificate of operation for any serviced unit.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a drafting item, though it should be raised in conjunction with the survival-period negotiation (Issue 1).

## 12. NDA Customer Non-Solicitation Is One-Way and Potentially Overly Broad Post-Closing

**Document / Section:** Mutual NDA § 5.3

**Issue.** Section 5.3 provides: "Mouren shall not use any Confidential Information to solicit any customer of Ascendra for elevator or escalator maintenance, repair or inspection services." This obligation runs for the NDA term (2 years from December 8, 2025, i.e., through December 8, 2027, per § 7.1).

**Why it matters.** After Closing (September 30, 2026), Mouren owns Ascendra and its Service Contracts. The Company's existing customers are now *Mouren's* customers. The NDA restriction on using "Confidential Information" to "solicit" those customers is technically inapplicable to organic retention, but it could be argued to restrict Mouren from using Ascendra's customer data (which is now Mouren's data as owner of the Company) to cross-sell other Mouren building-systems products to the same building owners. The restriction is one-way (it does not bind Stamox) and was negotiated pre-closing when the competitive dynamics were different.

**Recommendation.** Add a proviso to § 5.3: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, following the consummation of the Proposed Transaction, this Section 5.3 shall be of no further force or effect with respect to customers of the Company, which shall be customers of Mouren by virtue of its ownership of the Company." Alternatively, the NDA should be deemed terminated as to Mouren's obligations upon Closing, with only the trade-secret protections (§ 7.2) surviving.

**Client decision required.** No. This is a housekeeping drafting item, but the client should be aware that the NDA technically remains in force post-closing.
