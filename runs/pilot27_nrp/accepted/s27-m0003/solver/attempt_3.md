## Issue 1 – Survival Period for Non-Fundamental Representations Is Only ~5 Months Post-Closing

**Document / Section:** SPA § 9.1(a)

**Issue:** Section 9.1(a) provides that representations and warranties other than Fundamental Representations "shall survive the Closing until the date that is twelve (12) months after the date of this Agreement." The SPA is dated March 2, 2026. The Scheduled Closing Date is September 30, 2026 (SPA § 2.4(a)). Non-fundamental reps therefore expire on March 2, 2027 — only approximately five months after Closing.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The Fundamental Representations (SPA § 1.1) are limited to organization/authority, title to shares, capitalization, taxes, and brokers. Every other rep — financial statements (§ 4.4), material contracts (§ 4.6), title to assets (§ 4.7), intellectual property (§ 4.8), legal proceedings (§ 4.9), employees (§ 4.11), contractor licenses and code compliance (§ 4.12), environmental/OSHA (§ 4.13), and insurance (§ 4.14) — falls outside the Fundamental category and gets only ~5 months of post-closing protection. For an elevator maintenance business, this is dangerously short:

- The next state periodic inspection cycle (referenced in § 4.12(b)) may not occur until well after March 2027, meaning code-compliance defects could surface after the survival period expires.
- OSHA or environmental violations (§ 4.13) often emerge through inspections or employee reports months or years after the underlying event.
- Service contract disputes (§ 4.6) may not crystallize until a renewal cycle or a customer claim.
- The last audited financial statements are as of December 31, 2025 (§ 4.4); a 9-month gap to Closing is bridged only by the ordinary-course/MAE rep in § 4.5, which itself expires in ~5 months post-Closing.

**Recommendation:** Amend § 9.1(a) to provide survival of 18 months after the Closing Date (not the Agreement Date). Alternatively, tie survival of general reps to the later of (i) 18 months post-Closing or (ii) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations. Tax reps already get statute-of-limitations-plus-60-days under § 9.1(b); the same logic should apply to reps about regulatory compliance, environmental matters, and insurance.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** Mouren should decide whether to accept a shorter survival period in exchange for other economic terms (e.g., a lower purchase price or a larger escrow). If the deal is priced on the assumption of full rep protection, this is a material value issue.

## Issue 2 – NDA Customer Non-Solicitation Restriction Is Absurd Post-Closing

**Document / Section:** NDA § 5.3

**Issue:** Section 5.3 provides: "Mouren shall not use any Confidential Information to solicit any customer of Ascendra for elevator or escalator maintenance, repair or inspection services." This obligation has no express expiration tied to Closing; it runs for the NDA term (2 years from December 8, 2025, i.e., through December 8, 2027, per § 7.1) and, for trade-secret information, indefinitely (§ 7.2).

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** After Closing, Mouren owns Ascendra and its customer base. Servicing Ascendra's existing maintenance customers using information obtained during diligence (contract terms, building portfolios, service histories — all expressly covered by NDA § 1.2(b) and (c)) could technically violate § 5.3. The provision makes no exception for the acquirer's post-Closing ownership of the target. As drafted, it creates a real risk that Stamox could assert a breach claim against Mouren for ordinary post-closing operations.

**Recommendation:** Add a provision to the NDA (or a side letter) stating that § 5.3 terminates automatically at the Closing of the transaction contemplated by the SPA, or that it does not apply to Mouren's ownership and operation of the Company after Closing. Alternatively, the SPA should include a provision that the NDA is superseded in its entirety by the SPA's confidentiality covenant (§ 6.5) upon Closing.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** This is low-risk if the NDA simply expires (December 8, 2027) before any dispute arises, but Mouren should not operate its acquired business under a cloud of potential NDA breach. Recommend resolving before Closing.

## Issue 3 – IP License Back Is Overly Broad and Creates a Competitive Threat

**Document / Section:** IP Assignment §§ 4.2, 4.3, 4.4

**Issue:** The license back to Stamox is perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, and fully paid-up. It permits Stamox to "use, reproduce, modify, create derivative works of, and distribute in object-code form the LiftSense Software" (§ 4.2). Critically:

- **Sublicensing to customers** (§ 4.3): Stamox may sublicense to "Affiliates, contractors and customers to the extent necessary for the conduct of the Retained Business." "Customers" is undefined and could encompass any building owner or property manager in Stamox's modernization/installation portfolio.
- **Improvements owned by Seller** (§ 4.4): Any modification or enhancement Stamox makes under the license is owned by Stamox, with no obligation to share with the Company. Over time, Stamox could build a superior or differentiated version of the LiftSense platform for its retained business.
- **Free assignability by Seller** (§ 9.2(a)): Stamox can assign the license to any Person without consent, including a purchaser of its modernization business. A competitor of Mouren could acquire the license.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The LiftSense platform is described in the SPA as a key asset (SPA § 4.8(b); Recital C). Mouren is paying $48.5M for a company whose competitive moat includes this software. If Stamox can perpetually use, modify, and sublicense the platform (and its derivatives) to customers and third parties, the exclusivity value of the acquisition is significantly diminished. The 5-year non-compete (SPA § 6.6) covers maintenance/repair/inspection but expressly carves out Stamox's "modernization and new-installation business" (§ 6.6(c)) — the same business in which the license may be exercised.

**Recommendation:**

1. Narrow § 4.3 to permit sublicensing only to Affiliates and contractors (delete "customers"), or limit customer-facing distribution to object code only with no access to source materials or diagnostic algorithms.
2. Add a most-favored-nation or grant-back provision in § 4.4: if Stamox develops Improvements that are generally applicable to elevator/escalator monitoring (not specific to modernization/installation), the Company receives a non-exclusive, royalty-free license to those Improvements.
3. In § 9.2(a), require that any assignee of the license agree to be bound by the non-compete in SPA § 6.6 and by the confidentiality provisions of IP Assignment § 5.
4. Consider adding a sunset on the license (e.g., 5 years to match the non-compete) or a reversion clause if Stamox breaches the non-compete.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** The breadth of the license back is a business term. Mouren should decide how much competitive overlap with Stamox's retained modernization business it is willing to accept. If LiftSense is a core differentiator for the maintenance business, Mouren should push hard to narrow the license.

## Issue 4 – Seller Note Payment Blockage Can Trap Voluntary Prepayments

**Document / Section:** Note §§ 5, 7.2, 7.3

**Issue:** Section 5 permits Maker (Mouren) to prepay the Note "at any time, without premium or penalty." However, Section 7.2 provides that if a Senior Lender gives notice of a default under the Senior Credit Agreement, "Maker shall make no payment of principal under this Note" for up to 180 days. Section 7.3 adds that any payment made in violation of § 7.2 must be turned over to the Senior Lenders.

The "Senior Credit Agreement" is defined in § 1.2 as "any credit agreement, loan agreement or similar instrument under which Maker or any of its subsidiaries borrows money from one or more banks or other institutional lenders." This is extremely broad — it captures any bank facility at any level of the Mouren group.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** If Mouren (or a subsidiary) has a revolving credit facility and a technical default occurs (e.g., a reporting deadline is missed), Mouren would be blocked from voluntarily prepaying the $7.5M Seller Note for up to 180 days. This could:

- Strain the relationship with Stamox (who is also bound by a 5-year non-compete and confidentiality obligations);
- Create a dispute over whether the blockage applies to voluntary prepayments (the Note is silent on this point);
- In an extreme case, if Mouren wires payment anyway, the funds must be turned over to the Senior Lender under § 7.3.

**Recommendation:** Add an express carve-out in § 7.2: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Maker may prepay this Note in whole or in part pursuant to Section 5 at any time, and any such prepayment shall not be subject to the blockage in this Section 7.2." Alternatively, limit the blockage to mandatory (as opposed to voluntary) principal payments.

Also consider narrowing the definition of "Senior Credit Agreement" to exclude facilities below a specified threshold (e.g., $10M) or to exclude working-capital lines that are undrawn.

## Issue 5 – Fleet Assets Identified Only by Reference to Title Certificates, Not by VIN

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, Schedule A, Part 1

**Issue:** The 32 vehicles are listed by year, make, model, type, and title state, but the VIN column reads "Per title certificates" rather than providing actual VINs. For a conveyance of 32 vehicles across five states, this is a significant identification gap.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** Without specific VINs, there is no objective way to verify that the vehicles actually delivered at Closing are the ones contemplated by the agreement. If a vehicle is swapped, omitted, or double-conveyed (e.g., still subject to a lien from a prior financing), Mouren has no clear reference point to assert a claim. The SPA's indemnification (Article IX) would apply, but the short survival period (Issue 1) limits the window to discover and assert a claim.

**Recommendation:** Require that Schedule A, Part 1 be populated with full 17-character VINs for each vehicle before execution. If title certificates have not yet been collected, add a condition that Transferor deliver a complete VIN list at least 10 Business Days before Closing for Buyer's verification, and that the Closing deliverables include original titles bearing those VINs.

## Issue 6 – Asymmetric Assignment Rights in the IP Assignment

**Document / Section:** IP Assignment § 9.2

**Issue:** Section 9.2(a) permits Seller to assign the IP Assignment (and the License) to any Person without consent, including a successor by merger or a purchaser of assets. Section 9.2(b) provides that neither Buyer nor the Company may assign without Seller's prior written consent.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** If Mouren later restructures its corporate organization (e.g., moves Ascendra to a different holding entity, or sells a portion of its portfolio), it cannot assign the IP Assignment without Stamox's consent. Meanwhile, Stamox can freely transfer the license to any third party — potentially a direct competitor of Mouren in the elevator maintenance space. This asymmetry gives Stamox leverage in any future negotiation with Mouren.

**Recommendation:** Make § 9.2(a) subject to a requirement that the assignee agree in writing to be bound by SPA § 6.6 (non-compete) and IP Assignment § 5 (source code confidentiality). For § 9.2(b), permit Buyer to assign to an Affiliate or in connection with a restructuring, so long as the assignee assumes the Company's obligations under the IP Assignment.

## Issue 7 – Governing Law and Jurisdiction Conflict Between the Note and the SPA

**Document / Section:** Note § 12.1–12.2; SPA § 11.8; SPA § 11.12

**Issue:** The SPA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (§ 11.8). The Note is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York state and federal courts (§ 12.1–12.2). SPA § 11.12 provides that the Note "shall control with respect to matters arising under or relating to the Seller Note."

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The setoff right (SPA § 9.8 / Note § 6) sits at the intersection of both documents. A dispute over whether a setoff was properly exercised, whether a withholding was in "good faith," or whether a particular loss is subject to setoff could be litigated in New York (under the Note) or Delaware (under the SPA), potentially producing different outcomes. New York and Delaware law differ on setoff, good-faith standards, and the enforceability of liquidated-damage-style provisions.

**Recommendation:** Conform the Note's governing law and jurisdiction to Delaware (matching the SPA). If Stamox insists on New York law for the Note (perhaps because it has NY counsel or a NY-based lender group), add a provision that any dispute involving both the SPA and the Note shall be resolved exclusively in the Delaware Court of Chancery, with the Note's NY governing law applying only to purely note-based disputes (e.g., payment mechanics).

## Issue 8 – Non-Compete Carve-Out for Stamox's Retained Business

**Document / Section:** SPA § 6.6(c)

**Issue:** The 5-year non-compete in § 6.6(a)–(b) is subject to the carve-out in § 6.6(c): "Nothing in this Section 6.6 restricts Seller's modernization and new-installation business as conducted on the date of this Agreement."

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The carve-out is defined by reference to Stamox's business "as conducted on the date of this Agreement" (March 2, 2026). If Stamox is currently performing modernization work in the same Mid-Atlantic states, it can continue doing so for 5 years. Modernization projects often include new equipment installation, which may overlap with maintenance service contracts. A Stamox modernization crew on-site at a building where Ascendra holds the maintenance contract could create customer confusion, poach relationships, or undercut Ascendra's position at renewal.

**Recommendation:**

1. Require Stamox to identify its current modernization/installation projects and customers in a schedule (so Mouren knows the scope of the carve-out).
2. Add a non-solicitation covenant specific to the carve-out: even within its retained business, Stamox shall not solicit or accept maintenance, repair, or inspection work from Ascendra's existing customers for 5 years.
3. Clarify that the carve-out does not permit Stamox to use the LiftSense license (IP Assignment § 4.2) to offer monitoring services that compete with Ascendra's maintenance contracts.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** Mouren should assess how significant Stamox's modernization business is in the same geographic footprint. If it is substantial, the non-compete is less protective than it appears, and Mouren should price that risk or tighten the carve-out.

## Issue 9 – Insurance Tail May Be Inadequate for Elevator-Related Claims

**Document / Section:** SPA § 6.7

**Issue:** Seller must obtain, at its expense, extended reporting coverage (tail) under the Company's claims-made policies for "not less than three (3) years after the Closing Date."

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** Elevator and escalator work carries significant personal-injury and property-damage risk (entrapment, falls, equipment failure). In the Mid-Atlantic states:

- The statute of limitations for personal injury is 2 years (PA, NJ, DE, MD, VA), but for wrongful death it can be 2–3 years.
- Latent defects in elevator components (e.g., a faulty brake assembly installed during a prior repair) may not manifest for many years.
- The SPA's own rep in § 4.9(b) contemplates claims "asserted in the five (5) years before the date of this Agreement" — acknowledging a 5-year claims history.

A 3-year tail may not cover the full exposure window. If a claim arises in year 4 post-Closing relating to pre-Closing work, the claims-made policy (with only a 3-year tail) would not respond, and the indemnification claim against Seller would be time-barred under the short survival period (Issue 1).

**Recommendation:** Extend the tail to 5 years post-Closing, or at minimum to the expiration of the longest applicable statute of limitations for personal injury and property damage in the states where the Company operates. If cost is a concern, require Seller to maintain a run-off insurance policy (rather than just a tail) for 5 years.

## Issue 10 – Transferor (Stamox Fleet Services, LLC) Makes No Direct Representations

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale § 5.1; SPA § 4.7

**Issue:** The Bill of Sale is executed by Stamox Fleet Services, LLC ("Transferor"), an Affiliate of Seller. However, § 5.1 provides that "the representations and warranties concerning the Fleet Assets are those made by Seller in the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.7." Transferor itself makes no representations. The SPA's reps are made by Stamox Vertical Group, Inc. (Seller), not by Transferor.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** If the Fleet Assets have a title defect, an undisclosed lien, or a condition issue, Mouren's indemnification claim runs against Seller (Stamox Vertical Group) under SPA Article IX, not against Transferor. If Seller and Transferor have different financial positions (e.g., Transferor is a shell entity with no assets), recovery could be impaired. Additionally, the Bill of Sale's free-and-clear covenant (§ 2.1) is a Transferor obligation, but there is no direct indemnity from Transferor for breach of that covenant — only the SPA's general indemnification framework applies.

**Recommendation:** Add a direct representation and warranty from Transferor in the Bill of Sale (title, no liens, authority to convey) and a corresponding indemnification obligation from Transferor, or require Seller to guarantee Transferor's obligations under the Bill of Sale.

## Issue 11 – Seller Note Change-of-Control Trigger Restricts Future M&A Flexibility

**Document / Section:** Note § 8.1(d)

**Issue:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells all or substantially all of the Shares, or the Company sells all or substantially all of its assets, to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is repaid in full at the closing of that sale."

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** If Mouren later wishes to sell Ascendra (e.g., as part of a portfolio rationalization) or if Ascendra wishes to sell a major division of assets, the $7.5M Note must be paid off at closing. This is a cash outflow that must be modeled into any future exit. The "Affiliate" carve-out provides some flexibility (Mouren can move Ascendra within its corporate structure), but a sale to any third party triggers repayment.

**Recommendation:** This is largely standard for seller notes, but Mouren should be aware of the constraint. Consider adding a provision that permits assignment of the Note to the purchaser in a change-of-control transaction (so the buyer assumes the Note rather than Mouren paying it off), subject to the purchaser's creditworthiness.

## Issue 12 – "Net Working Capital" Is Undefined in the SPA

**Document / Section:** SPA § 2.3

**Issue:** The working capital adjustment mechanism uses the term "net working capital" (and "Closing Indebtedness" in § 2.2(a)) but does not define it. There is no formula specifying which balance-sheet items are included or excluded (e.g., whether cash, short-term investments, or current portion of long-term debt are netted).

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The Target Working Capital is $3,200,000, and the adjustment is dollar-for-dollar on the cash purchase price. Without a defined formula, the parties are likely to disagree on the calculation, triggering the dispute-resolution process in § 2.3(c) (independent accounting firm). This is a common post-closing friction point.

**Recommendation:** Add a definition of "Net Working Capital" in SPA § 1.1, specifying: current assets (excluding cash and cash equivalents, and excluding any current portion of long-term debt) minus current liabilities (excluding short-term borrowings and the current portion of long-term debt). Alternatively, attach a schedule showing the components as of the most recent balance sheet date.

## Issue 13 – Ancillary Documents Dated to Scheduled Closing, Not Actual Closing

**Document / Section:** IP Assignment (preamble); Bill of Sale (preamble); Note (preamble); SPA §§ 1.1, 2.4(b)

**Issue:** The IP Assignment, Bill of Sale, and Note are each "dated as of September 30, 2026" — the Scheduled Closing Date. However, SPA § 2.4(b) provides that if a closing condition is not satisfied on the Scheduled Closing Date, Closing occurs on the third Business Day after the condition is satisfied. The SPA defines these documents as "dated as of the Closing Date" (e.g., § 1.1, "Bill of Sale" definition). If Closing is delayed, the actual Closing Date will differ from the date printed in the ancillary documents.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** A mismatch between the stated effective date and the actual Closing Date could create gaps in coverage (e.g., the IP Assignment's transition cooperation period in § 3.4 runs 90 days from the "Effective Date"; the Note's interest accrues from the "Closing Date"). If the documents say September 30 but Closing occurs October 15, there is ambiguity about when obligations begin.

**Recommendation:** Add a conforming provision in each ancillary document: "If the Closing Date is not September 30, 2026, the Effective Date of this Agreement shall be automatically adjusted to the actual Closing Date." Alternatively, use "the Closing Date" as a defined term throughout rather than a fixed calendar date.

## Issue 14 – NDA Does Not Terminate at Closing; Potential Overlap with SPA Confidentiality

**Document / Section:** NDA § 7.1; SPA § 6.5

**Issue:** The NDA has a fixed 2-year term (through December 8, 2027) with no provision for termination at Closing. The SPA has its own post-Closing confidentiality covenant (§ 6.5). After Closing, both sets of obligations could apply simultaneously, with different scopes, standards, and durations.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The NDA's confidentiality obligations (§ 2.2–2.3) are mutual and cover information disclosed during diligence. The SPA's § 6.5 is one-way (Seller must hold Company information in confidence). If the NDA continues post-Closing, Mouren's own personnel remain bound by the NDA's restrictions on using Stamox's Confidential Information — even though Mouren now owns the Company and has a legitimate need to access that information in the ordinary course.

**Recommendation:** Add a provision to the NDA (or the SPA) stating that the NDA terminates in its entirety at the Closing, with the SPA's confidentiality provisions (§ 6.5) and the IP Assignment's source-code protections (§ 5) governing post-Closing confidentiality. Alternatively, provide that post-Closing, the NDA applies only to information that does not relate to the Company's business.

## Issue 15 – Transition Cooperation Period May Be Insufficient

**Document / Section:** IP Assignment § 3.4

**Issue:** Seller must make available "up to two of its personnel familiar with the LiftSense Software" for 90 days post-Closing, at no charge.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The LiftSense platform includes edge-device firmware, a cloud analytics service, and a technician mobile application (Schedule A, ¶ 1), plus controller-integration modules for "major traction and hydraulic controller families" (Schedule A, ¶ 2). Two personnel for 90 days may be insufficient to transfer institutional knowledge about a multi-component platform serving a large installed base of elevator units. If the personnel depart or become unavailable, Mouren has no further right to access them.

**Recommendation:** Extend the transition cooperation period to 180 days. Increase the personnel commitment to "up to four" or specify roles (e.g., one software architect, one firmware engineer, one product manager). Add a requirement that Seller deliver a comprehensive transition document (architecture overview, deployment guide, known issues, integration points) within 30 days post-Closing, independent of personnel availability.

## Issue 16 – Indemnification Cap Is Low Relative to Deal Size

**Document / Section:** SPA § 9.5(a)

**Issue:** The cap on Seller's indemnification liability for rep/warranty breaches is $4,850,000 (10% of the $48.5M Purchase Price). Combined with the $242,500 deductible (§ 9.5(b)) and the ~5-month survival period (Issue 1), Mouren's total recoverable exposure is limited.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** In a business with 32 vehicles, hundreds of elevator units under service, multi-year customer contracts, and regulatory compliance obligations, a single material issue (e.g., a systemic code-compliance deficiency across a portfolio of buildings, or a material contract dispute) could easily exceed $4.85M in losses. The cap means Mouren absorbs the excess.

**Recommendation:** Increase the cap to 15–20% of the Purchase Price ($7.3M–$9.7M). At minimum, ensure that the cap does not apply to breaches of § 4.12 (licenses/code compliance) or § 4.13 (environmental/OSHA), given the regulatory risk profile of the business.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** The cap is a negotiated economic term. Mouren should assess whether the current cap, combined with the short survival period, provides adequate protection given the risk profile of the target. If not, this should be a priority negotiating point.

## Issue 17 – SPA § 2.5(a)(v) Drafting Ambiguity (Resignations)

**Document / Section:** SPA § 2.5(a)(v)

**Issue:** The provision requires delivery of "resignations of the directors and officers of the Company designated by Buyer." On its face, this reads as the resignations of the people Buyer designates — which makes no sense (Buyer's designees would not need to resign). The intended meaning is almost certainly the resignations of the *current* directors and officers, to be delivered so that Buyer's designees can be installed.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** A court construing this provision literally could hold that the closing condition in § 7.2(e) (delivery of all items in § 2.5(a)) is not satisfied unless the resignations of Buyer's *designees* are delivered — an impossible condition.

**Recommendation:** Redraft as: "resignations of the then-sitting directors and officers of the Company, effective as of the Closing Date, in form and substance reasonably acceptable to Buyer."

## Issue 18 – Note § 7.4 Requires Stamox to Execute Additional Subordination Documents

**Document / Section:** Note § 7.4

**Issue:** "At the request of any Senior Lender, Holder shall execute a subordination agreement on customary terms consistent with this Section 7." This obligates Stamox to sign additional documents at the behest of Mouren's (or a subsidiary's) senior lenders.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** While this protects Mouren's senior lenders, it creates a dependency: if Stamox refuses or delays execution, Mouren's senior lenders may view this as a credit issue. Conversely, if a senior lender demands terms that go beyond "customary" or that are inconsistent with Section 7, Stamox could argue it is not obligated to sign.

**Recommendation:** Add a time limit (e.g., "within ten (10) Business Days of request") and a consequence for non-compliance (e.g., the obligation to execute shall be specifically enforceable, and failure to do so constitutes an Event of Default under the Note — though this would be unusual since it penalizes the Holder). Alternatively, require execution of the subordination agreement as a Closing deliverable if Mouren has an existing senior facility.

## Issue 19 – SPA § 4.8(b) Acknowledges License Back, Limiting "Free and Clear" Representation

**Document / Section:** SPA § 4.8(b); IP Assignment § 2.1

**Issue:** SPA § 4.8(b) represents that "Upon the Closing, the Company will own the LiftSense Software free and clear of all encumbrances, subject only to the license granted back to Seller under the IP Assignment Agreement." The IP Assignment § 2.1 similarly conveys the Assigned IP "free and clear of all liens, security interests and other encumbrances, other than the License."

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** The "free and clear" representation is expressly subject to the License. If the License is later found to be broader than intended (e.g., if the sublicense rights in § 4.3 are construed to permit competitive use), Mouren cannot claim a breach of § 4.8(b) because the encumbrance was disclosed. The protection is entirely in the scope of the License itself — which is why Issue 3 is critical.

**Recommendation:** No drafting change needed here, but this reinforces the importance of narrowing the License (Issue 3). Consider adding to § 4.8(b): "and subject only to the License as set forth in the IP Assignment Agreement in the form attached hereto as Exhibit [X]" (so that any post-signing modification to the License would constitute a breach of the rep).

## Issue 20 – No Escrow or Holdback for Indemnification

**Document / Section:** SPA (Article IX generally)

**Issue:** The SPA provides for indemnification but does not include an escrow, holdback, or security for Seller's indemnification obligations. Mouren's sole security for post-closing indemnification claims is the right of setoff against the Seller Note (§ 9.8) and an unsecured claim against Stamox Vertical Group, Inc.

**Why It Matters for Mouren:** If a material indemnification claim arises (e.g., a code-compliance deficiency or a tax liability), Mouren must pursue Stamox as an unsecured creditor (subject to setoff against the Note, which is itself subordinated to Mouren's senior debt). If Stamox has distributed the $41M cash proceeds and is no longer well-capitalized, recovery may be difficult. The setoff right is useful but limited to the $7.5M Note balance (plus accrued interest), and it is itself subject to the subordination provisions (Note § 7).

**Recommendation:** Negotiate an escrow of $2M–$3M from the cash purchase price, to be held for 18 months post-Closing (aligned with the recommended survival period in Issue 1) and released pro rata as the survival period expires. Alternatively, require Stamox to maintain a minimum net worth or provide a parent guarantee.

**[CLIENT DECISION]** Whether to require an escrow is a significant economic and relationship decision. If Stamox is a going concern with substantial assets (it retains its modernization business and the cash proceeds), Mouren may judge that setoff against the Note is sufficient security. But given the short survival period and the subordination of the Note, an escrow provides meaningful additional protection.
