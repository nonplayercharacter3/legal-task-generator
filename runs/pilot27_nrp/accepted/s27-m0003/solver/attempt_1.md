## Issue 1 – Survival Period Measured from Signing, Not Closing

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 9.1(a)

**Issue:** Non-fundamental representations and warranties survive only "until the date that is twelve (12) months after the date of this Agreement." The Agreement is dated March 2, 2026; the Scheduled Closing Date is September 30, 2026. Mouren therefore has approximately **five months post-Closing** (through March 2, 2027) to discover and assert claims for breaches of virtually all Company-level reps — financial statements, material contracts, IP, licenses and code compliance, environmental/OSHA, insurance, employees, and legal proceedings.

**Why It Matters:** Ascendra is an elevator and escalator service company with heavy regulatory exposure (state periodic inspections, ASME A17.1/A17.3 code compliance, contractor licenses in five states and D.C.), latent safety risk (entrapment, injury, property-damage claims per § 4.9), and OSHA-regulated work. Problems in maintenance control programs, inspection certificates, or code compliance (§ 4.12) may not surface for well over five months. The five-year claims look-back in § 4.9(b) is effectively neutered if Mouren cannot assert a claim until after the survival period has lapsed.

**Recommendation:** Amend § 9.1(a) to provide that non-fundamental representations survive until **eighteen (18) or twenty-four (24) months after the Closing Date** (not the signing date). At minimum, the tax rep (already a Fundamental Representation) and the environmental/OSHA rep should have longer survival. If Seller resists a blanket extension, negotiate category-specific survival: 24 months for § 4.12 (licenses/code compliance), § 4.13 (EHS), and § 4.9 (legal proceedings); 18 months for the remainder.

## Issue 2 – License Back to Seller Is Perpetual, Irrevocable, and Permits Modification and Sublicensing to Customers

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement, §§ 4.2, 4.3, 4.4

**Issue:** The Company grants Stamox a "perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, fully paid-up license" to "use, reproduce, modify, create derivative works of, and distribute in object-code form the LiftSense Software" (§ 4.2). Stamox may sublicense to its "Affiliates, contractors and **customers**" (§ 4.3). Stamox owns all post-closing Improvements and has no obligation to share them with the Company (§ 4.4).

**Why It Matters:** The "Retained Business" is defined as Stamox's modernization and new-installation business (§ 1.2, cross-referencing SPA § 6.6(c)). The license expressly covers "monitoring and diagnosis of elevators and escalators that Seller modernizes or installs" (§ 4.2). Combined with the right to modify and create derivative works, and to sublicense to customers, Stamox can build a parallel LiftSense-based monitoring offering for its modernization customers — potentially in the same six-state footprint where the non-compete carves out its modernization business (SPA § 6.6(c)). Over time, Stamox's version (to which it retains all improvements) could diverge from and surpass the Company's version, eroding the competitive value of the asset Mouren is paying for.

**Recommendation:**
- Limit the license to **internal use** in the Retained Business; delete the right to sublicense to customers.
- Delete or narrow the right to "modify" and "create derivative works." If modification is conceded for internal compatibility, require that any modification that enhances the core diagnostic or monitoring functionality be licensed back to the Company on the same royalty-free terms.
- Add a **most-favored-customer** or **non-discrimination** clause: if Stamox grants any sublicense to a customer, the Company is entitled to the same terms.
- **[CLIENT DECISION]** The breadth of the license back is a business judgment call. If Mouren's strategic view is that LiftSense's value lies in its deployment base and data rather than its code, the current terms may be acceptable. If Mouren intends to productize or extend LiftSense, the current license back is a material competitive risk.

## Issue 3 – Asymmetric Assignment Rights in the IP Assignment Agreement

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement, § 9.2

**Issue:** § 9.2(a) permits Seller to assign the Agreement "in whole or in part, together with the License and its other rights and obligations," to "any Person, including to any successor by merger, consolidation or other business combination, or to any purchaser of all or any portion of Seller's assets or equity," **without the consent of any other party**. § 9.2(b) provides that "Neither Buyer nor the Company may assign this Agreement or any of its rights or obligations under it without the prior written consent of Seller."

**Why It Matters:** Stamox can transfer its perpetual, royalty-free license (including modification and sublicensing rights) to any third party — a direct competitor of the Company, a private equity buyer of Stamox's modernization business, or an unrelated entity — with no consent right for Mouren or the Company. Conversely, if Mouren wishes to restructure (e.g., move the Company to a different holding entity, or sell a portion of the business), it needs Stamox's consent. This asymmetry is the inverse of market-standard and gives Stamox a free option to monetize or transfer the license.

**Recommendation:** Make assignment rights **mutual**: neither party may assign without the other's prior written consent, not to be unreasonably withheld. If Seller insists on free assignability for its own M&A flexibility, limit it to a successor that assumes all of Seller's obligations under the Agreement (including the non-compete in SPA § 6.6) and that is not a direct competitor of the Company in elevator maintenance or inspection.

## Issue 4 – No Independent Title Representation for Fleet Assets

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, § 5.1; Stock Purchase Agreement, § 4.7

**Issue:** Bill of Sale § 5.1 states: "The representations and warranties concerning the Fleet Assets are those made by Seller in the Purchase Agreement, including Section 4.7 of the Purchase Agreement." However, SPA § 4.7 represents that "The Company has good and valid title to, or a valid leasehold interest in, **all of its tangible assets**." The Fleet Assets are owned by **Stamox Fleet Services, LLC** (an Affiliate of Seller), not by the Company. The Company has no title to the Fleet Assets before the Bill of Sale is delivered. Therefore, § 4.7 does not actually represent that Transferor has good and marketable title to the 32 vehicles, hoisting equipment, test weights, and tooling.

**Why It Matters:** If any vehicle is subject to a lien (e.g., a fleet financing arrangement at Stamox Fleet Services that was not disclosed), or if any piece of hoisting equipment is leased rather than owned, the Company receives defective title. The UCC-3 termination requirement in Bill of Sale § 2.3 is a covenant, not a representation — breach of a covenant is indemnifiable under SPA § 9.2(b), but the cap and deductible still apply, and the survival period (Issue 1) limits the window to discover the problem.

**Recommendation:** Add a standalone representation in the Bill of Sale (or in the SPA) that Transferor is the sole record and beneficial owner of the Fleet Assets, free and clear of all liens, security interests, leases and encumbrances, and has good and marketable title. Alternatively, add a specific rep in SPA Article III (Seller's reps) covering title to the Fleet Assets as held by its Affiliate.

## Issue 5 – Governing Law Conflict Between the SPA and the Seller Note

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 11.8(a) (Delaware); Seller Promissory Note, § 12.1 (New York); Stock Purchase Agreement, § 11.12 (Order of Precedence)

**Issue:** The SPA is governed by Delaware law. The Seller Note is governed by New York law. SPA § 11.12 provides that in the event of a conflict, the SPA prevails "except that the Seller Note shall control with respect to matters arising under or relating to the Seller Note." This creates a two-law regime for a single integrated transaction.

**Why It Matters:** The most likely flashpoint is the **setoff/withholding mechanism**. SPA § 9.8 (Delaware law) grants Mouren the right to set off indemnification claims against Note payments. Note § 6 (New York law) mirrors this right but is interpreted under NY law. If a dispute arises over whether a setoff was properly exercised (e.g., whether a Claim Notice was sufficient, whether the good-faith estimate was reasonable, or whether the withholding constituted a default), the applicable law is ambiguous. NY and DE differ on certain aspects of setoff, notice requirements, and the standards for good faith. Additionally, Note § 8.1(a) carves out setoff and payment blockage from the payment-default trigger — but the interpretation of that carve-out may differ under NY vs. DE law.

**Recommendation:** Conform the Note's governing law to Delaware to match the SPA and the other Ancillary Documents. If Seller insists on NY law (perhaps due to its banking relationships), add an express provision in the SPA stating that the setoff and withholding rights in § 9.8 shall be construed under Delaware law notwithstanding the Note's choice of NY law, and that any conflict between § 9.8 and Note § 6 shall be resolved in favor of § 9.8.

## Issue 6 – Non-Compete Carve-Out for Modernization and New-Installation Business

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 6.6(c)

**Issue:** The five-year non-compete in § 6.6(a) is expressly subject to the carve-out: "Nothing in this Section 6.6 restricts Seller's modernization and new-installation business as conducted on the date of this Agreement."

**Why It Matters:** The LiftSense license back (Issue 2) is scoped to the "Retained Business," which is defined as this same modernization and new-installation business (IP Assignment § 1.2). Stamox can therefore continue to operate in all six states (DE, MD, NJ, PA, VA, DC), use LiftSense to monitor the elevators it modernizes or installs, and sublicense that monitoring to its customers. If Ascendra's Service Contracts include modernization scope or if customers expect integrated maintenance-plus-modernization service, Stamox remains a direct competitor with access to the same technology platform.

**Recommendation:**
- **[CLIENT DECISION]** Determine whether Ascendra's revenue includes meaningful modernization or new-installation components. If so, the carve-out is more dangerous.
- Narrow the carve-out geographically (e.g., limit it to states where Stamox's modernization business was "primarily conducted" as of the signing date, with a revenue threshold).
- Add a covenant that Stamox will not use the LiftSense license to provide monitoring services to any building for which the Company holds a Service Contract.
- Consider shortening the non-compete carve-out: after two years, the modernization business should also be restricted.

## Issue 7 – Deductible (True Basket) Rather Than Tipping Threshold

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 9.5(b)

**Issue:** "Seller shall not be liable under Section 9.2(a) until the aggregate Losses under that Section exceed $242,500, **and then only for Losses in excess of that amount**." This is a true deductible: Mouren absorbs the first $242,500 and never recovers it. A "tipping basket" (or threshold) would provide that once losses exceed $242,500, Seller is liable for the **full** amount from dollar one.

**Why It Matters:** In a deal with the regulatory and safety profile of an elevator service company, it is not uncommon to have multiple small claims (e.g., individual inspection citations, minor contract breaches, small tax adjustments) that individually are below the deductible but collectively exceed it. Under a true deductible, Mouren always loses the first $242,500. Under a tipping basket, once the threshold is crossed, Mouren recovers everything.

**Recommendation:** Convert to a tipping basket: "Seller shall not be liable under Section 9.2(a) until the aggregate Losses under that Section exceed $242,500, and then Seller shall be liable for all Losses under that Section from the first dollar." If Seller resists, consider reducing the deductible to $150,000 (≈ 0.3% of the Purchase Price) as a compromise.

## Issue 8 – Change-of-Control Trigger on the Seller Note

**Document / Section:** Seller Promissory Note, § 8.1(d)

**Issue:** An Event of Default occurs if "Maker sells all or substantially all of the Shares, or the Company sells all or substantially all of its assets, to a Person that is not an Affiliate of Maker, unless this Note is repaid in full at the closing of that sale."

**Why It Matters:** This restricts Mouren's future strategic flexibility. If Mouren wishes to (a) sell Ascendra as part of a portfolio rationalization, (b) contribute Ascendra's shares to a joint venture or new subsidiary, or (c) sell substantially all of Ascendra's assets (e.g., divest a geographic market), it must repay the $7.5M Note in full at closing. This could be a significant cash outflow at an inopportune time. The "Affiliate" exception provides some relief for internal restructurings, but a sale to a strategic buyer or a carve-out to a different Mouren subsidiary that is not structured as an Affiliate transaction would trigger the default.

**Recommendation:**
- **[CLIENT DECISION]** Determine whether Mouren's medium-term strategy contemplates any divestiture, JV, or restructure involving Ascendra. If so, negotiate the trigger down.
- As a drafting fix: limit the trigger to a sale to a **direct competitor** of the Company, or require repayment only if the transferee assumes the Company's elevator service business. Alternatively, replace the mandatory repayment with a **yield maintenance** or make-whole provision.
- Clarify that a sale of the Shares to an Affiliate of Maker that is not itself a competitor does not trigger the default (the current "Affiliate" language should cover this, but confirm the definition of Affiliate in the SPA is broad enough).

## Issue 9 – Working Capital Closing Statement Prepared by Buyer

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 2.3(b)

**Issue:** "Within ninety (90) days after the Closing Date, **Buyer** shall deliver a statement of actual closing net working capital."

**Why It Matters:** In the typical structure, Seller prepares the post-closing working capital statement and Buyer objects. Here, the roles are reversed: Mouren (as Buyer) prepares the statement. While this gives Mouren control over the process, it also means Mouren bears the preparation cost and the risk of error. If Mouren's statement is challenged by Seller and the independent accountant finds the statement was prepared in bad faith or with material error, Mouren could face reputational and cost consequences. More practically, if Mouren's finance team is stretched at integration, a 90-day deadline to produce a defensible working capital statement for a multi-state elevator service company (with complex inventory of parts, test weights, and vehicle-related working capital) may be tight.

**Recommendation:** This is a lower-priority item. If Mouren's team is comfortable preparing the statement, the current structure is acceptable (and arguably favorable since Mouren controls the narrative). If there is concern about the 90-day deadline, extend to 120 days. Ensure the definition of "net working capital" (not shown in the excerpts provided) excludes the Fleet Assets and LiftSense Software (which are transferred separately) to avoid double-counting.

## Issue 10 – NDA Customer Non-Solicitation Is One-Way

**Document / Section:** Mutual NDA, § 5.3

**Issue:** "Mouren shall not use any Confidential Information to solicit any customer of Ascendra for elevator or escalator maintenance, repair or inspection services." The restriction applies only to Mouren, not to Stamox. There is no reciprocal provision preventing Stamox from using information learned about Mouren's existing building-services customers to solicit them.

**Why It Matters:** If the deal breaks, Mouren is barred from approaching Ascendra's customers using any information gained during the deal process, but Stamox faces no similar restriction regarding Mouren's existing customer base. Even if the deal closes, this provision could create ambiguity about whether Mouren's post-Closing solicitation of Ascendra customers (now its own) constitutes a breach — though practically it should not, since the customers are then Mouren's.

**Recommendation:** Make the restriction mutual: "Neither Party shall use the other Party's Confidential Information to solicit any customer of the other Party or its Affiliates." Alternatively, if the deal closes, add a provision that § 5.3 terminates automatically at the Closing.

## Issue 11 – Existing Customer Licenses to LiftSense Not Disclosed

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement, § 6.1(b)

**Issue:** Seller represents that it "has not granted any license, option or other right in the Assigned IP that remains in effect, **other than non-exclusive licenses to the Company and to customers in the ordinary course**."

**Why It Matters:** The "customers in the ordinary course" exception is unbounded. It could encompass a significant number of building owners or property managers who have been granted access to LiftSense monitoring as part of their service agreements with Ascendra. These licenses survive the assignment (they are not terminated by it) and could impose obligations on the Company (e.g., continued hosting, support, data access) that are not reflected in the Purchase Price or the Service Contract schedules. Mouren should know the scope of these licenses before Closing.

**Recommendation:** Require Seller to identify all existing customer licenses to LiftSense in the Disclosure Schedule (or in a new schedule to the IP Assignment Agreement), including the terms, duration, and any support or hosting obligations. Add a rep that no such license grants the customer the right to modify, reverse-engineer, or sublicense the software.

## Issue 12 – Transition Support Is Limited to 90 Days and 2 Personnel

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement, § 3.4

**Issue:** For 90 days post-Closing, Seller makes available "up to two of its personnel familiar with the LiftSense Software" to answer "reasonable questions concerning its architecture, deployment and maintenance."

**Why It Matters:** LiftSense is a multi-component platform (edge-device firmware, cloud analytics service, technician mobile application, fault-code libraries, controller-integration modules — per Schedule A). Two personnel for 90 days may be insufficient if the Company's IT team encounters deployment issues, firmware compatibility problems with specific elevator controllers, or cloud infrastructure migration challenges. The provision does not require the personnel to be dedicated (they could be splitting time with Stamox's own business), nor does it cover post-90-day issues.

**Recommendation:**
- Extend the transition period to **180 days**.
- Require that the personnel be **dedicated** (or at minimum 50% allocated) to the Company during the transition period.
- Add a tiered structure: 2 personnel for the first 90 days, 1 personnel for days 91–180.
- Include a right to extend by mutual agreement if critical issues remain unresolved.

## Issue 13 – No Requirement for Interim Financials or Quality-of-Earnings Report

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 4.4 (financial statements through December 31, 2025); § 4.5 (ordinary course / no MAE since December 31, 2025)

**Issue:** The most recent financial statements are as of December 31, 2025. The Scheduled Closing Date is September 30, 2026 — a nine-month gap. There is no covenant requiring Seller to deliver interim (e.g., quarterly) financial statements between signing and Closing, nor any quality-of-earnings report.

**Why It Matters:** Mouren is paying $48.5M based on financials that will be nine months old at Closing. The § 4.5 rep (ordinary course, no MAE) provides some protection, but it is a binary rep — either the business was conducted in the ordinary course or it wasn't. It does not give Mouren visibility into revenue trends, working capital drift, or margin compression during the interim period. The working capital adjustment (§ 2.3) addresses only net working capital at Closing, not P&L performance.

**Recommendation:**
- Add a covenant requiring Seller to deliver unaudited interim financial statements (balance sheet and income statement) within 45 days after the end of each fiscal quarter during the interim period.
- Consider adding a **covenant not to take specific actions** that would erode value (e.g., not to defer maintenance, not to accept below-market contract renewals, not to increase headcount beyond a stated level) — some of this is covered by § 6.1(c), but a more specific list would be protective.
- **[CLIENT DECISION]** If Mouren's diligence team is comfortable with the nine-month gap based on the strength of the ordinary-course covenant and the MAE closing condition, this may be acceptable. Otherwise, interim financials are a meaningful protection.

## Issue 14 – Bill of Sale: Vague Retitling Timeline

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, § 3.2

**Issue:** "Transferor shall cooperate with the Company to retitle and reregister each Vehicle in the Company's name **as promptly as practicable** after the Effective Date."

**Why It Matters:** There is no fixed deadline. The 32 vehicles are titled in five different states (MD, PA, NJ, VA, DE per Schedule A, Part 1). Multi-state retitling can take weeks to months depending on DMV backlogs. Until retitling is complete, the vehicles remain registered in Transferor's name. If an accident occurs, the registered owner (Transferor) may be served with process, and the Company's insurance may be complicated by the title mismatch. § 3.2 does provide that Transferor shall maintain registration and permit use, but this is a covenant, not a rep, and its breach is subject to the cap and deductible.

**Recommendation:** Add a firm deadline: "Transferor shall use commercially reasonable efforts to complete retitling and reregistration of all Vehicles within **sixty (60) days** after the Effective Date." Add a rep that all Vehicles are currently registered and in compliance with the motor vehicle laws of their title states as of the Effective Date.

## Issue 15 – SPA § 4.8(b) Acknowledges the License Back as the Only Encumbrance on LiftSense

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 4.8(b)

**Issue:** "Upon the Closing, the Company will own the LiftSense Software free and clear of all encumbrances, **subject only to the license granted back to Seller under the IP Assignment Agreement**."

**Why It Matters:** This rep effectively blesses the license back as the sole encumbrance. If the license back is later found to be broader than intended (e.g., if the "customers in the ordinary course" exception in IP Assignment § 6.1(b) reveals additional licenses), the § 4.8(b) rep may not be breached because the rep only accounts for the "license granted back." The rep does not address the customer licenses separately.

**Recommendation:** Amend § 4.8(b) to read: "subject only to (i) the license granted back to Seller under the IP Assignment Agreement and (ii) the customer licenses identified in Schedule [X]." This forces Seller to disclose all encumbrances specifically.

## Issue 16 – Order of Precedence May Create Gaps

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 11.12

**Issue:** "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Document, this Agreement shall prevail, **except that the Seller Note shall control with respect to matters arising under or relating to the Seller Note**."

**Why It Matters:** The IP Assignment Agreement and the Bill of Sale are also Ancillary Documents. If there is a conflict between the SPA and the IP Assignment (e.g., on the scope of the license back, or on survival of IP reps), the SPA prevails. But the SPA's treatment of the LiftSense Software (in § 4.8) is less detailed than the IP Assignment. A conflict could arise on, for example, whether the "Assigned IP" includes future versions (IP Assignment § 1.2 defines it as versions "in existence on the Effective Date" — future versions are not included, but the SPA's definition of "LiftSense Software" in § 1.1 says "together with all related source code, object code, documentation and intellectual property rights" without a temporal limit). The order-of-precedence clause would resolve this in favor of the SPA's broader definition, but only if the conflict is "arising under or relating to" the SPA rather than the Note. The clause is silent on conflicts between the IP Assignment and the Bill of Sale (neither is the Note).

**Recommendation:** Add a sentence: "In the event of a conflict between the IP Assignment Agreement and the Bill of Sale, the IP Assignment Agreement shall prevail with respect to intellectual property matters and the Bill of Sale shall prevail with respect to tangible asset matters." Also clarify that the SPA's definition of "LiftSense Software" in § 1.1 is subject to the more specific definition in the IP Assignment Agreement.

## Issue 17 – NDA Term May Expire Before All Integration Issues Resolve

**Document / Section:** Mutual NDA, § 7.1

**Issue:** The NDA remains in effect for two years from December 8, 2025 — i.e., until **December 8, 2027**. The SPA's non-fundamental rep survival (as currently drafted) ends March 2, 2027 (see Issue 1). If the survival period is extended to 18 months post-Closing (as recommended), it would end approximately March 30, 2028 — **after** the NDA expires.

**Why It Matters:** This is not a direct conflict, but it highlights a timing issue: if Mouren discovers a rep breach in early 2028 (within an extended survival period) that involves information first received under the NDA, the NDA's confidentiality obligations have lapsed. This is generally not a problem (Mouren would not be disclosing Stamox's confidential information; it would be asserting a claim), but it is worth being aware of.

**Recommendation:** No change needed, but if the survival period is extended, confirm that the NDA's trade-secret carve-out (§ 7.2) adequately protects any LiftSense source code that remains a trade secret beyond December 8, 2027.

## Issue 18 – Seller Note: No Periodic Payment (Bullet Maturity)

**Document / Section:** Seller Promissory Note, § 2

**Issue:** The entire $7.5M principal is due in a single bullet payment at month 36. Only interest is paid quarterly.

**Why It Matters:** From Mouren's perspective, a bullet structure is actually **favorable** — it preserves cash flow during the integration period. However, it creates a refinancing or liquidity risk at month 36. If Mouren's cash position is tight at that time (e.g., due to capex needs for the elevator fleet or working capital requirements), the $7.5M balloon could be a strain. The prepayment right (§ 5) mitigates this — Mouren can pay down the Note early without penalty.

**Recommendation:** **[CLIENT DECISION]** This is a business/treasury matter. If Mouren's financing plan assumes the Note will be outstanding for the full 36 months, no change is needed. If there is uncertainty about liquidity at month 36, consider negotiating an amortizing schedule (e.g., equal quarterly principal payments beginning at month 12) to reduce the balloon. Alternatively, confirm that the prepayment right is sufficient and that Mouren's treasury team has a plan for the month-36 payment.

## Issue 19 – SPA § 6.1(c): Dividend Carve-Out Is Ambiguous

**Document / Section:** Stock Purchase Agreement, § 6.1(c)

**Issue:** Seller shall cause the Company not to, without Buyer's consent, "declare any dividend other than cash dividends paid before the Closing." The phrase "cash dividends paid before the Closing" is ambiguous. Does it mean (a) dividends that were **declared** before Closing but paid after, or (b) dividends that were both declared and paid before Closing? If (a), a dividend declared in February 2026 and paid in October 2026 (post-Closing) would be permitted without consent, draining cash from the Company after Mouren owns it.

**Recommendation:** Clarify: "other than cash dividends **declared and paid** in full before the date of this Agreement." This prevents Seller from declaring a large dividend just before signing and paying it after Closing.

## Issue 20 – IP Assignment: Schedule B (Short-Form Assignment) Not Yet Attached

**Document / Section:** LiftSense IP Assignment Agreement, Schedule B

**Issue:** Schedule B is marked "[To be attached.]" This is the form of short-form assignment to be recorded with the USPTO and Copyright Office.

**Why It Matters:** Without a finalized Schedule B, the Company cannot perfect its recordation of the assignment with federal agencies. While the assignment is effective between the parties without recordation, recordation is important to protect against subsequent assignments by Seller to a bona fide purchaser. Given that Seller has the right to freely assign the Agreement (Issue 3), the risk of a competing recordation is not theoretical.

**Recommendation:** Require that Schedule B be finalized and attached before signing. Ensure the short-form assignment covers all categories in the "Assigned IP" definition (software, source code, data models, fault-code libraries, diagnostic algorithms, the LIFTSENSE trademark, and claims/causes of action).
