## Issue 1 – Non-Renewal Notice Is Two Days Late; Fosais May Be Locked In for Three More Years

**Where:** Agreement §14.1; Side Letter §4.1–4.3.

**Analysis.** Section 14.1 requires written notice of non-renewal "at least 120 days before the end of the then-current term." The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. Counting back 120 days from February 28, 2027 yields October 31, 2026 as the deadline. Fosais delivered its Non-Renewal Notice on November 2, 2026 (Side Letter §4.1) — two days late. Dreitoul has asserted in writing that the notice is "untimely and of no effect" (Side Letter §4.2) and that the Agreement will renew for a three-year Renewal Term through February 28, 2030. Fosais disputes this (Side Letter §4.3) but the Side Letter does not resolve the dispute.

**Why it matters for Fosais.** If Dreitoul's position prevails, Fosais is contractually bound for three additional years, including the exclusivity obligation (Issue 2 below), the Parent Guaranty (Issue 3), and any fee increases up to 5% per renewal (Agreement §5.4). The fee reduction in the Side Letter (§2.1) applies only through the current billing period and does not extend the commercial benefit of the reduced rate beyond what was already negotiated.

**Recommendation.**

- **Urgent:** Assess whether there is a viable argument that the notice was timely. The math is clear (118 days, not 120), so the strongest angles are (a) whether Dreitoul's course of performance or the Side Letter itself constitutes a waiver or estoppel — note that Dreitoul agreed to the fee reduction and additional Seats in the same Side Letter that records its position that the notice is void, which could support an argument that it elected to treat the relationship as continuing on modified terms rather than as renewed; and (b) whether the 120-day requirement is a condition or a covenant, and whether substantial compliance suffices under Delaware law.
- **Practical:** Regardless of the legal merits, negotiate a resolution with Dreitoul. Options include: (i) Dreitoul agrees the notice is effective and the term expires February 28, 2027; (ii) the parties agree to a short extension (e.g., 90 days) to allow Fosais to transition; or (iii) Fosais accepts renewal but negotiates a material reduction in scope (see Issue 2) as the quid pro quo.
- **Flag:** This must be resolved before February 28, 2027. If it goes unresolved, the Agreement renews by its own terms and Fosais's leverage drops significantly.

## Issue 2 – Exclusivity Scope Is Far Broader Than What Was Commercially Agreed

**Where:** Agreement §4.3 (exclusivity), §1.1(c) (definition of "Competing Product"), §1.1(n) (definition of "Territory"); Side Letter Recital B (Term Sheet reference), §1.2 (scope of amendment).

**Analysis.** Agreement §4.3 prohibits Fosais from using, licensing, or developing any Competing Product for **any** Solar Project in the **Territory** (defined as the entire United States and Canada) during the Term. "Competing Product" is defined broadly to include any software providing solar design, panel layout, shading/production analysis, single-line diagram generation, or permit package preparation.

Side Letter Recital B acknowledges that the pre-signing (non-binding) Term Sheet limited exclusivity to "residential rooftop projects in Arizona and Nevada only." However, Side Letter §1.2 expressly states that the Side Letter "does not amend, modify or waive any provision of the Agreement … except for the fee terms set out in Sections 2 and 3." The exclusivity scope was never carried into a binding document.

**Why it matters for Fosais.** As written, Fosais cannot use any competing design or permitting tool for any project type (commercial, utility-scale, etc.) anywhere in the US or Canada for the duration of the Term. If the non-renewal notice fails (Issue 1), this restriction extends to February 2030. Even if the term expires as intended, the restriction applies through February 28, 2027. This likely prevents Fosais from using alternative tools for commercial or utility-scale projects, or for projects in states outside AZ/NV, which may be core to its business.

**Recommendation.**

- Request that Dreitoul amend §4.3 to conform to the Term Sheet scope (residential rooftop, AZ and NV only), or at minimum to exclude commercial and utility-scale projects. Cite Recital B as evidence of the parties' original commercial understanding.
- If Dreitoul resists, negotiate a carve-out: e.g., exclusivity applies only to residential projects in AZ and NV, or only to projects where the Platform is actually used for design.
- **Do not sign any further Order Forms** that add Seats or modules until this is resolved, as each new Order Form is governed by the Agreement (including §4.3) and may be construed as reaffirming the current scope.
- **Confirm with client:** What competing tools does Fosais currently use or plan to use, and for what project types and geographies? This will frame the negotiation.

## Issue 3 – Parent Guaranty Backstops an Unlimited Indemnity for Installation Claims

**Where:** Agreement §11.2 (Licensee indemnity), §12.3(a) (indemnity excluded from liability cap); Guaranty §1.2(c) (indemnity obligations are Guaranteed Obligations), §2.1 (absolute, unconditional, primary obligor).

**Analysis.** Section 11.2 requires Fosais to indemnify Dreitoul for **all** third-party claims arising from any Installation, including bodily injury, property damage, roof or water damage, fire, electrical failure, code violations, and permitting deficiencies. This indemnity is not subject to the liability cap (§12.3(a) carves out §11 obligations). The Parent Guaranty makes Fosais Holdings Corp. a primary obligor for all of this, on demand, within 10 business days (Guaranty §2.2), with broad waivers of surety defenses (§4.1).

**Why it matters for Fosais.** The parent entity is exposed to uncapped, first-dollar liability for installation-related claims that have nothing to do with the software. A single major roof damage or electrical fire claim at a utility-scale project could generate eight- or nine-figure exposure. The guaranty continues through any Renewal Term (Guaranty §3.2(a)), compounding the risk if Issue 1 resolves against Fosais.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a carve-out or cap on the guaranty as it applies to §11.2 indemnification. At minimum, the guaranty should not cover indemnity obligations arising from claims that are not related to the Platform's design output (e.g., construction defects, contractor error).
- Alternatively, require that the §11.2 indemnity be subject to the §12.1 cap (i.e., delete §11 from the §12.3(a) carve-out) or at least subject to a super-cap (e.g., 3× annual fees).
- **Confirm with client:** What is Fosais Holdings Corp.'s balance sheet and ability to absorb a large guaranty call? Are there other creditors whose security could be impaired?
- **Flag:** If the term renews (Issue 1), the guaranty exposure extends three years with no additional consideration to the parent. This should be a condition of any settlement on the non-renewal dispute.

## Issue 4 – Minimum Seat Count Creates a Fee Cliff

**Where:** Side Letter §2.3.

**Analysis.** The reduced Seat fee of $2,160 (down from $2,400) is conditioned on maintaining at least 40 active Seats from December 1, 2026. If active Seats drop below 40, the original $2,400 fee "immediately" applies from the first day of the following calendar month — a retroactive-style reset that could generate a significant true-up.

**Why it matters for Fosais.** Solar installation work is seasonal. If Fosais experiences a downturn or restructures its workforce, it could dip below 40 and trigger the higher rate for the entire following month (or longer if the shortfall persists). The 15 additional Seats permitted under Side Letter §2.2 are optional, so there is no obligation to add seats to maintain the minimum.

**Recommendation.**

- **Confirm with client:** How many active Seats does Fosais currently have, and what is the realistic low point during a slow season? If the margin below 40 is thin, negotiate a buffer (e.g., minimum of 35) or a grace period (e.g., the fee resets only if the count is below 40 for two consecutive months).
- Request clarification on what constitutes an "active" Seat — is it a Seat that has logged in during the month, or a Seat that is merely assigned?

## Issue 5 – Workers' Compensation Coverage May Not Match Operating Footprint

**Where:** Agreement §13.1(b); Insurance Certificate §3.1 (table).

**Analysis.** The Agreement requires workers' compensation "as required by applicable law." The certificate shows workers' comp coverage for "Arizona and Nevada" only. The Territory is the US and Canada, and the exclusivity clause (Issue 2) contemplates Fosais working on Solar Projects broadly. If Fosais has employees or contractors performing work in other states, it likely needs workers' comp in those states as well, and the current certificate may not evidence compliance with §13.1(b).

**Recommendation.**

- **Confirm with client:** In which states does Fosais currently employ workers or perform installations? If the answer is only AZ and NV, the coverage is adequate. If it includes other states, Fosais needs to either (a) add those states to the workers' comp policy and deliver an updated certificate, or (b) confirm that the "as required by applicable law" language means only the states where it actually has a workforce.
- This is a compliance item, not a negotiation item, but a gap here would put Fosais in technical breach of §13.1 and could trigger the suspension right in §5.5 if coupled with a payment dispute.

## Issue 6 – Data Return Window Is Short for a Design Firm

**Where:** Agreement §7.4.

**Analysis.** Upon expiration or termination, Licensor makes Licensee Data available for export for only 30 days, after which it may delete it. For a solar design firm with complex, multi-phase projects (design → permitting → construction → as-built), 30 days may be insufficient to extract all relevant data, particularly if the transition is contested (as it may be under Issue 1).

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate an extension to 60 or 90 days, or tie the return period to the resolution of any termination dispute.
- In the interim, confirm with the client that Fosais has a current data export/backup process in place so it is not dependent on the 30-day window.

## Issue 7 – Side Letter Precedence Language Undercuts Amendment Power

**Where:** Agreement §16.2; Side Letter §1.1–1.2.

**Analysis.** Agreement §16.2 provides that the main Agreement "prevails over the Side Letter and any Order Form." Side Letter §1.1 characterizes the Side Letter as an amendment under §16.5, and §1.2 limits the amendment to fee terms only. The practical effect is that on any point of conflict, the main Agreement controls. This is why the exclusivity gap (Issue 2) cannot be cured by pointing to Recital B — the Side Letter does not (and, under §16.2, could not) amend §4.3 unless the main Agreement itself is restated or a new amendment is executed.

**Recommendation.**

- Any fix to the exclusivity scope must be done through a proper amendment to the Agreement (or a new Side Letter that expressly amends §4.3 and overrides §16.2 to the extent necessary). Do not rely on recitals or course of dealing.
- If negotiating a global settlement (Issues 1–3 together), structure it as a formal amendment to the Agreement rather than a second Side Letter, to avoid the precedence problem recurring.
