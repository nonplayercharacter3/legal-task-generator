## Summary

The deal set contains three issues that require immediate attention before the Initial Term expires on February 28, 2027: (1) the non-renewal notice appears to be two days late, which would lock Fosais into another three-year term; (2) the exclusivity covenant in the Agreement is far broader than what the parties originally negotiated per the Term Sheet referenced in the Side Letter; and (3) the indemnification clause, read with the definition of "Installation," effectively makes Fosais (and its parent guarantor) liable for the entire installation business regardless of whether the software was used. The Parent Guaranty amplifies each of these exposures.

## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Likely Untimely

**Documents:** Side Letter §§4.1–4.4; Agreement §14.1

**Facts.** Section 14.1 requires written notice of non-renewal "at least 120 days before the end of the then-current term." The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. One hundred twenty days before that date is October 31, 2026. The Side Letter (§4.1) states the Non-Renewal Notice was "delivered on November 2, 2026" by email to legal@dreitoul.com with a courtesy copy by overnight courier. That is two days after the deadline. Licensor's position (§4.2) is that the notice is of no effect and the Agreement will renew. Licensee disputes (§4.3).

**Why it matters.** If the notice fails, Fosais is bound for a Renewal Term through approximately February 2030, with all obligations—including exclusivity, the Parent Guaranty, and the fee structure—continuing. The Side Letter §4.4 expressly disclaims any waiver, so the dispute remains live.

**Possible arguments for Fosais.** Section 16.3 provides that email notice is effective "upon… email with confirmation of transmission." If the email was actually transmitted on October 31 and the confirmation arrived on November 2, there is an argument that the notice was effective on October 31. However, the Side Letter's own recital that the notice "was delivered on November 2, 2026" is a signed statement by both parties that will be very difficult to overcome in litigation.

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm with the client the exact date and time the email was sent and whether a transmission confirmation exists showing an October 31 send date. This is the single most important fact to establish immediately.
- If the email was sent on or before October 31, prepare a position paper asserting timeliness under §16.3 and consider sending a supplemental notice now to cure any ambiguity (though a late cure may not be effective as a matter of Delaware law).
- If the email was sent on November 2, the notice is late. In that case, the strategic question shifts to whether Fosais wants to negotiate a release from the Renewal Term (e.g., in exchange for a fee or a shortened commitment) or whether it is prepared to perform for three additional years.
- Either way, flag to the client that this dispute will likely need to be resolved before the February 28, 2027 expiration to avoid operating under uncertainty.

**Status: URGENT – resolve before February 28, 2027.**

## Issue 2: Exclusivity Covenant Is Broader Than Originally Negotiated

**Documents:** Agreement §4.3, §1.1(c), §1.1(n); Side Letter Recital B, §1.2

**Facts.** Section 4.3 prohibits Licensee from using, licensing, or developing any "Competing Product" for any "Solar Project" in the "Territory" during the Term. The defined terms make this extremely broad:
- "Competing Product" (§1.1(c)) covers any software providing solar PV design, panel layout, shading/production analysis, single-line diagram generation, or permit package preparation.
- "Solar Project" (§1.1(l)) covers all sizes and types: residential, commercial, industrial, community, utility-scale; rooftop, ground-mounted, carport.
- "Territory" (§1.1(n)) is the entire United States and Canada.

Side Letter Recital B acknowledges that the pre-signing Term Sheet (January 10, 2024) agreed exclusivity would extend to "residential rooftop projects in Arizona and Nevada only." However, Side Letter §1.2 states the Side Letter does not amend the Agreement except for the fee terms in §§2–3. Agreement §16.2 provides the Agreement prevails over the Side Letter.

**Why it matters.** If the non-renewal notice fails (Issue 1), Fosais is locked into three more years of exclusivity across the entire US and Canada for all project types. This would prevent Fosais from using any alternative design software, developing in-house tools, or licensing competing products for utility-scale or commercial projects in any state. That is a severe business restriction that goes well beyond what the Term Sheet contemplated.

Even if the non-renewal succeeds, the current term (through February 2027) is still subject to the broad exclusivity, and the discrepancy is relevant to any claim for reformation or to the negotiating posture in the non-renewal dispute.

**Recommendation.**
- Raise the Term Sheet discrepancy with Dreitoul as part of the non-renewal negotiation. The Side Letter's own recital acknowledges the narrower scope was the parties' understanding. While the Term Sheet was non-binding, the recital in a signed Side Letter creates a factual record that supports a reformation argument or at least strong commercial leverage.
- If Fosais intends to renew or is forced to renew, negotiate an amendment to §4.3 conforming it to the Term Sheet scope (residential rooftop, AZ and NV only) or a materially narrower formulation.
- Confirm with the client whether Fosais currently uses or plans to use any other design or permitting tools. If so, there may already be a technical breach of §4.3 that Dreitoul could leverage.

**Status: HIGH – directly tied to Issue 1 outcome.**

## Issue 3: Indemnification Covers All Installations Whether or Not the Platform Was Used

**Documents:** Agreement §11.2, §1.1(g), §12.3(a); Parent Guaranty §1.2(c)

**Facts.** Section 11.2 requires Licensee to indemnify Licensor against "all losses… arising from any claim brought by a third party arising out of or relating to any Installation, including claims for bodily injury, death, property damage, roof or water damage, fire, electrical failure, code violations or permitting deficiencies." "Installation" is defined in §1.1(g) as any solar PV system, battery storage, mounting structure, electrical interconnection or related equipment "designed, sold, installed, maintained or serviced by or on behalf of Licensee, **whether or not designed using the Platform**."

Section 12.3(a) carves indemnification obligations out of the liability cap, so this exposure is uncapped. The Parent Guaranty §1.2(c) explicitly includes "all of Licensee's defense, indemnification and hold-harmless obligations" as Guaranteed Obligations.

**Why it matters.** This is not a software indemnity. It is a full business-line indemnity. If a home catches fire because of a wiring error on a project that had nothing to do with HelioDraft, Fosais must defend Dreitoul and pay all resulting losses, and Fosais Holdings Corp. guarantees that obligation as primary obligor with no cap. The "whether or not designed using the Platform" language makes the software license a conduit for Dreitoul's protection against the entire installation business.

This is compounded by §8.3, which classifies Platform-generated designs as "Licensee Data," and §11.2's reference to "any claim arising from Licensee Data." If the Platform generates a flawed design, the output is Licensee Data, and a third-party claim arising from it triggers Fosais's indemnity to Dreitoul.

**Recommendation.**
- Push to delete "whether or not designed using the Platform" from the definition of "Installation" in §1.1(g), or alternatively narrow §11.2 to claims "arising from or relating to the Platform or Licensee's use thereof."
- If Dreitoul resists, seek at minimum a cap on the §11.2 indemnity (e.g., tied to insurance limits or a multiple of annual fees) and an express carve-out for claims caused by the Platform's own defects.
- Confirm with the client: what is Fosais's current CGL insurance tower? The $2M per occurrence limit in §13.1(a) is the minimum contractually required, but the actual indemnity exposure from a serious installation claim (e.g., a fire causing property damage and bodily injury) could exceed that. The parent guaranty means the exposure is not limited to the LLC.
- Flag to the client that Fosais Holdings Corp. should understand the full scope of what it has guaranteed before the next renewal cycle.

**Status: HIGH – structural risk that persists regardless of the non-renewal outcome.**

## Issue 4: Parent Guaranty Is Open-Ended and Auto-Continues on Renewal

**Documents:** Parent Guaranty §§2.1, 3.1, 3.2(a), 3.3

**Facts.** The Guaranty is:
- Absolute, unconditional, irrevocable, and given as primary obligor (§2.1).
- Continuing until all Guaranteed Obligations are paid in full (§3.1).
- Not affected by any renewal, amendment, or change in the subsidiary's ownership (§3.2).
- Reinstated if any payment is rescinded in the subsidiary's bankruptcy (§3.3).
- Covers all indemnification obligations (see Issue 3) and all covenants.

**Why it matters.** If the Agreement renews for a three-year term (per Issue 1), the Guaranty continues automatically with no new action by Fosais Holdings Corp. and no opportunity for the parent to reassess. There is no dollar cap, no sunset, and no requirement that the parent re-authorize. The parent's board (or equivalent governing body) should be aware that its exposure extends through at least February 2030 if the renewal takes effect, and potentially beyond if further renewals occur.

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm with the client whether Fosais Holdings Corp.'s board or investment committee has approved the Guaranty for the full potential duration (including renewal terms) or only for the Initial Term.
- If the non-renewal is successful, the Guaranty will still cover accrued obligations and surviving indemnities, but the ongoing exposure ends. If the renewal takes effect, consider negotiating a release or amendment to the Guaranty as part of any settlement of the non-renewal dispute.
- At minimum, ensure the parent's treasury and risk functions are modeling the uncapped indemnity exposure (Issue 3) as a contingent liability.

**Status: HIGH – contingent on Issue 1 outcome.**

## Issue 5: Minimum Seat Count Creates a Fee Penalty

**Documents:** Side Letter §2.3

**Facts.** The reduced Seat fee of $2,160 (down from $2,400) is conditioned on maintaining at least 40 active Seats from December 1, 2026. If active Seats drop below 40, the fee reverts to $2,400 "from the first day of the following calendar month."

**Why it matters.** This is a one-way ratchet. Fosais gets the discount only by committing to a minimum headcount. If the company downsizes, loses contractors, or if the non-renewal is successful and Fosais is winding down its use of the Platform, it could trigger the higher rate retroactively to the start of the month. The 10% penalty ($240/seat/year) on potentially 40+ seats is $9,600+ per year.

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm with the client the current number of active Seats and whether there is any planned reduction in the near term.
- If the non-renewal is successful and Fosais will not use the Platform after February 28, 2027, negotiate the release of the minimum seat condition as part of the wind-down.
- If the renewal takes effect, consider whether the 40-seat minimum is operationally realistic given hiring plans.

**Status: MODERATE – financial impact is limited but the condition is one-directional.**

## Issue 6: Workers' Compensation Coverage May Not Match Operating Footprint

**Documents:** Insurance Certificate §3.1; Agreement §13.1(b)

**Facts.** The certificate shows workers' compensation at statutory limits for Arizona and Nevada only. The Agreement requires workers' comp "as required by applicable law." The Territory is the entire US and Canada.

**Why it matters.** If Fosais performs installations or employs workers in any state other than Arizona or Nevada, the current policy may not satisfy §13.1(b) for those jurisdictions, putting Fosais in technical breach of the insurance covenant. A breach of §13.1 is a material breach that could support termination by Dreitoul under §14.2 (though Dreitoul would need to give 30 days to cure).

**Recommendation.**
- Confirm with the client in which states Fosais currently operates and employs workers. If only AZ and NV, the coverage is adequate. If there are (or will be) operations elsewhere, additional workers' comp coverage is needed before the next renewal date (October 1, 2027) or before expanding into new states.
- Note that the policy period runs October 1, 2026 to October 1, 2027. If the Agreement renews, coverage must be in place continuously.

**Status: MODERATE – confirm facts with client.**

## Issue 7: Data Return Window May Be Operationally Insufficient

**Documents:** Agreement §7.4

**Facts.** Upon expiration or termination, Licensor makes Licensee Data available for export for only 30 days, after which it may be deleted.

**Why it matters.** A solar installation company may have multi-year projects in various stages of design, permitting, and construction. If the Agreement terminates (whether by expiration after a successful non-renewal or by cause), Fosais has 30 days to export all project data, drawings, and permit packages. Missing that window means losing access to data that §7.1 says Fosais owns.

**Recommendation.**
- Negotiate an extension of the export window to 90 days, or at minimum tie it to the completion of any projects in active permitting or construction.
- If the non-renewal is successful, begin planning the data migration well before February 28, 2027. Identify which projects are in-flight and what data is needed for ongoing permitting and construction.
- Confirm with the client what format the Platform exports in and whether it is compatible with Fosais's other systems.

**Status: MODERATE – becomes urgent if non-renewal is successful.**

## Issue 8: Dispute Resolution Forum Is Inconvenient

**Documents:** Agreement §15.2; Parent Guaranty §7.2

**Facts.** All proceedings must be brought exclusively in New Castle County, Delaware. Both the Agreement and the Guaranty submit the parties to Delaware's personal jurisdiction.

**Why it matters.** Fosais is headquartered in Phoenix. Any dispute (including the non-renewal notice issue) must be litigated in Delaware, adding travel, local counsel, and cost. This is not unusual in commercial agreements, but combined with the live non-renewal dispute, it means Fosais will need Delaware litigation counsel if the parties cannot resolve the issue commercially.

**Recommendation.**
- For the immediate non-renewal dispute, assess whether the parties can resolve it by negotiation (see Issue 1) before incurring Delaware litigation costs.
- For any renegotiation or amendment, consider whether the forum can be moved to Arizona or whether arbitration (with a neutral seat) is preferable. This is a lower-priority ask given the current dispute, but worth raising if the parties are already at the table.

**Status: LOW – cost consideration, not a substantive risk.**
