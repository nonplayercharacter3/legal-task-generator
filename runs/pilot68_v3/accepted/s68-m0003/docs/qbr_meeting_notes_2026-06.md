## 5. FORECAST ACCURACY AND DEMAND OUTLOOK (AGENDA ITEM 3)

5.1 **Forecast Delivery.** Customer delivered its rolling six-month Forecasts under Section 4.1 of the Agreement on or before the fifteenth (15th) day of each month during the review period.

5.2 **Accuracy.** Supplier presented a comparison of forecast and actual order quantities for Stocked Items over the review period. Aggregate accuracy at the part-number level was 88%. This compares with 84% in the Q1 2026 review period. The largest variances were:

(a) **TM-8130 brake lining kits (geared machine).** Actual orders exceeded the forecast by about 30%. Ms. Montague attributed this to a concentration of annual brake inspections falling in April and May across the Metro Portfolio.

(b) **CB-3315 hydraulic controller main boards.** Actual orders were about 20% below the forecast. Two planned hydraulic controller replacements in Brooklyn had been deferred by the building owners to the fourth quarter.

5.3 **Q3 2026 Outlook.** Customer expects the following trends in Q3 2026:

(a) higher demand for door equipment, particularly DE-4015 light curtain door edges and DR-2210 door restrictor kits. This reflects a group of residential buildings scheduled for periodic inspection in August and September;

(b) steady demand for controller and electrical components, at approximately Q2 levels; and

(c) two complete traction controller orders (Part No. CT-5100) expected to be placed in July for modernization projects scheduled to start in the fourth quarter.

Ms. Abernathy-Ruiz asked Customer to send the job survey data for the two CT-5100 orders with the Purchase Orders. This will allow Supplier to start submittal drawings promptly under Section 5.2 of the Order Form. Mr. Oyelaran agreed.

## 10. QUALITY – FIELD PERFORMANCE REPORTS AND DOOR OPERATOR TREND REVIEW (AGENDA ITEM 8)

10.1 **Field Performance Reports Received.** Mr. Bratislav presented the summary of Field Performance Reports and completed failure analyses for the quarter, as required by Section 11.3 of the Quality Agreement. Customer submitted seven Field Performance Reports during the review period:

| Part No. | Number of Reports | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| DO-7741 | 4 | Intermittent door reversal and failure to complete closing cycle after several months in service |
| CB-3310 | 1 | Unexpected controller reset; analysis found external power quality issue at building; no product defect |
| TM-8200 | 1 | Encoder signal loss; analysis found damaged cable from installation; no product defect |
| SG-1505 | 1 | Final limit switch stuck; analysis found debris ingress from hoistway construction work; no product defect |

10.2 **DO-7741 Trend.** Ms. Kowalczyk described the DO-7741 reports in more detail. The four units were installed between August 2025 and January 2026 in Serviced Units in Manhattan and Brooklyn. In each case, technicians observed the same pattern:
   - the door intermittently reversed mid-cycle without an obstruction; and
   - in two cases, the door stalled before full closing, causing a car to be removed from service until a technician reset the operator.

Customer's technicians suspect a fault in the motor and encoder assembly or in the control board's closing-force logic. They have not been able to reproduce the fault on demand. Ms. Kowalczyk noted that one of the stalls resulted in a brief entrapment with no injury. Customer's technicians cleared the entrapment within twenty-five minutes.

10.3 **Supplier Analysis to Date.** Mr. Bratislav reported that Supplier has received two of the four removed units back under Section 11.2 of the Quality Agreement. Supplier's failure analysis on the first unit found no fault on the bench under standard test conditions. Analysis of the second unit is in progress, including extended thermal cycling. Supplier has not yet received the other two units.

10.4 **Recurring Failure Mode.** The Parties agreed that the DO-7741 reports describe a failure mode that has recurred in more than one Serviced Unit, within the meaning of Section 11.3 of the Quality Agreement. Ms. Montague noted that the same failure mode has now appeared in more than three units within twelve months. She requested that Supplier perform a root cause analysis and deliver a written corrective action plan under Section 9.5 of the Agreement and Section 10 of the Quality Agreement. Customer also made a request under Section 10.1(d) of the Quality Agreement.

Mr. Bratislav agreed that Supplier will open a root cause analysis using the 8D method. He agreed to treat the matter as a priority under Section 11.4 of the Quality Agreement because of the entrapment. Supplier will also assess whether notice is required under Section 10.5 of the Agreement and will tell Customer the outcome of that assessment.

10.5 **Interim Field Guidance.** Pending the root cause analysis, Ms. Montague asked whether Supplier could issue interim field guidance on any adjustment parameters that might reduce the occurrence of the fault. Mr. Bratislav said he would consult Supplier's door equipment engineering team and respond in writing. Any guidance would be issued as a revision to the Documentation under Section 4.3 of the Quality Agreement.

10.6 **Return of Remaining Units.** Ms. Kowalczyk will arrange return of the two remaining removed DO-7741 units, with the technician reports, by June 26, 2026. Supplier will pay return costs under Section 11.2 of the Quality Agreement.

10.7 **Possible For-Cause Audit.** Ms. Montague noted that Customer may wish to observe door operator assembly and testing at Supplier's facility once the root cause analysis is underway. Customer will decide after reviewing the containment description. Any visit would be scheduled as part of Customer's annual quality audit under Section 12.1 of the Quality Agreement, which Customer has not yet used for the current Contract Year.
