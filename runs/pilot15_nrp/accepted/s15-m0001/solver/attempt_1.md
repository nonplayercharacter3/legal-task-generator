## Issue 1 (Highest Risk): IP Assignment Agreement Section 8.3 Overrides the SPA's IP Carve-Out from the Indemnification Cap

**Documents and Sections:**
- Confirmatory IP Assignment Agreement, § 8.3
- Stock Purchase Agreement, § 9.5(a) (Cap = 10% of Purchase Price), § 9.5(b)(iii) (IP breaches excluded from the 10% Cap; subject instead to a cap of 100% of Purchase Price), § 11.10 (Order of Precedence)

**Why it matters:**

Section 8.3 of the IP Assignment Agreement provides:

> "Assignor's aggregate liability for Losses arising out of any breach of this Agreement or of the representations and warranties in Section 4.12 of the Purchase Agreement, whether claimed under this Agreement or under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, shall be subject to the Cap set forth in Section 9.5(a) of the Purchase Agreement, **notwithstanding Section 9.5(b) of the Purchase Agreement**."

Section 9.5(a) of the SPA sets the general Cap at 10% of the Purchase Price. Section 9.5(b)(iii) of the SPA expressly carves out IP representation breaches from that 10% Cap, substituting a cap of 100% of the Purchase Price. Section 8.3 of the IP Assignment Agreement negates that carve-out by its own terms.

SPA § 11.10 provides that "[i]n the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, the Ancillary Agreement controls as to its subject matter." The IP Assignment Agreement is an Ancillary Agreement (SPA § 1.1, definition of "Ancillary Agreements"), and § 8.3 addresses the very subject matter of the IP indemnification. On its face, therefore, § 8.3 controls over § 9.5(b)(iii), reducing Buyer's maximum recovery for IP-related claims from 100% of the Purchase Price to 10%.

This is particularly acute because the very existence of the confirmatory assignment suggests that Seller's representation in SPA § 4.12(b) — that "neither Seller nor any of its Affiliates holds any right, title or interest in any Company Intellectual Property" — may have been inaccurate at Closing. If so, Buyer's primary recourse is an indemnification claim for breach of that representation, and § 8.3 caps it at 10%.

**Recommended fix:**

Before Buyer enforces or relies on the IP Assignment Agreement, we should seek to amend § 8.3 to conform to the SPA. Proposed replacement language:

> "Assignor's aggregate liability for Losses arising out of any breach of this Agreement or of the representations and warranties in Section 4.12 of the Purchase Agreement, whether claimed under this Agreement or under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement, shall be subject to the limitations set forth in Section 9.5 of the Purchase Agreement, including the exclusions from the Cap set forth in Section 9.5(b)."

If Seller resists, we should at minimum add: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, this Section 8.3 shall not reduce the Cap applicable to breaches of Section 4.12 of the Purchase Agreement below the amount set forth in Section 9.5(b)(iii) thereof."

**Open question:** Was § 8.3 negotiated and agreed by Buyer, or was it inserted by Seller's counsel without Buyer's awareness? If the latter, we have a stronger argument that it was not the product of informed consent and should be stricken. I need to confirm with the client how this section came to be included.

## Issue 2 (High Risk): Buyer's Notice of Disagreement on Working Capital Appears Untimely

**Documents and Sections:**
- Stock Purchase Agreement, § 2.4(a) (Seller to deliver Closing Statement within 45 days after Closing), § 2.4(b) (Buyer to deliver Notice of Disagreement within 30 days after delivery of Closing Statement; otherwise the statement is "final and binding")
- Confirmatory IP Assignment Agreement, Recital C (Closing Statement delivered August 3, 2026) and Recital D (Notice of Disagreement delivered September 18, 2026; Seller rejects as untimely)

**Why it matters:**

The Closing occurred July 1, 2026. Seller delivered the Closing Statement on August 3, 2026 (within the 45-day window under § 2.4(a)). Under § 2.4(b), Buyer had 30 days from August 3 to deliver a Notice of Disagreement — i.e., by September 2, 2026. Buyer delivered its notice on September 18, 2026, which is 16 days late. Section 2.4(b) provides that if Buyer does not deliver the notice within the period, "the Closing Statement and Seller's calculation of Closing Working Capital shall be final and binding on the Parties."

Seller has expressly invoked this point (IP Assignment Recital D). If the deadline is enforced, Buyer loses the right to dispute the working capital number and any resulting overpayment under § 2.3/§ 2.4(e).

**Recommended fix / analysis:**

- **Check for tolling or waiver.** Did Seller do anything between August 3 and September 18 that could constitute a waiver of the strict deadline or an acknowledgment that the statement was still open? For example, did Seller engage in informal discussions about disputed items? If so, we may have an argument under New York law that Seller waived the time limit or that the deadline should be equitably tolled.
- **Check for ambiguity in "delivery."** Section 11.1 defines when a notice is "deemed given." If Buyer's September 18 notice was actually deposited with an overnight courier on or before September 2 but not received until September 18, the notice may be timely under the SPA's own mechanics. I need to confirm the actual dispatch date.
- **If the notice is truly untimely**, our fallback is to argue that the working capital number is so manifestly erroneous that enforcement of the deadline would be unconscionable, or that Seller's rejection was itself a breach of the good-faith negotiation obligation implied in § 2.4(c). This is a weaker position.

**Open questions (need facts from client):**
1. What was the actual dispatch date of the Notice of Disagreement (not the date Seller received it)?
2. Was there any communication between the parties regarding the Closing Statement between August 3 and September 18?
3. What is the dollar amount in dispute, and does it justify the effort of a contested resolution?

## Issue 3 (High Risk): Key Employee Agreement's Definition of "Business" Is Narrower Than the SPA's, Creating a Gap in the Non-Compete

**Documents and Sections:**
- Key Employee Agreement, § 1 (definition of "Business": "the preparation of hot inflight meals for regional air carriers")
- Stock Purchase Agreement, § 1.1 (definition of "Business": "inflight catering, galley provisioning and crew meal services for regional air carriers")
- Key Employee Agreement, § 7.1 (non-compete tied to "the Business")
- Stock Purchase Agreement, § 6.9 (requires Key Employee Agreement to include a covenant "not to engage in the Business")

**Why it matters:**

SPA § 6.9 requires the Key Employee Agreement to include a covenant that the Key Employee "not to engage in the Business." The SPA defines "Business" broadly to include inflight catering, galley provisioning, *and* crew meal services. But the Key Employee Agreement defines "Business" narrowly as only "the preparation of hot inflight meals for regional air carriers." This omits galley provisioning and crew meal services entirely.

If Renata Solvig were to join a competitor that provides galley provisioning or crew meal services (but not hot inflight meal preparation), she would not be in breach of § 7.1 of her agreement. This undermines the protection SPA § 6.9 was meant to secure and creates a material gap given that the Company's actual operations (per SPA Recital B) include all three service lines.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend the definition of "Business" in the Key Employee Agreement to conform to the SPA. Proposed language:

> "**Business**" means inflight catering, galley provisioning and crew meal services for regional air carriers, including the preparation of hot inflight meals, the provisioning of galley carts and equipment, and the preparation and supply of crew meals.

If Solvig resists, we should at minimum add: "or any line of business that is substantially similar to or competitive with the Company's business as conducted on the Effective Date."

**Open question:** Was this definitional discrepancy intentional (e.g., a concession made to get Solvig to sign) or an oversight? I need to confirm with the client.

## Issue 4 (Moderate Risk): Governing Law and Forum Conflict Between the SPA and the IP Assignment Agreement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Stock Purchase Agreement, § 11.8 (New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in New York County state and federal courts)
- Confirmatory IP Assignment Agreement, § 10.1 (Delaware law) and § 10.2 (exclusive jurisdiction in the Court of Chancery of Delaware, with fallback to Delaware Superior Court or the U.S. District Court for the District of Delaware)

**Why it matters:**

The SPA and the IP Assignment Agreement are interrelated: the IP Assignment is delivered under SPA § 6.10, incorporates SPA definitions (§ 1.1 of the IP Assignment), and its indemnification provision (§ 8.3) directly modifies the SPA's indemnification regime (Article IX). Yet they are governed by different states' laws and require litigation in different forums.

This creates practical and legal problems:
- A dispute over whether § 8.3 validly modifies § 9.5(b)(iii) of the SPA could require litigation in Delaware (under the IP Assignment's forum clause) to interpret a New York-governed contract.
- Inconsistent choice-of-law principles (New York vs. Delaware) could yield different results on issues of contract interpretation, implied covenants, or enforceability.
- The IP Assignment's forum clause covers "any action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Agreement **or the Assigned IP**" — a broad sweep that could capture claims that a court in New York would also assert jurisdiction over under the SPA.

**Recommended fix:**

Amend IP Assignment § 10.1 and § 10.2 to conform to the SPA:

> "10.1 Governing Law. This Agreement, and all claims and causes of action arising out of or relating to it, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of New York, without regard to conflict of laws principles.
>
> 10.2 Exclusive Forum. Each of Assignor, Assignee and Buyer irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in New York County, New York, for any action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Assigned IP."

If Seller insists on Delaware (perhaps because it is a Delaware LLC), we should at least add a savings clause: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, any claim that arises under both this Agreement and the Purchase Agreement shall be brought exclusively in the forum specified in Section 11.8 of the Purchase Agreement."

## Issue 5 (Moderate Risk): Linen and Cart Washing Agreement — Consequential Damages Waiver May Bar Recovery for Loss of Carrier Contracts

**Documents and Sections:**
- Linen and Cart Washing Services Agreement, § 8.4 (Exclusion of Consequential Damages)
- Linen and Cart Washing Services Agreement, § 3.1 (Sanitation compliance), § 3.4 (Nonconforming Services)

**Why it matters:**

Section 8.4 provides that neither party is liable for "indirect, incidental, special or consequential damages, including lost profits," except for (a) third-party claim indemnification under §§ 8.1–8.2 and (b) breaches of § 9 (confidentiality).

In an inflight catering business, a food-safety failure caused by the vendor (e.g., inadequate sanitization of galley carts leading to contamination) could result in an air carrier terminating or reducing its contract with the Company. The lost profits from that carrier relationship would almost certainly be characterized as consequential damages and thus barred under § 8.4. The Company's recovery would be limited to the cost of rewashing or replacing the affected items under § 3.4 and § 5.2, which is a fraction of the actual economic harm.

This is a vendor agreement outside the core deal documents, but it is a Material Contract under SPA § 4.8 (it is a contract with "a supplier of … linen … or galley services") and represents an ongoing operational risk that Buyer has now inherited.

**Recommended fix:**

Negotiate an amendment to § 8.4 to carve out food-safety-related losses. Proposed language:

> "The exclusions in this Section 8.4 shall not apply to losses arising from the Vendor's failure to comply with Section 3.1 (Sanitation) or Section 3.2 (Chemicals), including any loss of customer contracts, regulatory fines, or recall costs resulting from such failure."

Alternatively, if the vendor resists, ensure the Company's product liability and operations insurance policies (referenced in SPA § 4.17) expressly cover first-party losses from vendor-caused contamination events, and confirm that the insurance is sufficient.

**Open question:** I do not have the Company's insurance policies in the file. I need to confirm whether product liability or business interruption coverage addresses this gap.

## Additional Observations (Not Rising to the Level of a Discrete Issue)

1. **IP Assignment § 2.1 vs. § 2.2 tension.** Section 2.1 has Assignor "confirm" that no member of the Assignor Group retains any interest in the Assigned IP as of Closing. Section 2.2 then assigns "to the extent any member of the Assignor Group holds any right, title or interest." These are not logically inconsistent (the assignment is a safety net), but the confirmation in § 2.1, if false, would itself be a breach of the § 7.4 representation (that SPA § 4.12 was true and correct) and trigger indemnification under § 8.1 — subject, of course, to the problematic cap in § 8.3 (see Issue 1).

2. **Key Employee Agreement § 5.3 (Good Reason) — 50-mile relocation trigger.** If Buyer plans to consolidate the Company's commissary operations post-closing, a relocation of Solvig's principal place of work by more than 50 miles would constitute Good Reason, entitling her to 12 months' severance (§ 5.4) and potentially triggering the non-compete clock early. This is not a defect in the agreement but an operational consideration for the client's integration planning.

3. **Linen Agreement § 6.1 — automatic renewal.** The initial 3-year term expires January 6, 2028, and the agreement renews for successive 1-year terms unless either party gives 90 days' notice of non-renewal (i.e., by October 8, 2027 for the first renewal). If Buyer wishes to exit or renegotiate, it should calendar the non-renewal deadline. Alternatively, § 6.4 permits termination for convenience on 120 days' notice at any time after January 6, 2026, so the auto-renewal is not a trap — but the 120-day notice period is longer than ideal if a quick exit is needed.
