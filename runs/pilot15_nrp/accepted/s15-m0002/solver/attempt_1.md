## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The matter folder reveals several serious problems with Glogair's current position. The two most urgent are: (1) the non-renewal notice in the September 15, 2026 letter appears to be **outside the contractual window** and may be ineffective, potentially triggering an automatic five-year renewal; and (2) the Kestrune acquisition **constitutes an Assignment under the Agreement** that requires Nulind's prior written consent, contrary to the letter's stated position. If the Kestrune deal closes on December 1, 2026 without that consent, Nulind has an express termination right and significant leverage.

Additional structural issues arise from material differences between the November 2021 draft and the executed agreement, including a dramatically expanded exclusivity scope, a broader "Products" definition, and a one-sided assignment clause that permits Nulind to assign freely while restricting Glogair.

## II. DETAILED ISSUES

### Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Untimely (CRITICAL)

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.2; Notice Letter § 4.

**Analysis:** Section 3.2 provides that the Agreement auto-renews for successive five-year terms "unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal **not more than 210 nor fewer than 180 days** before expiration of the then-current term." The Initial Term ends December 31, 2026. Working backward:

| Deadline | Approximate Date |
|----------|-----------------|
| 210 days before Dec 31, 2026 | ~June 4, 2026 |
| 180 days before Dec 31, 2026 | ~July 4, 2026 |

The notice letter is dated **September 15, 2026**, which is approximately **107 days** before expiration. It falls well outside the 180–210 day window. On its face, the notice is late and therefore ineffective to prevent renewal.

**Why it matters:** If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement automatically renews for a new five-year term (through December 31, 2031) on January 1, 2027. Glogair would be locked into the exclusive distribution relationship with Nulind for another five years — immediately after the Kestrune acquisition closes. This would severely constrain Glogair's (and Kestrune's) ability to serve other channels or distributors in the Territory.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether any earlier non-renewal notice was sent (e.g., by email, by a different officer, or informally). The folder contains no evidence of one.
- If no earlier notice exists, assess whether Nulind has acquiesced or waived the timing requirement (e.g., through course of dealing, a response to the September letter, or conduct inconsistent with renewal).
- Prepare a fallback position: if the Agreement renews, Glogair's exit options are limited to termination for cause under § 15.1 (material breach) or negotiation.
- **Client input needed:** Whether any prior communication to Nulind expressed non-renewal intent, and whether Nulind has responded to the September 15 letter in any way.

### Issue 2: Kestrune Acquisition Constitutes an Assignment Requiring Nulind's Consent (CRITICAL)

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§ 1.1(b), 1.1(c), 16.2; Notice Letter § 2.

**Analysis:** The notice letter states:

> "Because the transaction is not an assignment of the Agreement by Glogair, no consent of Nulind is required under Section 16 of the Agreement."

This assertion is contradicted by the Agreement's own definitions:

- **§ 1.1(b)** defines "Assign" to include conveyance "by operation of law, by merger or consolidation, **or otherwise**," and then adds: "**With respect to Supplier, 'Assign' includes any Change of Control of Supplier.**"
- **§ 1.1(c)** defines "Change of Control" to include "(i) the sale, transfer or other disposition, in one transaction or a series of related transactions, of **a majority of the outstanding voting stock of Supplier**."
- The notice letter itself states that Kestrune will "acquire **all** of the outstanding stock of Glogair" — squarely within the Change of Control definition.
- **§ 16.2** provides: "Supplier shall not Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Distributor. Any purported Assignment by Supplier without that consent is void."

The transaction is an Assignment. Nulind's consent is required. The letter's legal analysis is incorrect.

**Why it matters:**
1. If the Kestrune deal closes December 1, 2026 without Nulind's consent, the Assignment is "void" under § 16.2. While the practical effect of a void assignment in a stock purchase is unclear (Glogair remains the same legal entity), Nulind can argue the Agreement was breached.
2. More immediately, **§ 15.2(c)** gives Nulind the right to terminate the Agreement if "Supplier undertakes any Assignment without Distributor's prior written consent under Section 16.2." This provides Nulind with a termination trigger the day after closing.
3. Glogair has now put its (apparently incorrect) position in writing in a formal notice letter, which Nulind can cite in any dispute.

**Recommended fix:**
- **Immediately** seek Nulind's written consent to the Kestrune transaction before the December 1, 2026 closing. This should be treated as urgent.
- If consent is withheld, assess whether the Kestrune definitive agreement has a closing condition or outside date that can be adjusted.
- Consider whether to send a corrected or supplemental letter acknowledging that consent is required (though this concedes the point).
- **Client input needed:** (a) Has Glogair sought Nulind's consent? (b) What are the closing conditions and outside date in the Kestrune definitive agreement? (c) Is the Kestrune deal document available for review? (It is not in the folder.)

### Issue 3: Nulind's Termination Right Under § 15.2(c) Creates Leverage

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 15.2(c), § 15.3.

**Analysis:** If the Kestrune deal closes without Nulind's consent, Nulind may terminate under § 15.2(c). The consequences under § 15.3 include:
- § 15.3(b): "Supplier shall fill all Purchase Orders accepted before the effective date of termination **unless terminated by Supplier under Section 15.1**." Because this would be a termination by *Distributor* under § 15.2, the exception does not apply. Glogair would be obligated to fill all accepted POs even after termination.
- § 15.3(d): Nulind gets a 90-day sell-off period.

**Why it matters:** Nulind can use the threat of termination (or actual termination) to extract concessions from Glogair — for example, demanding a buyout of inventory, extended supply commitments, or financial compensation. Glogair, which wants a clean exit, is in a weak negotiating position if it has breached § 16.2.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain consent before closing to eliminate this trigger.
- If consent is not obtained, model the financial exposure of being required to fill all accepted POs through the 90-day sell-off period.
- **Client input needed:** What is the volume of outstanding and expected POs for the period December 1–March 31, 2027?

**Where:** Notice Letter § 2, final paragraph.

**Analysis:** As detailed in Issue 2, the letter states that "no consent of Nulind is required under Section 16 of the Agreement." This is incorrect based on the plain text of §§ 1.1(b), 1.1(c), and 16.2.

**Recommended fix:**

### Issue 5: Materially One-Sided Assignment Provisions (Current vs. Prior Draft)

**Where:** Current Agreement §§ 16.1–16.2; Prior Draft § 16.1.

**Comparison:**

| Provision | Prior Draft (Nov 2021) | Current Agreement (Executed) |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------------------|
| Who can assign freely | Neither (mutual consent required) | **Distributor** (no consent needed) |
| Consent standard | "Not unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed" | **No standard** — Nulind may refuse for any reason |
| CoC included for both parties | Yes ("either Party") | **No** — only Supplier's CoC triggers the restriction |
| Competitive restriction | Either party could withhold consent for assignment to a competitor | No such provision (moot given Distributor's free assignment right) |

**Why it matters:**
- Nulind can assign the Agreement to **anyone** — including a direct competitor of Glogair or Kestrune — with mere written notice. Glogair has no consent right and no competitive protection.
- Glogair cannot undergo any Change of Control (including the pending Kestrune acquisition) without Nulind's consent, and Nulind can refuse for any reason or no reason.
- This asymmetry was not present in the prior draft. It appears to have been negotiated into the final version in Nulind's favor.

**Recommended fix:**
- If the relationship is to continue (due to the late non-renewal notice), negotiate an amendment to restore mutual consent with a reasonableness standard, or at minimum add a competitive restriction on Nulind's assignees.
- If the relationship is ending, this issue is less pressing but still relevant to negotiating the wind-down terms.
- **Client input needed:** Does the client recall how this provision was negotiated? Was there a business reason for accepting the one-sided structure?

### Issue 6: Exclusivity Scope Expanded From Foodservice-Only to All Channels

**Where:** Current Agreement § 2.1; Prior Draft §§ 1.1(e), 2.1.

**Comparison:**

- **Prior Draft § 2.1:** Supplier restricted from selling to the "**Foodservice Channel**" in the Territory. The "Foodservice Channel" was defined (§ 1.1(e)) to **exclude** "sales to grocery retailers, club stores and other retail outlets for resale to consumers."
- **Current Agreement § 2.1:** "Supplier and its Affiliates shall not sell, offer for sale or otherwise distribute, directly or indirectly, any Products in the Territory through **any channel of trade** except through Distributor."

**Why it matters:**
- The notice letter states that Kestrune "operates commercial bakeries that **supply grocery retailers in New Jersey and New York**."
- Post-closing, Kestrune is Glogair's parent and therefore an "Affiliate" under § 1.1(a).
- If any of Kestrune's grocery products qualify as "Products" (see Issue 7), then Kestrune's existing grocery sales in NY and NJ would violate § 2.1's prohibition on selling Products in the Territory through any channel other than Distributor.
- This creates an immediate compliance risk for the ~30-day period between the Kestrune closing (Dec 1) and the Agreement expiration (Dec 31) — and an ongoing risk if the non-renewal notice is ineffective and the Agreement renews.

**Recommended fix:**
- Determine whether any Kestrune products currently sold to grocery retailers in NY/NJ would meet the definition of "Products."
- If there is overlap, consider whether to (a) carve those products out of the definition by amendment, (b) obtain Nulind's consent to the Kestrune transaction with a side letter addressing this point, or (c) accept the risk if the relationship is ending.
- **Client input needed:** What products does Kestrune currently sell to grocery retailers in the Territory? Do any overlap with Glogair's product lines (par-baked breads, rolls, laminated pastry)?

### Issue 7: "Products" Definition Broadened

**Where:** Current Agreement § 1.1(f); Prior Draft § 1.1(g).

**Comparison:**

- **Prior Draft:** "the par-baked breads, rolls and laminated pastry **manufactured by Supplier at its own bakeries** and offered by Supplier for sale to the **Foodservice Channel** from time to time."
- **Current:** "**all baked goods** manufactured by or for **Supplier or any of its Affiliates**, including par-baked breads, rolls and laminated pastry, in each case as offered by Supplier from time to time."

The current definition is broader in three respects:
1. Covers "all baked goods" (not just par-baked breads, rolls, and laminated pastry).
2. Includes products of "Affiliates" (not just Supplier's own bakeries).
3. Removes the Foodservice Channel limitation.

**Why it matters:** Post-Kestrune acquisition, Kestrune's other bakeries become Affiliates. If those bakeries produce "baked goods" that are "offered by Supplier" (Glogair), they become "Products" subject to the exclusivity restriction in § 2.1. Even if Glogair does not actively sell Kestrune's other products, the "manufactured by or for... Affiliates" language could be read broadly.

The saving phrase is "as offered by Supplier from time to time" — this could limit Products to those Glogair actually offers. But the interaction between "manufactured by or for Affiliates" and "offered by Supplier" is ambiguous.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek clarification from the client on whether Glogair intends to offer any Kestrune Affiliate products post-closing.
- If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), negotiate a clarification or narrowing of the Products definition.
- **Client input needed:** Post-closing, will Glogair offer any products manufactured by Kestrune's other bakeries?

### Issue 8: No "Not Unreasonably Withheld" Qualifier on Nulind's Consent

**Where:** Current Agreement § 16.2; Prior Draft § 16.1.

**Analysis:** The prior draft required consent "which shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed." The current § 16.2 contains no such qualifier. Nulind may refuse consent to Glogair's Change of Control for any reason, no reason, or a pretextual reason, and Glogair has no contractual recourse short of arguing bad faith (which is a high bar under New York law for a consent provision without an express reasonableness standard).

**Why it matters:** This gives Nulind effectively a veto over Glogair's corporate transactions. If Nulind refuses consent to the Kestrune deal, Glogair's options are limited to: (a) not close the Kestrune deal, (b) close and face termination under § 15.2(c), or (c) litigate whether the refusal was in bad faith.

**Recommended fix:**
- In seeking consent, frame the request in a way that makes refusal difficult to justify (e.g., emphasize that Glogair's operations, staff, and quality personnel remain in place; that the Agreement's terms are unchanged; that Nulind's economic interests are unaffected).
- If Nulind refuses, document the business justification for the refusal and assess litigation risk.
- **Client input needed:** Has Nulind been informally approached about the Kestrune deal? What is their stated position?

### Issue 9: Nulind's Free Assignment Right Creates Competitive Risk

**Where:** Current Agreement § 16.1.

**Analysis:** "Distributor may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, to any person without the consent of Supplier, upon written notice to Supplier."

**Why it matters:** Nulind could assign the Agreement (or any right under it) to a competitor of Glogair or Kestrune. The assigning party would step into Nulind's shoes as exclusive distributor in the Territory. Glogair has no consent right, no competitive restriction, and no termination trigger if Nulind assigns to a competitor. The prior draft's mutual consent provision with a competitive carve-out was eliminated in the final agreement.

**Recommended fix:**
- Monitor for any notice of assignment from Nulind.
- If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), negotiate to add a competitive restriction or consent right.
- In the interim, ensure that Confidential Information protections (§ 11) and the trademark license (§ 10.1, which is non-transferable "except with an Assignment permitted under Section 16") are enforced if Nulind does assign.
- **Client input needed:** Is there any indication that Nulind is considering a sale or assignment?

### Issue 10: Quality Agreement Post-Termination Obligations May Extend Well Beyond December 31, 2026

**Where:** Quality Agreement § 12.2; Distribution Agreement § 15.4.

**Analysis:** Quality Agreement § 12.2 provides that certain sections survive "for so long as any Product supplied under the Distribution Agreement remains within its shelf life or remains subject to a recall, and for the record retention periods in Section 10." Section 10.1 requires record retention for "at least two (2) years after the Batch's expiration date."

**Why it matters:** Frozen par-baked products can have shelf lives of 12–18 months or longer. Products shipped in late 2026 could remain within shelf life into 2028. During that period, Glogair remains subject to:
- Audit confidentiality (§ 7.5)
- Recall cooperation (§ 9)
- Record retention (§ 10)
- Regulatory inspection notification (§ 11)

These obligations survive regardless of whether the Agreement expires or is terminated, and they bind Glogair (and post-closing, Kestrune as parent) for a substantial period.

**Recommended fix:**
- Inventory all products shipped to Nulind in 2026 and their expected shelf-life expiration dates.
- Ensure Glogair's quality team is prepared to maintain records and respond to regulatory inquiries for the survival period.
- **Client input needed:** What are the typical shelf lives of Glogair's frozen and ambient products?

### Issue 11: Force Majeure Substitute Sourcing Is One-Way

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 17.2, final sentence.

**Analysis:** "If a force majeure event prevents Supplier from supplying Products for more than thirty (30) days, Distributor may obtain substitute products from third parties for the duration of the event without breach of this Agreement."

**Why it matters:** This is a one-way right. If Nulind is unable to distribute (e.g., warehouse fire, labor strike), there is no corresponding right for Glogair to sell to alternative distributors. While this is not immediately problematic given Glogair's intent to exit, it would be relevant if the Agreement renews and Glogair is locked in for another five years.

**Recommended fix:** Note for any renewal negotiation. Low priority given current circumstances.
