## ISSUE 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Outside the Contractual Window

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 3.2 (Renewal)
- Notice Letter, § 4 (Notice of Non-Renewal)

**The Problem:**

Section 3.2 provides that the Agreement auto-renews for successive five-year terms "unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal not more than 210 nor fewer than 180 days before expiration of the then-current term."

The Initial Term expires December 31, 2026. Working backward:

| Deadline | Approximate Date |
|----------|---------------|
| 210 days before Dec 31, 2026 | ~June 3, 2026 |
| 180 days before Dec 31, 2026 | ~July 3, 2026 |

The notice letter is dated **September 15, 2026**—roughly 107 days before expiration. This is well outside the 180–210 day window. Under New York law, which governs (§ 18.1), contractual notice periods of this kind are strictly construed. The notice is almost certainly ineffective.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- The Agreement will auto-renew for a five-year Renewal Term beginning January 1, 2027.
- Glogair will be locked into exclusivity with Nulind for five more years, during which it cannot sell Products in NY, NJ, or CT through any other channel (§ 2.1).
- This is particularly damaging post-acquisition because Kestrune (Glogair's new parent) already supplies grocery retailers in New Jersey and New York (Notice Letter, § 3). The renewed Agreement would restrict Kestrune-group operations in the Territory.
- Glogair's stated business objective—exiting the Nulind relationship at year-end—is frustrated.

**Recommended Fix:**

- **Immediately** open a dialogue with Nulind to negotiate a mutual termination or a consent to non-renewal. Frame it around the Kestrune acquisition as a changed-circumstances discussion.
- If Nulind is amenable, document the agreement to let the Agreement expire (or to terminate early) in a written amendment signed by both parties (§ 18.3 requires a written instrument signed by both Parties for any amendment).
- If Nulind resists, assess whether Nulind's own termination right under § 15.2(c) (for the unauthorized Change of Control/Assignment) can be leveraged: Glogair could offer Nulind the termination it is contractually entitled to in exchange for a clean exit without the sell-off obligations or other post-termination entanglements.
- **Do not** send another non-renewal notice; it would be equally late and would only compound the evidentiary problem.

**Client Input Needed:**

- What is Glogair's commercial priority: (a) exit the relationship entirely by year-end, or (b) if renewal is unavoidable, renegotiate the terms (e.g., narrow the Territory, carve out Kestrune products, reduce exclusivity)?
- Has Kestrune expressed a view on whether it wants to continue the Nulind relationship post-closing?
- What is the current status of the Kestrune definitive agreement—any conditions precedent that could delay or prevent closing?

## ISSUE 2: The Kestrune Acquisition Is a Deemed "Assignment" Requiring Nulind's Consent

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 1.1(b) (definition of "Assign"), § 1.1(c) (definition of "Change of Control"), § 16.2 (Assignment by Supplier), § 15.2(c) (Termination by Distributor)
- Notice Letter, § 2 (Pending Acquisition)

**The Problem:**

Section 1.1(b) of the executed Agreement defines "Assign" to include, "With respect to Supplier, any Change of Control of Supplier." Section 1.1(c) defines "Change of Control" to include "(i) the sale, transfer or other disposition, in one transaction or a series of related transactions, of a majority of the outstanding voting stock of Supplier."

The Kestrune transaction is a purchase of all outstanding stock of Glogair (Notice Letter, § 2). This squarely meets prong (i) of the Change of Control definition. Therefore, the acquisition is an "Assignment" under the Agreement.

Section 16.2 provides: "Supplier shall not Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Distributor. Any purported Assignment by Supplier without that consent is void."

Section 15.2(c) gives Nulind the right to terminate if "Supplier undertakes any Assignment without Distributor's prior written consent under Section 16.2."

The notice letter states: "Because the transaction is not an assignment of the Agreement by Glogair, no consent of Nulind is required under Section 16 of the Agreement." **This is incorrect.** The Agreement's own definition makes the Change of Control an Assignment irrespective of whether the contract document itself is transferred. The legal fiction that "no contract will be transferred" is irrelevant because the parties contractually agreed that a Change of Control of Supplier *is* an Assignment.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- Nulind has a present, exercisable right to terminate the Agreement under § 15.2(c) upon closing of the Kestrune acquisition (expected December 1, 2026).
- The "void" language in § 16.2 creates ambiguity: does it mean the Assignment is void (i.e., the Change of Control is ineffective as an Assignment, and the Agreement continues with Glogair as the same entity), or could Nulind argue the Agreement itself is void? The more natural reading is that the Assignment is void (ineffective), but the ambiguity is a litigation risk.
- If Nulind terminates under § 15.2(c), § 15.3(b) still requires Glogair to fill all Purchase Orders accepted before the termination date.
- The notice letter's incorrect statement creates an evidentiary problem: it is a signed, delivered document in which Glogair affirmatively misstated the legal effect of the transaction. Nulind could use it to argue Glogair acted in bad faith or to rebut any later claim of misunderstanding.

**Recommended Fix:**

- **Before closing** (i.e., before December 1, 2026), seek Nulind's written consent to the Change of Control under § 16.2. This is the cleanest path.
- If seeking consent, pair the request with a proposal for the commercial terms going forward (see Issue 1). For example: "We are asking for your consent to the Kestrune acquisition. In connection with that, we propose [mutual termination / narrowed exclusivity / other terms]."
- If Nulind withholds consent, Glogair should be prepared for Nulind to terminate under § 15.2(c) at or after closing. Model the operational and financial impact of that scenario (fill obligations under § 15.3(b), 90-day sell-off under § 15.3(d), confidentiality return under § 15.3(c)).
- **Do not rely on the notice letter's statement** that no consent is required. If the client wants to preserve flexibility, consider whether a supplemental or corrective letter to Nulind is advisable (though this risks drawing attention to the error). Discuss with the client before taking any further written action.

**Client Input Needed:**

- Has Kestrune's deal team been briefed on this issue? The Kestrune definitive agreement may have conditions or covenants relevant to obtaining Nulind's consent.
- What is Glogair's fallback position if Nulind refuses consent and terminates? Is Glogair prepared to lose the Nulind channel in the Territory?
- Does the client want us to send a corrective/supplemental letter acknowledging the consent requirement, or to approach Nulind informally first?

## ISSUE 3: The Executed Agreement Is Substantially Broader Than the Prior Draft—Exclusivity and Product Scope

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 1.1(f) (Products), § 2.1 (Exclusivity)
- Prior Draft (Nov 2021), § 1.1(e) (Foodservice Channel), § 1.1(g) (Products), § 2.1 (Exclusivity)

**The Problem:**

Three material changes occurred between the draft and the executed Agreement:

| Element | Prior Draft | Executed Agreement |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Channel limitation** | Exclusivity limited to the "Foodservice Channel" (defined in § 1.1(e) as restaurants, institutional kitchens, hotels, caterers, schools, hospitals; expressly excluding grocery retailers, club stores, and other retail outlets) | No channel limitation. Exclusivity covers "any channel of trade" (§ 2.1). The "Foodservice Channel" definition was deleted. |
| **Product scope** | "the par-baked breads, rolls and laminated pastry manufactured by Supplier at its own bakeries" (§ 1.1(g)) | "all baked goods manufactured by or for Supplier or any of its Affiliates, including par-baked breads, rolls and laminated pastry" (§ 1.1(f)) |
| **Affiliate reach** | Exclusivity binds only "Supplier" (§ 2.1) | Exclusivity binds "Supplier and its Affiliates" (§ 2.1) |

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

Post-acquisition, Kestrune becomes Glogair's parent, and Kestrune's other subsidiaries become Glogair's Affiliates (under the >50% control definition in § 1.1(a)). Kestrune "operates commercial bakeries that supply grocery retailers in New Jersey and New York" (Notice Letter, § 3).

If any Kestrune-group bakery manufactures "baked goods" in or for the Territory (NY, NJ, CT), those products fall within the "Products" definition, and Kestrune (as an Affiliate) is prohibited from selling them "through any channel of trade" except through Nulind. This could:

- Restrict Kestrune's existing grocery retail business in NJ and NY.
- Create a conflict between Kestrune's pre-existing operations and Glogair's contractual obligations.
- Give Nulind a basis to claim Glogair (or its Affiliate Kestrune) is in breach of § 2.1 if Kestrune continues its grocery sales.

**Recommended Fix:**

- **Urgently inventory** all products manufactured by Kestrune and its subsidiaries in the Territory. Determine whether any fall within "all baked goods manufactured by or for Supplier or any of its Affiliates."
- If there is overlap, this must be addressed as part of any negotiation with Nulind (whether for consent to the Change of Control, for termination, or for amendment). Potential solutions:
  - Carve out Kestrune's pre-existing product lines from the "Products" definition.
  - Narrow the exclusivity to the Foodservice Channel (restoring the draft's approach).
  - Limit the Affiliate restriction to Glogair's direct subsidiaries, excluding the parent.
- If the Agreement renews (per Issue 1), Glogair is bound by these terms for five years. The cost of inaction is high.

**Client Input Needed:**

- What products does Kestrune currently manufacture and sell in NY, NJ, or CT? Through what channels?
- Are any Kestrune products "baked goods" that could be caught by the broad "Products" definition?
- Is Kestrune willing to make operational changes (e.g., stop selling certain products in the Territory) to avoid a conflict, or does it need a contractual carve-out?

## ISSUE 4: One-Sided Assignment Provisions

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 16.1 (Assignment by Distributor), § 16.2 (Assignment by Supplier)
- Prior Draft, § 16.1 (Consent Required—mutual)

**The Problem:**

| | Prior Draft | Executed Agreement |
|--|------------|-------------------|
| **Distributor assignment** | Requires Supplier's consent (§ 16.1) | Permitted without consent, on notice only (§ 16.1) |
| **Supplier assignment** | Requires Distributor's consent (§ 16.1) | Requires Distributor's consent; void without it (§ 16.2) |
| **Supplier Change of Control** | Deemed Assignment for *either* Party (§ 1.1(b)) | Deemed Assignment for *Supplier only* (§ 1.1(b)) |

The executed Agreement allows Nulind to assign to anyone—potentially a competitor of Glogair—without Glogair's consent. Meanwhile, Glogair cannot even undergo a Change of Control without Nulind's consent. This asymmetry is stark.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- Nulind could assign the Agreement to a competitor of Kestrune or Glogair, and Glogair would have no say.
- If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), Glogair is locked in with a counterparty that can change at will.
- The "void" consequence in § 16.2 (for unauthorized Supplier Assignments) has no parallel for Distributor assignments.

**Recommended Fix:**

- If negotiating with Nulind (for consent, termination, or amendment), seek to restore mutual consent for assignments, or at minimum require that any assignee of Nulind be a non-competitor.
- If the relationship is ending, this issue is less critical but should be noted for any transition agreement.

**Client Input Needed:**

- Does Glogair have any concern about who Nulind might assign to? (Less urgent if the relationship is ending, but relevant if renewal is the fallback.)

## ISSUE 5: Asymmetric Termination Rights

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 15.1 (Termination by Either Party), § 15.2 (Termination by Distributor)
- Prior Draft, § 15.1(c) (mutual assignment termination right)

**The Problem:**

In the prior draft, § 15.1(c) allowed *either* Party to terminate if the other undertook an Assignment without consent. In the executed Agreement, this mutual right was deleted from § 15.1. Instead, only Nulind has an assignment-based termination right (§ 15.2(c)), and it is triggered by a Supplier Assignment without consent.

Glogair has no termination right if Nulind assigns the Agreement (because Nulind doesn't need consent in the first place, per § 16.1).

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- Glogair's only termination rights are: (a) Nulind's material breach (30-day cure), or (b) Nulind's insolvency/bankruptcy.
- If Nulind assigns to an undesirable party, Glogair is stuck.
- The asymmetry compounds the assignment issue (Issue 4).

**Recommended Fix:**

- Same as Issue 4: address in any renegotiation. If the relationship is ending, this is moot.

## ISSUE 6: The Notice Letter Contains a Materially Incorrect Legal Statement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Notice Letter, § 2 (last two sentences)
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 1.1(b), § 16.2

**The Problem:**

The notice letter states: "Because the transaction is not an assignment of the Agreement by Glogair, no consent of Nulind is required under Section 16 of the Agreement."

As analyzed in Issue 2, this is incorrect. The Agreement defines a Change of Control of Supplier as an "Assignment" (§ 1.1(b)), and § 16.2 requires Distributor consent for any Supplier Assignment. The fact that the legal entity does not change and no contract document is transferred is irrelevant under the Agreement's own terms.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- **Evidentiary risk:** The letter is a signed, couriered document. If a dispute arises, Nulind will cite this letter to show Glogair affirmatively represented that no consent was needed, undermining any later good-faith argument.
- **Waiver/estoppel risk:** While unlikely to constitute a legal waiver, the letter could support an argument that Glogair induced Nulind to forgo protections (e.g., Nulind might argue it did not independently investigate the assignment issue because Glogair told it no consent was needed).
- **Negotiating leverage:** The error weakens Glogair's position in any discussion with Nulind. It is better to correct the record proactively than to have Nulind's counsel raise it.

**Recommended Fix:**

- Decide whether to send a corrective letter. Arguments for: it cleans up the record, shows good faith, and preempts Nulind from using the error. Arguments against: it draws attention to the problem and may be seen as an admission.
- If a corrective letter is sent, it should: (a) acknowledge that the Change of Control constitutes an Assignment under § 1.1(b); (b) request Nulind's consent under § 16.2; and (c) be paired with a commercial proposal (termination, amendment, or consent in exchange for specific terms).
- Alternatively, address the issue verbally in a call with Nulind before putting anything further in writing.

**Client Input Needed:**

- Does the client want to correct the record in writing, or handle it informally first?
- Who at Glogair signed the notice letter, and were they aware of the § 1.1(b) definition? (This bears on internal accountability and on whether the error was a good-faith mistake or a deliberate position.)

## ISSUE 7: Quality Agreement Audit Rights Extend to Recipes and Formulations

**Documents and Sections:**
- Quality Agreement, § 7.2(e) (Scope of Audit)

**The Problem:**

Section 7.2(e) permits Nulind's audits to cover "the recipes and formulations of the Products, including ingredient percentages, process parameters and par-bake profiles." While § 7.5 subjects audit information to the confidentiality provisions of the Distribution Agreement (§ 11), the right to inspect core IP is broad.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- Post-acquisition, Kestrune is in the same business. If Kestrune has proprietary formulations that are similar to or derivative of Glogair's, the audit right could expose competitive information.
- Even absent direct overlap, the ability of a distributor to inspect recipes is a significant IP risk, particularly if the relationship sours or if Nulind assigns the Agreement to a competitor (see Issue 4).
- The confidentiality obligation in § 11.4 survives only five years post-termination (indefinitely for trade secrets, but the line between a "recipe" and a "trade secret" is fact-specific).

**Recommended Fix:**

- If the relationship continues (renewal), negotiate a narrowing of § 7.2(e) to limit recipe/formulation review to verification of specific allergen or safety claims, rather than full disclosure of ingredient percentages and process parameters.
- Ensure that Glogair's most sensitive formulations are designated as trade secrets (with appropriate internal safeguards) to invoke the indefinite survival period in § 11.4.
- If the relationship is ending, ensure the return/destruction obligation in § 15.3(c) is enforced, and consider a post-termination IP protection agreement.

**Client Input Needed:**

- Are there specific Glogair formulations that Kestrune considers particularly sensitive?
- Has Nulind ever exercised its audit right? If so, what was the scope?

## ISSUE 8: Force Majeure Permits Nulind to Source from Third Parties

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 17.2 (Force Majeure, last sentence)

**The Problem:**

"If a force majeure event prevents Supplier from supplying Products for more than thirty (30) days, Distributor may obtain substitute products from third parties for the duration of the event without breach of this Agreement."

This provision is identical in the prior draft, so it is not a "change" issue, but it is a structural risk.

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- If Glogair experiences a supply disruption (fire, equipment failure, labor issue, etc.) lasting more than 30 days, Nulind can source from competitors.
- In an exclusive distribution relationship, this can be fatal: once Nulind's customers are supplied by a competitor, they may not switch back.
- Post-acquisition, if there is any integration disruption at Glogair's bakeries, this clause could be triggered.

**Recommended Fix:**

- If renegotiating, seek to add a "right of first refusal" or a requirement that Nulind resume ordering from Glogair within a specified period after the force majeure event ends.
- Operationally, ensure Glogair has adequate business continuity planning to minimize the risk of a 30+ day supply interruption.

## ISSUE 9: Post-Termination Fill Obligation

**Documents and Sections:**
- Executed Distribution Agreement, § 15.3(b)

**The Problem:**

"Supplier shall fill all Purchase Orders accepted before the effective date of termination unless terminated by Supplier under Section 15.1."

**Why It Matters for Glogair:**

- If Nulind terminates under § 15.2(c) (for the unauthorized Change of Control), Glogair must still fill all previously accepted Purchase Orders.
- During a period of corporate transition (Kestrune integration), this could create operational strain.
- The exception ("unless terminated by Supplier under § 15.1") does not help Glogair here, because Glogair would not be the terminating party.

**Recommended Fix:**

- In any negotiated exit, seek to limit or eliminate the fill obligation, or at minimum cap it at a specific number of days' worth of orders.
- Operationally, ensure that as the termination date approaches, Glogair is not accepting new Purchase Orders beyond what it can comfortably fill during the transition.
