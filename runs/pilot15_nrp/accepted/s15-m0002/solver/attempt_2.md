## ISSUE 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Outside the Contractual Window

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §3.2 (Renewal); Notice Letter §4

**What the contract says:**
> "Upon expiration of the Initial Term, this Agreement shall automatically renew for successive five-year terms (each, a 'Renewal Term') unless either Party gives the other Party written notice of non-renewal **not more than 210 nor fewer than 180 days** before expiration of the then-current term."

**The math:**
- Initial Term expires: **December 31, 2026**
- 210 days before Dec 31, 2026: **approximately June 4, 2026**
- 180 days before Dec 31, 2026: **approximately July 4, 2026**
- Notice Letter dated: **September 15, 2026**

The non-renewal notice in the letter is roughly **73 days late**. The contractual window closed on or about July 4, 2026.

**Why it matters:**
Section 3.2 is drafted as an automatic renewal with a narrow exception. If the notice is outside the window, the most natural reading is that the exception was not satisfied and the Agreement **auto-renews for a five-year Renewal Term through December 31, 2031**. Glogair would be contractually bound to Nulind for another five years—precisely the outcome the letter was meant to prevent. There is no cure provision in §3.2, and no general "substantial compliance" savings clause in the Agreement.

**Compounding risk:** If the Agreement auto-renews, Glogair's exclusivity obligations (including the expanded "Products" definition—see Issue 4) continue for five more years, and the Kestrune integration problem becomes significantly worse.

**Recommended fix:**
- Determine whether any earlier communication (email, letter, meeting minutes) constituted notice of non-renewal within the June 4 – July 4 window. If so, preserve and document it.
- If no earlier notice exists, assess whether Nulind has affirmatively waived the timing requirement or is estopped from relying on it (e.g., if Nulind acknowledged the non-renewal in a response to the September 15 letter).
- If neither is available, the practical options are: (a) negotiate a mutual agreement to treat the Agreement as expiring on December 31, 2026 (essentially asking Nulind to waive the auto-renewal), or (b) accept the renewal and plan an exit at the next opportunity (which would be 210–180 days before December 31, 2031, i.e., mid-2031).
- **Do not** send a second, corrected non-renewal notice without first understanding Nulind's likely response, as it may be construed as an admission that the first was deficient.

- Has Nulind responded to the September 15 letter? If so, what did it say?

## ISSUE 2: The Kestrune Acquisition Is an "Assignment" Requiring Nulind's Consent

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §1.1(b) (definition of "Assign"), §1.1(c) (definition of "Change of Control"), §16.2 (Assignment by Supplier); Notice Letter §2

**What the contract says:**

§1.1(b):
> "'**Assign**' means to assign, transfer, delegate or otherwise convey this Agreement or any right or obligation under it, whether voluntarily, by operation of law, by merger or consolidation, or otherwise. **With respect to Supplier, 'Assign' includes any Change of Control of Supplier.**"

§1.1(c):
> "'**Change of Control**' means (i) the sale, transfer or other disposition, in one transaction or a series of related transactions, of a majority of the outstanding voting stock of Supplier; …"

§16.2:
> "Supplier shall not Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Distributor. Any purported Assignment by Supplier without that consent is void."

**The problem:**
The Kestrune transaction is a purchase of all outstanding stock of Glogair. That is squarely within §1.1(c)(i). Under §1.1(b), a Change of Control of Supplier **is** an "Assign." Under §16.2, Glogair may not Assign without Nulind's prior written consent.

The Notice Letter §2 states:
> "Because the transaction is not an assignment of the Agreement by Glogair, no consent of Nulind is required under Section 16 of the Agreement. We are providing this notice as a courtesy…"

**This argument is incorrect.** The contract defines "Assign" to include Change of Control. The fact that Glogair remains the same legal entity and no contract is literally "transferred" is irrelevant—the defined term controls. By characterizing the notice as a "courtesy," the letter also waives any argument that the notice was given to satisfy a contractual obligation.

**Why it matters:**
- If Nulind treats the Kestrune closing as an unauthorized Assignment, it has an express termination right under §15.2(c): Distributor may terminate if "Supplier undertakes any Assignment without Distributor's prior written consent under Section 16.2."
- §16.2 also provides that any purported Assignment without consent "is void." While the stock purchase itself is not void (it is a transaction between Glogair's shareholders and Kestrune, not an assignment of the Agreement), the practical effect is that Glogair would be in material breach, giving Nulind both a termination right and a potential damages claim.
- Nulind could also argue that the "void" language means the Agreement should be treated as though the Change of Control never occurred for purposes of the consent requirement—i.e., Glogair remains bound by the original terms and cannot rely on any post-closing integration with Kestrune.

**Comparison to prior draft:**
In the November 2021 draft, §16.1 provided: "Neither Party shall Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party, **which shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed**." The final agreement deleted the "not unreasonably withheld" qualifier. Nulind now has an **absolute veto** over any Change of Control of Glogair. This was a significant concession from the draft position.

**Recommended fix:**
- **Before the Kestrune closing on December 1, 2026**, Glogair should seek Nulind's written consent under §16.2. The consent request should:
  - Correct the mischaracterization in the September 15 letter (acknowledge that the CoC is an Assignment under §1.1(b) and that consent is required under §16.2).
  - Provide Nulind with reasonable information about Kestrune and the post-closing structure.
  - Address Nulind's likely concerns (see Issue 4 below) by proposing protective language (e.g., a carve-out for Kestrune's existing grocery retail products from the "Products" definition, or a narrowing of the exclusivity covenant).
- If Nulind withholds consent, Glogair should not close the Kestrune transaction without first assessing the termination and breach exposure. Closing without consent triggers §15.2(c).
- The September 15 letter should be supplemented or, if possible, withdrawn and replaced with a consent request. At minimum, Glogair should not rely on the "courtesy" framing in any further communication.

**⚠ NEEDS CLIENT INPUT:**
- Has Nulind responded to the September 15 letter?
- Is the Kestrune definitive agreement available for review? We need to confirm the exact structure (stock purchase vs. merger vs. asset sale) and the closing date.
- Is there any side letter, term sheet, or prior communication with Nulind regarding the acquisition?
- What is the business rationale for the non-renewal? If Glogair wants out of the Nulind relationship regardless, the consent negotiation becomes a leverage exercise rather than a protection of the ongoing relationship.

## ISSUE 3: Nulind's Termination Right Under §15.2(c)

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §15.2(c)

**What the contract says:**
> "Distributor may terminate this Agreement upon written notice to Supplier if: … (c) Supplier undertakes any Assignment without Distributor's prior written consent under Section 16.2."

**Why it matters:**
This is the enforcement mechanism for Issue 2. If the Kestrune closing occurs without Nulind's consent, Nulind can terminate immediately upon written notice. There is no cure period for this ground (unlike §15.1(a), which allows 30 days to cure a material breach). The termination right is discretionary—Nulind need not show damages or materiality.

If Nulind terminates under §15.2(c):
- The Agreement ends on the effective date of termination.
- Under §15.3(b), Glogair must still fill all Purchase Orders accepted before the termination date.
- Under §15.3(d), Nulind gets a 90-day sell-off period.
- Glogair loses the exclusive distribution relationship in the Territory on short notice, potentially during the Kestrune integration period.

**Interaction with Issue 1:** If the non-renewal notice was ineffective (Issue 1), the Agreement auto-renews. If Nulind then terminates under §15.2(c), Glogair is in the same practical position (no distribution agreement) but may face a breach claim or lose leverage in any negotiation.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Nulind's consent before closing (see Issue 2). This is the only clean path.
- If consent is refused, evaluate whether to (a) delay the Kestrune closing, (b) close and accept the termination risk (and negotiate a transition), or (c) restructure the Kestrune transaction to avoid triggering the Change of Control definition (e.g., a structure where Glogair's shareholders sell to Kestrune but Glogair is then merged into a new entity—though this would likely still be a CoC under §1.1(c)(ii)).
- **Do not** assume that because Glogair remains the same legal entity, §15.2(c) is inapplicable. The defined term "Assign" makes the CoC an Assignment regardless of corporate form.

## ISSUE 4: Expanded "Products" Definition Captures Kestrune's Existing Business

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §1.1(f) (definition of "Products"), §2.1 (Exclusivity); Notice Letter §3

**What the contract says:**

§1.1(f) (final agreement):
> "'**Products**' means **all baked goods** manufactured by or for Supplier **or any of its Affiliates**, including par-baked breads, rolls and laminated pastry, in each case as offered by Supplier from time to time."

§2.1:
> "During the Term, Supplier **and its Affiliates** shall not sell, offer for sale or otherwise distribute, directly or indirectly, **any Products** in the Territory **through any channel of trade** except through Distributor."

Notice Letter §3:
> "Kestrune is a privately held baking company. It operates commercial bakeries that **supply grocery retailers in New Jersey and New York**."

**The problem:**
After the Kestrune acquisition:
1. Kestrune becomes an "Affiliate" of Glogair (Supplier) under §1.1(a) (Kestrune will own 100% of Glogair's voting stock, exceeding the 50% control threshold).
2. All baked goods manufactured by Kestrune (an Affiliate) are "Products" under §1.1(f).
3. Under §2.1, Glogair **and its Affiliates** (i.e., Kestrune) may not sell any Products in the Territory (NY, NJ, CT) through any channel except Nulind.
4. Kestrune currently supplies grocery retailers in NJ and NY—both within the Territory and a channel other than Nulind.

**This means that on the day after the Kestrune closing, Kestrune's existing grocery retail distribution in NJ and NY would be a breach of §2.1**, unless those products are excluded from the "Products" definition or Kestrune's operations are carved out.

**Comparison to prior draft:**
The November 2021 draft contained two critical limitations that were removed in the final agreement:
- §1.1(e) defined "Foodservice Channel" and **excluded** "sales to grocery retailers, club stores and other retail outlets for resale to consumers."
- §1.1(g) limited "Products" to items "manufactured by Supplier **at its own bakeries** and offered by Supplier for sale **to the Foodservice Channel**."
- §2.1 limited exclusivity to the Foodservice Channel.

The final agreement removed all of these limitations. The exclusivity now covers **all channels** and **all baked goods of all Affiliates**. This was a material concession from Glogair's draft position.

**Why it matters:**
- If Glogair closes the Kestrune acquisition and Kestrune continues its grocery retail business in NY/NJ, Glogair is in ongoing breach of §2.1.
- Nulind could claim damages, seek injunctive relief (forcing Kestrune to stop selling to grocery retailers in the Territory), or terminate under §15.1(a) (material breach, 30-day cure).
- The "cure" for this breach would require either (a) Kestrune ceasing all grocery retail sales in NY/NJ/CT, or (b) routing all such sales through Nulind—both of which are likely commercially unworkable and would undermine the strategic rationale for the acquisition.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the consent request to Nulind (see Issue 2), propose an amendment that either:
  - Carves out Kestrune's pre-existing grocery retail products and customers from the "Products" definition and the §2.1 exclusivity covenant; or
  - Restores the "Foodservice Channel" limitation from the prior draft; or
  - Limits "Products" to items "manufactured by Supplier at its own bakeries" (excluding Affiliate-manufactured products).
- If Nulind refuses to amend, Glogair should assess whether the Kestrune acquisition can proceed without triggering the §2.1 breach (e.g., by having Kestrune's grocery retail operations held at a separate entity that is not an "Affiliate" of Glogair—though this may be difficult given the 100% ownership structure).
- **This issue is the strongest leverage Glogair has in the consent negotiation.** Nulind's exclusivity is the crown jewel of the agreement for it; Glogair can make clear that closing without a carve-out creates an immediate, ongoing breach that Nulind would have to enforce (or waive).

**⚠ NEEDS CLIENT INPUT:**
- What specific products does Kestrune currently sell in NY, NJ, and CT? To which customers? Through which channels?
- What is the revenue from Kestrune's Territory operations? (This quantifies the exposure.)
- Is the strategic rationale for the Kestrune acquisition dependent on Kestrune's grocery retail business in the Territory?
- Are there any other Kestrune Affiliates (subsidiaries, sister companies) that manufacture baked goods?

## ISSUE 5: No "Not Unreasonably Withheld" Standard on Nulind's Consent

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §16.2; compare Prior Draft §16.1

**What the contract says (final):**
> "Supplier shall not Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of Distributor. Any purported Assignment by Supplier without that consent is void."

**What the prior draft said:**
> "Neither Party shall Assign this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other Party, **which shall not be unreasonably withheld, conditioned or delayed**. Either Party may withhold consent to any Assignment to a person that manufactures or distributes bakery products in competition with it."

**Why it matters:**
- The final agreement gives Nulind an **unfettered veto**. It can refuse consent for any reason, no reason, or as leverage in a commercial negotiation (e.g., demanding better pricing, extended term, or other concessions).
- The prior draft at least required the veto to be reasonable and provided a specific ground for refusal (competition). The final agreement removed both protections.
- In the context of the Kestrune acquisition, Nulind knows that Kestrune is a competitor (it "operates commercial bakeries"). Under the prior draft, Nulind could have refused consent on competition grounds, but it could not have refused for unrelated reasons or used the consent as a bargaining chip for unrelated concessions. Under the final agreement, it can do all of the above.

**Recommended fix:**
- In the consent negotiation, Glogair should argue that the purpose of §16.2 is to protect Nulind against being stuck with an unknown or hostile counterparty, not to give Nulind a general veto over Glogair's corporate transactions.
- Propose that consent not be unreasonably withheld (restoring the prior draft standard) or that consent be deemed given if Nulind does not respond within a specified period (e.g., 30 days).
- If Nulind insists on absolute discretion, Glogair should price that risk into the Kestrune deal (e.g., make the Kestrune closing conditional on obtaining Nulind's consent, or include a walk-away right).

## ISSUE 6: Asymmetric Termination Rights

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §15.1, §15.2

**What the contract says:**
- §15.1 (Either Party): Termination for material breach (30-day cure) or insolvency/bankruptcy (60-day cure).
- §15.2 (Distributor only): Additional termination rights for (a) failure to fill orders in 3+ months, (b) repeated Class I recalls, (c) unauthorized Assignment.

**Why it matters:**
- Glogair has **no** corresponding right to terminate if Nulind fails to use commercially reasonable efforts (§2.3), fails to maintain adequate warehouse capacity, or underperforms on sales. The only recourse for Glogair is the general material breach provision in §15.1(a), which requires a 30-day cure period and is harder to trigger.
- Nulind, by contrast, has three specific termination triggers in §15.2 that require no cure period and no showing of materiality.
- This asymmetry was present in the prior draft as well (the prior draft §15.2 had the same structure), so it is not a regression, but it is worth flagging for the client's awareness.

**Recommended fix:**
- In any renegotiation or consent discussion, Glogair should seek to add a parallel right for Supplier to terminate if Distributor fails to use commercially reasonable efforts for two consecutive quarters, or fails to maintain the warehouse capacity required by §2.3.
- This is a secondary issue but strengthens Glogair's negotiating position.

## ISSUE 8: Quality Agreement Audit Rights Over Recipes

**Document / Section:** Quality Agreement §7.2(e)

**What the contract says:**
> "An audit under Section 7.1 may cover the following: … (e) the **recipes and formulations of the Products, including ingredient percentages, process parameters and par-bake profiles**. These may be reviewed to verify conformity with the Specifications and allergen declarations."

**Why it matters:**
- This gives Nulind (or its third-party auditor) the right to inspect Glogair's actual recipes, including precise ingredient percentages and process parameters. This is core proprietary information.
- While §7.5 subjects audit information to the confidentiality provisions of the Distribution Agreement (§11), the practical risk is that a Nulind employee or auditor sees the recipe and that information is difficult to truly compartmentalize.
- Post-Kestrune-acquisition, if Kestrune's products are "Products" (see Issue 4), Nulind's audit rights could extend to Kestrune's bakeries and recipes as well (Quality Agreement §7.1: "Distributor and its successors and permitted assigns may audit and inspect Supplier's bakeries, warehouses and other facilities used to manufacture, store or ship Products").
- The audit is limited to once per calendar year on 10 business days' notice (§7.1), which provides some protection, but the scope is still very broad.

**Recommended fix:**
- In any amendment negotiation, seek to narrow §7.2(e) to allow verification of conformity with Specifications without requiring disclosure of exact ingredient percentages or process parameters (e.g., allow testing of finished product against Specifications rather than inspection of the recipe itself).
- If the Agreement is terminating (or will terminate), this becomes less urgent, but the survival provisions (Quality Agreement §12.2) keep §§7.5, 9, 10, and 11 alive for products within shelf life. The audit right itself (§7) does not survive, so this is a non-issue post-termination.

## ISSUE 9: Force Majeure Substitute Product Right

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §17.2 (second sentence)

**What the contract says:**
> "If a force majeure event prevents Supplier from supplying Products for more than thirty (30) days, **Distributor may obtain substitute products from third parties** for the duration of the event without breach of this Agreement."

**Why it matters:**
- In an exclusive distribution agreement, this is unusual. It allows Nulind to source from Glogair's competitors during a supply disruption without breaching the exclusivity covenant.
- The practical risk is limited (force majeure events are uncommon and temporary), but it means that during a prolonged disruption (e.g., a bakery fire), Nulind could introduce competing products to its customer base, potentially displacing Glogair's products even after the disruption ends.
- This provision was identical in the prior draft, so it is not a regression.

**Recommended fix:**
- Low priority. If renegotiating, consider adding a requirement that Nulind give Glogair first right to re-supply once the force majeure event ends, or limiting the substitute product right to the specific customers affected by the disruption.

## ISSUE 10: Confidentiality Survival and Kestrune Integration

**Document / Section:** Distribution Agreement §11.4; §15.3(c)

**What the contract says:**
- §11.4: Confidentiality obligations survive for **five (5) years** after expiration or termination, and **indefinitely** for trade secrets.
- §15.3(c): Upon termination, each Party shall return or destroy the other Party's Confidential Information.

**Why it matters:**
- "Confidential Information" includes "pricing, customer lists, recipes, formulations and manufacturing processes" (§11.1).
- If the Agreement expires or terminates on December 31, 2026, Glogair must return or destroy Nulind's Confidential Information, and Nulind must return or destroy Glogair's.
- However, the 5-year survival means that for five years post-termination, neither party can use the other's Confidential Information (e.g., Nulind's customer list, Glogair's pricing structure).
- In the Kestrune context: if Glogair wants to use Nulind's customer relationships (e.g., to sell directly to Nulind's foodservice customers after the Agreement ends), it must be careful not to rely on Nulind's Confidential Information (customer lists, pricing data) in doing so.
- Conversely, if Kestrune wants to use any information it receives about Glogair's operations post-acquisition, it must respect the confidentiality obligations that Glogair owes to Nulind (and vice versa).

**Recommended fix:**
- Ensure that the transition plan (discussed in Notice Letter §5) includes a clear protocol for return/destruction of Confidential Information.
- Advise the client that for 5 years post-termination, Glogair and Kestrune should not use Nulind's customer lists, pricing data, or other Confidential Information in their own commercial activities in the Territory.
- If Glogair intends to sell directly to former Nulind customers post-termination, it should do so based on its own independently developed customer relationships, not on Nulind's Confidential Information.
