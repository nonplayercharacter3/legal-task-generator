## Issue 1 – CRITICAL / TIME-SENSITIVE: The Sablecrest Merger Triggers the Change-of-Control Clause; the Notice Letter Is Legally Defective

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 15.3 (Change of Control)
- Notice Letter, ¶¶ 1–2

**What the documents say:**

MSA § 15.3 provides that a "change of control of either Party, including by merger, consolidation, sale of equity or sale of all or substantially all of its assets, requires the other Party's prior written consent." "Control" is defined as ownership of more than 50% of voting interests or the power to direct management and policies. A change of control occurs "when any person or group that did not control a Party on the Catering MSA Effective Date acquires control of that Party."

The Notice Letter (¶ 1) discloses that on October 17, 2026, Lebix entered into a merger agreement with Sablecrest Aviation Holdings, LLC. Upon closing (expected December 15, 2026), Merger Sub will merge into Lebix, and Lebix will become a wholly owned subsidiary of Sablecrest. Sablecrest did not control Lebix on March 1, 2025.

The Notice Letter (¶ 2) states: "no consent or other action is required of Provider under the MSA in connection with the merger." It justifies this by arguing that Lebix survives as the same legal entity and no assignment occurs.

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

The Notice Letter's analysis is incorrect. Section 15.3 is not an anti-assignment provision; it is a change-of-control consent requirement that applies independently of whether the contracting entity survives. The section expressly includes "merger" as a trigger. After closing, Sablecrest will own 100% of Lebix—well above the 50% threshold—and Sablecrest did not control Lebix on the MSA Effective Date. This is unambiguously a change of control of Customer.

The Notice Letter also fails the procedural requirements of § 15.3 in two ways:

1. **It does not request consent.** The section requires the Party undergoing the change to "request consent by written notice." The letter states the opposite—that no consent is required. Dugein will argue it never received a valid request to which it could respond.

2. **It was sent too late.** The section requires notice "at least sixty (60) days before the change of control takes effect." The expected closing is December 15, 2026. The letter is dated October 20, 2026—56 days before closing. Even if we count from the date of dispatch, the 60-day minimum is not met.

**Consequences if Dugein treats this as an unconsented change of control (MSA § 15.3, final sentence):**

- Dugein may terminate the MSA on 30 days' written notice, given within 120 days after it "learns of the change of control." If "learns" means receipt of the Notice Letter (~October 21, 2026), the window closes approximately **February 19, 2027**. If "learns" means the actual closing (December 15, 2026), the window closes approximately **April 14, 2027**.
- On such termination, Lebix must pay an **early termination fee equal to six months' average monthly fees** (calculated under MSA § 6.5) within 30 days after termination takes effect.

Losing Dugein as its caterer mid-merger-integration would be operationally disruptive and would undermine the "continuity of services" representation in the Notice Letter (¶ 3).

**Recommendation:**

- **Immediately** send a corrected notice to Dugein that (a) expressly requests Dugein's written consent to the change of control under § 15.3, and (b) acknowledges that the prior letter was provided for informational purposes. Do this before the December 15 closing if at all possible, or in any event well within the 120-day window.
- Prepare a fallback: if Dugein withholds consent or terminates, assess the operational impact of losing catering at all Stations (including MDT) and the cost of the early termination fee. We will need the client's fee data to quantify the exposure (see Issue 7 below).
- Consider whether Sablecrest's merger agreement with Lebix contains any representation or covenant regarding the MSA that could give Sablecrest recourse against Lebix if the termination fee is triggered.

## Issue 2 – CRITICAL: The "Food Safety Incident" Definition Is Materially Narrower in the Quality Agreement Than in the MSA, and the Quality Agreement Controls

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 1.1 (definition of "Food Safety Incident")
- Quality Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Food Safety Incident")
- MSA § 1.3 (Order of Precedence)

**What the documents say:**

The MSA defines "Food Safety Incident" as:

> "any actual or reasonably suspected contamination, undeclared allergen, temperature excursion or foreign object in a Meal."

The Quality Agreement defines "Food Safety Incident" as:

> "a confirmed contamination of a Meal that results in illness of a passenger or crew member and is reported to a regulatory authority."

MSA § 1.3(a) provides that "the Quality Agreement prevails over the MSA on food safety and quality matters, including related defined terms, including the meaning of any defined term relating thereto." The definition of "Food Safety Incident" is squarely a defined term relating to food safety matters. The Quality Agreement's narrow definition therefore controls throughout the entire arrangement.

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

The narrow definition requires three cumulative elements: (1) *confirmed* contamination (not merely suspected), (2) *resulting in illness* of a passenger or crew member, and (3) *reported to a regulatory authority*. This eliminates from the definition:

- Temperature excursions (a major category in the MSA's broad definition)
- Foreign objects
- Undeclared allergens
- Any incident that is suspected but not yet confirmed
- Any incident that causes illness but is not reported to a regulator (e.g., a minor illness handled internally)
- Any incident that is reported to a regulator but does not result in confirmed illness

The practical consequences ripple through the entire agreement:

| Provision | Effect of narrow definition |
|---|---|
| MSA § 3.3 (notification and quarantine) | Provider's duty to notify and quarantine attaches only to confirmed contamination causing illness and reported to a regulator. A temperature excursion or foreign object triggers no contractual notification duty. |
| MSA § 3.4 / Quality Agreement § 4.7 (recall costs) | Provider bears recall costs only for a "Food Safety Incident" as narrowly defined. Costs of withdrawing meals after a temperature excursion or discovering a foreign object are not contractually allocated to Provider. |
| MSA § 16.3(a) (termination for serious illness/death) | Requires a "Food Safety Incident" (confirmed + illness + regulatory report) that *additionally* results in serious illness or death. A passenger who becomes seriously ill from a confirmed contamination that was never reported to a regulator does not trigger this right. |
| MSA § 16.3(c) (termination for repeat incidents) | Requires three or more "Food Safety Incidents" at one Station in a rolling 90-day period. Under the narrow definition, each incident must involve confirmed contamination, actual illness, and a regulatory report. This threshold is extremely difficult to reach. |
| Quality Agreement § 4.2 (2-hour notification) | Provider's obligation to call and email within two hours applies only to the narrow category. |
| Quality Agreement § 4.4 (withdrawals and recalls) | Provider's duty to execute a withdrawal or recall is triggered only by a "Food Safety Incident" as narrowly defined. |
| MSA § 12.3(b) as amended (see Issue 3) | The uncapped-liability carve-out applies only to the narrow category *and* only for gross negligence or wilful misconduct. |

In short, the most common and operationally significant food safety problems—temperature excursions, foreign objects, suspected contamination—fall outside the contractual food safety regime entirely.

**Recommendation:**

- Negotiate a conforming amendment to the Quality Agreement's definition of "Food Safety Incident" to align it with the MSA's broader definition, or at minimum to include temperature excursions, foreign objects, and undeclared allergens as independent triggers.
- In the interim, ensure that Lebix's internal food safety monitoring and complaint-handling procedures do not rely on the contractual definition. Operations should treat any contamination, temperature excursion, foreign object, or allergen issue as a reportable event internally, regardless of whether it meets the narrow contractual threshold.
- If a food safety problem arises that does not meet the narrow definition, Lebix should document it carefully and consider whether Applicable Law (e.g., FDA regulations) independently imposes obligations on Provider that can be enforced outside the contract.

## Issue 3 – MAJOR: Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Food Safety Liability Carve-Out from the Cap and Consequential Damages Exclusion

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 12.1 (liability cap), § 12.2 (consequential damages exclusion), § 12.3 (exclusions from cap and exclusion)
- Amendment No. 1, § 2.1

**What the documents say:**

Original MSA § 12.3(b) provided that the liability cap and consequential damages exclusion do not apply to:

> "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident."

Amendment No. 1, § 2.1 deleted and replaced this with:

> "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident caused by Provider's gross negligence or wilful misconduct."

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

Under the original provision, *any* Food Safety Incident—regardless of the degree of Provider's fault—was uncapped and not subject to the consequential damages exclusion. After Amendment No. 1, only Food Safety Incidents caused by *gross negligence or wilful misconduct* are uncapped. For a Food Safety Incident caused by ordinary negligence (the most common scenario), Provider's liability is:

- Capped at the fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the event (MSA § 12.1), and
- Excluded for indirect, incidental, special, consequential, or punitive damages, lost profits, or lost revenue (MSA § 12.2).

This is a substantial reduction in protection. In a food safety context, the most damaging losses for an airline—passenger medical claims, flight disruptions, reputational harm, regulatory fines—are often consequential or indirect. Under the amended provision, Lebix cannot recover those from Dugein unless it can prove gross negligence or wilful misconduct, a high bar.

**Compounding effect with Issue 2:** Because the Quality Agreement's narrow definition of "Food Safety Incident" controls (see Issue 2), the uncapped carve-out now requires *both* (a) confirmed contamination resulting in illness and reported to a regulatory authority, *and* (b) gross negligence or wilful misconduct. The circumstances in which Dugein's liability is truly uncapped are now very rare.

**Recommendation:**

- This amendment is already in effect (July 1, 2026) and was signed by both parties. It cannot be unilaterally reversed. However, if Lebix is in a position to negotiate (e.g., in connection with the change-of-control consent request under Issue 1), it should seek to restore the original § 12.3(b) or at least lower the fault threshold from "gross negligence or wilful misconduct" to "negligence."
- Ensure that Dugein's product contamination and recall insurance (MSA § 8.1(d), minimum $5,000,000) is adequate to cover the gap. Request a current certificate of insurance and confirm the policy responds to food safety incidents involving ordinary negligence.
- Review whether Lebix's own insurance program (MSA § 8.3) covers passenger claims arising from food safety incidents that fall within Dugein's capped liability.

## Issue 4 – MAJOR: Customer's Uncapped Airside Indemnity to Provider Is One-Sided

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 11.2 (indemnity by Customer)
- MSA § 12.3(c) (exclusion of § 11.2 from the cap)

**What the documents say:**

MSA § 11.2 provides:

> "Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Provider, its Affiliates and their respective directors, officers, employees and agents from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to Services performed Airside or aboard Customer's aircraft, **regardless of cause**, including bodily injury, death and damage to property."

MSA § 12.3(c) excludes "Customer's obligations under Section 11.2" from both the liability cap and the consequential damages exclusion.

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

This is a broad, fault-agnostic indemnity running from Customer to Provider for all airside and in-cabin services. The phrase "regardless of cause" means that even if Dugein's own negligence causes an airside injury or property damage, Lebix must indemnify Dugein. This is unusual and significantly one-sided.

Consider a scenario: a Dugein catering truck strikes a Lebix aircraft on the apron, causing hull damage and injuring a ground crew member. Under § 11.2, Lebix must defend and indemnify Dugein for the resulting losses, regardless of Dugein's fault. Meanwhile, Dugein's own liability to Lebix for the aircraft damage would be subject to the 12-month fee cap (MSA § 12.1) because aircraft damage is not a Food Safety Incident and does not fall within any § 12.3 exclusion.

The net effect is that for airside incidents, Lebix's recovery from Dugein is capped while its indemnity exposure to Dugein is uncapped.

**Recommendation:**

- In any future renegotiation (see Issue 1), seek to limit § 11.2 to losses "to the extent caused by the negligence or wilful misconduct of Customer or its personnel," or at minimum to delete "regardless of cause."
- Confirm that Lebix's aviation hull and liability insurance (MSA § 8.3) and Dugein's aviation general liability insurance (MSA § 8.1(a), $25,000,000 per occurrence, including hangarkeepers' coverage) are structured so that the insurance, not the indemnity, is the primary source of recovery for airside incidents.
- Review the insurance certificates to confirm that Dugein's policies name Lebix as additional insured and are primary and non-contributory (MSA § 8.2).

## Issue 5 – MODERATE: The Notice Letter's "Continuity of Services" Representation May Be Unenforceable If Dugein Terminates

**Documents and sections:**
- Notice Letter, ¶ 3

**What the documents say:**

The Notice Letter (¶ 3) states: "Lebix expects no change to the Services, the Stations, the Catering Orders currently in effect, or Lebix's operational contacts as a result of the transaction."

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

This is a statement of expectation, not a contractual obligation. If Dugein exercises its termination right under § 15.3 (see Issue 1), this representation provides no remedy. More practically, if the Sablecrest merger agreement contains a representation to Sablecrest that the MSA will continue uninterrupted, the Notice Letter's language could create an expectation that is difficult to meet if Dugein terminates.

**Recommendation:**

- Ensure that the Sablecrest merger agreement does not contain a representation or covenant that the MSA will survive the transaction without qualification. If it does, add a carve-out for Dugein's rights under § 15.3.
- Do not rely on the Notice Letter's continuity language as a legal protection. It is a courtesy statement.

## Issue 7 – MINOR: Press Release – No Apparent Breach, but Worth Noting

**Documents and sections:**
- Press Release (dated September 14, 2026)
- MSA § 14.2 (Publicity)
- MSA § 13.1 (Confidential Information)

**What the documents say:**

The Press Release announces Dugein's new Middletown, Pennsylvania flight kitchen, describing its capacity (9,000 meals/day), food safety certifications (GFSI-benchmarked), staffing (85 employees), and facilities. It does not name Lebix or any specific carrier.

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

MSA § 14.2 prohibits either Party from using the other's name, trademarks, or logos in a press release without prior written consent. The Press Release does not use Lebix's name, so § 14.2 is not violated.

MSA § 13.1 defines Confidential Information to include "security procedures" and other non-public information. The Press Release describes general facility features (allergen-controlled room, blast chillers, data logging) but does not disclose specific security procedures, flight schedules, or passenger data. There is no apparent confidentiality breach.

The Press Release is contextually relevant because it confirms that Dugein has built out the Middletown facility in anticipation of the MDT Station added by Amendment No. 1. It also confirms that the facility has a GFSI certification, satisfying Quality Agreement § 3.3.

**Recommendation:**

- No action required. File the Press Release for reference.
- If Lebix becomes aware that the Middletown facility will be serving the MDT Station, confirm that Dugein has delivered the GFSI certificate and audit report (Quality Agreement § 3.3) and that the Food Safety Plan is in place (Amendment No. 1, § 3.3; Quality Agreement § 3.1) before the first Catering Order for MDT is issued.

## Issue 8 – ADMINISTRATIVE: Automatic Renewal Date

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 16.1 (Term)

**What the documents say:**

The MSA has an initial term of five years from March 1, 2025, expiring February 28, 2030. It then renews automatically for successive one-year terms unless either Party gives notice of non-renewal at least 180 days before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters for Lebix:**

The first non-renewal notice deadline is approximately **September 1, 2029** (180 days before February 28, 2030). If neither party gives notice by that date, the MSA renews for another year (March 1, 2030 – February 28, 2031).

**Recommendation:**

- Calendar the September 1, 2029 date (and each subsequent 180-day mark) for the client.
- In the context of the Sablecrest merger, the new parent may want to reassess the catering relationship at the first renewal. This gives roughly three years to make that decision.
