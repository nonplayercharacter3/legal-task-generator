## Issue 1 – CRITICAL / TIME-SENSITIVE: Sablecrest Merger Triggers Change-of-Control Consent Requirement; Notice Letter Fails to Comply

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 15.3 (Change of Control)
- Notice letter, ¶¶ 1–2 (dated October 20, 2026)

**What the documents say:**

MSA § 15.3 provides that a "change of control" occurs "when any person or group that did not control a Party on the Catering MSA Effective Date acquires control of that Party." "Control" means ownership of more than 50% of voting interests or the power to direct management and policies. The section requires the Party undergoing the change of control to "request consent by written notice at least sixty (60) days before the change of control takes effect." If a change of control occurs without prior written consent, the non-consenting Party may terminate on 30 days' notice within 120 days after it learns of the change of control, and the Party undergoing the change of control must pay an early termination fee equal to six months' average monthly fees.

The notice letter states (¶ 2): "Lebix will survive the merger as the same legal entity. Lebix will remain the contracting party under the MSA, and the MSA will not be assigned or transferred. Section 15.2 of the MSA restricts assignment or transfer of the MSA, and no assignment or transfer will occur in connection with the merger. Accordingly, no consent or other action is required of Provider under the MSA in connection with the merger."

**Why it matters:**

The notice letter's reasoning is legally incorrect. Section 15.3 is not an anti-assignment provision; it is a change-of-control consent requirement. The fact that Lebix survives as the same legal entity and the MSA is not "assigned" is irrelevant. After the merger closes (expected December 15, 2026), Sablecrest will own 100% of Lebix. Sablecrest did not control Lebix on the Catering MSA Effective Date (March 1, 2025). This is a textbook change of control under § 15.3.

Two specific compliance failures:

1. **No consent was requested.** The letter affirmatively states that no consent is required. Section 15.3 requires the Party undergoing the change of control to "request consent by written notice." A courtesy notice that disclaims the need for consent does not satisfy this obligation.

2. **The 60-day advance notice may not be met.** The expected closing is December 15, 2026. Sixty days before that date is October 16, 2026. The notice letter was sent October 20, 2026—four days late. Even if the letter had properly requested consent, it was sent after the deadline.

**Consequences if Dugein asserts its rights:**

- Dugein may terminate the MSA on 30 days' written notice at any time within 120 days after it "learns of the change of control." Dugein received the notice letter on or about October 20, 2026. The 120-day window therefore closes on or about **February 17, 2027** (if measured from receipt of the letter) or **April 14, 2027** (if measured from the actual closing on December 15, 2026). The ambiguity itself is a risk.
- On such termination, Lebix (or Sablecrest, as the Party undergoing the change of control) would owe an early termination fee equal to six months' average monthly fees, payable within 30 days after termination takes effect.

**Recommendation:**

- **Immediately** send a supplemental letter to Dugein that (a) expressly requests Dugein's consent to the change of control under § 15.3, (b) acknowledges that the October 20 letter was intended to serve as the § 15.3 notice, and (c) asks Dugein to confirm in writing that it consents or states its position. This should be done before the December 15 closing if at all possible.
- If Dugein withholds consent or does not respond, assess the commercial cost of the early termination fee (six months' average monthly fees under MSA § 6.5) and the operational risk of losing the catering provider mid-contract.
- If the closing date shifts, recalculate the 60-day deadline and the 120-day termination window.
- **This is the most time-sensitive item in the file.** The window for Dugein to act is open now and will close no later than April 14, 2027.

**Information needed from client:**
- The actual expected closing date (the letter says December 15, 2026, but this depends on regulatory approvals—has it shifted?).
- Whether Sablecrest or its advisors have had any informal discussions with Dugein about the merger.
- The current average monthly fees (to quantify the potential early termination fee). We would need the fee data from Schedule C, which is not in the folder.

## Issue 2 – CRITICAL: Definitional Conflict on "Food Safety Incident" Guts Multiple MSA Protections

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 1.1 (definition of "Food Safety Incident")
- Quality Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Food Safety Incident")
- MSA § 1.3(a) (order of precedence)
- MSA §§ 3.3, 16.3(c), 12.3(b) (provisions that use the defined term)

**What the documents say:**

The MSA defines "Food Safety Incident" broadly: "any actual or reasonably suspected contamination, undeclared allergen, temperature excursion or foreign object in a Meal." (MSA § 1.1.)

The Quality Agreement defines it narrowly: "a confirmed contamination of a Meal that results in illness of a passenger or crew member and is reported to a regulatory authority." (Quality Agreement § 1.1.)

MSA § 1.3(a) provides that "the Quality Agreement prevails over the MSA on food safety and quality matters, including related defined terms, including the meaning of any defined term relating thereto."

**Why it matters:**

Because the Quality Agreement definition controls, the following MSA provisions are effectively neutered:

| MSA Provision | Effect of Broad Definition | Effect of Narrow (Controlling) Definition |
|---|---|---|
| § 3.3 (notification) | Provider must notify of any suspected contamination, temperature excursion, or foreign object | Provider need only notify of confirmed contamination that causes illness AND is reported to a regulator |
| § 16.3(c) (termination right) | 3+ incidents at a Station in 90 days triggers termination | 3+ *confirmed illness events reported to a regulator* at one Station in 90 days—vanishingly rare |
| § 12.3(b) (liability carve-out, as amended) | All Food Safety Incident losses uncapped | Only losses from confirmed illness + regulatory report are uncapped (and only if caused by gross negligence/wilful misconduct—see Issue 3) |
| Quality Agreement § 4.2 (2-hour notification) | 2-hour notice for any suspected problem | 2-hour notice only for confirmed illness events reported to a regulator |

In practical terms, if Dugein has a temperature excursion on a catering truck, a metal fragment in a meal, or a suspected allergen cross-contamination that does not result in a passenger getting sick and a regulatory report, **Lebix has no contractual right to notification, no termination trigger, and no uncapped recovery.**

**Recommendation:**

- Negotiate a conforming amendment that either (a) deletes the Quality Agreement's narrow definition and allows the MSA's broader definition to control, or (b) splits the term into two defined terms (e.g., "Food Safety Event" for the broader category and "Food Safety Incident" for the narrower category) and maps each MSA provision to the appropriate term.
- In the interim, ensure that operational staff treat any temperature excursion, foreign object, or suspected contamination as reportable under the Quality Agreement's general non-conformance provisions (§ 7.1) and the MSA's regulatory inspection notification duty (§ 3.5), even if the event does not meet the narrow "Food Safety Incident" definition.

## Issue 3 – MAJOR: Amendment No. 1 Narrows the Food Safety Liability Carve-Out

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 12.1 (liability cap), § 12.2 (consequential damages exclusion), § 12.3(b) (original carve-out)
- Amendment No. 1 § 2.1 (replacement of § 12.3(b))

**What the documents say:**

Original MSA § 12.3(b) excluded from the cap and consequential damages bar: "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident." No fault qualifier—any Food Safety Incident, regardless of cause, was uncapped.

Amendment No. 1 § 2.1 replaces this with: "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident **caused by Provider's gross negligence or wilful misconduct**."

**Why it matters:**

The amendment adds a fault threshold. For ordinary negligence (the most common basis of food-safety liability), Provider's exposure is now:
- Capped at 12 months of fees (MSA § 12.1), and
- Excluded of consequential damages such as lost revenue from cancelled or delayed flights, reputational harm, and passenger claims (MSA § 12.2).

Combined with Issue 2 (the narrow "Food Safety Incident" definition), the practical effect is that Lebix's uncapped recovery for food-safety problems is limited to the rare case where (a) a confirmed contamination causes passenger or crew illness, (b) the event is reported to a regulatory authority, AND (c) the contamination was caused by Dugein's gross negligence or wilful misconduct. In all other food-safety scenarios—including ordinary negligence resulting in illness—Lebix's recovery is capped and consequential damages are excluded.

**Recommendation:**

- If the relationship is to continue (particularly post-merger, when Sablecrest may be more risk-averse), negotiate to restore the original § 12.3(b) or at minimum lower the fault threshold to "negligence."
- If the amendment is not reopened, ensure that Dugein's product contamination and recall insurance (MSA § 8.1(d), minimum $5,000,000) is adequate to cover the gap between the cap and actual losses. Request a current certificate of insurance.
- Quantify the cap: 12 months of fees. We need Schedule C to do this. (See Issue 5.)

## Issue 4 – MODERATE: Customer Indemnity in MSA § 11.2 Is Broad and Uncapped

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 11.2 (Customer's indemnity to Provider)
- MSA § 12.3(c) (exclusion from cap)

**What the documents say:**

MSA § 11.2 requires Lebix to "defend, indemnify and hold harmless Provider... from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to Services performed Airside or aboard Customer's aircraft, **regardless of cause**, including bodily injury, death and damage to property." Section 12.3(c) carves this obligation out of the liability cap, making it unlimited.

**Why it matters:**

The "regardless of cause" language means that if Dugein's own negligent loading causes damage to a Lebix aircraft or injures a ramp worker, Lebix must indemnify Dugein. This is an unusual and one-sided provision. It also means that Dugein's own insurance (which includes hangarkeepers' coverage under § 8.1(a)) is the first line of defense, but if a loss exceeds Dugein's insurance, Lebix's indemnity obligation has no cap.

**Recommendation:**

- Flag this for the next renewal or amendment cycle. Seek to delete "regardless of cause" or to limit the indemnity to third-party claims not caused by Provider's negligence.
- In the interim, confirm that Dugein's aviation general liability policy (minimum $25,000,000 per occurrence under § 8.1(a)) includes hangarkeepers' coverage and that the policy is current. Request a certificate.

## Issue 7 – LOW: Dugein Press Release – No Clear Breach but Worth Noting

**Documents and sections:**
- Dugein press release (September 14, 2026), §§ 1–3
- MSA § 14.2 (Publicity), § 13 (Confidentiality)
- Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 (addition of Harrisburg/MDT)

**What the documents say:**

The press release announces Dugein's new Middletown, Pennsylvania flight kitchen "minutes from Harrisburg International Airport (MDT)." It describes the facility's capabilities, food safety plan, and GFSI certification. It does not name Lebix, use Lebix's trademarks, or identify Lebix as a client.

**Why it matters:**

MSA § 14.2 prohibits a Party from using the other's "name, trademarks or logos in any press release, advertising or public statement without the other's prior written consent." The press release does not appear to violate § 14.2 because it does not reference Lebix by name or trademark. However, it does publicly associate the new facility with MDT, which is a Station under the MSA. If the client considers the identity of its Stations to be Confidential Information (MSA § 13.1 lists "flight schedules" as confidential but does not explicitly list "Stations"), there is a thin argument that the press release disclosed confidential information. In practice, this is a weak argument because the addition of MDT was agreed in a signed amendment and the facility's location is a matter of public record.

The press release does confirm that the Middletown kitchen has a 21 C.F.R. Part 117 food safety plan and GFSI certification, which satisfies Amendment No. 1 § 3.3 (the precondition for beginning Services at the new Station).

**Recommendation:**

No action required. If the client is sensitive about public association with the MDT Station, it could raise the point with Dugein, but the legal basis is weak.
