## Issue 1 – TIME-SENSITIVE: Change of Control Consent Not Sought; Notice Letter Misstates the Law

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §15.3 (Change of Control)
- Notice Letter, ¶¶1–2 (dated October 20, 2026)
- MSA §15.2 (Assignment) – cited in the letter but not the operative provision

**Explanation:**

MSA §15.3 provides that "[a] change of control of either Party, including by merger, consolidation, sale of equity or sale of all or substantially all of its assets, requires the other Party's prior written consent." "Control" is defined as ownership of more than 50% of voting interests or the power to direct management and policies. A "change of control" occurs when a person or group that did not control a Party on the Catering MSA Effective Date (March 1, 2025) acquires control.

Under the Sablecrest merger described in the notice letter, Sablecrest will own 100% of surviving Lebix. Sablecrest did not control Lebix on March 1, 2025. This is unambiguously a change of control under §15.3.

The notice letter (¶2) argues that no consent is required because "Lebix will survive the merger as the same legal entity" and "no assignment or transfer will occur," citing MSA §15.2. This analysis is incorrect. Section 15.2 governs assignment of the contract; §15.3 separately governs change of control. The two provisions are independent. The survival of the corporate entity does not avoid §15.3, which is triggered by a change in who controls the entity, not by a change in the entity's legal identity.

Section 15.3 further requires that "the Party undergoing the change of control shall request consent by written notice at least sixty (60) days before the change of control takes effect." For a December 15, 2026 closing, the consent request was due by October 16, 2026. The October 20 letter was sent four days after that deadline and, in any event, does not request consent—it affirmatively states that none is required.

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

If the change of control occurs without Dugein's prior written consent, §15.3 provides two consequences:

- **(a)** Dugein may terminate the MSA on 30 days' written notice, given within 120 days after it "learns of the change of control." Because Dugein received the notice letter on or about October 20–22, 2026, there is a risk it will argue the 120-day window began then (expiring approximately February 18, 2027). More likely, the window runs from actual closing (December 15, 2026), expiring approximately April 14, 2027. Either way, Dugein will have a termination right for a meaningful period after closing.
- **(b)** On such termination, Lebix (the Party undergoing the change of control) must pay an early termination fee equal to six months' average monthly fees, payable within 30 days after termination takes effect.

Critically, unlike §15.2 (which provides that consent "shall not be unreasonably withheld"), §15.3 contains no reasonableness qualifier. Dugein may withhold consent for any reason or no reason.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. **Immediately** (and in any event before December 15, 2026) send a supplemental written notice to Dugein that (a) corrects the October 20 letter's statement that no consent is required, and (b) formally requests Dugein's prior written consent under §15.3 to the change of control that will occur at closing. Acknowledge that the 60-day advance notice period has not been met and explain why (e.g., the merger agreement was signed October 17, 2026, three days after the 60-day deadline).
2. Seek to obtain Dugein's consent in writing before closing. If Dugein withholds consent, assess commercial leverage: the early termination fee (six months' average monthly fees) plus the cost of re-tendering catering at all stations (including the newly added Harrisburg station) may be less than the strategic cost of delaying or restructuring the Sablecrest merger.
3. If consent is obtained, document it in a written instrument signed by both parties (per MSA §18.7).
4. Consider whether to seek a waiver of the 60-day timing requirement as part of the consent negotiation.

**Information Needed from Client:**
- The executed Sablecrest merger agreement (to confirm the structure, closing conditions, and whether there is any provision addressing third-party contract consents).
- Confirmation of whether Dugein has responded to the October 20 notice letter.
- The average monthly fees for the trailing 12 months (to quantify the potential early termination fee).
- Whether Sablecrest or Lebix has any commercial relationship with or interest in Dugein that might affect the consent negotiation.

## Issue 2: Direct Conflict in the Definition of "Food Safety Incident"

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §1.1 (Definitions): "Food Safety Incident" means "any actual or reasonably suspected contamination, undeclared allergen, temperature excursion or foreign object in a Meal."
- Quality Agreement §1.1 (Definitions): "Food Safety Incident" means "a confirmed contamination of a Meal that results in illness of a passenger or crew member and is reported to a regulatory authority."
- MSA §1.3(a) (Order of Precedence): "the Quality Agreement prevails over the MSA on food safety and quality matters, including related defined terms, including the meaning of any defined term relating thereto."

**Explanation:**

The two definitions are radically different in scope. The MSA definition captures any actual or suspected problem—temperature excursions, foreign objects, undeclared allergens—regardless of whether anyone is harmed. The Quality Agreement definition requires three cumulative elements: (1) confirmed contamination, (2) resulting illness of a passenger or crew member, and (3) reporting to a regulatory authority.

Under the express order-of-precedence rule in MSA §1.3(a), the Quality Agreement's narrow definition controls. This is not a close question; §1.3(a) specifically addresses "the meaning of any defined term relating thereto" (i.e., relating to food safety and quality matters).

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

The defined term "Food Safety Incident" appears in multiple operative provisions, and the narrow definition materially weakens each:

| Provision | Effect of Narrow Definition |
|-----------|----------------------------|
| MSA §3.3 (Notification & Quarantine) | Provider's duty to notify and quarantine is triggered only by confirmed contamination causing illness and reported to a regulator. Temperature excursions, foreign objects, and suspected (unconfirmed) contamination do not trigger the notification obligation. |
| MSA §3.4 (Recall Costs) | Provider bears recall costs only for the narrow category. Costs of withdrawals for temperature excursions or foreign objects are not allocated to Provider under this section. |
| MSA §16.3(c) (Termination Trigger) | The right to terminate for "three or more Food Safety Incidents at any one Station in any rolling 90-day period" is nearly impossible to trigger. Three confirmed illnesses reported to a regulator at a single station in 90 days is an extreme threshold. |
| Quality Agreement §4.2 (2-Hour Notification) | The 2-hour telephone and email notification duty applies only to the narrow definition. |
| MSA §12.3(b) (as amended – see Issue 3) | The exclusion from the liability cap applies only to the narrow definition. |

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Negotiate a conforming amendment to the Quality Agreement's definition of "Food Safety Incident" to align it with the broader MSA definition, or at minimum to include temperature excursions and foreign objects as standalone triggers (even if they don't result in illness).
2. If Dugein resists, consider adding a separate defined term (e.g., "Food Safety Non-Conformance") that captures the broader category and attaching the notification, quarantine, and cost-allocation obligations to that term.
3. In the interim, ensure that operational procedures (complaint logging, temperature monitoring, crew reports) capture all events that would qualify under the broader MSA definition, so that Lebix has the factual record to argue a broader interpretation if a dispute arises.

**Information Needed from Client:**
- Any historical food safety events (temperature excursions, foreign objects, allergen complaints) that occurred under the narrow definition but would have qualified under the MSA definition. This will help assess real-world exposure.
- Whether the parties discussed the definitional conflict at the time of signing or in connection with Amendment No. 1.

## Issue 3: Amendment No. 1 Narrows the Liability Cap Exclusion for Food Safety Incidents

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §12.1 (Liability Cap): aggregate liability capped at fees paid or payable in the preceding 12 months.
- MSA §12.2 (Consequential Damages Exclusion): no liability for indirect, incidental, special, consequential or punitive damages, lost profits or lost revenue.
- MSA §12.3 (Exclusions from Cap and Consequential Damages Exclusion): originally subpara. (b) excluded "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident" from both §12.1 and §12.2.
- Amendment No. 1 §2.1: replaces §12.3(b) with "Provider's liability for Losses arising from a Food Safety Incident **caused by Provider's gross negligence or wilful misconduct**."

**Explanation:**

The original §12.3(b) meant that any Food Safety Incident—however caused—was excluded from both the liability cap and the consequential damages exclusion. Provider's liability for food safety problems was effectively unlimited.

The amendment adds a fault threshold: the exclusion from the cap and consequential damages exclusion now applies only where the Food Safety Incident was "caused by Provider's gross negligence or wilful misconduct." For any Food Safety Incident caused by ordinary negligence (the most common scenario in food safety failures), Provider's liability is subject to the 12-month-fee cap and the consequential damages exclusion.

This must be read in conjunction with Issue 2: "Food Safety Incident" already has the narrow Quality Agreement definition. So the combined effect is that Provider's liability is uncapped and includes consequential damages only where:

1. There is confirmed contamination (not merely suspected);
2. A passenger or crew member is actually ill;
3. The incident is reported to a regulatory authority; **and**
4. The contamination was caused by Provider's gross negligence or wilful misconduct (not ordinary negligence).

This is an extremely high bar. In a typical food safety failure involving ordinary negligence (e.g., a temperature log is missed, a cleaning procedure is not followed, an ingredient is mislabeled), even if passengers become ill and the incident is reported to the FDA, Provider's liability would be capped at 12 months of fees and consequential damages (such as lost ticket revenue, brand damage, and regulatory fines passed through) would be excluded.

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

- The 12-month-fee cap may be insufficient to cover the costs of a serious food safety event (regulatory fines, passenger claims, rebranding, lost contracts).
- The consequential damages exclusion means Lebix cannot recover lost revenue from diverted passengers, reputational harm, or the cost of finding a replacement caterer following a termination under §16.3.
- The gross negligence / wilful misconduct threshold is difficult to prove in food safety cases, which often involve systemic or procedural failures that constitute ordinary negligence.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Negotiate to restore the original §12.3(b) (no fault threshold), or at minimum lower the threshold to "negligence" (i.e., any negligence, not just gross negligence or wilful misconduct).
2. If Dugein will not move on the fault threshold, negotiate a higher cap specifically for food safety matters (e.g., 3x or 5x the standard 12-month-fee cap) that applies to Food Safety Incidents caused by ordinary negligence.
3. Ensure the product contamination and recall insurance required by MSA §8.1(d) ($5,000,000 minimum) is adequate to cover the gap between the insurance proceeds and the cap. Request current certificates of insurance.
4. Consider whether the insurance requirement in §8.1(d) should be increased given the narrowed contractual protection.

**Information Needed from Client:**
- Current product contamination and recall insurance limits actually carried by Dugein (the MSA requires a minimum of $5M, but Dugein may carry more).
- An estimate of 12 months of fees (to quantify the cap).
- Whether the Amendment No. 1 negotiation was documented in side letters or emails that might evidence a different understanding.

## Issue 4: Customer's Broad Airside Indemnity (MSA §11.2)

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §11.2: "Customer shall defend, indemnify and hold harmless Provider, its Affiliates and their respective directors, officers, employees and agents from and against all Losses arising out of or relating to Services performed Airside or aboard Customer's aircraft, **regardless of cause**, including bodily injury, death and damage to property."

**Explanation:**

This indemnity is extraordinarily broad. The phrase "regardless of cause" means that Lebix must indemnify Dugein even where the loss is caused entirely by Dugein's own negligence or wilful misconduct. For example:

- If a Dugein high-lift vehicle strikes an aircraft on the ramp (Airside), causing $2M in hull damage, Lebix indemnifies Dugein for that loss even though Dugein's operator was at fault.
- If a Dugein employee is injured Airside due to Dugein's own failure to follow safety procedures, Lebix indemnifies Dugein.
- If Dugein's catering truck causes a third-party property damage claim on the ramp, Lebix indemnifies Dugein.

This operates in tandem with MSA §11.1, which requires Dugein to indemnify Lebix for third-party claims caused by Dugein's breach, negligence, or food safety failures. The net effect is that for Airside events, Lebix's indemnity to Dugein (§11.2) may swallow Dugein's indemnity to Lebix (§11.1), because §11.2 applies "regardless of cause."

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

- Aircraft damage claims can be very large (a regional turboprop or narrow-body can represent $10M–$50M in hull value).
- The indemnity is not subject to the liability cap in §12.1 because §12.3(c) expressly excludes "Customer's obligations under Section 11.2" from the cap.
- This creates uncapped, no-fault exposure for Lebix for Airside events.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Negotiate to delete "regardless of cause" and replace with "to the extent not caused by the negligence or wilful misconduct of Provider or its personnel." This would make the indemnity mutual in principle: each party indemnifies the other for third-party claims to the extent the indemnifying party is at fault.
2. If Dugein resists, at minimum carve out from §11.2 any loss "to the extent caused by the negligence or wilful misconduct of Provider or its personnel or subcontractors."
3. Verify that Lebix's aviation hull and liability insurance (required by MSA §8.3) adequately covers Airside third-party claims and that the policy does not exclude loss caused by the negligence of a contractor (which could create a gap between the insurance and the indemnity).

**Information Needed from Client:**
- Lebix's current aviation hull and liability insurance policy, including any contractor-negligence exclusions or endorsements.
- History of any Airside incidents or claims under the MSA.

## Issue 6: Dugein Press Release – Publicity and Competitive Concerns

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §14.2 (Publicity): "Neither Party may use the other's name, trademarks or logos in any press release, advertising or public statement without the other's prior written consent."
- Dugein Press Release, dated September 14, 2026: announces the new Middletown, PA flight kitchen "minutes from Harrisburg International Airport (MDT)."

**Explanation:**

The press release does not name Lebix, use its trademarks, or display its logos. On its face, it does not violate §14.2. However, it publicly announces that Dugein has opened a new production facility specifically positioned to serve Harrisburg International Airport—the same station added to Schedule A by Amendment No. 1 for Lebix's benefit. A reasonable reader in the regional airline industry would infer that Dugein is building capacity for a new carrier client at MDT.

Additionally, MSA §13.1 includes "flight schedules" and "pricing" in the definition of Confidential Information. While the station list itself is not explicitly listed, the identity of a new station and the associated capacity build could be argued to be non-public information disclosed "in connection with this Agreement."

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

- Competitors operating at or near MDT (or considering entry) now know that Dugein has dedicated flight-kitchen capacity in central Pennsylvania, which reduces Lebix's competitive moat in that market.
- The press release may make it more difficult for Lebix to negotiate exclusive or preferred catering arrangements at MDT in the future.
- If Dugein is also courting other carriers for the MDT facility, the public announcement signals available capacity that could attract competition.

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Send a brief written note to Dugein acknowledging the press release and requesting that future communications about the Middletown facility not reference specific airports or imply a particular carrier client without prior written consent under §14.2.
2. In the consent negotiation under Issue 1, raise the press release as a goodwill item and seek a commitment to coordinate future publicity.
3. Assess whether Lebix wants to pursue exclusivity or first-right-of-refusal for the Middletown facility's capacity, given that it was built in anticipation of the Harrisburg station.

**Information Needed from Client:**
- Whether Lebix gave any prior written consent to the press release or was aware of it in advance.
- Whether other carriers operate at or near MDT and whether Lebix considers the Harrisburg station strategically important.

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §6.4: "Fees may be adjusted once in each contract year, on the anniversary of the Catering MSA Effective Date, by the percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (Food Away from Home) over the preceding twelve months. No adjustment may exceed four percent (4%)."

**Explanation:**

The Catering MSA Effective Date is March 1, 2025. The first anniversary adjustment occurred on March 1, 2026. The next adjustment will occur on March 1, 2027—approximately four months from the date of the notice letter. The adjustment is based on CPI (Food Away from Home) over the preceding 12 months (March 2026–February 2027), capped at 4%.

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

- Food Away from Home CPI has been elevated in recent periods. A 4% increase on the full catering spend could be material.
- The adjustment is automatic ("may be adjusted" is permissive in form but in practice will be invoked by Dugein if CPI is positive).
- We cannot assess the dollar impact without Schedule C (see Issue 5).

**Recommended Fix:**

1. Obtain Schedule C to model the dollar impact of a 4% increase.
2. In the consent negotiation under Issue 1, consider requesting a waiver of or cap on the March 2027 adjustment as a concession.
3. Ensure that any adjustment is properly calculated (12-month trailing CPI, not annualized or forward-looking) and that Dugein provides the supporting CPI data.

**Information Needed from Client:**

- Whether Dugein has already communicated an intended adjustment for March 1, 2027.

## Issue 8: No Minimum Volume Commitment – Double-Edged Sword

**Documents and Sections:**
- MSA §2.1: "This Agreement does not require Customer to purchase any minimum volume of Services."

**Explanation:**

Lebix is not contractually obligated to purchase any minimum quantity of catering. This is favorable in that Lebix can scale down or exit individual stations (via §16.5, termination for convenience on 90 days' notice per Catering Order) without penalty. However, it also means Dugein has no guaranteed revenue floor, which may affect:

- Dugein's willingness to invest in the new Middletown facility (it has built 38,000 sq. ft. of capacity with no volume commitment from Lebix);
- Dugein's leverage in the consent negotiation under Issue 1 (Dugein may be more willing to grant consent if it perceives the Sablecrest merger as strengthening Lebix's creditworthiness and long-term viability);
- Service quality if Dugein feels under-compensated for dedicated capacity.

**Why It Matters for Lebix:**

- If Dugein withholds change-of-control consent (Issue 1), it may be because it perceives the no-minimum-volume structure as making the relationship fragile, particularly under new ownership.
- Conversely, if the Sablecrest merger strengthens Lebix, Dugein may be more amenable to consent.

**Recommended Fix:**

No immediate contractual fix required. However, in the consent negotiation, be prepared for Dugein to request a minimum volume commitment or a longer non-renewal notice period as the price of its consent. Assess whether such a concession is acceptable given the strategic importance of the Sablecrest merger.
