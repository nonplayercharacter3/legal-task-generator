## Issue 3 – Exclusivity Obligation Dramatically Expanded

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Term Sheet | §7.1 | Exclusivity for "voyage-optimization software **only**, for dry-bulk vessels **owned by Nipusk**" |
| Term Sheet | §7.2 | "Nipusk remains free to select and use other providers for **all other functions and vessels**" |
| Agreement | §3.4 | "Licensee shall not… license, procure, develop, deploy or use **any Competing Product** on or in respect of **any Vessel**" |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Competing Product") | Covers: (a) fleet management; (b) voyage optimization, weather routing or speed and consumption planning; (c) emissions monitoring, calculation or reporting (CII, EEXI, EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime, IMO DCS); (d) hull, propeller or engine performance monitoring; (e) crew management, crew planning or crew scheduling |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Vessel") | "any vessel **owned, chartered, managed or operated** by Licensee or **any Affiliate** of Licensee, **wherever located in the world**" |

**Why it matters:**

The Term Sheet contemplated a narrow exclusivity: voyage-optimization only, Nipusk-owned dry-bulk vessels only. The Agreement expands this to:

- **Five functional categories** (fleet management, voyage optimization, emissions compliance, performance monitoring, crew management) — effectively the entire digital operations stack for a bulk carrier.
- **All vessels** in Nipusk's and its Affiliates' portfolios, whether owned, chartered, managed or operated.
- **Affiliate vessels** worldwide.

This means Nipusk cannot use a competing crew-management tool, a third-party emissions reporting platform, or a different fleet-management system on any vessel it operates or its Affiliates operate, for the entire Term. For a bulk shipping company that may charter in vessels or operate through multiple corporate entities, this is a significant operational constraint.

Additionally, the exclusivity runs to Affiliates (Agreement §3.4: "shall procure that its Affiliates do not"), meaning Nipusk is contractually responsible for its Affiliates' compliance — a risk if an Affiliate independently procures a competing tool.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement is still in force (i.e., the non-renewal fails), negotiate an amendment to narrow §3.4 to match the Term Sheet: voyage-optimization only, Nipusk-owned vessels only, with an express carve-out for Affiliates' independently operated vessels.
2. If the Agreement expires as intended, this issue becomes moot, but it is relevant to any future negotiation with Lusen or a successor provider.
3. **Internally**, issue a compliance notice to all Affiliates and to Fleet Operations confirming that, until December 31, 2026, no Competing Product may be deployed on any Vessel. Document any existing third-party tools in use to assess current breach risk.

**Information needed from client:** Does Nipusk currently use any third-party software that falls within the "Competing Product" definition on any vessel (owned, chartered, or operated by an Affiliate)? If so, Nipusk may already be in breach of §3.4.

## Issue 4 – Lusen's Unilateral Assignment Right (Including to Competitors)

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Term Sheet | §10.3 | "neither party may assign the Definitive Agreement without the other party's prior written consent" |
| Agreement | §16.2 | Mutual consent required (consistent with Term Sheet) |
| Agreement | §16.3 | "Licensor may assign, transfer or novate this Agreement, in whole or in part, together with its rights in and to Vessel Data under Schedule 2, to any **Permitted Transferee** without the consent of or notice to Licensee." |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Permitted Transferee") | "any person, **including any shipowner, ship operator or charterer**, that acquires… all or any part of Licensor's business or assets, and any Affiliate of Licensor" |

**Why it matters:**

The Term Sheet provided for mutual consent to assignment. The Agreement adds §16.3, which carves out a broad unilateral right for Lusen to assign to any "Permitted Transferee" — expressly including **shipowners, ship operators and charterers** — without Nipusk's consent or even notice.

This is a serious concern for a shipping company:

- If Lusen is acquired by (or merges with) a competing shipowner or operator, that entity would step into Lusen's shoes with access to Nipusk's Vessel Data (AIS positions, fuel consumption, route data, charter-party particulars, emissions data) and no obligation to notify Nipusk.
- The "Permitted Transferee" definition is not limited to software companies. A major bulk carrier could acquire Lusen and gain insight into Nipusk's fleet operations, trading patterns, and commercial data.
- Nipusk has no consent right, no notice right, and no termination right triggered by such an assignment.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement continues (renewal scenario), negotiate an amendment to §16.3 to: (a) require at least 30 days' prior written notice to Nipusk; (b) give Nipusk a termination right (without penalty) if the Permitted Transferee is a direct competitor or a shipowner/operator/charterer; and (c) in all cases, require the transferee to assume Lusen's confidentiality and data-protection obligations.
2. If the Agreement expires, this is moot, but it should be a key term in any future agreement with Lusen or a successor.
3. **Internally**, assess whether Nipusk's Vessel Data (particularly charter-party particulars and route data) would be competitively sensitive in the hands of a rival shipowner. If so, the risk is acute.

## Issue 5 – Split Governing Law and Forum for Vessel Data

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §15.1 | Delaware law; exclusive jurisdiction of Wilmington, Delaware courts |
| Agreement | §1.3 | Schedules prevail over the main body in case of conflict |
| Schedule 2 | ¶9 | "This Schedule 2, and any dispute or claim arising out of or in connection with Vessel Data, is governed by… the laws of **England and Wales**. Any such dispute or claim shall be referred to **arbitration in London**… before a tribunal of three arbitrators." |
| Term Sheet | §13 | Delaware law; Wilmington courts (no carve-out for data disputes) |

**Why it matters:**

The Term Sheet provided for a single governing law and forum (Delaware / Wilmington). The final Agreement introduces a split regime: Vessel Data disputes are governed by English law and resolved by London maritime arbitration (LMAA, three arbitrators).

Practical consequences for Nipusk:

- **Cost:** London arbitration with a three-arbitrator tribunal is significantly more expensive than Delaware litigation, particularly for data-protection disputes that may not involve large sums.
- **Complexity:** Nipusk must maintain two legal strategies and potentially two sets of counsel (Delaware and English) for a single contractual relationship.
- **Inconsistent outcomes:** A Delaware court and a London tribunal could reach different conclusions on overlapping issues (e.g., whether a data breach constitutes a material breach of the Agreement generally vs. a breach of Schedule 2 specifically).
- **Enforcement:** An LMAA award is enforceable under the New York Convention, but the process is still slower and more costly than a Delaware judgment.
- **No basis in Term Sheet:** This appears to have been inserted by Lusen without flagging it as a departure from the agreed commercial terms.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement continues, negotiate to delete Schedule 2 ¶9 and bring Vessel Data disputes under the main Agreement's Delaware law / Wilmington forum clause. Alternatively, if English law is preferred for data-protection reasons (e.g., alignment with GDPR-adjacent concepts), at least reduce the tribunal to a sole arbitrator to control costs.
2. If the Agreement expires, this is moot, but note it for any future data-processing agreement.
3. **Flag to client:** If Nipusk experiences a data security incident before December 31, 2026, the dispute will be governed by English law and referred to London arbitration. Ensure the client understands this before any incident occurs.

## Issue 6 – Service Level Schedule Stripped of Protections from Lusen's Own Prior Draft

**Where it appears:**

The prior draft (circulated by Lusen on November 15, 2023, marked "DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION / Subject to contract") contained the following provisions that are **absent** from the final Schedule 3 in the executed Agreement:

| Prior Draft Provision | Content | Final Schedule 3 |
|---|---|---|
| ¶1.3 (Definitions) | Defined "Availability" as "the percentage of minutes in a calendar month during which the hosted components of the Software are **accessible and materially functional**" | No definition of "Availability." Final ¶1 says "available at least 99.5% of the time" without specifying that the software must be "materially functional." |
| ¶2.2 (Monitoring Records) | "Licensor shall measure Availability using automated monitoring tools. Licensor shall keep monitoring records for at least twelve (12) months and make them available to Licensee on reasonable request." | Absent. |
| ¶3.3 (Maintenance Planning) | "Licensor shall use reasonable efforts to schedule maintenance so that routing outputs for Vessels at sea are not interrupted." | Absent. |
| ¶4.2 (Severity Confirmation) | "Licensor shall confirm or reasonably reclassify the Severity on first response." | Absent. |
| ¶5.1 (Response Time Trigger) | "Response time runs from Licensor's receipt of the Incident report through a support channel listed in paragraph 4.1." | Absent (arguably implied, but not stated). |
| ¶5.2 (Severity 1 Progress Updates) | "Licensor shall keep Licensee informed of progress on Severity 1 Incidents at intervals of no more than two (2) hours until the Incident is resolved or a workaround is in place." | Absent. |
| ¶8.2 (Quarterly Review) | "At either party's request, the parties shall meet each quarter to review the monthly service reports and any recurring Incidents." | Absent. |

**Why it matters:**

- **The "materially functional" gap is the most significant.** Without it, Lusen could argue the Software is "available" (i.e., the server responds) even if routing outputs are wrong, incomplete, or the platform is in a degraded state. For a voyage-optimization tool used by masters at sea, this distinction is critical.
- **No monitoring records obligation** means Nipusk has no independent way to verify Lusen's availability claims when disputing a service credit.
- **No Severity 1 progress updates** means that if the platform goes down while vessels are at sea, Nipusk has no contractual right to hourly status updates.
- **No quarterly review** removes a structured forum to address recurring issues before they escalate.

The fact that these provisions were in Lusen's own draft and were removed in the final version suggests they were negotiated out, but the removal is adverse to Nipusk and should be understood.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement continues, negotiate a revised Schedule 3 that restores at minimum: (a) the "materially functional" definition of Availability; (b) the monitoring records obligation; (c) the Severity 1 progress update requirement; and (d) the quarterly review meeting.
2. If the Agreement expires, these points are relevant to any transition-period service expectations (see Issue 8) and to any future agreement.
3. **Internally**, document any service-level failures during the remainder of the Initial Term (through December 31, 2026) with timestamps and impact descriptions. This creates a record that (a) supports any service credit claims and (b) could evidence a pattern of chronic failure or material breach if the renewal is triggered.

## Issue 7 – Service Credit Calculation Is Ambiguous

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement, Schedule 3 | ¶5 | Credit is "the following percentage of **one-twelfth of the annual license fee**" |
| Agreement, Schedule 1 | ¶3 | "the annual license fee is US$18,500 **per Vessel** per contract year" |

**Why it matters:**

The "annual license fee" in Schedule 1 is expressed on a per-vessel basis. It is unclear whether the service credit in Schedule 3 ¶5 is calculated:

- (a) per vessel (i.e., 20% × $18,500/12 = ~$308 per affected vessel per month), or
- (b) on the total fleet-wide annual fee (i.e., 20% × [total fleet fee]/12).

If (a), the credits are trivially small and provide little real incentive for Lusen to maintain availability. If (b), they are more meaningful. The prior draft has the same ambiguity, so this was not resolved during negotiation.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Seek clarification from Lusen in writing (this can be done without amendment — a request for confirmation of interpretation).
2. If the Agreement continues, negotiate an amendment to specify that the credit is calculated on the total annual license fee for all Covered Vessels.
3. In the interim, when claiming service credits, calculate on the fleet-wide basis (the more favourable interpretation) and state the basis in the claim letter.

## Issue 9 – "Vessel" Definition Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Term Sheet | §3.3 | "Nipusk-**owned** dry-bulk vessels listed in an onboarding plan" |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Vessel") | "any vessel **owned, chartered, managed or operated** by Licensee or **any Affiliate** of Licensee, **wherever located in the world**, from time to time during the Term" |

**Why it matters:**

This broader definition affects:

- **Exclusivity** (§3.4): Nipusk cannot use a Competing Product on chartered or Affiliate-operated vessels.
- **Fees** (Schedule 1, ¶2): "Covered Vessels" are those "owned by Licensee and listed in the onboarding plan," but the "Vessel" definition is broader. There is a potential mismatch: are chartered vessels "Covered Vessels" for fee purposes? The fee structure is per-vessel, so if a chartered vessel is a "Vessel" but not a "Covered Vessel," the exclusivity applies but no fee is charged — which is fine for Nipusk. But if Lusen argues that all "Vessels" must be configured and paid for, this becomes a fee dispute.
- **Data scope** (Schedule 2, ¶1): "Vessel Data" relates to "any Vessel," so data from chartered or Affiliate vessels processed through the Software is subject to the Schedule 2 terms.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Clarify with Lusen in writing whether "Covered Vessels" for fee purposes are limited to Nipusk-owned vessels (as in Schedule 1, ¶2) or extend to all "Vessels" (as in the definition).
2. If the Agreement continues, negotiate to align the "Vessel" definition with the Term Sheet (Nipusk-owned dry-bulk vessels only) or to at least carve out vessels on time charter where Nipusk does not control the technical/operational decisions.

## Issue 10 – Feedback Clause Is Overly Broad

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §8.2 | "Licensor may use any suggestions or feedback that Licensee provides about the Software **without restriction or obligation**." |
| Term Sheet | — | No feedback provision |

**Why it matters:**

This clause was not in the Term Sheet. It gives Lusen an unrestricted right to use any feedback Nipusk provides — which could include Nipusk's proprietary operational insights, preferred routing strategies, fuel-efficiency parameters, or fleet-specific requirements. There is no limitation to feedback that is "general" or "non-confidential," and no obligation on Lusen to keep it separate from Nipusk's Confidential Information (though §10.1 would arguably cover information "marked confidential or would reasonably be understood to be confidential").

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement continues, negotiate to limit §8.2 to feedback that does not incorporate Nipusk's Confidential Information, and to require Lusen to use such feedback only to improve the Software generally (not for the benefit of Nipusk's competitors).
2. **Internally**, advise Nipusk's technical team to be cautious about providing detailed operational feedback to Lusen before December 31, 2026. Mark any feedback as "Confidential – Nipusk proprietary" to bring it within §10.1.

## Issue 11 – Software Warranty Remedy Is Limited

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §9.2 | Remedy: "commercially reasonable efforts to correct" or, if that fails, termination of the affected module with refund of prepaid fees |
| Agreement | §11.3 | Exclusion of indirect, consequential, special or punitive damages, loss of profits, revenue or charter hire |
| Agreement | §11.4 | Liability cap: Fees paid and payable in the relevant 12-month period |

**Why it matters:**

If the Software fails to perform materially in accordance with the Documentation, Nipusk's remedy is limited to:
- Lusen trying to fix it (no deadline specified beyond "reasonable time"), or
- Terminating the affected module and getting a refund of prepaid fees for that module.

Nipusk cannot claim damages for, e.g., a vessel taking a sub-optimal route because the Software gave wrong output, or for lost charter hire. The §11.3 exclusion of "loss of profits, revenue or charter hire" is particularly pointed for a shipping company.

This is not unusual in software licensing, but combined with the broad exclusivity obligation (Issue 3), Nipusk is in a position where it must use a potentially defective product as its sole solution and has limited recourse if it underperforms.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement continues, negotiate to: (a) add a time limit for Lusen to correct (e.g., 30 days) after which Nipusk may terminate the affected module; (b) clarify that the warranty covers all four modules listed in Schedule 1; and (c) add a carve-out from §11.3 for losses arising from Lusen's gross negligence or willful misconduct in the Software's operation.
2. **Internally**, ensure that masters and officers are reminded of §9.3 (the Software is "decision support only" and the master remains responsible for safe navigation). This is both a safety issue and a limitation on Nipusk's ability to claim that the Software "caused" a navigational error.

## Issue 12 – Non-Renewal Letter: Drafting Observations

**Where it appears:** Non-Renewal Letter, throughout.

**Observations:**

1. **§2.1 cites the wrong document.** The letter says the notice is given "in accordance with the renewal terms agreed between the parties in the term sheet." It should cite Agreement §12.2. This is not fatal (the notice is still a valid communication of non-renewal intent), but it gives Lusen an easy argument that the notice is defective in form.

3. **§4.2 (Reservation of Rights)** is well-drafted and appropriately preserves Nipusk's position that continued use or payment before year-end does not constitute acceptance of a renewal.

4. **No reference to the 90-day deadline.** The letter does not acknowledge that the Agreement requires 90 days. This omission, combined with the express citation of the Term Sheet, makes the letter vulnerable to challenge.

**Recommended fix:**

This feeds into Issue 1. The immediate priority is to contact Lusen and seek confirmation that the notice is accepted. If Lusen pushes back, consider whether a supplemental letter can be sent that (a) re-states the non-renewal intent, (b) acknowledges the 90-day requirement in the Agreement, and (c) requests that Lusen waive the 18-day shortfall as a matter of commercial fairness given that Nipusk's intent was clear and communicated well before year-end.
