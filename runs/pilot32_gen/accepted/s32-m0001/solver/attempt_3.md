## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nipusk has issued a non-renewal notice dated 20 October 2026, but that notice is **likely ineffective** under the executed Agreement because it gives only 72 days' notice where 90 days are required, and it cites the superseded Term Sheet rather than the Agreement. If the notice fails, the Agreement auto-renews for a **three-year** Renewal Term on 1 January 2027.

## ISSUE 1: NON-RENEWAL NOTICE IS LIKELY INVALID (URGENT)

**Where:**
- Agreement §12.2 (Renewal): requires written notice of non-renewal "at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term."
- Non-Renewal Letter ¶2.1: cites Term Sheet §5.2 (30-day notice) as the basis.
- Term Sheet §5.2: provided for one-year renewal terms with 30 days' notice.
- Agreement §1.3 and §17.4: "This Agreement supersedes the Term Sheet."

**Why it matters:**
The Initial Term ends 31 December 2026. The letter is dated 20 October 2026, giving **72 days** (11 remaining days in October + 30 in November + 31 in December). Under §12.2, 90 days' notice would have been required by **1 October 2026** (90 days before 31 December). The notice is 18 days late.

The letter's reliance on the Term Sheet's 30-day provision is unavailing because the Agreement expressly supersedes the Term Sheet (§1.3, §17.4). There is no contractual basis for the shorter period.

If the notice is held ineffective, the Agreement renews automatically for a **three-year** Renewal Term (not the one-year term in the Term Sheet) beginning 1 January 2027, with fees increased by 7% (Schedule 1 ¶5).

**Recommended fix:**
1. **Immediately** send a supplemental or re-issued notice under §12.2, acknowledging the 90-day requirement and arguing that the original notice, though late, should be given effect (or that Lusen's conduct estops it from relying on the technical deficiency). This is a weak argument but worth putting on record.
2. **In parallel**, open a commercial dialogue with Lusen to confirm by agreement (a written side letter or amendment) that the parties intend the Agreement to expire on 31 December 2026. Given that Nipusk has already signalled its intention to leave, Lusen has limited commercial leverage to insist on renewal, but we need their written confirmation.
3. **Preserve evidence** of the courier dispatch and email delivery of the 20 October letter. If Lusen received and acknowledged it without objecting to the timing, there may be an argument of waiver or estoppel, though this is fact-dependent.
4. **Flag to client:** If Lusen refuses to agree to expiry, Nipusk may be locked in for three years at increased fees. The client needs to understand this risk now and decide on a commercial response (e.g., offering to pay a break fee in exchange for mutual termination).

## ISSUE 2: EXCLUSIVITY SCOPE IS FAR BROADER THAN THE TERM SHEET

**Where:**
- Term Sheet §7.1: exclusivity limited to "voyage-optimization software only, for dry-bulk vessels owned by Nipusk."
- Term Sheet §7.2: "Nipusk remains free to select and use other providers for all other functions and vessels."
- Agreement §3.4: prohibits use of any "Competing Product" "on or in respect of any Vessel."
- Agreement §1.1, definition of "Competing Product": covers (a) fleet management; (b) voyage optimization, weather routing, speed and consumption planning; (c) emissions monitoring, calculation or reporting (CII, EEXI, EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime, IMO DCS); (d) hull, propeller or engine performance monitoring; (e) **crew management, crew planning or crew scheduling**.
- Agreement §1.1, definition of "Vessel": "any vessel owned, **chartered, managed or operated** by Licensee or any **Affiliate** of Licensee, wherever located in the world."

**Why it matters:**
The Term Sheet carved out a narrow exclusivity (voyage optimization, owned dry-bulk vessels only). The executed Agreement expands it to:
- **All functions** listed in the Competing Product definition (including crew management and fleet management, which are unrelated to voyage optimization);
- **All vessels** in the Nipusk group (not just owned dry-bulk, but also chartered, managed, or operated vessels, and vessels of Affiliates);
- **All Affiliates** (via §3.4's "shall procure that its Affiliates do not" language).

If Nipusk or any Affiliate is currently using, or plans to use, a crew management system, a separate emissions reporting tool, or a fleet management platform on any vessel (including a time-chartered vessel), it may be in breach of §3.4. The breach would persist for the duration of the Term (including any Renewal Term if Issue 1 is not resolved).

**Recommended fix:**
1. **Conduct an immediate internal audit** to identify any Competing Products currently in use across Nipusk and its Affiliates, on any vessel type.
2. If any breach exists, assess exposure (Lusen could claim termination for material breach under §12.3, or damages up to the §11.4 cap).
3. In the transition/renewal negotiation with Lusen, seek a **written confirmation** that Lusen waives any claim of past breach of §3.4, or an amendment narrowing §3.4 to match the Term Sheet scope (voyage optimization only, Nipusk-owned dry-bulk vessels only).
4. If the Agreement does renew (Issue 1 not resolved), this becomes a more pressing compliance matter.

## ISSUE 3: LICENSOR'S UNILATERAL ASSIGNMENT RIGHT CONTRADICTS THE TERM SHEET

**Where:**
- Term Sheet §10.3: "neither party may assign the Definitive Agreement without the other party's prior written consent."
- Agreement §16.2: mutual consent required for assignment (consistent with Term Sheet).
- Agreement §16.3: **carve-out** allowing Licensor to assign "in whole or in part, together with its rights in and to Vessel Data under Schedule 2, to any Permitted Transferee **without the consent of or notice to Licensee**."
- Agreement §1.1, "Permitted Transferee": "any person, including any shipowner, ship operator or charterer, that acquires (by merger, purchase of equity or assets, or otherwise) all or any part of Licensor's business or assets, and any Affiliate of Licensor."

**Why it matters:**
§16.3 effectively nullifies the mutual-consent protection in the Term Sheet. Lusen can sell the Agreement (and Nipusk's Vessel Data rights) to:
- A competitor shipowner or operator (expressly named in the definition);
- Any Affiliate of Lusen;
- Any acquirer of "any part" of Lusen's business or assets (not just all of it).

No notice to Nipusk is required. Nipusk's sensitive operational data (AIS positions, fuel consumption, charter-party particulars – see Schedule 2 ¶1) could end up in the hands of a commercial competitor without Nipusk knowing.

**Recommended fix:**
1. In the transition negotiation, seek Lusen's **written confirmation** that no assignment under §16.3 has occurred or is contemplated.
2. If the Agreement continues (Issue 1), press for an amendment deleting or narrowing §16.3 (e.g., requiring at least notice to Licensee, or limiting Permitted Transferees to acquirers of Lusen's *entire* business, excluding competitors).
3. Review Schedule 2 ¶3 (License to Licensor) and ¶4 (Aggregated Data) – these data rights transfer with the Agreement under §16.3. Confirm with Lusen what aggregated data sets have been created and whether any have been shared with third parties.

## ISSUE 4: SPLIT GOVERNING LAW AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION FOR VESSEL DATA

**Where:**
- Term Sheet §13: all disputes under Delaware law, Wilmington courts.
- Agreement §15.1: Delaware law, Wilmington courts (for the Agreement generally).
- Schedule 2 ¶9: Vessel Data disputes governed by **English law**, referred to **arbitration in London** (LMAA, three arbitrators).
- Agreement §1.3: "If there is any conflict or inconsistency between the main body of this Agreement and any Schedule, the Schedule prevails over the main body."

**Why it matters:**
Any dispute about Vessel Data (ownership, security, return, deletion, the license in ¶3, aggregated data in ¶4) will be litigated under English law in London arbitration, not in Delaware. This:
- Increases cost and complexity (three-arbitrator LMAA panel);
- Creates a risk of inconsistent outcomes if a single dispute touches both data issues and general contract issues;
- Was not flagged in the Term Sheet, so Nipusk may not have appreciated it when signing.

**Recommended fix:**
1. For the immediate transition: ensure the data return request (Non-Renewal Letter ¶3.2) is framed as a contractual obligation under Schedule 2 ¶8, not as a "dispute," to avoid triggering the arbitration clause.
2. If Lusen resists or delays data return, be prepared for a London arbitration. Identify counsel with LMAA experience now.
3. If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), consider negotiating a unified dispute resolution mechanism.

## ISSUE 5: FINAL SCHEDULE 3 OMITS PROTECTIONS FROM LUSEN'S OWN PRIOR DRAFT

**Where:**
- Lusen's prior draft Schedule 3 (circulated 15 November 2023) vs. final Schedule 3 in the executed Agreement.

| Provision in Prior Draft | Final Schedule 3 | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| ¶1.3: Definitions of "Availability," "Excluded Downtime," "Incident," "Severity" | Absent | Ambiguity in measuring compliance; "materially functional" standard in draft definition of Availability is gone |
| ¶2.2: Automated monitoring, 12-month record retention, availability of records to Licensee on request | Absent | Nipusk has no right to audit Lusen's monitoring data; disputes about availability become he-said-she-said |
| ¶3.3: Reasonable efforts to avoid interrupting routing outputs for Vessels at sea during maintenance | Absent | No specific protection for vessels at sea during scheduled maintenance windows |
| ¶4.2: Incident reporting procedure; Licensor to confirm or reclassify severity on first response | Absent | No mechanism to challenge Lusen's severity classification, which affects response/resolution targets |
| ¶5.1: Response time runs from receipt through a support channel | Absent | Ambiguity about when the clock starts |
| ¶5.2: Severity 1 progress updates at no more than 2-hour intervals | Absent | No obligation to keep Nipusk informed during a critical incident (vessel at sea with no routing) |
| ¶8.2: Quarterly review meetings at either party's request | Absent | No structured forum to raise recurring issues before they become disputes |

**Why it matters:**
These omissions systematically reduce Nipusk's visibility and control over service performance. The most operationally significant are the loss of the 2-hour update obligation during Severity 1 incidents (a vessel at sea without routing output) and the loss of the monitoring records audit right (which would be critical if Nipusk needed to claim service credits or establish chronic failure).

**Recommended fix:**
1. If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), this is a priority renegotiation item. The prior draft represents Lusen's own position, so there is a strong argument that these provisions should be restored.
2. Even in the transition context, if Nipusk is claiming service credits for the remainder of the Initial Term, the absence of monitoring records is a practical problem. Request Lusen's availability data for the period to date under the general cooperation obligation in §4.1 (though that is a Licensee obligation, not a Licensor one – so this may not work). Alternatively, frame it as necessary for Nipusk to assess its rights under Schedule 3 ¶7 (Chronic Failure).
3. Document the discrepancy now (this memo serves that purpose) in case it becomes relevant to any dispute about what was agreed.

## ISSUE 6: SERVICE CREDITS ARE INADEQUATE REMEDY

**Where:**
- Schedule 3 ¶5: Service credit is a percentage of **one-twelfth of the annual license fee** (per the Agreement, this is per-vessel: $18,500/12 ≈ $1,542 per vessel per month).
- Maximum credit: 20% of that amount ≈ **$308 per vessel per month** for total outage.
- Schedule 3 ¶6: Credit must be claimed within 30 days; capped at 20% of one-twelfth of annual fee.

**Why it matters:**
For a bulk carrier, a day without voyage optimization can cost significantly more than $308 in fuel inefficiency, late arrival penalties, or charter hire losses. The service credit regime is a token remedy. The real protection is the Chronic Failure termination right (¶7), but that requires three months below 98% in a rolling 12-month period – a high threshold.

Additionally, §11.3 excludes "loss of profits, revenue or charter hire" from damages, and §11.4 caps liability at fees paid/payable in the 12-month period. So if the software fails and Nipusk suffers charter hire loss, it has no remedy beyond the service credit.

**Recommended fix:**
1. For the current term: ensure Nipusk is actively monitoring availability and claiming service credits within the 30-day window (Schedule 3 ¶6). If Lusen is not providing the monthly service reports required by Schedule 3 ¶8, put them on notice.
2. If the Agreement renews: negotiate (a) higher service credit percentages or a different baseline (e.g., percentage of total annual fees rather than one-twelfth); (b) a carve-out from §11.3 for losses directly caused by software unavailability during a vessel's voyage; (c) restoration of the monitoring records right (Issue 5) so Nipusk can actually verify availability.

## ISSUE 7: DATA RETURN TIMELINE AND FORMAT

**Where:**
- Schedule 2 ¶8: Within 30 days after expiry/termination, Licensor shall return Vessel Data "in a commonly used machine-readable format" and then delete it.
- Non-Renewal Letter ¶3.2: Nipusk elected return (not just deletion) and requested confirmation of format and timetable by **6 November 2026**.
- Agreement §12.5(c): cross-references Schedule 2 for return/deletion.

**Why it matters:**
The 30-day post-termination window means Lusen has until approximately **30 January 2027** to return data. For a shipping company transitioning to a new platform, this is tight. Nipusk needs the data (AIS history, fuel consumption, performance baselines, charter-party particulars) to configure its replacement system. The "commonly used machine-readable format" standard is vague – Lusen could argue that a proprietary export meets it.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Track the 6 November 2026 deadline for Lusen's confirmation. If Lusen does not respond, escalate promptly.
2. Specify the format Nipusk requires (e.g., CSV for tabular data, JSON for structured data, original file formats for documents) in the response to Lusen's confirmation, so there is no later dispute.
3. Request that Lusen provide a **data inventory** (categories of data held, volume, format) as part of its confirmation, so Nipusk can verify completeness of the return.
4. Confirm that "Vessel Data" as defined in Schedule 2 ¶1 includes "all reports, analyses and outputs derived from" the raw data – Nipusk should receive these derived outputs as well, not just the raw inputs.

## ISSUE 8: FEES AND INVOICING AT EXPIRY

**Where:**
- Schedule 1 ¶3: Annual license fee invoiced "annually in advance on the Effective Date and each anniversary of it."
- Non-Renewal Letter ¶3.3: Requests confirmation that no further invoices will be raised after 31 December 2026.
- Agreement §12.5(b): Licensee shall pay all Fees "accrued up to the date of expiry or termination."

**Why it matters:**
The fee was invoiced in advance on 1 January 2026 (the third anniversary) for the period 1 January 2026 – 31 December 2026. If the Agreement expires on 31 December 2026, the full year's fee has been paid and no refund should be due. However, if there is any ambiguity about whether the invoice covered a period extending beyond 31 December 2026 (e.g., if it was invoiced as a "contract year" that Lusen interprets differently), Nipusk should confirm in writing that the 2026 invoice is the final one.

If the Agreement renews (Issue 1), the next invoice would be due 1 January 2027 at the increased rate (7% uplift under Schedule 1 ¶5). Nipusk should be prepared to dispute that invoice if it believes the renewal is invalid.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Await Lusen's confirmation per the letter (¶3.3). If not received by a reasonable date, follow up.
2. If the Agreement renews against Nipusk's intention, be prepared to invoke §5.4 (Disputed Invoices) to withhold the 2027 invoice pending resolution of the renewal dispute.

## ISSUE 9: FEEDBACK CLAUSE IS OVERLY BROAD

**Where:**
- Agreement §8.2: "Licensor may use any suggestions or feedback that Licensee provides about the Software without restriction or obligation."

**Why it matters:**
In the course of a three-year relationship, Nipusk's personnel (including fleet operations staff) will inevitably provide feedback to Lusen's support team. That feedback may incorporate Nipusk's proprietary operational knowledge (e.g., specific routing preferences, fuel consumption patterns, port approach procedures). Under §8.2, Lusen can use that feedback "without restriction" – potentially to improve its product for Nipusk's competitors.

This is compounded by §16.3: if Lusen assigns the Agreement to a Permitted Transferee (e.g., a competitor shipowner), that transferee inherits the benefit of §8.2.

**Recommended fix:**
1. Issue an internal reminder to Nipusk staff that all communications with Lusen support should be treated as potentially becoming Lusen's unrestricted IP. Avoid sharing proprietary operational data in feedback channels.
2. In any ongoing or renewed relationship, negotiate a carve-out: feedback should not include Vessel Data or Confidential Information (as defined in §10.1), and Lusen's use of feedback should be subject to the confidentiality obligations in §10.
