## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Appears to Be Late Under the Agreement

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §12.2 | Non-renewal notice required "at least ninety (90) days before the end of the then-current term" |
| Agreement | §1.3, §17.4 | "This Agreement supersedes the Term Sheet" |
| Term Sheet | §5.2 | Notice of non-renewal "at least thirty (30) days before the end of the then-current term"; renewal terms are "one-year" |
| Non-Renewal Letter | §2.1 | Cites Term Sheet §5.2 as the basis; states notice is given "more than thirty (30) days before December 31, 2026" |

**Why it matters:**

The Initial Term ends 31 December 2026 (Agreement §12.1). Ninety days before that date is **2 October 2026**. The non-renewal letter is dated **20 October 2026** – 72 days before expiry – and is therefore 18 days short of the 90-day requirement in Agreement §12.2.

The letter expressly relies on the Term Sheet's 30-day period (§2.1), but the Agreement states twice that it supersedes the Term Sheet (§1.3, §17.4). Nipusk executed the Agreement, so its 90-day notice period and three-year renewal term govern. If Lusen disputes the effectiveness of the notice, the Agreement would auto-renew for a three-year Renewal Term beginning 1 January 2027, with fees increased by 7% (Schedule 1, ¶5).

Additionally, the Term Sheet contemplated **one-year** renewal terms, whereas the Agreement provides for **three-year** renewal terms. Even if the notice were timely under a 30-day standard, Nipusk would be locked into three years rather than one.

**Recommended fix:**

1. **Immediately** write to Lusen (ideally today) drawing its attention to the notice and requesting written confirmation that Lusen accepts the non-renewal as effective, notwithstanding the 90-day period. Frame it as a request for confirmation, not a concession.
2. Preserve the argument that the parties' course of dealing and the Term Sheet reflect a mutual understanding of a 30-day window, and that enforcing the 90-day period would be inequitable. (This is a weaker argument given the express supersession clauses, but it is worth raising.)
3. If Lusen refuses to confirm, assess whether to (a) negotiate a mutual agreement to treat the notice as valid, or (b) prepare for the possibility of a Renewal Term and begin planning an exit strategy (e.g., termination for chronic service-level failure under Schedule 3 ¶7, or negotiation of early termination).
4. Going forward, ensure the client's contract management system flags renewal notice deadlines based on the **executed Agreement**, not the Term Sheet.

**Flag:** I do not have evidence of when the letter was actually received by Lusen (only that it was sent by courier and email on 20 October). If there is any question of when it was received, that could affect the analysis, though it would not cure the 18-day shortfall.

## Issue 2: Exclusivity Obligation Is Far Broader Than the Term Sheet

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Term Sheet | §7.1 | Exclusivity limited to "voyage-optimization software only, for dry-bulk vessels owned by Nipusk" |
| Term Sheet | §7.2 | "Nipusk remains free to select and use other providers for all other functions and vessels" |
| Agreement | §3.4 | "Licensee shall not… license, procure, develop, deploy or use any Competing Product on or in respect of any Vessel" |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Competing Product") | Covers: (a) fleet management; (b) voyage optimization/weather routing/speed and consumption planning; (c) emissions monitoring/calculation/reporting (CII, EEXI, EU ETS, FuelEU Maritime, IMO DCS); (d) hull/propeller/engine performance monitoring; (e) crew management/planning/scheduling |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Vessel") | "any vessel owned, **chartered, managed or operated** by Licensee or **any Affiliate** of Licensee, wherever located in the world" |

**Why it matters:**

The Term Sheet drew a clear line: exclusivity applies to voyage-optimization software only, and only for Nipusk-owned dry-bulk vessels. The Agreement expands the restriction in at least four dimensions:

- **Function scope:** From "voyage-optimization software only" to five categories including fleet management, emissions compliance, performance monitoring, and crew management.
- **Vessel scope:** From "dry-bulk vessels owned by Nipusk" to any vessel "owned, chartered, managed or operated" by Nipusk or its Affiliates, "wherever located in the world."
- **Entity scope:** From Nipusk alone to Nipusk and its Affiliates.
- **Activity scope:** Includes "develop" – Nipusk cannot even build its own internal tool that falls within the Competing Product definition.

For a bulk shipping company that may charter vessels, operate through affiliates, or use separate systems for crewing or emissions compliance, this is a significant operational constraint. A breach of §3.4 would constitute a material breach entitling Lusen to terminate under §12.3, and could expose Nipusk to damages (subject to the cap in §11.4).

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the non-renewal is successful (see Issue 1), this issue becomes less pressing but still relevant for the remainder of the Initial Term (through 31 December 2026). Confirm with the client that it is not currently in breach – i.e., it is not using or developing any Competing Product on any Vessel (including chartered or affiliate vessels).
2. If the Agreement renews (worst case), make narrowing §3.4 a top priority in any renegotiation: revert to voyage-optimization only, limit to Nipusk-owned vessels, and delete "develop."
3. Document internally which systems Nipusk currently uses for crew management, emissions reporting, and fleet management to identify any potential existing breaches.

## Issue 3: Lusen Has a Unilateral Assignment Right Not in the Term Sheet

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Term Sheet | §10.3 | "neither party may assign the Definitive Agreement without the other party's prior written consent" |
| Agreement | §16.2 | General rule: no assignment without prior written consent |
| Agreement | §16.3 | "Licensor may assign, transfer or novate this Agreement, in whole or in part, together with its rights in and to Vessel Data under Schedule 2, to any Permitted Transferee **without the consent of or notice to Licensee**" |
| Agreement | §1.1 ("Permitted Transferee") | "any person… that acquires (by merger, purchase of equity or assets, or otherwise) all or any part of Licensor's business or assets, and any Affiliate of Licensor" |

**Why it matters:**

The Term Sheet provided for mutual consent to assignment. The Agreement carves out a broad unilateral right for Lusen to assign to any Permitted Transferee – including any Affiliate of Lusen – without Nipusk's consent or even notice. This means:

- Lusen could sell the contract (and Nipusk's Vessel Data rights) to a competitor of Nipusk, to a financially weaker entity, or to an Affiliate with different security practices, and Nipusk would have no say.
- The assignment includes "its rights in and to Vessel Data under Schedule 2," so the transferee steps into Lusen's shoes regarding Nipusk's operational data.
- Nipusk would only find out after the fact (if at all, since no notice is required).

**Recommended fix:**

1. If the Agreement renews, negotiate deletion of §16.3 or at minimum require (a) prior written notice to Nipusk, (b) Nipusk's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld), and (c) the transferee to assume Lusen's obligations in writing.
2. For the current term, send a letter to Lusen requesting confirmation that no assignment has taken place and that Lusen remains the counterparty. This creates a paper trail.
3. Note that §16.3 does not appear to be limited to assignments in connection with a sale of the entire business – "any part" of Lusen's business or assets suffices. This is unusually broad.

## Issue 4: Final Schedule 3 Dropped Several Protections From Lusen's Own Prior Draft

**Where it appears:**

| Protection | Prior Draft (Nov 2023) | Final Schedule 3 (Agreement) |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring records | Draft §2.2: Licensor to use automated monitoring, keep records ≥12 months, make available to Licensee on request | **Absent** |
| Maintenance and vessels at sea | Draft §3.3: "reasonable efforts to schedule maintenance so that routing outputs for Vessels at sea are not interrupted" | **Absent** |
| Severity reclassification | Draft §4.2: Licensor to "confirm or reasonably reclassify the Severity on first response" | **Absent** |
| Severity 1 progress updates | Draft §5.2: "keep Licensee informed of progress on Severity 1 Incidents at intervals of no more than two (2) hours" | **Absent** |
| Quarterly review meetings | Draft §8.2: "the parties shall meet each quarter to review the monthly service reports and any recurring Incidents" | **Absent** |
| Definition of "Availability" and "Excluded Downtime" | Draft §1.3: express definitions | **Absent** (final §1 uses inline language that is broadly similar but less precise) |

**Why it matters:**

These were not Nipusk's proposals – they were in **Lusen's own draft** circulated on 15 November 2023. Their absence from the final Schedule 3 means:

- Nipusk has no contractual right to access Lusen's monitoring records, making it difficult to verify availability claims or support service credit disputes.
- There is no obligation to protect vessels at sea from maintenance interruptions, despite the fact that the Severity 1 definition specifically contemplates "Vessels at sea."
- No mandatory progress updates during Severity 1 incidents (software down for a vessel at sea) – Nipusk could be left in the dark for hours.
- No quarterly review mechanism to address recurring problems before they escalate.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If renegotiating (e.g., if the Agreement renews), restore these provisions. They were Lusen's own drafting, so there is no basis for resisting them.
2. For the current term, Nipusk should implement internal monitoring (e.g., its own uptime checks, incident logging) to create an independent record it can use in any service credit claim or dispute.
3. Note that the final Schedule 3 does retain the service credit regime, the chronic-failure termination right, and the monthly reporting obligation – so the core commercial protections are in place. The gaps are in process and transparency.

## Issue 5: Split Governing Law and Dispute Resolution for Vessel Data

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §15.1 | Delaware law; exclusive jurisdiction of Wilmington, Delaware courts |
| Schedule 2 | ¶9 | "governed by… the laws of England and Wales"; disputes "referred to arbitration in London… before a tribunal of three arbitrators" (LMAA terms) |

**Why it matters:**

Any dispute involving Vessel Data – including a data breach, a dispute over the scope of the aggregated data right (Schedule 2 ¶4), or a disagreement about return/deletion on termination – is governed by English law and resolved by three-arbitrator LMAA arbitration in London. All other disputes go to Delaware courts.

This creates:
- **Cost:** Three-arbitrator LMAA arbitration is significantly more expensive than Delaware litigation, particularly for disputes that may not involve huge sums.
- **Complexity:** A single factual scenario (e.g., a data breach that also constitutes a service-level failure) could implicate both regimes, raising threshold questions about which forum has jurisdiction.
- **Inconsistency risk:** English and Delaware law may interpret similar concepts (e.g., "material breach," "reasonableness") differently.

The Term Sheet (§13) provided for a single Delaware forum for all disputes. This split was introduced in the definitive Agreement without apparent discussion in the Term Sheet.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If renegotiating, seek to unify the governing law and forum. At minimum, argue for single-arbitrator LMAA arbitration (rather than three) to reduce cost, or for the Vessel Data provisions to fall under the same Delaware regime as the rest of the Agreement.
2. For the current term, be aware that any Vessel Data dispute (including the post-termination return/deletion process) will be subject to London arbitration. Budget accordingly if a dispute arises.

## Issue 6: Aggregated Data Right May Survive Termination

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule 2 | ¶3 | License to Lusen to use Vessel Data is expressly limited to "during the Term" |
| Schedule 2 | ¶4 | "Licensor may create aggregated and de-identified data sets derived from Vessel Data… and may use them to improve its products and services." **No temporal limitation stated.** |

**Why it matters:**

Paragraph 3 expressly limits Lusen's license to raw Vessel Data to the Term. Paragraph 4, by contrast, contains no such limitation. On a straightforward reading, Lusen could argue that aggregated data sets created during the Term can be used **indefinitely** after the Agreement ends. In a shipping context where a limited number of large dry-bulk operators exist, "de-identified" data sets may still be commercially sensitive or attributable.

**Recommended fix:**

1. If renegotiating, add "during the Term" or "for a period of [X] years after the Term" to Schedule 2 ¶4, and tighten the de-identification standard (e.g., "data that cannot reasonably be re-identified to Licensee, any Vessel, or any charterer using available information").
2. For the current term, in the non-renewal/transition correspondence, request written confirmation from Lusen that its use of aggregated data will cease upon expiry (or clarify the scope of any continuing right).

## Issue 7: Limited Warranty Remedy Combined with Liability Cap

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §9.2 | Remedy for software non-conformity: "commercially reasonable efforts to correct" or termination of affected module + refund of prepaid fees |
| Agreement | §11.3 | Mutual exclusion of indirect, consequential, special, punitive damages, loss of profits, revenue, or charter hire |
| Agreement | §11.4 | Liability cap: Fees paid and payable in the relevant 12-month period |

**Why it matters:**

If the Software fails to perform materially in accordance with the Documentation, Nipusk's remedy is limited to fixing the problem or getting a refund of the module fee. Nipusk cannot recover:
- Lost charter hire or revenue caused by the failure (§11.3 expressly excludes "charter hire")
- Costs of alternative routing, demurrage, or other operational consequences (§11.3)
- Any amount above the 12-month fee cap (§11.4)

For a vessel at sea relying on routing outputs, a software failure could have significant financial consequences that are entirely unrecoverable. The cap (12 months of fees) may be modest depending on fleet size – at US$18,500 per vessel, a 20-vessel fleet yields a cap of approximately US$370,000.

**Recommended fix:**

1. This is a standard (if licensee-unfriendly) risk allocation for software licenses. If renegotiating, seek to (a) carve out data breach and confidentiality from the indirect loss exclusion, and (b) raise the cap for Licensor's obligations (e.g., 2× or 3× annual fees).
2. For the current term, ensure the client's operational risk management (insurance, master's discretion under §9.3) accounts for the fact that the software provider bears no liability for consequential losses.
3. Note that §9.3 ("Navigational Decisions") confirms the master remains responsible for safe navigation – this is appropriate and not an issue, but it reinforces that Nipusk cannot shift operational risk to Lusen.

## Issue 8: No Transition Assistance or Parallel-Running Provision

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | §12.5 | On expiry: license ends, Licensee ceases use, Fees paid, Vessel Data returned/deleted per Schedule 2 |
| Schedule 2 | ¶8 | Return/deletion within 30 days after expiry |
| Non-Renewal Letter | §3.2 | Nipusk elects return of Vessel Data; requests confirmation of format and timetable by 6 November 2026 |

**Why it matters:**

There is no provision for:
- A transition or parallel-running period during which Nipusk can access the Software while implementing a replacement.
- Lusen's obligation to provide reasonable transition assistance (e.g., data mapping, API access, knowledge transfer).
- Any extension of the license for a defined post-termination period.

On 1 January 2027, Nipusk loses access to the Software immediately. If the client has not secured a replacement by then, it faces an operational gap in voyage optimization and emissions reporting for its fleet.

**Recommended fix:**

1. In the transition correspondence (the letter already requests a timetable by 6 November 2026), also request that Lusen agree to a **transition assistance period** of 60–90 days post-expiry during which Nipusk retains read-only access to the Software and Vessel Data to facilitate migration.
2. Begin (if not already underway) the procurement process for a replacement or alternative solution well before 31 December 2026.
3. Ensure the Vessel Data return format is sufficient for import into a replacement system (request a data dictionary or schema from Lusen).

## Issue 9: Non-Renewal Letter Cites the Wrong Instrument

**Where it appears:**

| Document | Section | Provision |
|---|---|---|
| Non-Renewal Letter | §2.1 | "This notice is given in accordance with the renewal terms agreed between the parties in the term sheet dated October 16, 2023" |
| Non-Renewal Letter | §2.2 | "This notice is given more than thirty (30) days before December 31, 2026" |

**Why it matters:**

The letter anchors its validity in the Term Sheet, not the Agreement. While this may have been a deliberate tactical choice (arguing the shorter 30-day period applies), it has two problems:

1. It acknowledges the Term Sheet as the operative document for renewal, which is difficult to reconcile with the Agreement's express supersession clauses (§1.3, §17.4).
2. If Lusen challenges the notice, it can point to the letter's own words as evidence that Nipusk was relying on a 30-day period that the Agreement does not contain.

That said, citing the Term Sheet is not necessarily fatal – it can be framed as Nipusk's position that the parties' true agreement on renewal was 30 days, and the Agreement's 90-day period was a drafting error or an unagreed escalation. But the letter as drafted does not make that argument explicitly.

**Recommended fix:**

1. In any follow-up correspondence to Lusen, clarify the position: Nipusk's notice is given under §12.2 of the Agreement, and Nipusk contends that the 90-day period is not enforceable against it because (a) it represents a material departure from the Term Sheet that was not agreed, and/or (b) Lusen's conduct (if any) estops it from relying on the longer period.
2. Alternatively, if the commercial relationship permits, simply ask Lusen to confirm acceptance of the non-renewal without litigating the notice period.
