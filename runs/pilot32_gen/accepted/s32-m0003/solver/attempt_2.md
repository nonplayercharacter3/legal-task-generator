## Executive Summary

The current document set leaves Vaikiv materially exposed in three interconnected ways: (1) the survival period for Seller's regulatory and quality representations is only 90 days post-closing, which is grossly inadequate given the ongoing Class II recall and the FDA's Voluntary Action Indicated classification of the CMO; (2) the Disclosure Schedule expressly subjects recall-related indemnification to the cap and deductible, stripping away the protection that Section 9.5(b) would otherwise provide for Excluded Liabilities; and (3) the Transition Services Agreement is governed by New York law with New York exclusive jurisdiction, creating a direct conflict with the Delaware governing law and forum provisions in the Asset Purchase Agreement. Additional issues include a narrow non-compete, a low TSA liability cap, and service-end-date misalignments that leave Vaikiv without quality and supply chain support in the final three months of the transition.

## Issue 1: Survival Period for Non-Fundamental Representations Is Only 90 Days

**Where:** APA §9.1; Escrow Agreement §4.1 (definition of "Release Date")

**Explanation:** APA §9.1 provides that Seller's representations and warranties in Article IV, "other than the Fundamental Representations, including the representations and warranties in Sections 4.5, 4.6 and 4.7, shall survive the Closing until the Release Date." The Escrow Agreement defines the Release Date as "ninety (90) days after the Closing Date" (§4.1). This means the regulatory compliance rep (§4.5), the product quality rep (§4.6), and the recall rep (§4.7) all die 90 days after closing.

Given the facts in the Disclosure Schedule, this is the single most dangerous provision in the deal. The 2025 Recall is ongoing—Seller has recovered only 81% of distributed units and has not received FDA notice of termination (Disclosure Schedule 2.4(c), ¶2). The FDA classified the CMO inspection as Voluntary Action Indicated (Disclosure Schedule 4.5, ¶1), which is a serious finding that typically precedes more aggressive regulatory action if corrective measures are deemed insufficient. Additional adverse events from the recalled lots, follow-up FDA correspondence, or discovery that additional lots are affected could all surface after day 90. At that point, Vaikiv would have no contractual indemnity for breach of these representations.

The 90-day period also aligns with the escrow release: after 90 days, the $18.6M escrow is disbursed to Seller (less pending claims), removing Vaikiv's primary source of recovery.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** Vaikiv is paying $186M for a product with an active Class II recall, a VAI-classified CMO, and open FDA matters. The reps that protect Vaikiv against exactly these risks expire before the risks have had time to mature.

**Recommended fix:** Extend the survival period for §§4.5, 4.6, and 4.7 to the later of (a) 24 months after the Closing Date or (b) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations. Correspondingly, extend the Release Date in the Escrow Agreement.

**Proposed language (APA §9.1, first sentence):**

> "The representations and warranties of Seller in Sections 4.5, 4.6 and 4.7 shall survive the Closing until the later of (a) twenty-four (24) months after the Closing Date or (b) the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations. The remaining representations and warranties of Seller in Article IV, other than the Fundamental Representations, shall survive the Closing until the Release Date (as defined in the Escrow Agreement)."

**Proposed conforming change (Escrow Agreement §4.1):**

> "The 'Release Date' means the date that is twenty-four (24) months after the Closing Date, or, if that date is not a Business Day, the next Business Day."

If Seller resists 24 months as the general survival, we should at minimum carve out §§4.5–4.7 for the longer period and argue that the escrow should be split: a portion released at 90 days (covering general reps) and a portion (e.g., $10M) held until 18 or 24 months (covering regulatory/quality/recall reps).

## Issue 2: 2025 Recall Indemnification Subject to Cap and Deductible

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule 2.4(c), ¶3; APA §9.5(b)

**Explanation:** Disclosure Schedule 2.4(c), ¶3 provides:

> "Seller's indemnification of the Buyer Indemnitees under Section 9.2(c) for Losses arising out of or relating to the 2025 Recall shall be subject to the Cap and the Deductible under Section 9.5 notwithstanding Section 9.5(b)."

APA §9.5(b) otherwise provides that "the Cap and the Deductible shall not apply to Losses arising out of or relating to (i) any Excluded Liability under Section 9.2(c)." The 2025 Recall is an Excluded Liability under APA §2.4(c). So the Disclosure Schedule is using the disclosure mechanism to contractually override the APA's own limitation structure, pulling the recall back inside the cap and deductible.

The practical effect: Vaikiv's maximum recovery for the 2025 Recall is $18.6M (the Cap, which equals 10% of the Purchase Price) minus $1.395M (the Deductible), or approximately $17.2M. And because the Deductible is a true deductible (not a tipping basket—APA §9.5(a) says Seller is liable "only for Losses in excess of the Deductible"), Vaikiv absorbs the first $1.395M of recall losses.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** The recall is the largest known risk in this transaction. It is ongoing, FDA-terminated status is not yet confirmed, and only 81% of units have been recovered. If the recall expands (additional lots, additional markets, patient injury claims), losses could easily exceed $17.2M. Vaikiv would bear the excess with no recourse.

**Recommended fix:** Delete the last sentence of Disclosure Schedule 2.4(c), ¶3 in its entirety. The recall is an Excluded Liability; Section 9.5(b)(i) already provides that the Cap and Deductible do not apply to Excluded Liabilities. If Seller insists on some limitation, propose a "super-cap" of 25% of the Purchase Price ($46.5M) with no deductible, applied only to recall losses.

**Proposed language (replacement for last sentence of Disclosure Schedule 2.4(c), ¶3):**

> "Notwithstanding the foregoing, Seller's aggregate liability for Losses arising out of or relating to the 2025 Recall shall not exceed $46,500,000. The Deductible under Section 9.5(a) shall not apply to such Losses."

## Issue 3: FDA Voluntary Action Indicated Classification of CMO

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule 4.5, ¶1; APA §4.5(c)

**Explanation:** The Disclosure Schedule reveals that following the May–June 2025 inspection, the FDA classified the CMO's Raleigh facility as "Voluntary Action Indicated" by letter dated November 4, 2025. A VAI is a serious regulatory finding—more severe than a default classification—and signals that the FDA expects the facility to implement corrective actions voluntarily or face more stringent enforcement (e.g., an import alert, consent decree, or product hold).

The rep in APA §4.5(c) is qualified by "Except as set forth on Schedule 4.5," so the Form 483 and VAI are technically disclosed. However, the rep only covers written notices "since January 1, 2022." It does not represent that the CMO's corrective actions are adequate, that the FDA has accepted them, or that no further enforcement action is pending or likely. The Disclosure Schedule notes the CMO "implemented revised in-process moisture controls on July 28, 2025" but does not state whether the FDA has confirmed resolution.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** If the FDA determines the CMO's corrective actions are insufficient, it could issue a warning letter, place the facility on import alert, or require a new inspection. Any of these outcomes could disrupt supply of the Product. Because the Manufacturing Agreement is an Assigned Contract and the CMO is the sole manufacturer, a supply disruption would be existential for this single-product acquisition.

**Recommended fix:**

(a) Add a specific representation in APA §4.5 that, as of the Closing Date, (i) the FDA has not issued any further enforcement action beyond the VAI, (ii) the CMO's corrective and preventive actions have been accepted by the FDA or are under active review with no indication of rejection, and (iii) no import alert, consent decree or product hold is in effect for the CMO facility.

(b) Add a closing condition or specific indemnity for regulatory actions against the CMO facility that arise from pre-Closing conditions.

**Proposed language (new APA §4.5(d)):**

> "(d) As of the Closing Date, (i) the FDA has not issued any warning letter, import alert, consent decree or product hold with respect to the CMO's facility at which the Product is manufactured, (ii) the CMO's corrective and preventive action plan submitted in response to the Form 483 described on Schedule 4.5 has not been rejected by the FDA, and (iii) to Seller's knowledge, no further inspection or enforcement action by the FDA at such facility is pending or threatened."

## Issue 4: Governing Law and Forum Conflict Between TSA and APA

**Where:** TSA §9.3; APA §11.8–11.9

**Explanation:** TSA §9.3 provides:

> "This Agreement, and any claim, controversy or dispute arising out of or relating to this Agreement **or the Purchase Agreement**, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of New York … Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in New York County, New York for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Purchase Agreement."

The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery (APA §§11.8–11.9). The TSA's jurisdictional clause purports to sweep in disputes "relating to … the Purchase Agreement," which directly conflicts with the APA's forum selection.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** If a dispute arises that touches both agreements (e.g., Seller's failure to provide transition services constitutes a breach of the APA covenant in §6.3 or §10.2, or a TSA claim is intertwined with an APA indemnity claim), the Parties will litigate over which forum has jurisdiction. This creates delay, expense, and uncertainty. It also means Vaikiv could be hauled into New York courts for what should be a Delaware matter.

**Recommended fix:** Delete the reference to "the Purchase Agreement" from TSA §9.3 and conform the TSA's governing law and forum to the APA (Delaware law, Delaware Court of Chancery).

**Proposed language (TSA §9.3):**

> "This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law provision or rule that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction. Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, only if that court lacks subject matter jurisdiction, the Superior Court of the State of Delaware or the United States District Court for the District of Delaware) for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement."

## Issue 5: TSA Liability Cap Is Inadequate for the Risk Profile

**Where:** TSA §8.3

**Explanation:** TSA §8.3 caps each party's aggregate liability at "the total Service Fees paid and payable under this Agreement." Based on Schedule A, the total Service Fees over the 9-month term are approximately:

| Service | Monthly Fee | Duration | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-1 Regulatory | $42,000 | 9 months | $378,000 |
| A-2 Pharmacovigilance | $58,000 | 9 months | $522,000 |
| A-3 Quality/Batch Release | $47,500 | 6 months | $285,000 |
| A-4 Supply Chain | $21,000 | 6 months | $126,000 |
| A-5 Distribution | $36,500 | 9 months | $328,500 |
| A-6 Recall Support | T&M | as needed | variable |
| A-7 Data Extraction | $15,000 | 9 months | $135,000 |
| **Total (excl. A-6)** | | | **~$1.77M** |

If Seller's pharmacovigilance failure results in a missed adverse event report that triggers FDA action, or if a batch release error causes a product quality issue, Vaikiv's recovery under the TSA is limited to roughly $1.8M. That is a fraction of the potential harm.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** The TSA services include pharmacovigilance, batch release, and regulatory support—functions where a failure can have consequences far exceeding the fee amount (FDA enforcement, product recall, patient harm).

**Recommended fix:** Raise the TSA liability cap to at least $10M, or carve out regulatory and quality service failures from the cap entirely. At minimum, exclude from the cap any losses arising from Seller's breach of Section 5 (Regulatory and Quality Matters).

**Proposed language (TSA §8.3, first sentence):**

> "Except for Losses arising out of a Party's gross negligence, willful misconduct, fraud, breach of Section 5, breach of Section 6, or indemnification obligations under this Section 8 for third-party claims, neither Party's aggregate liability under this Agreement shall exceed the greater of (a) the total Service Fees paid and payable under this Agreement or (b) $10,000,000."

## Issue 6: Quality and Supply Chain Services End at 6 Months, Leaving a 3-Month Gap

**Where:** TSA Schedule A (items A-3 and A-4 end dates); TSA §7.1

**Explanation:** The overall TSA term is 9 months (TSA §7.1). However, Quality and Batch Release (A-3) and Supply Chain (A-4) both end at "Six months after the APA Closing Date." This means for months 7 through 9, Vaikiv has no contractual right to Seller's quality unit for batch release or to Seller's supply chain team for purchase orders and CMO coordination.

Given the VAI classification and the recent recall, Vaikiv's own quality organization will need time to stand up batch release capability and CMO oversight. The assumption that Vaikiv can independently manage CMO batch release and supply chain by month 6 is aggressive and unsupported by anything in the document set.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** If Vaikiv is not ready to assume batch release at month 6, it faces a gap in which no one is contractually responsible for releasing product. This could mean either (a) no product ships (revenue loss) or (b) product ships without proper quality review (regulatory and patient safety risk).

**Recommended fix:** Extend A-3 and A-4 to the full 9-month term, or add a mechanism for Vaikiv to extend those specific services beyond 6 months if its quality organization is not yet ready.

**Proposed language (TSA Schedule A, items A-3 and A-4, End Date column):**

> "End of term (i.e., nine months after the APA Closing Date), unless earlier terminated by Buyer under Section 7.2."

Additionally, add to TSA §7.1:

> "Buyer may extend the Service Period for any Service ending before the end of the term by up to 90 days by written notice to Seller given at least 30 days before the scheduled end of that Service Period."

(This sentence already exists in §7.1 but is limited to extensions "by up to 90 days"—confirm it applies to A-3 and A-4, which currently end before the general term.)

## Issue 7: Non-Competition Covenant Is Too Narrow

**Where:** APA §6.6

**Explanation:** The non-compete prohibits Seller and its Affiliates from "directly or indirectly develop[ing], market[ing] or sell[ing] in the United States any oral extended-release product containing the same active ingredient as the Product" for three years. This excludes:

- Immediate-release or other non-ER formulations of the same active ingredient
- Non-oral routes (IV, sublingual, transdermal, etc.)
- Foreign markets
- Products containing the same active ingredient in combination with other drugs

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** Taigon is a pharmaceutical company that "develops, manufactures and markets branded and specialty pharmaceutical products" (Press Release §7). It has the infrastructure to develop a different formulation of the same molecule. If Taigon launches an IR version or a combination product, it could erode Vaikiv's franchise without breaching the covenant.

**Recommended fix:** Broaden the non-compete to cover all dosage forms and routes of administration of the same active ingredient, at minimum in the United States. Ideally, add a combination-product carve-in.

**Proposed language (APA §6.6):**

> "For three years after the Closing Date, Seller shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to, directly or indirectly develop, manufacture, market or sell in the United States any product (in any dosage form or route of administration) that contains the same active ingredient as the Product, whether alone or in combination with one or other active ingredients."

## Issue 8: Press Release States Closing Has Occurred and NDA Transfer Letters "Have Been Submitted"

**Where:** Closing Press Release ¶¶2–3

**Explanation:** The draft press release is dated March 2, 2026 (the anticipated Closing Date) and states that "the closing occurred on March 2, 2026" and that "The NDA transfer letters have been submitted to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration." This is a draft for use at closing, so the timing is appropriate. However:

(a) The release states the purchase price is "$186,000,000 in cash." In fact, $18.6M of that amount is held in escrow and is not "paid" to Seller in the ordinary sense. While the total consideration is $186M, describing it all as "cash" paid at closing is slightly misleading.

(b) The release does not mention the 2025 Recall or the VAI classification. While the Disclosure Schedule is a private document and the press release need not disclose it, the forward-looking statements section (¶8) lists "regulatory developments" as a risk without specifically referencing the open FDA matters. If the release is made public, investors and the FDA will see that Vaikiv has acquired a product with an active Class II recall and a VAI-classified manufacturer, and the release's tone ("well-established product with a durable patent position") may be viewed as omitting material information.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** If Vaikiv is a public company (or plans to be), the press release could create securities law exposure if it is deemed to omit material information about known regulatory risks. Even if private, the FDA may view the public characterization as inconsistent with the known regulatory status.

**Recommended fix:**

(a) Revise the price description: "for a purchase price of $186,000,000, a portion of which is held in escrow to secure Seller's indemnification obligations."

(b) Add to the forward-looking statements risk factors: "the status of the FDA's review of the contract manufacturing facility, including any ongoing recall-related matters."

(c) **Client input needed:** Confirm whether Vaikiv is publicly reporting and whether its disclosure committee has reviewed the release.

## Issue 9: TSA Section 2.2 Carve-Out Could Limit Service Quality

**Where:** TSA §2.2

**Explanation:** TSA §2.2 requires services to be "substantially similar in nature, scope, quality and priority" to what Seller provided in the prior 12 months, but then provides: "Seller is not required to provide any Service in a manner that would require it to hire additional personnel, acquire new systems or violate Applicable Law."

The second sentence (which says this does not excuse Seller from providing Schedule A services) mitigates but does not eliminate the risk. In practice, if Seller's existing team has been reduced post-closing (e.g., employees leave, Seller divests other product lines and scales back shared services), Seller could argue it cannot meet the "substantially similar" standard without hiring. The carve-out gives Seller an easy out.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** Vaikiv is paying for transition services on the assumption that Seller will staff them at the same level. If Seller quietly reduces its pharma operations post-closing, service quality could degrade.

**Recommended fix:** Add a minimum staffing commitment or a service-level metric.

**Proposed language (add to TSA §2.2):**

> "Seller shall maintain, for the duration of the Service Period, a team dedicated to the Services with personnel whose qualifications and experience are substantially equivalent to those assigned to the Product immediately before the APA Closing Date. Seller shall not reassign such personnel to other products or business units without Buyer's prior written consent."

## Issue 10: No Representation Regarding Remaining Term of the Manufacturing Agreement

**Where:** APA §4.9; Disclosure Schedule 2.1(d), item 1

**Explanation:** APA §4.9 represents that each Assigned Contract "is valid, binding and in full force and effect" and that no party is in material breach. However, it does not represent the remaining term of the Manufacturing Agreement. The Manufacturing Agreement is dated May 1, 2020, as amended January 15, 2023. If it has a fixed term that expires within 12–18 months post-closing, Vaikiv would face a renegotiation with the CMO at a critical time (while still in transition, with a VAI-classified facility).

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** The CMO is the sole manufacturer. Losing the Manufacturing Agreement or being forced to renegotiate on unfavorable terms would be catastrophic for a single-product acquisition.

**Recommended fix:** Add a representation that the Manufacturing Agreement has a remaining term of not less than [X] years, or disclose the expiration date and add a covenant that Seller will use commercially reasonable efforts to renew it before expiration.

**Proposed language (new APA §4.9(b)):**

> "(b) The Manufacturing Agreement has a remaining term of not less than three (3) years from the Closing Date, absent early termination for cause. A true and complete copy of the Manufacturing Agreement, including all amendments, has been made available to Buyer in the virtual data room."

## Issue 11: Escrow Amount Equals the Cap—No Cushion for Multiple Claims

**Where:** APA §§2.6, 9.5(a); Escrow Agreement §2.1

**Explanation:** The Escrow Amount is $18.6M. The Cap on Seller's indemnification for rep/warranty breaches is also $18.6M (10% of the Purchase Price). This means the escrow is sized to exactly cover the maximum recovery. If multiple claims arise (e.g., a regulatory rep breach and a quality rep breach), the first claim to be resolved depletes the escrow, and subsequent claims must be pursued against Seller directly—potentially an LLC with limited assets post-closing.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** After closing, Seller will have received $167.4M in cash. If Seller distributes that cash to its members or uses it for other purposes, collecting on a direct indemnity claim could be difficult. The escrow is Vaikiv's only secured source of recovery, and it is exactly sized to the cap with no buffer.

**Recommended fix:** Increase the escrow to 15% of the Purchase Price ($27.9M) or, alternatively, extend the escrow hold period (per Issue 1) so that claims are more likely to be resolved while funds are still available. If the escrow size cannot be increased, ensure the Release Date extension (Issue 1) gives Vaikiv adequate time to assert claims before the escrow is released.

## Issue 12: TSA Section 8.2(b)—Buyer Indemnifies Seller for Performance Per Buyer's Direction

**Where:** TSA §8.2(b)

**Explanation:** TSA §8.2(b) provides that Buyer indemnifies Seller for "Seller's performance of a Service in accordance with Buyer's written direction, except to the extent of Seller's own negligence or breach." This means if Buyer directs Seller to take a particular regulatory action (e.g., "file this supplemental application this way" or "release this batch despite the deviation"), and that direction causes a problem, Buyer bears the loss.

While this is common in TSA structures, it is risky in a regulatory context where the "direction" may be influenced by incomplete information. If Buyer's direction is based on Seller's own data or recommendations, the line between "Buyer's direction" and "Seller's negligence" becomes blurred.

**Why it matters to Vaikiv:** In the early post-closing period, Vaikiv's team may not yet have full familiarity with the Product's regulatory history. If Vaikiv gives a direction that is later found to be wrong, it could be liable to Seller for the resulting losses.

**Recommended fix:** Narrow the indemnity to apply only where Buyer's direction is specific, written, and given with full information from Seller. Add: "provided that Seller has disclosed to Buyer all information reasonably necessary for Buyer to make an informed decision."

**Proposed language (TSA §8.2(b), add at end):**

> "…provided that Seller has furnished to Buyer all data, records and information in Seller's possession that are reasonably necessary for Buyer to make an informed decision with respect to such direction."
