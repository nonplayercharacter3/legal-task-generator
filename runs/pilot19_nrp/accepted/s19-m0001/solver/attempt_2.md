## II. ISSUES BY ORDER OF IMPORTANCE

### Issue 1: Northvale Compute LLC Consent Not a Condition to Closing

**Where it appears:**
- SPA, Schedule 4.6, item 1: identifies the Northvale Compute LLC Thermal Systems Master Supply Agreement as a Material Contract, states it "requires Northvale Compute LLC's prior written consent to any change of control of the Company," and notes that "[i]f that consent is not obtained, Northvale Compute LLC may terminate the agreement on 30 days' notice." The schedule further states this agreement "represented approximately 41% of the Company's revenue for fiscal year 2025."
- SPA, Schedule 7.2(c) (Required Consents): lists only one consent — the landlord consent for the Austin, Texas facility. The Northvale Compute consent is absent.
- SPA, Section 6.5 (Third-Party Consents): requires Seller to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain consents described in Schedule 4.4 or 4.6, but expressly provides that "[o]btaining any such consent, other than the consents listed in Schedule 7.2(c), shall not be a condition to Closing."
- SPA, Section 7.2(c): conditions Closing only on delivery of "each of the consents listed in Schedule 7.2(c)."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The sale of the Shares constitutes a change of control of the Company. Without Northvale's consent, Northvale may terminate its supply agreement on 30 days' notice after Closing. At 41% of FY2025 revenue, loss of this customer would fundamentally undermine the investment thesis and likely constitute a Material Adverse Effect — but by then the deal will have closed and Buyer will have paid the full Purchase Price. The SPA as drafted allows Closing to proceed even if Northvale has not consented and has signaled its intent to terminate.

**Recommendation:**
Add the Northvale Compute consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a condition to Closing. Alternatively, if the business judgment is that the deal should close regardless (e.g., because the parties believe Northvale will consent), at minimum:
- Require delivery of the Northvale consent (or written evidence that Northvale has waived its termination right) as a Closing deliverable under Section 3.2(a); and
- Add a specific indemnity from Seller for Losses arising from Northvale's termination of its agreement post-Closing due to the change of control, outside the general cap and deductible.

**Proposed language for Schedule 7.2(c):**

> 2. Consent of Northvale Compute LLC to the change of control of the Company resulting from the sale of the Shares under the Thermal Systems Master Supply Agreement identified in item 1 of Schedule 4.6, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

### Issue 2: Survival Period for General Representations Dramatically Shorter Than LOI

**Where it appears:**
- LOI, Section 4.1: "The general representations and warranties of Seller will survive Closing for 18 months after Closing."
- SPA, Section 1.1 (definition of "Survival Date"): "the date that is sixty (60) days after delivery of the Closing Statement."
- SPA, Section 2.4(a): Buyer shall deliver the Closing Statement "within 30 days after the Closing Date."
- SPA, Section 9.1: "The representations and warranties in this Agreement survive the Closing until the Survival Date; provided that the Fundamental Representations survive until sixty (60) days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations."
- SPA, Section 9.6: No claim may be made under Section 9.2(a) unless noticed "before the Survival Date."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The Survival Date is 60 days after delivery of the Closing Statement, which is itself due within 30 days after Closing. In the typical case, the Survival Date falls approximately 90 days after Closing. This is a reduction from 18 months (the LOI term) to roughly 3 months. During this window, Buyer must discover, investigate, and give written notice of any breach of a general representation (financial statements, material contracts, product warranty, environmental, IP, litigation, compliance) or lose the right to indemnification entirely under Section 9.6. Many representation breaches — environmental liabilities, product warranty claims, undisclosed litigation — do not surface within 90 days.

**Recommendation:**
Restore the 18-month survival period for general representations and warranties as stated in the LOI.

**Proposed language for Section 1.1 (definition of "Survival Date"):**

> "**Survival Date**" means the date that is eighteen (18) months after the Closing Date.

And conforming edits to Section 9.1.

### Issue 3: Missing Tax Representation

**Where it appears:**
- LOI, Section 3.1(h): lists "taxes" among the topics for Seller's representations and warranties.
- SPA, Article IV: contains no tax representation. Section 8.1 (Pre-Closing Taxes) is a covenant allocating tax responsibility, not a representation about the accuracy of filed returns, the completeness of disclosed tax liabilities, or the absence of unasserted tax claims.

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
Without a tax representation, Buyer has no indemnification trigger (under Section 9.2(a)) for inaccuracies in the Company's tax posture. If, for example, the Company under-reported income, failed to collect sales tax, or has unaccrued transfer pricing exposures, Buyer's only recourse would be under Section 9.2(c) (pre-closing Taxes for which Seller is responsible under Section 8.1) — but that provision is limited to Taxes "for which Seller is responsible under Section 8.1," which covers periods ending on or before the Closing Date. It does not cover, for example, a position taken on a filed return that is later challenged, or a failure to file a required return. A tax representation would give Buyer a clear indemnity claim for any inaccuracy.

**Recommendation:**
Add a tax representation to Article IV, covering at minimum: (a) all Tax returns required to be filed by the Company have been filed; (b) all Taxes due have been paid; (c) the Company is not under audit or investigation; (d) no Tax has been assessed that is not reflected in the Financial Statements; and (e) the Company has no unprovided Tax liabilities.

**Proposed language (new Section 4.13):**

> **4.13 Taxes.** All Tax returns and reports required to be filed by the Company have been duly filed, and all Taxes shown to be due thereon have been paid. The Company is not under audit or investigation by any Governmental Authority with respect to any Tax matter. No Tax has been assessed, proposed or threatened against the Company that is not reflected in the Financial Statements. The Company has established on its books and records, in accordance with GAAP, reasonable reserves for all material Taxes that are or may become due.

### Issue 4: TSA Service Fees Not Populated

**Where it appears:**
- TSA, Schedule A: all four monthly service fees (ERP, Payroll, Field-Service Dispatch) are shown as "$[●]."
- LOI, Section 5.2: "Pricing will be at Seller's cost, without mark-up."
- TSA, Section 5.1: confirms fees are "equal to Provider's actual cost of providing the Services, without mark-up."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
While the cost-plus-zero methodology is agreed, the absence of dollar figures means Buyer cannot model the post-Closing cost of operations for the 9-month transition period. More importantly, without a baseline, it will be difficult to verify at invoicing that Provider is not embedding mark-up in "allocated personnel costs" or "a reasonable allocation of system and license costs" (TSA Section 5.1). The blank fees also create an ambiguity that could be exploited.

**Recommendation:**
Require Seller to populate Schedule A with specific monthly fee amounts (or, at minimum, a detailed cost build-up by category) as a condition to executing the TSA at Closing. Add a covenant that Provider will provide quarterly cost-support detail upon request.

**Proposed addition to TSA Section 5.2:**

> Provider shall, upon the Company's request no more than once per quarter, provide reasonable supporting detail for the allocation of personnel costs, system and license costs, and third-party charges included in the Service Fees for the preceding quarter.

### Issue 5: TSA Governed by Texas Law; Jurisdiction in Harris County

**Where it appears:**
- TSA, Section 12.2: "This Agreement … shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Texas … Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in Harris County, Texas … Sections 11.9 and 11.10 of the Purchase Agreement do not apply to this Agreement, and this Section 12.2 expressly provides otherwise for purposes of Section 11.4 of the Purchase Agreement."
- SPA, Section 11.9: Delaware governing law.
- SPA, Section 11.10: Exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware (Court of Chancery or Superior Court).
- SPA, Section 11.4 (Order of Precedence): "In the event of any conflict or inconsistency between this Agreement and any Ancillary Agreement, this Agreement shall prevail unless an Ancillary Agreement expressly provides otherwise."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The TSA is an Ancillary Agreement. Section 11.4 of the SPA would normally give the SPA's Delaware law and jurisdiction provisions priority, but the TSA expressly carves itself out. The result is that disputes over transition services — potentially involving the Company's ERP data, payroll records, and field-service dispatch (all operationally critical) — must be litigated in Harris County, Texas under Texas law, while all other deal disputes are in Delaware under Delaware law. This creates: (a) a risk of inconsistent legal standards across related disputes; (b) increased cost and complexity of enforcement; and (c) a tactical disadvantage for Buyer, which is a Delaware LLC, having to litigate in a forum chosen by Seller.

**Recommendation:**
Negotiate to conform the TSA's governing law and jurisdiction to the SPA (Delaware law; Court of Chancery or Superior Court). If Seller resists (perhaps because the services are performed in Texas), at minimum ensure the TSA's dispute resolution is consistent with the SPA's escalation mechanics and that Buyer can seek injunctive relief in Delaware for data-related breaches (Section 8 of the TSA).

**Proposed language for TSA Section 12.2:**

> This Agreement, and all claims, disputes and causes of action arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Services, shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law provision that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction. Each Party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts specified in Section 11.10 of the Purchase Agreement for any action arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the Services.

### Issue 6: TSA Lacks Service Level Credits or Remedies for Dispatch Performance

**Where it appears:**
- TSA, Schedule A (Service Levels): "Provider shall perform Service 3 (Field-Service Dispatch) so that customer service requests are logged and assigned to a technician within the response times that applied to the Company's customer contracts before the Closing. Provider shall report monthly to the Company on its performance against those response times."
- TSA, Section 9 (Liability): provides a general cap and exclusion of consequential damages but no specific remedy for failure to meet the dispatch service levels.

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The Company's customer contracts (including the Northvale Compute agreement) likely contain their own service-level commitments with potential penalties or termination rights. If Provider fails to meet dispatch response times, the Company could be in breach with its customers, and the Company's only recourse against Provider would be a general breach claim subject to the liability cap in Section 9.3 (total Service Fees paid and payable). There is no service-level credit mechanism, no termination right specific to chronic SLA failure, and no pass-through of customer penalties.

**Recommendation:**
Add a service-level credit provision and a specific termination right for chronic failure.

**Proposed language (new TSA Section 4.7):**

> **4.7 Service Level Credits.** If Provider fails to meet the response-time service levels set forth in Schedule A for any calendar month, the Company shall be entitled to a credit against the Service Fees for the following month equal to [●]% of the monthly Service Fee for Service 3 for each percentage point by which the monthly on-time response rate falls below [95]%. If the monthly on-time response rate falls below [85]% for two (2) consecutive months, the Company may terminate Service 3 immediately by written notice without penalty.

### Issue 8: Ostrander Freight Agreement — Rates Blank; Renewal Timing

**Where it appears:**
- Ostrander Agreement, Schedule A (Parts 1, 2, and 3): all rates, minimums, and fuel surcharge parameters are "$[●]."
- Ostrander Agreement, Section 5.5: estimated annual spend of ~$1,400,000.
- Ostrander Agreement, Section 4.1–4.2: Initial Term of 3 years from March 1, 2024 (expiring February 28, 2027); auto-renews for successive 1-year terms unless either party gives 90 days' written notice before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The Ostrander Agreement is a Material Contract (SPA Schedule 4.6, item 3) and represents a significant operational dependency for shipping finished cooling units to customer sites. The blank rates prevent us from confirming the $1.4M estimate or assessing whether the rates are market. The renewal timing is relevant: the first renewal notice deadline is approximately December 1, 2026 (90 days before February 28, 2027). If the deal has not closed by that date, the Company (still Seller-owned) could be locked into another year. Post-Closing, Buyer would inherit the agreement and the auto-renewal. This is not necessarily adverse, but Buyer should be aware of the commitment.

**Recommendation:**
- Confirm the actual rates from the executed agreement (the version in the folder appears to be a redacted or template copy).
- Calendar the December 1, 2026 renewal notice deadline and determine whether Buyer wants the Company to have the option to non-renew or to renegotiate.
- No consent from Ostrander is required for the change of control (Ostrander Agreement Section 12.3 expressly provides that a change in ownership or control is not an assignment), so this agreement does not need to be added to Schedule 7.2(c).

### Issue 10: No Employee or Benefits Representation

**Where it appears:**
- SPA, Article IV: no representation addressing employees, employment agreements, benefits plans, worker classification, or compliance with employment laws.
- LOI, Section 3.1: does not specifically call for an employee representation, but the list is described as including representations that are "customary for a transaction of this type."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
The Company operates a field-service dispatch function (TSA Schedule A, Service 3) and presumably employs technicians, engineers, and administrative staff. Post-Closing, Buyer inherits all employment liabilities. Without a representation, Buyer has no indemnity for, e.g., misclassified workers, unpaid wages, benefits plan violations, or pending employment claims.

**Recommendation:**
Add an employee and benefits representation.

**Proposed language (new Section 4.14):**

> **4.14 Employees; Benefits.** Schedule 4.14 lists all employees of the Company. The Company is in compliance in all material respects with all applicable Laws relating to employment, including Laws governing wages, hours, worker classification, occupational safety and health, and anti-discrimination. The Company maintains all employee benefit plans required by Law. There are no pending or, to Seller's knowledge, threatened claims, charges or proceedings by or on behalf of any current or former employee relating to employment, benefits or worker classification.

### Issue 11: Bill of Sale Lacks a Title Representation from Transferor

**Where it appears:**
- Bill of Sale, Section 3.1 (Transfer): conveys assets "free and clear of all Encumbrances" as part of the grant language.
- Bill of Sale: no separate section in which Transferor represents that it has good and marketable title, that the Transferred IP is valid and enforceable, or that the transfer does not violate any agreement.

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
While the SPA's Section 4.10 (IP) and Section 6.4 (Transfer of Test Assets) provide some protection, the Bill of Sale is the operative transfer document. A title representation in the Bill of Sale itself would give the Company (and Buyer as third-party beneficiary under Bill of Sale Section 8.2) a direct claim against Transferor for any defect in title, without having to route through the SPA's indemnification mechanics (with their deductible, cap, and survival limitations).

**Recommendation:**
Add a representation section to the Bill of Sale.

**Proposed language (new Bill of Sale Section 3.4):**

> **3.4 Representation of Transferor.** Transferor represents and warrants that: (a) it is the sole and exclusive owner of the Transferred Assets, free and clear of all Encumbrances; (b) it has full right, power and authority to transfer the Transferred Assets as provided herein; (c) the Transferred IP is valid and enforceable, and to Transferor's knowledge does not infringe or misappropriate the Intellectual Property of any third party; and (d) the transfer of the Transferred Assets does not violate any agreement to which Transferor is a party.

### Issue 12: Working Capital Target and Escrow — Need Client Confirmation

**Where it appears:**
- SPA, Section 1.1: "Target Working Capital" = $18,750,000.
- SPA, Section 2.2(a): Adjustment Escrow Amount = $2,000,000.
- LOI, Section 2.2: target "to be agreed on the basis of Buyer's financial due diligence."
- LOI, Section 2.3: escrow "in an amount to be agreed."

**Why it matters to Voutuv:**
We need to confirm with the client that (a) the $18.75M target reflects the output of Buyer's financial due diligence and is the number the client is comfortable with, and (b) the $2M escrow is adequate to cover the expected range of working capital adjustment. If the expected true-up range exceeds $2M, the escrow is insufficient and the buyer would be relying on Seller's credit for the excess (SPA Section 2.4(d): "to the extent the Adjustment Escrow Amount is insufficient, by Seller").

**Recommendation:**
Confirm with client. If the expected adjustment range exceeds $2M, negotiate a higher escrow or a holdback.
