### 1. Velloran's Change of Control Consent Is Missing from the MIPA's Required Consents

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 14.2 (Change of Control of Distributor deemed an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent); § 14.3 (Supplier may terminate immediately if Change of Control occurs without consent)
- MIPA § 1.15 (definition of "Required Consents" as those listed on Schedule 3.6)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (lists only Brandywine Flats Industrial LLC and Keystone Commonwealth Bank)
- MIPA § 7.1(d) (Closing condition: all Required Consents obtained)
- MIPA § 6.4 (Sellers' obligation to obtain Required Consents)
- MIPA § 3.5(c) (No Conflicts representation)

**Why It Matters:**
Section 14.2 of the Distribution Agreement provides that any Change of Control of Harrow Creek "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Distributor and requires the prior written consent of Supplier." Section 14.3 gives Velloran the right to terminate the agreement immediately upon written notice if the Change of Control occurs without that consent. The acquisition by Quillon is unambiguously a Change of Control under the definition in Distribution Agreement § 1.3(a) (sale of more than 50% of equity interests).

Velloran's consent is not listed on Schedule 3.6. Consequently:

- There is no contractual obligation on the Sellers to seek or obtain Velloran's consent (MIPA § 6.4 covers only "Required Consents listed on Schedule 3.6").
- The Closing condition in § 7.1(d) does not protect against the absence of Velloran's consent.
- The No Conflicts representation in § 3.5(c) states that the transaction does not "give any Person a right of termination… under any Material Contract," except for the Required Consents. Because Velloran's consent is not a Required Consent, the Sellers are representing that no such right exists. But § 14.3 of the Distribution Agreement gives Velloran exactly that right. **This representation is false on its face.**
- Velloran accounts for approximately 91% of the Company's cost of goods purchased (MIPA Schedule 3.13). Loss of the Distribution Agreement would be existential to the business.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Add Velloran's consent to Schedule 3.6 as a Required Consent.
- Begin the consent request to Velloran immediately, well ahead of the Target Closing Date of November 30, 2026.
- If Velloran conditions its consent on modifications (e.g., revised minimum purchase requirements, a new side letter, or a consent fee), those terms should be negotiated now and reflected in the MIPA.
- If Velloran refuses consent, the transaction as structured is not viable; this must be escalated to the client and Quillon at once.

### 2. The Distribution Agreement Renewal Notice Deadline Has Likely Passed; the Agreement May Expire Before Closing

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 3.1 (Initial Term ends November 14, 2026)
- Distribution Agreement § 3.2 (Renewal Notice must be delivered at least 90 days before expiration of the Initial Term; no automatic renewal)
- MIPA § 2.2 (Target Closing Date: November 30, 2026)
- MIPA § 1.12 (Outside Date: January 29, 2027)
- MIPA § 3.12(c) (representation that the Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029")
- MIPA Schedule 3.12 (disclosure: "The Initial Term of the Distribution Agreement ends November 14, 2026. The Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing.")

**Why It Matters:**
The 90-day renewal notice deadline is **August 16, 2026** (90 days before November 14, 2026). The MIPA was signed on **October 5, 2026**—nearly two months after the deadline. The Target Closing Date is **November 30, 2026**—16 days after the Initial Term expires.

The Schedule 3.12 disclosure states that the Company "intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing." This language strongly suggests the notice has **not yet been delivered**. If it was not delivered by August 16, 2026, the Distribution Agreement expires by its own terms on November 14, 2026, before the Closing. In that scenario:

- The § 3.12(c) representation that the Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029" is false as of the Signing Date and will remain false at Closing.
- Quillon would have a basis to terminate the MIPA under § 9.1(c) (breach of representation such that § 7.1(a) would not be satisfied) or to claim indemnification post-closing under § 8.2(a).
- The Company would be operating without its primary supply agreement for at least 16 days between expiration and Closing, with no contractual right to purchase Products.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Confirm immediately with the client whether the Renewal Notice was in fact delivered to Velloran on or before August 16, 2026. Obtain a copy of the notice and proof of delivery.
- If it was delivered, correct the Schedule 3.12 disclosure to state the delivery date and confirm that the representation in § 3.12(c) is accurate.
- If it was **not** delivered, this is a deal-threatening problem. The only potential remedy is to ask Velloran to waive the deadline or to agree to a short extension or bridge arrangement. This must be raised with Quillon's counsel promptly because it goes to the accuracy of a fundamental representation and to the viability of the transaction.
- In either case, the Outside Date of January 29, 2027 provides a narrow window: if the agreement has already expired, Quillon may be reluctant to close a company that no longer has its core supply contract.

## Additional Issues

### 3. The Side Letter's Non-Competition Binds the Company (and Thus Quillon) in Six States, While the MIPA's Seller Non-Competition Covers Only Four

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter § 1.1 (Territory defined as Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, **New York and Virginia**)
- Side Letter § 2.1 (non-compete covenant during the Restricted Period)
- Side Letter § 1.3 (Restricted Period = Term of Distribution Agreement + 18 months post-expiration/termination)
- Side Letter § 5.2 (Sections 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 survive expiration/termination)
- MIPA § 6.7 (Seller non-competition limited to "the States of Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey or Pennsylvania")

**Why It Matters:**
The Company is bound by a non-compete covering six states that survives for 18 months after any expiration or termination of the Distribution Agreement. Post-closing, Quillon owns a company that cannot distribute competing products in New York or Virginia for the duration of the Restricted Period. However, the individual Sellers (Esterly and Hollister) are restricted by the MIPA only in four states. This creates a gap: the Sellers could personally compete in New York and Virginia while the Company (Quillon's asset) cannot.

Additionally, if Velloran terminates the Distribution Agreement (for example, for an unconsented Change of Control under § 14.3), the 18-month post-termination non-compete would begin running, further constraining Quillon's operations at the worst possible time.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Flag to Quillon that the Company's non-compete is broader (6 states) than the Sellers' personal non-compete (4 states). Quillon may want to negotiate an expansion of the Seller non-compete in MIPA § 6.7 to include New York and Virginia, or a corresponding carve-out.
- Model the financial impact of the non-compete if the Distribution Agreement is terminated pre- or post-closing.

### 4. Split Governing Law and Forum Between the Side Letter and the Distribution Agreement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter § 6.1 (New York law); § 6.2 (exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, New York)
- Distribution Agreement § 18.1 (Delaware law); § 18.2 (exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware)
- Distribution Agreement § 19.4 (order of precedence: First Amendment > Side Letter > body > Exhibits)

**Why It Matters:**
A dispute with Velloran regarding the Change of Control consent or the non-compete could implicate both instruments. The Side Letter's non-compete and its remedy provisions (§ 4) are governed by New York law and must be litigated in New York, while any dispute about the Distribution Agreement itself (e.g., termination rights, minimum purchase compliance) is governed by Delaware law and litigated in Delaware. This creates the risk of parallel proceedings in two jurisdictions and complicates any negotiation with Velloran.

**Recommended Fix:**
- When requesting Velloran's consent, be prepared for Velloran to assert that any dispute about the consent or its conditions is subject to New York law (if tied to the Side Letter) or Delaware law (if tied to the Distribution Agreement).
- If a consent negotiation breaks down, assess which forum is more favorable and whether the issues can be framed to fit within a single jurisdiction.

### 5. Year 3 Minimum Purchase Requirement Is Not Yet Confirmed as Met

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 5.1 and Exhibit B, Part 2 (Year 3 minimum: $3,100,000 for the period November 15, 2025 – November 14, 2026)
- Distribution Agreement § 5.2 (remedies: conversion to non-exclusive or termination)
- First Amendment § 3.2 (Accessory Products count toward the Minimum Purchase Requirement)
- MIPA § 3.12(c) (confirms only Years 1 and 2 were met; Year 3 is not addressed)

**Why It Matters:**
Contract Year 3 runs through November 14, 2026. At the Signing Date (October 5, 2026), approximately five weeks of Year 3 remain. The MIPA representation confirms compliance only for the years ended November 14, 2024 and November 14, 2025. If the Company fails to reach $3.1M by November 14, 2026, Velloran has the right (as its sole remedy) to convert the appointment to non-exclusive or to terminate. This risk sits squarely between the Signing Date and the Target Closing Date.

The addition of the Accessories product line (First Amendment, effective June 1, 2026) at a lower discount (25% vs. 28–32%) may have compressed margins and affected purchasing patterns, making the Year 3 target harder to achieve.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Request from the client a current Year 3 purchase-to-date figure and a projection for the remaining five weeks.
- If the Company is at risk of missing the $3.1M target, consider whether a final purchase order can be placed before November 14, 2026 to close the gap.
- If the target will be missed, assess whether to disclose this to Quillon now (to avoid a post-closing indemnity claim) or to seek Velloran's waiver.

### 6. The MIPA's No Conflicts Representation (§ 3.5(c)) Is Likely False

**Documents and Sections:**
- MIPA § 3.5(c) (transaction does not "give any Person a right of termination… under any Material Contract," except for Required Consents)
- Distribution Agreement § 14.3 (Velloran's termination right upon unconsented Change of Control)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (Velloran's consent not listed)

**Why It Matters:**
As noted in Issue 1, Velloran has a contractual right to terminate the Distribution Agreement if the Change of Control occurs without its consent. Because Velloran's consent is not a Required Consent, the Sellers' representation in § 3.5(c) that no Material Contract gives any person a termination right (other than via the Required Consents) is inaccurate. If Quillon discovers this before Closing, it may assert a breach under § 9.1(c) and terminate the MIPA, or it may demand a specific indemnity or escrow increase.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Correct the disclosure schedules to identify Velloran's consent as a Required Consent (see Issue 1). Once added, the § 3.5(c) representation becomes accurate because the exception for Required Consents would cover it.
- If the schedule is not corrected before the Closing, the Sellers face personal indemnification exposure under MIPA § 8.2(a) for the inaccuracy.

### 7. Saltmarsh Warehouse Agreement: Termination for Convenience and Absence of a Change of Control Provision

**Documents and Sections:**
- Warehouse Agreement § 4.2 (either party may terminate for convenience on 60 days' notice)
- Warehouse Agreement § 11.1 (free assignability upon written notice; no consent required)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (Saltmarsh consent not listed—correctly, because none is required)

**Why It Matters:**
The Saltmarsh agreement is freely assignable (§ 11.1), so no consent is needed for the transaction—this is favorable. However, Saltmarsh may terminate for convenience on 60 days' notice (§ 4.2). Post-closing, Quillon could lose its 3PL provider on relatively short notice, forcing a disruptive transition of warehouse operations. The agreement has no change-of-control trigger, so the sale itself does not give Saltmarsh a termination right, but the convenience termination right remains a latent operational risk.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Note for the client and Quillon that the 3PL relationship is not locked in beyond 60 days. If continuity of logistics is important to the deal value, consider whether Quillon would want to negotiate a longer-term commitment with Saltmarsh post-closing.
- No action is required in the MIPA itself, but the risk should be disclosed in the deal materials if not already apparent.

### 8. Side Letter Section 3.3: Ongoing Obligation to Notify Velloran of Competing Product Inquiries

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter § 3.3 (Distributor shall promptly notify Supplier of any request from a Customer to supply Competing Products in the Territory)
- Side Letter § 1.2 (broad definition of "Competing Products")

**Why It Matters:**
This obligation continues for the Term and is binding on the Company (and thus on Quillon post-closing). The definition of "Competing Products" is broad—"substantially similar in function to, or… marketed as substitutes for" the Products. In a six-state territory. If Quillon has or acquires other product lines that could be characterized as competing, the Company has an affirmative duty to disclose customer inquiries about those products to Velloran. This could create commercial friction and, if breached, would constitute a material breach of the Distribution Agreement (Side Letter § 4.3).

**Recommended Fix:**
- Advise the client (and Quillon) of this ongoing obligation.
- If Quillon has other product lines in precision measurement or adjacent fields, assess whether they fall within the "Competing Products" definition and whether customer inquiries about them would trigger the notification duty.
