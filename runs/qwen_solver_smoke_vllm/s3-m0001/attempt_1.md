## SUMMARY

Review of the Velloran distribution agreement stack (Distribution Agreement, First Amendment, Side Letter), the Saltmarsh warehouse agreement, and the MIPA with disclosure schedules reveals several material issues. The two most urgent are: (1) Velloran's consent to the Change of Control is not listed as a Required Consent under the MIPA, and (2) the deadline for delivering the Renewal Notice under the Distribution Agreement appears to have already passed as of the MIPA signing date. Both issues threaten the continuity of the Company's most important commercial relationship (Velloran represents approximately 91% of cost of goods purchased) and create significant indemnification exposure for the Sellers.

## ISSUE 1 – VELLORAN'S CHANGE-OF-CONTROL CONSENT IS MISSING FROM SCHEDULE 3.6 (REQUIRED CONSENTS)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 14.2 (Change of Control deemed assignment; Supplier consent required)
- Distribution Agreement § 14.3 (Supplier termination right upon unconsented Change of Control)
- Distribution Agreement § 15.4 (Termination for Change of Control cross-reference)
- MIPA § 3.6 (Consents representation)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (lists only Brandywine Flats landlord consent and Keystone Commonwealth Bank consent)
- MIPA § 7.1(d) (Required Consents as closing condition)

**Why it matters:**

Section 14.2 of the Distribution Agreement provides that any Change of Control of the Distributor "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Distributor and requires the prior written consent of Supplier." Section 1.3 defines Change of Control to include the sale of more than 50% of equity interests. The MIPA contemplates a 100% sale of the Membership Interests to Quillon. This is unambiguously a Change of Control requiring Velloran's prior written consent.

Schedule 3.6 to the MIPA lists only two Required Consents: (1) the Brandywine Flats landlord consent and (2) the Keystone Commonwealth Bank consent. Velloran's consent is absent. MIPA § 3.6 represents that, except for the Required Consents, no consent of any person is required. That representation is inaccurate as a matter of the Distribution Agreement's plain terms.

Because Velloran's consent is not a Required Consent, it is not a closing condition under MIPA § 7.1(d). The deal can therefore close without Velloran's consent. If it does, Velloran may terminate the Distribution Agreement immediately under § 14.3. Given that Velloran accounts for approximately 91% of the Company's cost of goods purchased (MIPA Schedule 3.13), loss of the distribution agreement would be existential for the business.

From the Sellers' perspective, closing without Velloran's consent also creates immediate indemnification exposure. The inaccuracy of the § 3.6 representation (and the § 3.5(c) "no conflicts / no termination right" representation, discussed in Issue 3) gives Buyer a claim against the Sellers under MIPA § 8.2(a), recoverable from the $4.85M Indemnity Escrow and potentially beyond.

**Recommendation:**

- **Raise with Quillon's counsel promptly.** Velloran's consent must be added to Schedule 3.6 as a Required Consent, making it a condition to Buyer's closing obligation under § 7.1(d). Alternatively, the parties should amend the MIPA to add a specific closing condition requiring Velloran's written consent.
- Begin the consent request to Velloran immediately. The consent process should be started well before the Target Closing Date of November 30, 2026, and the Outside Date of January 29, 2027.
- If Velloran is likely to condition its consent on modifications to the Distribution Agreement (e.g., pricing, minimums, or a consent fee), those terms need to be negotiated now and reflected in the MIPA or a side agreement with Quillon.

## ISSUE 2 – RENEWAL NOTICE DEADLINE HAS LIKELY ALREADY PASSED; DISTRIBUTION AGREEMENT MAY EXPIRE BEFORE CLOSING

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 3.1 (Initial Term ends November 14, 2026)
- Distribution Agreement § 3.2 (Renewal Notice required at least 90 days before expiration of Initial Term; no automatic renewal)
- MIPA § 2.2 (Target Closing Date: November 30, 2026)
- MIPA § 3.12(c) (representation that Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029")
- MIPA Schedule 3.12, note to item 3 ("The Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing")

**Why it matters:**

The Initial Term expires on November 14, 2026. Section 3.2 requires the Distributor to deliver a Renewal Notice "at least ninety (90) days prior to the expiration of the Initial Term." Ninety days before November 14, 2026 is **August 16, 2026**. The MIPA was signed on **October 5, 2026** — nearly two months after the Renewal Notice deadline.

The Schedule 3.12 note states the Company "intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing," but the Target Closing Date is November 30, 2026, which is 16 days *after* the Initial Term expires. If the Renewal Notice was not delivered by August 16, 2026, the Distribution Agreement expired on November 14, 2026, and the Company will have no distribution agreement with Velloran at Closing.

MIPA § 3.12(c) represents that the Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029." That representation is true only if the Renewal Notice was timely delivered. If it was not, the representation is false as of the Signing Date (October 5, 2026), giving Buyer a potential indemnification claim and potentially a basis to walk from the deal under § 7.1(a) or § 9.1(c).

We need to confirm with the client whether the Renewal Notice was in fact delivered by August 16, 2026. The Schedule 3.12 language ("intends to deliver") suggests it had not been delivered as of the Signing Date, which would be a serious problem.

**Recommendation:**

- **Confirm immediately with the client** whether a Renewal Notice was delivered to Velloran on or before August 16, 2026, and obtain a copy.
- If the notice was NOT delivered: (a) the representation in § 3.12(c) is inaccurate and must be disclosed to Quillon; (b) the Company should request that Velloran accept a late renewal notice or negotiate a new agreement; (c) the MIPA may need to be amended to address the gap between the expiration of the Initial Term and the Closing Date; and (d) the Outside Date may need to be extended if a new agreement must be negotiated.
- If the notice WAS delivered: obtain and preserve the evidence (delivery confirmation) and ensure it is reflected in the disclosure schedules or closing deliverables.

## ISSUE 3 – MIPA SECTION 3.5(c) "NO CONFLICTS / NO TERMINATION RIGHT" REPRESENTATION IS LIKELY INACCURATE

**Documents and Sections:**
- MIPA § 3.5(c) (no conflict, breach, default, or termination right under any Material Contract, except for Required Consents)
- Distribution Agreement § 14.3 (Velloran termination right upon unconsented Change of Control)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (Velloran consent not listed)

**Why it matters:**

Section 3.5(c) represents that the transaction does not "give any Person a right of termination, acceleration or modification under, any Material Contract," except for the Required Consents. The Distribution Agreement is a Material Contract (Schedule 3.12, item 1). Section 14.3 of the Distribution Agreement gives Velloran an express right to terminate if a Change of Control occurs without its consent. Because Velloran's consent is not a Required Consent, the carve-out in § 3.5(c) does not protect this representation.

This is a separate and independent inaccuracy from the § 3.6 consent representation (Issue 1). Even if the parties were to add Velloran's consent to Schedule 3.6 (fixing § 3.6), the § 3.5(c) representation would still be problematic unless the consent is also added to the § 3.5(c) carve-out.

**Recommendation:**

- Amend MIPA § 3.5(c) to carve out Velloran's consent, or add Velloran's consent to Schedule 3.6 (which would bring it within the existing "except for the Required Consents" language in § 3.5(c)).
- This should be addressed in the same amendment that fixes Schedule 3.6.

## ISSUE 4 – SIDE LETTER NON-COMPETE IMPOSES SIGNIFICANT POST-CLOSING RESTRICTIONS ON QUILLON

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter § 1.1 (Territory expanded to six states: DE, MD, NJ, PA, NY, VA)
- Side Letter § 1.2 (Competing Products defined broadly)
- Side Letter § 1.3 (Restricted Period: Term + 18 months post-termination)
- Side Letter § 2.1 (covenant not to market, sell, distribute Competing Products)
- Side Letter § 2.2 (indirect activities and Affiliate restriction)
- Side Letter § 4.2 (tolling for breach)
- Side Letter § 4.3 (breach = material breach of Distribution Agreement)
- Distribution Agreement § 1.12 (Territory limited to four states: DE, MD, NJ, PA)

**Why it matters:**

The Side Letter binds the "Distributor" — which post-closing will be the Quillon-owned entity. The non-compete prohibits Harrow Creek (and its Affiliates, which post-closing would include Quillon) from distributing Competing Products in a six-state territory for the duration of the Distribution Agreement Term plus 18 months.

Key concerns for the client to raise with Quillon:

1. **Territory mismatch.** The exclusive distribution right covers only four states (DE, MD, NJ, PA) under Distribution Agreement § 1.12, but the non-compete covers six states (adding NY and VA) under Side Letter § 1.1. Harrow Creek is restricted from competing in New York and Virginia without receiving any exclusive distribution rights there. This is an asymmetry that reduces the value of the exclusive appointment.

2. **Breadth of "Competing Products."** Section 1.2 defines Competing Products as "any precision measurement instruments, laboratory balances, calibration standards, or related accessories or consumables that are substantially similar in function to, or are marketed as substitutes for, any of the Products." This is very broad and could capture a wide range of Quillon's other product lines or future acquisitions.

3. **Affiliate restriction.** Section 2.1 extends the restriction to Affiliates. Post-closing, Quillon is an Affiliate of Harrow Creek. If Quillon has other subsidiaries or portfolio companies that sell competing products in the six-state territory, the Side Letter could be violated.

4. **18-month tail and tolling.** Even if the Distribution Agreement is terminated, the non-compete continues for 18 months. If there is a breach, the tail is tolled (extended) by the duration of the breach (§ 4.2).

5. **Governing law conflict.** The Side Letter is governed by New York law and subject to New York exclusive jurisdiction (§ 6.1–6.2), while the Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law (§ 18.1). This creates a potential conflict in how the non-compete is interpreted and enforced, particularly given that New York and Delaware have different approaches to the enforceability of non-competes in the commercial context.

**Recommendation:**

- **Raise with the client and Quillon.** Quillon needs to understand that, post-closing, it will be restricted from using Harrow Creek (or any Affiliate) to distribute competing products in six states for the Term plus 18 months. If Quillon's business plan involves expanding Harrow Creek's product lines or integrating it with other portfolio companies, the Side Letter may be a significant constraint.
- Consider whether to request a modification of the Side Letter as a condition of Velloran's consent to the Change of Control (e.g., narrowing the Territory to match the Distribution Agreement's four-state territory, or narrowing the Competing Products definition).
- Confirm that Quillon has no existing business in the six-state territory that would constitute a Competing Product.

## ISSUE 5 – YEAR 3 MINIMUM PURCHASE REQUIREMENT AT RISK

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 5.1 (Minimum Purchase Requirement each contract year)
- Distribution Agreement § 5.2 (Supplier's remedy: convert to non-exclusive or terminate)
- Distribution Agreement Exhibit B, Part 2 (Year 3 minimum: $3,100,000 for Nov. 15, 2025 – Nov. 14, 2026)
- MIPA § 3.12(c) (represents only that Years 1 and 2 minimums were met)

**Why it matters:**

Contract Year 3 (November 15, 2025 – November 14, 2026) requires minimum net purchases of $3,100,000. The MIPA was signed on October 5, 2026, with approximately five weeks remaining in Year 3. The MIPA represents only that Years 1 and 2 minimums were met (§ 3.12(c)); it does not represent that Year 3 will be met.

If the Company fails to meet the Year 3 minimum, Velloran may (a) convert the exclusive appointment to non-exclusive for the remainder of the Term, or (b) terminate the agreement (§ 5.2). Either outcome would materially impair the value of the business at or shortly after Closing.

Additionally, if the Renewal Term is in effect, the minimum for the first year of the Renewal Term would be 105% of Year 3 = $3,255,000 (Exhibit B, Part 2, last row).

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client's controller current Year 3 purchase volumes to date and projected Year 3 total. If the Company is on track to meet $3,100,000, document that for the file.
- If the Company is at risk of missing the Year 3 minimum, this should be disclosed to Quillon and factored into the purchase price or closing conditions.
- Consider whether to request a waiver or adjustment from Velloran as part of the consent negotiation.

## ISSUE 6 – GOVERNING LAW AND FORUM CONFLICT BETWEEN SIDE LETTER AND DISTRIBUTION AGREEMENT

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement § 18.1 (Delaware law), § 18.2 (Wilmington, DE forum)
- Side Letter § 6.1 (New York law), § 6.2 (New York County forum)
- Distribution Agreement § 19.4 (Order of Precedence: Side Letter ranks above Agreement body)

**Why it matters:**

The Side Letter is governed by New York law and subject to exclusive New York jurisdiction, while the Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law with exclusive Wilmington jurisdiction. The Side Letter has higher precedence under § 19.4. This creates a split in the governing law and forum for what are closely related obligations (exclusivity and non-competition). In a dispute, the parties could face threshold litigation over which law and forum applies to a given claim, increasing cost and uncertainty.

This is of particular concern because a breach of the non-compete (Side Letter § 2) is also a material breach of the Distribution Agreement (Side Letter § 4.3), potentially triggering termination under the Distribution Agreement (Delaware law) while the underlying non-compete claim is governed by New York law.

**Recommendation:**

- Note this as a structural risk. If the client is negotiating Velloran's consent, consider requesting alignment of governing law and forum across the agreement stack as a condition.
- At minimum, flag this to Quillon as a consideration in its post-closing risk assessment.

## ISSUE 7 – WAREHOUSE AGREEMENT: NO CONSENT REQUIRED BUT AUTO-RENEWAL AND ASSIGNMENT TERMS WORTH NOTING

**Documents and Sections:**
- Warehouse Agreement § 4.1 (one-year initial term; auto-renews for successive one-year terms)
- Warehouse Agreement § 4.2 (termination for convenience on 60 days' notice)
- Warehouse Agreement § 11.1 (either Party may assign upon written notice; no consent required)
- MIPA Schedule 3.6 (Saltmarsh consent not listed — correctly, as none is required)

**Why it matters:**

The Saltmarsh agreement does not require a change-of-control consent or an assignment consent. Section 11.1 permits assignment on notice alone. This is not a deal blocker.

However, two points warrant attention:

1. **Auto-renewal.** The agreement auto-renews for successive one-year terms. There is no long-term commitment, which provides flexibility but also means the Company (or Quillon post-closing) could lose its warehouse provider on 60 days' notice from either side. Given that Saltmarsh is the Company's sole 3PL provider (Schedule 3.13), this is an operational concentration risk.

2. **Assignment by Saltmarsh.** Section 11.1 allows Saltmarsh to assign its obligations to a third party on notice alone. Post-closing, Quillon could find itself dealing with an unknown successor provider without having consented to the change.

**Recommendation:**

- No action required for the deal itself. Note the auto-renewal and assignment terms in the client's post-closing integration planning.
- If Quillon wants more stability, it may wish to negotiate a longer-term agreement with Saltmarsh post-closing, or add a consent requirement for Saltmarsh's assignment.

## ISSUE 8 – MIPA CONDUCT-OF-BUSINESS COVENANT MAY CONSTRAIN NEGOTIATIONS WITH VELLORAN

**Documents and Sections:**
- MIPA § 6.1(c) (Company shall not "amend, terminate or waive any material right under any Material Contract" between Signing and Closing without Buyer consent)
- Distribution Agreement § 14.2 (consent required for Change of Control)

**Why it matters:**

Between the Signing Date (October 5, 2026) and Closing, the Company is covenanted not to amend, terminate or waive any material right under any Material Contract without Buyer's written consent. If the Company needs to negotiate with Velloran to obtain its Change of Control consent — and in that negotiation Velloran requests modifications to the Distribution Agreement (e.g., adjusted pricing, revised minimums, or a consent fee) — the Company may need Quillon's consent under § 6.1(c) to agree to any such modification.

This could slow down the consent process and create friction between the Sellers and Quillon.

**Recommendation:**

- Coordinate with Quillon's counsel in advance about the scope of modifications the Company can negotiate with Velloran without separate Buyer consent under § 6.1(c).
- Seek a pre-agreed framework or a specific consent from Quillon to negotiate with Velloran, including authority to agree to reasonable modifications.
