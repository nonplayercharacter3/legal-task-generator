## Summary

Review of the matter folder reveals several material issues, the most urgent of which is that the Velloran Distribution Agreement appears to expire on November 14, 2026—sixteen days before the Target Closing Date—because the contractual deadline for delivering the Renewal Notice (August 16, 2026) has already passed as of the MIPA Signing Date (October 5, 2026). Compounding this, the MIPA does not treat Velloran's consent to the Change of Control as a Required Consent or a condition to Closing, leaving the company exposed to termination by Velloran immediately after or even before Closing. These issues should be raised with the client and Quillon's counsel promptly.

## Issue 1 – Distribution Agreement Expires Before Target Closing; Renewal Notice Deadline Has Passed

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement, Section 3.1 (Initial Term ends November 14, 2026) and Section 3.2 (Renewal Notice required at least 90 days prior to expiration of the Initial Term)
- MIPA, Section 2.2 (Target Closing Date: November 30, 2026)
- MIPA, Schedule 3.12 (disclosure: "The Initial Term of the Distribution Agreement ends November 14, 2026. The Company intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing.")
- MIPA, Section 3.12(c) (represents the Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029")

**Why It Matters:**
Section 3.2 of the Distribution Agreement requires the Renewal Notice to be delivered at least 90 days before the end of the Initial Term. The Initial Term ends November 14, 2026, making the deadline **August 16, 2026**. The MIPA was signed on October 5, 2026—nearly two months after that deadline. Schedule 3.12 states the Company "intends to deliver the Renewal Notice prior to Closing" (November 30, 2026), which is both after the contractual deadline and after the expiration date itself. If the Renewal Notice was not in fact delivered on or before August 16, 2026, the Distribution Agreement expires by its own terms on November 14, 2026, and cannot be renewed.

Velloran accounts for approximately 91% of the Company's cost of goods purchased (MIPA, Schedule 3.13). Loss of the exclusive distribution rights before Closing would constitute a Material Adverse Effect (MIPA, Section 1.10) and would likely defeat the condition in Section 7.1(e). Even if Closing occurs on November 30, 2026, the company would operate for 16 days (November 14–30) with no contractual basis to purchase or sell Velloran Products.

Additionally, the representation in Section 3.12(c) that the Distribution Agreement "remains in effect through November 2029" appears inaccurate as of the Signing Date if the Renewal Notice was not timely delivered, creating a potential indemnification claim under MIPA Section 8.2(a) and a breach of the bring-down condition in Section 7.1(a).

**Recommended Fix:**
1. **Immediately** confirm with the client whether a Renewal Notice was delivered to Velloran on or before August 16, 2026. If it was, obtain a copy and file it in the matter record.
2. If it was not delivered, raise this with Quillon's counsel immediately. Options include: (a) negotiating a short extension or bridge agreement with Velloran to cover the gap through and beyond Closing; (b) amending the MIPA to make Velloran's consent (and a renewed or extended Distribution Agreement) a condition to Closing; or (c) accelerating the Closing to before November 14, 2026.
3. The Sellers should be advised of their potential indemnification exposure under MIPA Section 8.2(a) if the Section 3.12(c) representation is inaccurate.

**⚠ FLAG: Raise with client and Quillon's counsel promptly.**

## Issue 2 – Velloran's Change-of-Control Consent Is Not a Required Consent Under the MIPA

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement, Section 1.3 (definition of "Change of Control"), Section 14.2 (CoC deemed an assignment requiring Supplier's prior written consent), Section 14.3 (Supplier may terminate immediately if CoC occurs without consent)
- MIPA, Section 1.15 (definition of "Required Consents" = consents listed on Schedule 3.6)
- MIPA, Schedule 3.6 (lists only: (1) Brandywine Flats Industrial LLC landlord consent; (2) Keystone Commonwealth Bank lender consent)
- MIPA, Section 7.1(d) (Closing condition: all Required Consents obtained)
- MIPA, Section 6.4 (Sellers' obligation to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain Required Consents)

**Why It Matters:**
The sale of 100% of the Membership Interests is unambiguously a "Change of Control" under Section 1.3(a) of the Distribution Agreement (sale of more than 50% of equity interests). Section 14.2 deems this an assignment requiring Velloran's prior written consent, and Section 14.3 gives Velloran the right to terminate the Distribution Agreement immediately if the CoC occurs without consent.

However, Velloran's consent is **not** listed in Schedule 3.6, is not a "Required Consent" under the MIPA, and is therefore not a condition to Closing under Section 7.1(d). The Sellers have no contractual obligation under Section 6.4 to seek Velloran's consent. This means the MIPA can close on November 30, 2026 without Velloran's consent, at which point Velloran could exercise its termination right under Section 14.3—potentially on the same day as or immediately after Closing.

This creates a scenario in which Quillon pays $48.5 million for a company whose primary revenue source can be terminated at will by Velloran the moment the deal closes.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Add Velloran's consent to Schedule 3.6 as a Required Consent, making it a condition to Closing under Section 7.1(d).
2. Alternatively, add a specific closing condition requiring that the Distribution Agreement remain in full force and effect (or be renewed) as of the Closing Date.
3. If Quillon's counsel resists, at minimum add a specific indemnity or escrow holdback for the risk of Velloran termination post-Closing.
4. Begin the consent request to Velloran immediately; do not wait for a MIPA amendment.

**⚠ FLAG: Raise with client and Quillon's counsel promptly.**

## Issue 3 – Year 3 Minimum Purchase Requirement Not Represented as Met

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement, Section 5.1 and Exhibit B, Part 2 (Year 3 minimum: $3,100,000 for the period November 15, 2025 – November 14, 2026)
- Distribution Agreement, Section 5.2 (remedies for shortfall: conversion to non-exclusive or termination)
- MIPA, Section 3.12(c) (represents compliance with Minimum Purchase Requirement only for contract years ended November 14, 2024 and November 14, 2025)
- First Amendment, Section 3.2 (Accessory Products count toward the Minimum Purchase Requirement)

**Why It Matters:**
The MIPA represents compliance with the Minimum Purchase Requirement for Year 1 and Year 2 but is silent on Year 3 (the period ending November 14, 2026). Because the Target Closing Date (November 30, 2026) falls after the end of the Year 3 measurement period, Velloran could assert a shortfall under Section 5.2 and either convert the appointment to non-exclusive or terminate the agreement. This would be particularly damaging if combined with the Change of Control termination right (Issue 2).

The omission from the representation may be because the Sellers could not confirm compliance as of the Signing Date (October 5, 2026), since the Year 3 period had not yet ended. However, this leaves a gap: if the Year 3 minimum is not met, Velloran has an independent termination trigger that is not addressed in the MIPA.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Request from the client a projection or actuals report of Year 3 net purchases through the most recent date to assess whether the $3.1 million minimum is likely to be met.
2. If there is a risk of shortfall, raise it with Quillon's counsel and consider adding a specific disclosure to Schedule 3.12 or a condition to Closing that the Year 3 minimum has been met (or that Velloran has waived any shortfall remedy).
3. Note that the First Amendment (effective June 1, 2026) added Accessories Products that count toward the minimum; confirm whether these have contributed meaningfully to Year 3 purchases.

## Issue 4 – Side Letter Non-Compete Covers a Broader Territory Than the Distribution Agreement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter, Section 1.1 (defines "Territory" as Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, **New York and Virginia**)
- Distribution Agreement, Section 1.12 (defines "Territory" as Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey and Pennsylvania only)
- Side Letter, Section 2.1 (non-compete applies in the Side Letter's broader "Territory")
- Side Letter, Section 1.3 (Restricted Period = Term of Distribution Agreement + 18 months post-expiration/termination)
- MIPA, Section 6.7 (Seller non-compete limited to DE, MD, NJ, PA only)

**Why It Matters:**
The non-compete in the Side Letter restricts Harrow Creek from distributing Competing Products in six states (including NY and VA), while the exclusive distribution rights under the Distribution Agreement cover only four states. After the sale, Quillon will own Harrow Creek and be bound by the broader non-compete. If Quillon has existing or planned distribution activities in New York or Virginia involving precision measurement instruments or related products, those activities would violate the Side Letter.

The Restricted Period extends 18 months beyond the expiration or termination of the Distribution Agreement. If Velloran terminates the agreement (as it can under Section 14.3 if CoC consent is not obtained), the non-compete would still bind Harrow Creek for 18 months from the termination date, during which the company cannot distribute competing products in six states.

The inconsistency with MIPA Section 6.7 (which limits the Sellers' personal non-compete to four states) suggests the parties may not have fully appreciated the broader scope of the Side Letter.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Confirm with the client whether Quillon or its Affiliates have any business in the precision measurement or related product space in New York or Virginia.
2. If so, negotiate a narrowing of the Side Letter territory (to match the Distribution Agreement's four-state territory) as a condition to or deliverable at Closing, or obtain a waiver from Velloran.
3. Ensure Quillon's counsel is aware of the six-state scope so they can assess post-closing operational constraints.

## Issue 5 – Conflicting Governing Law Clauses Between the Side Letter and the Distribution Agreement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter, Section 6.1 (governed by New York law; exclusive jurisdiction in New York County, NY)
- Distribution Agreement, Section 18.1 (governed by Delaware law) and Section 18.2 (exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware)
- Distribution Agreement, Section 19.4 (order of precedence: First Amendment > Side Letter > body of Agreement > Exhibits)

**Why It Matters:**
The Side Letter is governed by New York law and subject to New York courts, while the Distribution Agreement is governed by Delaware law and subject to Delaware courts. The Side Letter expressly references and incorporates provisions of the Distribution Agreement (e.g., Section 4.3 makes a Side Letter breach a "material breach of the Distribution Agreement for purposes of Section 15.1"). A dispute involving both agreements could raise threshold conflicts-of-law questions about which law governs the interplay between the two documents. New York and Delaware courts may analyze the enforceability of the non-compete differently (New York courts apply a reasonableness test; Delaware courts do as well but with different doctrinal emphases).

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Flag the conflict to Quillon's counsel as a risk factor.
2. If a negotiation with Velloran is opened (to obtain CoC consent or address the Renewal issue), consider requesting a conforming amendment to align the governing law and forum provisions.
3. In the interim, ensure that any consent or waiver obtained from Velloran is drafted to avoid creating ambiguity about which law governs.

## Issue 6 – Extreme Supplier Concentation (91% of COGS from Velloran)

**Documents and Sections:**
- MIPA, Schedule 3.13 (Velloran = approximately 91% of cost of goods purchased in both fiscal year 2025 and the eight months ended August 31, 2026)
- MIPA, Recital B (Company is the "exclusive distributor in the mid-Atlantic region" of Velloran products)

**Why It Matters:**
The Company's entire business model depends on a single supplier. Any disruption to the Velloran relationship—whether through expiration (Issue 1), termination for unconsented CoC (Issue 2), termination for minimum purchase shortfall (Issue 3), or a commercial dispute—would be existential. Quillon is paying $48.5 million for a company whose value is almost entirely derivative of one contract that is currently in a state of uncertainty.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Ensure this concentration risk is prominently disclosed in any presentation to Quillon's board or investment committee.
2. The combination of Issues 1–3 means the Velloran relationship is at risk on multiple independent grounds. Address all of them in a single negotiation with Velloran rather than piecemeal.
3. Consider whether the Purchase Price should be adjusted or whether additional protections (escrow, specific indemnity, or a condition to Closing) are warranted given this concentration.

## Issue 7 – Side Letter Section 4.3: Non-Compete Breach Is a Material Breach of the Distribution Agreement

**Documents and Sections:**
- Side Letter, Section 4.3 ("A breach of Section 2 during the Term is a material breach of the Distribution Agreement for purposes of Section 15.1 of the Distribution Agreement.")
- Distribution Agreement, Section 15.1 (termination for material breach with 30-day cure period)

**Why It Matters:**
This provision gives Velloran an additional termination trigger beyond the Change of Control and minimum purchase shortfall grounds. If, after the sale, Quillon causes Harrow Creek to take any action that could be characterized as marketing or distributing Competing Products in the six-state territory (even inadvertently, through a subsidiary or affiliate), Velloran could argue a material breach of the Distribution Agreement and terminate on 30 days' notice. Given the breadth of the "Competing Products" definition (Side Letter, Section 1.2: "substantially similar in function to, or... marketed as substitutes for, any of the Products"), the risk of an inadvertent breach is non-trivial for a buyer with diversified industrial holdings.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Advise the client to brief Quillon's operational team on the scope of the non-compete and the definition of "Competing Products" before Closing.
2. If a negotiation with Velloran is opened, consider requesting a clarification or narrowing of the "Competing Products" definition.
3. Ensure the MIPA's conduct-of-business covenant (Section 6.1) is understood to require the Company to avoid any action that could trigger this provision during the pre-Closing period.

## Issue 8 – Warehouse Agreement: No Change-of-Control Restriction (Favorable) but 60-Day Termination for Convenience

**Documents and Sections:**
- Warehouse Agreement, Section 11.1 (assignment permitted upon written notice; no consent required)
- Warehouse Agreement, Section 4.2 (either party may terminate for convenience on 60 days' notice)
- Warehouse Agreement, Section 4.1 (auto-renewing one-year terms; currently in third term through January 31, 2027)

**Why It Matters:**
The Saltmarsh agreement does not require consent for the Change of Control and can be assigned on notice alone. This is favorable and requires no action. However, the 60-day termination-for-convenience right cuts both ways: Saltmarsh could terminate the agreement effective approximately 60 days after Closing, which would disrupt the Company's fulfillment operations. Given that the warehouse is integral to the distribution business, Quillon should be aware of this exit risk.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. No action required for the transaction itself.
2. Advise the client that post-Closing, Quillon may wish to negotiate a longer-term commitment or removal of the convenience termination right with Saltmarsh to ensure operational continuity.
3. Confirm that the warehouse at 615 Pigeon Point Road, New Castle, DE (the "Facility" under Section 1.1) is distinct from the leased warehouse at 1840 Ridge Pike, Conshohocken, PA (listed on MIPA Schedule 3.11). If both are in use, confirm the role of each.

## Issue 9 – MIPA Section 3.5 (No Conflicts) Representation May Be Inaccurate

**Documents and Sections:**
- MIPA, Section 3.5(c) (represents the transaction does not "conflict with, result in a breach of or default under, or give any Person a right of termination... under, any Material Contract," except for Required Consents)
- MIPA, Schedule 3.6 (Velloran's consent not listed)
- Distribution Agreement, Sections 14.2–14.3 (CoC gives Velloran a right of termination)

**Why It Matters:**
The sale of 100% of the Membership Interests gives Velloran a right of termination under Section 14.3 of the Distribution Agreement. Because Velloran's consent is not a "Required Consent," the carve-out in Section 3.5(c) ("except for the Required Consents") does not protect the Sellers. The representation that the transaction does not "give any Person a right of termination... under any Material Contract" appears to be inaccurate on its face. This is a separate inaccuracy from the Section 3.12(c) issue (Issue 1) and could independently support an indemnification claim under MIPA Section 8.2(a).

**Recommended Fix:**
1. Add Velloran's consent to Schedule 3.6 (as recommended in Issue 2), which would bring the CoC within the "except for the Required Consents" carve-out.
2. Alternatively, add a specific disclosure to the Disclosure Schedules under Section 3.5 noting that the CoC gives Velloran a termination right under Section 14.3 of the Distribution Agreement.
3. Advise the Sellers of their indemnification exposure if this representation is not corrected before Closing.

## Issue 10 – Timing Gap Between Distribution Agreement Expiration and Target Closing Date

**Documents and Sections:**
- Distribution Agreement, Section 3.1 (Initial Term ends November 14, 2026)
- MIPA, Section 2.2 (Target Closing Date: November 30, 2026)
- MIPA, Section 1.12 (Outside Date: January 29, 2027)

**Why It Matters:**
Even if the Renewal Notice issue is resolved (e.g., by confirming it was timely delivered), there is a structural timing problem: the Initial Term expires November 14, 2026, and the Target Closing Date is November 30, 2026. If the Renewal Notice was properly delivered, the Renewal Term would begin November 15, 2026, and the agreement would continue without a gap. However, if there is any ambiguity or dispute about whether the Renewal Notice was validly delivered, the company could be in a gap period with no contractual rights to purchase Velloran Products.

**Recommended Fix:**
1. If the Renewal Notice was timely delivered, obtain written confirmation from Velloran that the Renewal Term has been triggered.
2. If it was not, see Issue 1 for remediation options.
3. Consider accelerating the Target Closing Date to before November 14, 2026 to eliminate the gap entirely.
