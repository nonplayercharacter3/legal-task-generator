## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Was Likely Untimely

**Where:** Agreement §12.2; Order Form §1.1–1.2; Side Letter Recital C and §4.

**Explanation:** The Initial Subscription Term is 36 months from 1 February 2024, so it ends on **31 January 2027**. Section 12.2 requires non-renewal notice at least **120 days** before the end of the then-current Subscription Term. Counting back 120 days from 31 January 2027 gives a deadline of **3 October 2026**. The Side Letter (Recital C) confirms that Praidein delivered its non-renewal notice on **14 October 2026**—eleven days after the deadline. Zousen has already taken the position in Side Letter §4.2 that the notice is "untimely and ineffective."

**Why it matters:** If the notice is ineffective, the Subscription Term auto-renews for **24 months** (i.e., through 31 January 2029) under §12.2. Praidein would be locked in for two more years at the then-current fees, with no exit right short of material breach or insolvency.

**Additional risk – delivery method:** Section 16.1 requires notices to be delivered "by hand, by nationally recognized overnight courier, or by email with confirmation copy by courier." The documents do not state how the 14 October 2026 notice was delivered. If it was sent by email alone without a confirmation copy by courier, Zousen could argue it was ineffective even if it had been timely. We need to confirm the delivery method.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm immediately how the notice was delivered and when it was received. If it was delivered by a method compliant with §16.1, the only issue is the 11-day lateness.
- Assess whether Zousen would accept the notice as effective in exchange for something (e.g., the Cleveland site addition, which is already in the Side Letter). The fact that Zousen agreed to add the Cleveland site in the same Side Letter where it disputes the notice suggests commercial leverage on both sides.
- If the notice is truly ineffective, evaluate whether to (a) negotiate a mutual release from the renewal, (b) accept the 24-month extension and plan for the next non-renewal window (which would open 120 days before 31 January 2029, i.e., by 3 October 2028), or (c) explore whether Zousen's own conduct (adding the Cleveland site, invoicing pro rata through 31 January 2027) could support an argument of waiver or estoppel. Note that Side Letter §4.3 expressly disclaims any waiver, so this would be a litigation argument, not a contractual one.

## Issue 2: Pending Ostrander Merger Triggers Change of Control Provision

**Where:** Agreement §15.3; Side Letter Recital B.

**Explanation:** Side Letter Recital B discloses that on 11 September 2026, Praidein entered into a Merger Agreement under which Ostrander Aviation Hospitality Group, Inc. will acquire **100%** of Praidein's equity. Section 15.3 defines "Change of Control" as any transaction in which a person acquires **more than 50%** of Licensee's equity or voting interests, and provides that a Change of Control "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee requiring Licensor's prior written consent under Section 15.1."

The consequence of closing without consent: Zousen may terminate on 30 days' notice, and **"all Fees for the remainder of the Subscription Term shall be accelerated and become immediately due and payable."**

**Why it matters:**
- If the non-renewal notice is ineffective (Issue 1), the "remainder of the Subscription Term" is 24 months. Accelerated fees would be approximately **$466,800** ($233,400/year × 2 years, including the Cleveland site) plus any overage.
- Even if the non-renewal notice is effective, there is a remainder of the current term (1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027) for which fees could be accelerated.
- Zousen has not given consent. The Side Letter is silent on the Change of Control. There is no evidence in the folder of any consent or waiver.
- This gives Zousen significant leverage over the merger closing.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Zousen's written consent to the Change of Control **before the merger closes**. This is the cleanest solution.
- In negotiating consent, seek to (a) waive the fee acceleration, (b) confirm that the Agreement will survive the merger and be binding on Ostrander as successor, and (c) tie the consent to resolution of the non-renewal dispute (Issue 1).
- If Zousen withholds consent, assess whether the acceleration provision is enforceable as a penalty under Delaware law. A fee acceleration triggered by a change of control (which is not itself a breach) may be vulnerable to challenge, but this is uncertain and would be a litigation position, not a safe planning assumption.
- **Action needed from client:** Confirm the expected closing date of the Ostrander merger and whether Zousen has been formally asked for consent.

## Issue 3: "Authorized User" Definition Excludes Agency Workers

**Where:** Agreement §1.1 (definition of "Authorized User"); Order Form §2.1 (redefinition); Agreement §1.3 (order of precedence); Order Form §2.4 (overage).

**Explanation:** The Agreement defines "Authorized User" broadly to include "employees, **contractors and agency workers** of Licensee and its Affiliates." However, Order Form §2.1 redefines "Authorized User" as "a **named employee** of Licensee who works at an Authorized Site, who has been issued a unique user credential… and whom Licensee has registered in the Software's user administration console."

Under the order of precedence (Agreement §1.3), the Order Form controls over the Agreement body. The practical effect is that **contractors and agency workers are not Authorized Users** under the operative definition.

The Recitals (¶B) state that "Licensee staffs its flight kitchens **largely with agency galley crews**." If those agency workers access the Software, they are using it as non-Authorized Users.

**Why it matters:** Order Form §2.4 charges an overage of **$145 per individual per month** (or part of a month) for "access to or use of the Software by any individual other than an Authorized User." Zousen may audit quarterly. If, for example, 50 agency workers use the system, that is $7,250/month or $87,000/year in overage—more than half the base subscription fee. The exposure scales with headcount.

**Recommended fix:**
- Determine how many agency workers and contractors currently have access to or use the Software.
- Negotiate with Zousen to either (a) amend Order Form §2.1 to restore the broader definition from the Agreement, or (b) increase the Subscription Quantity to cover the actual user population at a negotiated per-seat rate lower than the overage rate.
- In the interim, ensure that only named employees with registered credentials can access the Software, and that agency workers do not use shared or unregistered logins.
- This issue should be raised as part of the broader negotiation with Zousen (see Issues 1 and 2), where Praidein has leverage through the Cleveland site addition and the merger consent request.

## Issue 4: Asymmetric Assignment Rights

**Where:** Order Form §7; Agreement §15.1.

**Explanation:** Agreement §15.1 provides that **neither party** may assign without the other's prior written consent. However, Order Form §7 (which prevails under §1.3) states that **Licensor may assign without Licensee's consent** to any Affiliate or to any acquirer of all or part of its business or assets. Licensee has no equivalent right.

**Why it matters:** Zousen can be acquired by, or merge with, any entity—including a direct competitor of Praidein or a company with inferior security practices—and Praidein would have no consent right. The Agreement's confidentiality and data protection obligations would bind the successor, but Praidein would have no contractual basis to object to the identity of its counterparty.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a deletion of Order Form §7 or a mutualization of the assignment provision so that both parties have consent rights (or at least so that Licensee has the right to terminate if Licensor assigns to a direct competitor).
- This is a lower-priority item compared to Issues 1–3 but should be included in any renegotiation package.

## Issue 5: No Service Credit or Remedy for Availability Shortfalls

**Where:** Agreement §3.2.

**Explanation:** Section 3.2 commits Zousen to 99.5% monthly availability (excluding scheduled maintenance). However, the Agreement provides **no service credit, fee reduction, or other remedy** if Zousen fails to meet this threshold. The only available remedy is termination for material breach under §13.1, which requires a 30-day cure period.

**Why it matters:** For an airline caterer, Software unavailability during peak loading windows could mean missed flight loads, contractual penalties to airlines, and wasted galley crew time. A 0.5% monthly allowance translates to roughly 3.6 hours of downtime per month—potentially enough to cause operational disruption. Without a service credit, Praidein bears the full cost of Zousen's underperformance.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a service credit schedule (e.g., 5% of monthly fees for each 0.1% below the 99.5% threshold, capped at 30% of monthly fees).
- Alternatively, add a right to terminate the affected Order Form without penalty if availability falls below 99.0% in any two consecutive months.

## Issue 6: Liability Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** Agreement §10.1–10.3.

**Explanation:** The aggregate liability cap is "Fees paid and payable by Licensee in the twelve months preceding the event giving rise to the claim" (§10.2). At current fees, this is approximately **$233,400**. Combined with the exclusion of consequential damages (§10.1), Praidein cannot recover lost revenue, missed-flight penalties, or crew overtime costs resulting from Software failure or data breach.

The carve-outs in §10.3 (indemnification, breach of confidentiality §9, breach of security §5.3, payment obligations, gross negligence/willful misconduct/fraud) are helpful but narrow.

**Why it matters:** If a Software outage causes Praidein to miss multiple flight loads, or if a data breach exposes airline-contract pricing to competitors, the recoverable amount is capped at roughly one year's fees. The real-world impact could be multiples of that.

**Recommended fix:**
- Seek to raise the cap to 2× or 3× annual fees, or to a fixed dollar amount (e.g., $1,000,000).
- Ensure that the §5.3 (security) and §9 (confidentiality) carve-outs from the cap are clear and unambiguous—they are in the current draft, which is favorable.
- This is a standard negotiation point and Zousen may resist, but it is worth raising given the operational criticality of the Software.

## Issue 7: Data Return Window May Be Insufficient in a Disputed Exit

**Where:** Agreement §5.4.

**Explanation:** After termination or expiry, Zousen must make Licensee Data available for export in a standard machine-readable format for **60 days**, then delete it (subject to backup retention).

**Why it matters:** If the non-renewal dispute (Issue 1) or the Change of Control (Issue 2) results in a contested termination, Praidein may need more than 60 days to negotiate, litigate, or transition to a replacement vendor. The Licensee Data includes menu specifications, recipes, airline-contract pricing, meal-load plans, production volumes, and galley configurations—core operational data that would be difficult to reconstruct.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an extension of the data return period to 90 or 120 days, particularly in the event of a disputed termination.
- Request that Zousen confirm in writing the export format and provide a sample export before termination occurs, so Praidein can validate that the data is usable.
- As a precaution, Praidein should maintain its own copies of critical data (recipes, galley configurations, airline pricing) outside the Software to the extent practicable.

## Issue 8: Cleveland Site Addition Complicates the Non-Renewal Position

**Where:** Side Letter §2, §3, §4.3.

**Explanation:** The Side Letter adds the Cleveland site effective 1 November 2026 with an annual fee of $58,200, while simultaneously preserving both parties' positions on the non-renewal notice (§4.3). The pro rata invoice runs from 1 November 2026 to 31 January 2027.

**Why it matters:**
- If the non-renewal is effective, Praidein pays for roughly 3 months of Cleveland site access before the term ends on 31 January 2027. The pro rata charge is approximately $14,550 ($58,200 × 3/12). This is a sunk cost.
- If the non-renewal is ineffective, Praidein is committed to the Cleveland site for 24+ months at $58,200/year, on top of the base subscription.
- Zousen could argue in a future dispute that Praidein's willingness to expand the footprint (adding Cleveland) demonstrates an intent to continue the relationship, undermining any claim that Praidein intended to exit. Although §4.3 disclaims waiver, the commercial signal is not ideal.

**Recommended fix:**
- Treat the Cleveland site addition as a negotiating chip. If Zousen agrees to accept the non-renewal notice (or to a negotiated exit date), Praidein can offer to continue the Cleveland site under a new, shorter-term arrangement or a separate order form.
- Ensure that the pro rata invoice for November–January is not followed by a full annual invoice for 2027 if the term is to end on 31 January 2027.

## Issue 9: No Transition or Exit Assistance Obligation

**Where:** Agreement generally (no provision).

**Explanation:** The Agreement contains no obligation on Zousen to provide transition services, knowledge transfer, or cooperation with a replacement vendor upon termination or expiry. The only post-termination obligation is the 60-day data export window (§5.4).

**Why it matters:** If Praidein exits (whether by successful non-renewal, Change of Control termination, or negotiated departure), it will need to migrate to alternative software. Without a transition assistance obligation, Zousen has no contractual duty to cooperate, document integrations, or support the handover. The passenger-manifest feed integrations (Order Form §3, §6.1(c)) are particularly complex to replicate.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate a transition assistance clause requiring Zousen to provide reasonable cooperation (e.g., 30–60 days of transition support, documentation of API integrations, and assistance with data migration) for a specified fee.
- If Zousen resists, at minimum ensure that the Documentation (defined in §1.1 to include "technical specifications and release notes") is sufficiently detailed to allow a third party to replicate the integrations.

## Issue 10: Support Commitment Is Thin for Severity 1

**Where:** Agreement §3.3.

**Explanation:** Section 3.3 provides 24/7 support for "Severity 1 issues (Software unavailable or tray-assembly functions inoperable)" but only "during business hours for all other issues." There are no defined response times, resolution times, or escalation paths for Severity 1. The support is provided "in accordance with its standard support policy," which is not attached to or incorporated in the Agreement.

**Why it matters:** For an airline caterer, "tray-assembly functions inoperable" during a loading window is an emergency. Without a defined response time or escalation, Praidein has no contractual basis to demand rapid resolution or to claim a breach if Zousen is slow to respond.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate defined response and resolution times for Severity 1 (e.g., response within 30 minutes, resolution or workaround within 4 hours).
- Attach or incorporate Zousen's standard support policy by reference so that the commitment is fixed and auditable.
- Add an escalation path to Zousen's engineering management if the Severity 1 issue is not resolved within a specified time.

## Operational Note: Lorvex Agreement – Cleveland Site Gap

**Where:** Lorvex Agreement §1.1 (definition of "Service Locations"); Schedule A.

**Explanation:** The Lorvex Fleet Refrigeration Maintenance Agreement defines "Service Locations" as Pittsburgh (PIT), Columbus (CMH), and Buffalo (BUF). Schedule A lists 21 vehicles based at those three locations. The Cleveland site is being added under the Zousen Side Letter effective 1 November 2026.

**Why it matters:** This is not a Zousen issue per se, but if Praidein will operate refrigerated catering vehicles from the Cleveland kitchen (as it presumably will, given that it is a flight kitchen), those vehicles are **not covered** under the Lorvex agreement. Cleveland is not a Service Location, and no Cleveland-based vehicles appear in Schedule A. Praidein would need to add Cleveland as a Service Location and register the new vehicles under §2.4 of the Lorvex agreement.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm with the client whether refrigerated vehicles will be based at Cleveland. If so, add them to Schedule A under the Lorvex agreement and add Cleveland as a Service Location. This should be done before the 1 November 2026 Site Addition Date to avoid a coverage gap.
