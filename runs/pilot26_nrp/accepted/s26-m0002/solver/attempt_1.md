## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is 11 Days Late

**Where:** Agreement § 12.2; Side Letter, Recital C and § 4.2; Order Form § 1.2

**Analysis:**

The Initial Subscription Term is 36 months from 1 February 2024, ending 31 January 2027 (Order Form § 1.2). Section 12.2 requires a party to give written notice of non-renewal "at least one hundred twenty (120) days before the end of the then-current Subscription Term." Counting back 120 days from 31 January 2027 yields a deadline of **3 October 2026**. The Side Letter confirms the notice was delivered on **14 October 2026** (Recital C) — eleven days after the deadline.

Licensor has expressly taken the position that the notice is "untimely and ineffective to prevent renewal" (Side Letter § 4.2). Both parties have reserved rights (Side Letter § 4.3).

**Why it matters:**

If the notice is ineffective, the Subscription Term auto-renews for 24 months through 31 January 2029 (Agreement § 12.2). Praidein would be contractually committed for two more years, with annual fees of at least $175,200 plus the Cleveland site fee of $58,200 (Side Letter § 3.1), subject to a further 5% increase on any subsequent renewal (Agreement § 4.5). Given that Praidein is being acquired by Ostrander, a forced 24-month renewal may conflict with the acquirer's integration plans or its own software strategy.

**Additional complication — delivery method:** Section 16.1 requires notices to be delivered "by hand, by nationally recognized overnight courier, or by email with confirmation copy by courier." The documents do not state how the 14 October notice was delivered. If it was sent by email alone (without a confirmation copy by courier), Licensor could argue a second, independent ground of invalidity. I need to confirm the delivery method with the client.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm with the client exactly how and when the non-renewal notice was sent and received (email timestamp, courier tracking, etc.).
- If the notice was in fact sent before 3 October 2026 but only received on 14 October, there may be an argument under § 16.1 (notices are "effective on receipt" — this cuts against Praidein) or under general contract principles regarding mailbox rule, but Delaware law generally follows receipt for contractual notices of this type. This is a weak argument.
- The more practical path is to negotiate with Zousen: given that Zousen has agreed to add the Cleveland site in the same Side Letter where it disputes the notice, there is a commercial relationship still functioning. Seek a written waiver or amendment confirming that the Subscription Term will not renew, or alternatively that if it does renew, Praidein may exit on shortened notice (e.g., 90 days) without penalty.
- If negotiation fails, assess whether the 11-day delay is material under Delaware law or whether Zousen's conduct (agreeing to the Cleveland site addition while knowing the notice was late) constitutes a waiver or estoppel. This is fact-intensive and I would need to see the full correspondence file.

**Cannot resolve from documents alone:** Delivery method of the notice; full correspondence between the parties around October 2026; whether Zousen has raised the issue in any demand letter or invoice.

## Issue 2: Change of Control Consent Not Obtained

**Where:** Agreement § 15.3; Side Letter, Recital B

**Analysis:**

Section 15.3 provides that any Change of Control of Licensee "shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee requiring Licensor's prior written consent under Section 15.1." Change of Control is defined as any transaction in which a person or group acquires more than 50% of Licensee's equity or voting interests. The Side Letter confirms that on 11 September 2026, Praidein entered into a Merger Agreement under which Ostrander will acquire **100%** of Praidein's equity (Recital B).

The Side Letter does **not** record Licensor's consent to the Change of Control. Section 4.3 of the Side Letter reserves rights only in respect of the non-renewal notice; it is silent on the merger.

If a Change of Control occurs without Licensor's prior written consent, § 15.3 provides that:
1. Licensor may terminate the Agreement and all Order Forms on 30 days' written notice; and
2. **All Fees for the remainder of the Subscription Term shall be accelerated and become immediately due and payable.**

**Why it matters:**

The financial exposure depends on whether the non-renewal notice is effective:

| Scenario | Term end | Accelerated fees on CoC termination |
|---|---|---|
| Non-renewal effective (term ends 31 Jan 2027) | 31 Jan 2027 | Fees for remaining days only (minimal) |
| Non-renewal ineffective (term renews to 31 Jan 2029) | 31 Jan 2029 | Up to ~$466,800 (24 months × $175,200 + Cleveland fees) |

In the worst case, Ostrander (or Praidein pre-closing) would owe roughly $467,000 in accelerated fees upon a CoC termination, on top of any fees already paid. This is a material deal point that should be addressed before the merger closes.

**Recommended fix:**

- Obtain written consent from Zousen to the Change of Control **before** the Ostrander merger closes. This is the cleanest solution and should be a condition to closing or a pre-closing covenant in the Merger Agreement (if still negotiable).
- If Zousen withholds consent, negotiate a termination-for-CoC provision that does not include fee acceleration, or that caps acceleration at a reasonable amount (e.g., 6 months' fees).
- If the non-renewal notice is ultimately held effective, the CoC issue becomes less financially acute (the term is ending anyway), but Zousen could still argue it is entitled to terminate early under § 15.3 if the merger closes before 31 January 2027.
- Confirm with the client: Has any consent request been made to Zousen? What is the expected closing date of the Ostrander merger? Is there a regulatory or other condition that could delay closing past 31 January 2027?

**Cannot resolve from documents alone:** Whether a consent request has been made; the expected merger closing date; whether the Merger Agreement contains any covenant regarding the Zousen relationship.

## Issue 3: Asymmetric Assignment Rights Favor Licensor

**Where:** Agreement § 15.1; Order Form § 7; Agreement § 1.3 (order of precedence)

**Analysis:**

Agreement § 15.1 provides that neither party may assign without the other's prior written consent. However, Order Form § 7 provides that **Licensor** may assign the Order Form and Agreement "without Licensee's consent, to any Affiliate or to any acquirer of all or part of its business or assets," subject only to 30 days' after-the-fact notice.

Under the order of precedence in Agreement § 1.3 (Side Letter → Order Form → Agreement body), the Order Form controls over the Agreement body. The net effect is:

- **Zousen** can assign freely to an affiliate or acquirer (e.g., if Zousen is sold or merges).
- **Praidein** cannot assign without Zousen's consent, and any Change of Control is deemed an assignment requiring consent (§ 15.3).

**Why it matters:**

Praidein is entrusting Zousen with highly sensitive data: menu specifications, recipes, airline-contract pricing, meal-load plans, and production volumes (Agreement § 1.1, "Licensee Data"). If Zousen is acquired by a competitor or a company with competing caterer clients, Praidein's data could end up in the hands of a competitive threat, with no consent right and no termination trigger. The confidentiality obligations (§ 9) and data-use restrictions (§ 5.2) would bind the assignee, but enforcement against a new owner is less certain than a contractual right to object.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate an amendment to Order Form § 7 to require Licensor's consent for assignment to a direct competitor of Licensee, or at minimum to give Licensee a termination right (with pro rata refund) if Licensor assigns to a competitor.
- Alternatively, add a provision that if Licensor assigns, Licensee may terminate the affected Order Form on 60 days' notice without penalty.
- This is a lower-priority fix compared to Issues 1 and 2, but should be raised in any renegotiation.

## Issue 4: Authorized User Definition Excludes Agency Workers and Contractors

**Where:** Agreement § 1.1 ("Authorized User"); Order Form § 2.1 ("Authorized User"); Order Form § 2.4 (Overage); Agreement Recital B

**Analysis:**

The Agreement defines "Authorized User" broadly to include "employees, contractors and agency workers of Licensee and its Affiliates" (Agreement § 1.1). However, the Order Form narrows the definition to "a named employee of Licensee who works at an Authorized Site, who has been issued a unique user credential by Licensee's system administrator, and whom Licensee has registered in the Software's user administration console" (Order Form § 2.1).

Under the order of precedence (Agreement § 1.3), the Order Form definition controls. Agency workers and contractors are **not** Authorized Users.

Recital B of the Agreement expressly states that "Licensee staffs its flight kitchens largely with agency galley crews, who work alongside Licensee's employees and contractors." If any of those agency workers or contractors access the Software, they are using it outside the license, and each such individual triggers the overage charge of **$145 per individual per month** (Order Form § 2.4). Zousen may audit user activity once per quarter (Order Form § 2.4).

**Why it matters:**

Given that Praidein "staffs its flight kitchens largely with agency galley crews," it is highly likely that some agency personnel have access to the Software (e.g., for tray-assembly sequencing or meal-load planning). If, for example, 20 agency workers across the three (soon four) sites have been using the system, the overage exposure is 20 × $145 × 12 = **$34,800 per year**, accruing from the point of first unauthorized use. This is in addition to the risk that a quarterly audit identifies the issue and Zousen demands retroactive overage fees.

There is also a compliance risk: Agreement § 2.4 makes Licensee "responsible for its Authorized Users' compliance," and § 8.2 requires Licensee to indemnify Licensor for claims arising from Licensee's use of the Software "in breach of this Agreement." Unauthorized use by agency workers could be characterized as a breach.

**Recommended fix:**

- Conduct an internal audit of the Software's user administration console to identify all active credentials and determine how many are assigned to non-employees (agency workers, contractors).
- If the number is small, deactivate those accounts and ensure agency workers do not access the Software going forward.
- If the number is significant or operational needs require agency worker access, negotiate an amendment to Order Form § 2.1 to restore the broader definition from Agreement § 1.1, or to include a defined number of agency-worker seats within the Subscription Quantity at a reduced per-seat rate.
- Address this in the same negotiation as Issues 1–3 to avoid multiple rounds of amendment.

**Cannot resolve from documents alone:** The actual number of non-employee users currently in the system; whether Zousen has conducted any quarterly audits to date.

If the Cleveland kitchen operates its own refrigerated vehicle fleet (high-lift trucks, delivery vans), those vehicles are not covered by the Lorvex agreement. Praidein would need either (a) to add Cleveland vehicles to the Lorvex agreement under § 2.4 (14 days' notice + initial inspection at $195/vehicle), or (b) to arrange separate refrigeration maintenance coverage. Without coverage, there is a food-safety and operational risk at the new site, and no contractual recourse if refrigeration equipment fails.

This is a separate vendor issue, but it is directly triggered by the Zousen Side Letter and should be addressed as part of the Cleveland site go-live.

**Recommended fix:**

- If yes, issue a written notice under Lorvex § 2.4 to add the vehicles (14 days' lead time + initial inspection). Budget for the additional monthly fees ($425/truck or $260/van per month) and the one-time inspection fee ($195/vehicle).
- If the Cleveland site uses a different maintenance provider, ensure that provider is in place before the site goes live.

**Cannot resolve from documents alone:** Whether Cleveland has a vehicle fleet; if so, how many vehicles and what types.

## Issue 6: Data Export Window May Be Insufficient for Transition

**Where:** Agreement § 5.4

**Analysis:**

Section 5.4 provides that for **60 days** after termination or expiry, Zousen shall make Licensee Data available for export in a standard machine-readable format, after which it will be deleted (subject to backup retention).

If the Subscription Term ends 31 January 2027 (non-renewal effective), the export window closes **1 March 2027**. If the Ostrander merger closes around that time, Praidein/Ostrander may be juggling integration work, personnel changes, and the data export simultaneously.

**Why it matters:**

Licensee Data includes menu specifications, recipes, airline-contract pricing, meal-load plans, production volumes, and galley configurations. Losing access to this data without a complete export would be operationally devastating. The 60-day window is workable but leaves no margin for error, particularly if there are disputes about the format or completeness of the export.

**Recommended fix:**

- Begin planning the data export well before the term end date. Identify all data categories, confirm the "standard machine-readable format" Zousen will provide, and test the export process during the current term (e.g., request a sample export now).
- If negotiating a resolution to the non-renewal dispute, seek an extension of the export window (e.g., 90 or 120 days) as part of any settlement.
- Ensure the Ostrander integration team is aware of the data dependency and the hard deadline.

## Issue 7: Fee Acceleration on Change of Control Termination Is Uncapped

**Where:** Agreement § 15.3 (last sentence); Agreement § 10.2 (liability cap)

**Analysis:**

Section 15.3 provides that if a Change of Control occurs without consent and Licensor terminates, "all Fees for the remainder of the Subscription Term shall be accelerated and become immediately due and payable." Section 10.3 carves out "Licensee's payment obligations" from the liability cap in § 10.2. Accelerated fees are payment obligations. Therefore, the fee acceleration is **not subject to the 12-month fee cap** in § 10.2.

**Why it matters:**

In the worst case (non-renewal ineffective + CoC without consent + termination), Praidein could owe the full 24 months of renewal-term fees immediately: approximately $175,200 × 2 + $58,200 × 2 = **$466,800**, plus any overage fees. This is a significant, uncollateralized liability that could survive the merger and become Ostrander's problem.

**Recommended fix:**

- This is best addressed by obtaining CoC consent (Issue 2) or by negotiating a cap on fee acceleration (e.g., limited to 6 months' fees or to fees actually incurred through the termination date).
- In the Merger Agreement, ensure that Ostrander is aware of this exposure and that the purchase price or indemnity structure accounts for it.

## Issue 8: Licensor's Right to Increase Fees on Renewal

**Where:** Agreement § 4.5

**Analysis:**

If the Subscription Term renews (because the non-renewal notice is ineffective), Zousen may increase fees by up to 5% on the next renewal by giving 150 days' notice before the end of the then-current term. The 5% cap is per renewal cycle.

**Why it matters:**

Over a 24-month renewal term, this is a one-time 5% increase at the start. On $175,200 + $58,200 = $233,400, a 5% increase is approximately $11,670 per year. Not enormous, but it compounds the lock-in effect of Issue 1.

**Recommended fix:**

- If negotiating a resolution to the non-renewal dispute, seek a fee-freeze provision for any renewal term.
- Lower priority than Issues 1–3.

## Issue 9: No Service-Level Credits for Downtime

**Where:** Agreement § 3.2

**Analysis:**

Section 3.2 obligates Zousen to use "commercially reasonable efforts" to make the Software available 99.5% of the time each calendar month. However, there is no service-level credit, fee reduction, or termination right if the availability target is missed. The only remedy for a failure of the Software to perform in accordance with Documentation is the warranty in § 7.2 (correct within 30 days or terminate the affected Order Form with refund).

**Why it matters:**

For an airline caterer, Software downtime directly impacts operational readiness: tray-assembly sequencing, meal-load forecasting, and galley planning all feed into time-critical flight operations. A month with less than 99.5% availability (roughly 3.6 hours of downtime) could cause missed flight loads. Praidein has no contractual remedy short of a full warranty claim for non-conformity.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a service-level credit schedule (e.g., 5% of monthly fees per hour of downtime beyond the 99.5% threshold, capped at 50% of monthly fees).
- Add a termination right if availability falls below 95% in any month or below 97% in two consecutive months.
- This is a standard ask in SaaS agreements and Zousen should be receptive, particularly if it wants to preserve the relationship through the merger.

## Issue 10: Lorvex Agreement Assignment Clause Highlights Zousen Asymmetry

**Where:** Lorvex Agreement § 11.2; Agreement § 15.3; Order Form § 7

**Analysis:**

The Lorvex agreement permits either party to assign to an Affiliate or "a successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets, whether by merger, equity sale, asset sale or otherwise" without the other party's consent (Lorvex § 11.2). This means that when Ostrander acquires Praidein, the Lorvex agreement transfers automatically to Ostrander (or to Praidein as a subsidiary of Ostrander) with no consent required.

By contrast, the Zousen agreement requires consent for any Change of Control of Licensee (§ 15.3) and permits Licensor to assign freely (Order Form § 7).

**Why it matters:**

This asymmetry means that the Ostrander merger creates a consent problem only with Zousen. Praidein's other vendor relationships (at least Lorvex) are unaffected. This isolates Zousen as the critical vendor to resolve before closing.

**Recommended fix:**

- No direct fix in the Lorvex agreement needed. The point is to confirm to the client that Zousen is the only vendor relationship requiring pre-closing action in connection with the merger.
- Check whether any other vendor agreements contain Change of Control or assignment restrictions (I do not have those documents).
