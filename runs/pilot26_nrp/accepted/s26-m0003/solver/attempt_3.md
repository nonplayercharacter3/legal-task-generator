## SUMMARY

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA), Bill of Sale, Seller Disclosure Schedule, Side Letter, and Newark Office Lease. I identify fourteen issues, four of which I recommend be resolved before signing. The most dangerous gaps are: (1) the Nimbara Cloud consent—the Company's largest customer at 38% of revenue—is not a closing condition and Seller has no contractual obligation to obtain it; (2) the Kelvara Fluids price-lock extension option deadline has almost certainly already passed, making the Side Letter covenant to exercise it before Closing impossible to perform; (3) the Scheduled Refrigerant Matter indemnity likely expires twelve months post-closing while the underlying Consent Order obligations run through June 2029; and (4) the Side Letter strips Fundamental Representations of their uncapped status.

## TIER 1 – RESOLVE BEFORE SIGNING

### Issue 1: Nimbara Cloud Change-of-Control Consent Is Not a Closing Condition

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Required Consents"): "the consents, approvals and notices listed in Schedule 6.4 of the Disclosure Schedule."
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 6.4: lists only the Ostrander Park Associates (Newark Lease) consent.
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.4, Item 1: discloses that the Nimbara Cloud Master Supply Agreement "requires the prior written consent of Nimbara to any change of control of the Company. If that consent is not obtained, Nimbara may terminate the agreement and claim service credits."
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.10(a), Item 1: confirms Nimbara is the Company's largest contract, accounting for 38% of FY2025 revenue, with a term through March 1, 2029.
- SPA § 6.4(c): closing condition that "all Required Consents shall have been obtained."
- SPA § 5.5: Seller's pre-closing covenant to use "reasonable best efforts" to obtain "all Required Consents."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Because the Nimbara consent is disclosed in Schedule 4.4 but omitted from Schedule 6.4, it is not a "Required Consent." Consequently: (a) it is not a condition to Buyer's obligation to close under § 6.4(c); (b) Seller has no affirmative covenant under § 5.5 to obtain it; and (c) the deal can close with Seller in no breach, leaving Starev to inherit a company whose largest customer (38% of revenue) holds an unexercised termination right. If Nimbara terminates post-closing, the loss would fall on Starev with no indemnification trigger (the rep in § 4.10 that Material Contracts are "valid, binding and in full force" would not be breached by a consent right that has not yet been exercised).

**Recommendation:**
Add the Nimbara consent to Schedule 6.4 as a Required Consent. This makes it a closing condition under § 6.4(c) and triggers Seller's "reasonable best efforts" obligation under § 5.5. As a fallback, add a specific closing condition in § 6.4 that the Nimbara consent has been obtained or that Nimbara has confirmed in writing it will not exercise its termination right. Additionally, consider requiring Seller to deliver, as a closing deliverable under § 3.2, evidence of Nimbara's written consent.

If Seller resists making this a hard closing condition (perhaps arguing Nimbara is unlikely to withhold consent), at minimum require: (a) a specific covenant in § 5.5 that Seller will use reasonable best efforts to obtain the Nimbara consent before Closing; (b) a representation that, to Seller's Knowledge, Nimbara has no intention of withholding consent or terminating; and (c) an extension of the survival period for the § 4.10 Material Contracts representation to cover the full term of the Nimbara agreement.

### Issue 2: Kelvara Fluids Price-Lock Extension Option Deadline Has Likely Already Passed

**Documents and sections:**
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.11, Item 3: the Kelvara Fluids Supply Agreement Initial Term expires December 29, 2026. The Company "must deliver written notice of exercise to Kelvara not less than ninety (90) days before the expiration of the Initial Term."
- SPA: dated October 6, 2026.
- Side Letter § 2.1: "Before the Closing, Seller shall cause the Company to exercise the price-lock extension option."
- Side Letter § 2.3: classifies the Kelvara obligation as a pre-closing covenant subject to § 6.4(b).

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Ninety days before December 29, 2026 is approximately September 30, 2026. The SPA was signed on October 6, 2026—six days after the contractual deadline. If the Company did not deliver notice to Kelvara by September 30, 2026, the option has lapsed. The Side Letter then requires Seller to perform an impossible covenant before Closing. Under § 6.4(b), Seller's failure to perform would prevent the condition from being satisfied, giving Buyer the right to terminate under § 10.1(c). But Starev presumably wants to close.

If the option has lapsed, the Kelvara agreement continues month-to-month at Kelvara's "then-current list pricing" after the Initial Term, terminable by either party on 30 days' notice (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.11, Item 3). Dielectric immersion fluid is a critical single-source input for the Company's core product. Loss of the Locked Price exposes Starev to uncontrolled pricing on a key component, and the 30-day termination right gives Kelvara significant leverage.

**Facts needed from client:**
- Did the Company deliver the exercise notice to Kelvara on or before September 30, 2026? If yes, obtain a copy. If no, confirm whether Kelvara has waived the deadline or whether the Company has any other basis to claim the option remains available.
- What is the spread between the Locked Price and Kelvara's current list pricing?

**Recommendation:**
If the option has lapsed and cannot be revived:
- Negotiate a specific indemnity from Seller for the incremental cost of dielectric fluid above the Locked Price for the 24-month period the option would have covered, or
- Require Seller to use best efforts to negotiate a new price-lock or extension with Kelvara before Closing, with a specific closing condition that the Company has a supply agreement with Kelvara on terms no less favorable than the Locked Price for a minimum of 12 months, or
- Adjust the Purchase Price downward to reflect the loss of the price lock.

If the option is still available (e.g., Kelvara agreed to extend the deadline), require delivery of the exercise notice and Kelvara's written acknowledgment as a closing deliverable under § 3.2, and add a specific closing condition in § 6.4.

In all cases, the Side Letter § 2.3 classification of this as a § 6.4(b) covenant should be retained so that failure to perform gives Buyer a walk-right.

### Issue 3: Scheduled Refrigerant Matter Indemnity Survival Is Inadequate for the Consent Order Timeline

**Documents and sections:**
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.15, Item 1: the DNREC Consent Order requires remediation and monitoring "through June 30, 2029." Estimated remaining compliance costs are $3,900,000, with "most of that amount relates to remediation completion and monitoring to be performed in 2027 through 2029."
- SPA § 9.2(d): Seller indemnifies Buyer for "the Scheduled Refrigerant Matter, including all costs of investigation, remediation, monitoring and reporting, all fines and penalties, and all costs of complying with any consent order relating to it, whether incurred before or after the Closing."
- SPA § 9.1(a): representations and warranties survive 12 months after the Closing Date (except Fundamental Reps and Tax reps).
- SPA § 9.1(b): "The covenants and agreements of Seller in Sections 9.2(b) through 9.2(d) shall survive the Closing until the expiration of the survival period of the representation and warranty to which such covenant relates."
- SPA § 9.1(c): covenants to be performed after Closing survive until fully performed.
- SPA § 4.15: the Environmental Matters representation (the rep to which § 9.2(d) relates), which survives 12 months under § 9.1(a).

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Section 9.1(b) explicitly references § 9.2(d) and ties its survival to "the survival period of the representation and warranty to which such covenant relates." The related representation is § 4.15 (Environmental Matters), which survives 12 months under § 9.1(a). The expected Closing Date is November 20, 2026, so the § 9.2(d) indemnity would expire approximately November 20, 2027.

However, the Consent Order obligations—and the bulk of the $3.9M in estimated remaining costs—run through June 30, 2029. This creates a gap of approximately 19 months during which the Company will incur significant costs for which Seller's indemnity has expired. Section 9.1(c) (survival until fully performed) could arguably apply, but § 9.1(b) is more specific because it expressly names § 9.2(d). Under the specific-over-general canon, § 9.1(b) likely controls, and the indemnity expires at 12 months.

Section 9.1(d) (claims asserted before expiration survive until resolved) provides partial protection: if Buyer asserts a claim in writing before month 12, it survives until resolved. But this does not help for costs that are first incurred and first become claimable after month 12.

**Recommendation:**
Amend § 9.1(b) to provide that the § 9.2(d) Scheduled Refrigerant Matter indemnity survives until the later of (a) the expiration of the Consent Order (June 30, 2029) or (b) 60 days after the final monitoring report is accepted by DNREC. Alternatively, add a standalone survival provision: "Notwithstanding Section 9.1(b), the indemnification obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(d) shall survive until June 30, 2029, or such later date as all obligations under the Consent Order are fully performed and accepted by DNREC."

This should be non-negotiable given that the Consent Order is a disclosed, quantified, ongoing obligation with a known end date.

### Issue 4: Side Letter Strips Fundamental Representations of Uncapped Status

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 9.5(a): "The aggregate amount of all Losses for which Seller is liable under Section 9.2(a) shall not exceed 10% of the Purchase Price ($18,400,000) (the 'Cap')."
- SPA § 9.5(b): "The Cap shall not apply to Losses arising from Fraud, breach of Fundamental Representations, and the Section 9.2(d) Scheduled Refrigerant Matter indemnity."
- SPA § 1.1: "Fundamental Representations" means §§ 4.1 (Organization), 4.2 (Authority), 4.3 (Capitalization), 4.5 (Title to Shares), and 4.17 (Brokers).
- Side Letter § 3: "Seller and Buyer agree that the exclusions from the Cap under Section 9.5(b) of the Agreement are limited solely to Fraud. Sections 9.5(a) and 9.5(c) through 9.5(e) of the Agreement continue to apply in accordance with their terms."
- SPA § 11.11 (Order of Precedence): "first, the Side Letter; second, this Agreement."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Because the Side Letter prevails over the SPA under § 11.11, the effective § 9.5(b) excludes only Fraud from the Cap. Breaches of Fundamental Representations—particularly § 4.5 (Title to Shares) and § 4.3 (Capitalization)—are now subject to the $18.4M Cap and the $1.38M deductible (since § 9.5(c) excludes only Fundamental Reps and Fraud from the deductible, and the Side Letter does not modify § 9.5(c), the deductible still does not apply to Fundamental Reps—confirm: § 9.5(c) says "other than for breaches of Fundamental Representations or Fraud," so Fundamental Reps are still excluded from the deductible).

The practical impact: if it emerges post-closing that Seller did not have clean title to the Shares (e.g., a undisclosed pledge, a prior buyer's claim, or a capitalization defect), Starev's recovery is capped at $18.4M against an $184M investment. In a worst-case title failure, this cap is grossly inadequate.

Note: the reference to § 9.2(d) in the original § 9.5(b) was largely redundant because the Cap in § 9.5(a) applies only to § 9.2(a) losses. The Side Letter's deletion of the § 9.2(d) reference does not practically change the environmental indemnity's cap status. The meaningful change is the loss of uncapped treatment for Fundamental Reps.

**Recommendation:**
Delete Side Letter § 3 in its entirety, restoring the SPA's original § 9.5(b) with all three exclusions (Fraud, Fundamental Reps, and § 9.2(d)). If Seller insists on some modification, the minimum acceptable position is to retain the Fundamental Representations exclusion from the Cap. The Title to Shares rep (§ 4.5) is the one Starev cannot afford to have capped: a title failure goes to the heart of the transaction.

If this point cannot be resolved before signing, consider increasing the Escrow Amount to provide additional security against Fundamental Rep breaches, or adding a specific condition that Seller delivers a bring-down certificate at Closing confirming title is free and clear.

## TIER 2 – SIGNIFICANT ISSUES TO ADDRESS IN NEGOTIATION

### Issue 5: Bill of Sale Governed by New York Law; Inconsistent with SPA

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 11.8(a): Delaware governing law.
- Bill of Sale § 6.1: "governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of New York."
- Bill of Sale § 6.2: exclusive jurisdiction in New York County courts.
- SPA § 11.11: "the Bill of Sale shall control with respect to any matter relating to the conveyance of the Contributed Assets."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The Contributed Assets (three U.S. patents, one European patent validated in four countries, one PCT application, and all design files for the CIT product lines) are the core IP of the Company's immersion-tank business. The Bill of Sale controls over the SPA for IP conveyance matters, and it subjects those matters to New York law and New York courts. IP Holdings is a Delaware LLC; the Company is a Delaware corporation; the SPA is Delaware-governed. There is no apparent connection to New York.

This creates a risk of inconsistent interpretation (e.g., what constitutes "free and clear of all encumbrances" or what "further assurances" require) and forces Starev to litigate IP conveyance disputes in New York rather than Delaware, where the rest of the deal is anchored.

**Recommendation:**
Amend Bill of Sale §§ 6.1 and 6.2 to Delaware governing law and Delaware jurisdiction, consistent with the SPA. If Seller resists (perhaps IP Holdings has a New York counsel relationship), at minimum add a provision that in the event of a conflict between the Bill of Sale and the SPA on a matter of interpretation (as opposed to a matter of conveyance mechanics), the SPA's Delaware law governs.

### Issue 6: IP Holdings Is Not a Party to the SPA; Structural Enforcement Gap

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA Recital B: IP Holdings "will cause IP Holdings to convey those assets to the Company at the Closing."
- SPA § 4.2: represents IP Holdings has authority to execute the Bill of Sale.
- SPA § 5.4: "Seller shall cause IP Holdings to execute and deliver the Bill of Sale."
- SPA § 3.2(b): Bill of Sale is a closing deliverable.
- Bill of Sale: executed by IP Holdings (Assignor) and the Company only.

**Why it matters to Starev:**
IP Holdings is an Affiliate of Seller but is not a party to the SPA and has no direct obligation to Buyer. If IP Holdings fails to execute the Bill of Sale, fails to deliver prosecution files (Bill of Sale § 2.3), or fails to cooperate on inventor declarations (Bill of Sale § 4.3), Buyer's only recourse is against Seller under the indemnification provisions. Given Issue 4 (Fundamental Reps now subject to the Cap), and the fact that a failure to deliver the Bill of Sale would be a breach of a covenant (§ 9.2(b), not subject to the Cap but subject to the overall $184M limit in § 9.5(d)), the remedy is adequate in theory but requires litigation against Seller rather than direct enforcement against IP Holdings.

**Recommendation:**
Require IP Holdings to execute a joinder or direct agreement acknowledging its obligations under the Bill of Sale and agreeing to be bound by the SPA's dispute resolution provisions. Alternatively, add IP Holdings as a guarantor of Seller's obligations under § 5.4. At minimum, require delivery of a certified resolution of IP Holdings authorizing the conveyance as a closing deliverable under § 3.2.

### Issue 7: Escrow Amount May Be Inadequate Given Known Liabilities

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 2.2(a): Escrow Amount is $9,200,000 (5% of Purchase Price).
- SPA § 9.5(a): Cap is $18,400,000 (10% of Purchase Price).
- SPA § 9.6: Buyer must first seek recovery of § 9.2(a) losses from the Escrow Amount.
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.15: estimated remaining environmental costs of $3,900,000.

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The escrow covers only half the Cap. For § 9.2(a) claims (rep breaches), after the $1.38M deductible, the first $9.2M of losses come from escrow, and any amount between $9.2M and $18.4M must be collected from Seller directly. If Seller distributes the remaining Purchase Price ($174.8M) to its members post-closing, collecting on the excess becomes a credit risk.

The $3.9M in known environmental costs (even though not subject to the Cap or the escrow under § 9.6, which applies only to § 9.2(a)) reduces the practical headroom in the escrow for other rep breaches.

**Recommendation:**
Increase the Escrow Amount to at least $18.4M (the full Cap) or, alternatively, extend the escrow release period from the current terms (which I note are in the Escrow Agreement, not provided in the folder—confirm the release timing) to at least 18 months post-closing to cover the 12-month survival period plus the 90-day working capital adjustment period. If Seller resists a larger escrow, require a parent guarantee from Seller's ultimate parent (if any) or a letter of credit for the difference between the escrow and the Cap.

### Issue 8: Company Employees Participate in a Seller-Affiliated Health and Welfare Plan

**Documents and sections:**
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.14, Item 2: "Drasiv Industrial Group, LLC Health and Welfare Plan, in which Company employees participate."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Post-closing, the Company's employees will be covered under a plan sponsored by Seller (or its affiliate). This creates several issues: (a) the plan may need to be amended to remove the Company as a participating entity, which could trigger COBRA or other continuation obligations; (b) there may be accrued but unpaid employer contributions; (c) the plan document may contain provisions that are unfavorable to the Company (e.g., cost allocation, governance); and (d) the transition to a Buyer-affiliated plan must occur without a gap in coverage, or employees may lose benefits and file ERISA claims.

The SPA's § 4.14 representation that plans are "maintained in all material respects in compliance with its terms and with applicable law" does not address the transition mechanics or the allocation of pre-closing vs. post-closing costs.

**Facts needed from client:**
- How many Company employees participate in the Drasiv plan?
- What is the annual cost of the plan attributable to Company employees?
- Does the plan document permit a partial termination or spin-off for the Company's participants?
- What is the plan's fiscal year and contribution schedule?

**Recommendation:**
Add a specific covenant in Article V requiring Seller to, before Closing: (a) deliver a plan-by-plan transition plan for the Drasiv Health and Welfare Plan participants; (b) confirm in writing that all employer contributions through the Closing Date have been made; and (c) cooperate in good faith to effect a clean separation or spin-off of Company participants. Add a representation that no plan amendment, termination, or cutback has been adopted or proposed that would affect Company participants. Allocate any transition costs (COBRA, administrative separation fees) to Seller as a pre-closing expense in the working capital calculation.

### Issue 9: No Representation Regarding Adequacy of Insurance Coverage

**Documents and sections:**
- Newark Lease § 9.1: requires the Company to maintain, among other policies, pollution legal liability insurance of not less than $2,000,000 per claim.
- SPA Article IV: contains no representation regarding the Company's insurance coverage.
- SPA § 9.5(e): "Losses shall be calculated net of any insurance proceeds actually received by the Indemnified Party in respect of them, less the costs of recovery."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The Company has been the subject of an HFC release and a DNREC Consent Order. Its pollution legal liability insurer may have reserved rights, subrogated, or even cancelled coverage. If the Company's insurance is inadequate or in dispute, two problems arise: (a) the Company may be in default under the Newark Lease (which requires maintaining the specified policies); and (b) the § 9.5(e) insurance offset means that any indemnity recovery is reduced by insurance proceeds "actually received"—but if coverage is disputed, the timing and amount of proceeds is uncertain.

**Recommendation:**
Add a representation in Article IV that: (a) the Company maintains all insurance required under its Material Contracts (including the Newark Lease); (b) no insurer has given notice of cancellation, non-renewal, or reservation of rights with respect to any policy covering the Company; and (c) all premiums through the Closing Date have been paid. Require delivery of certificates of insurance as a closing deliverable under § 3.2.

## TIER 3 – ADDITIONAL ISSUES TO RAISE

### Issue 10: Nimbara Most-Favored-Customer Pricing Provision

**Documents and sections:**
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.10(a), Item 1: "Section 7.4 of the agreement contains a most-favored-customer pricing provision for immersion-tank units."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
A most-favored-customer (MFC) clause can ratchet the Company's pricing: if the Company gives a better price to any other customer, it must extend that price to Nimbara. This constrains Starev's post-closing pricing strategy and could erode margins on the Company's other customer relationships (Halverin, Corvanta, and others). The full text of § 7.4 is not in the folder.

**Facts needed from client:**
- What is the scope of the MFC provision (product line, geographic, customer class)?
- Has the Company ever been required to extend a price to Nimbara under this provision?
- What is the revenue impact in a typical year?

**Recommendation:**
Request the full text of Nimbara MSA § 7.4 from the data room. If the provision is broad, consider requiring a specific representation that no MFC price adjustment has been triggered in the past 24 months and that the Company is not aware of any pending claim. Factor the MFC constraint into the valuation model.

### Issue 11: No Representation on Backlog or Revenue Pipeline

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA Article IV: no representation addressing the Company's backlog, unfulfilled orders, or sales pipeline.
- SPA § 4.10: represents Material Contracts are valid and in full force, but does not address expected future performance.

**Why it matters to Starev:**
Starev is paying $184M for a company whose revenue is heavily concentrated in Nimbara (38%) and a handful of other contracts. The financial statements cover through August 31, 2026. There is no representation about the status of the Company's order book, pending proposals, or expected Q4 2026 revenue. If the Company has lost significant pipeline between August 31 and Closing, the "no MAE" rep (§ 4.7) may not be triggered unless the loss is truly material and adverse.

**Recommendation:**
Add a representation that, since August 31, 2026, the Company has not lost any material customer or had any material reduction in its backlog or pipeline. Alternatively, require delivery of a backlog report as of a date within 10 days of Closing as a condition under § 6.4.

### Issue 12: Working Capital "Peg" Mechanism Lacks a Fixed Target

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 2.3(a): Seller delivers a "good-faith estimate" of Closing Working Capital at least 3 Business Days before Closing.
- SPA § 2.3(c): adjustment is based on the difference between the estimate and the final determination.

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The "peg" mechanism uses Seller's estimate as the target. Seller has an incentive to set the estimate low (reducing the working capital it must leave in the company) or high (increasing the amount Buyer must pay). The "good-faith" and "reasonable supporting detail" qualifiers provide some protection but are difficult to enforce. The 3-business-day delivery window is very short for Buyer to review and object.

**Recommendation:**
Either (a) agree on a fixed target working capital number in the SPA (a "collar" or "target" mechanism) so that both parties know the number before Closing, or (b) extend the delivery deadline for the estimate to at least 10 Business Days before Closing and give Buyer the right to object to the estimate if it deviates more than 5% from the trailing four-quarter average working capital.

### Issue 13: Termination Drop-Dead Date May Be Inadequate

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 10.1(b): either party may terminate if Closing has not occurred by January 15, 2027.
- SPA § 3.1: scheduled Closing is November 20, 2026.

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The drop-dead date provides approximately 2 months after the scheduled Closing. Given the issues identified above (Nimbara consent, Kelvara option, environmental survival negotiation), the deal may require additional time. If the parties are still negotiating fixes to the Tier 1 issues in mid-December, the January 15 date creates pressure that could be used by Seller to extract concessions.

**Recommendation:**
Extend the drop-dead date to February 28, 2027, or tie it to the satisfaction of conditions (i.e., the later of January 15, 2027 or 30 days after all conditions are satisfied or waived). This is a low-priority ask but provides negotiating flexibility.

### Issue 14: Section 4.11 Supply Arrangements Representation Is Limited to "Written Notice"

**Documents and sections:**
- SPA § 4.11: "No such supplier has notified the Company in writing that it intends to terminate, materially reduce or adversely modify its supply relationship with the Company."

**Why it matters to Starev:**
The representation is qualified by "in writing." If a supplier has expressed concerns orally, through its sales force, or by simply reducing allocation without formal notice, the rep is not breached. Given the Kelvara situation (Issue 2) and the criticality of dielectric fluid supply, this qualifier is too narrow.

**Recommendation:**
Broaden § 4.11 to cover any notice (written or oral) or any action by a supplier that constitutes a material reduction in supply, increase in price, or deterioration in service levels. Add: "and no such supplier has taken any action that would reasonably be expected to result in a material adverse effect on the Company's ability to source critical components on terms no less favorable than those in effect as of the date of this Agreement."

## ORDER OF PRECEDENCE NOTE

SPA § 11.11 establishes: (1) Side Letter, (2) SPA, (3) Disclosure Schedule, (4) other Ancillary Documents, with the Bill of Sale controlling on Contributed Assets matters. Several of the issues above (particularly Issues 1, 2, and 4) arise from the interaction of the Side Letter with the SPA and the Disclosure Schedule. Any amendments should be made in the Side Letter (to preserve its precedence) or in the SPA itself, with conforming changes to the Disclosure Schedule. Do not attempt to fix these issues by amending only the Disclosure Schedule, as it has the lowest precedence among the three.
