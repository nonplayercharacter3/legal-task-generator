## Summary

Review of the six deal documents reveals several material issues that leave Kiveth Interactive, Inc. ("Kiveth" or "Buyer") exposed. The most critical is that the Character License Agreement from Vantrell Comics Group cannot be assigned to Kiveth without Vantrell's sole-discretion consent—because Kiveth is controlled by a console manufacturer—and that consent is not listed as a Required Consent, meaning Kiveth has no walk-right if Vantrell refuses. Additional significant issues include a conflict between the Escrow Agreement and the APA that effectively caps all recovery (including for fraud) at the $4.85M escrow, a missed non-renewal deadline on the Lumen Engine License that locks Kiveth into $1.8M annual minimums for three years, an unreasonably short 90-day survival period for IP representations, and an uncapped indemnity running from Kiveth to Brennt for IP claims.

## Issue 1: Character License Agreement Cannot Be Assigned to Kiveth Without Vantrell's Sole-Discretion Consent; No Walk-Right

**Where:** APA §4.6; APA §2.1(c); APA §2.7(a); APA §7.2(c); Disclosure Schedule §3.10(b) Item 1 (Assignment, Section 14.2); Disclosure Schedule §7.2(c) (Schedule 7.2(c))

**The problem.** APA §4.6 represents that Kiveth "is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Pellucid Systems Co., the manufacturer of the Pellucid video game console." The Character License Agreement ("CLA"), listed as Assigned Contract No. 2 on Disclosure Schedule §2.1(c), contains the following assignment restriction in Section 14.2 (summarized at Disclosure Schedule §3.10(b), Item 1):

> "any assignment or transfer of this Agreement, including by sale of assets, merger, change of control or operation of law, to a Person that is a Hardware Platform Holder or is Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder requires Vantrell's prior written consent. Vantrell may grant or withhold that consent in its sole discretion. Any assignment or transfer made without a required consent is void and is a material breach, entitling Vantrell to terminate under Section 10.3."

"Hardware Platform Holder" is defined as "any Person that manufactures, markets or distributes, under its own brand, a video game console, handheld gaming device or other dedicated gaming hardware platform." "Controlled" means possession of "more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities of a Person, or of the power to direct its management and policies."

Pellucid Systems Co. manufactures the Pellucid video game console and holds 100% of Kiveth (indirectly). Kiveth is therefore "Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder" within the meaning of the CLA. Assignment of the CLA to Kiveth requires Vantrell's prior written consent, which Vantrell may withhold in its sole discretion.

Critically, Disclosure Schedule §7.2(c) lists only one Required Consent: the Lumen Engine License consent. The Vantrell consent is not listed. Because it is not a Required Consent, APA §7.2(c) does not give Kiveth a walk-right if Vantrell refuses. Under APA §2.7(a), if the consent is not obtained, "the Closing shall nevertheless proceed. That Purchased Asset shall not be assigned or transferred at the Closing, and Seller shall continue to hold it, with no adjustment to the Purchase Price."

**Why it matters.** The CLA grants the exclusive worldwide license to use the Licensed Properties—Seraphine Vael, the Ashen Warden, Corvin Tallow and the Ninefold Choir—in interactive video games (Disclosure Schedule §3.10(b), Item 1, Grant, Section 2). These are the central characters of the Ashfall Requiem franchise. Without the CLA, Kiveth cannot legally develop, publish or commercialize any Ashfall Requiem Title. Kiveth would pay $48.5M for a franchise it cannot exploit. There is no price adjustment, no specific indemnity for this gap, and no termination right.

The prior draft (APA Prior Draft §2.7(a)) contained the language "subject to Section 7.2(c)," which at least preserved the theoretical interaction with the Required Consent condition. The execution version deleted that qualifier and added "with no adjustment to the Purchase Price," making the lack of protection explicit.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Add the Vantrell consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent, giving Kiveth a walk-right under §7.2(c) if Vantrell refuses.
2. Alternatively (or additionally), require Seller to obtain Vantrell's consent as a condition to Closing, with the economic terms adjusted if consent is not obtained (e.g., reduction in Purchase Price proportional to the value of the CLA, or termination right with return of any deposits).
3. If the business decision is to proceed regardless, Kiveth should require Seller to negotiate a consent from Vantrell before signing, or to provide a specific indemnity (uncapped, outside the escrow) for Losses arising from the inability to assign the CLA.
4. Consider whether the "Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder" restriction could be addressed structurally (e.g., acquisition through a subsidiary that is not controlled by Pellucid, though this would raise its own issues given §4.6).

## Issue 2: Escrow Agreement §6.2 Makes the Escrow Fund the "Sole and Exclusive Source of Recovery" for All Indemnification Claims, Overriding the APA's Uncapped Provisions

**Where:** Escrow Agreement §6.2; APA §9.5(a)–(b); APA §9.6; APA §11.9

**The problem.** Escrow Agreement §6.2 provides:

> "The Escrow Fund shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery for all Losses of the Buyer Indemnified Parties under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement."

APA §9.5(b) expressly carves certain categories out of the $4.85M cap: Fraud, breaches of Fundamental Representations (capped at the full Purchase Price of $48.5M), and Excluded Liabilities (uncapped). APA §9.6 confirms that losses "not subject to Section 9.5(a)" are to be "paid by Seller directly to Buyer by wire transfer."

However, APA §11.9 (Order of Precedence) states: "with respect to matters relating to the Escrow Fund and the satisfaction of Losses from the Escrow Fund, the Escrow Agreement shall control."

Read together, the Escrow Agreement's "sole and exclusive source of recovery" language, which controls under §11.9, effectively limits Kiveth's recovery for ALL Article IX claims—including Fraud and Fundamental Representation breaches—to the $4.85M Escrow Fund. This nullifies the APA's uncapped and Purchase-Price-capped provisions for those categories.

**Why it matters.** If Seller commits fraud in its representations (e.g., concealing a material IP defect or a pending litigation), Kiveth's maximum recovery would be $4.85M rather than the full $48.5M Purchase Price or unlimited (for fraud). Similarly, if Seller lacks title to the Purchased Assets (a Fundamental Representation under §3.4), Kiveth's recovery is capped at the escrow rather than the Purchase Price.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Escrow Agreement §6.2 to provide that the Escrow Fund is the sole and exclusive source of recovery only for Losses subject to the cap in APA §9.5(a). For Losses excluded from the cap under APA §9.5(b) (Fraud, Fundamental Representation breaches, Excluded Liabilities), the Escrow Fund shall be the first source of recovery but shall not limit Kiveth's right to seek additional amounts directly from Seller. Alternatively, delete "sole and exclusive" and replace with "first" source of recovery, consistent with APA §9.6.

## Issue 3: Lumen Engine License Non-Renewal Notice Deadline Has Already Passed; Kiveth Will Be Locked Into $1.8M Annual Minimums for Three Years

**Where:** APA §6.8; Disclosure Schedule §3.10(a) Item 1 (Term and Renewal, Section 13.1; Royalties, Section 6 and Amendment No. 1(b))

**The problem.** APA §6.8 requires Seller to "deliver to Lumen Engine Technologies, Inc. written notice of non-renewal of the Lumen Engine License, in accordance with the notice provisions of the Lumen Engine License and prior to the deadline for delivery of such notice under it."

Per the Disclosure Schedule (§3.10(a), Item 1, Term and Renewal), the Lumen Engine License "renews automatically for successive three-year renewal terms unless either party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 120 days prior to the expiration of the then-current term." The current term ends December 31, 2026. One hundred twenty days before December 31, 2026 is approximately September 2, 2026.

The Agreement Date is September 21, 2026. The non-renewal notice deadline passed 19 days before the parties signed the APA. Seller cannot deliver timely notice. The Lumen Engine License will automatically renew for a three-year term beginning January 1, 2027.

Upon renewal, per Disclosure Schedule §3.10(a), Item 1, Royalties (b): "For each contract year of any renewal term commencing on or after January 1, 2027, Licensee shall pay a minimum annual royalty guarantee of $1,800,000."

**Why it matters.** APA §6.8 states that "Buyer intends to migrate the Ashfall Requiem Titles to a proprietary engine following the Closing." Kiveth is therefore paying $48.5M for a business that will be contractually locked into the Lumen Engine for at least three years (January 2027 through December 2029) with a $1.8M minimum annual guarantee ($5.4M total), even if Kiveth migrates off the engine and the titles generate no Lumen-attributable revenue. The migration plan is undermined because Kiveth cannot avoid the minimums during the renewal term.

Additionally, because the notice deadline has passed, Seller is in technical breach of §6.8 as of the Agreement Date. This could give Kiveth a termination right under §8.1(c) (breach of a covenant that would cause a §7.2 condition to fail), but that is a blunt instrument for what is really an economic problem.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Require Seller to negotiate with Lumen for a waiver of the non-renewal deadline or a mutual early termination of the renewal term, as a condition to Closing or as a post-closing covenant with a specific indemnity for the $1.8M annual minimums if Lumen refuses.
2. Alternatively, reduce the Purchase Price by the present value of the $1.8M annual minimums for the forced renewal term (up to $5.4M, less any revenue that would otherwise have been generated).
3. If Lumen agrees to a shortened renewal term or a buyout, require that agreement as a closing deliverable.
4. At minimum, add a specific indemnity from Seller for all royalties and minimums payable under the Lumen Engine License during any renewal term that results from Seller's failure to deliver timely non-renewal notice.

## Issue 4: IP Representations Survive Only 90 Days Post-Closing

**Where:** APA §1.1 ("IP Survival Date"); APA §9.1(a)(ii); APA Prior Draft §9.1(a)

**The problem.** APA §1.1 defines "IP Survival Date" as "the date that is 90 days after the Closing Date." APA §9.1(a)(ii) provides that "the representations and warranties of Seller in Section 3.9 (Intellectual Property) survive until the IP Survival Date."

Section 3.9 contains the core IP representations: ownership of Seller IP (§3.9(b)), non-infringement (§3.9(c)), employee/contractor assignments (§3.9(d)), open source compliance (§3.9(f)), and trade secret protection (§3.9(g)). For a video game studio acquisition where IP is the primary asset, these are the most important representations in the agreement.

The prior draft (APA Prior Draft §9.1(a)) contained a single survival period of "[●] months" for all non-fundamental representations. The execution version introduced the 90-day IP-specific survival, which is dramatically shorter than the 15-month General Survival Date applicable to all other representations.

**Why it matters.** IP infringement claims, open source contamination issues, and gaps in employee assignment chains often surface only after the acquirer begins active development or commercialization. A 90-day window means that if a third party asserts a copyright or trade secret claim against the Ashfall Requiem code four months after closing, or if an open source licensing violation is discovered during a post-closing code audit, Kiveth has no indemnification recourse against Seller for breaches of §3.9. The claim time-barred 90 days post-closing.

This is particularly acute given Issue 1: if the CLA is not assigned (because Vantrell withholds consent), Kiveth may not discover the full scope of its IP gap until it attempts to develop Ashfall Requiem II, which is targeted for "second half of 2028" (Disclosure Schedule §2.1(a), Title 4). By then, the 90-day IP survival period will have long expired.

**Recommended fix.** Extend the IP Survival Date to no less than the General Survival Date (15 months), or ideally to the full statute of limitations for IP infringement claims. At minimum, extend to 12 months to cover a full development cycle for Ashfall Requiem II's vertical slice. If Seller resists, consider a tiered approach: 12 months for §3.9(b) (ownership) and §3.9(c) (non-infringement), and the General Survival Date for §3.9(d) (employee assignments) and §3.9(f) (open source).

## Issue 5: Brennt Key Employee Agreement §8.2 Imposes Uncapped IP Indemnification on Kiveth

**Where:** Brennt Agreement §8.2; APA §9.5(a)–(b)

**The problem.** Brennt Agreement §8.2 provides:

> "The Company shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Executive from and against any and all Claims arising out of or relating to the development of the Titles, including any Claim by a third party alleging that any Title, or any code, art asset, audio asset, narrative material, character, tool or other element of any Title, infringes, misappropriates or otherwise violates the Intellectual Property of that third party, in each case whether arising before or after the Effective Date and without limitation as to amount."

This is an uncapped, non-time-limited indemnity from Kiveth to Brennt for any IP claim relating to the Titles, including claims arising from pre-Closing development.

**Why it matters.** Kiveth is paying $48.5M for the Ashfall Requiem franchise, which includes titles released as far back as October 2023. If a third party brings an IP infringement claim against any Title (e.g., a copyright claim over art assets, a trade secret claim over code, or a patent claim over game mechanics), Kiveth must defend and indemnify Brennt without limitation. Meanwhile, Kiveth's own recourse against Seller for IP representation breaches is capped at $4.85M (APA §9.5(a)) and, per Issue 2, may be limited to the Escrow Fund even for fundamental breaches.

This creates an asymmetric risk: Kiveth has uncapped downside exposure to Brennt for IP claims, but capped (and potentially escrow-limited) upstream recovery from Seller. The net effect is that Kiveth bears the full economic risk of pre-Closing IP defects, with no pass-through to Seller beyond $4.85M.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Cap the §8.2 indemnity at a reasonable amount (e.g., the Purchase Price, or a multiple of Brennt's total compensation).
2. Add a carve-out for claims arising from Seller's breach of its IP representations under APA §3.9, so that Kiveth can pass through to Seller (or at least not be stuck with the net loss).
3. Require that the §8.2 indemnity be subject to the same procedural protections as APA §9.4 (notice, control of defense, settlement consent).
4. Ensure Kiveth maintains adequate IP infringement insurance (e.g., a media and entertainment liability policy) that covers both the Titles and Brennt, so the uncapped exposure is backstopped by insurance.

## Issue 6: No Price Adjustment or Walk-Right if Assigned Contracts Are Not Transferred at Closing

**Where:** APA §2.7(a); APA §7.2(c); Disclosure Schedule §7.2(c)

**The problem.** APA §2.7(a) provides that if a third-party consent is not obtained before Closing, "the Closing shall nevertheless proceed. That Purchased Asset shall not be assigned or transferred at the Closing, and Seller shall continue to hold it, with no adjustment to the Purchase Price."

The only Required Consent listed on Disclosure Schedule §7.2(c) is the Lumen Engine License consent. All other Assigned Contracts (the CLA, the Resonar Audio license, the Fracturelight SDK license, the platform agreements, and the other contracts on Schedule 3.10(c)) are not Required Consents. If any of them cannot be assigned (whether due to a consent requirement, a change-of-control provision, or any other reason), Kiveth closes and pays the full $48.5M without that asset, with no price adjustment.

**Why it matters.** While the CLA is the most obvious example (see Issue 1), the platform agreements (Disclosure Schedule §3.13) are also critical. They require "written notice to the platform holder of the transfer of publisher responsibility" and the platform holder "processes the transfer of product listings, developer accounts and entitlement records under its standard title transfer procedures" (Disclosure Schedule §3.3, Item 5). If a platform holder delays or refuses the transfer, Kiveth may not be able to receive revenue from the Titles on that platform, with no price adjustment and no walk-right.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Expand Schedule 7.2(c) to include all consents that are conditions to assignment of any Assigned Contract, not just the Lumen consent.
2. Add a price adjustment mechanism: if any Assigned Contract is not transferred at Closing, the Purchase Price is reduced by the fair market value of that contract (to be determined by an independent expert if disputed).
3. Add a materiality threshold: if the aggregate value of non-transferred Assigned Contracts exceeds a specified percentage of the Purchase Price (e.g., 5%), Kiveth has the right to terminate.

## Issue 7: APA §9.5(c) Deductible of $242,500 Reduces Effective Recovery

**Where:** APA §9.5(c); APA Prior Draft §9.5(c)

**The problem.** APA §9.5(c) provides that "Seller is not liable to the Buyer Indemnified Parties for indemnification under Section 9.2(a) until the aggregate amount of all Losses in respect of indemnification under Section 9.2(a) exceeds $242,500 (the 'Deductible'), in which event Seller is liable only for Losses in excess of the Deductible."

This is a true deductible (not a tipping basket): the first $242,500 of losses is borne entirely by Kiveth. The prior draft had this amount as "$[●]," indicating it was a negotiated point.

**Why it matters.** Combined with the $4.85M cap (§9.5(a)), Kiveth's effective recovery range for general representation breaches is $242,501 to $4,850,000. For a $48.5M transaction, the deductible represents 0.5% of the Purchase Price, which is within the market range (typically 0.5%–1%), but it means that smaller IP defects, employment claims, or contract breaches that aggregate to less than $242,500 provide no recovery at all.

This issue is less critical than the others but compounds them: if Kiveth faces multiple smaller IP issues (e.g., a gap in one employee assignment, a minor open source compliance issue), it may not reach the deductible threshold and would bear the full cost.

**Recommended fix.** Convert the deductible to a tipping basket (threshold): once aggregate losses exceed $242,500, Seller is liable for ALL losses from the first dollar, not just the excess. Alternatively, reduce the deductible to $100,000 (approximately 0.2% of Purchase Price).

## Issue 8: Brennt Agreement §6.4(b) – Low "Good Reason" Threshold Triggers Substantial Severance

**Where:** Brennt Agreement §6.4(b); Brennt Agreement §7.2

**The problem.** "Good Reason" under Brennt Agreement §6.4 includes "a material diminution in Executive's title, authority, duties or responsibilities, including her removal from creative leadership of the Titles." If Good Reason is triggered, Kiveth must pay under §7.2:

- 12 months Base Salary: $410,000
- Prorated Target Bonus: up to ~$205,000
- Unpaid Retention Bonus installments: up to $1,200,000
- Accelerated RSU vesting (12 months of the $1.5M award): up to ~$375,000
- COBRA premiums: ~$15,000–$30,000

Total potential exposure: approximately $2.2M–$2.2M+.

**Why it matters.** Post-acquisition integrations frequently involve reorganizing creative leadership. If Kiveth (or its parent, Pellucid) reassigns Brennt's role, reduces her authority over the Titles, or integrates the Ashfall Requiem team into a broader studio structure, Brennt could argue a "material diminution" and trigger Good Reason. The threshold is subjective ("material"), and the specific reference to "removal from creative leadership of the Titles" makes it easy to argue that any reduction in her franchise-specific role qualifies.

This is a retention risk: the very structure that is supposed to incentivize Brennt to stay (the Retention Bonus, the RSUs, the Franchise Performance Award tied to Ashfall Requiem II) is also the structure that gives her a low-cost exit with substantial severance if Kiveth reorganizes.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Narrow §6.4(b) to require that the diminution be both material AND not part of a bona fide reorganization affecting peers at the same level.
2. Add a requirement that the Good Reason condition continue to exist for a specified period (e.g., 90 days) before Brennt can resign, giving Kiveth time to cure.
3. Consider reducing the Retention Bonus acceleration: currently, ALL unpaid installments accelerate on Good Reason (§4.3). Limit acceleration to the installment that would have been paid in the next 12 months.

## Issue 9: APA §6.8 Requires Seller to Consult with Buyer on Lumen Transition, But No Obligation on Buyer to Fund Migration

**Where:** APA §6.8; Brennt Agreement §4.5

**The problem.** APA §6.8 states that "Buyer intends to migrate the Ashfall Requiem Titles to a proprietary engine following the Closing" and requires Seller to consult with Buyer "regarding any communications with Lumen Engine Technologies, Inc. relating to the transition." However, the APA imposes no obligation on Kiveth to actually fund or execute the migration, and no timeline for it.

Meanwhile, Brennt Agreement §4.5 provides Brennt a "Franchise Performance Award" equal to 0.5% of Net Franchise Revenue from Ashfall Requiem II in its first 24 months post-release. Ashfall Requiem II is in full production with a target release of "second half of 2028" (Disclosure Schedule §2.1(a), Title 4). If the Lumen Engine License renews (per Issue 3) and Kiveth is slow to migrate, the development of Ashfall Requiem II may be constrained by Lumen engine limitations or royalty costs, affecting the revenue on which Brennt's performance award is based.

**Why it matters.** This is a secondary issue, but it highlights a structural tension: Kiveth's stated strategy (migrate off Lumen) is undermined by the missed notice deadline (Issue 3), and the Brennt compensation structure creates an additional cost layer (0.5% of Ashfall Requiem II revenue) that is sensitive to the engine migration timeline. If the migration is delayed by the forced Lumen renewal, Kiveth pays both the $1.8M/year Lumen minimums AND the 0.5% revenue share to Brennt, during a period when the engine transition would have eliminated the Lumen costs.

**Recommended fix.** Address as part of the fix for Issue 3. If Lumen consent to early termination or a reduced minimum can be obtained, do so. If not, model the combined cost (Lumen minimums + Brennt performance award) and reflect it in the Purchase Price negotiation.

## Issue 10: Transition Services Agreement Not Provided for Review

**Where:** APA §1.1 ("Transition Services Agreement"); APA §5.2(b); Seller Member Consent §2.4(d), §4.3(e); Disclosure Schedule §3.9(a) Part C

**The problem.** The APA references a Transition Services Agreement as an Ancillary Agreement and a Closing deliverable. The Disclosure Schedule indicates that the "play.zerexforge.com/ashfall" subdomain will be "redirected under the Transition Services Agreement." However, the TSA is not in the review folder, and its terms cannot be assessed.

**Why it matters.** The TSA will govern the post-Closing transition of administrative and infrastructure services. Key questions that cannot be answered without seeing it: (a) what services are covered (IT, HR, finance, build infrastructure); (b) the duration and termination rights; (c) the fee structure; (d) service level commitments; (e) data migration obligations; and (f) what happens if Seller fails to provide services (e.g., if Seller dissolves or is acquired by a competitor). Given that Seller's 3-year non-compete (APA §6.12) restricts it from developing competing games but does not restrict it from providing services to others, there is a risk that Seller could prioritize other clients over Kiveth's transition needs.

**Recommended fix.** Obtain and review the TSA before Closing. Ensure it includes: (a) service level agreements with specific remedies for failure; (b) a maximum term of no more than 6–9 months; (c) data migration deliverables with specific timelines; (d) Seller's obligation to cooperate in good faith; and (e) a termination right for Kiveth if Seller materially fails to perform.

## Issue 11: APA §2.7(b) – Post-Closing "Commercially Reasonable Efforts" to Obtain Consents Is Weak

**Where:** APA §2.7(b); Seller Member Consent §7.2

**The problem.** For assets not assigned at Closing due to missing consents, APA §2.7(b) requires Seller to "continue to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain any such consent" post-Closing. While the Seller Member Consent (§7.2) directs the Authorized Officers to use such efforts, "commercially reasonable efforts" is a low standard. It does not require Seller to (a) pay a consent fee, (b) grant accommodations to the third party, (c) litigate to compel consent, or (d) meet any deadline for obtaining the consent.

APA §6.3 confirms: "Neither Party will be required to pay any consent fee or grant any material accommodation to obtain any such consent."

**Why it matters.** If the Lumen consent (the only Required Consent) is delayed post-Closing, Kiveth is in a limbo state: it has paid $48.5M, it has the Titles, but it cannot lawfully use the Lumen Engine to develop or update them (because the license is still in Seller's name and the post-termination rights in the Lumen license apply only to "titles released before the effective date of expiration" per Disclosure Schedule §3.10(a), Item 1, Post-Termination Rights). Meanwhile, Seller has no economic incentive to push hard for the consent—it has already received its $43.65M net payment.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Add a deadline for Seller to obtain post-Closing consents (e.g., 90 days), with a specific indemnity from Seller for Losses if the consent is not obtained by that date.
2. Require Seller to use "best efforts" (a higher standard) rather than "commercially reasonable efforts" for consents relating to Assigned Contracts that are critical to the operation of the Business.
3. Consider requiring Seller to post additional security (e.g., a letter of credit) for the period during which consents are pending post-Closing.

## Issue 12: APA §9.1(c) – Survival Periods Replace Statute of Limitations

**Where:** APA §9.1(c)

**The problem.** APA §9.1(c) states: "The Parties specifically intend that the survival periods in this Section 9.1 replace any otherwise applicable statute of limitations."

**Why it matters.** This is a double-edged sword. For Kiveth, it means that the 90-day IP survival period (Issue 4) is not just a contractual limitation on indemnification claims—it replaces the applicable statute of limitations entirely. In Delaware, the statute of limitations for breach of contract is three years, and for IP infringement claims it can be longer. By contractually replacing the SOL with 90 days, Kiveth has given up any ability to bring a direct claim (outside the indemnification framework) for IP-related breaches of representation after 90 days. This is more restrictive than a typical survival period, which limits indemnification claims but preserves the underlying cause of action.

Combined with §9.7 (Exclusive Remedies), which makes Article IX the "sole and exclusive remedy" for any breach of representation (other than Fraud), Kiveth's ability to pursue IP-related claims against Seller after 90 days is effectively eliminated.

**Recommended fix.** Delete the sentence "The Parties specifically intend that the survival periods in this Section 9.1 replace any otherwise applicable statute of limitations." If Seller insists on retaining it, limit it to the General Survival Date and Fundamental Representation survival periods, and exclude it from the IP Survival Date. Alternatively, extend the IP Survival Date (per Issue 4) to make the replacement less painful.
