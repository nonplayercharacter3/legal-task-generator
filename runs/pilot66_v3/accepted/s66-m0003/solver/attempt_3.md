## Summary

I have reviewed all six documents in the matter folder. I identify fourteen issues ranging from critical to moderate. Three issues are deal-threatening and should be resolved before Kiveth re-engages the other side: (1) the Lumen Engine License non-renewal notice deadline has already passed, locking Kiveth into a three-year renewal with $1.8M annual minimums; (2) the Character License Agreement cannot be validly assigned to Kiveth without Vantrell's sole-discretion consent because Kiveth is controlled by a hardware platform holder, and this consent is not listed as a Required Consent; and (3) the Escrow Agreement's "sole and exclusive source of recovery" provision directly conflicts with the APA's uncapped indemnification for Fraud, Fundamental Representation breaches, and Excluded Liabilities.

## Issue 1: Lumen Engine License Will Auto-Renue; Non-Renewal Notice Deadline Has Passed

**Where:** APA § 6.8; Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.10(a) Item 1 (Term and Renewal, § 13.1; Royalties, § 6(b)); Prior Draft (no § 6.8 existed)

**The problem.** The Lumen Engine License provides that the current term ends December 31, 2026, and the license "renews automatically for successive three-year renewal terms unless either party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 120 days prior to the expiration of the then-current term" (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.10(a) Item 1, Term and Renewal). One hundred twenty days before December 31, 2026 is **September 2, 2026**. The APA was signed on **September 21, 2026**—nineteen days after the deadline.

APA § 6.8 directs Seller to deliver written notice of non-renewal "prior to the deadline for delivery of such notice under it." That deadline has already passed. The license will therefore renew automatically for a three-year term beginning January 1, 2027.

Under the renewal-term royalty provision (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.10(a) Item 1, Royalties § 6(b)), each contract year of a renewal term commencing on or after January 1, 2027 carries a **minimum annual royalty guarantee of $1,800,000**, payable in four equal quarterly installments in advance. Over the three-year renewal term, Kiveth faces up to **$5,400,000** in minimum royalties it would not have incurred had the notice been timely delivered.

APA § 6.8 states that "Buyer intends to migrate the Ashfall Requiem Titles to a proprietary engine following the Closing." That intent is now frustrated by the auto-renewal.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Kiveth is paying $48.5M for a business it believes it can run on its own engine. Instead, it is contractually locked into the Lumen Engine for at least three additional years with a $1.8M annual floor, on top of the 4.5% royalty on Net Receipts above the $1M per-title quarterly threshold. This is an unpriced, unbudgeted cost of up to $5.4M that was not reflected in the Purchase Price.

**Recommended fix.**

- Confirm with Seller whether the non-renewal notice was in fact delivered before September 2, 2026. If it was, obtain a copy and file it in the data room.
- If it was not delivered, negotiate one of the following:
  - (a) A purchase price reduction of $5.4M (the three-year minimum royalty guarantee) or, alternatively, a specific indemnity from Seller for the renewal-term minimums, carved out from the cap and deductible;
  - (b) A covenant requiring Seller to negotiate in good faith with Lumen Engine Technologies to terminate or modify the renewal term, with Seller bearing any termination fee;
  - (c) If neither (a) nor (b) is achievable, reassess whether the deal economics still work for Kiveth given the additional royalty burden.
- In any event, delete or rework APA § 6.8, which as drafted is impossible to perform (it requires delivery of a notice "prior to the deadline" that has already passed).

## Issue 2: Character License Agreement Cannot Be Validly Assigned to Kiveth Without Vantrell's Sole-Discretion Consent; Consent Not Listed as a Required Consent

**Where:** APA § 4.6; APA § 7.2(c); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.10(b) Item 1 (Assignment, § 14.2; Definitions); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 7.2(c); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.3 Item 2

**The problem.** APA § 4.6 represents that "Buyer is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Pellucid Systems Co., the manufacturer of the Pellucid video game console."

The Character License Agreement (§ 14.2, as summarized in Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.10(b) Item 1) provides:

> "any assignment or transfer of this Agreement, including by sale of assets, merger, change of control or operation of law, **to a Person that is a Hardware Platform Holder or is Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder requires Vantrell's prior written consent. Vantrell may grant or withhold that consent in its sole discretion.** Any assignment or transfer made without a required consent is **void** and is a **material breach**, entitling Vantrell to terminate under Section 10.3."

"Hardware Platform Holder" is defined as "any Person that manufactures, markets or distributes, under its own brand, a video game console, handheld gaming device or other dedicated gaming hardware platform." Pellucid Systems Co. manufactures the Pellucid video game console. Kiveth is its indirect wholly owned subsidiary. The assignment of the Character License Agreement to Kiveth therefore **requires Vantrell's prior written consent, which Vantrell may withhold in its sole discretion**.

Yet Schedule 7.2(c) of the Disclosure Schedule lists only **one** Required Consent: the Lumen Engine Technologies assignment consent. The Vantrell consent is **not** listed. Schedule 3.3 Item 2 describes the Character License Agreement assignment as requiring only "written notice of an assignment to a successor to the licensed business within thirty (30) days after the assignment"—this is the general notice provision, not the Hardware Platform Holder consent provision. The Disclosure Schedule omits the sole-discretion consent requirement entirely.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** The Character License Agreement covers the Licensed Properties (Seraphine Vael, the Ashen Warden, Corvin Tallow, the Ninefold Choir, and the Ninefold characters) used throughout the Ashfall Requiem franchise. If the assignment is void for lack of Vantrell's consent, Vantrell can terminate the license. Kiveth would lose the right to use the core characters in the franchise it just paid $48.5M to acquire. Because the consent is in Vantrell's **sole discretion**, Kiveth has no leverage to compel it. And because it is not a Required Consent under § 7.2(c), Kiveth does not have a condition-precedent walk-right tied to it.

Under APA § 2.7(a), if the consent is not obtained, "the Closing shall nevertheless proceed" and "Seller shall continue to hold it, with no adjustment to the Purchase Price." Kiveth would pay the full $48.5M and not receive the Character License Agreement.

**Recommended fix.**

- **Immediately** add the Vantrell consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent. This gives Kiveth a condition-precedent right under APA § 7.2(c) to refuse to close if Vantrell withholds consent.
- Require Seller to submit the consent request to Vantrell **before** the Outside Date (December 15, 2026) and to use best efforts (not merely commercially reasonable efforts) to obtain it.
- Negotiate a specific remedy if Vantrell withholds consent: either (a) a proportionate reduction in the Purchase Price reflecting the loss of the character license, or (b) a right for Kiveth to terminate without penalty.
- Require Seller to disclose, in the Disclosure Schedule, the full text of the Hardware Platform Holder consent provision in § 14.2 of the Character License Agreement. The current summary in Schedule 3.3 Item 2 is materially incomplete and could constitute a breach of the § 3.10(b) representation that "The summaries of the Assigned Contracts in the Disclosure Schedule are accurate in all material respects."
- Consider whether Kiveth can restructure the transaction (e.g., through an intermediate entity not "Controlled by" Pellucid under the CLA's definition, which requires >50% voting control) to avoid triggering the consent requirement. Note that the definition of "Controlled" in Exhibit C of the CLA tracks the APA's definition of "Control" (power to direct management and policies), so even indirect control through a subsidiary may trigger it. This should be analyzed with the CLA's full text.

## Issue 3: Escrow Agreement "Sole and Exclusive Source of Recovery" Provisions Conflict with APA's Uncapped Indemnification

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 6.2; APA §§ 9.2, 9.5(b), 9.6, 11.9

**The problem.** Escrow Agreement § 6.2 states:

> "The Escrow Fund shall be the **sole and exclusive source of recovery** for **all Losses** of the Buyer Indemnified Parties under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement."

APA § 9.5(b) provides that the $4.85M cap does **not** apply to Losses arising from (i) Fraud, (ii) breach of a Fundamental Representation, or (iii) any Excluded Liability. APA § 9.6 provides that losses "not subject to Section 9.5(a)" (i.e., the uncapped losses) "shall be paid by Seller directly to Buyer by wire transfer."

APA § 11.9 (Order of Precedence) provides that the APA prevails over Ancillary Agreements, "provided, however, that with respect to matters relating to the Escrow Fund and the satisfaction of Losses from the Escrow Fund, the Escrow Agreement shall control."

The conflict: The Escrow Agreement says the $4.85M Escrow Fund is the **sole and exclusive** source for **all** Article IX losses. The APA says certain losses (Fraud, Fundamental Reps, Excluded Liabilities) are **uncapped** and payable **directly by Seller**. The precedence clause says the Escrow Agreement controls for "the satisfaction of Losses from the Escrow Fund"—but the Escrow Agreement's language goes further, making the Fund the exclusive source for losses that the APA says should be paid outside the Fund entirely.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** If a Fraud claim or an Excluded Liability claim arises (e.g., a pre-Closing IP infringement suit, or a tax liability), Kiveth's recovery may be limited to the $4.85M Escrow Fund rather than being recoverable from Seller's general assets without limit. Seller will argue that § 6.2 of the Escrow Agreement, which the APA's own precedence clause elevates for escrow-related matters, caps all recovery at the escrow. This would gut the uncapped indemnification for the most serious claims.

**Recommended fix.**

- Amend Escrow Agreement § 6.2 to provide: "The Escrow Fund shall be the sole and exclusive source of recovery for Losses that are subject to the cap in Section 9.5(a) of the Purchase Agreement. Losses that are not subject to that cap (including Losses arising from Fraud, breach of a Fundamental Representation, or an Excluded Liability) shall be recoverable from Seller in accordance with Section 9.6 of the Purchase Agreement and shall not be limited to the Escrow Fund."
- Alternatively, conform APA § 11.9 to clarify that the Escrow Agreement controls only the mechanics of disbursement **from** the Escrow Fund, not the **scope** of Seller's indemnification obligations.

## Issue 4: IP Survival Period Is Only 90 Days

**Where:** APA § 1.1 ("IP Survival Date"); APA § 9.1(a)(ii)

**The problem.** The representations and warranties in APA § 3.9 (Intellectual Property) survive only until the "IP Survival Date," defined as **90 days after the Closing Date**. All other non-fundamental reps survive until the General Survival Date (15 months post-Closing).

Section 3.9 covers: ownership of Seller IP (§ 3.9(b)); non-infringement (§ 3.9(c)); employee/contractor IP assignments (§ 3.9(d)); licenses granted by Seller (§ 3.9(e)); source code confidentiality and open source compliance (§ 3.9(f)); and trade secret protection (§ 3.9(g)).

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** In a game studio acquisition, the IP representations are the most substantive. Issues like a missing employee IP assignment, open source contamination that requires disclosure of proprietary code, or a latent ownership defect in the Seller Tools may not surface within 90 days. Once the IP Survival Date passes, Kiveth has no indemnification claim under § 9.2(a) for any inaccuracy in § 3.9, even if the inaccuracy is discovered later.

The 90-day period is especially short given that *Ashfall Requiem II* is in full production with a target release in the second half of 2028 (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.1(a) Item 4). IP issues in the development pipeline may not manifest until well after 90 days.

Note that pre-Closing IP infringement claims are Excluded Liabilities under § 2.4(a) and are indemnifiable under § 9.2(c) without regard to the IP Survival Date. But direct claims for inaccuracy of the § 3.9 reps (e.g., "Seller does not actually own this IP" or "this employee never signed an assignment") are time-barred after 90 days.

**Recommended fix.**

- Extend the IP Survival Date to match the General Survival Date (15 months) or, at minimum, to 12 months.
- Alternatively, carve out § 3.9(d) (employee/contractor IP assignments) and § 3.9(f) (open source) from the shortened survival period, given that these are the reps most likely to have latent issues.

## Issue 5: No Purchase Price Adjustment for Non-Assignable Assets

**Where:** APA § 2.7(a)

**The problem.** Section 2.7(a) provides that if a third-party consent is not obtained before Closing, "the Closing shall nevertheless proceed. That Purchased Asset shall not be assigned or transferred at the Closing, and Seller shall continue to hold it, **with no adjustment to the Purchase Price**."

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Kiveth pays the full $48.5M regardless of how many Assigned Contracts fail to transfer. If both the Lumen Engine License consent and the Vantrell consent (see Issues 1 and 2) are not obtained, Kiveth pays $48.5M for a business missing two of its most critical contracts. The only mitigation is the post-Closing cooperation obligation in § 2.7(b), which is unenforceable in practical terms if the counterparty simply refuses consent.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add a mechanism for proportionate Purchase Price reduction for each material Assigned Contract that is not assigned at Closing. For example, if the Character License Agreement is not assigned, reduce the Purchase Price by the present value of the character license royalties and the franchise value attributable to the Licensed Properties.
- Alternatively, make the assignment of each material Assigned Contract (at minimum, the Character License Agreement and the Lumen Engine License) a condition to Closing under § 7.2, rather than proceeding with a gap.

## Issue 6: Brennt Key Employee Agreement – Unlimited IP Indemnification Creates Uncovered Gap

**Where:** Brennt Agreement § 8.2; APA §§ 2.4(a), 9.2, 9.5(a), 9.5(b)

**The problem.** Brennt Agreement § 8.2 provides:

> "The Company shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Executive from and against any and all Claims arising out of or relating to the development of the Titles, including any Claim by a third party alleging that any Title, or any code, art asset, audio asset, narrative material, character, tool or other element of any Title, infringes, misappropriates or otherwise violates the Intellectual Property of that third party, in each case **whether arising before or after the Effective Date and without limitation as to amount**."

This is an **unlimited** indemnification from Kiveth to Brennt for IP infringement claims, including claims arising from pre-Closing development.

Under the APA, pre-Closing IP infringement claims are Excluded Liabilities (§ 2.4(a)) and are indemnifiable by Seller under § 9.2(c). However, Seller's indemnification for Excluded Liabilities, while excluded from the cap under § 9.5(b)(iii), is subject to the Escrow Agreement's "sole and exclusive source" language (see Issue 3). Even setting that conflict aside, the practical recovery for Kiveth from Seller is limited to the $4.85M Escrow Fund plus whatever can be collected from Seller's remaining assets post-Closing (Seller will have received $43.65M at Closing and may distribute it to its members).

Kiveth's obligation to Brennt, by contrast, is unlimited and not subject to any cap, deductible, or survival period.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** If a third party brings a significant IP infringement claim related to the pre-Closing development of the Titles (e.g., a claim that the art style infringes a third party's copyright, or that the Seller Tools incorporate misappropriated trade secrets), Kiveth must defend and indemnify Brennt without limitation. Kiveth's ability to recover from Seller for the same claim is constrained by the escrow mechanics and Seller's post-Closing financial condition. The gap between Kiveth's unlimited obligation to Brennt and its limited recovery from Seller is Kiveth's uncovered risk.

**Recommended fix.**

- Amend Brennt Agreement § 8.2 to limit the pre-Effective Date IP indemnification to the extent Kiveth is not reimbursed by Seller under the APA. Alternatively, cap the pre-Effective Date portion at the Escrow Amount.
- Add a provision requiring Kiveth to assert its indemnification rights against Seller under the APA before paying Brennt for pre-Effective Date claims, and to credit any recovery against its obligation to Brennt.
- Confirm that the D&O insurance required under Brennt Agreement § 8.4 (maintained for 6 years post-employment) would cover IP infringement claims asserted against Brennt in her individual capacity. If it does, the practical gap is narrower, but the indemnification obligation in § 8.2 is still primary.

## Issue 7: Brennt Franchise Performance Award – Employment Condition Is Likely a Drafting Error

**Where:** Brennt Agreement § 4.5

**The problem.** Section 4.5 provides that Brennt is "eligible for a franchise performance award equal to one-half of one percent (0.5%) of the Net Franchise Revenue of *Ashfall Requiem II* in the first twenty-four (24) months after its commercial release, payable within ninety (90) days after the end of that period, **provided Executive is employed by the Company on the commercial release date**."

The only employment condition is that Brennt be employed on the single day of commercial release. If she is employed on that date and resigns (or is terminated without Cause) the next day, she remains "eligible" for 0.5% of all Net Franchise Revenue for the subsequent 24 months.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** *Ashfall Requiem II* is the franchise's next major title with a target release in H2 2028. If the game is successful, 0.5% of 24 months of revenue could be a substantial sum. As drafted, Kiveth would owe this amount to a former employee who contributed nothing during the revenue-generating period.

**Recommended fix.**

- Amend the condition to require that Brennt be employed by the Company **throughout the 24-month measurement period**, or at minimum through the end of the period, to receive the award. If employment ends before the measurement period concludes, provide for a pro-rata reduction or forfeiture.
- Alternatively, if the business intent is to provide a release-date-only condition (as a retention incentive tied to the launch), add a clawback or reduction for termination without Cause during the measurement period.

## Issue 8: Brennt RSU Award Subject to Third-Party Approval

**Where:** Brennt Agreement § 4.4

**The problem.** The $1.5M RSU Award is "Subject to approval by the Compensation Committee of the board of directors of Pellucid Systems Co." Kiveth has no direct control over this approval. If the Compensation Committee withholds or delays approval, Brennt does not receive the equity component of her compensation package.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** The Brennt Employment Agreement is a condition to Kiveth's obligation to close (APA § 7.2(e)). If Brennt feels the compensation package is incomplete because the RSU Award was not approved by Pellucid's committee, she could refuse to execute the agreement or could repudiate it, causing the closing condition to fail. Even if she signs, the lack of a guaranteed equity component may affect her retention and motivation post-Closing, which is the entire point of the key employee arrangement.

Additionally, because the approval is by Pellucid (Kiveth's parent), there is a structural conflict: Pellucid is the party whose console platform the Ashfall Requiem Titles will be released on. The Compensation Committee may have different incentives than Kiveth regarding the size and terms of the award.

**Recommended fix.**

- Obtain written confirmation from Pellucid's Compensation Committee that the RSU Award has been approved or will be approved, as a condition to Kiveth's obligation to close. Alternatively, make the RSU Award unconditional (not subject to further approval) in the Brennt Agreement.
- If the approval condition must remain, add a fallback: if the RSU Award is not approved within 90 days after the Effective Date, Kiveth shall pay Brennt a cash bonus equal to the grant-date value ($1.5M) in lieu.

## Issue 9: Prior Draft Removed "Subject to Section 7.2(c)" Qualifier from Non-Assignable Asset Provision

**Where:** Prior Draft § 2.7(a); Execution Version § 2.7(a)

**The problem.** The prior draft (August 10, 2026) provided: "If any such consent is not obtained before the Closing, the Closing shall nevertheless proceed, **subject to Section 7.2(c)**, and Seller shall continue to hold that Purchased Asset."

The execution version deleted the phrase "subject to Section 7.2(c)." While § 7.2(c) remains a standalone condition to Buyer's closing obligation, the deletion removes the explicit cross-reference that tied the non-assignable asset mechanism to the Required Consents condition. This makes it less clear that the "Closing shall nevertheless proceed" language in § 2.7(a) is itself subject to Buyer's right to refuse closing under § 7.2(c) if a Required Consent has not been obtained.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** In a dispute, Seller could argue that § 2.7(a) unambiguously requires the Closing to proceed even without consents, and that the "subject to Section 7.2(c)" language was deliberately removed in negotiation. While § 7.2(c) still exists as a condition, the removal of the cross-reference weakens Kiveth's position that the two provisions operate together.

**Recommended fix.**

- Restore the "subject to Section 7.2(c)" language in § 2.7(a), or add an express statement that § 2.7(a) does not override Buyer's right to terminate under § 7.2(c) or § 8.1(c) if a Required Consent is not obtained.

## Issue 10: Kiveth Is Taxed on Escrow Fund Earnings

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 10.1

**The problem.** Section 10.1 provides that "Buyer will be treated as the owner of the Escrow Fund until it is disbursed, and all interest and earnings on the Escrow Fund will be reported as income of Buyer." The Escrow Fund is $4.85M held for up to 15 months. Even at a conservative 4% annual yield, Kiveth will recognize approximately $160,000–$320,000 in taxable income on funds that are not truly "its" money—they are security for Seller's indemnification obligations.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Kiveth pays tax on escrow earnings it does not economically own. The funds are at risk of being disbursed to Kiveth (if claims are made) or to Seller (if released). This is a cash tax cost with no offsetting economic benefit.

**Recommended fix.**

- Negotiate a tax gross-up or cost-sharing provision: Seller should reimburse Kiveth for the tax on escrow earnings, or the earnings should be split between the parties.
- Alternatively, direct the Escrow Agent to invest in a non-interest-bearing account (Exhibit B, third option) to eliminate the issue, at the cost of forgoing earnings that would otherwise offset the Escrow Agent's fees.

## Issue 11: Assumed Liabilities "Ordinary Course" Qualifier Creates Counterparty Risk

**Where:** APA § 2.3(a)

**The problem.** Assumed Liabilities include obligations under Assigned Contracts "only to the extent they... were incurred in the ordinary course." If a liability under an Assigned Contract was incurred outside the ordinary course before Closing, it is not an Assumed Liability and therefore is an Excluded Liability. However, the counterparty to the Assigned Contract may not distinguish between Assumed and Excluded Liabilities and may look to Kiveth (as the assignee and continuing party) for payment.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Kiveth could be sued by a contract counterparty for a pre-Closing obligation that is technically an Excluded Liability. Kiveth's remedy is to indemnify itself from Seller under § 9.2(c), but as noted in Issue 3, the Escrow Agreement may limit recovery to the $4.85M Fund.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add a provision requiring Seller to notify Kiveth before Closing of any liability under an Assigned Contract that Seller believes was not incurred in the ordinary course, so Kiveth can make an informed decision about whether to close.
- Require Seller to remain primarily liable to the counterparty for any such non-ordinary-course obligations, with Kiveth's assumption being secondary and subject to Kiveth's right of indemnification from Seller.

## Issue 12: Seller's $250,000 Settlement Approval Threshold May Delay Escrow Claims

**Where:** Seller Member Consent § 7.5(c)

**The problem.** The Seller Member Consent provides that "The Authorized Officers shall not settle any claim involving an amount in excess of $250,000 without the prior approval of the Board of Managers."

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Under the Escrow Agreement, if Seller objects to a Claim Notice, the Disputed Amount is held until resolved by Joint Written Instruction or Final Order (Escrow Agreement § 5.3). If the disputed amount exceeds $250,000, Seller's officers cannot agree to a Joint Written Instruction releasing the funds without going back to the full Board of Managers. This adds an internal governance step that can delay resolution and keep Kiveth's money (or the money Kiveth is entitled to receive from the escrow) locked up for longer.

**Recommended fix.**

- This is a Seller-side governance issue that Kiveth cannot directly fix in the APA. However, Kiveth should be aware of the delay risk when modeling the timeline for escrow claims. If a claim is likely to exceed $250,000, Kiveth should be prepared for a longer resolution process and should ensure the Escrow Agreement's dispute resolution mechanisms (including the right to submit to court under § 5.4) are understood as the fallback.

## Issue 13: China Trademark Is Only a Pending Application

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 3.9(a) Part A

**The problem.** The ASHFALL REQUIEM word mark in China (CNIPA) is listed as "App. No. 71559320 – Pending" in Class 9 only. It is not a registration. All other major jurisdictions (US, EU, UK, Japan, Korea) show registered marks.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** If the franchise has meaningful revenue or planned expansion in China, the absence of a registered trademark creates vulnerability to squatting, enforcement difficulties, and potential brand dilution. The APA § 3.9(a) representation that all listed registrations and applications are "subsisting and, to the Knowledge of Seller, valid and enforceable" does not guarantee that the China application will mature into a registration.

**Recommended fix.**

- Confirm with Seller the status and expected timeline for the China application. If registration is unlikely in the near term, consider whether this affects the valuation or whether Seller should provide a specific indemnity for China trademark issues.
- Add the China application to the list of items requiring further assurances under APA § 6.13, with Seller cooperating in prosecution through registration.

## Issue 14: Player Data and Social Media Handles Qualified by Transferability

**Where:** APA § 2.1(f)

**The problem.** Section 2.1(f) includes "all player data, community accounts and social media handles relating to the Business, **to the extent transferable under applicable Law and the applicable privacy policies**." This qualifier means that if player data cannot be transferred due to privacy law constraints or if a platform's terms do not permit account transfer, Kiveth simply does not receive those assets, with no price adjustment or alternative remedy.

**Why it matters for Kiveth.** Player data (purchase history, in-game progress, account information) and social media handles (which carry community goodwill and SEO value) are part of the franchise's operational infrastructure. If Kiveth cannot access player data, it may be unable to provide continuity of service to existing players or to implement post-launch updates. If it cannot transfer social media handles, it loses the community presence built over years.

**Recommended fix.**

- Require Seller to complete a transferability analysis before Closing and to disclose in the Disclosure Schedule which specific data sets and accounts are not transferable and why.
- For non-transferable player data, require Seller to provide Kiveth with a complete export or copy in a usable format, even if legal "title" cannot transfer.
- For social media handles, require Seller to cooperate in the transfer process and to maintain the accounts in good standing until the transfer is complete.

## Additional Observations

**Transition Services Agreement not in folder.** The APA references a Transition Services Agreement as an Ancillary Agreement (APA § 1.1), and the Seller Member Consent approves it (§ 2.4(d), § 4.3(e)). However, the TSA is not in the review folder. Kiveth should ensure the TSA is reviewed before Closing, particularly with respect to the scope and duration of services, service level commitments, and the mechanism for wind-down.

**Brennt Agreement § 2.1 – Automatic termination if deal fails.** If the APA is terminated before Closing, the Brennt Agreement "will automatically terminate and be void, and neither Party will have any liability or obligation under it." This is clean and protects Kiveth from any pre-Closing employment claims by Brennt. No action needed.

**Knowledge definition includes Brennt.** APA § 1.1 defines "Knowledge of Seller" to include the actual knowledge of "Odalys Brennt." Because Brennt becomes a Kiveth employee at Closing, her knowledge is imputed to Seller for pre-Closing representations. This is favorable to Kiveth and requires no change.

**Materiality scrape (APA § 9.5(d)).** The execution version adds a materiality scrape that was not in the prior draft. This is favorable to Kiveth: for purposes of determining whether a breach occurred and calculating losses, materiality qualifiers in the reps are disregarded. No action needed, but Kiveth should be aware that this cuts both ways in the sense that Seller could also argue the scrape applies to Buyer's reps under § 9.3.
