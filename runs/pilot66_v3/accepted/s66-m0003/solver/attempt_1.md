## Summary

I have reviewed all six documents in the matter folder. The deal documents contain several provisions that create material exposure for Kiveth Interactive, Inc. ("**Kiveth**" or "**Buyer**"). The most serious issues are: (1) the Character License Agreement from Vantrell Comics Group likely cannot be assigned to Kiveth without Vantrell's sole-discretion consent, yet the deal documents treat the assignment as routine and omit the consent from the Required Consents schedule; (2) the deadline to give non-renewal notice under the Lumen Engine License has already passed, locking Kiveth into a $1.8 million annual minimum royalty for an engine it intends to abandon; and (3) a direct conflict between the Escrow Agreement and the Asset Purchase Agreement effectively caps Kiveth's total recovery from Seller at the $4.85 million escrow, even for claims the APA says are uncapped.

Each issue is addressed below with the specific document and section, the risk to Kiveth, and a recommended fix.

## Issue 1: Character License Agreement Cannot Be Assigned to Kiveth Without Vantrell's Sole-Discretion Consent

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §4.6** (Buyer's Ownership Representation): Kiveth is "an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Pellucid Systems Co., the manufacturer of the Pellucid video game console."
- **Disclosure Schedule §15, Item 1 (Character License Agreement), "Assignment (Section 14.2)"**: "any assignment or transfer of this Agreement, including by sale of assets, merger, change of control or operation of law, to a Person that is a Hardware Platform Holder or is Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder requires Vantrell's prior written consent. Vantrell may grant or withhold that consent in its sole discretion. Any assignment or transfer made without a required consent is void and is a material breach, entitling Vantrell to terminate under Section 10.3."
- **Disclosure Schedule §15, Item 1, "Definitions (Section 14.2 and Exhibit C)"**: "Hardware Platform Holder" means "any Person that manufactures, markets or distributes, under its own brand, a video game console, handheld gaming device or other dedicated gaming hardware platform." "Controlled" means possession of "more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities of a Person, or of the power to direct its management and policies."
- **Disclosure Schedule §19 (Schedule 7.2(c): Required Consents)**: Lists only the Lumen Engine Technologies consent. The Vantrell consent is **not** listed.
- **APA §2.1(c)**: The Character License Agreement is an Assigned Contract.
- **APA §7.2(c)**: Closing is conditioned on obtaining only the Required Consents listed on Schedule 7.2(c).

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

Pellucid Systems Co. manufactures the Pellucid video game console, making it a "Hardware Platform Holder" under the Character License Agreement. Kiveth is an indirect wholly owned subsidiary of Pellucid, meaning Kiveth is "Controlled by" a Hardware Platform Holder. Therefore, the assignment of the Character License Agreement to Kiveth at Closing **requires Vantrell's prior written consent, which Vantrell may withhold in its sole discretion**. If the assignment is made without that consent, it is **void** and constitutes a **material breach** entitling Vantrell to terminate the license.

The Licensed Properties (the characters Seraphine Vael, the Ashen Warden, Corvin Tallow, and the Ninefold Choir from Vantrell's *Requiem of Ash* comic series) are integral to the Ashfall Requiem franchise. Loss of the Character License Agreement would gut the IP foundation of the titles Kiveth is paying $48.5 million to acquire.

Critically, the deal documents do not treat this as a risk. The Vantrell consent is not a Required Consent under Schedule 7.2(c), so Kiveth has no closing condition protecting it. The APA §2.7(a) mechanism for non-assignable assets provides no purchase price adjustment. Kiveth would close, pay $43.65 million wire plus $4.85 million escrow, and potentially hold a void assignment of its most important content license.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add the Vantrell consent to Schedule 7.2(c) as a Required Consent, making it a condition to Kiveth's obligation to close under APA §7.2(c).
2. Alternatively, if Vantrell's consent is not obtained before Closing, add a specific price adjustment or walk-right for Kiveth tied to the Character License Agreement.
3. Require Seller to use its best efforts (not merely "commercially reasonable efforts") to obtain Vantrell's consent before the Outside Date, and to disclose the status of that request in the bring-down certificate at Closing.
4. Consider requiring a specific indemnity from Seller (outside the cap) if the Character License Agreement is terminated by Vantrell post-Closing due to the void assignment.

## Issue 2: Lumen Engine License Non-Renewal Notice Deadline Has Already Passed

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §6.8 (Lumen Engine License)**: "prior to the Closing, Seller shall deliver to Lumen Engine Technologies, Inc. written notice of non-renewal of the Lumen Engine License, in accordance with the notice provisions of the Lumen Engine License and prior to the deadline for delivery of such notice under it."
- **Disclosure Schedule §14, Item 1 (Lumen Engine License), "Term and Renewal (Section 13.1)"**: "the license renews automatically for successive three-year renewal terms unless either party gives the other written notice of non-renewal at least 120 days prior to the expiration of the then-current term." The current term ends **December 31, 2026**.
- **Disclosure Schedule §14, Item 1, "Royalties (Section 6 and Amendment No. 1), clause (b)"**: "For each contract year of any renewal term commencing on or after January 1, 2027, Licensee shall pay a minimum annual royalty guarantee of $1,800,000."
- **APA §1.1 (Agreement Date)**: September 21, 2026.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

120 days before December 31, 2026 is approximately **September 2, 2026**. The Agreement Date is September 21, 2026. The non-renewal notice deadline **passed 19 days before the parties signed the APA**. Seller is contractually unable to perform its covenant under APA §6.8.

As a result, the Lumen Engine License will **automatically renew** for a three-year term beginning January 1, 2027. Kiveth will be obligated to pay a minimum annual royalty of $1,800,000 per year (totaling **$5,400,000 over three years**) for an engine it has expressly stated it intends to migrate away from (APA §6.8, first sentence: "Buyer intends to migrate the Ashfall Requiem Titles to a proprietary engine following the Closing").

The post-termination rights in the Lumen Engine License (Disclosure Schedule §14, Item 1, "Post-Termination Rights (Section 13.4)") would have allowed Kiveth to continue selling existing titles without a minimum guarantee had the license simply expired. But because the notice deadline was missed, the license renews with the full minimum guarantee attached.

This is a **$5.4 million avoidable cost** that the deal documents do not address. The APA §6.8 covenant is a dead letter.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Confirm with Seller immediately whether notice was in fact delivered before September 2, 2026 (the Disclosure Schedule does not state this, and the absence of a disclosure to Schedule 3.6 suggests it was not).
2. If notice was not delivered, negotiate one of the following:
   - A purchase price reduction of $5,400,000 (the present value of the three-year minimum royalty obligation); or
   - A specific indemnity from Seller for the excess of actual Lumen royalties paid over the $4.5% royalty that would have been payable absent the minimum guarantee, uncapped and outside the deductible; or
   - An escrow holdback of $5,400,000 (in addition to or in substitution for the existing $4,850,000 escrow) specifically securing the Lumen minimum royalty obligation.
3. Delete or restructure APA §6.8 to acknowledge the missed deadline and allocate the resulting cost.

## Issue 3: Escrow Agreement §6.2 Conflicts with APA §9.6 and Caps All Recovery at $4.85 Million

**Where it appears:**

- **Escrow Agreement §6.2 (Source of Recovery)**: "The Escrow Fund shall be the **sole and exclusive source of recovery** for all Losses of the Buyer Indemnified Parties under Article IX of the Purchase Agreement."
- **APA §9.5(b) (Exclusions from Cap)**: The $4,850,000 cap does **not** apply to Losses based on (i) Fraud, (ii) breach of Fundamental Representations, or (iii) any Excluded Liability.
- **APA §9.5(a) (Cap)**: Caps only apply to "Losses for which Seller is liable under Section 9.2(a)" (breach of representations and warranties). Claims under §9.2(b) (covenant breach), §9.2(c) (Excluded Assets/Liabilities), and §9.2(d) (Fraud) are not subject to the cap.
- **APA §9.6 (Manner of Payment)**: "To the extent the Escrow Fund is insufficient or has been released, **or the Losses are not subject to Section 9.5(a)**, they shall be paid by Seller directly to Buyer by wire transfer of immediately available funds within ten (10) Business Days after the final determination of the amount owed."
- **APA §11.9 (Order of Precedence)**: "with respect to matters relating to the Escrow Fund and the satisfaction of Losses from the Escrow Fund, **the Escrow Agreement shall control**."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

APA §9.6 expressly contemplates that Kiveth can recover from Seller **directly** (outside the escrow) when: (a) the escrow is insufficient, (b) the escrow has been released, or (c) the losses are not subject to the cap (i.e., Fraud, Fundamental Rep breaches, Excluded Liabilities, and covenant breaches under §9.2(b)).

But Escrow Agreement §6.2 states the Escrow Fund is the "sole and exclusive source of recovery for **all** Losses under Article IX." Under APA §11.9, the Escrow Agreement controls on matters relating to the Escrow Fund and the satisfaction of Losses from it.

Read together, the effect is that **Kiveth's maximum recovery for any post-Closing claim against Seller is $4,850,000**, regardless of whether the claim is for Fraud, a Fundamental Representation breach, an Excluded Liability, or a covenant breach. The direct-payment obligation in APA §9.6 is rendered inoperative by the Escrow Agreement's "sole and exclusive" language, which prevails under the order-of-precedence clause.

This is particularly dangerous given:
- The uncapped nature of Fraud claims (APA §9.5(b)(i));
- The Purchase Price cap on Fundamental Representation claims (APA §9.5(b)(ii) – up to $48.5M);
- The fact that Excluded Liability claims (APA §9.2(c)) are expressly uncapped.

If Seller commits fraud or breaches a Fundamental Representation, Kiveth should be able to recover up to the full Purchase Price. Instead, it appears limited to $4.85M.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Amend Escrow Agreement §6.2 to carve out claims that are not subject to the cap under APA §9.5(b) (Fraud, Fundamental Representations, Excluded Liabilities) and claims under APA §9.2(b) (covenant breaches). The revised language should provide that the Escrow Fund is the sole source of recovery only for Losses subject to the cap in APA §9.5(a), and that for all other Losses, Kiveth may seek direct recovery from Seller in accordance with APA §9.6.
2. Alternatively, amend APA §11.9 to clarify that the Escrow Agreement controls only as to the mechanics of disbursement from the Escrow Fund, not as to the availability of remedies outside the Fund.

## Issue 4: IP Representations Survive Only 90 Days Post-Closing

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §1.1 (IP Survival Date)**: "the date that is **ninety (90) days** after the Closing Date."
- **APA §9.1(a)(ii)**: "the representations and warranties of Seller in Section 3.9 (Intellectual Property) survive until the IP Survival Date."
- **APA §9.1(c)**: "No claim for indemnification in respect of any representation or warranty may be made after the expiration of the applicable survival period."
- **APA §9.5(c) (Deductible)**: The $242,500 deductible applies to §9.2(a) claims (which include IP rep breaches) and is not waived for IP claims.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

For a video game studio acquisition, the IP representations (APA §3.9) are arguably the most critical: they cover ownership of all Seller IP, non-infringement, employee/contractor assignments, open source compliance, and the sufficiency of the IP portfolio. Latent IP problems—missing assignments from former contractors, open source contamination discovered during a code audit, a third-party infringement claim that surfaces after a new title launches—often take well over 90 days to identify.

Ninety days is less than one quarter. Kiveth will have approximately three months to:
- Complete its post-Closing IP audit;
- Identify any gaps in the chain of title;
- Discover any open source compliance issues;
- Receive and evaluate any third-party infringement claims that arise from pre-Closing development.

If a problem is discovered on day 91, Kiveth has **no indemnification remedy** under the APA for it. The claim is time-barred under §9.1(c).

This is especially acute because:
- The IP reps are subject to the $242,500 deductible (§9.5(c));
- The IP reps are subject to the $4.85M cap (§9.5(a));
- And per Issue 3 above, recovery may be limited to the escrow in any event.

Compare: the General Survival Date is 15 months. The Fundamental Representations survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations. The 90-day IP survival is an outlier that heavily favors Seller.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Extend the IP Survival Date to no less than 15 months (matching the General Survival Date), or alternatively to the earlier of (a) 15 months after Closing and (b) 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations for IP infringement claims.
2. If Seller resists extending survival, at minimum exclude the IP representations from the Deductible (so Kiveth recovers dollar-one for IP breaches).
3. Consider adding a specific "IP Audit Period" of 12 months during which Kiveth may conduct a comprehensive audit of the Seller IP and assert claims discovered during that audit, even if beyond the general IP Survival Date.

## Issue 5: Brennt's Uncapped Personal Indemnity Creates Asymmetric Risk

**Where it appears:**

- **Brennt Agreement §8.2 (Development of the Titles)**: "The Company shall indemnify, defend and hold harmless Executive from and against any and all Claims arising out of or relating to the development of the Titles, including any Claim by a third party alleging that any Title... infringes, misappropriates or otherwise violates the Intellectual Property of that third party, in each case **whether arising before or after the Effective Date and without limitation as to amount**."
- **APA §9.5(a)**: Seller's indemnity for rep breaches capped at $4,850,000.
- **APA §9.5(b)**: Even for Fundamental Rep breaches, Seller's cap is the Purchase Price ($48,500,000).
- **Escrow Agreement §6.2**: Per Issue 3, effectively limits all Article IX recovery to $4,850,000.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

If a third party (e.g., Vantrell, a former employee, or an IP holder) asserts an infringement claim against the Ashfall Requiem Titles, Kiveth is obligated to indemnify Brennt **without limitation as to amount** under §8.2. Kiveth's ability to pass that cost through to Seller is limited to $4.85M (and potentially less, given the deductible and the escrow-as-sole-source issue).

The asymmetry is stark:
- Kiveth's exposure to Brennt: **unlimited**.
- Kiveth's recovery from Seller: **$4,850,000 maximum** (and subject to $242,500 deductible for non-fundamental claims).

For a franchise with 118 employees, multiple titles in development, and complex IP including character licenses, engine technology, and open source components, the risk of a significant IP infringement claim is non-trivial. A single successful claim could easily exceed $4.85M in damages plus defense costs.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Cap Brennt's §8.2 indemnity at a stated amount (e.g., $10M or $15M), or at least align it with the maximum Kiveth can recover from Seller.
2. Add a "pass-through" provision: if Kiveth pays Brennt under §8.2 for a claim that gives rise to indemnification from Seller under the APA, Kiveth is entitled to recover from Seller an amount equal to what it paid Brennt, outside the APA cap and deductible.
3. Require Seller to maintain a pre-Closing IP infringement insurance policy (or confirm existing coverage) that names Kiveth and Brennt as additional insureds, with a tail period of at least 3 years.
4. At minimum, require Brennt to cooperate with Kiveth in pursuing indemnification from Seller for any claim covered by both §8.2 and Article IX of the APA.

## Issue 6: No Purchase Price Adjustment for Non-Assignable Assets

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §2.7(a)**: "If any such consent is not obtained before the Closing, the Closing shall nevertheless proceed. That Purchased Asset shall not be assigned or transferred at the Closing, and Seller shall continue to hold it, **with no adjustment to the Purchase Price**."
- **APA §2.7(b)**: Post-Closing, Seller uses "commercially reasonable efforts" to obtain consent. Until then, Seller cooperates in "any lawful and commercially reasonable arrangement" to provide Kiveth the benefits.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

This provision means Kiveth pays the full $48.5M even if material contracts fail to assign. There is no price reduction, no specific indemnity, and no walk-right triggered by a failed assignment (other than the general Required Consents condition, which per Issue 1 does not cover the Vantrell consent).

For the Character License Agreement specifically, the "arrangement" under §2.7(b) would likely be a sublicense from Seller to Kiveth. But if the assignment is void (as it would be without Vantrell's consent under the Character License Agreement), Seller may not even have the right to grant a sublicense. Kiveth would be paying $48.5M for a franchise it cannot lawfully exploit.

Even for contracts where a sublicense is feasible, Kiveth bears ongoing counterparty risk: Seller could breach the sublicense, become insolvent, or refuse to cooperate.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Add a per-asset or per-contract price adjustment for any Assigned Contract that is not assigned at Closing. The adjustment should reflect the present value of the lost contract rights.
2. For the Character License Agreement specifically, either (a) make Vantrell's consent a Required Consent (see Issue 1) or (b) provide Kiveth with a termination right if the Character License Agreement is not validly assigned at Closing, with return of all funds paid.
3. Shorten the period during which Seller holds non-assigned assets. Add a drop-dead date (e.g., 180 days post-Closing) after which Kiveth may terminate the APA and demand return of the purchase price allocated to the unassigned asset, plus interest.

## Issue 7: Required Consents Schedule Omits Vantrell Consent

**Where it appears:**

- **Disclosure Schedule §19 (Schedule 7.2(c))**: Lists only one Required Consent: "Written consent of Lumen Engine Technologies, Inc. to the assignment of the Lumen Engine License to Buyer, under Section 16.1 of the Lumen Engine License."
- **APA §7.2(c)**: Closing is conditioned on "each of the consents listed on Schedule 7.2(c) of the Disclosure Schedule (the 'Required Consents') shall have been obtained in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer."
- **Disclosure Schedule §15, Item 1, "Assignment (Section 14.2)"**: Describes the Vantrell consent requirement for Hardware Platform Holders.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

This is the procedural mechanism that fails to protect Kiveth from Issue 1. Because the Vantrell consent is not listed on Schedule 7.2(c), Kiveth has no closing condition that would allow it to walk if Vantrell withholds consent. Kiveth is contractually obligated to close (assuming all other conditions are met) even if it knows the Character License Agreement assignment will be void.

The omission appears to be either (a) an oversight by Seller's counsel, who may not have connected the Hardware Platform Holder restriction to Kiveth's ownership by Pellucid, or (b) a deliberate choice by Seller to avoid giving Kiveth a walk-right on this issue.

**Recommended fix:**

Add the following to Schedule 7.2(c):

> 2. Written consent of Vantrell Comics Group, LLC to the assignment of the Character License Agreement to Buyer, as required by Section 14.2 of the Character License Agreement, given Buyer's status as a Person Controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder.

This gives Kiveth the right to refuse to close if Vantrell withholds consent.

## Issue 8: Brennt's Franchise Non-Competition Is Shorter Than Seller's

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §6.12 (Non-Competition; Non-Solicitation)**: Seller is restricted for **three (3) years** after the Closing Date from developing, publishing or commercializing any Ashfall Requiem sequel, spin-off, or game using the franchise's characters, settings or trademarks.
- **Brennt Agreement §1.1 (Restricted Period)**: "the period of Executive's employment with the Company and the **twelve (12) months** following the termination of that employment for any reason."
- **Brennt Agreement §11.3 (Franchise Non-Competition)**: During the Restricted Period, Brennt shall not "develop, direct or provide creative services for any video game that uses the characters, settings, storylines or trademarks of the Ashfall Requiem franchise."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

Brennt is the Studio Creative Director who "has led the creative direction of the Ashfall Requiem Titles since their inception" (Brennt Agreement, Recital B). She is a closing condition (APA §7.2(e)) and the deal's key talent retention.

However, her franchise non-compete expires 12 months after she leaves Kiveth. If she departs at month 14 post-Closing (within her 3-year Initial Term), she is free to work on a competing Ashfall Requiem title at another studio by month 26 post-Closing. Meanwhile, Seller (the entity) remains restricted until month 36 post-Closing.

The practical effect: the person with the deepest creative knowledge of the franchise can walk and immediately compete, while the shell entity that no longer has that knowledge is bound for a year longer. Kiveth paid a premium for Brennt's continued involvement (she is a closing condition; her agreement includes a $1.2M retention bonus and $1.5M RSU award), but its protection against her competitive use of franchise knowledge expires relatively quickly.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Extend Brennt's franchise non-compete (§11.3) to no less than 24 months post-termination, or align it with Seller's 3-year restriction from the Closing Date (whichever is later).
2. Add a "franchise knowledge" non-disclosure provision that survives beyond the Restricted Period, specifically prohibiting Brennt from using or disclosing the creative direction, narrative architecture, and design philosophy of the Ashfall Requiem franchise for any competing project for 3 years post-termination.
3. Consider adding a clawback provision: if Brennt breaches §11.3, she forfeits all unpaid Retention Bonus installments and unvested RSUs.

## Issue 9: Brennt's RSU Award Is Subject to Parent Board Approval

**Where it appears:**

- **Brennt Agreement §4.4 (Equity Award)**: "Subject to approval by the Compensation Committee of the board of directors of **Pellucid Systems Co.**, Executive will be granted, within ninety (90) days after the Effective Date, restricted stock units with a grant-date value of $1,500,000 under the applicable equity incentive plan of Pellucid Systems Co."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

The RSU Award is a material component of Brennt's compensation package ($1.5M grant-date value, vesting over 4 years). Its grant is conditioned on approval by Pellucid's Compensation Committee—a body over which Kiveth (as a subsidiary) has no direct control.

If Pellucid's Compensation Committee withholds or delays approval:
- Brennt may feel the deal's economic terms have been materially altered, creating a retention risk;
- Kiveth could face a "Good Reason" claim under Brennt Agreement §6.4(d) (material breach of the Agreement by the Company), triggering full severance under §7.2;
- The closing condition in APA §7.2(e) (Brennt Employment Agreement executed and not repudiated) could be implicated if Brennt repudiates based on the failed grant.

This is a structural risk: Kiveth's ability to retain its most important employee depends on an approval from its parent that is outside the deal documents.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Obtain written confirmation from Pellucid's Compensation Committee (or its chair) that the RSU Award will be approved, and attach it as an exhibit to the Brennt Agreement or the APA.
2. Alternatively, make the RSU Award unconditional (delete the "Subject to approval" language) and have Kiveth's board approve the grant as part of the deal authorization.
3. If the condition must remain, add a provision that if the RSU Award is not granted within 90 days due to the approval condition, Brennt is entitled to a cash payment equal to the grant-date value, vested on the same schedule.

## Issue 10: Transition Services Agreement Not Available for Review

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §1.1 (Ancillary Agreements)**: Includes "the Transition Services Agreement."
- **APA §5.2(b)**: Seller must deliver the Transition Services Agreement at Closing.
- **Disclosure Schedule §11, Part C**: The subdomain "play.zerexforge.com/ashfall" is "to be redirected under the Transition Services Agreement."
- **Seller Member Consent §2.4(d)**: References the Transition Services Agreement.
- **The Transition Services Agreement is not in the review folder.**

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

The Transition Services Agreement will govern the post-Closing period during which Kiveth depends on Seller's infrastructure (server hosting, build systems, the zerexforge.com subdomain, and potentially other services). Without reviewing it, Kiveth cannot assess:
- The scope and duration of services;
- The pricing (whether at cost, marked up, or free);
- Service level commitments and remedies for failure;
- Data migration obligations and timelines;
- The exit mechanism and whether Kiveth is locked in.

Given that the Lumen Engine migration (APA §6.8) will take time, and that the cloud build infrastructure is with Stratoline Cloud Services (Disclosure Schedule §16, Item 6), Kiveth may be dependent on Seller's transition services for a significant period.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Obtain and review the Transition Services Agreement before agreeing to close.
2. Ensure it includes: service level agreements with specific uptime commitments; a defined end date no later than 12 months post-Closing; pricing at or below Seller's current cost; data migration assistance; and a termination right for Kiveth if services fail to meet the SLAs.
3. Confirm that the Stratoline Cloud Services arrangement (month-to-month, assignable upon notice per Disclosure Schedule §16, Item 6) can be assigned directly to Kiveth at Closing, reducing dependence on the Transition Services Agreement for build infrastructure.

## Issue 11: APA §2.7(a) Removed the "Subject to Section 7.2(c)" Qualifier from the Prior Draft

**Where it appears:**

- **Prior Draft APA §2.7(a)**: "If any such consent is not obtained before the Closing, the Closing shall nevertheless proceed, **subject to Section 7.2(c)**, and Seller shall continue to hold that Purchased Asset."
- **Execution Version APA §2.7(a)**: "If any such consent is not obtained before the Closing, the Closing shall nevertheless proceed. That Purchased Asset shall not be assigned or transferred at the Closing, and Seller shall continue to hold it, with no adjustment to the Purchase Price."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

The prior draft contained a cross-reference to §7.2(c) (the Required Consents closing condition), which would have preserved Kiveth's right to refuse to close if a Required Consent was not obtained. The execution version **deleted this qualifier**, meaning that even if a Required Consent (currently, the Lumen consent) is not obtained, the Closing proceeds and Kiveth has no walk-right under §2.7.

This change, combined with the fact that the Lumen consent is the only Required Consent (Issue 7), means:
- If Lumen withholds consent, Kiveth must still close and pay $48.5M;
- The Lumen Engine License remains with Seller;
- Kiveth gets no price adjustment;
- Kiveth's only protection is the general indemnity (capped, subject to deductible, and per Issue 3, potentially limited to the escrow).

**Recommended fix:**

1. Restore the "subject to Section 7.2(c)" language in §2.7(a), so that failure to obtain a Required Consent gives Kiveth the right to terminate under §8.1(c) or refuse to close.
2. Add the Vantrell consent to Schedule 7.2(c) (per Issue 7), so that the restored qualifier also protects against the Character License Agreement problem.
3. For any non-Required Consent that is not obtained, add a specific price adjustment formula.

## Issue 12: Seller's Broad Authority to Make Changes at Signing

**Where it appears:**

- **Seller Member Consent §4.2**: Authorizes each Authorized Officer to execute the Purchase Agreement "with such changes as the Authorized Officer executing it approves, that approval to be conclusively evidenced by that execution." The limitation is only that changes "not materially and adversely alter the Purchase Price, the Escrow Amount or the scope of the Purchased Assets without further approval."
- **Seller Member Consent §4.3**: Same broad authority for the Ancillary Agreements.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

Seller's Authorized Officers (Tobias Renwick and Ilsa Marchetti) can unilaterally make changes to the APA and Ancillary Agreements at signing, so long as the changes don't "materially and adversely" alter price, escrow, or asset scope. This could include:
- Modifying representation language;
- Adding Disclosure Schedule items that qualify representations;
- Changing covenant terms;
- Modifying the Escrow Agreement.

Kiveth's protection is only that the changes must be "conclusively evidenced by that execution"—meaning if Kiveth's signatory signs the changed document, Kiveth is bound. There is no requirement that changes be communicated to Kiveth in advance or that Kiveth's signatory flag them.

This is a process risk rather than a substantive one, but in a deal where the documents are complex and the signing may occur remotely (APA §5.1 contemplates remote closing), there is a risk that changes are made in the execution version that Kiveth's team did not review.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Require that any changes made between the version Kiveth approved and the execution version be identified in a redline delivered to Kiveth at least 24 hours before signing.
2. Add a provision to the APA (or as a side letter) that if any change made by Seller's Authorized Officers under the Member Consent is not disclosed to Kiveth before execution, Kiveth may treat the change as void and rely on the last mutually agreed draft.
3. Ensure Kiveth's signatory has a clear internal process for comparing the execution version against the last agreed draft before signing.

## Issue 13: Deductible Applies to IP and General Representation Claims

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §9.5(c) (Deductible)**: "Seller is not liable to the Buyer Indemnified Parties for indemnification under Section 9.2(a) until the aggregate amount of all Losses in respect of indemnification under Section 9.2(a) exceeds **$242,500** (the 'Deductible'), in which event Seller is liable only for Losses in excess of the Deductible."
- **APA §9.5(c), second sentence**: "The Deductible does not apply to Losses arising from Fraud or from any inaccuracy in or breach of any Fundamental Representation."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

The $242,500 deductible (5% of the Purchase Price) is a threshold that Kiveth must clear before recovering **any** amount for breaches of non-Fundamental representations, which includes:
- All IP representations (§3.9) – the most critical reps for this deal;
- Financial statement accuracy (§3.5);
- Material contract validity (§3.10);
- Employment compliance (§3.11);
- Tax compliance (§3.12);
- Platform relationships (§3.13).

The deductible is not a basket (where Kiveth recovers everything once the threshold is crossed) but a true deductible (Kiveth recovers only the excess). This means for a $300,000 IP infringement claim, Kiveth recovers only $57,500.

Combined with the 90-day IP survival (Issue 4) and the escrow-as-sole-source problem (Issue 3), the effective protection for IP claims is:
- Must be discovered within 90 days;
- Must exceed $242,500 in aggregate;
- Recovery is limited to the excess over $242,500;
- Total recovery capped at $4,850,000;
- And potentially limited to the escrow even for larger claims.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Convert the deductible to a tipping basket: once aggregate losses exceed $242,500, Seller is liable for **all** losses from the first dollar (not just the excess).
2. Exclude IP representation breaches (§3.9) from the deductible entirely, given their criticality to the deal.
3. Reduce the deductible to $100,000 or less.

## Issue 14: Kiveth's Exposure on Brennt's Severance Package

**Where it appears:**

- **Brennt Agreement §7.2 (Termination Without Cause or for Good Reason)**: 12 months base salary ($410,000) + prorated target bonus (up to $205,000) + unpaid Retention Bonus (up to $1,200,000) + accelerated RSU vesting (up to $1,500,000 grant value) + 12 months COBRA.
- **Brennt Agreement §4.3 (Retention Bonus)**: $1,200,000 in three equal annual installments; accelerated on without-Cause termination or Good Reason resignation.
- **Brennt Agreement §6.4 (Good Reason)**: Includes "a material diminution in Executive's title, authority, duties or responsibilities, **including her removal from creative leadership of the Titles**."

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

Kiveth's total severance exposure if it terminates Brennt without Cause (or if she resigns for Good Reason) is approximately **$3,315,000+** (base salary + bonus + retention bonus + RSU acceleration + COBRA premiums). This is a contingent liability that Kiveth assumes at Closing.

The Good Reason definition is broad: any "material diminution" in her role, including removal from creative leadership of the Titles, triggers full severance. Given that Kiveth is a subsidiary of a console manufacturer (Pellucid) and may have its own creative priorities for the franchise, there is a realistic risk that Kiveth's integration decisions could trigger a Good Reason claim.

This $3.3M+ exposure is not reflected in the Purchase Price or the Assumed Liabilities (it is Kiveth's own obligation under the Brennt Agreement, not an assumed liability of Seller).

**Recommended fix:**

1. This is a business decision, but ensure Kiveth's management is aware of the full severance exposure before Closing.
2. Consider narrowing the Good Reason definition: delete "including her removal from creative leadership of the Titles" or replace "material diminution" with "material and adverse diminution" to raise the threshold.
3. Add a provision that the Retention Bonus acceleration under §4.3 does not apply if Brennt's employment ends due to a Good Reason claim that is later determined (by arbitration or litigation) to have been without merit.

## Issue 15: APA §3.9(b) Representation Is Inconsistent with the Character License Assignment Problem

**Where it appears:**

- **APA §3.9(b)**: "The Seller Intellectual Property, together with the Intellectual Property licensed under the Assigned Contracts, **constitutes all of the Intellectual Property necessary to operate the Business as currently conducted and to develop, publish and commercialize the Ashfall Requiem Titles**."
- **Disclosure Schedule §15, Item 1**: The Licensed Properties (Vantrell characters) are used in the Ashfall Requiem Titles.
- **APA §4.6**: Kiveth is controlled by a Hardware Platform Holder.

**Why it matters for Kiveth:**

Seller represents that the Seller IP plus the licensed IP (including the Vantrell Licensed Properties) constitutes **all** IP necessary to operate the Business. If the Character License Agreement cannot be validly assigned to Kiveth (per Issue 1), then Kiveth will **not** have all the IP necessary to operate the Business. This would be a breach of §3.9(b).

However, §3.9(b) is an IP representation with only 90 days survival (Issue 4), subject to the $242,500 deductible (Issue 13), and subject to the $4.85M cap (and potentially the escrow-as-sole-source limitation per Issue 3). The remedy for what could be the most damaging breach in the deal is structurally inadequate.

**Recommended fix:**

1. Address the root cause (Issues 1 and 7): make the Vantrell consent a Required Consent.
2. If the Vantrell consent cannot be obtained, add a specific representation that the Character License Agreement is assignable to Buyer without further consent, and make it a Fundamental Representation (so it is uncapped and not subject to the deductible).
3. Alternatively, add a specific condition to Closing that the Character License Agreement be validly assigned to Buyer, with a termination right if it cannot be.

## Additional Observations (Lower Severity)

**A. APA §6.12 Non-Compete Scope.** The Seller non-compete prohibits developing "any video game that is a sequel to, spin-off of, or uses the characters, settings or trademarks of the Ashfall Requiem franchise." This is appropriately broad. However, it does not prohibit Seller from developing an *original* IP that is competitive with Ashfall Requiem in genre or audience. Given that Seller retains its development capabilities (it is not being dissolved), this is a residual risk. Consider adding a general non-compete in the "dark fantasy action RPG" genre for 2 years.

**B. APA §6.6(a) Employee Offer Timeline.** Kiveth must make employment offers to Transferred Employees at least 10 Business Days before Closing. With 118 employees (Disclosure Schedule §17), this is a significant logistical task. Ensure Kiveth's HR team is prepared and that the offer process does not become a bottleneck that delays Closing past the Outside Date (December 15, 2026).

**C. Escrow Agreement Exhibit B (Investment Direction) Is Unchecked.** None of the three investment options on Exhibit B is selected. If the Escrow Fund is held in a non-interest-bearing account (the default under §3.3(a) absent a written direction), Kiveth will bear the tax on any earnings (Escrow Agreement §10.1 treats Buyer as owner of the Fund for tax purposes) but the Fund will earn nothing, reducing its real value over the 15-month hold period. Ensure the government money market fund option is selected.

**D. APA §11.4 Assignment to Affiliates.** Kiveth may assign its rights under the APA to its Affiliates without Seller's consent. This is favorable to Kiveth (it can move the agreement within the Pellucid corporate structure). However, confirm that this does not trigger the "change of control" provision in the Character License Agreement (which requires Vantrell's consent for transfers to a Hardware Platform Holder or its Controlled persons). If Kiveth assigns the APA to another Pellucid subsidiary, is that a "transfer" of the Character License Agreement? The analysis under Issue 1 applies with equal or greater force.

**E. Seller Member Consent §13.1 (Manager/Member Interests).** Each Manager discloses "any arrangement for employment with, or consulting for, Buyer after the Closing. No Manager has any such interest." This is relevant because if a Seller manager were to join Kiveth post-Closing, it could create conflicts with the non-compete (§6.12) or the confidentiality covenant (§6.7). The current disclosure is clean, but monitor for changes before Closing.
