## Summary

Review of the six documents in the matter folder reveals fourteen issues. The three most consequential are: (1) the pending Nordhaven acquisition of Mibaix Holdings Corp. gives Supplier a termination right under the Change of Control clause; (2) the non-competition covenant effectively forces Mibaix to abandon its existing exhaust gas scrubber servicing business worldwide; and (3) the SOW fee schedule contains no BX-900 rate, creating a pricing vacuum before the first BX-900 commissionings in March 2027.

## Issue 1 – Change of Control Termination Right Triggered by Pending Nordhaven Acquisition

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 17.2; Parent Guaranty, Recital B.

**What:** Section 17.2 permits Supplier to terminate the Agreement on 90 days' written notice if a Change of Control of Distributor occurs without Supplier's prior written consent. "Change of Control" is defined as any transaction by which a person acquires more than 50% of the voting securities of Distributor *or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Distributor.* Recital B of the Parent Guaranty discloses that Mibaix Holdings Corp. (which owns 100% of Mibaix) has signed a share purchase agreement with Nordhaven Maritime Group plc, under which Nordhaven will acquire all of Mibaix Holdings' shares, with closing expected in Q1 2027.

**Why it matters:** The Nordhaven closing will constitute a Change of Control of Mibaix (through its parent). Supplier can terminate the entire distribution relationship—including the exclusive appointment, the BX-900 launch program, and the SOW—on 90 days' notice at any time within 12 months after it first learns of the transaction. Because the Guaranty (to which Supplier is a party) expressly recites the Nordhaven deal, Supplier already knows. Termination would strand Mibaix's $6.2M Minimum Annual Purchase commitment, its installed-base service obligations, and its customer relationships built under the exclusive appointment. It would also eliminate the basis for the credit limit increase and the joint launch program.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Nordhaven closing, Mibaix should obtain Supplier's prior written consent to the Change of Control under § 17.2, or negotiate a side letter in which Supplier agrees not to exercise its termination right in connection with the Nordhaven transaction. If consent is conditioned, Mibaix should seek to limit any conditions (e.g., no change to the credit support package, no increase in the Minimum Annual Purchase). As an alternative, the share purchase agreement with Nordhaven should include a condition precedent that Mibaix obtains Supplier's consent, with the deal timeline adjusted accordingly.

## Issue 2 – Overbroad Non-Competition Covenant Eliminates Mibaix's Existing Scrubber Business

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 3.4; Recital B.

**What:** Section 3.4 prohibits Mibaix, during the Term and for 24 months thereafter, from directly or indirectly "manufactur[ing], distribut[ing], sell[ing], market[ing] or servic[ing] any ballast water treatment, exhaust gas scrubber or other marine environmental compliance equipment other than Products, anywhere in the world." Recital B confirms that Mibaix "currently operates an exhaust gas scrubber servicing business at Gulf and Caribbean ports."

**Why it matters:** As drafted, the covenant forces Mibaix to cease its existing scrubber servicing operations immediately upon the Effective Date (March 3, 2025) and bars it from re-entering that market—or any adjacent marine environmental compliance market—anywhere in the world for 24 months after the Agreement ends. This is a substantial restriction on a going concern that predates the distribution relationship. The "anywhere in the world" geographic scope and the inclusion of "other marine environmental compliance equipment" (an undefined, potentially limitless category) make the covenant vulnerable to challenge as an unreasonable restraint of trade under New York law (the governing law under § 19.1), but Mibaix would have to litigate to establish that, and the interim operational harm is real. The clause also restricts Mibaix's Affiliates "under its control," which could implicate Mibaix Holdings' other subsidiaries.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to § 3.4 that (a) carves out Mibaix's existing exhaust gas scrubber servicing business as conducted on the Effective Date, or at minimum permits Mibaix to continue servicing scrubbers it installed before the Effective Date; (b) limits the geographic scope to the Territory rather than worldwide; and (c) replaces the open-ended "other marine environmental compliance equipment" language with a defined list of product categories. If Supplier resists, Mibaix should at minimum secure a written acknowledgment that the covenant does not prevent Mibaix from performing maintenance and repair services on non-BWTS equipment it has historically serviced.

## Issue 3 – No BX-900 Fee in SOW Schedule

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Annex 1, Part 1; Amendment No. 1, § 2.6.

**What:** The fixed fee table in Annex 1, Part 1 of the SOW lists rates only for BX-300, BX-500, and BX-700 Retrofit Installations and Commissioning. There is no BX-900 row. The SOW was executed June 16, 2025, before the BX-900 existed. Amendment No. 1 (September 15, 2026) adds the BX-900 to the product range and provides for free field service engineer attendance at the first three BX-900 commissionings (§ 2.6), but it does not amend the SOW fee schedule.

**Why it matters:** The first BX-900 units are scheduled for commissioning in March and April 2027 (Q4 2026 Forecast, § 7.3). If any of those commissionings involve a Reserved Account (Gulfline or Tavros), or if Supplier engages Mibaix for BX-900 warranty work, there is no agreed fee. The SOW's time-and-materials rates in Annex 1, Part 3 could apply as a fallback, but they were calibrated for smaller units and do not reflect the greater complexity of a triple-reactor, 1,000 m³/h system with remote monitoring gateway and ballast control system interface module (Amendment, Annex 1). The absence of a fixed fee also weakens Mibaix's position in crew planning and customer proposals, since it cannot quote a firm installation price for the BX-900.

**Recommended fix:** Execute an amendment to SOW No. 1 (or a new SOW No. 2) adding a BX-900 row to Annex 1, Part 1 with appropriate fixed fees for Retrofit Installation and Commissioning and Commissioning Only. The fees should reflect the BX-900's greater size, complexity, and integration requirements. This should be done before the first BX-900 Work Order is issued (target: before January 2027, to allow time for crew scheduling for the March 2027 commissionings).

## Issue 4 – Guaranty Subject to English Law and Exclusive English Jurisdiction

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §§ 14.1–14.2.

**What:** The Guaranty is governed by the laws of England and Wales, and the courts of England and Wales have exclusive jurisdiction over any dispute. Mibaix Holdings Corp. is a Delaware corporation with its principal place of business in Houston, Texas. The underlying Distribution Agreement is governed by New York law with exclusive New York forum (§ 19.1–19.2).

**Why it matters:** If a dispute arises over the Guaranty (e.g., a Demand under § 3.1 that Mibaix Holdings considers invalid), Mibaix Holdings must litigate in English courts under English law, while any related dispute under the Distribution Agreement must be litigated in New York under New York law. This creates the risk of inconsistent outcomes, duplicated proceedings, and substantially increased legal costs. For a Delaware/Houston entity with no UK operations, English litigation is particularly burdensome. The jurisdictional mismatch also complicates any attempt to consolidate related claims.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to § 14 of the Guaranty to conform the governing law and forum to New York, matching the Distribution Agreement. If Supplier insists on English law (which is common for parent guaranties given to UK suppliers), at minimum seek (a) a provision permitting Mibaix Holdings to bring defensive proceedings in New York in response to a claim brought in England, and (b) a carve-out allowing Mibaix Holdings to seek injunctive relief in any court of competent jurisdiction to protect its rights under § 2.4 (Distributor's defenses).

## Issue 5 – Guaranty Covers "All Present and Future Obligations" Without Consent for Amendments

**Where:** Parent Guaranty §§ 1.1 (definition of "Guaranteed Obligations"), 5.1(a).

**What:** The Guaranteed Obligations include "all present and future obligations of Distributor to pay money to Supplier under the Agreement." Section 5.1(a) provides that the Guaranty is not affected by "any amendment, supplement, extension, renewal or replacement of the Agreement or any Statement of Work, including any change to the Products, the Price List, the credit limit or the payment terms," with the sole exception that an increase in the revolving credit limit above $2.5M requires Guarantor's written consent under § 5.2.

**Why it matters:** If the parties amend the Distribution Agreement to, for example, extend payment terms, add new fee categories, increase the Minimum Annual Purchase, or introduce new SOWs with additional payment obligations, Mibaix Holdings is automatically guarantying those new obligations without any further consent or negotiation. The only protected threshold is the credit limit. Given that the Agreement has a 5-year initial term plus 2-year renewals, and that the Minimum Annual Purchase is already being renegotiated at each renewal (§ 2.3), Mibaix Holdings' exposure can grow substantially over time without its active approval.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 5.1(a) to require Mibaix Holdings' written consent for any amendment that increases the aggregate Guaranteed Obligations by more than a stated percentage (e.g., 15%) or that adds new categories of payment obligations. Alternatively, cap the Guaranty at a stated maximum amount (e.g., $5M in the aggregate) that can be increased only by written agreement.

## Issue 6 – Insurance Cancellation Notice Gap for Non-Payment

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 7.2; Distribution Agreement § 14.3.

**What:** Section 14.3 requires 30 days' written notice of cancellation or material reduction. The insurers' notice of cancellation endorsements provide 30 days' notice for voluntary cancellation but only 10 days' notice for cancellation for non-payment of premium. The cover letter acknowledges this gap and states that "Mibaix has confirmed to us that it will itself give Bupaiv Ballast Technologies Ltd. thirty (30) days' written notice under Section 14.3 of the Agreement if any of its required coverage is to be cancelled for any reason."

**Why it matters:** Mibaix pays its premiums under a premium finance arrangement with automatic monthly debits from its operating account. If that arrangement fails (e.g., insufficient funds, bank error), the insurer can cancel on 10 days' notice. Mibaix would then have only 10 days to discover the cancellation and notify Bupaiv—far short of the 30 days required by § 14.3. A technical breach of the insurance covenant could give Supplier grounds to claim material breach under § 17.1, or at minimum leverage in a negotiation. The risk is heightened during the Nordhaven transition, when Mibaix Holdings' treasury arrangements may be in flux.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Ensure the premium finance arrangement has adequate controls and backup funding to prevent non-payment; (b) request the insurers to extend the non-payment notice period to 30 days (some carriers will agree for commercial accounts); or (c) add a provision in the next renewal certificate confirming that Mibaix will monitor its premium account and will treat any missed payment as an internal event triggering immediate notice to Bupaiv, independent of the insurer's cancellation.

## Issue 7 – BX-900 Trademark Is Only a Pending Application

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 6.3; Distribution Agreement § 9.1.

**What:** Schedule C (as amended) lists the BX-900 mark as "U.S. Application Serial No. 98/447,215 (pending)." The license in § 9.1 extends to "the trademarks, trade names and logos of Supplier listed in Schedule C."

**Why it matters:** A pending application is not a registered trademark. If the USPTO refuses the application or Supplier abandons it, the mark never registers, and the scope of the license becomes uncertain. Mibaix may have invested in marketing materials, signage, and customer communications using the "BX-900" designation under the assumption of a valid license. Additionally, because the mark is not yet registered, third parties could potentially register a confusingly similar mark in the interim, creating infringement risk that the IP indemnity in § 10.3 would need to cover (and which Supplier would bear, but the disruption to Mibaix's marketing would be significant).

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision to the Amendment (or a side letter) confirming that the license in § 9.1 extends to the BX-900 mark whether or not the application matures to registration, and that if the application is refused or abandoned, Supplier will either (a) register an alternative mark and update Schedule C, or (b) confirm in writing that Mibaix may continue to use the "BX-900" designation as a product name (not as a trademark) without infringement risk.

## Issue 8 – Minimum Annual Purchase Increase of 29%

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 3.1; Distribution Agreement § 5.1.

**What:** The Minimum Annual Purchase increases from $4.8M to $6.2M for the Contract Year beginning March 3, 2027—a 29% increase. The Q4 2026 Forecast (§ 9.3) projects $10.8M in purchases through December 31, 2027 alone, well above the new minimum.

**Why it matters:** While Mibaix is currently tracking comfortably above the minimum, the increased commitment reduces flexibility. If market conditions shift (e.g., a major customer defers dry-dockings, a competitive BWTS manufacturer undercuts on price, or the Nordhaven integration disrupts operations), Mibaix is locked into a higher floor. The remedy for shortfall is conversion to non-exclusive (§ 5.3), which would destroy the value of the exclusive appointment and Mibaix's investment in the Territory. The increase also means that the Guaranty (which covers payment obligations) underwrites a larger exposure.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a Most Favoured Nations-style protection or a market-adjustment mechanism: if BWTS retrofit demand in the Territory falls below a stated threshold (e.g., fewer than X vessels with scheduled dry-dockings in a given year), the Minimum Annual Purchase for that Contract Year is reduced proportionally. Alternatively, seek to reduce the increase to a more moderate level (e.g., $5.5M) with a step-up to $6.2M in the second Renewal Term if sales justify it.

## Issue 9 – Liquidated Damages for Late Delivery Are Inadequate

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 7.2.

**What:** If Supplier fails to make a BWTS unit available within 14 days after the confirmed delivery date, liquidated damages are 0.5% of the unit price per full week of delay, capped at 10% of the unit price. For a BX-900 at $648,000, the maximum recovery is $64,800. Cancellation is available only after 60 days of delay.

**Why it matters:** The real cost of a delayed BWTS unit is the vessel missing its dry-docking window. A single day of vessel detention at a Gulf Coast yard can cost $50,000–$150,000 in lost revenue, port fees, and charter hire. If a BX-900 is delayed by 8 weeks (the point at which liquidated damages max out), Mibaix's customer may face $400,000+ in detention costs, yet Mibaix's recovery from Supplier is capped at $64,800. Mibaix, as the party that sold the system to the customer, bears the reputational and commercial risk of the gap. The 60-day cancellation right does not help if the vessel's dry-docking window has already passed.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an increase in the liquidated damages rate (e.g., 1% per week) and/or the cap (e.g., 25% of the unit price). Alternatively, add a provision that if the delay causes a vessel to miss its scheduled dry-docking window (as evidenced by the yard's schedule), Supplier reimburses Mibaix's documented customer claims for detention costs up to a stated amount, in addition to the liquidated damages.

## Issue 10 – No Marine Cargo Insurance Identified in Mibaix's Program

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 7.1; Insurance Certificate Cover Letter (entire).

**What:** Products are delivered Ex Works Southampton (§ 6.3, § 7.1). Title and risk pass to Mibaix on delivery at Supplier's facility (§ 7.3). Section 7.1 provides that Supplier *may* arrange ocean freight and marine cargo insurance at Mibaix's cost, but only "where Distributor requests." The insurance certificate delivered to Bupaiv covers only liability lines (CGL, ship repairer's legal liability, workers' compensation, auto). No marine cargo insurance is mentioned.

**Why it matters:** From the moment a Product leaves Supplier's Southampton facility, it is at Mibaix's risk. If Mibaix arranges its own ocean freight (the more likely scenario for a Houston-based distributor), it needs marine cargo insurance to cover the value of the Products in transit. A total loss of a BX-900 ($648,000) in the Atlantic would be a significant uninsured loss if Mibaix has not placed cargo coverage. The absence of cargo insurance from the certificate package suggests it may not be in place, or at least has not been communicated to Supplier.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm whether Mibaix carries marine cargo insurance (either as a separate policy or as part of a commercial package). If not, place coverage for goods in transit from Southampton to the Territory ports, with limits adequate to cover the value of a full shipment. If Mibaix prefers to have Supplier arrange freight and cargo insurance under § 7.1, issue a standing request to that effect so the arrangement is documented.

## Issue 11 – SOW Termination for Convenience Creates Revenue/Commitment Mismatch

**Where:** SOW No. 1, § 11.2; Distribution Agreement § 5.1.

**What:** Either Party may terminate the SOW on 90 days' written notice. Termination does not affect accepted Work Orders but ends future engagements. The Minimum Annual Purchase obligation under the Distribution Agreement is independent of the SOW.

**Why it matters:** If Supplier terminates the SOW, Mibaix loses its installation revenue stream (fixed fees for Reserved Account jobs, warranty work reimbursement, and survey fees) but retains its $6.2M Minimum Annual Purchase commitment. Mibaix would still be required to buy Products it cannot install for Reserved Accounts (since the SOW is the vehicle for those engagements). While Mibaix could still install Products for its own customers under § 8.1, the Reserved Account work and warranty work reimbursement—significant revenue sources—would disappear.

**Recommended fix:** Add a provision to the SOW (or the next amendment) stating that if Supplier terminates the SOW for convenience, the Minimum Annual Purchase is reduced by a proportionate amount reflecting the lost Reserved Account installation volumes, or that Supplier continues to offer Mibaix the Reserved Account installation work under a new SOW on no less favorable terms.

## Issue 12 – Guaranty Financial Reporting Obligations May Conflict with Nordhaven Information Rights

**Where:** Parent Guaranty § 10.1; Guaranty Recital B.

**What:** Mibaix Holdings must deliver to Supplier audited consolidated annual financials within 120 days of fiscal year end and unaudited quarterly financials within 60 days of each of the first three quarters. These are "consolidated" financials, meaning they would reflect the combined Mibaix Holdings group.

**Why it matters:** Once Nordhaven acquires Mibaix Holdings, the consolidated financials will include Nordhaven's other portfolio companies (if any) or will be prepared on a basis that Nordhaven's own reporting requirements dictate. Supplier will have ongoing access to Mibaix Holdings' (and potentially Nordhaven group's) financial information. Section 10.2 requires Supplier to treat the information as Confidential Information under the Agreement, but this is a contractual promise to a counterparty, not a statutory protection. In a competitive market, the risk of information leakage—whether through Supplier's own personnel or through a future dispute—is non-trivial.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a limitation on the financial information to be delivered: (a) financials should be for Mibaix Holdings and its subsidiaries only, not for any Nordhaven group entities; (b) add a provision that if Mibaix Holdings is acquired, the financial reporting obligation is satisfied by delivery of Mibaix Holdings' standalone (not consolidated) financials; and (c) tighten the confidentiality obligations in § 10.2 to include a specific prohibition on using the financial information for competitive purposes or sharing it with Supplier's other distributors.

## Issue 13 – BX-900 Capacity Commitment Reduced to 100% During Ramp-Up

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, § 3.5; Distribution Agreement § 5.5.

**What:** Section 5.5 of the Agreement requires Supplier to maintain capacity for 120% of the most recent forecast. Amendment § 3.5 reduces this to 100% for the BX-900 during the period from the Amendment Effective Date (September 15, 2026) until March 2, 2027.

**Why it matters:** The Q4 2026 Forecast shows 7 BX-900 units expected to ship in calendar 2027, with the first two already on open Purchase Orders (delivery February 26, 2027). If Supplier's capacity is constrained to exactly 100% of forecast during the ramp-up period, any unplanned demand (e.g., a large tanker operator adding vessels to its retrofit program) cannot be met without Supplier exceeding its commitment. The forecast also notes that several Grade C and D opportunities are competitive tenders where BX-900 lead times are a differentiating factor (§ 11.4). A supply constraint would directly harm Mibaix's ability to win those tenders.

**Recommended fix:** Seek to restore the 120% capacity commitment for the BX-900 no later than December 1, 2026 (giving Supplier time to scale up), or add a provision that if Supplier cannot meet 100% of the forecast due to capacity constraints, it must notify Mibaix at least 8 weeks before the affected delivery date so Mibaix can manage customer expectations and offer alternative models.

## Issue 14 – Order of Precedence Favors SOWs Over the Agreement in Practice

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 21.3.

**What:** Section 21.3 provides: "If there is any conflict or inconsistency between documents forming part of the arrangement between the Parties, the documents prevail in the following order: Amendments, then the Distribution Agreement, then Statements of Work."

**Why it matters:** On its face, this means the Agreement prevails over the SOW. However, the SOW contains specific operational terms (fees, acceptance procedures, indemnities, workmanship warranty) that are more detailed than the Agreement. If a conflict arises—for example, if the SOW's installation indemnity (§ 6.2) is read more broadly than the Agreement's indemnification framework (§ 10)—the Agreement controls. This is generally favorable to Mibaix (since the Agreement's indemnities are more balanced), but it creates uncertainty in day-to-day operations where the SOW is the working document. The practical risk is that Supplier's field service team may operate on the assumption that the SOW terms control (since it is the more specific document), leading to disputes when Mibaix asserts the Agreement's more protective terms.

**Recommended fix:** Add a clarifying sentence to § 21.3 (or to the next SOW amendment) stating that where the SOW is silent on a matter governed by the Agreement, the Agreement applies, and that the SOW's specific operational terms (fees, schedules, acceptance) are intended to supplement rather than override the Agreement's substantive protections (indemnities, liability caps, warranty). This reduces the risk of the field team asserting SOW terms in a manner inconsistent with the Agreement.
