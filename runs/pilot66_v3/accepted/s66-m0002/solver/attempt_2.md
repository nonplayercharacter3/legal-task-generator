## Issue 1 – Non-Competition Clause Captures Existing Scrubber Business

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 3.4

**The Problem:** Section 3.4 prohibits Mibaix, during the Term and for 24 months thereafter, from "directly or indirectly manufactur[ing], distribut[ing], sell[ing], market[ing] or servic[ing] any ballast water treatment, exhaust gas scrubber or other marine environmental compliance equipment other than Products, anywhere in the world." Recital B of the Agreement expressly acknowledges that Mibaix "currently operates an exhaust gas scrubber servicing business at Gulf and Caribbean ports." As drafted, the non-compete forces Mibaix to abandon or divest that existing revenue stream for the full five-year Initial Term plus any Renewal Terms, plus a further 24-month tail. The restriction is also global in scope, not limited to the Territory.

**Why It Matters:** This is potentially the single most damaging clause in the Agreement for Mibaix. The scrubber business is an independent line of revenue that predates the Bupaiv relationship. If Mibaix must cease scrubber servicing to comply, it loses that income while simultaneously bearing the $4.8M (now $6.2M) Minimum Annual Purchase obligation. The 24-month post-termination tail compounds the problem: even if the Agreement ends, Mibaix cannot resume scrubber work for two more years.

**Recommended Fix:** Negotiate a carve-out from Section 3.4 for Mibaix's existing exhaust gas scrubber servicing business as conducted on the Effective Date. At minimum, the restriction should be limited to the Territory (not worldwide) and should not capture "servicing" of equipment Mibaix installed before the Effective Date. If Supplier resists a full carve-out, propose limiting the post-termination tail to 12 months and restricting the geographic scope to the U.S. Gulf Coast.

## Issue 2 – Pending Nordhaven Change of Control Triggers Supplier Termination Right; Guaranty Survives

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 17.2; Parent Guaranty, Recital B, Sections 5.1(d), 5.1(h), and 12.1

**The Problem:** Recital B of the Guaranty discloses that Mibaix Holdings Corp. has entered into a share purchase agreement dated August 28, 2026 with Nordhaven Maritime Group plc, under which Nordhaven will acquire all shares of Mibaix Holdings, with closing expected in Q1 2027. Section 17.2 of the Agreement defines "Change of Control" to include any transaction by which a person acquires more than 50% of the voting securities of "any entity that directly or indirectly controls Distributor." The Nordhaven acquisition is squarely within that definition. Supplier may terminate the Agreement on 90 days' notice at any time within 12 months after it first learns of the Change of Control.

Critically, the Guaranty is drafted to survive this event. Section 5.1(d) provides that a "change in the name, constitution, legal form or ownership of Distributor or Guarantor" does not affect Guarantor's obligations. Section 5.1(h) provides that the Guaranty continues for obligations arising before or after termination under surviving provisions. Section 12.1 keeps the Guaranty in effect until the later of Agreement expiration/termination and full payment of all Guaranteed Obligations.

**Why It Matters:** Mibaix faces a binary risk in Q1 2027: either (a) it obtains Supplier's consent to the Nordhaven transaction and the distribution relationship continues (with the increased $6.2M minimum now in effect), or (b) Supplier terminates, in which case Mibaix loses its exclusive Gulf Coast distribution rights and Mibaix Holdings (now a Nordhaven subsidiary) remains on the hook under the Guaranty for all past-due and future payment obligations. The 12-month window in Section 17.2 means Supplier can wait to see how the Nordhaven deal develops before exercising its termination right, giving it maximum leverage.

Additionally, Section 17.2 requires Mibaix to give Supplier "written notice of any proposed Change of Control as soon as permitted by Applicable Law and any applicable confidentiality obligations." If Mibaix has not yet given that notice, it is in technical breach of a covenant, which could itself constitute a material breach under Section 17.1.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Confirm immediately whether Mibaix has given Supplier the notice required by Section 17.2. If not, do so now to avoid a technical breach.
- Seek Supplier's written consent to the Nordhaven transaction as a condition to closing, or negotiate a side letter confirming that Supplier will not exercise its Section 17.2 termination right in connection with the Nordhaven acquisition.
- If consent is not obtainable, model the termination scenario: Mibaix would have 180 days to sell off inventory (Section 17.5(c)), Supplier must fulfill accepted POs (Section 17.5(b)), and the Guaranty remains in force. Assess whether the Guaranty's exposure in a termination scenario is acceptable to Mibaix Holdings / Nordhaven.
- Consider whether the Section 12.3 replacement guaranty mechanism can be used post-closing to substitute a Nordhaven entity or letter of credit, potentially on more favorable terms.

## Issue 3 – Uncapped Environmental Indemnity in SOW No. 1

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Section 6.2; Distribution Agreement, Section 11.3(a)

**The Problem:** Section 6.2 of the SOW requires Mibaix to indemnify Supplier for "all Losses arising from any Environmental Claim arising out of or relating to the Installation Services performed by Distributor or its subcontractors." Section 11.3(a) of the Agreement expressly excludes "indemnification obligations with respect to Environmental Claims" from both the consequential damages waiver (Section 11.1) and the liability cap (Section 11.2). The result is that Mibaix's environmental indemnity to Supplier is unlimited in amount.

"Environmental Claim" is defined in Section 1.1 of the Agreement as "any claim, fine or penalty arising from a discharge of ballast water or treatment residuals." In the Gulf Coast context, a single significant discharge event could generate eight- or nine-figure liability under the Clean Water Act and the Vessel Incidental Discharge Act, both of which are incorporated into the definition of "Applicable Law."

**Why It Matters:** Mibaix is performing the physical installation and commissioning work. If a defect in Mibaix's installation (e.g., a piping tie-in failure, a valve left open during commissioning, a control system misconfiguration) causes a ballast water discharge that fails to meet the 33 C.F.R. § 151.2030 standards, Mibaix's indemnity to Supplier is uncapped. Meanwhile, Supplier's own environmental indemnity to Mibaix (Agreement Section 10.2) covers only claims resulting from a "Defect in a Product" – not from installation error. The risk allocation is therefore one-directional: product defects are Supplier's problem, but installation-caused environmental damage is Mibaix's problem, without limit.

Mibaix's ship repairer's legal liability insurance ($5M per occurrence) and CGL policy ($5M per occurrence) provide some backstop, but a major environmental event could exceed those limits. The marine pollution liability insurance that Supplier is required to carry (Agreement Section 14.1(b), $10M) covers "discharges from the Products" – it is unclear whether that extends to discharges caused by Mibaix's installation error rather than a product defect.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Negotiate a cap on the SOW Section 6.2 indemnity, tied to Mibaix's insurance limits (e.g., $5M per occurrence, matching the ship repairer's liability policy).
- Alternatively, make the indemnity subject to the general liability cap in Section 11.2 by deleting it from the Section 11.3(a) exclusion, or by adding a specific carve-back in the SOW.
- At minimum, clarify that the indemnity does not apply where the Environmental Claim results from a Defect in the Product (which is already covered by Supplier's Section 10.2 indemnity) or from Supplier's error in the integration drawings or instructions (already excluded by SOW Section 8.3).
- Confirm with Mibaix's insurance broker (Calloway Brennan) whether the ship repairer's liability policy responds to third-party environmental claims arising from installation work, and whether the $50,000 deductible is the only reduction in available coverage.

## Issue 4 – Guaranty: Primary Obligor Status and Breadth of Guaranteed Obligations

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Sections 1.1, 2.1, 2.2, 2.4, and 4.3

**The Problem:** The Guaranty is structured as a guaranty of payment (not collection), and Mibaix Holdings is a "primary obligor and not merely as surety" (Section 2.1). Supplier need not first pursue Mibaix or exhaust any other remedy before demanding payment from Mibaix Holdings (Section 4.3). The "Guaranteed Obligations" definition (Section 1.1) sweeps in "all present and future obligations of Distributor to pay money to Supplier under the Agreement, whether actual or contingent," including obligations arising under future amendments. Mibaix Holdings cannot assert defenses arising from Mibaix's insolvency or from any Insolvency Proceeding (Section 2.4).

**Why It Matters:** In a downside scenario (e.g., Mibaix fails to meet the increased $6.2M minimum, or the Nordhaven transaction disrupts Mibaix's operations), Supplier can bypass Mibaix entirely and demand payment from Mibaix Holdings. Because the Guaranty covers "future" obligations and is not affected by amendments (Section 5.1(a)), any future increase in the Minimum Annual Purchase or credit limit (up to $2.5M without further consent, per Section 5.2) automatically expands Mibaix Holdings' exposure. The primary obligor status means Mibaix Holdings has no right to require Supplier to collect from Mibaix first – a significant departure from traditional suretyship protections.

**Recommended Fix:**
- The $2.5M cap in Section 5.2 is a useful limit, but it applies only to the "price of Products" under clause (a) of the Guaranteed Obligations definition. Other categories (freight, field service fees, interest, enforcement costs) are not subject to that cap. Negotiate an overall aggregate cap on the Guaranty.
- Seek to convert the primary obligor language to a traditional surety structure, or at minimum add a requirement that Supplier give Mibaix 30 days' notice and an opportunity to pay before making a Demand on Mibaix Holdings. The current 10-Business-Day notice period (Section 3.1) is short.
- Clarify that the Guaranty does not extend to obligations arising under amendments that increase the Minimum Annual Purchase by more than a stated percentage without Mibaix Holdings' separate written consent (Section 5.1(a) currently requires consent only for credit limit increases above $2.5M).

## Issue 5 – Guaranty: English Law and London Jurisdiction (Split Forum)

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Sections 14.1 and 14.2

**The Problem:** The Agreement is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York County courts (Agreement Sections 19.1–19.2). The Guaranty is governed by English law with exclusive jurisdiction in the courts of England and Wales (Guaranty Sections 14.1–14.2). Mibaix Holdings has appointed a London process agent (Lexmere Process Services Limited, Section 14.3).

**Why It Matters:** If a dispute arises involving both the Agreement and the Guaranty (e.g., Supplier claims Mibaix breached the Agreement and simultaneously demands payment under the Guaranty), Mibaix will face parallel proceedings in two different jurisdictions under two different bodies of law. English law on guaranties and sureties differs from New York law in material respects (e.g., the interpretation of "primary obligor" language, the availability of equitable defenses, and the treatment of set-off). Mibaix Holdings may find that defenses available to Mibaix under New York law (e.g., a good-faith invoice dispute under Agreement Section 6.5, which Section 2.4 of the Guaranty preserves) are interpreted more narrowly under English law.

**Recommended Fix:** Seek to conform the Guaranty's governing law and jurisdiction to New York, matching the Agreement. If Supplier insists on English law (likely, given it is a UK company), at minimum negotiate: (a) a provision that the Guaranty shall be construed consistently with the Agreement to the extent possible; (b) confirmation that the defenses preserved in Section 2.4 carry the same scope under English law; and (c) a stay provision allowing Mibaix Holdings to seek a stay of London proceedings while related New York proceedings are pending.

## Issue 6 – No Agreed Fees for BX-900 Installation Services

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Annex 1, Part 1; Amendment No. 1, Section 8.2

**The Problem:** The SOW was executed on June 16, 2025, before the BX-900 was added to the product range. Annex 1, Part 1 of the SOW lists fixed fees only for the BX-300 ($38,500), BX-500 ($46,000), and BX-700 ($57,500) for Retrofit Installation and Commissioning. There is no fee for the BX-900. Section 8.2 of the Amendment states that the Parties "may enter into Statements of Work for Installation Services for the BX-900 under Section 8.2 of the Agreement," but no such SOW has been executed.

The forecast (Section 7.3) shows Mibaix expecting to commission at least three BX-900 units in the first half of 2027, and the port breakdown (Section 5.1) shows seven BX-900 units across the forecast period. Mibaix will be performing substantial BX-900 installation work without agreed compensation.

**Why It Matters:** Without a fixed fee or agreed rate for BX-900 work, Mibaix's compensation is uncertain. The SOW's time-and-materials rates (Annex 1, Part 3) could apply by analogy, but the BX-900 is a "high-capacity triple-reactor modular" system with a "remote monitoring gateway and ballast control system interface module" (Amendment Annex 1) – it is materially more complex than the BX-700. The BX-700 fixed fee is $57,500; a BX-900 retrofit could reasonably be expected to cost 30–50% more in labor. If the parties later dispute the appropriate fee, Mibaix is in a weak position because it will have already performed the work.

**Recommended Fix:** Execute a SOW No. 2 (or an amendment to SOW No. 1) that establishes fixed fees for BX-900 Retrofit Installation and Commissioning, and Commissioning Only, before the first BX-900 Work Order is issued. Use the BX-700 fees as a floor and adjust upward to reflect the additional complexity (triple reactor, remote monitoring gateway, BCS interface module). The forecast shows the first BX-900 commissioning expected in March 2027, so this should be done by at least January 2027 to allow for negotiation.

The forecast (Section 7.4) shows Mibaix proposing the week of November 16, 2026, at its Houston facility. That leaves only two weeks of buffer between the proposed training week and the contractual deadline.

Section 8.3 of the Agreement requires Mibaix to "use only technicians who have completed Supplier's training under Section 4.3(a)" for Installation Services. The first BX-900 commissionings are expected in March and April 2027 (Forecast Section 7.3). If training slips past November 30, 2026, Mibaix is in breach of the Amendment, and it may not have trained technicians available for the March 2027 commissionings, which would delay revenue and potentially trigger the liquidated damages in Section 7.2 of the Agreement (if the delay is attributed to Mibaix rather than Supplier).

**Why It Matters:** The training deadline is a hard contractual obligation. A delay puts Mibaix in breach and jeopardizes the March 2027 commissioning schedule. The forecast already assumes trained technicians are available for those jobs.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Confirm in writing with Supplier's training team (by email to fieldservice@bupaiv-ballast.co.uk, the operational contact in SOW Section 12.3) that trainers will be in Houston the week of November 16, 2026, and that the training will be completed by November 30.

## Issue 8 – Ex Works Delivery Terms Place Freight and Transit Risk on Mibaix

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Sections 6.3 and 7.1; Amendment No. 1, Annex 2

**The Problem:** All Products are priced Ex Works (Incoterms® 2020) Southampton. Mibaix bears the cost and risk of ocean freight from England to the Gulf Coast. For a BX-900 unit at $648,000 (or $622,080 net of the introductory credit), ocean freight, marine cargo insurance, and port handling could add $15,000–$30,000 per unit. Title and risk pass at Southampton (Section 7.3), so if a unit is damaged or lost in transit, Mibaix bears the loss (subject to its marine cargo insurance, which it must arrange at its own cost under Section 7.1).

**Why It Matters:** With 26 forecast units in 2027 plus 4 open POs, the aggregate freight and insurance cost is material. More importantly, the risk allocation means that any transit damage delays Mibaix's installation schedule and requires Mibaix to manage the claim against the cargo insurer while the vessel is in port waiting. The liquidated damages in Section 7.2 (0.5% per week, capped at 10%) apply only to Supplier's failure to make the Product "available for collection" by the confirmed delivery date – they do not cover transit delays after collection.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Negotiate a change to Delivered (Incoterms® 2020) at the port of destination, or at minimum to Cost and Freight (CFR), so that Supplier bears the ocean freight cost and risk of loss in transit.
- If Supplier resists (likely, given the Ex Works pricing is baked into the Schedule B prices), ensure Mibaix's marine cargo insurance program (arranged under Section 7.1) provides adequate coverage for the full replacement value of in-transit units, including delay in delivery cover for critical retrofit schedules.
- Add a provision that if a Product is lost or damaged in transit, the confirmed delivery date is reset and the Section 7.2 liquidated damages clock restarts from the date of loss, so that Mibaix is not penalized for a transit event beyond its control.

## Issue 9 – Guaranty: Ongoing Financial Statements Covenant

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Section 10.1

**The Problem:** For as long as the Guaranty remains in effect (which, per Section 12.1, could be many years after the Agreement ends), Mibaix Holdings must deliver to Supplier: (a) audited consolidated annual financial statements within 120 days of fiscal year-end; and (b) unaudited quarterly financial statements certified by the CFO within 60 days of quarter-end. Section 10.2 requires Supplier to treat these as Confidential Information under the Agreement.

**Why It Matters:** Post-Nordhaven acquisition, Mibaix Holdings will be a subsidiary of Nordhaven Maritime Group plc. Nordhaven may have its own confidentiality obligations, public disclosure requirements (if listed), or internal policies that conflict with providing quarterly financials to a counterparty. The 120-day audit deadline is also tight for a newly acquired subsidiary that may be integrating into Nordhaven's reporting calendar. A failure to deliver on time could be characterized as a breach of the Guaranty, giving Supplier leverage.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Before the Nordhaven closing, confirm with Nordhaven's finance and legal teams that the Section 10.1 covenant is compatible with their post-acquisition reporting structure.
- Negotiate an extension of the delivery deadlines (e.g., 150 days for annual, 75 days for quarterly) to accommodate integration.
- Add a provision that a failure to deliver financial statements on time does not constitute a default under the Guaranty or give rise to any remedy other than a request for the statements, unless the failure continues for more than 30 days after written notice.
- Confirm that Section 10.2's confidentiality obligation is mutual in practice – i.e., that Supplier will not use Mibaix Holdings' financial information for any purpose other than credit monitoring, and will not disclose it to Nordhaven's competitors or in any regulatory filing.

## Issue 10 – Insurance: Premium Finance Dependency and 10-Day Cancellation Gap

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter, Sections 7.2 and 10.1

**The Problem:** Mibaix's insurance premiums are paid under a "premium finance arrangement" providing for "automatic monthly payments from Mibaix's operating account" (Cover Letter Section 7.2). The insurer's notice of cancellation for non-payment of premium is only 10 days (Cover Letter Section 7.1(b)), whereas the Agreement requires 30 days' notice of cancellation (Agreement Section 14.3). Mibaix has confirmed it will "itself give Bupaiv... thirty (30) days' written notice under Section 14.3" if coverage is to be cancelled, but this is a self-imposed obligation, not a contractual one enforceable by Supplier.

**Why It Matters:** If Mibaix's operating account is strained – for example, by the increased $6.2M Minimum Annual Purchase requiring larger inventory purchases, or by the working capital demands of multiple concurrent BX-900 retrofits – a missed premium payment could result in cancellation on 10 days' notice. If Mibaix fails to independently give Supplier 30 days' notice (because it is unaware of the premium finance failure until the insurer's 10-day notice arrives), Mibaix would be in breach of Section 14.3 of the Agreement at the moment coverage is cancelled.

**Recommended Fix:**
- Ensure the premium finance arrangement has adequate reserves or a backup payment source so that a single missed operating account debit does not trigger cancellation.
- Set up an internal alert in Mibaix's treasury function to monitor the premium finance account and to trigger the 30-day notice to Supplier well before the insurer's 10-day notice would expire.
- Consider asking the insurer to extend the non-payment cancellation notice to 30 days to align with the Agreement, or obtaining a rider that the 10-day notice is deemed to satisfy the 30-day requirement in the Agreement (this would require Supplier's consent).

## Issue 12 – Guaranty: Reinstatement Clause Survives Release

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Sections 7.1 and 7.2

**The Problem:** Section 7.1 provides that if any payment under the Guaranty is "avoided, rescinded or must otherwise be returned in any Insolvency Proceeding or for any other reason," the Guaranty is reinstated as to that payment. Section 7.2 extends this reinstatement even after the Guaranty has been formally released under Section 12, provided Supplier notifies Mibaix Holdings within two years after the release.

**Why It Matters:** In a scenario where Mibaix becomes insolvent and a trustee avoids a payment made by Mibaix Holdings under the Guaranty (e.g., as a fraudulent conveyance or preferential transfer), Mibaix Holdings' liability is revived. The two-year post-release tail means that even after Mibaix Holdings receives a written release, it remains exposed for two more years. This is particularly relevant given the Nordhaven transaction: if the acquisition triggers a restructuring that impairs Mibaix's ability to pay, and Mibaix Holdings has already made payments under the Guaranty, those payments could be clawed back.

**Recommended Fix:** Negotiate a shorter reinstatement tail (e.g., 90 days after release) or limit reinstatement to payments avoided in an Insolvency Proceeding (deleting "or for any other reason"). At minimum, ensure that the release under Section 12.2 is in a form that expressly states the date of release so that the two-year period in Section 7.2 is clearly defined.

## Issue 13 – Order of Precedence May Disadvantage Mibaix on SOW Terms

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 21.3

**The Problem:** Section 21.3 provides that in case of conflict, the order of precedence is: (1) Amendments, (2) the Distribution Agreement, (3) Statements of Work. This means that if a SOW term conflicts with the Agreement, the Agreement controls. While this might seem neutral, it cuts against Mibaix in one specific respect: the SOW contains terms that are more favorable to Mibaix than the Agreement (e.g., Section 8.4's presumption that installation satisfies the Section 12.6(a) warranty condition). If a future dispute arises and Supplier argues that the Agreement's general warranty exclusion language controls over the SOW's specific presumption, Mibaix loses the benefit of the SOW term.

**Why It Matters:** This is a latent issue that may not surface unless there is a warranty dispute. But if a product fails and Supplier argues the failure was caused by Mibaix's installation, the SOW Section 8.4 presumption (that installation satisfies the manual-compliance requirement unless Supplier affirmatively shows otherwise) would be Mibaix's best defense. If the Agreement's Section 12.6(a) exclusion is read to control, the burden shifts to Mibaix to prove its installation was proper.

**Recommended Fix:** Add a proviso to Section 21.3 (by amendment or side letter) providing that where a SOW contains a term specifically negotiated for the benefit of the performing party (Distributor) that is more specific than a general provision of the Agreement, the SOW term controls. Alternatively, restate SOW Section 8.4 in the next amendment to the Agreement so that it sits at the same precedence level.

## Issue 14 – Most Favoured Pricing Clause: Scope and Verification

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 6.4; Amendment No. 1, Section 4.4

**The Problem:** Section 6.4 provides that prices charged to Mibaix "shall be no higher than the prices Supplier charges any other distributor in North America for the same Product in comparable quantities and on comparable terms." The Amendment extends this to the BX-900. However, the clause has no verification mechanism: Mibaix has no right to audit Supplier's pricing to other distributors, no notice requirement when Supplier grants a lower price, and no remedy specified if the clause is breached (other than general damages, subject to the Section 11 cap).

**Why It Matters:** If Supplier has other North American distributors (e.g., on the East Coast or West Coast) receiving lower prices, Mibaix would be overpaying without knowing it. The "comparable quantities and on comparable terms" qualifier gives Supplier an easy defense – it could argue that different volume tiers or payment terms justify different prices.

**Recommended Fix:** Add a verification right: Mibaix may request confirmation from Supplier, no more than once per Contract Year, that the prices in the Price List comply with Section 6.4. If Mibaix identifies evidence (e.g., from a customer conversation or public tender) that another North American distributor is receiving a lower price for the same Product in comparable quantities, Mibaix may require Supplier to demonstrate compliance within 30 days. Add a remedy: if Supplier is in breach, it shall issue a credit note for the difference applied to future invoices.

## Issue 15 – SOW: Deemed Acceptance Period May Be Too Short for Complex BX-900 Jobs

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Section 5.4

**The Problem:** Section 5.4 provides that a Job is "deemed accepted" if neither the Owner nor Supplier signs the Acceptance Certificate or delivers a written list of material non-conformities "within five (5) Business Days after presentation." For a BX-900 retrofit – a high-capacity triple-reactor system with remote monitoring and BCS interface – five Business Days may be insufficient for the Owner's representative (who may be offshore or in another time zone) to review the commissioning results and either sign or raise non-conformities.

**Why It Matters:** If deemed acceptance triggers prematurely, Mibaix's 40% mobilization payment and 60% acceptance payment (Section 7.3) become due, but more importantly, the 12-month workmanship warranty clock (Section 8.1) starts running. If a latent issue with the BX-900 integration surfaces after deemed acceptance but before the Owner has had a reasonable opportunity to inspect, Mibaix may face a warranty claim outside the deemed-acceptance protection.

Conversely, if the Owner does sign and raises non-conformities, Mibaix must correct them "at its own cost" (Section 5.5). For a complex BX-900 installation, the line between a "material non-conformity" (Mibaix's cost) and a "punch-list item" (does not delay acceptance) may be disputed.

**Recommended Fix:** Extend the deemed acceptance period to 10 Business Days for Jobs involving a BX-900 unit, or add a provision that the period runs from the later of (a) presentation of the Acceptance Certificate and (b) the Owner's receipt of the commissioning data package. Clarify in the SOW that "material non-conformity" means a deficiency that affects safe operation, type approval compliance, or the BWTS's ability to meet discharge standards – consistent with the existing language in Section 5.5 – and that aesthetic or minor calibration items are punch-list by default.
