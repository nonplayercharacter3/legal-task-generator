## Priority 1 – Critical Issues

### 1. Non-Compete Prohibits Mibaix's Existing Exhaust Gas Scrubber Business

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 3.4 (Non-Competition); Recital B.

**Issue:** Section 3.4 prohibits Mibaix, during the Term and for 24 months thereafter, from directly or indirectly "manufactur[ing], distribut[ing], sell[ing], market[ing] or servic[ing] any ballast water treatment, exhaust gas scrubber or other marine environmental compliance equipment other than Products, anywhere in the world." Recital B expressly acknowledges that Mibaix "currently operates an exhaust gas scrubber servicing business at Gulf and Caribbean ports." The non-compete therefore captures Mibaix's existing scrubber servicing revenue stream and bars it worldwide for the duration of the Term (five-year Initial Term plus potential two-year Renewal Terms) plus 24 months—potentially nine or more years. The restriction also extends to Affiliates under Mibaix's control and prohibits holding any interest in, or acting as agent or consultant for, any person engaged in these activities.

**Why it matters:** This is the single most commercially destructive provision in the arrangement. If enforced as written, Mibaix must abandon or divest its scrubber servicing business—a business it operated before the Agreement and that is unrelated to Bupaiv's Products. The worldwide geographic scope and the catch-all phrase "other marine environmental compliance equipment" make the restriction even broader than the express reference to scrubbers. A court in New York (the governing forum under Section 19.2) might find the restriction overbroad, but Mibaix would face significant litigation cost and uncertainty, and the restriction chills competitive activity even if ultimately unenforceable.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to Section 3.4 that (a) carves out Mibaix's exhaust gas scrubber servicing business as it existed on the Effective Date (with a schedule identifying the relevant vessels, customers and service contracts); (b) narrows "other marine environmental compliance equipment" to equipment that directly competes with a specific Bupaiv Product line; (c) limits the geographic scope to the Territory rather than worldwide; and (d) reduces the post-termination tail from 24 months to 12 months. If Supplier resists, Mibaix should at minimum obtain a written acknowledgment that the scrubber servicing business is excluded, documented as a side letter or amendment.

### 2. Pending Nordhaven Change of Control Gives Supplier a Termination Right

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 17.2 (Change of Control) and Section 21.5 (Waiver); Parent Guaranty, Recital B.

**Issue:** Recital B of the Parent Guaranty discloses that Mibaix Holdings Corp. entered into a share purchase agreement dated August 28, 2026 with Nordhaven Maritime Group plc, under which Nordhaven will acquire all shares of Mibaix Holdings, with closing expected in Q1 2027. Section 17.2 of the Distribution Agreement permits Supplier to terminate on 90 days' notice if a Change of Control of Distributor occurs "without Supplier's prior written consent." The defined term "Change of Control" (Section 17.2) captures any acquisition of more than 50% of the voting securities of Distributor "or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Distributor"—squarely describing the Nordhaven transaction. Critically, Section 21.5 provides that "Supplier's knowledge of any fact, event or circumstance, including any proposed or completed transaction affecting the ownership of Distributor, does not constitute consent or waiver." Although Supplier knew of the transaction when it executed Amendment No. 1 and the Guaranty on September 15, 2026, that knowledge is expressly deemed not to constitute consent.

**Why it matters:** Once the Nordhaven acquisition closes (expected Q1 2027), Supplier has the right to terminate the entire Distribution Agreement on 90 days' notice. It may exercise that right at any time within 12 months after it "first learns" of the Change of Control. Because Supplier learned of the transaction no later than August 28, 2026 (the SPA signing date, disclosed in the Guaranty), the 12-month window runs to approximately August 28, 2027. Termination would destroy Mibaix's exclusive distribution rights, its investment in the BX-900 launch, its trained technicians, and its customer pipeline. The timing is particularly dangerous: the first BX-900 retrofits are scheduled for March–April 2027, and the joint launch program runs through June 30, 2027.

**Recommended fix:** Before the Nordhaven closing, Mibaix should obtain Supplier's prior written consent to the Change of Control, as Section 17.2 requires. This should be documented as a written consent or waiver executed by Supplier, specifically referencing the Nordhaven transaction and stating that Supplier will not exercise its termination right under Section 17.2 in connection with it. If Supplier conditions consent on commercial concessions (e.g., a higher MAP or extended term), Mibaix should evaluate those terms before agreeing. If Supplier refuses consent, Mibaix should consider whether to proceed with the Nordhaven closing at all, or whether to negotiate a buyout or transition arrangement with Supplier in advance.

### 3. SOW No. 1 Lacks BX-900 Installation Fees

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Annex 1, Part 1 (Fixed Fees for Reserved Account Jobs); Amendment No. 1, Section 8.2.

**Issue:** SOW No. 1 was executed June 16, 2025, before the BX-900 existed. Annex 1, Part 1 sets fixed fees only for the BX-300 ($38,500), BX-500 ($46,000), and BX-700 ($57,500) for Retrofit Installation and Commissioning, and only for the BX-300 ($6,800), BX-500 ($7,900), and BX-700 ($9,400) for Commissioning Only. There is no fee schedule for the BX-900. Amendment No. 1, Section 8.2 states that existing SOWs "remain in full force and effect" and that the Parties "may enter into Statements of Work for Installation Services for the BX-900"—but no such SOW has been executed. The Q4 2026 forecast (Section 7.1) shows three Reserved Account jobs expected during 2027, and Section 7.3 shows three BX-900 commissionings for which Supplier's field engineer will attend. If Supplier issues a Work Order under SOW No. 1 for a BX-900 Retrofit Installation, there is no applicable fixed fee.

**Why it matters:** The BX-900 is a larger, more complex system (triple-reactor modular, up to 1,000 m³/h, with a remote monitoring gateway and ballast control system interface module per Annex 1 to Amendment No. 1). Its installation will require more labor, more specialized skills, and longer vessel downtime than the BX-700. Without an agreed fee, Mibaix faces either (a) performing the work at a fee that doesn't cover its costs, or (b) declining the Work Order and losing the Minimum Annual Purchase credit under Section 3.2 of the Agreement (which requires Mibaix to "perform the Installation Services" to earn the credit). The order of precedence in Section 21.3 of the Agreement (Amendments > Agreement > SOWs) does not help because no Amendment or SOW addresses BX-900 fees.

**Recommended fix:** Execute an amendment to SOW No. 1 (or a new SOW No. 2) that adds BX-900 fixed fees to Annex 1, Part 1. The fee should reflect the increased complexity: we recommend a Retrofit Installation and Commissioning fee of no less than $72,000 (approximately 25% above the BX-700 rate, reflecting the triple-reactor configuration and additional integration scope) and a Commissioning Only fee of no less than $12,000. Also add a time-and-materials fallback for any BX-900 work that falls outside the standard scope. This should be done before the first BX-900 Work Order is issued (expected Q1 2027 based on the forecast).

## Priority 2 – High-Impact Issues

### 4. Parent Guaranty Subject to English Law and Exclusive English Jurisdiction

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Section 14 (Governing Law and Jurisdiction); Distribution Agreement, Section 19 (Governing Law and Dispute Resolution).

**Issue:** The Distribution Agreement is governed by New York law with exclusive jurisdiction in New York courts (Sections 19.1–19.2). The Parent Guaranty is governed by English law with exclusive jurisdiction in the courts of England and Wales (Sections 14.1–14.2). Mibaix Holdings has appointed a process agent in London (Lexmere Process Services Limited, Section 14.3) and waived immunity from jurisdiction (Section 14.4).

**Why it matters:** This creates a jurisdictional fork. If Supplier seeks to enforce the Guaranty (e.g., after Mibaix defaults on payment), it must sue Mibaix Holdings in England. If Supplier seeks to enforce the Distribution Agreement (e.g., for a non-payment default under Section 17.1), it must sue Mibaix in New York. Supplier could file in both forums simultaneously, forcing Mibaix to defend itself in two jurisdictions under two different bodies of law. English law may construe guaranty provisions (particularly the broad waivers in Sections 6.1–6.2, the reinstatement clause in Section 7, and the subrogation restrictions in Section 8) differently from New York or Delaware law. Mibaix Holdings, as a Delaware corporation, is less familiar with English procedural rules and may face higher costs defending in London.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to Section 14 of the Parent Guaranty to conform the governing law and jurisdiction to New York, matching the Distribution Agreement. If Supplier insists on English law (perhaps because it is a UK company and its counsel prefers home-turf litigation), Mibaix should at minimum: (a) confirm that the New York forum clause in the Distribution Agreement applies to any dispute about whether a Guaranteed Obligation is validly due (i.e., the underlying payment dispute is resolved in New York first, and only the enforcement of the Guaranty goes to England); and (b) ensure that the Guaranty expressly provides that any determination in New York that a Guaranteed Obligation is not validly due is binding on the English court for purposes of the Guaranty.

### 5. Parent Guaranty Covers Future Amendments Without Mibaix Holdings' Consent

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Section 5.1(a); Amendment No. 1, Section 5.2.

**Issue:** Section 5.1(a) of the Guaranty provides that Guarantor's obligations are not affected by "any amendment, supplement, extension, renewal or replacement of the Agreement or any Statement of Work, including any change to the Products, the Price List, the credit limit or the payment terms," with the sole exception that an increase in the revolving credit limit above $2,500,000 requires Guarantor's written consent under Section 5.2. This means that if the Distribution Agreement is amended in the future—whether to add new products, increase prices, extend the Term, change payment terms, or add new obligations—Mibaix Holdings automatically guarantees those new obligations without any further action or consent.

**Why it matters:** Mibaix Holdings' exposure is open-ended. The current Guaranteed Obligations already include the price of all Products, freight, insurance, handling charges, field service engineer fees, interest, and enforcement costs (Section 1.1). If the Agreement is amended to, for example, add a new product line at higher price points, extend the Term by multiple renewal periods, or impose new fee structures, Mibaix Holdings is on the hook for all of it. The only protection is the $2.5M cap on the product price component (Section 5.2), which does not apply to the other components of the Guaranteed Obligations.

**Recommended fix:** Amend Section 5.1(a) of the Guaranty to require Mibaix Holdings' written consent for any amendment that (a) increases the Maximum Annual Purchase, (b) extends the Term beyond the Initial Term plus one Renewal Term, (c) adds new Products not listed in Schedule A as of the Amendment Effective Date, or (d) increases the unit prices by more than the Section 6.3 cap. Alternatively, add an overall cap on Mibaix Holdings' aggregate liability under the Guaranty (e.g., not to exceed $5,000,000 in the aggregate).

### 6. Minimum Annual Purchase Increase to $6.2M with Loss of Exclusivity as Sole Remedy

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Section 3.1; Distribution Agreement, Section 5.3 (Shortfall).

**Issue:** The MAP increases from $4,800,000 to $6,200,000 (a 29.2% increase) effective for the Contract Year beginning March 3, 2027. Under Section 5.3 of the Agreement, if Mibaix fails to meet the MAP, Supplier's sole remedy is to convert the exclusive appointment to non-exclusive. The Q4 2026 forecast projects $10,837,840 in purchases for the Contract Year beginning March 3, 2027 (through December 31, 2027 only), which appears to exceed the MAP comfortably. However, the forecast includes 26 units at varying confidence levels (6 Grade A, 9 Grade B, 8 Grade C, 3 Grade D per Section 4.4), and Section 11.3 acknowledges that 10–15% of forecast units typically shift by one quarter between successive forecasts.

**Why it matters:** If the BX-900 launch underperforms, or if several Grade C/D opportunities are lost in competitive tenders (Section 11.4), Mibaix could miss the $6.2M threshold. The consequence—loss of exclusivity in the four-port Territory—would be existential for Mibaix's distribution business. Supplier could then appoint competing distributors in Houston, New Orleans, Mobile, and Corpus Christi, undermining Mibaix's customer relationships, its installed base service revenue, and the value of its investment in BX-900 training and launch marketing.

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate one or more of the following: (a) a phase-in of the increased MAP (e.g., $5,500,000 for the first year at the new level, $6,200,000 thereafter); (b) a higher threshold for triggering the non-exclusive conversion (e.g., Mibaix must miss the MAP by more than 15% rather than any amount); (c) a cure period longer than 90 days; or (d) a requirement that Supplier give Mibaix notice and an opportunity to discuss the shortfall before converting to non-exclusive. Also consider negotiating a most-favored-nations clause on the MAP itself, so that if Supplier grants a lower MAP to any other distributor in a comparable territory, Mibaix's MAP is reduced accordingly.

### 7. SOW Installation Indemnity for Environmental Claims

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Section 6.2 (Installation Indemnity); Distribution Agreement, Sections 10.2 (Environmental Indemnity by Supplier) and 12.6(a) (Warranty Exclusion for Improper Installation).

**Issue:** SOW Section 6.2 requires Mibaix to indemnify Supplier for "any Environmental Claim arising out of or relating to the Installation Services performed by Distributor or its subcontractors." An "Environmental Claim" is defined in Section 1.1 of the Agreement as "any claim, fine or penalty arising from a discharge of ballast water or treatment residuals." Meanwhile, Section 10.2 of the Agreement provides that Supplier indemnifies Mibaix for Environmental Claims "to the extent the Environmental Claim results from a Defect in a Product." Section 12.6(a) excludes from the product warranty any Defect "resulting from installation not performed in accordance with Supplier's installation manuals."

**Why it matters:** The interaction of these provisions creates a gap and a potential trap. If a BWTS fails to meet discharge standards and an Environmental Claim results, the allocation of indemnity depends on whether the failure is attributed to a Product Defect (Supplier indemnifies Mibaix under Section 10.2) or to the Installation Services (Mibaix indemnifies Supplier under SOW Section 6.2). In practice, the line between a product design/manufacturing defect and an installation error can be difficult to draw—particularly for a complex system like the BX-900 with its remote monitoring gateway and ballast control system interface module. If a vessel is detained or fined because the BWTS doesn't meet 33 C.F.R. § 151.2030, and the cause is ambiguous, Mibaix could face the Environmental Claim AND lose the product warranty (under Section 12.6(a)) AND owe an indemnity to Supplier (under SOW Section 6.2). Environmental Claims can involve significant fines (the Vessel Incidental Discharge Act provides for penalties up to approximately $28,000 per violation per day), remediation costs, and third-party claims.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SOW Section 6.2 to clarify that Mibaix's installation indemnity applies only to Environmental Claims "to the extent resulting from Distributor's failure to perform the Installation Services in accordance with Supplier's installation manuals and commissioning procedures then in effect," and does not apply where the Environmental Claim results from a Defect in the Product or from an error in Supplier's drawings, specifications or instructions. Add a cooperation mechanism: if the cause of an Environmental Claim is disputed, the Parties shall engage a mutually agreed independent marine engineer to determine whether the cause was product-related or installation-related, and the indemnity follows that determination.

## Priority 3 – Moderate-Impact Issues

### 8. BX-900 Training Deadline Creates Schedule Risk

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Section 2.5 (BX-900 Training); Distribution Agreement, Section 8.3 (Installation Standards); Q4 2026 Forecast, Section 7.4.

**Issue:** Section 2.5 of Amendment No. 1 requires Supplier to complete BX-900 training for up to eight of Mibaix's technicians "by no later than November 30, 2026." The forecast proposes the week of November 16, 2026 at Mibaix's Houston facility. Section 8.3 of the Agreement requires Mibaix to use "only technicians who have completed Supplier's training" for Installation Services. The first BX-900 retrofits are scheduled for March–April 2027 (forecast Sections 3.2 and 7.3). If the training is delayed beyond November 30, 2026, Mibaix has a narrow window (approximately 3.5 months) to train its technicians before the first installations. More critically, Section 5.1 of the Agreement provides MAP proration only for delays in the *initial* training under Section 4.3(a)—not for the additional BX-900 training under Amendment Section 2.5. So if BX-900 training is delayed, Mibaix gets no MAP relief but cannot perform BX-900 installations.

**Why it matters:** A training delay could cascade into missed retrofit windows (vessel dry-dockings are fixed by charter schedules), lost customer confidence, and potential MAP shortfall if BX-900 units are ordered but cannot be installed. The forecast assumes training completion by November 30, 2026; if this slips, the entire BX-900 launch timeline is at risk.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Confirm in writing with Supplier that the training will take place the week of November 16, 2026, and obtain a commitment that Supplier's trainers will be at Mibaix's Houston facility by November 15. (b) Negotiate an extension of the training deadline in Amendment No. 1 to January 31, 2027, with a corresponding extension of the first BX-900 retrofit window. (c) Add a provision that if Supplier fails to complete the BX-900 training by the agreed date, the MAP for the affected period is prorated in the same manner as Section 5.1 of the Agreement.

### 9. Insurance Cancellation Notice Gap for Non-Payment

**Where:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter, Sections 7.1(b) and 7.2; Distribution Agreement, Section 14.3.

**Issue:** Section 14.3 of the Agreement requires 30 days' written notice of cancellation or material reduction of any required coverage. The insurance certificates provide 30 days' notice for cancellation other than for non-payment, but only 10 days' notice for cancellation for non-payment of premium (Section 7.1(b) of the cover letter; endorsements SHC-NC-030, MSR-NC-02, WC 99 06 31, and SHC-NC-031 in Attachment B). The cover letter (Section 7.2) acknowledges this gap and states that Mibaix "will itself give Bupaiv Ballast Technologies Ltd. thirty (30) days' written notice under Section 14.3 of the Agreement if any of its required coverage is to be cancelled for any reason." However, if the insurer cancels for non-payment on 10 days' notice, Mibaix may learn of the cancellation at the same time as Bupaiv (or even after, if the insurer's notice reaches Bupaiv first), leaving Mibaix unable to provide the full 30 days' notice.

**Why it matters:** A technical breach of Section 14.3 could give Supplier grounds to argue that Mibaix is in material breach of the Agreement (Section 17.1), potentially supporting a termination for cause. While this seems like a remote scenario (Mibaix's premiums are currently paid under a premium finance arrangement with no past-due installments), the risk is elevated if Mibaix experiences cash flow difficulties—precisely the situation in which a cancellation for non-payment is most likely.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Ensure the premium finance arrangement has adequate reserves to cover at least 60 days of premiums, reducing the risk of non-payment. (b) Request that the insurers extend the non-payment cancellation notice to 30 days for the certificate holder, even if the standard market position is 10 days. (c) Add a provision to the Agreement (or a side letter) confirming that a good-faith effort by Mibaix to provide 30 days' notice, where the insurer's own notice period is shorter, does not constitute a breach of Section 14.3.

### 10. SOW Termination for Convenience Could Cut Off Installation Revenue

**Where:** SOW No. 1, Section 11.2 (Termination for Convenience).

**Issue:** Either party may terminate SOW No. 1 on 90 days' written notice. Termination does not affect Work Orders accepted before the notice, but it prevents Supplier from issuing new Work Orders. The SOW is the vehicle through which Mibaix earns revenue from Reserved Account installations, warranty work, and recall work.

**Why it matters:** If Supplier terminates the SOW (e.g., after a Change of Control termination of the main Agreement, or for strategic reasons), Mibaix loses its pipeline of Reserved Account installation work and its right to perform warranty work for fee. While Mibaix can still perform installations for its own customers under the Distribution Agreement, the SOW work represents additional revenue and, for Reserved Account jobs, credit toward the MAP (Section 3.2 of the Agreement).

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate an amendment to SOW Section 11.2 to (a) require Supplier to provide 180 days' notice (rather than 90) if it terminates for convenience; (b) provide that Supplier shall pay Mibaix a transition fee equal to the projected fees for Work Orders in the pipeline at the time of notice; and (c) confirm that SOW termination does not affect Mibaix's right to perform Installation Services for its own customers under the Distribution Agreement or to service the installed base under the warranty provisions.

### 11. Order of Precedence Subordinates SOW to Agreement and Amendments

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 21.3 (Order of Precedence).

**Issue:** Section 21.3 provides that in case of conflict, "Amendments" prevail over the "Distribution Agreement," which prevails over "Statements of Work." This means that any term in SOW No. 1 that is more favorable to Mibaix than the corresponding term in the Agreement or an Amendment is effectively overridden.

**Why it matters:** For example, SOW Section 7.5 gives Mibaix a set-off right against amounts Supplier owes under the SOW. If the Agreement were amended to restrict set-off rights generally, the SOW set-off right would be overridden. Similarly, SOW Section 8.4 provides that Mibaix's Installation Services "satisfy the requirement in Section 12.6(a)" of the Agreement (that installation be performed in accordance with Supplier's manuals) "except to the extent Supplier shows that a particular installation did not conform." If the Agreement were amended to remove this safe harbor, the SOW provision would fall away. While this is a standard precedence clause, Mibaix should be aware that its SOW protections are only as durable as the Agreement provisions they reference.

**Recommended fix:** No immediate action required, but Mibaix should review the SOW each time the Agreement or an Amendment is changed to confirm that no SOW provision has been inadvertently overridden. If a critical SOW provision is at risk, it should be elevated into the Agreement or an Amendment.

### 12. Parent Guaranty Reinstatement Tail

**Where:** Parent Guaranty, Sections 7.1 and 7.2 (Reinstatement).

**Issue:** Section 7.1 provides that if any payment under the Guaranty is avoided, rescinded or must be returned in an Insolvency Proceeding (or for any other reason), the Guaranty is reinstated as to that payment. Section 7.2 extends this reinstatement even after the Guaranty has been released under Section 12, provided Supplier notifies Mibaix Holdings within two years after the release.

**Why it matters:** If Mibaix (the Distributor) becomes insolvent and a court avoids a payment made by Mibaix Holdings under the Guaranty (e.g., as a fraudulent transfer), Mibaix Holdings must pay again—even if the Guaranty was formally released. The two-year tail after release means Mibaix Holdings cannot be certain it is fully clear of the Guaranty for at least two years after receiving a release. This is a standard feature of guaranties but represents a long-tail risk that Mibaix Holdings should factor into its financial planning, particularly given the pending Nordhaven transaction (which could itself trigger insolvency-related scrutiny).

**Recommended fix:** Negotiate a reduction of the reinstatement tail from two years to one year. Alternatively, obtain a representation from Supplier that it will not seek reinstatement if the underlying payment was made in the ordinary course and for fair consideration. Document Mibaix Holdings' solvency at the time of any payment under the Guaranty to protect against fraudulent transfer challenges.
