### 1. Kestrune Engine License – Anti-Assignment Consent Missing from Required Consents

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9, Item 3; Schedule 4.5; APA § 2.1(c), § 7.2(d)

Schedule 4.9, Item 3 discloses that Section 15.3 of the Kestrune Engine License prohibits assignment "without the prior written consent of Kestrune," and that "[a]ny assignment made without that consent is void." The license covers the proprietary game engine, runtime, and editor tools on which every Emberfall Saga title and the unreleased sequel are built.

Schedule 4.5 (Required Consents) lists only Halvern Console Network and Ostrava Entertainment Systems. Kestrune is absent. Because the closing condition in APA § 7.2(d) requires only that "each Required Consent set forth on Schedule 4.5" be obtained, Kestrune's consent is not a condition to Closing.

**Why it matters:** Without Kestrune's written consent, the assignment of the Engine License to Treikaith is void. Treikaith would close a $48 million transaction and not have a valid license to the engine underlying the entire franchise. The sequel build (APA § 2.1(b)(v)) cannot be developed or released without it. This is an existential deal risk.

**Recommendation:**
- Add Kestrune to Schedule 4.5 as a Required Consent before re-signing or as a Schedule Supplement.
- Make Kestrune's consent a condition to Closing under § 7.2(d).
- Begin the consent request immediately; do not assume Kestrune will cooperate.
- If Kestrune refuses or is unresponsive, assess whether the deal can proceed and consider a specific indemnity or price adjustment.

### 2. Side Letter Eliminates Excluded Liabilities Carve-Out from the Indemnity Cap

**Where:** Side Letter (Oct. 21, 2026) § 2.1 (restating APA § 9.5(b)); APA § 9.5(a), § 9.5(b) (original), § 2.4, § 9.2(c)

Original APA § 9.5(b) provided that the Cap ($4,800,000) and the threshold ($240,000) do not apply to Losses arising from (i) Fraud, (ii) Fundamental Representations, or (iii) "Seller's obligations in respect of Excluded Liabilities pursuant to Section 9.2(c)."

The Side Letter restates § 9.5(b) to carve out only Fraud and Fundamental Representations. Clause (iii) is deleted. Excluded Liabilities are now subject to the $4.8 million Cap.

**Why it matters:** The Excluded Liabilities include the *Calloway* putative nationwide consumer class action (Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.4, Item 1), which alleges violations of the Texas DTPA and other state consumer protection statutes regarding randomized "Ember Cache" purchases, seeking restitution, statutory damages, attorneys' fees, and injunctive relief. Under the Texas DTPA, knowing violations carry treble damages up to $20,000 per consumer. A nationwide class could produce a judgment well in excess of $4.8 million. The related Texas Attorney General inquiry (Schedule 4.7, Item 2) presents additional exposure.

After the Side Letter, Treikaith's maximum recovery for all Excluded Liabilities combined is $4,800,000—the same amount held in escrow. Any excess is unrecoverable.

**Recommendation:**
- This appears to be a negotiated concession, but the partner should confirm whether Treikaith's commercial team understood the practical effect before the Side Letter was signed.
- If the deal is still in play, consider restoring the Excluded Liabilities carve-out or, at minimum, negotiating a separate, uncapped indemnity for the *Calloway* litigation and the Texas AG matter.
- If the Side Letter stands, ensure the escrow is not released prematurely (see Issue 4 below) and evaluate whether the purchase price should be reduced to reflect the capped exposure.

### 3. Supplement No. 1 – Deemed Acceptance Likely; Orvenna Claim May Be Treated as Disclosed and Cured

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule, Part 3 (Supplement No. 1, dated Oct. 2, 2026); APA § 6.3(b); Side Letter § 1.2(c)–(d)

Supplement No. 1 was delivered by email on October 2, 2026 (a Friday). APA § 6.3(b) gives Buyer five (5) Business Days after receipt to object. Counting from October 2: Monday Oct. 5 (1), Tuesday Oct. 6 (2), Wednesday Oct. 7 (3), Thursday Oct. 8 (4), Friday Oct. 9 (5). The objection deadline was **October 9, 2026**.

Buyer's written objection was delivered **October 16, 2026**—two Business Days late. The Side Letter (§ 1.2(d)) confirms that Seller contends the Supplement was deemed accepted and Buyer disputes that contention.

Under § 6.3(b), a deemed-accepted Supplement "is treated as having cured any inaccuracy in or breach of any representation or warranty disclosed in it, for purposes of Article IX and of the condition in Section 7.2(a)."

**Why it matters:** Supplement No. 1 discloses the Orvenna Sound Collective copyright claim (main theme and combat suite of the Emberfall Saga soundtrack). If deemed accepted:
- The inaccuracy in Seller's representation under § 4.12(a) (Title to IP—a **Fundamental Representation**) is cured for indemnification and closing-condition purposes.
- Treikaith cannot claim indemnification for the Orvenna matter, even though it implicates a Fundamental Rep.
- The closing condition in § 7.2(a) (Fundamental Reps true and correct in all respects) is satisfied despite the title defect.

The 30-day deadline in Orvenna's demand letter (received Sept. 28, 2026) expired approximately October 28, 2026. It is unclear whether Orvenna has filed for injunctive relief.

**Recommendation:**
- **Urgently confirm** whether Orvenna has filed suit or a TRO/PI action in any court. If injunctive relief has been sought or is imminent, the practical risk is severe regardless of the indemnity question.
- Assess whether there is any factual basis to argue the email was not "received" on October 2 (e.g., confirmation of transmission timestamp, server logs). The APA § 11.2 notice provision deems email notice effective "when sent by email with confirmation of transmission," which likely fixes the date at October 2.
- If deemed acceptance stands, negotiate a specific indemnity for the Orvenna matter outside the general framework, or require Seller to resolve the claim pre-Closing.
- Consider whether to make Orvenna resolution a condition to Closing.

### 4. Escrow Release Date (6 Months) Is Inconsistent with General Rep Survival (18 Months)

**Where:** Escrow Agreement § 4.2 (Release Date = 6 months post-Closing); APA § 9.1 (General Reps survive 18 months); APA § 9.6 (escrow is "sole and exclusive source of recovery" for General Rep breaches)

APA § 9.6 provides that the Indemnity Escrow Funds "are the sole and exclusive source of recovery" for Losses under § 9.2(a) arising from General Representation breaches (except Fraud). The Escrow Agreement releases all remaining funds to Seller at 6 months (less Pending Claims). General Reps survive for 18 months.

**Why it matters:** For any General Rep breach discovered or asserted between months 7 and 18 post-Closing, Treikaith has no escrow funds to draw on and no right to recover directly from Seller. The 18-month survival period is illusory for General Reps. In practice, Treikaith's protection for General Reps is limited to 6 months.

This is particularly problematic for reps like § 4.8 (Compliance with Laws, including data privacy and randomized purchase laws), § 4.9 (Material Contracts), and § 4.10 (Employees), where issues may not surface until after 6 months.

**Recommendation:**
- Extend the Escrow Release Date to 18 months to match the survival period, or shorten the General Rep survival to 6 months (the former is preferable for Treikaith).
- Alternatively, amend § 9.6 to permit direct recovery from Seller for General Rep breaches asserted after the Release Date.
- If Seller resists, consider a partial release (e.g., 50% at 6 months, 50% at 18 months).

## OTHER SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 5. IP Assignment Agreement Does Not Cover the Full Scope of Purchased IP

**Where:** IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1, Exhibit A; APA § 2.1(b) (Purchased IP definition)

The IP Assignment Agreement defines "Assigned IP" as only the registered trademarks and copyrights listed on Exhibit A (4 marks, 2 copyrights). The APA's "Purchased IP" (§ 2.1(b)) is far broader and includes:
- All source code, object code, build tools, scripts, pipelines, development tools
- All registered **and unregistered** copyrights
- Art assets, audio assets, animations, models, design documents
- Unreleased sequel builds, prototypes, works in progress
- Domain names (emberfallsaga.com, emberfall.gg) and social media accounts
- Goodwill
- Rights to sue for past, present, and future infringement

The IP Assignment Agreement's power of attorney (§ 3.3), recordation authorization (§ 3.1), and further assurances (§ 3.2) all operate only on "Assigned IP" as narrowly defined.

**Why it matters:** There is no dedicated assignment instrument for the source code, domain names, social media accounts, unregistered copyrights in art/audio/design documents, or the unreleased sequel. While the APA itself is an operative assignment (§ 2.1), the absence of a comprehensive IP assignment creates:
- Recording gaps at the USPTO and Copyright Office (only Exhibit A items can be recorded under this instrument).
- No mechanism to transfer domain names (which require registrar-level action).
- No specific assignment of social media accounts (which are governed by platform ToS).
- Ambiguity about whether the APA alone suffices to assign trade secrets and unregistered copyrights in source code.

**Recommendation:**
- Expand the IP Assignment Agreement's definition of "Assigned IP" (or add a new section) to cover all Purchased IP as defined in APA § 2.1(b), including unregistered copyrights, source code, domain names, social media accounts, and trade secrets.
- Add specific covenants for domain name transfer (registrar authorization) and social media account migration.
- Ensure the power of attorney (§ 3.3) covers the expanded scope.
- Confirm that the Bill of Sale (Exhibit A to the APA) does not inadvertently exclude intangible IP.

### 6. Key Employee Non-Compete Likely Unenforceable Under California Law

**Where:** Key Employee Agreement § 6.1 (non-compete), § 9.1 (California governing law, LA venue); APA § 6.10

Fenwright's non-compete restricts her for 24 months post-Closing from working on "premium action role-playing or fantasy adventure video games for console or PC platforms" anywhere in the US, Canada, UK, or EU. The agreement is governed by California law.

California Business & Professions Code § 16600 voids post-employment non-competes. Section 16601 (sale-of-business exception) permits a non-compete only when the restricting party is a seller of a business ownership interest and the restriction is in the sale agreement or as consideration for the sale. Here, the non-compete is in Fenwright's **employment agreement with Buyer**, not in the purchase agreement or as consideration for her sale of membership interests.

APA § 6.10 includes acknowledgments that Fenwright "holds a substantial economic interest in Seller" and that the covenants are "necessary to protect the goodwill," which supports a § 16601 argument. But the covenant's placement in the employment agreement, rather than the purchase agreement, weakens that argument under California precedent.

**Why it matters:** If the non-compete is unenforceable, Fenwright could leave Treikaith after Closing and immediately join a competitor or start her own studio developing a substantially similar game. Given her role as Creative Director and 34% former owner of Seller, she possesses the creative vision, relationships, and institutional knowledge that make the Emberfall Saga franchise valuable. The 24-month restriction is a key protection for the $48 million purchase price.

**Recommendation:**
- Move the non-compete (or a parallel covenant) into the APA or a separate agreement executed as part of the sale transaction, with consideration expressly tied to her sale of membership interests.
- Alternatively, negotiate a California-compliant "non-solicitation of customers/partners" covenant (which is permissible under § 16601) combined with robust confidentiality and IP assignment provisions.
- Consider whether the forfeiture provision (Key Employee Agreement § 8.2) provides any independent deterrent even if the non-compete is void.
- Consult California employment counsel before Closing.

### 7. Orvenna Sound Collective – Potential Injunction Risk and Chain-of-Title Defect

**Where:** Disclosure Schedule, Supplement No. 1 § 4.2 (Schedule 4.7, Item 3); Schedule 4.9, Item 8 (original); APA § 4.12(a)

Orvenna's demand asserts the opposite: that it owns copyright in the main theme and combat suite based on a 2019 co-writing arrangement with Hallorin Bridge members.

If Orvenna's claim has merit, then:
- The work-made-for-hire characterization in Schedule 4.9, Item 8 is false.
- Seller's § 4.12(a) representation (title to IP, a Fundamental Rep) is breached.
- The soundtrack (a core asset of the franchise) is subject to a competing ownership claim.
- Orvenna has threatened injunctive relief to "cease distribution of the affected tracks."

**Why it matters:** An injunction against distribution of the main theme and combat suite would be devastating to the franchise's marketability and could trigger platform compliance issues (APA § 4.13). Even if the claim is ultimately without merit, the litigation cost and distraction are significant. The 30-day response deadline (from Sept. 28) has likely passed.

**Recommendation:**
- Immediately confirm whether Orvenna has filed any action or TRO motion.
- Require Seller to produce the 2019 agreement (or lack thereof) with Hallorin Bridge and any correspondence with Orvenna.
- If the claim is not resolved pre-Closing, negotiate a specific escrow holdback or indemnity for the Orvenna matter, separate from the general Cap.
- Assess whether the Hallorin Bridge license (Schedule 2.1(c), Item 8) contains indemnification or warranty provisions that Seller can enforce.

### 8. Austin Lease – Transition Gap and Cost Considerations

**Where:** Austin Studio Office Lease § 1.1 (Commencement Date: Jan. 1, 2027); APA § 8.1(b) (outside date: Dec. 31, 2026); Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.6, Item 2 (Seller's current lease at 1100 East Sixth Street is an Excluded Asset)

Seller's existing Austin lease is excluded from the transaction. Treikaith's new lease at Comal Yard does not commence until January 1, 2027. If Closing occurs in late December 2026, the 38-person development team (Schedule 4.10) has no lease-covered space for the transition period.

Additional cost items:
- After-hours HVAC: $65/hour/floor (Lease § 6.2). A game studio routinely works evenings and weekends.
- Supplemental cooling tap fee: $750/ton/year for the server/build room (Lease § 6.4).
- TI allowance forfeiture: $1,202,500 allowance is forfeited if not requested by December 31, 2027 (Lease § 9.2).

**Why it matters:** The transition gap could disrupt operations and morale immediately post-Closing. The after-hours HVAC costs, if the team works 10 hours/day beyond the standard 7 a.m.–7 p.m. window, could add $65 × 10 × 22 working days × 12 months ≈ $171,600/year in incremental costs not reflected in the base rent.

**Recommendation:**
- Use the early access provision (Lease § 2.4) to begin fit-out before Closing if possible.
- Negotiate a short-term extension of Seller's current lease or a bridge arrangement for the gap period.
- Model after-hours HVAC and supplemental cooling costs into the post-Closing budget.

The Steamforge PC Digital Distribution Agreement may be assigned "on written notice to Steamforge." This is a notice obligation, not a consent, so it correctly does not appear in Schedule 4.5. However, no APA closing deliverable or covenant specifically requires Seller to give that notice.

**Why it matters:** If the notice is not given, the assignment may not be effective against Steamforge, and Treikaith could lose the ability to distribute on that platform.

### 10. Member Consent – Broad Delegation to Managing Member

**Where:** Daibaind Member Consent § 2.1

The consent authorizes the Managing Member (Venmoor) to approve "such changes as the Managing Member approves, with that approval conclusively evidenced by the Managing Member's execution of them." This means any amendment to the APA or Ancillary Agreements (e.g., the Side Letter) is validly authorized on the Seller side by Venmoor's signature alone, without further member vote.

**Why it matters:** For Treikaith, this is largely neutral—it means Seller can move quickly on amendments. However, it also means that if a future member (e.g., Barrowgate Ventures) challenges an amendment as beyond the scope of the original consent, there is a governance risk. The Side Letter's removal of the Excluded Liabilities carve-out (Issue 2 above) was authorized under this delegation. If a member later disputes the Side Letter's validity, it could create uncertainty about the indemnification framework.

**Recommendation:** Low priority for Treikaith, but note the risk. Ensure all amendments are documented with clear reference to the Member Consent authorization.
