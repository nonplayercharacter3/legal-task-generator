## Issue 1: The Non-Renewal Notice Is Invalid (Sent Too Early)

**Where:** Notice Letter §2.1; Agreement §12.2.

**Analysis.** Section 12.2 provides that non-renewal notice must be given "not less than ninety (90) days and not more than one hundred twenty (120) days before the end of the then-current term." It further states: "A notice of non-renewal given outside that period is not effective notice of non-renewal under this Section 12.2."

The Initial Term ends February 28, 2027. Working backward:

| Deadline | Date |
|---|---|
| 120 days before Feb. 28, 2027 | **October 31, 2026** |
| 90 days before Feb. 28, 2027 | **November 30, 2026** |

The valid notice window is therefore **October 31 through November 30, 2026**. The notice letter is dated **October 12, 2026**—nineteen days before the window opens. Under the plain text of §12.2, it is not effective.

**Why it matters.** If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement automatically renews for a successive 36-month term beginning March 1, 2027, under §12.2. Trilund would be locked in through February 2030 on terms it intended to avoid.

**Recommendation.** Send a compliant non-renewal notice immediately, well within the October 31 – November 30 window. The notice should be in writing, delivered in accordance with §15.1 (email plus overnight courier or hand delivery), and should reference §12.2 specifically. Do not rely on the October 12 letter.

**Facts needed from client.** Confirm whether the October 12 letter was actually delivered (and when) by the overnight courier. If the courier delivery date fell on or after October 31, there is an argument that the notice was "given" within the window even though it was drafted earlier. The §15.1 effectiveness rules tie email notices to the accompanying hard-copy delivery. We should confirm the courier tracking date.

## Issue 2: Change of Control – Consent Required but Not Sought

**Where:** Notice Letter §§1.1–1.4; Agreement §14.2; Agreement §1.3(a).

**Analysis.** Section 1.3(a) defines "Change of Control" to include "the acquisition by any person or group, directly or indirectly, of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities of Licensee **or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Licensee**." Ostrander Media Holdings, LP's acquisition of 100% of Trilund Entertainment Group, Inc.—the sole member and parent of Licensee—falls squarely within this definition.

Section 14.2 provides: "Any Change of Control of Licensee shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee and **requires the prior written consent of Licensor, which Licensor may grant or withhold in its sole discretion**." If a Change of Control occurs without prior written consent, "Licensor may terminate this Agreement on thirty (30) days' written notice."

The notice letter characterizes the transaction as "for informational purposes only" and states "No action is required of Licensor in connection with the transaction" (§1.4). That characterization is incorrect as a matter of contract. Simev's written consent is a condition precedent to the transaction closing without giving Simev a termination right.

**Why it matters.** If the Ostrander transaction closes on December 15, 2026 without Simev's prior written consent, Simev can terminate the Agreement on 30 days' notice—i.e., by mid-January 2027. This would:

- Collapse Trilund's transition planning (the letter assumes an orderly wind-down at the end of the Initial Term in February 2027);
- Potentially trigger the paid transition assistance under §13.2 (at Simev's "then-current professional services rates") rather than the no-charge transition available only for termination under §12.3 for Simev's breach; and
- Create operational risk if Trilund is mid-catalog or mid-delivery-cycle when Simev pulls the platform.

**Recommendation.** Before the Ostrander closing, Trilund should formally request Simev's written consent under §14.2. The request should:

- Acknowledge that the transaction constitutes a Change of Control under §1.3(a);
- Provide Simev with the information it reasonably needs (identity of acquirer, expected closing date, confirmation that Licensee remains the same entity);
- Be sent in compliance with §15.1; and
- Be timed so that consent (or a reasoned refusal) is in hand before December 15, 2026.

If Simev withholds consent, Trilund should negotiate a waiver or a side letter, or consider whether the Ostrander transaction documents should include a condition that Simev consent be obtained.

**Facts needed from client.** (a) Has Simev responded to the October 12 letter in any way? (b) Is the Ostrander closing date of December 15, 2026 firm, or is there flexibility? (c) Are there any representations in the Ostrander definitive agreement regarding vendor consents or change-of-control notifications? (d) Does Trilund have any commercial leverage (e.g., volume, multi-year commitment) that would make Simev reluctant to terminate?

## Issue 3: Amendment No. 1 Narrowed the Liability Exclusion for Data Security Breaches

**Where:** Agreement §10.3(b) (original); Amendment No. 1 §4.1 (replacing §10.3(b)).

**Analysis.** The original §10.3(b) excluded from both the indirect-damages waiver (§10.1) and the liability cap (§10.2) any "breach of Section 7 (Confidentiality and Data Security) **as a whole**." Section 7 encompasses non-disclosure (§7.1), exceptions (§7.2), security of Licensee Content (§7.3), security incident response (§7.4), audits (§7.5), and return/deletion (§7.6).

Amendment No. 1 §4.1 replaced this with: "breach of Section 7.1 (Non-Disclosure)." The effect is that breaches of §7.3 (failure to maintain CDSA-equivalent security safeguards, encryption, watermarking, access restrictions, access logs), §7.4 (failure to notify within 48 hours of a Security Incident), and §7.6 (failure to delete content on termination) are now **subject to** the §10.1 indirect-damages exclusion and the §10.2 cap of 12 months' fees ($186,000).

**Why it matters.** Trilund's Licensee Content includes unreleased screeners, delivery masters, and sensitive rights/royalty data (Agreement §1.9). A Security Incident involving unauthorized access to unreleased content could cause damages far exceeding $186,000—lost theatrical value, breach of downstream exhibition or streaming deals, reputational harm. Under the amended provision, Trilund's recovery for such an incident is capped at $186,000 in direct damages only, with no consequential or lost-profits recovery.

The order of precedence in §16.4 (Amendments > Agreement > Service Level Schedule) confirms that the amended §10.3(b) controls.

**Recommendation.**

- **Short-term:** Accept the risk for the remainder of the Initial Term (through February 2027), but ensure Simev's insurance (Agreement §11.1: $5M cyber/media liability) is current and that certificates are on file. A cyber liability claim may be Trilund's practical recovery path even if the contractual cap limits direct damages from Simev.
- **If Trilund renews or extends for any reason:** Negotiate restoration of the broader §10.3(b) exclusion, or at minimum an exclusion for breach of §7.3 and §7.4.
- **Operational:** Confirm that Simev's most recent third-party security assessment (required under §7.5) is on file and that the independent health-check remediation described in the August root cause summary (Email Thread, Message 5, §5.2) is verified.

**Facts needed from client.** (a) Has Trilund received Simev's most recent security assessment or certification report for the Contract Year beginning March 1, 2026? (b) Does Trilund's own cyber insurance cover loss from a vendor data breach, and is the policy limit sufficient? (c) What is the approximate value of unreleased content currently hosted on the Platform?

## Issue 4: Interaction of Issues 1 and 2 – Compounding Risk

**Where:** Agreement §§12.2, 14.2; Notice Letter §§1, 2.

**Analysis.** If the non-renewal notice is invalid (Issue 1), the Agreement renews for 36 months on March 1, 2027. If the Change of Control closes without consent (Issue 2), Simev can terminate on 30 days' notice at any time after December 15, 2026. The worst-case scenario:

1. Ostrander closes December 15, 2026 without Simev consent.
2. Simev serves a 30-day termination notice in mid-January 2027.
3. The Agreement terminates in mid-February 2027—just before the end of the Initial Term.
4. Trilund is entitled to transition assistance under §13.2, but at Simev's "then-current professional services rates" (not free, because the termination is not for Simev's breach under §12.3).
5. Trilund has lost the orderly 90-day transition it planned for post-February 28, 2027.

Alternatively, if Trilund obtains Simev's consent for the Change of Control but the non-renewal notice remains invalid, Trilund is locked into a 36-month renewal (March 2027 – February 2030) at potentially increased fees (see Issue 5).

**Recommendation.** Address both issues simultaneously and on an expedited basis. The non-renewal notice (Issue 1) can be cured by re-notice within the window. The consent request (Issue 2) should be made immediately. Trilund should not allow the Ostrander closing to proceed until Simev's position is known.

## Issue 5: Fee Increase Risk on Renewal

**Where:** Agreement §4.3; Notice Letter §2.

**Analysis.** If the Agreement renews (because the non-renewal notice is invalid), §4.3 permits Simev to increase the Annual License Fee by up to 10% for the Renewal Term, provided Simev gives written notice no later than 150 days before the end of the then-current term.

150 days before February 28, 2027 ≈ **September 30, 2026**.

We have no document in the folder indicating whether Simev has given (or will give) such notice. If it does, the renewed fee could be as high as $204,600 per year.

**Why it matters.** A 10% increase on a 36-month renewal is $65,880 in additional fees over the term, on top of the fact that Trilund did not intend to renew.

**Recommendation.** If a valid non-renewal notice is sent (curing Issue 1), this point becomes moot. If for any reason Trilund must accept renewal, it should negotiate the fee in connection with the consent request under Issue 2.

**Facts needed from client.** Has Simev sent or indicated any fee-increase notice under §4.3? Check email and courier records for anything received around or before September 30, 2026.

## Issue 6: Transition Assistance Rates Are Unspecified

**Where:** Agreement §13.2; Notice Letter §3.2.

**Analysis.** Section 13.2 provides that transition assistance (read-only access, content export) is at "Licensor's then-current professional services rates" unless the termination is for Licensor's breach under §12.3. Neither the Agreement, the Amendment, nor the Service Level Schedule defines or schedules those rates. The notice letter asks Simev to "confirm the applicable professional services rates," but there is no contractual benchmark.

**Why it matters.** If Simev quotes high professional services rates for the export of all rights/windows records, participation statements, and royalty accounting histories, Trilund could face a significant unexpected cost at the worst time (post-termination, with a 90-day window to complete migration).

**Recommendation.** In the transition planning conversation requested in Notice Letter §3.2, Trilund should:

- Request Simev's current professional services rate card in writing;
- Negotiate a fixed-fee or capped-fee transition package before the Initial Term ends;
- Confirm export formats (CSV, XML, or other) in writing to avoid disputes about "commonly used, non-proprietary format"; and
- Agree a timetable that front-loads the export so it is complete well before day 90.

**Facts needed from client.** Does Trilund have any prior invoices or communications reflecting Simev's professional services rates? What is the approximate volume of Licensee Content to be exported (number of titles, TB of media, years of royalty history)?

## Issue 8: The Notice Letter's Characterization of the Change of Control Is Misleading

**Where:** Notice Letter §1.4.

**Analysis.** Beyond the legal error identified in Issue 2, the letter's framing—"for informational purposes only, as a courtesy to a valued vendor. No action is required of Licensor"—could be used by Simev to argue that Trilund acknowledged no consent was needed, potentially estopping Trilund from later claiming it did not understand its obligations. Alternatively, if Simev does not respond and the transaction closes, Simev may argue it was given adequate notice and that Trilund's failure to seek consent was Trilund's own election.

**Why it matters.** In a dispute, the letter cuts against Trilund. It affirmatively tells Simev that no action is required, when the Agreement says the opposite.

**Recommendation.** If Trilund sends a follow-up consent request (per Issue 2), it should expressly state that the October 12 letter was provided as a courtesy notice and that Trilund now formally requests consent under §14.2. This corrects the record without conceding that the first letter was a consent request (or was not).

## Issue 9: Simev's Right to Assign Without Consent

**Where:** Agreement §14.3.

**Analysis.** Section 14.3 permits Simev to assign the Agreement without Trilund's consent to a successor to "all or substantially all of its business or assets relating to the Platform," provided the successor agrees in writing to be bound and Trilund receives notice. There is no right of termination for Trilund upon such an assignment.

**Why it matters.** If Simev is acquired or sells the Reelpath business during the remaining term (or during a renewal), Trilund could find itself dealing with an unknown successor. The data security obligations (§7.3), the CDSA-standard requirement, and the insurance requirements (§11.1) would bind the successor, but Trilund has no say in who that successor is.

**Recommendation.** This is a lower-priority item given the short remaining Initial Term, but if the non-renewal notice is invalid and the term renews for 36 months, Trilund should consider negotiating a right to terminate (or at least a consent requirement) if Simev assigns the Platform business. Alternatively, monitor Simev's corporate developments.

**Facts needed from client.** None at this time. Flag for the renewal negotiation if Issue 1 is not cured.
