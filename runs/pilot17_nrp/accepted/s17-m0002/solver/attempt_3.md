## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Ineffective (Sent Too Early)

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 12.2 (Renewal)
- Notice Letter § 2.1 (Notice of Non-Renewal)

**Explanation:**

Section 12.2 provides that the Agreement automatically renews for successive 36-month terms unless a Party gives written notice of non-renewal "not less than ninety (90) days and not more than one hundred twenty (120) days before the end of the then-current term." It further states: "A notice of non-renewal given outside that period is not effective notice of non-renewal under this Section 12.2."

The Initial Term ends **February 28, 2027**. The valid notice window is therefore:

| Boundary | Date |
|---|---|
| No later than 120 days before end of term | **October 31, 2026** |
| No earlier than 90 days before end of term | **November 30, 2026** |

The notice letter is dated **October 12, 2026**, which is 139 days before February 28, 2027—19 days outside (too early of) the 120-day boundary. Under the plain language of § 12.2, this notice is **not effective**.

**Why It Matters:**

If no valid non-renewal notice is given by November 30, 2026, the Agreement will **automatically renew** on March 1, 2027 for a further 36-month term (through February 28, 2030). Trilund would be locked in for another three-and-a-half years, contrary to its stated intent in the notice letter. This also has downstream consequences:

- Trilund would be subject to a potential fee increase of up to 10% under § 4.3 (see Issue 5 below).
- The Transition Period under § 13.2 would not be triggered, and Trilund would have no right to demand export of its Licensee Content.
- Trilund's planned migration off the Reelpath platform is disrupted.

**Recommendation:**

Trilund must send a **new, valid notice of non-renewal** between **October 31, 2026 and November 30, 2026**. The notice should be sent in compliance with § 15.1 (email plus overnight courier to the address designated for Simev's General Counsel). We recommend sending it no later than **November 15, 2026** to allow time for delivery confirmation and to avoid any dispute about the effective date. The new notice should be clean and reference only § 12.2; it need not repeat the Change of Control information (which should be handled separately—see Issue 2).

**Urgency:** The window closes November 30, 2026. This is time-sensitive.

## Issue 2: Pending Ostrander Transaction Is a Change of Control Requiring Simev's Prior Written Consent

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 1.3 (definition of "Change of Control")
- Software License Agreement § 14.2 (Change of Control of Licensee)
- Notice Letter §§ 1.1–1.4 (Transaction Involving Our Parent Company)

**Explanation:**

Section 1.3 defines "Change of Control" to include, in clause (a), "the acquisition by any person or group, directly or indirectly, of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities of Licensee **or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Licensee**."

The notice letter confirms that Trilund Entertainment Group, Inc. is "the parent and sole member of Trilund Pictures Releasing, LLC" (§ 1.1). As sole member of an LLC, Trilund Entertainment Group directly controls Licensee. Ostrander Media Holdings, LP's acquisition of 100% of Trilund Entertainment Group's equity therefore constitutes a Change of Control of Licensee under § 1.3(a).

Section 14.2 provides:

> "Any Change of Control of Licensee shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee and **requires the prior written consent of Licensor, which Licensor may grant or withhold in its sole discretion**. If a Change of Control of Licensee occurs without Licensor's prior written consent, Licensor may terminate this Agreement on thirty (30) days' written notice to Licensee."

The notice letter, however, characterizes the disclosure as "provided for informational purposes only, as a courtesy to a valued vendor" and states that "[n]o action is required of Licensor in connection with the transaction" (§ 1.4). This is a **material mischaracterization** of Trilund's obligations under the Agreement.

**Why It Matters:**

1. **Simev holds a termination right.** If the Ostrander transaction closes on December 15, 2026 without Simev's prior written consent, Simev may terminate the Agreement on 30 days' notice—effective approximately **January 14, 2027**. This is six weeks before the natural end of the Initial Term (February 28, 2027) and would cut short Trilund's planned use of the Platform.

2. **The mischaracterization in the notice letter is adverse.** By putting the transaction in writing and affirmatively stating that no action is required, Trilund has created a document that Simev could cite to show Trilund was on notice of the consent requirement and chose not to comply. This undermines any argument that Trilund was unaware of its obligation.

3. **Sole discretion standard.** Simev may withhold consent "in its sole discretion"—Trilund has no right to consent and no argument that withholding would be unreasonable. This is a powerful leverage point for Simev.

4. **Interaction with Issue 1.** If the non-renewal notice is cured (Issue 1), the Agreement expires February 28, 2027. But if Simev terminates in mid-January 2027 under § 14.2, Trilund loses active Platform access earlier than planned. The Transition Period (§ 13.2) would still apply (90 days from termination, i.e., through approximately April 14, 2027), but Trilund would be on read-only access for the final six weeks of what it expected to be active use.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Immediately send a supplemental letter to Simev** that (a) identifies the Ostrander transaction as a Change of Control under § 1.3(a), (b) expressly requests Simev's prior written consent under § 14.2, and (c) withdraws or corrects the statement in § 1.4 of the October 12 letter that "no action is required." This should be done well before the expected December 15, 2026 closing.

2. **Prepare for Simev to withhold consent or condition it.** Given the sole-discretion standard, Trilund should develop a fallback plan. Options include:
   - Negotiating a consent (possibly in exchange for agreeing to the renewal, a fee increase, or other commercial terms).
   - Seeking a waiver or amendment to § 14.2 before closing.
   - Accelerating its migration off the Platform so that a mid-January termination is less disruptive.

3. **Confirm with the client** whether any side letter, email, or oral communication with Simev regarding the Ostrander transaction has already occurred. If Simev has informally indicated it will consent, that should be reduced to writing before closing.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- Has Simev responded to the October 12 notice letter in any way?
- Is there any prior communication between the parties about the Ostrander transaction?
- What is the strategic importance of maintaining Platform access through February 28, 2027 versus an earlier date? (This affects how aggressively to pursue consent.)
- Is there any flexibility in the Ostrander closing date?

## Issue 3: Amendment No. 1 Materially Narrowed the Liability Carve-Out for Data Security Breaches

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 10.1 (Exclusion of Indirect Damages), § 10.2 (Cap), § 10.3 (Exclusions) [original]
- Amendment No. 1 § 4.1 (replacing § 10.3)
- Software License Agreement § 7.3 (Security of Licensee Content), § 7.4 (Security Incidents)

**Explanation:**

**Original § 10.3(b)** provided that the liability cap and indirect-damages exclusion do not apply to:

> "breach of Section 7 (Confidentiality and Data Security) **as a whole**"

This carved out all of Section 7—non-disclosure (§ 7.1), security of Licensee Content (§ 7.3), security incidents (§ 7.4), audits (§ 7.5), and return/deletion (§ 7.6)—from both the cap and the consequential-damages exclusion.

**Amended § 10.3(b)** (effective September 15, 2025) provides:

> "breach of Section 7.1 (Non-Disclosure)"

This narrows the carve-out to **only** the non-disclosure obligation. Breaches of §§ 7.3, 7.4, 7.5, and 7.6 are now subject to:
- The **cap** in § 10.2: total aggregate liability limited to fees paid or payable in the 12 months preceding the claim (currently **$186,000**).
- The **exclusion of indirect, consequential, and special damages** in § 10.1, including "loss of profits or revenue."

**Why It Matters:**

Trilund's most sensitive data on the Platform consists of unreleased screeners, delivery masters, and rights/windowing data (defined as "Licensee Content" in § 1.9). For an independent film distributor, the real-world harm from a Security Incident is overwhelmingly consequential: a leaked screener can destroy a theatrical release, compromise negotiated distribution windows, and trigger cross-defaults with studio partners. Under the original § 10.3, Trilund could recover its full actual losses (including consequential damages) from a Simev data security breach. Under the amended § 10.3, Trilund's recovery is capped at $186,000 and excludes consequential damages.

This change was embedded in an amendment whose stated purpose (Recital B) was to "increase the hosted storage included in the Annual License Fee, revise the rates for additional storage, and make certain other changes." The liability narrowing is not mentioned in the Recitals and appears to have been a "sneak" provision.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Assess whether to seek to restore the original carve-out.** If Trilund intends to renew (or is forced into a renewal under Issue 1), this is a natural point to negotiate. Even in the transition context, Trilund has content on the Platform through at least April 2027 (end of Transition Period), and Simev's security obligations continue during that time.

2. **Confirm Trilund's own cyber insurance coverage.** Given the $186,000 cap, Trilund should verify that its cyber liability policy covers first-party losses from a third-party vendor's data breach, and that the policy limit is adequate for the value of its unreleased content pipeline. If not, Trilund should consider increasing its coverage before the Ostrander transaction (which may change its risk profile and insurance requirements).

3. **Document the concern for any future negotiation.** If Trilund renews or extends, the restoration of the original § 10.3(b) language (or at minimum a carve-out for § 7.3 and § 7.4) should be a priority ask.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- Who negotiated and approved Amendment No. 1? Was the § 10.3 change flagged internally at the time?
- What is Trilund's current cyber insurance limit, and does it cover vendor-caused data breaches?
- What is the approximate value of unreleased content currently on the Platform?

## Issue 4: Potential Fee Increase on (Ineffective) Renewal

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 4.3 (Fee Adjustments on Renewal)
- Software License Agreement § 12.2 (Renewal)

**Explanation:**

Section 4.3 permits Simev to increase the Annual License Fee for any Renewal Term by up to 10% over the fee in effect for the last Contract Year of the preceding term. Simev must give written notice "no later than one hundred fifty (150) days before the end of the then-current term."

150 days before February 28, 2027 is **September 30, 2026**. That date has already passed as of the October 12 notice letter.

**Why It Matters:**

If Trilund's non-renewal notice is ineffective (Issue 1) and the Agreement renews, Simev may have already issued (or may imminently issue) a fee increase notice. A 10% increase on $186,000 would bring the Annual License Fee to **$204,600** for the renewal term. Alternatively, if Simev did not give notice by September 30, 2026, the fee remains at $186,000 for the renewal term (Simev would have waived its right to increase).

**Recommendation:**

- **Confirm with the client** whether Simev has sent a fee increase notice under § 4.3. Check email and mail for any communication from Simev between September 1 and September 30, 2026 referencing a fee adjustment.
- If a fee increase notice was received, calculate the impact on the renewal term and factor it into the decision about whether to negotiate consent to the Change of Control (Issue 2) in exchange for a fee concession.
- If no notice was received, Simev is locked into the current $186,000 fee for the first renewal Contract Year. This is a minor bargaining chip.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- Has Simev sent any fee increase notice under § 4.3? If so, when and for what amount?

## Issue 5: Transition Planning Complicated by Interacting Issues

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 13.2 (Transition Assistance)
- Notice Letter § 3.1–3.2 (Transition)
- Service Level Schedule § 7.2 (Timing and Refund for Chronic Failure Termination)

**Explanation:**

The notice letter requests that Simev export all Licensee Content "in a commonly used, non-proprietary format" during the Transition Period (§ 13.2). The Transition Period is 90 days from termination or expiration. However, the actual start date of the Transition Period depends on how Issues 1 and 2 resolve:

| Scenario | Expiration/Termination Date | Transition Period End |
|---|---|---|
| Valid non-renewal; no CoC termination | February 28, 2027 | ~May 29, 2027 |
| Valid non-renewal; Simev terminates for CoC (~Jan 14, 2027) | January 14, 2027 | ~April 14, 2027 |
| Ineffective non-renewal (auto-renewal); no CoC termination | No expiration until Feb 28, 2030 | N/A |
| Ineffective non-renewal; Simev terminates for CoC | ~January 14, 2027 | ~April 14, 2027 |

In the third scenario, Trilund has **no right to transition assistance** and no right to demand export. Its 64+ TB of content (see invoice, Message 8) remains on Simev's platform for 36 months.

Additionally, § 13.2 provides that transition assistance is at Simev's "then-current professional services rates" unless Trilund terminated for Simev's breach under § 12.3. In none of the scenarios above would Trilund be terminating for Simev's breach, so Trilund should expect to pay for the export. The notice letter (§ 3.2) appropriately asks to confirm these rates, but no response from Simev is in the file.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Prioritize curing the non-renewal notice (Issue 1)** to ensure the Agreement actually expires on February 28, 2027.
2. **Seek consent to the Change of Control (Issue 2)** to eliminate the risk of early termination and compress the transition timeline.
3. **Engage Simev's account team now** (as the notice letter requests) to agree on:
   - Export formats (confirm "commonly used, non-proprietary format" means, e.g., CSV for rights data, MP4/MXF for media, and specify which formats Simev can deliver).
   - Professional services rates for the export.
   - A transition timetable that accounts for the 64+ TB of content.
4. **Begin internal planning** for migration to an alternative platform or in-house solution, targeting completion before the Transition Period ends.

**Facts Needed from Client:**
- What is Trilund's backup plan if it must migrate off Reelpath on an accelerated timeline (e.g., by April 2027 rather than May 2027)?
- What formats does Trilund need for its rights and windows data, participation statements, and royalty accounting histories?
- Has Simev responded to the request in Notice Letter § 3.2 to contact Trilund's Head of Business and Legal Affairs?

## Issue 7: Minor – Title Discrepancy in Service Level Schedule

**Documents and Sections:**
- Service Level Schedule, title line: "RELEASE PATH SERVICE LEVEL SCHEDULE"
- Service Level Schedule, § 1 (body): "Reelpath Service Level Schedule"
- Software License Agreement § 1.12: "Reelpath Service Level Schedule"

**Explanation:**

The document title reads "RELEASE PATH" rather than "REEPATH" or "REEPATH." The body of the document and the Agreement both use "Reelpath." This appears to be a typographical error in the title.

**Why It Matters:**

Immaterial in practice, but if a dispute arose over which document constitutes the "Service Level Schedule" referenced in § 1.12 of the Agreement, a pedantic counterparty could argue the titled document is not the one incorporated. Under § 16.4 (Order of Precedence), the Service Level Schedule is the lowest-priority document, so any ambiguity would be resolved against it.

**Recommendation:**

No action required unless Trilund is already in a dispute with Simev, in which case the point should not be raised (it cuts both ways). If a future amendment or restatement is negotiated, correct the title.
