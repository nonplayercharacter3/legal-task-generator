## Summary

Review of the five documents in the matter folder reveals six issues, two of which are urgent and interrelated. The most significant are (1) the non-renewal notice in the October 12, 2026 letter was sent outside the contractual window and is likely ineffective, and (2) the Change of Control disclosure in that same letter failed to request the consent that Section 14.2 of the Agreement requires. ## Issue 1: Non-Renewal Notice Is Outside the Contractual Window and Likely Ineffective

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 12.2 (Renewal)
- Trilund Notice Letter dated 2026-10-12, § 2

**Explanation:**

Section 12.2 provides that the Agreement "shall not renew if either Party gives the other written notice of non-renewal **not less than ninety (90) days and not more than one hundred twenty (120) days** before the end of the then-current term." It further states: "A notice of non-renewal given outside that period is **not effective** notice of non-renewal under this Section 12.2."

The Initial Term ends **February 28, 2027** (Agreement § 12.1). Working backward:

| Deadline | Date |
|---|---|
| 120 days before Feb. 28, 2027 (earliest effective date) | **October 31, 2026** |
| 90 days before Feb. 28, 2027 (latest effective date) | **November 30, 2026** |

The notice letter is dated **October 12, 2026**—19 days before the earliest effective date. Even accounting for the one-business-day effectiveness rule for overnight courier delivery under § 15.1(b) (making it effective October 13 at the earliest), the notice was delivered approximately 138 days before term end, well outside the 90–120 day window.

**Why it matters:**

If the notice is ineffective, the Agreement **automatically renews** for a successive 36-month term beginning March 1, 2027 (through approximately February 28, 2031), at fees that may increase by up to 10% under § 4.3. Trilund's stated business plan—let the Agreement expire and transition off the Platform—is frustrated. Trilund would be locked in for three more years with a new parent (Ostrander) and would need to either (a) perform under the renewal or (b) seek termination for cause, which is a far more difficult position.

**Recommendation:**

Trilund should send a **second, timely non-renewal notice** between **October 31 and November 30, 2026**. This is urgent; the window closes in less than three weeks from the date of the original letter. The new notice should be sent in compliance with § 15.1 (email + overnight courier to the designated address and attention line). We should also consider whether the October 12 letter can be argued to satisfy § 12.2 under a substantial-compliance or waiver theory, but the plain language is unfavorable ("not effective"), and we should not rely on that argument as our primary position.

**Open question for client:** Confirm whether any communication with Simev between October 12 and the present has acknowledged or relied on the non-renewal notice. If Simev has treated the Agreement as non-renewing (e.g., by beginning transition planning or by not invoicing for the renewal term), that could support a waiver or estoppel argument as a fallback.

## Issue 2: Change of Control Requires Licensor Consent That Was Not Requested

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 1.3 (Change of Control definition), § 14.2 (CoC consent requirement)
- Trilund Notice Letter dated 2026-10-12, §§ 1.1–1.4

**Explanation:**

Section 1.3(a) defines "Change of Control" to include "the acquisition by any person or group, directly or indirectly, of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting securities of Licensee **or of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Licensee**."

The notice letter (§ 1.1) states that Trilund Entertainment Group, Inc. is "the parent and sole member" of Trilund Pictures Releasing, LLC, and that Ostrander Media Holdings, LP will acquire 100% of Trilund Entertainment Group's equity. This is unambiguously a Change of Control of Licensee under § 1.3(a).

Section 14.2 provides:

> "Any Change of Control of Licensee shall be deemed an assignment of this Agreement by Licensee and **requires the prior written consent of Licensor, which Licensor may grant or withhold in its sole discretion**. If a Change of Control of Licensee occurs without Licensor's prior written consent, **Licensor may terminate this Agreement on thirty (30) days' written notice to Licensee**."

The notice letter, however, states in § 1.4: "This notice is provided for **informational purposes only**, as a courtesy to a valued vendor. **No action is required** of Licensor in connection with the transaction."

The letter does not request consent. It does not acknowledge that consent is required. It affirmatively tells Simev that no action is needed.

**Why it matters:**

If the Ostrander transaction closes on **December 15, 2026** (as stated in the letter) without Simev's prior written consent:

1. Simev has the right to terminate the Agreement on 30 days' notice—i.e., by approximately **January 14, 2027**.
2. That termination would occur roughly six weeks before the scheduled February 28, 2027 expiration, cutting into Trilund's planned wind-down and transition period.
3. Under § 13.2, transition assistance (read-only access, data export) is provided "at its then-current professional services rates" unless Trilund terminated for Simev's breach under § 12.3. A Simev termination under § 14.2 is the exercise of a contractual right, not a breach, so **Trilund would pay Simev's professional services rates** for the transition work it needs.
4. If Issue 1 is not cured (i.e., the non-renewal notice is ineffective and the Agreement renews), a Simev termination under § 14.2 would land in the middle of a new 36-month term, making the early termination even more disruptive.

Additionally, Simev's "sole discretion" standard means it can withhold consent for any reason or no reason. Trilund has no contractual leverage to compel consent.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Immediately** send a supplemental letter to Simev (a) acknowledging that § 14.2 requires prior written consent for the Change of Control, (b) formally requesting that consent, and (c) clarifying that the October 12 letter was intended to provide early notice of the transaction and should not be read as a representation that no consent is needed. Do this well before the December 15, 2026 expected closing.
2. If Simev withholds consent or does not respond, evaluate whether to (a) delay closing (if the Ostrander agreement permits), (b) negotiate a consent or a side letter, or (c) accept the termination risk and accelerate transition planning.
3. Confirm with the client whether the definitive agreement with Ostrander contains any provision addressing vendor consent or change-of-control notifications to third parties.

**Open question for client:** What are the closing conditions and timing flexibility in the Ostrander definitive agreement? Can closing be delayed if Simev consent is not obtained? Is there a reverse termination fee or other remedy if closing is delayed?

## Issue 3: Amendment No. 1 Materially Narrowed Data-Security Liability Protection

**Documents and Sections:**
- Software License Agreement § 10.1 (Exclusion of Indirect Damages), § 10.2 (Cap), § 10.3 (Exclusions) [original]
- Amendment No. 1 § 4.1 (replacing Agreement § 10.3)
- Agreement § 16.4 (Order of Precedence)

**Explanation:**

Original § 10.3(b) excluded from both the indirect-damages waiver (§ 10.1) and the liability cap (§ 10.2) any "breach of **Section 7 (Confidentiality and Data Security) as a whole**." Section 7 encompasses:

- § 7.1 – Non-Disclosure
- § 7.2 – Exceptions
- § 7.3 – Security of Licensee Content (encryption, watermarking, access restrictions, logs, no unauthorized use)
- § 7.4 – Security Incidents (48-hour notice, investigation, remediation)
- § 7.5 – Audits
- § 7.6 – Return and Deletion

Amendment No. 1, § 4.1 replaced § 10.3(b) with: "breach of **Section 7.1 (Non-Disclosure)**" only.

Under § 16.4 (Order of Precedence), the Amendment prevails over the base Agreement. Therefore, as of September 15, 2025, a breach of § 7.3, 7.4, 7.5, or 7.6 is **subject to** both the indirect/consequential damages exclusion (§ 10.1) and the 12-month-fees cap (§ 10.2).

**Why it matters:**

Trilund's Licensee Content (§ 1.9) includes "unreleased screeners, delivery masters, trailers and other audiovisual works; rights and windowing data; and participation statements, royalty reports and related financial data." A Security Incident involving unreleased content could trigger:

- Breach of Trilund's own distribution agreements with studios (which typically carry significant liquidated damages or indemnification obligations for unauthorized disclosure).
- Loss of windowing value (e.g., a film leaks before its theatrical or streaming window).
- Regulatory exposure.

Most of these damages are consequential or indirect. Under the amended § 10.3, Trilund cannot recover them. Its maximum recovery for a data-security breach is capped at the fees paid in the preceding 12 months—approximately **$186,000** plus any additional usage fees (in the most recent quarter, $3,600 in storage overage). For a distributor handling major studio titles, that cap is likely a small fraction of actual exposure.

This change was made in the context of a storage increase and rate reduction (Amendment §§ 2–3), which may have led the client to focus on the commercial terms and underappreciate the liability shift.

**Recommendation:**

1. Flag this to the client as a known, accepted risk as of September 15, 2025. If the client did not consciously trade away this protection, document that understanding now.
2. For the remaining term (through February 28, 2027, or longer if renewal occurs), ensure Trilund's own cyber insurance and media liability coverage is adequate to fill the gap left by the narrowed contractual remedy. Confirm with the client's risk manager.
3. If the Agreement renews (see Issue 1), consider negotiating a restoration of the broader § 7 exclusion as part of any renewal discussion or future amendment.
4. During the Transition Period, ensure the data export and deletion under § 7.6 is completed and certified in writing, since a post-termination retention or leak would also be subject to the cap.

**Open question for client:** Did the client's negotiation team consciously accept the narrowing of § 10.3(b) as part of the storage/rate trade in Amendment No. 1? If not, is there any correspondence or side agreement that could support a different reading? (Unlikely given the plain text, but worth confirming.)

## Issue 4: Compounding Risk – Ineffective Non-Renewal Plus Unconsented Change of Control

**Documents and Sections:**
- Agreement §§ 12.2, 14.2
- Notice Letter §§ 1, 2
- Amendment No. 1 (no relevant modification to §§ 12.2 or 14.2)

**Explanation:**

Issues 1 and 2 interact to create a worst-case scenario:

- If the non-renewal notice is ineffective (Issue 1), the Agreement renews for 36 months on March 1, 2027.
- If the Change of Control closes on December 15, 2026 without consent (Issue 2), Simev can terminate by ~January 14, 2027.
- In that scenario, Simev terminates the Agreement roughly three weeks before the renewal would have taken effect. The termination is effective, and the Agreement ends. But Trilund is left having (a) not secured a clean expiration on its own timeline, (b) paid for transition services at Simev's professional rates (§ 13.2), and (c) potentially disrupted its distribution operations during a period when it was expecting a smooth wind-down.

Alternatively, if Simev does **not** terminate under § 14.2 (perhaps because it values the renewal revenue), Trilund is locked into a 36-month renewal with Ostrander as ultimate parent, at up to 10% higher fees, with the narrowed liability protection from Issue 3 still in place.

**Recommendation:**

Address Issues 1 and 2 simultaneously and on an urgent timeline. The non-renewal notice window closes **November 30, 2026**. The CoC consent should be requested well before **December 15, 2026**. A single supplemental letter to Simev can accomplish both: (a) re-issue the non-renewal notice within the valid window, and (b) formally request CoC consent under § 14.2. Coordinate timing with the Ostrander deal team.

## Issue 5: August 2026 Outage – Service Credit Properly Applied; Monitor for Chronic Failure

**Documents and Sections:**
- Service Level Schedule §§ 2.1, 2.2, 2.5, 4.1, 4.4, 7.1
- Email Thread, Messages 1–9

**Explanation:**

The August 19, 2026 outage (252 minutes) resulted in 99.43% monthly availability, below the 99.5% commitment. The 5% service credit tier was correctly applied: 5% × $15,500 Monthly Fee = $775. The credit was properly offset against invoice SIM-INV-2026-0911 per § 4.4. Rachel Okafor confirmed the math and closed the matter (Message 9). Simev's root cause analysis (Message 5) correctly classified the event as Downtime (not Scheduled Maintenance or force majeure).

No issue with the credit calculation or application.

However, for forward-looking risk: Section 7.1 of the Service Level Schedule gives Trilund a termination right if availability falls below **98.0%** in (a) three consecutive calendar months, or (b) four calendar months in any rolling twelve-month period. The August figure (99.43%) is well above 98.0%, so no chronic-failure trigger was met. But if the Agreement renews (Issue 1), Trilund should track monthly availability reports carefully. Three or four sub-98% months in a row would give Trilund a termination right with a pro rata refund (§ 7.2)—a potentially valuable exit if the renewal proves unworkable.

**Recommendation:**

- No action needed on the August credit; it was handled correctly.
- Implement an internal tracking log of monthly availability figures from the § 2.5 reports. Flag any month below 98.0%.
- At the Q3 service review (referenced in Message 9), confirm with Simev that the new independent health-check failover trigger is operational and request evidence of testing. The original failure mode (standby controller not triggering because the fault suppressed its own health signal) is precisely the kind of single-point-of-failure that § 6 (Backup and Disaster Recovery) is designed to prevent.

## Issue 6: Transition Planning – Timing and Cost Depend on Termination Path

**Documents and Sections:**
- Agreement § 13.2 (Transition Assistance)
- Notice Letter § 3

**Explanation:**

Section 13.2 provides a 90-day Transition Period after termination or expiration, during which Simev must (a) maintain read-only access and (b) export all Licensee Content in a commonly used, non-proprietary format. The cost rule: transition is **free** only if Trilund terminated for Simev's breach under § 12.3. In all other scenarios (expiration, Simev termination under § 14.2, mutual agreement), Trilund pays Simev's "then-current professional services rates."

The notice letter (§ 3.2) asks Simev to contact Trilund's Head of Business and Legal Affairs "within the next few weeks" to agree a timetable, export formats, and rates.

The transition timeline depends on which termination path materializes:

| Scenario | Termination Date | Transition Period End |
|---|---|---|
| Clean expiration (non-renewal effective) | Feb. 28, 2027 | ~May 29, 2027 |
| Simev terminates under § 14.2 after Dec. 15 CoC | ~Jan. 14, 2027 | ~Apr. 14, 2027 |
| Renewal occurs, then later termination for chronic failure | Variable | Variable |

**Why it matters:**

If Simev terminates early under § 14.2, Trilund loses approximately six weeks of Platform access compared to the planned February 28 expiration. Given that Trilund's operations include festival screener deliveries (as referenced in Message 4), a compressed transition could create operational gaps. Additionally, Trilund would be paying Simev's professional services rates for the export work rather than receiving it free.

**Recommendation:**

1. Begin transition planning now, assuming the earliest possible date (mid-January 2027) to avoid being caught off guard.
2. Request Simev's current professional services rate card in writing so Trilund can budget for transition costs.
3. Confirm the export formats Trilund needs (the letter requests "commonly used, non-proprietary format"—specify: CSV for rights/windows data, PDF or native format for participation statements, etc.) so there are no disputes during the Transition Period.
4. If the CoC consent is obtained and the non-renewal notice is re-issued timely, the transition should proceed on the February 28 / May 29 timeline. Lock that in with Simev's account team.

**Open question for client:** What is Trilund's fallback if it loses Platform access in mid-January 2027? Does it have an alternative system or manual process for rights tracking and royalty accounting that could bridge a six-week gap? What are the specific export formats and data volumes needed?
