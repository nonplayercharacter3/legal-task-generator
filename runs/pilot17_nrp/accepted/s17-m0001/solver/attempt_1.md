## Issue 1 (Critical): Assumption of Pre-Closing Liabilities Under Assigned Contracts Exposes Zaibeind to the $410,000 Hudson Valley Wedding Collective Claim with No Indemnification Recourse

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 2.3(a): Buyer assumes "all liabilities and obligations arising under the Assigned Contracts, **whether arising before or after the Closing**."
- APA § 4.9: Seller represents that, except as set forth on Schedule 4.9, no party is in material breach of any Assigned Contract.
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.9, Item 1: Discloses that on March 1, 2026, Seller cancelled the contracted room block and garden pavilion event block for June 12–14, 2026, and that Hudson Valley Wedding Collective, LLC has asserted the cancellation breaches the agreement.
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.10, Item 1: Discloses a demand letter dated March 2, 2026 for **$410,000** (liquidated cancellation fees, non-refundable vendor deposits, and lost commissions), with a threatened action in Columbia County Supreme Court if not resolved within 45 days.
- APA § 2.1(c): The Hudson Valley Wedding Collective Agreement is an Assigned Contract.
- APA § 9.2(a)–(c): Indemnification triggers.
- APA § 9.5: Cap and basket.

**Why it matters:**

The phrase "whether arising before or after the Closing" in § 2.3(a) is unusually broad. It causes Zaibeind to assume the $410,000 claim that arose from **Seller's** pre-Closing cancellation of the event block. Zaibeind did not cause this breach and had no operational control over the Hotel when it occurred.

There is no effective indemnification path:

- **§ 9.2(a) (breach of representation):** Inapplicable because Schedule 4.9, Item 1 expressly discloses the cancellation, qualifying the § 4.9 representation. Under Disclosure Schedule § 1.3, the disclosure is deemed made for any section where its relevance is "reasonably apparent," which would include § 4.6 (Absence of Changes).
- **§ 9.2(b) (Excluded Liability):** Inapplicable because the claim is an *Assumed* Liability under § 2.3(a), not an Excluded Liability under § 2.4.
- **§ 9.2(c) (breach of covenant):** Seller's cancellation could arguably breach § 5.1 (Conduct of Business – ordinary course), but the 15-day cure period in § 8.1(c) would have expired well before Closing, and the claim would be time-barred under the 9-month survival period (§ 9.1) if not asserted promptly. Even if viable, recovery is subject to the $1,840,000 cap in § 9.5.

The practical result: **Zaibeind bears the full $410,000 exposure with no recourse against Seller.**

**Recommendation:**

Before Closing, negotiate one or more of the following:

1. **Amend § 2.3(a)** to limit Assumed Liabilities to those "arising from Buyer's performance or operation of the Assigned Contracts **after** the Closing," expressly excluding liabilities resulting from Seller's pre-Closing acts or omissions.
2. **Add a specific indemnity** in § 9.2 for the Hudson Valley Wedding Collective claim, carved out from the cap in § 9.5 (similar to the existing carve-out for Excluded Liabilities and fraud).
3. **Reduce the Purchase Price** by $410,000 (or a negotiated amount reflecting the likelihood of payout) to compensate for the assumed risk.
4. **Require Seller to resolve or escrow** the claim before Closing as a condition to Buyer's obligation under § 7.1.

*Facts to confirm with client:* (a) Whether Zaibeind is willing to operate the June 12–14 event block as contracted (which would mitigate the claim) or whether it intends to honor Seller's cancellation; (b) the current status of negotiations with Hudson Valley Wedding Collective as of the most recent date; (c) whether the senior lender's approval is required for any purchase price adjustment.

## Issue 2 (High): Note § 6.2 Restricts Zaibeind's Ability to Encumber or Transfer Assets Across Its Entire Hotel Portfolio

**Documents and Sections:**
- Note § 1.2: Defines "Business" as "all hotels and hospitality operations owned, leased or managed by Maker or any of its Affiliates."
- Note § 6.2: "Maker shall not, and shall not permit any of its Affiliates to, without the prior written consent of Holder, sell, transfer, lease, refinance or encumber any material portion of the assets of the Business."
- Note § 6.2(a)–(c): Exceptions limited to (a) ordinary-course inventory and FF&E replacement, (b) the Senior Indebtedness lien, and (c) the Mortgage.
- APA § 1.1: Defines "Business" narrowly as "the ownership and operation of the Hotel by Seller."

**Why it matters:**

The Note's definition of "Business" is dramatically broader than the APA's. For the 36-month term of the Note (through April 15, 2029), Zaibeind and **all of its Affiliates** cannot sell, transfer, lease, refinance, or encumber any material portion of the assets of **any** hotel or hospitality operation in the group without Seller's written consent. The exceptions do not cover:

- New acquisition financing on other properties;
- Refinancing of existing debt on other properties;
- Asset sales or joint ventures involving other properties;
- Encumbrances granted to lenders for working capital or revolver facilities.

This effectively gives Seller (a single property seller) a veto over Zaibeind's capital structure and strategic transactions across its entire portfolio for three years.

**Recommendation:**

Amend Note § 6.2 to limit the restricted "Business" to **the Hotel and its assets only** (i.e., conform to the APA's definition), or alternatively add exceptions for:

- Encumbrances and financings on properties other than the Hotel;
- Asset sales of other properties that do not reduce the net worth or liquidity of Maker below a specified threshold;
- Refinancings of any indebtedness of Maker or its Affiliates in a principal amount not exceeding the outstanding amount being refinanced.

*Facts to confirm with client:* (a) How many other properties does Zaibeind or its Affiliates currently own, lease, or manage? (b) Are there any planned refinancings, acquisitions, or dispositions in the next 36 months that would be restricted? (c) What is the group's typical capital structure (revolver, term loan, unencumbered assets)?

## Issue 3 (High): 9-Month Survival Period Is Inadequate for a Hotel Acquisition

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 9.1: General survival period is **nine (9) months** following the Closing Date. Fundamental Representations (§§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.7) survive six years.
- APA § 9.2: All indemnification claims must be made within the applicable survival period.

**Why it matters:**

For a 42-room hotel with a restaurant, event spaces, and real property, nine months is short. The following risks may not manifest within that window:

- **Latent building and systems defects** (§ 4.8 – Condition of Assets): HVAC, roof, plumbing, or structural issues often surface seasonally. The first full winter and summer cycles after Closing will stress systems.
- **Environmental liabilities** (§ 4.14): Contamination issues (e.g., from the carriage house, kitchen, or parking lot) may not be discovered without a Phase II environmental site assessment, which takes months.
- **Tax exposures** (§ 4.12): While Schedule 4.12 states "None," pre-Closing tax periods (particularly the Columbia County hotel room occupancy tax and NYS sales tax on food and beverage) may be audited after the survival period expires.
- **Employment claims** (§ 4.13): WARN Act or wage-and-hour claims related to pre-Closing employment decisions may surface later.
- **Contract disputes** (§ 4.9): Beyond the Hudson Valley Wedding Collective matter, other Assigned Contracts may reveal breaches or cost overruns in the first full operating cycle under Zaibeind.

Only the Fundamental Representations (Organization, Authority, Title) survive beyond nine months. All operational and financial representations expire.

**Recommendation:**

Negotiate to extend the general survival period to **eighteen (18) months** or at minimum **twelve (12) months**, with the following longer tails:

- § 4.8 (Condition of Assets): 18 months (to cover a full seasonal cycle);
- § 4.12 (Taxes): until expiration of the applicable statute of limitations (consistent with § 10.2);
- § 4.14 (Environmental): 18 months or until the statute of limitations expires.

Alternatively, if Seller will not agree to a longer period, require a **pre-Closing Phase I (and if warranted, Phase II) environmental site assessment** and a **pre-Closing building systems inspection** so that known issues are addressed before the survival clock starts.

*Facts to confirm with client:* (a) Has a Phase I ESA been performed? If so, what were the findings? (b) Has a pre-Closing inspection of building systems been conducted? (c) What is Zaibeind's typical survival period in comparable acquisitions?

## Issue 4 (Moderate): Setoff Right Against the Note Is Limited to Claims Asserted Within the Survival Period

**Documents and Sections:**
- Note § 4.1: "This setoff right applies only to indemnity claims **asserted in writing within the survival period** set forth in Section 9.1 of the Purchase Agreement."
- Note § 4.2: "Finally determined" means agreed in writing or final non-appealable judgment.
- APA § 9.6: "Buyer may set off amounts finally determined to be owed to it under this Article 9 against payments due under the Seller Note, on the terms set forth in the Seller Note."
- APA § 11.9: "the Seller Note shall control as to payment terms, Maker covenants and Events of Default."

**Why it matters:**

The setoff right is Zaibeind's primary self-help remedy against the $3.2M Note. However, it is doubly constrained:

1. The claim must be **asserted in writing within 9 months** of Closing (the survival period).
2. The amount must be **"finally determined"** (mutual written agreement or final non-appealable judgment) before it can actually be applied as a setoff.

This creates a gap: Zaibeind may assert a valid claim in month 8, but if the dispute is not resolved by a final judgment until month 18, the setoff is still available (because the claim was asserted within the survival period). However, if Zaibeind does not assert the claim in writing until month 10—because it was still investigating—the setoff right is lost entirely, even though the APA's general survival period for bringing an indemnity action may arguably still be running (it is not, since 9 months is the hard stop).

Additionally, APA § 11.9 provides that the Note controls as to payment terms. If there is any tension between APA § 9.6 and Note § 4, the Note's stricter limitation prevails.

**Recommendation:**

- Extend the survival period (per Issue 3), which automatically extends the window for asserting setoff-eligible claims.
- Clarify in the Note that a claim "asserted in writing" includes claims asserted through the indemnification notice procedure in APA § 9.4, and that the setoff right is preserved for the duration of any resulting litigation or dispute resolution, regardless of when final determination occurs.

## Issue 5 (Moderate): Indemnification Cap of $1,840,000 May Be Insufficient

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 9.5: "The aggregate liability of each Party under this Article 9 shall not exceed **10% of the Purchase Price ($1,840,000)**." Carve-outs: Excluded Liabilities (§ 9.2(b)) and fraud.
- APA § 9.5: Basket of $92,000 (tipping – liable from first dollar once exceeded).

**Why it matters:**

The $1,840,000 cap applies to claims under § 9.2(a) (breach of representations) and § 9.2(c) (breach of covenants). In a hotel acquisition, multiple representation breaches can occur simultaneously:

- Condition of Assets (§ 4.8): Capital expenditures for a 42-room hotel can easily exceed $1M if systems are worn.
- Financial Statements (§ 4.5): If actual revenue or costs differ materially from the delivered financials.
- Compliance/Permits (§ 4.11): Regulatory fines or remediation costs.
- Absence of Changes (§ 4.6): Deterioration between December 31, 2025 and Closing.

While the cap does not apply to Excluded Liabilities (which is favorable), the practical effect is that if Zaibeind's total representation-breach claims exceed $1.84M, it absorbs the excess. Given the $410K Hudson Valley Wedding Collective issue (which, as noted in Issue 1, likely falls outside the cap entirely because it's an Assumed Liability rather than an indemnifiable claim), the cap provides limited additional protection.

**Recommendation:**

Negotiate to increase the cap to **15–20% of the Purchase Price** ($2.76M–$3.68M), or add a separate uncapped (or higher-capped) indemnity for breaches of § 4.8 (Condition of Assets) and § 4.5 (Financial Statements).

## Issue 6 (Moderate): Liquor License Gap at Closing

**Documents and Sections:**
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 2.1(f), Item 3: The On-Premises Liquor License (Serial No. 1298844) "is not transferable. Buyer will apply for its own license under Section 5.6 of the Agreement."
- APA § 5.6: "Buyer shall promptly apply to the New York State Liquor Authority for the issuance of a license or temporary retail permit for the Hotel, and Seller shall cooperate with that application."
- Integration Notes, Item 7.1: Application for a temporary retail permit filed March 20, 2026. "If the permit has not issued by the Closing, the Director of Hotel Operations will prepare a contingency plan for restaurant and bar service."
- Bill of Sale § 2.4(a): Confirms the liquor license is not conveyed.

**Why it matters:**

The Hotel's restaurant and bar revenue depends on the ability to serve alcohol. If the TRLA temporary retail permit has not issued by April 15, 2026, Zaibeind takes over the Hotel **without the ability to sell alcohol** until its own license is granted. The "contingency plan" referenced in the Integration Notes is not documented in the transaction files and its adequacy is unknown. For a boutique hotel where the restaurant and event spaces are key revenue drivers (and where the garden pavilion is marketed for weddings), a loss of alcohol service could materially impact revenue and guest experience during the critical summer season.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client whether the TRLA temporary retail permit has issued as of the most recent date.
- If not yet issued, require Seller to cooperate in expediting the application and consider making issuance (or a committed timeline) a condition to Closing under § 7.1.
- Document the contingency plan and assess its revenue impact.
- Confirm that the APA's § 5.6 covenant (Seller cooperation) is being honored and that Seller has provided all required documentation.

*Facts to confirm with client:* (a) Current status of the TRLA application; (b) whether a temporary permit has issued; (c) estimated revenue impact of a 30–60 day alcohol service gap; (d) whether the contingency plan involves non-alcoholic beverage service only or a full menu adjustment.

## Issue 7 (Moderate): Cross-Default to Senior Indebtedness Accelerates the Seller Note

**Documents and Sections:**
- Note § 7.1(e): "a default occurs under the Senior Indebtedness that results in its acceleration" is an Event of Default.
- Note § 7.2: Upon an Event of Default (other than insolvency under § 7.1(g)), Holder may declare the entire Note immediately due and payable by written notice.
- Note § 8: The Note and Mortgage are subordinate to the Senior Indebtedness.

**Why it matters:**

If Zaibeind's senior acquisition lender accelerates the senior loan (due to a financial covenant breach, a technical default, or a market event), the Seller Note also becomes an Event of Default. Seller could then accelerate the $3.2M Note and foreclose on the Mortgage (subject to the subordination in § 8). While the subordination means Seller cannot foreclose while the Senior Indebtedness is outstanding, the acceleration of the Note itself triggers the Default Rate (10% per annum – 6% + 4% under Note § 1.2 and § 2.5) and the late charge, increasing carrying costs.

More practically, a cross-acceleration could complicate any refinancing or restructuring of the senior debt, as Seller would have leverage to demand concessions (e.g., prepayment, additional security, or a higher interest rate) as a condition to waiving the default.

**Recommendation:**

- Confirm with the client's senior lender that the subordination agreement (required under Note § 8) includes a provision that Seller's remedies are limited to those permitted under the subordination, and that Seller cannot accelerate the Note solely due to a senior default that is being cured or waived by the Senior Lender.
- Negotiate a **grace period** in Note § 7.1(e) (e.g., 30 days after the senior acceleration, during which Maker may cure or obtain a waiver from the Senior Lender).
- Ensure the subordination agreement is executed **at Closing** (not after) to lock in the terms.

*Facts to confirm with client:* (a) What are the financial covenants in the senior loan? (b) Has a draft subordination agreement been circulated? (c) What is the senior lender's position on seller note cross-defaults?

## Issue 8 (Low-Moderate): Definitional Conflict Between APA and Note Regarding "Business"

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 1.1: "Business" means "the ownership and operation of the Hotel by Seller."
- Note § 1.2: "Business" means "all hotels and hospitality operations owned, leased or managed by Maker or any of its Affiliates."
- Note § 1.2 (preamble): "Capitalized terms used but not defined in this Note have the meanings given in the Purchase Agreement. **In addition**, the following terms have the following meanings: …"

**Why it matters:**

The Note expressly overrides the APA's definition of "Business" for purposes of the Note. This is the mechanism by which Note § 6.2 (Issue 2) captures Zaibeind's entire portfolio. While this is likely intentional on Seller's part, it creates an inconsistency that could confuse third parties (e.g., the Senior Lender reviewing the Note) and may not have been fully appreciated by Zaibeind during negotiation. The APA's narrow definition governs all other references to "Business" in the APA (e.g., § 4.6 Absence of Changes, § 5.1 Conduct of Business), but the Note's broad definition governs the covenants in Note § 6.

**Recommendation:**

Address as part of the fix for Issue 2. If Note § 6.2 is narrowed to the Hotel only, the definitional conflict becomes less material but should still be harmonized to avoid ambiguity.

## Issue 10 (Low): Bulk Sale Notification Timing Risk

**Documents and Sections:**
- APA § 5.5: Seller shall cooperate in delivering the bulk sale notification "at least ten (10) days before the Closing."
- APA § 3.2(g): Evidence of the filed Form AU-196.10 is a Seller Closing Delivery.
- Integration Notes, Item 8.1: Target filing date is April 3, 2026 (12 days before the April 15 Closing). Seller was asked to provide required information by March 31.

**Why it matters:**

The 12-day buffer is adequate if Seller provides information on time. However, if Seller delays or the NYS Department of Taxation and Finance requires amendments, the filing could slip past the 10-day mark, making it a failed Closing Delivery under § 3.2(g) and potentially giving Zaibeind a condition failure under § 7.1(c). While this would technically protect Zaibeind (it could refuse to close), it creates deal uncertainty and could delay the transaction.

**Recommendation:**

- Track the filing as a critical path item. Confirm with the client that Seller has provided all required information as of March 31.
- If the filing has not been made by April 5, escalate to Seller's counsel.
- Consider whether a late filing (e.g., 8 days before Closing) would be acceptable as a practical matter, or whether Zaibeind would insist on the 10-day requirement as a condition.
