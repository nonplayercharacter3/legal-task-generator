## 1. Ridgeview Farm Nitrate Contamination Is Subject to the Indemnification Cap (Critical)

**Where:**
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities")
- SPA § 9.5(a) (Cap) and § 9.5(c) (uncapped carve-out)
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.17, Part A(1) vs. Part B(1)
- LOI § 5.3

**Issue:** The SPA defines "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" as only those matters "set forth in Part B of Schedule 4.17." Part B contains a single item: the Hollis Farm decommissioned manure pond (estimated closure cost $640,000). The Ridgeview Farm groundwater nitrate contamination—estimated at **$3,400,000 to $7,800,000** for remediation and monitoring (Disclosure Schedule, Sch. 4.17, Part A(1)(d))—sits in Part A, not Part B.

Section 9.5(c) provides that only "Losses arising from Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" are excluded from the Cap. Section 9.5(a) caps all other environmental losses under § 9.2(c) at 10% of the Purchase Price ($4,620,000). The result: the Ridgeview contamination, the single largest environmental exposure in this deal, is subject to the $4.62M cap. If remediation costs land at the high end of the range ($7.8M), Lepund absorbs roughly $3.2M with no recourse.

The LOI is unambiguous on the parties' intent. Section 5.3 states: *"The parties agree that pre-closing environmental liabilities, including Ridgeview Farm nitrate contamination, not subject to the general cap."* The SPA departs from this agreed term.

**Why it matters:** This is the largest quantified risk in the transaction. A $3.4M–$7.8M exposure that the LOI expressly took outside the cap has been silently pulled back inside it through the Part A / Part B structural distinction in the Disclosure Schedule.

**Recommended fix:** Amend the definition of "Pre-Closing Environmental Liabilities" in SPA § 1.1 to read "set forth in **Parts A and B** of Schedule 4.17," or add the Ridgeview matter specifically to Part B of Schedule 4.17. Alternatively, add to § 9.5(c): "Losses arising from the Ridgeview Farm groundwater nitrate contamination described in Schedule 4.17, Part A(1) shall not be subject to the Cap or the Deductible." Confirm the conforming survival period in § 9.1(b) (see Issue 3 below).

## 2. IP Assignment Agreement's Definition of "Business" Excludes Yogurt, Butter, and Cheese (Critical)

**Where:**
- IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Business")
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Business")
- LOI § 3.1
- IP Assignment Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Assigned IP")

**Issue:** The IP Assignment Agreement defines "Business" as *"the production, processing and sale of **organic fluid milk**, as conducted by Assignee as of the date of the Purchase Agreement."* The SPA defines "Business" more broadly as *"the production, processing, marketing and sale of organic fluid milk, **yogurt, butter and cheese**."*

"Assigned IP" is defined as IP "used or held for use in the **Business**" (IP Agreement § 1.1). Because the IP Agreement uses its own, narrower definition, any trademark, recipe, starter culture, or other IP used in the Company's yogurt, butter, or cheese operations falls outside the assignment.

The LOI § 3.1 expressly contemplates that the IP includes *"the Company's yogurt and cheese recipes, and its proprietary starter cultures."* The starter cultures in particular are likely shared across product lines (e.g., a yogurt culture may also be used in cheese). If the "Business" definition captures only fluid milk, the assignment may not reach the full scope of IP the Company needs to operate its existing product portfolio.

**Why it matters:** Lepund is paying $46.2M for a company that makes yogurt, butter, and cheese. If the IP for those product lines is not assigned, the Company may lack the right to make its own products post-closing, or the Seller's affiliate could retain ownership of recipes and cultures central to the business.

**Recommended fix:** Conform the IP Assignment Agreement's definition of "Business" to the SPA's definition (i.e., include "yogurt, butter and cheese" and "marketing"). Alternatively, replace the "Business" qualifier in the "Assigned IP" definition with a broader standard such as "used or held for use in the Company's operations." Add a specific representation in § 4.2 that the Assigned IP includes all IP used in the production of the Company's yogurt, butter, and cheese product lines.

## 3. Pre-Closing Tax Indemnity Survival Period Is Shorter Than the LOI (High)

**Where:**
- SPA § 9.1(b)
- SPA § 9.2(b)
- LOI § 5.2
- Seller Note § 1.3 (Maturity Date = 18 months)

**Issue:** SPA § 9.1(b) provides that "The obligations of Seller under Section 9.2(b) [Pre-Closing Taxes] shall survive until the **Maturity Date** (as defined in the Seller Note)." The Maturity Date is 18 months after closing (Seller Note § 1.3). If closing occurs December 1, 2026, the tax indemnity expires approximately June 1, 2028.

The LOI § 5.2 provided: *"This tax indemnity would survive until 60 days after expiration of the applicable statute of limitations."* For corporate income taxes, the federal statute of limitations is generally three years from filing (six years for substantial omissions). A pre-closing tax year could therefore remain open well beyond 18 months.

**Why it matters:** If the IRS or a state opens an examination of a pre-closing tax year in, say, 2029, Lepund would have no indemnification claim under the SPA even though the LOI contemplated protection for the full limitations period. Pre-closing taxes are a classic post-closing surprise, and 18 months is insufficient.

**Recommended fix:** Amend SPA § 9.1(b) to provide that Seller's obligations under § 9.2(b) survive "until the later of (i) the Maturity Date and (ii) sixty (60) days after the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations (including any extensions) for the relevant taxable period." This tracks the LOI and is market-standard for tax indemnities.

## 4. "Estimated Indebtedness" Is Undefined in the SPA (High)

**Where:**
- SPA § 2.2(a)
- SPA Article I (no definition)
- LOI § 2.2(a)
- Email thread, Msg. 3 (Priya Raman, Nov. 16 – funds flow memo)

**Issue:** SPA § 2.2(a) provides that the cash payment is "$40,200,000, **less the Estimated Indebtedness**." The term "Estimated Indebtedness" does not appear in the defined terms in § 1.1 and is not defined anywhere else in the SPA. The LOI § 2.2(a) uses the phrase "less the Company's indebtedness outstanding at Closing," and the funds flow memo in the email thread uses "Estimated Indebtedness (Green Mountain payoff)" with the amount left as "[per payoff letter]."

**Why it matters:** Without a definition, there is ambiguity about (a) whether the subtraction is based on an estimate agreed at signing or the actual payoff amount at closing, (b) whether it includes only principal or also accrued interest, per diem, and prepayment fees, and (c) what happens if the actual payoff differs from the estimate. A dispute over even $50,000 of payoff fees could create a closing-day friction point.

**Recommended fix:** Add a definition in § 1.1: "'**Estimated Indebtedness**' means the payoff amount set forth in the payoff letter delivered by Green Mountain Farm Credit, ACA in accordance with Section 3.2(e), including principal, accrued and unpaid interest, per diem charges and any prepayment or yield-maintenance fees, as of the Closing Date." Alternatively, tie the cash payment directly to the payoff letter: "$40,200,000, less the amount actually paid to Green Mountain Farm Credit, ACA at the Closing pursuant to the payoff letter."

## 5. Transition Services Agreement: Recipient's Indemnity Is Broad and Uncapped (Moderate)

**Where:**
- TSA § 8.2 (Recipient's indemnity)
- TSA § 8.4 (liability cap and carve-out)
- TSA § 8.1 (Provider's indemnity – for comparison)

**Issue:** TSA § 8.2 requires Recipient (Lepund) to indemnify Provider (Seller) for *"all Losses arising out of or relating to the provision of the Services or Recipient's use of the Services, except to the extent caused by Provider's willful misconduct."* This is an extremely broad trigger—it captures losses "arising out of" the services themselves, not just Lepund's own fault.

TSA § 8.4 caps each party's aggregate liability at "the total Fees paid by Recipient under this Agreement" but then carves out: *"The limitations in Section 8.3 and this Section 8.4 shall not apply to **Recipient's obligations under Section 8.2**."* So Lepund's indemnity to Seller is **unlimited**.

By contrast, Seller's indemnity to Lepund (§ 8.1) covers only "gross negligence or willful misconduct" and is subject to the fee cap.

**Why it matters:** Over the 9-month TSA term, the fees total roughly $1.2M–$1.5M (milk hauling at ~$1.42–$1.68/cwt plus $24,300/month for certification and payroll). If a milk-hauling accident causes a third-party injury, the loss "arises out of the provision of the Services" and Lepund's uncapped indemnity could expose it to significant liability, while Seller's own indemnity would not trigger unless its gross negligence or willful misconduct is shown.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Narrow § 8.2 to cover only Losses "caused by Recipient's gross negligence or willful misconduct" or "arising out of Recipient's breach of this Agreement," mirroring the standard in § 8.1. (b) Delete the carve-out in § 8.4 so that the fee cap applies to both parties' indemnification obligations. (c) At minimum, add "or Provider's gross negligence" to the exception in § 8.2 so that Lepund is not indemnifying Seller for Seller's own negligent performance.

## 6. TSA Allows Provider to Delegate Services Without Buyer's Consent (Moderate)

**Where:**
- TSA § 12.3 (Assignment)

**Issue:** TSA § 12.3 provides: *"Provider may assign this Agreement, in whole or in part, or delegate the performance of any Service to any Person upon written notice to Recipient and **without the consent of Recipient**."*

**Why it matters:** The milk hauling service requires bulk tank trucks dedicated to certified-organic milk, drivers with state-specific bulk milk hauler and sampler licenses, and compliance with the Pasteurized Milk Ordinance (TSA § 3.1). The organic certification support service requires familiarity with the Company's organic system plans and certifying agent relationships (TSA § 3.2). Allowing Seller to hand these off to an unknown third party on notice alone could compromise organic certification status, food safety compliance, and producer relationships—risks that would fall on Lepund post-closing.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 12.3 to require Recipient's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any delegation of the Services, or at minimum for the milk hauling and organic certification support services. Add a requirement that any delegate meet the same licensing, equipment, and insurance standards set forth in §§ 3.1 and 9.

## 7. Closing Condition 8.1(e) Is Overbroad Given Post-Closing Notices in Schedule 4.4 (Moderate)

**Where:**
- SPA § 8.1(e)
- Disclosure Schedule, Schedule 4.4, items (b) and (d)

**Issue:** SPA § 8.1(e) makes closing conditional on "receipt of all third-party **consents** listed in Schedule 4.4." Schedule 4.4 lists four items:

- (a) Green Mountain Farm Credit – consent required (satisfied by payoff)
- (b) Harvest Basket Markets – **notice** within 10 days after change of control; "No consent is required"
- (c) Northeast Natural Grocers – **consent** required
- (d) Vermont Agency of Agriculture – **notice** within 30 days **after** Closing

Items (b) and (d) are notices, not consents, and item (d) cannot be given before closing (it is due 30 days *after* closing). As drafted, § 8.1(e) could be read to require all four items to be satisfied before closing, which is impossible for item (d).

**Why it matters:** A technical failure of a closing condition could give either party an argument to delay or walk. More practically, it creates ambiguity that Seller's counsel could exploit if the deal encounters other friction.

**Recommended fix:** Amend § 8.1(e) to read: "receipt of all third-party **consents** (as distinguished from notices) listed in Schedule 4.4 that are required to be obtained **prior to** the Closing." Alternatively, restate the condition as: "receipt of the Northeast Natural Grocers Cooperative consent described in Schedule 4.4(c) and the Green Mountain Farm Credit payoff described in Schedule 4.4(a)."

## 8. Non-Competition Geographic Scope Is Narrower Than the LOI (Low-Moderate)

**Where:**
- SPA § 6.5
- LOI § 4.2

**Issue:** LOI § 4.2 contemplated a non-compete "covering the Business in the **northeastern United States**." SPA § 6.5 limits the restriction to five states: Vermont, New York, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, and Maine. This omits Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Pennsylvania (and arguably other states that could be included in "northeastern United States").

**Why it matters:** If Seller or its affiliates launch a competing organic dairy operation in, e.g., upstate New York is covered, but western Massachusetts borders Connecticut. A competitor in Connecticut could supply the same retail accounts (Harvest Basket, Greenleaf, etc.) that the Company serves. The five-state restriction covers where the Company currently operates, but the LOI's broader language suggests the parties agreed to a wider protection.

**Recommended fix:** Expand § 6.5 to include Connecticut and Rhode Island, or use the LOI's language: "the northeastern United States (meaning the States of Vermont, New York, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, Maine, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Pennsylvania)." If Seller resists, the five-state version is defensible given where the Farms and Plant are located, but the deviation from the LOI should be a conscious business decision by the client, not a drafting oversight.

## 9. Authority Gap: Company's "President" Signs IP Assignment but Board Resolutions Name Different Officers (Low)

**Where:**
- IP Assignment Agreement, signature block (Company signs as "President")
- Lepund Board Resolutions § 3.7 (Authorized Officers = CEO, CFO, Secretary)
- SPA § 3.2(b) (IP Assignment as closing deliverable)

**Issue:** The IP Assignment Agreement signature block for the Company (Assignee) calls for a "President." The Lepund board resolutions authorize only the CEO, CFO, and Secretary as "Authorized Officers" who may execute Transaction Documents. The resolutions do not reference a "President" of the Company. At closing, the Company's existing officers (pre-resignation) would need authority to sign, or the new officers (post-resignation) would need to be in place first. The sequence in SPA § 3.2 lists the IP Assignment (item b) before the resignations (item d), suggesting the Company's current management signs it.

**Why it matters:** If the person signing for the Company lacks authority, the assignment could be challenged. This is unlikely to be litigated given the economic reality, but it creates a technical defect in the closing deliverables.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm who will sign for the Company on the IP Assignment and ensure that person is either (a) an officer with existing authority under the Company's bylaws to execute the assignment as part of the sale transaction, or (b) covered by a resolution of the Company's board (the existing board, pre-closing) authorizing the specific signatory. Add the Company's board resolution or secretary's certificate to the closing checklist.

## 10. SPA § 4.2 (Authority) Does Not Extend to the IP Assignment Agreement (Low)

**Where:**
- SPA § 4.2
- SPA § 1.1 (definition of "Fundamental Representations")
- IP Assignment Agreement § 4.1

**Issue:** SPA § 4.2 represents that Seller has authority to execute "this Agreement and the Ancillary Agreements **to which it is a party**." Seller is not a party to the IP Assignment Agreement (Brouloul Farmstead IP Holdings, LLC is the Assignor). Therefore, § 4.2 does not cover the IP Assignment. Because § 4.2 is a Fundamental Representation (SPA § 1.1), it carries the longest survival period and is uncapped (SPA §§ 9.1(a), 9.5(c)). The IP Assignment's own authority rep (§ 4.1) is a general representation subject to the 15-month survival and the cap.

**Why it matters:** If Brouloul Farmstead IP Holdings lacked authority to assign the IP, Lepund's claim would be a general rep breach (capped at $4.62M, 15-month survival) rather than a Fundamental Rep breach (uncapped, survival until 60 days after statute of limitations). Given that the IP is central to the business value, this is an unintended downgrade in protection.

**Recommended fix:** Either (a) add Brouloul Farmstead IP Holdings as a guarantor or joiner to the SPA's authority representation, or (b) add to SPA § 4.2: "Seller has caused Brouloul Farmstead IP Holdings, LLC to have full power and authority to execute, deliver and perform the IP Assignment Agreement." Alternatively, elevate the IP Assignment's authority and title reps to Fundamental Representation status in SPA § 1.1.
