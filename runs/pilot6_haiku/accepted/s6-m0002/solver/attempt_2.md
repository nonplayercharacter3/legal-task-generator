## 2. Priority 1: Address Now

### Issue 1. The DA §3.3 non-compete may already be breached

- **Where:** DA §3.3 (Distributor Non-Competition). Compare Recital B, SOW §1.2, DA §19.4, and SOW §9.1.
- **Why it matters:** During the Term, Glakin "shall not, and shall cause its Affiliates not to" manufacture, market, promote, distribute or sell "any veterinary diagnostic instrument, reagent, test kit or reference-laboratory service, other than the Products, anywhere in North America." Recital B says Glakin "distributes veterinary diagnostic and animal health products." Unless Glakin's current lines are limited to Sumeith products, it may be in breach now. The clause is far broader than the hematology-only scope in SOW §1.2, and it reaches Canada and Mexico even though the Territory is the United States (DA §1.1(r)). A material breach allows termination after a 60-day cure period (DA §15.1), and DA §16.3 gives Glakin no inventory buyback if Sumeith terminates on that basis. Our best argument that SOW §1.2 narrows §3.3 is weak, because DA §19.4 ranks the SOW last and SOW §9.1 says the SOW changes nothing in the DA.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Obtain Glakin's and its Affiliates' full list of veterinary diagnostic, reagent, test kit and reference-lab offerings.
  - Confirm compliance, or seek a signed written waiver or amendment (DA §19.5 requires a signed writing). The amendment should limit §3.3 to hematology analyzers and cartridges, grandfather existing lines, and limit geography to the U.S.
  - Advise Glakin not to add any new diagnostic line until this is resolved.
  - I have not researched enforceability of §3.3 under New York law (see Part 5).

### Issue 2. Amend. §3.1 (new DA §12.3) creates an uncapped, broad regulatory indemnity from Glakin

- **Where:** Amend. §3.1 (replacing "[Reserved]" DA §12.3); Amend. §1.2 (new definitions (s) and (t)); Amend. §3.2 (new DA §11.3(f)); DA §§11.3(a), 12.4 and 1.1(g); Amend. Recital D.
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Scope.** Glakin must indemnify Sumeith against "all Losses arising out of or relating to any Regulatory Claims relating to Distributor's storage, handling, marketing or sale of Products, including any failure to obtain or maintain a Distribution License." "Regulatory Claims" include inspection observations, untitled letters, and "any third-party claim alleging a violation of law ... relating to the Products" (Amend. §1.2(t)). "Losses" include fines, penalties and investigation costs (DA §1.1(g)).
  - **Seller exposure.** Glakin is the seller of every Product it distributes. A regulatory matter about a Product can therefore be argued to "relate to" Glakin's sale, even where the cause is Sumeith's design, labeling or manufacture.
  - **No fault limit.** The existing indemnities in DA §12.1 and §12.2 apply "to the extent arising from" the indemnitor's conduct. New §12.3 has no such limit, so Glakin could bear the entire loss even where Sumeith is partly at fault.
  - **Uncapped.** DA §11.3(a) already excludes all Section 12 indemnities from the §11.1 and §11.2 limits. The new §11.3(f) (Amend. §3.2) largely duplicates that exclusion. The practical change is the new indemnity itself.
  - **Defense control.** Amend. §3.1 applies DA §12.4, which gives the indemnifying party "sole control of the defense." If Glakin is the indemnitor, Glakin could control Sumeith's response to an FDA or USDA matter. Under the DA and QA, complaint investigation and regulatory reporting belong to Sumeith (DA §7.1; QA §§6.3, 8.4).
  - **Asymmetry.** Sumeith gives no reciprocal regulatory indemnity (Issue 8).
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Limit §12.3 to Regulatory Claims "to the extent" caused by Glakin's storage, handling, transport, marketing or sale, or by Glakin's breach of DA §7.2 or of its license obligations.
  - Exclude claims to the extent caused by Sumeith's design, manufacture, labeling, registration, release or Field Action conduct.
  - Carve regulatory matters out of §12.4 sole control. Each party should control its own regulator communications, with cooperation.
  - Subject §12.3 to a cap or sublimit, such as the §11.2 cap or a defined insurance limit. Delete §11.3(f) or keep it only together with that cap.
  - Do not sign any further amendment in the current form.

### Issue 4. Carrier-caused excursions are Glakin's responsibility to Sumeith, but Pellbrook recovery is limited

- **Where:**
  - Sumeith side: DA §7.2(a) (as amended by Amend. §2.1); QA §4.4 ("Distributor remains responsible for compliance with this Section 4.4 when it uses third-party carriers"); DA §9.3 and QA §9.4 (Supplier's recall-cost obligation excludes costs caused by Glakin's breach of §7.2 or QA §4); DA §11.2.
  - Pellbrook side: PA §7.2 (measure of liability); PA §9.1 (consequential damages excluded); PA §9.2 (cap); PA §9.3 (carve-outs); PA §8.1 (indemnity); PA §13.1 and §7.2(c) (force majeure); PA Schedule C (fees).
- **Why it matters:**
  - If a Pellbrook excursion is the cause, Sumeith can argue Glakin breached DA §7.2 and QA §4.4. That would shift Sumeith's recall costs to Glakin (DA §9.3; QA §9.4). Sumeith's direct claim would then be subject to the DA §11.2 cap, which is 12 months of Glakin's fees. Given Exhibit C minimums of $2.4M to $4.4M, that cap could be in the millions.
  - Glakin's recovery from Pellbrook is much smaller:
    - **Measure of loss (PA §7.2).** Recovery is at replacement cost "as shown on Customer's invoice cost records," which is Glakin's transfer price, plus the shipment charges. It does not include Sumeith's recall costs or Glakin's margin.
    - **Consequential loss (PA §9.1).** Consequential damages and lost profits are excluded. Only §7.2 amounts are expressly "direct."
    - **Cap (PA §9.2).** The cap is the greater of $1,000,000 and 12 months of Glakin's fees, and it applies in aggregate for each 12-month period. Pellbrook's per-package fees are $21.25 to $79.00 (Schedule C), so Glakin's fees are probably below $1M and the cap is likely $1M for all claims in that year.
    - **Weather (PA §13.1; §7.2(c)).** "Severe weather" is force majeure, and Pellbrook is not liable for force-majeure-caused excursions. Weather is a principal cause of temperature excursions. The carve-out that makes mechanical breakdowns, labor shortages and refrigeration failures non-force-majeure (PA §13.1) does not cover ordinary heat or cold.
    - **Third-party claims (PA §§8.1, 9.3(a)).** Third-party injury and similar claims caused by Pellbrook are indemnified without the cap. That gives back-to-back coverage for third-party claims but not for Sumeith's direct recall claim against Glakin.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - **Amend the PA** to: (i) treat Sumeith-related recall and replacement costs as direct damages; (ii) raise the cap for temperature-controlled shipments, for example per shipment or by reference to inventory value; (iii) narrow the weather force majeure so that ordinary seasonal heat and cold are not excused; and (iv) require refrigeration breakdown cover at inventory value (Issue 15).
  - **Negotiate with Sumeith.** Seek to treat carrier-caused excursions as shared responsibility, or to limit Glakin's exposure to replacement and handling costs.
  - **Operationally,** treat every excursion as a potential Sumeith claim and follow the notice steps in Issue 13.

### Issue 5. Recall-cost cap conflict: QA §9.4 versus DA §11.3(d)–(e), with QA priority under DA §19.4

- **Where:** QA §9.4, last sentence ("Supplier's liability for recall costs and for Nonconformities under this Quality Agreement is subject to Section 11.2 of the Distribution Agreement"); DA §11.3(d) and (e); DA §19.4 (order of precedence: Amendment, then QA "quality matters only," then DA, then SOW); DA §9.3.
- **Why it matters:** DA §11.3(d) takes Supplier's recall liability outside the §11.2 cap, and §11.3(e) does the same for breach of quality obligations. QA §9.4 says the opposite. Under DA §19.4, the QA controls "quality matters," and recall costs are a quality matter. Sumeith can therefore argue that its recall-cost liability is capped at 12 months of Glakin's fees. For Glakin this is a major financial exposure, and it sits on a point where Glakin's claimed protection looks clear on the DA but is contradicted by the QA.
- **Recommended fix:** In the next amendment, delete the "subject to Section 11.2" sentence in QA §9.4, or state expressly that DA §11.3(d) and (e) control. Also define "quality matters" in DA §19.4. Until fixed, assume the cap may apply.

### Issue 6. The 9% cartridge price increase: objection window has likely closed; confirm facts

- **Where:** DA §5.4 (notice, 60-day minimum, 45-day objection window, deemed acceptance); DA §18.1 (notice formalities); Amend. Recital B; Amend. §4.1 (otherwise unchanged).
- **Why it matters:**
  - The notice was dated July 20, 2026, with an effective date of October 1, 2026. That is 73 days, which satisfies the 60-day minimum.
  - Glakin had 45 days after receipt to object. If receipt was July 20, the deadline was September 3, 2026. If Glakin did not timely object, the increase is "deemed accepted" and Exhibit B is amended (DA §5.4).
  - Glakin's CEO signed Amend. on September 14, 2026, and Recital B recites the notice without reservation. Sumeith may argue acceptance. DA §19.5 provides that no waiver is effective unless in writing, which helps only partly, since the Amendment is a signed writing.
  - The increase is about $37.13 per SDX-RC25 pack ($412.50 base) and $140.40 per SDX-RC100 pack ($1,560 base). Glakin sets its own resale prices (DA §3.5), so it bears the margin impact.
  - Under DA §18.1, notices must go by hand, courier, or email with courier confirmation. If the July 20 notice was sent by email alone, the 45-day window may never have started.
- **Recommended fix:** Establish the receipt date, the delivery method, and whether any objection was sent. Confirm whether the price increase was part of the trade for the Amendment's regulatory indemnity. Advise on pass-through strategy and on the effect of the increase on the minimum commitment, since it raises invoiced value (DA §4.3).

### Issue 8. No reciprocal regulatory indemnity from Sumeith

- **Where:** DA §12.1 (Supplier indemnity for "third-party claim" arising from design, manufacture or labeling defects or Supplier breach); DA §8.2 (compliance warranty); DA §7.1(b) and (c) (Supplier responsible for labeling and registrations); Amend. §3.1.
- **Why it matters:** Because Glakin is the seller, it can be the target of a federal or state enforcement action caused by Sumeith's labeling, registration or manufacturing failures. Under the new §12.3, Glakin would indemnify Sumeith for Regulatory Claims "relating to" Glakin's sale. Sumeith has no express obligation to indemnify Glakin for regulatory action arising from its own conduct. §12.1 refers to "third-party claims," which may not clearly include regulator enforcement. DA §8.5 also disclaims other warranties.
- **Recommended fix:** Add a Supplier indemnity for Regulatory Claims to the extent arising from Sumeith's design, manufacture, labeling, registration, release or Field Action conduct. Mirror the language of §12.3 and give it the same uncapped treatment.

### Issue 9. No inventory repurchase on non-renewal or expiry

- **Where:** DA §2.2 (non-renewal); DA §16.2 (six-month sell-off); DA §16.3 (repurchase only where Glakin terminates under §15.1 or §15.3).
- **Why it matters:** The Initial Term ends on or about January 31, 2028. Either party may give non-renewal notice not earlier than 120 and not later than 90 days before the end, which is approximately October 3 to November 2, 2027. If Sumeith non-renews, Glakin has no repurchase right and only a six-month sell-off. Glakin's own non-renewal window is the same.
- **Recommended fix:** Negotiate a repurchase right at the transfer price for any expiry, non-renewal, or termination not caused by Glakin's breach. Calendar October 3 to November 2, 2027.

### Issue 10. Disposition of the Demonstration Units

- **Where:** SOW §5.1 (up to 12 SDX-HA200 units on loan, title with Sumeith); SOW §5.2 (care and insurance under DA §14.1); SOW §5.3 (return, or purchase at 50% of Exhibit B price, at the end of the Launch Period); SOW §1.3 (Launch Period ended January 31, 2025).

### Issue 11. Recall definitions do not match

- **Where:** DA §1.1(l) ("Recall," which does not include software or firmware corrections); DA §9.1 ("promptly" notice); DA §9.3 (recall costs); QA §1.2(d) ("Field Action," which includes software or firmware corrections and Customer advisories); QA §9.1–9.4 (Supplier notice within one business day after decision; cost coverage for "each Field Action").
- **Why it matters:** Cost-shifting under DA §9.3 uses the narrower term. QA §9.4 uses the broader "Field Action." Under DA §19.4 the QA controls on quality matters, so Glakin has an argument that the broader term governs, but the point is open to dispute. The "promptly" and "one business day" standards are also inconsistent.
- **Recommended fix:** Conform the definitions. Use "Field Action" consistently in the DA and QA, and define it to include firmware corrections and Customer advisories.

### Issue 12. Sharing Sumeith information with Pellbrook

- **Where:** DA §13.1 (disclosure only to "employees, Affiliates and advisors"); SOW §8.3 (reports are confidential); PA §11.1 (protects Glakin's data, not Sumeith's).
- **Why it matters:** Excursion response and traceability may require Glakin to give Pellbrook Sumeith non-public material, such as validated packaging data, CoA details, or lot information. DA §13.1 does not permit disclosure to carriers. Published Specifications (DA §1.1(o)) are probably public, but other material may not be.
- **Recommended fix:** Identify what Pellbrook receives. Limit it to public labeling and storage conditions, or obtain Sumeith's written consent. Add a carrier confidentiality clause that tracks DA §13.

### Issue 13. Traceability and notice timelines do not fit together

- **Where:**
  - QA §5.1 (Glakin must produce traceability records within 1 business day after a Field Action request).
  - PA §4.3 (carrier data within 1 business day after Glakin's request).
  - QA §5.3 versus PA §6.1 (record retention).
  - QA §4.2 and §10.1 (2 business days to notify Sumeith).
  - PA §4.1 (carrier must notify Glakin within 2 hours).
  - PA §7.3 (claims within 90 days after scheduled delivery).
- **Why it matters:**
  - If Sumeith asks for traceability during a Field Action, Glakin may need carrier data, which can take a full business day, and then must produce it within one business day. That leaves no margin.
  - PA §6.1 keeps records 5 years after delivery. QA §5.3 requires the longer of 5 years after the record or 2 years after the last lot expires. If a lot expires more than 3 years after shipment, PA retention falls short.
  - QA §10.1 applies only to "Nonconformities." It is unclear whether a post-shipment storage or carrier excursion is a Nonconformity, so the 2-business-day notice may not clearly apply. Glakin should treat it as applying.
  - PA §7.3 imposes a 90-day claim deadline per shipment, which could be missed.
- **Recommended fix:**
  - Require Pellbrook to deliver Sumeith-related data the same business day.
  - Keep copies of carrier data in Glakin's own systems.
  - Extend PA retention to match QA §5.3.
  - Calendar the 90-day claim window.
  - Adopt an internal rule: notify Sumeith's Quality Contact within 2 business days of any carrier excursion.

### Issue 14. Pellbrook does not cover all of Glakin's obligations to Sumeith

- **Where:**
  - PA §2.1 (delivery only in the contiguous United States) versus DA §1.1(r) (Territory includes U.S. territories and possessions).
  - PA §2.2 and Schedule C (ambient option with no temperature monitoring) versus QA Schedule 1 (Analyzer storage 5°C to 40°C), QA §4.4 and DA §7.2(a).
  - PA §1.1(i) (default Temperature Range of 2°C to 8°C).
  - DA §6.1 and §6.2 (FCA Durham; risk passes to Glakin when goods are handed to the carrier Glakin designates).
- **Why it matters:**
  - Shipments to Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Guam and other non-contiguous areas are outside the PA, so another qualified cold-chain carrier is needed (QA §4.4).
  - Analyzers shipped ambient have no monitoring obligation. Heat above 40°C would breach DA §7.2(a) without any recourse against the carrier.
  - Inbound transport from Durham is not covered by the PA. The carrier that handles it is unidentified.
  - Cartridge shipments must state the 2°C to 8°C range and the "Do Not Freeze" and "Protect from Light" marks in the Shipment Order (PA §§2.2(e), 3.1). If they do not, the carrier's protective obligations may not attach.
- **Recommended fix:** Identify the inbound carrier and confirm its qualification. Require temperature-monitored shipment for all Analyzers, or document ambient qualification with Sumeith. Add non-contiguous coverage or a second carrier. Standardize the Shipment Order template so it states the required range and marks.

### Issue 15. Pellbrook insurance is thin relative to inventory value

- **Where:** PA §12.1(a) through (c) (CGL $2M per occurrence and $4M aggregate; auto $2M; cargo $500,000 per vehicle per occurrence, including refrigeration breakdown); PA §12.1, final paragraph (Glakin named as additional insured only on CGL and auto).

- **Recommended fix:** Require cargo limits at least equal to the maximum inventory value on any vehicle. Name Glakin as loss payee on the cargo policy. Confirm that Glakin's own policy also meets DA §14.1 and Amend. §3.3.

### Issue 16. Pellbrook timing: renewal, convenience termination, and leverage

- **Where:** PA §10.1 (two-year initial term from March 1, 2025, then one-year renewals; 60 days' non-renewal notice); PA §10.2 (90 days' termination for convenience); PA §10.3 (immediate termination if the Temperature Compliance service level is missed in two consecutive months); PA Schedule B (99.5% target; 10% credit); PA §5.3 (CPI-capped fee changes with a right to terminate).

Otherwise the agreement renews for one year. Glakin does have leverage, because it can terminate for convenience on 90 days' notice and immediately for repeated temperature failures.
- **Recommended fix:** Decide before about December 29, 2026 whether to renegotiate (Issues 4 and 15) or to give notice. Do not let the PA renew on current terms while the Sumeith exposures are open.
