## 4. Issue Detail

### A. Critical: Act Now

**Issue 0. Folder copies appear unsigned.**
- **Where:** Signature blocks in DA, SOW, QA, Amend. (p. 2) and Pellbrook Agmt. Each "By:" line is blank; only the dates are filled in.
- **Why it matters:** An amendment must be "a written instrument signed by both Parties" (DA §19.5). If Sumeith never signed the Amendment, its new terms may not be effective. We should not assume that, though. Glakin's conduct since September 14 may be treated as accepting the Amendment, and the signature blocks may just reflect how the folder was prepared. We cannot advise on enforceability until we know.
- **Fix:** Obtain fully executed copies from the client, including any electronic-signature certificates (DA §19.7). Check the transmittal emails for the Amendment, and any side letters or negotiation correspondence.

**Issue A1. Amendment facility list appears overdue.**
- **Where:** Amend. §2.2 (facility list due within 30 days after the Amendment Date of September 14, 2026).
- **Why it matters:** The deadline was October 14, 2026, and today is November 2. If the list was not delivered, Glakin is in breach of a new covenant. Under DA §15.1, Sumeith would need to give written notice and Glakin would have 60 days to cure, so this is not an immediate termination risk. It is still an easy complaint for Sumeith to raise. Pellbrook Schedule A lists two Origin Facilities, including the Harrisburg Fulfillment Center in Pennsylvania. Amend. Recital C says Glakin has expanded its warehousing network, so the list may need more than the two Pellbrook facilities.
- **Fix:** Confirm today whether the list was delivered, when, and by what method (DA §18.1 requires hand delivery, overnight courier, or email with courier confirmation). If not delivered, send it now with a short cover letter. Confirm that each listed facility holds the required Distribution Licenses (see A2). Calendar the 30-day update duty for any future opening or closing.

**Issue A2. Distribution License coverage.**
- **Where:** Amend. §1.2 (new DA §1.1(s), defining "Distribution Licenses" as all licenses required by federal, state or local law "in each jurisdiction in the Territory in which it does so"); Amend. §2.1 (new DA §7.2(c): obtain and maintain all Distribution Licenses; copies within 10 business days of request; new DA §7.2(d): notice within 5 business days of any suspension, revocation, non-renewal or material restriction).
- **Why it matters:** The license covenant is broad. Any gap, whether a license never obtained or one that lapsed without the 5-day notice, is a trigger for the uncapped indemnity in new DA §12.3 ("including any failure to obtain or maintain a Distribution License"). Because the license covenant is part of DA §7.2, a license failure may also be used to deny Glakin recall-cost recovery under DA §9.3 and QA §9.4 (see B3). We have not verified which licenses apply in which states or at which facilities.
- **Fix:** Build a license inventory by facility and by state of sale. Have regulatory counsel confirm which licenses are required, including any in Pennsylvania for Harrisburg. Set up tracking for the 5-day notice and the 10-day copy request.

**Issue A3. Uncapped, non-fault-based regulatory indemnity.**
- **Where:** Amend. §3.1 (new DA §12.3); Amend. §3.2 (new DA §11.3(f)); Amend. §1.2 (new DA §1.1(t), "Regulatory Claims"); DA §1.1(g) ("Losses," including fines, penalties and costs of investigation); DA §§11.3(a), 12.1, 12.2, 12.4, 16.4; Amend. §4.2 (Amendment prevails under DA §19.4).
- **Why it matters:**
  - **Breadth.** Glakin must indemnify Sumeith for "all Losses arising out of or relating to any Regulatory Claims relating to Distributor's storage, handling, marketing or sale of Products." Regulatory Claims include inspection observations, warning letters, untitled letters, and "any third-party claim alleging a violation of law" relating to the Products (new DA §1.1(t)).
  - **No fault requirement.** The existing indemnities are "to the extent arising from" the indemnitor's breach or negligence (DA §§12.1, 12.2). New §12.3 uses "arising out of or relating to." Sumeith could argue that an FDA warning letter about Sumeith's labeling "relates to" Glakin's sale of the product.
  - **No cap.** DA §11.3(a) already excludes all Section 12 indemnities from the consequential-damages exclusion and the cap. Amend. §3.2 confirms this expressly, which means Glakin's exposure is unlimited.
  - **Defense control.** Under DA §12.4, which applies to new §12.3 by Amend. §3.1, the indemnifying party (Glakin) gets "sole control of the defense and settlement." That would give Glakin control of Sumeith's responses to regulators about matters Sumeith is responsible for under DA §7.1 and QA §§3.1 and 3.4 (design, manufacture, labeling). Sumeith will not accept that, and it is not workable.
  - **No time limit.** Section 12 survives termination (DA §16.4), with no end date.
  - **Consideration.** The original DA reserved §12.3 as "[Reserved.]" (DA §12.3). The Amendment's recitals do not identify any reciprocal concession from Sumeith. The 9% price increase was noticed the same month (see A5), but the Amendment does not link the two.
- **Fix (negotiating position):**
  1. Limit the indemnity to Losses "to the extent arising from" Glakin's breach of DA §7.2, its negligence, or willful misconduct in storage, handling, marketing or sale.
  2. Exclude Regulatory Claims arising from Product design, manufacture, testing, release, labeling, or Sumeith's breach of its obligations.
  3. Narrow "Regulatory Claims" to matters directed at Glakin's conduct or facilities.
  4. Sumeith controls regulatory responses that concern its own responsibilities. Glakin controls only matters concerning its own facilities and operations. Require Glakin's consent to any settlement involving an admission.
  5. Cap the indemnity (for example, at trailing 12-month fees), or delete Amend. §3.2 and make §12.3 subject to the general cap.
  6. Add a sunset.
- **If Sumeith refuses:** We should research New York law on whether an indemnity covers claims between the contracting parties (Sumeith's own losses, as opposed to third-party claims) and on whether the Amendment was supported by consideration. This memo cites no authority on either point; that research is still needed.

**Issue A4. Non-compete may already be breached.**
- **Where:** DA §3.3 (during the Term, Distributor and its Affiliates may not "manufacture, market, promote, distribute or sell any veterinary diagnostic instrument, reagent, test kit or reference-laboratory service, other than the Products, anywhere in North America"); DA Recital B (Glakin distributes "veterinary diagnostic and animal health products"); SOW §1.2 (the "agreed exclusivity scope" is hematology analyzers and cartridges, "United States only"); SOW §9.1 (SOW changes nothing in the DA); DA §19.4 (DA prevails over SOW); DA §§12.2(a), 15.1.
- **Why it matters:** The non-compete reaches all veterinary diagnostic products, reagents, test kits and reference-lab services, across North America (including Canada and Mexico, which are outside the Territory), and it binds Glakin's Affiliates. The SOW describes a much narrower scope, but SOW §9.1 and DA §19.4 mean the DA controls. The SOW is useful evidence of intent, but probably not a defense. If Glakin sells any other covered product, it may be in breach now, which could support termination under DA §15.1 (60-day cure) and an indemnity claim under DA §12.2(a).
- **Fix:** Confirm immediately every veterinary diagnostic instrument, reagent, test kit and reference-lab service that Glakin and its Affiliates sell or market in North America. If there is any conflict:

  - Ask for a reciprocal limit on Sumeith's direct sales (see D2).
  - Pending resolution, Glakin should not add any new covered line.

### B. High

**Issue B1. Pellbrook liability does not match Glakin's exposure to Sumeith.**
- **Where:**
  - *Glakin's side:* QA §4.4 ("Distributor remains responsible for compliance... when it uses third-party carriers"); QA §9.4 and DA §9.3 (Sumeith does not bear recall costs where the Field Action results from Glakin's breach of QA §4 or DA §7.2); DA §12.3 (see A3).
  - *Pellbrook's side:* Pellbrook §7.2 (liability measured by replacement cost plus charges; excludes Customer packaging failures and inherent defects); §7.3 (claims due within 90 days after scheduled delivery); §8.1 (indemnity only for third-party claims, fault-based); §9.1 (consequential damages excluded; §7.2 amounts are direct); §9.2 (cap of the greater of $1,000,000 and trailing 12-month fees, per 12-month period); §9.3 (indemnities uncapped).
- **Why it matters:** If a Pellbrook-caused excursion triggers a Sumeith recall or regulatory action, Glakin would:
  - bear the recall costs, because the QA §9.4 exclusion applies;
  - potentially face the new §12.3 indemnity;
  - recover from Pellbrook only replacement cost and freight under §7.2. Recall costs, regulatory costs and lost profits are unlikely to be recoverable, and the cap is $1M or 12 months of fees per 12-month period;
  - potentially lose the claim entirely if it is not submitted within 90 days of the scheduled delivery date (§7.3).
- **Fix:** Negotiate a Pellbrook amendment (or replacement agreement) to:
  - add a carrier indemnity for recall, field-action and regulatory costs that Sumeith passes through to Glakin, where caused by the carrier;
  - raise the cap or carve out those costs from it;
  - adopt product-specific handling standards that mirror QA Schedule 1 and QA §4.4;
  - extend the claim window to 180 days or to the lot expiry date;
  - add a right for Sumeith to audit the carrier through Glakin.
  - Use the Pellbrook §10.3 right to terminate immediately after two consecutive months of missed Temperature Compliance service levels as leverage. Also use the §10.2 convenience termination (90 days).

**Issue B2. Inbound transit from Sumeith's Durham facility is not covered.**
- **Where:** DA §6.1 (FCA Durham, to the carrier Glakin designates); DA §6.2 (title and risk pass on delivery to carrier); QA §4.2 (excursions before delivery to carrier are Nonconformities; excursions after are not); Pellbrook §2.1 and Schedule A (pickups only at Glakin's Origin Facilities, deliveries to clinics, contiguous U.S. only).
- **Why it matters:** Pellbrook does not cover the leg from Durham to Glakin's warehouses. The folder does not identify the inbound carrier. Because risk passes at FCA, an excursion during inbound transit after pickup is Glakin's loss. Sumeith has no QA §4.2 exposure for it. Glakin's recovery depends on whatever contract governs that carrier.
- **Fix:** Identify the inbound carrier and its contract. Confirm it has cold-chain qualification, data logging, adequate liability limits and cargo insurance. If it does not, either fix the carrier arrangement or negotiate with Sumeith to move inbound risk to Sumeith (for example, DAP instead of FCA). Any change to DA §6.1 requires an amendment (DA §19.5).

**Issue B3. Recall costs: conflict between the DA and QA on whether they are capped.**
- **Where:** DA §11.3(d) (the cap and consequential-damages exclusion do not apply to Supplier's Recall liability, "including under Section 9.3"); QA §9.4, last sentence ("Supplier's liability for recall costs... is subject to Section 11.2 of the Distribution Agreement"); DA §19.4 (Amendment, then QA "quality matters only," then DA, then SOW); DA §9.3 and QA §9.4 (exclusions tied to Glakin's breach of DA §7.2, as expanded by Amend. §2.1).
- **Why it matters:** Sumeith can argue that recall costs are a quality matter, so QA §9.4 controls and caps Sumeith's recall liability at trailing 12-month fees. Glakin can argue that DA §11.3(d) is the specific provision and is uncapped. The conflict matters for any serious Field Action. The expanded DA §7.2 also means a license lapse could be used to argue that Glakin's recall-cost recovery is excluded, even though a license lapse has nothing to do with the recall.
- **Fix:** By a countersigned QA amendment (QA §11.3), conform QA §9.4 to DA §11.3(d). Limit the exclusion to Field Actions caused by Glakin's breach of storage, handling or traceability obligations (DA §7.2(a)–(b), QA §4, QA §5). Require a causal link ("results from") and exclude license technicalities.

### C. Medium

**Issue C1. Storage marking dependency.**
- **Where:** Pellbrook §2.2(e) (no freezing or light exposure only for Goods "that the Shipment Order marks"); Pellbrook §3.1 (Shipment Order content); QA Sch. 1 (cartridges and QC material: 2–8°C, do not freeze, protect from light); QA §4.4.
- **Why it matters:** If Glakin's Shipment Orders do not carry these marks, Pellbrook has no duty to protect against freezing or light, though Glakin remains responsible under QA §4.4.
- **Fix:** Standardize the Shipment Order template for Sumeith products (2–8°C, do not freeze, protect from light). Seek a Pellbrook amendment that applies these protections by default for Products.

**Issue C2. Passive packaging shifts risk to Glakin.**
- **Where:** Pellbrook §2.2(a) (refrigerated vehicles or Customer's validated passive packaging, as stated on the Shipment Order); §3.3 (Customer conditions and packs passive packaging; Carrier delivers within the stated hold time); §7.2(a) (no Carrier liability where Customer failed to package per §3.3); QA §4.1 (Sumeith's 72-hour packaging standard).
- **Why it matters:** Where Glakin uses passive packaging, a failure is Glakin's responsibility to Pellbrook and to Sumeith. QA §4.4 requires Glakin to use "validated" packaging or qualified carriers.
- **Fix:** Document the qualification of each passive pack, including its hold time relative to actual transit times. Build the hold time into Shipment Order templates.

**Issue C3. Territory mismatch.**
- **Where:** Pellbrook §2.1 (delivery "in the contiguous United States"); DA §1.1(r) (Territory includes "its territories and possessions"); QA §4.4 (qualified carriers required).
- **Why it matters:** Glakin may have customers in Alaska, Hawaii or Puerto Rico who cannot be served under Pellbrook.
- **Fix:** Confirm whether Glakin has customers outside the contiguous U.S. If so, identify a second qualified cold-chain carrier.

**Issue C4. Analyzer transport conditions not addressed.**
- **Where:** QA Sch. 1 (analyzers: 5–40°C, non-condensing humidity, protect from shock and moisture); DA §7.2(a) (transport in accordance with labeling and QA); Pellbrook §2.2 (temperature commitments apply only to Temperature-Controlled Shipments); Pellbrook Sch. C (ambient rate).
- **Why it matters:** Pellbrook's ambient service has no shock or humidity commitment, and no monitoring. Glakin may be in breach of DA §7.2(a) when shipping analyzers.
- **Fix:** Add analyzer handling requirements to the carrier terms, or confirm that another arrangement covers analyzers.

**Issue C5. Carrier record retention.**
- **Where:** Pellbrook §6.1 (five years after delivery date); QA §5.3 (longer of five years after the record or two years after the last affected lot expires); QA §4.3 (Glakin's storage temperature records); QA §5.1 (traceability records).
- **Why it matters:** Carrier temperature data may be destroyed before Glakin's QA §5.3 obligation ends, especially for long-dated lots.
- **Fix:** Require the carrier to retain records for the QA §5.3 period, or have Glakin download and keep copies.

**Issue C6. Cargo insurance may be inadequate.**
- **Where:** Pellbrook §12.1(c) (cargo insurance including refrigeration breakdown, not less than $500,000 per vehicle per occurrence).
- **Why it matters:** The folder does not show typical load values. Cartridge pricing is high (SDX-RC100 at $1,560 per pack), so a full vehicle may exceed $500,000 in value.
- **Fix:** Confirm typical and maximum load values by vehicle. Increase the cargo limit if needed.

**Issue C7. Glakin's insurance and the additional-insured obligation are unverified.**
- **Where:** DA §14.1 (Glakin CGL, including products-completed operations, not less than $5M per occurrence and aggregate, for the Term plus two years); Amend. §3.3 (Distributor "shall cause Supplier to be named as an additional insured" under Glakin's CGL).
- **Why it matters:** Glakin's policy is not in the folder. Naming Sumeith as an additional insured may erode Glakin's own limits. Standard CGL policies often exclude fines and penalties and may not respond to contractual indemnity obligations like new §12.3. This is a general observation, and coverage counsel or the broker should confirm.
- **Fix:** Obtain the policy, endorsements and any certificate issued to Sumeith. Ask the broker whether the §12.3 indemnity is insurable and whether the additional-insured endorsement is in place.

**Issue C8. Sharing Sumeith confidential information with carriers.**
- **Where:** DA §13.1 (disclosure permitted only to "employees, Affiliates and advisors"); Pellbrook §11.1 (Pellbrook's own confidentiality, which covers information disclosed by Glakin).
- **Why it matters:** Carriers are not among the permitted recipients under DA §13.1. If Glakin has shared Sumeith confidential information (for example, non-public Specifications or shipping requirements) with Pellbrook, that may breach DA §13.1. Storage conditions printed on labeling are likely public, so the exposure is probably limited.

**Issue C9. Demonstration Units status.**
- **Where:** SOW §5.1 (up to 12 SDX-HA200 units on loan for the Launch Period); SOW §5.2 (title remains with Sumeith; insurance required under DA §14.1); SOW §5.3 (at the end of the Launch Period, return to Durham or purchase at 50% of the Exhibit B price; purchases count toward CY2 MPC).

**Issue C10. Exit and inventory repurchase gap.**
- **Where:** DA §16.3 (inventory repurchase only if Distributor terminates under §15.1 or §15.3); DA §16.2 (six-month sell-off); DA §16.4 (Section 12 survives indefinitely).
- **Why it matters:** Glakin has no repurchase right if it non-renews, or if Sumeith terminates (for example, for a Glakin breach under §15.1). Inventory could be stranded. Section 12 indemnities survive regardless.
- **Fix:** In any amendment, extend repurchase to any termination other than for Distributor's uncured breach, and extend the sell-off period. Limit surviving indemnities to a fixed period.

**Issue C11. Formal notice requirements.**
- **Where:** DA §18.1 (notices by hand, courier, or email with courier confirmation; effective on receipt); QA §11.5 (operational notices may be given by email between Quality Contacts); DA §5.4 (price objection by written notice); Amend. §2.1 (5-day and 10-day obligations).

- **Fix:** Adopt a notice protocol that sends formal notices under DA §18.1 whenever the DA requires written notice, and routine operational notices by email under QA §11.5.

**Issue C12. Quality operational deadlines are tight.**
- **Where:** QA §1.2(c) ("Complaint" includes oral communications); QA §6.1 (forward complaints within two business days); QA §6.2 (adverse events within 24 hours, by telephone and email); QA §6.5 (first-line support contacts that disclose a Complaint must be forwarded); QA §10.1 (Nonconformity notice within two business days); Pellbrook §4.1 (carrier excursion notice within two hours); SOW §4.3 (installation dates reported within 10 business days).

- **Fix:** Issue SOPs that link carrier excursion alerts to QA §10.1 notice, and train field technicians on oral complaint forwarding.
