## Bottom Line

1. **Closing status is unknown, and the outside date passed on June 30, 2026** (Issue 1).
2. **Side Letter ¶3(b) undoes the tax protection in SPA §8.4(b) and, read literally, caps Seller's Pre-Closing Tax indemnity** at $8.64M, not the $86.4M ceiling (Issue 2). The Side Letter controls under SPA §10.3.
3. **The Saltmarsh Pavilion landlord's consent is not a Required Consent**, and the Seller's own managers were told it is needed. Our reps and closing conditions do not cover it (Issue 3).
4. **Recovery beyond the escrow depends on a single-asset Seller that may distribute its proceeds**, and the escrow releases at 18 months while tax claims survive much longer (Issues 6–7).
5. **The escrow and the SPA conflict on scope, forum, and governing law** (Issues 8–9).

### 1. Deal status, outside date, and the unsigned escrow *(Immediate)*

- **Why it matters:** Today is November 2, 2026. The folder does not show whether Closing occurred. If it did not, either party may terminate under §9.1(b) (unless its own breach is the principal cause of the failure to close). Termination voids the SPA, except for willful-breach liability, and the Side Letter falls away with it. The Escrow Agreement in the folder is an unsigned, undated form, so we cannot confirm that the $8.64M escrow was funded.
- **Fix:** Confirm immediately (a) whether and when Closing occurred and (b) which Required Consents were delivered. If Closing has not occurred, decide with the client whether to extend the outside date by signed amendment (SPA §10.2; Side Letter ¶6(a)), close on amended terms (Issues 2–4 below), or terminate. - **What settles it:** The closing date and the closing set. If Closing occurred mid-2026, the 45-day Side Letter ¶4 schedule and the 90-day NWC statement under SPA §2.3(b) are already due or overdue. Check whether Rebond met its own §2.3(b) deadline.

### 2. Side Letter ¶3(b) reverses the tax cap carve-out *(Immediate)*

- **Where:** Side Letter ¶3(b); SPA §8.4(a)–(b); SPA §10.3 (Side Letter ranks first in precedence); Side Letter ¶6(a) (a Transaction Document, amendable by signed writing); Feb Draft §8.4(b) (same carve-out as the executed SPA).
- **Why it matters:**
  - SPA §8.4(b) keeps breaches of the Tax representation (§4.12), Fundamental Representations and Fraud outside the 10% Indemnity Cap. Liability for those items runs up to the full Purchase Price ($86.4M).
  - Side Letter ¶3(b) states that "the Indemnity Cap shall apply to all Losses under Section 8.2, including Losses arising from any breach of Section 4.12," and that only Fraud and Fundamental Representations are excluded.
  - Because the Side Letter ranks above the SPA (§10.3), Rebond's recovery for §4.12 breaches drops from $86.4M to $8.64M.
  - "All Losses under Section 8.2" also sweeps in §8.2(b) (covenants) and §8.2(c) (Pre-Closing Taxes). SPA §8.4(a) caps only §8.2(a), so the SPA as written does not cap §8.2(b) or (c). Those are the indemnities most relevant to the tour-settlement audits described in Side Letter ¶3(a) (nonresident performer withholding and admissions, amusement and sales taxes).
  - Seller will argue that ¶3(b) is limited to tour-settlement audits, because the paragraph opens "To clarify how Article VIII applies to any such examination." The operative words are not so limited.
  - The Feb Draft had the carve-out, so the reversal appears to have been introduced late. We do not know whether the deal team agreed to it or whether the CEO signed with authority.
- **Fix:** Amend the Side Letter by signed writing (SPA §10.2; Side Letter ¶6(a)). Delete ¶3(b), or restate it so that: (i) §4.12 and §8.2(c) claims remain outside the cap, as in SPA §8.4(b); (ii) §8.2(b) and (c) are expressly addressed; and (iii) the SPA controls over the Side Letter on Article VIII. Confirm internally that Rebond authorized the Side Letter as signed.
- **What settles it:** Negotiation history of ¶3(b), including who proposed it, and Rebond's internal approvals.

### 3. Saltmarsh Pavilion: the Conservancy's consent is omitted and the license is at risk *(Immediate)*

- **Where:** Managers' Consent §1.4(a)–(b) (change-of-control consent required; termination on 30 days' notice if not obtained); Managers' Consent §5 (Resolution 4: consent to be sought *after* Closing); SPA definition of "Required Consents" (items (a)–(c) only); SPA §4.4(c) and §4.5 (no third-party consent needed beyond Required Consents); SPA §4.9; SPA §6.3 (Seller's efforts covenant covers only Required Consents); SPA §7.2(d); Recital B (Saltmarsh is part of the business). The same omission appears in Feb Draft §§4.5 and 4.9.
- **Why it matters:** Seller's managers were advised that the Conservancy's prior written consent is required. The SPA says no such consent is needed. Because the Conservancy is not a Required Consent, Rebond has no closing condition, no Seller covenant to obtain it, and no pre-closing evidence requirement. Seller's own plan contemplates getting consent after Closing. If it is not obtained, the Conservancy may terminate on 30 days' notice, and Rebond would own the Company without its amphitheater venue. A claim for breach of §4.5 runs through §8.2(a), so it is subject to the $432,000 basket and the cap. Losses from loss of a venue may be hard to measure and may not meet the MAE standard in §7.2(e).
- **Fix:** If Closing has not occurred, add the Conservancy's consent to Required Consents, with evidence delivered at Closing (§3.2(d)). If Rebond closes without it, do so only by signed written waiver that reserves rights (§10.2), and add a special indemnity outside the cap and basket for losses from termination of the license or change-of-control claims. Consider a dedicated holdback. Amend §§4.4(c) and 4.5 if the Disclosure Schedule does not disclose the requirement.
- **What settles it:** The Disclosure Schedule (Issue 4); the license terms, which are not in the folder; and the Saltmarsh share of the Company's revenue.

### 4. The Disclosure Schedule is not in the file *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA Article IV lead-in ("Except as set forth in the Disclosure Schedule"); SPA §1.1 (definition: delivered "concurrently with the execution of this Agreement"); SPA §§4.8, 4.9, 4.13.
- **Why it matters:** Every Seller representation is qualified by the Disclosure Schedule. We cannot confirm whether the Conservancy requirement (Issue 3), any pending or threatened tax audit (§4.12(e)), the Material Contracts, the lease, or the benefit plans were disclosed. Disclosed matters generally cannot support a §8.2(a) claim. Side Letter ¶3(a) says the taxing authorities "periodically examine" the settlements. If any audit is ongoing, §4.12(e) may be inaccurate, unless it was disclosed.
- **Fix:** Obtain the Disclosure Schedule as delivered at signing, and compare it against Side Letter ¶3(a), Managers' Consent §1.4 and our diligence. Address any gaps in the amendment.
- **What settles it:** The Disclosure Schedule itself.

### 5. Seller controls tax audits, which conflicts with the SPA *(High)*

- **Where:** Side Letter ¶3(c) (Seller controls audits "relating solely to a pre-Closing tour settlement"; Buyer gets information, copies of "material" correspondence and participation rights; Buyer's consent is needed only for settlements that would increase taxes for periods after Closing); SPA §8.5 (Seller may not assume the defense of any Tax claim without Buyer's consent); SPA §6.6 (cooperation only); SPA §10.3 (Side Letter controls); Side Letter ¶5 (Seller's personnel available for only 90 days).
- **Why it matters:** Under §10.3, Side Letter ¶3(c) overrides the §8.5 bar. Seller would run the audits that determine the Company's withholding and sales tax liabilities and could settle them without Rebond's consent, unless the settlement affects post-closing periods. A settlement could also set filing or withholding practices that bind the Company after Closing. "Relating solely" invites dispute about mixed-period audits. The information rights are weak ("reasonably informed," "material" correspondence). The 90-day cooperation window is likely shorter than an audit.
- **Fix:** Amend ¶3(c) so that Rebond controls, or co-controls, any audit that affects the Company's future periods or practices. Require Rebond's consent to any settlement that the Company pays or that binds it going forward. Seller pays costs and provides complete copies of correspondence. Define the scope to cover mixed-period audits. Reconcile with §8.5. Extend the ¶5 cooperation period through the audit.
- **What settles it:** Whether any audit is pending now, and in which jurisdictions.

### 6. Seller credit risk beyond the escrow *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §8.6 (escrow first, then Seller directly within ten Business Days); SPA §2.1 (Seller holds the Shares as its principal asset); Managers' Consent §6 (Resolution 5: proceeds distributed under the Operating Agreement after reserves the Managing Manager "determines"); no guaranty, net-worth covenant or set-off right in the SPA.
- **Why it matters:** Seller is a single-purpose New York LLC that is selling its only apparent asset. Resolution 5 allows distribution of proceeds with no reserve for indemnity. The escrow ($8.64M) equals the cap, and any loss above it depends on Seller's solvency.
- **Fix:** Require a guaranty from Seller's members or a parent. Alternatively, require a covenant to keep minimum net assets or a retained reserve for the survival period. Consider a larger or longer escrow, and evaluate whether R&W insurance is an option (not referenced in the folder). Add an express set-off right (see Issue 12).
- **What settles it:** Seller's other assets, its members, and the distribution provisions of the Operating Agreement.

### 7. The escrow releases at 18 months, but tax recourse runs much longer *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §2.4(b) (release at 18 months less pending claims); SPA §8.1 (general reps 18 months; §4.12 and Fundamental Representations until 60 days after the statute of limitations); Escrow Agreement §1.1 ("Escrow Period"), §5.2 (release), §12.3.
- **Why it matters:** The $8.64M security expires at 18 months. Tax claims can surface after that, and Rebond would then depend on Seller's credit (Issue 6).
- **Fix:** Create a separate tax holdback that stays in place until 60 days after the statute of limitations for §4.12 and §8.2(c) claims, or extend the escrow for those claims. Alternatively, tie release to the survival periods.
- **What settles it:** The limitations periods for withholding and admissions/sales taxes in each relevant jurisdiction (tax advisor).

### 8. Gaps in survival and escrow coverage for the Pre-Closing Tax indemnity *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §8.2(c); SPA §8.1 (addresses reps and covenants only, and does not mention §8.2(c)); SPA §2.4(a) (escrow secures all Indemnifiable Losses, including §8.2(c)); Escrow Agreement §1.1 ("Indemnifiable Claim," limited to §8.2(a)), §4.2 (Claim Notices).
- **Why it matters:** The SPA never states how long the §8.2(c) indemnity lasts. Seller could argue it expires with the general reps at 18 months. The escrow's Claim Notice process reaches only §8.2(a) claims. A Pre-Closing Tax claim that is not framed as a rep breach may be unpayable from escrow without Seller's agreement. The Escrow Agreement's narrower scope conflicts with SPA §§2.4(a) and 8.6. The SPA controls under §10.3, but the Escrow Agent is not bound by the SPA (Escrow §7.1).
- **Fix:** Amend §8.1 so the §8.2(c) indemnity survives until 60 days after the statute of limitations. Conform the Escrow Agreement definition and claim process to cover §8.2(a) through (c). Add a covenant requiring Seller to sign Joint Written Instructions for §8.2(b) and (c) claims.

### 9. Forum and governing-law conflicts between the SPA and the escrow *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §10.8 (New York law); SPA §10.9 (exclusive jurisdiction in New York County); SPA §2.4(c) (escrow disputes go to §§10.8–10.9); Escrow Agreement §12.6 (Delaware law; exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery); Escrow §5.3 (Disputed Amounts released only on Joint Written Instructions or a Delaware Chancery Final Order); Escrow §7.5; Escrow §1.1 ("Final Order").
- **Why it matters:** SPA §2.4(c) sends escrow disputes to New York courts. Escrow §§5.3 and 12.6 send them to Delaware Chancery. A New York judgment would not release a Disputed Amount under Escrow §5.3. Seller could object to a claim and hold the funds until the forum question is resolved. Whether Chancery has jurisdiction over a New York-law damages claim is a question for the litigation team.
- **Fix:** Conform Escrow §§5.3, 7.5 and 12.6 to New York law and the New York County courts. Alternatively, define "Final Order" to include a final judgment of any court identified in SPA §10.9. Add a deemed-disposition clause if no action is taken within a stated period.

### 10. Seller authority: member approval and missing authorizations *(High)*

- **Where:** Managers' Consent preamble (adopted "under Section 6.7" of the Operating Agreement, which is not in the folder); Managers' Consent §1.5 and Resolutions 1–3 (§§2–4); SPA §4.2 (authorized "by all necessary action of Seller's managers and members"); SPA §1.1 ("Fundamental Representations" include §4.2); SPA §8.4(b) (uncapped to the Purchase Price for Fundamental Representations); SPA §3.2 (no secretary's certificate); Managers' Consent §4 (Resolution 3: Escrow Agreement approved "substantially in the form presented," without the change authority given in Resolutions 1 and 2).
- **Why it matters:** The consent is signed only by the three managers. SPA §4.2 says the transaction was authorized by managers *and members*, and no member approval is in the folder. If the Operating Agreement requires member approval for this sale, §4.2 is inaccurate. That is a Fundamental Representation, so Rebond would have a claim, but enforcing it against a Seller that lacked authority could be contested. Separately, Authorized Officers cannot agree changes to the Escrow Agreement under Resolution 3, so any revised escrow form may need fresh managers' approval.
- **Fix:** Obtain the Operating Agreement (including §6.7) and any member consent. Add a secretary's certificate, with the Operating Agreement extracts and the consent attached, to the §3.2 closing deliverables. Obtain a new managers' consent covering any revised Escrow Agreement.
- **What settles it:** The Operating Agreement's approval thresholds.

### 11. No adjustment for Company debt, cash or transaction expenses; payoff mechanics missing *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §2.1 (price of $86.4M, "free and clear of all liens"); SPA §2.2 (payments go only to the Escrow Agent and Seller); SPA §2.3 (working-capital adjustment only); SPA definition of "Required Consents," item (a) (lender consent "together with a customary payoff letter"); SPA §6.1(c) (revolver borrowings permitted before Closing); Article IV (no indebtedness or undisclosed-liabilities representation).
- **Why it matters:** The price is not reduced for the Company's revolver or other debt. The lender consent contemplates a payoff letter, but the funds flow sends nothing to the lender. If the revolver stays in place, Rebond inherits the debt on top of the price. Pre-closing borrowings are permitted, so the debt could grow without any price reduction. The SPA also has no cash or transaction-expense adjustment and no undisclosed-liabilities representation.
- **Fix:** Amend to deduct Closing Indebtedness and unpaid Transaction Expenses from the price, with payment to the lender under the payoff letter in the funds flow. Add a cash credit if the commercial deal supports one. Add indebtedness and undisclosed-liabilities representations. Add the payoff letter to the §3.2 deliverables.
- **What settles it:** The revolver balance and the lender's payoff letter.

### 12. Working-capital adjustment is undefined and incomplete *(High)*

- **Where:** SPA §2.3(a)–(b); SPA §1.1 (no definition of "net working capital" or accounting principles); SPA §2.3(a) ($3.2M target; Artist Deposits treated as current assets only if the engagement is not cancelled); SPA §2.2; SPA §8.6 (no set-off).
- **Why it matters:**
  - "Net working capital" is not defined, and there are no accounting principles or an illustrative calculation. The target is therefore not anchored to anything.
  - Seller prepares the estimate, and Rebond has no right to review or object before Closing. Because the estimate adjusts the price dollar-for-dollar and is paid at Closing, an inflated estimate pays Seller first.
  - §2.3(b) says "any resulting payment" without saying who pays, how much, or the true-up formula. It does not say what happens if Rebond's statement is late.
  - There is no set-off right, so Rebond cannot net an indemnity claim against an amount owed to Seller.
- **Fix:** Define net working capital with accounting principles and an illustrative calculation. Give Rebond a right to review and object to the pre-closing estimate. State the true-up formula and the payer. State the consequence of a late statement. Add a set-off right.
- **What settles it:** The Company's historical working-capital data and Rebond's accounting team's view of the target.

### 13. Artist Deposits and the Side Letter ¶4 reconciliation *(Medium)*

- **Where:** SPA §1.1 ("Artist Deposits"); SPA §2.3(a); SPA §6.1(a) ($500,000 cap per engagement, no aggregate cap); Side Letter ¶4 (schedule due 45 days after Closing; "for informational purposes"; "does not modify" §2.3(a)).
- **Why it matters:** Deposits for post-closing shows count as current assets, so they increase the price. The schedule Seller delivers after Closing is informational only, so Rebond has no contractual remedy if the balances or cancellations differ. The pre-closing covenant limits each deposit but not the total.
- **Fix:** Require a supported deposit schedule before Closing. Make the schedule binding for the working-capital true-up. Add an aggregate cap on pre-closing deposits. Add a representation on cancellations since signing.
- **What settles it:** The deposit register and the cancellation history since March 14.

### 14. Preserving claims: knowledge, fraud and waiver *(Medium)*

- **Where:** Managers' Consent §1.4 (Seller advised of the Conservancy requirement) and SPA §4.5 (no such consent needed); SPA §1.1 ("Fraud": "actual and intentional fraud ... in the making of the representations"); Side Letter ¶3(a) (Seller disclosed periodic audits); SPA Articles IV and VIII (no pro- or anti-sandbagging clause); SPA §10.2 (waivers must be in writing).
- **Why it matters:** The record cuts both ways.
  - Seller's knowledge of the Conservancy requirement, when §4.5 says no consent was needed, could support a Fraud claim. Fraud is outside the cap and the Purchase Price limit (§8.4(b)). But Fraud is narrowly defined and must be proven.
  - Rebond's own knowledge of the audit practice (Side Letter ¶3(a)) and of the Conservancy issue could be used to argue reliance or waiver against §8.2(a) claims. The SPA does not say whether Rebond's knowledge matters.
- **Fix:** Evaluate the Fraud question with the litigation team. Reserve rights in writing for any closing, waiver or amendment (§10.2). Consider adding an express clause on the effect of knowledge, as a client decision.
- **What settles it:** What Rebond knew before signing, and when it received the Managers' Consent.

### 15. Scope of the $432,000 basket *(Medium)*

- **Where:** SPA §8.4(a) (basket of $432,000, which is 0.5% of the price, applies to §8.2(a) claims, then from the first dollar); SPA §8.4(b) (carve-outs from the cap, silent on the basket).
- **Why it matters:** As written, the basket applies to every §8.2(a) claim, including Fundamental Representation and §4.12 claims that §8.4(b) exempts from the cap. Seller could argue the basket reduces recovery on tax claims. The tipping structure (first dollar once exceeded) is favorable to Rebond.
- **Fix:** Clarify that the basket does not apply to Fundamental Representations, §4.12, Fraud, or §8.2(b) and (c) claims.

### 16. Escrow Agreement administration and drafting *(Medium to Low)*

- **Schedule B (Escrow §2.2, §7.6):** Rebond's second authorized representative is "to be designated." Joint instructions require signatures from Schedule B, and call-back verification must go to someone other than the signer. Designate that representative now.
- **Schedule A (Escrow §3.1, §5.4):** Wire and account details are to be supplied by the Escrow Agent and verified by call-back. Treat this as a wire-fraud control point.
- **Encumbrance (Escrow §3.3):** The bar on encumbrance refers to §12.4, which does not permit encumbrance. This conflicts with SPA §10.4, which lets Rebond collaterally assign to its lenders after Closing. Add a carve-out.
- **Timing (Escrow §4.3 and §4.4):** The objection period runs from "receipt" in §4.3 and from "delivery" in §4.4. Conform them. Rebond must supply evidence of receipt (§4.5).
- **Tax (Escrow §§6.3, 11.1, 11.2):** Rebond is treated as owner and must report escrow income, but earnings are paid to Seller on release. Rebond also must deliver a W-9, which SPA §3.2(f) does not require. Refer to tax advisers.
- **Investment fund (Schedule A):** The fund name shares the Escrow Agent's name. Confirm whether it is an affiliate.
- **Escrow Agent indemnity (Escrow §8.1–8.2):** Rebond and Seller are jointly and severally liable to the Escrow Agent. Equal sharing between them does not protect Rebond against the agent's demand.
- **Blanks:** The date, signatures and the Escrow Agent's signatory name are blank.

### 17. Other representation, covenant and closing gaps *(Medium to Low)*

- **Interim financials:** §4.6 covers only audited statements for 2024 and 2025. §4.7 covers only the period since December 31, 2025 and has no undisclosed-liabilities or full no-changes representation.
- **Benefits and labor (§4.13):** The representation is thin and relies on the Disclosure Schedule.
- **Non-compete (§6.4):** It binds Seller and its "Affiliates," which are defined by control. Drorand's managers and members are not bound unless they control Seller. Confirm whether they have competing entertainment businesses.
- **Confidentiality (§6.5):** The covenant applies only after Closing. Confirm whether a pre-signing NDA covers the period before Closing (not in the folder).
- **Financing (§5.2 and §10.10):** Rebond has no financing condition, and Seller may seek specific performance. Confirm that Rebond's funds are available.
- **Closing deliverables (§3.2):** Add a secretary's certificate (Issue 10), the payoff letter (Issue 11), and evidence of the Conservancy consent (Issue 3).
- **Side Letter ¶¶2 and 6(c)–(d):** I found no issues.
