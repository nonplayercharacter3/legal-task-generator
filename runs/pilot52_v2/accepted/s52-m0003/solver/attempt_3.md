## 1. Pellbrook Transaction Triggers Deemed Assignment; Kevond MSA Missing from Consent List

**Documents:** MSA § 1.1 (Change of Control definition), § 16.2, § 16.3; GC Email § 2.1–2.3

**Issue.** Section 16.2 of the MSA provides that "Any Change of Control of Client is deemed an assignment" requiring Provider's prior written consent. The defined term "Change of Control" (MSA § 1.1) includes clause (b): "the acquisition by any person or group of more than 50% of the voting equity of any entity that directly or indirectly Controls Client." The GC's email (§ 2.1) confirms that Pellbrook Live Group is acquiring 100% of Diril Entertainment Holdings, Inc., the parent of Diril Live Presents LLC. This is squarely a Change of Control under clause (b), and therefore a deemed assignment under § 16.2.

The GC's consent list (§ 2.3) identifies six contracts requiring counterparty consent or notice before the December 15, 2026 closing. The Kevond MSA is not among them.

**Why it matters.** If Diril closes without Kevond's written consent, Kevond may terminate under § 16.3 within 90 days of learning of the assignment and demand (a) all fees for services performed plus (b) the Minimum Commitment Shortfall "for the remainder of the then-current term." Because the MSA auto-renews for three-year periods (see Issue 2 below), the "then-current term" could be a full three-year Renewal Term. At $4.2M per Contract Year, the theoretical maximum shortfall exposure is approximately $12.6M less fees already invoiced. Even on the reduced $2.9M figure for 2027, the exposure across a three-year renewal is substantial. This could derail or delay the Pellbrook closing.

**Recommendation.** Add the Kevond MSA to the consent list immediately. Request Kevond's written consent (or a waiver of the deemed-assignment trigger) before the December 8 target. In the consent request, Diril should emphasize that no contract is actually being assigned—Diril remains the contracting entity—and that Kevond's commercial relationship is unchanged. If Kevond resists, negotiate a side agreement confirming that the Change of Control will not be treated as a termination trigger, possibly in exchange for a commitment to maintain the minimum spend or a modest fee adjustment.

## 2. MSA Auto-Renewal for Three-Year Periods Deviates Materially from Term Sheet

**Documents:** MSA § 12.2; Term Sheet § 5.2

**Issue.** Term Sheet § 5.2 provides: "After the Initial Term, the MSA may be extended by one-year renewals only by mutual written agreement." MSA § 12.2 instead provides for automatic renewal "for successive three-year periods" unless a party gives non-renewal notice in a narrow 30-day window (not more than 120 days and not fewer than 90 days before the end of the then-current term).

**Why it matters.** This is a significant deviation from the negotiated term sheet that favors Kevond. Diril is locked into three-year commitments automatically unless it hits a precise notice window. For the Initial Term ending February 29, 2028, the non-renewal notice must be delivered between approximately October 31, 2027 and January 31, 2028. Missing that window by even one day results in a three-year renewal with the $4.2M minimum commitment continuing. Combined with the Change of Control issue (Issue 1), an auto-renewed term amplifies the Minimum Commitment Shortfall exposure if Kevond terminates.

**Recommendation.** This is now a signed term, so it cannot be unilaterally changed. However, Diril should (a) calendar the non-renewal notice window for 2027 and assign ownership to the GC's office well in advance; and (b) in any consent negotiation with Kevond regarding the Pellbrook transaction (Issue 1), seek a provision that the auto-renewal will not take effect if the Change of Control occurs, or that Kevond waives its § 16.3 termination right in connection with the renewal.

## 3. Order of Precedence Places MSA Body Above Amendment No. 1

**Documents:** MSA § 2.3; Amendment No. 1 § 3.4, § 5.1

**Issue.** MSA § 2.3 sets the order of precedence as: "MSA, then Amendment No. 1, then SOWs, then schedules and addenda, then side letters." This means that in the event of a conflict, the body of the MSA controls over Amendment No. 1. Amendment No. 1 § 3.4(a) reduces the 2027 Minimum Annual Commitment to $2,900,000 "for all purposes of the Agreement, including Section 4.4." But MSA § 4.3 states the commitment as "$4,200,000 per Contract Year" without qualification. Read strictly under the precedence rule, MSA § 4.3 could be argued to control over Amendment No. 1 § 3.4(a).

Amendment No. 1 § 5.1 attempts to cure this by stating that "each reference in the Agreement to 'this Agreement' means the Agreement as amended by this Amendment," but this is a definitional provision in the amendment itself, which ranks below the MSA in the precedence order.

**Why it matters.** Kevond is already disputing the reduction (see Issue 4). The precedence clause gives Kevond an additional textual argument: even if the Reduction Notice were timely, the MSA's $4.2M figure in § 4.3 could be read to override the amendment's $2.9M figure. This weakens Diril's position in the current dispute and creates ambiguity for future amendments.

**Recommendation.** In resolving the current dispute (Issue 4), Diril should argue that the precedence clause is designed to resolve conflicts among co-equal documents (e.g., a SOW vs. a side letter), not to nullify a duly executed amendment that expressly modifies a specific MSA section. Under New York law, a later-in-time amendment that specifically addresses a provision supersedes the general language of the base agreement. However, to eliminate the ambiguity going forward, Diril should propose a clarifying amendment (which can be bundled with the Pellbrook consent discussion) that either (a) reorders the precedence to place amendments above the MSA body, or (b) adds a sentence to § 2.3 stating that an amendment expressly modifying a numbered section controls over that section.

## 4. Reduction Notice Delivered Nine Days Late; Kevond Rejects as Untimely

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1 § 3.1, § 3.5; GC Email § 3.1–3.5

**Issue.** Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 requires delivery of a Reduction Notice "within 60 days after the Amendment Effective Date." The Amendment Effective Date is August 1, 2026. Sixty days later is September 30, 2026. The GC's email (§ 3.2) states the notice was delivered on October 9, 2026—nine days past the deadline. Kevond rejected the notice on October 20 as untimely (§ 3.3).

**Why it matters.** On the face of the amendment, Kevond appears correct. The deadline is unambiguous, and October 9 is outside it. If Kevond's position prevails, the 2027 commitment remains $4.2M. The Ember Season tour SOW fees are approximately $3.379M (SOW § 5.1), leaving a gap of roughly $821,000 that Diril would need to fill with other 2027 engagements or pay as a true-up under MSA § 4.4.

However, Diril has equitable arguments:
- Kevond discussed 2027 slate planning with Diril's CFO's team in late September (GC Email § 3.4) without raising the timing issue.
- Kevond continued to engage on the reduction after the deadline and did not object until 11 days after receiving the notice.
- Under New York law, a party may be estopped from asserting a contractual deadline where its conduct led the other party to reasonably believe the deadline would not be enforced. The standard is high, but the facts here (Kevond's silence during the planning discussions, followed by a delayed objection) are not nothing.

**Recommendation.** Diril's leverage is moderate. The deadline was missed, but Kevond's conduct undercuts a strict enforcement. I would recommend:
1. Lead with the estoppel/waiver argument in a written response to Kevond (if not already done), citing the September planning discussions and the 11-day delay in objecting.
2. Offer a commercial compromise: accept the $4.2M commitment but negotiate a one-time true-up credit or a reduction in 2028 fees, in exchange for Kevond's consent to the Pellbrook Change of Control (Issue 1). This bundles the two disputes and gives Kevond a face-saving outcome.
3. If Kevond holds firm, consider whether the $821K gap can be filled by pulling forward a 2028 festival engagement (which would count toward 2027 if the first show date falls in the 2027 Contract Year under MSA § 4.3) or by negotiating a supplemental SOW.
4. Avoid litigating or formally escalating under § 17.3 at this stage given the ongoing tour relationship and the Pellbrook timing.

## 5. Local Crew Definition Conflict Creates Gaps in Indemnity, Safety, and Certification Coverage

**Documents:** MSA § 1.1 ("Provider Personnel" definition); SOW § 3.2; Quality Agreement § 3.3; MSA § 10.1

**Issue.** MSA § 1.1 defines "Provider Personnel" as "individuals **employed by** Provider who perform Services." SOW § 3.2 states that "All rigging, flying and pyrotechnic work on the Tour will be performed by Provider Personnel, **including the Local Crew engaged by Provider as independent contractors**." The SOW thus purports to classify independent contractors as "Provider Personnel," but under MSA § 2.3 the MSA definition controls over the SOW.

Additionally, Quality Agreement § 3.3 provides: "Provider shall not assign local labor to performer flying or to pyrotechnic firing." SOW § 3.2 appears to authorize Local Crew to perform "flying and pyrotechnic work." Under the precedence order, the SOW (third) ranks above the Quality Agreement (fourth, as an addendum), so the SOW's authorization would technically override the Quality Agreement's prohibition.

**Why it matters.**
- **Indemnity gap:** MSA § 10.1 indemnifies Client against claims "caused by the negligence or willful misconduct of Provider or Provider Personnel." If Local Crew are not "Provider Personnel" under the MSA definition, their negligence may fall outside the express indemnity. While Kevond could be liable as their principal under general tort law, the contractual indemnity (with its defense-and-control mechanism in § 10.4) may not extend to them.
- **Safety/certification gap:** Quality Agreement § 3.1 sets certification requirements for "Provider Personnel." If Local Crew are excluded, the contractual mechanism for verifying their rigging and pyrotechnic certifications is weakened.
- **Prohibited work:** The SOW's authorization of Local Crew for performer flying and pyrotechnic firing directly contradicts the safety standard in the Quality Agreement. If an incident occurs, Diril could face regulatory scrutiny for permitting local labor to perform work the safety agreement prohibits.

**Recommendation.** Before the first rehearsal day (April 5, 2027), Diril should require a written clarification (via change order under MSA § 3.4 or SOW § 9.2) that:
1. Local Crew performing rigging, flying, or pyrotechnic work are expressly included within the scope of the § 10.1 indemnity, either by amending the "Provider Personnel" definition or by adding a specific indemnity for Local Crew in the SOW.
2. Local Crew are subject to the certification requirements in Quality Agreement § 3.1, with records delivered under SOW § 3.6.
3. The apparent conflict between SOW § 3.2 and Quality Agreement § 3.3 is resolved: if Local Crew are to perform any flying or pyrotechnic tasks, the Quality Agreement should be amended to permit it subject to the certification and supervision requirements; if they are not to perform those tasks, SOW § 3.2 should be corrected to exclude them.

**Issue.** Side Letter § 2.4 fixes rates "through February 28, 2026" (which has now passed), after which Provider may increase rates once per Contract Year by up to 4% with 90 days' notice. Side Letter § 1.1 limits the letter's application to festival engagements "where the first show date of that Engagement falls within the Initial Term" (i.e., on or before February 29, 2028).

2. If the MSA auto-renews (Issue 2), any festival engagement with a first show date on or after March 1, 2028 falls outside the side letter entirely. Diril would have no contractual rate protection for festival video packages in a Renewal Term and would be at Kevond's mercy on pricing.

(b) In connection with the Pellbrook consent negotiation or a future renewal discussion, seek to extend the side letter's rate protections into any Renewal Term, or at minimum negotiate a new rate schedule before the Initial Term expires.

## 7. True-Up Reduction Does Not Cover Force Majeure Affecting Provider

**Documents:** MSA § 4.4

**Issue.** MSA § 4.4 provides that the Minimum Annual Commitment "is reduced by the Fees for any Engagement that Provider fails to perform for reasons other than Client's breach or a Force Majeure Event affecting Client." The exception for "a Force Majeure Event affecting Client" means that if a force majeure event hits the venue or the client's operations (e.g., a hurricane closes a venue), and Provider consequently cannot perform, the commitment is NOT reduced. The logic appears to be that if the FM affects Client, Client is the party that cannot proceed, so the commitment stands.

**Why it matters.** For a concert promoter, weather and venue-related force majeure events are a realistic risk, particularly for outdoor festival dates. If a festival is cancelled due to a hurricane (an FM event "affecting Client" as the venue operator/promoter), Diril still owes the minimum commitment for those fees. Combined with MSA § 13.3 (cancellation does not reduce the minimum commitment), Diril bears the full commitment risk for weather-related cancellations.

**Recommendation.** This is a signed term and difficult to change unilaterally. However, Diril should: (a) ensure its festival SOWs include detailed force majeure cost-allocation provisions (as the side letter does for weather in § 5.1) to limit direct cancellation charges; and (b) in any future amendment or renewal negotiation, seek to broaden the § 4.4 reduction to cover FM events regardless of which party they affect, or at minimum to cover FM events that prevent the show from occurring (as opposed to FM events that merely delay it).

## 8. Equipment Insurance Has No Minimum Dollar Amount

**Documents:** MSA § 11.1(e)

**Issue.** Section 11.1(e) requires "inland marine or equipment floater coverage for the full replacement value of Provider equipment" but specifies no minimum dollar threshold, unlike the other coverage types in § 11.1(a)–(d), which have explicit minimums ($2M CGL, $10M umbrella, $1M auto, etc.).

**Why it matters.** The Ember Season tour involves a performer flying system, the "ember canopy" scenic element, a full lighting rig, pyrotechnic firing systems, and nine 53-foot trucks of equipment (SOW §§ 3.1, 4.1, 5.5). If Kevond's equipment floater is underinsured or if "full replacement value" is disputed after a loss, Diril could face gaps in coverage for equipment it is relying on Kevond to supply and maintain under MSA § 3.2.

**Recommendation.** Request Kevond's current equipment floater policy limits as part of the certificates of insurance due before the first rehearsal day (SOW § 9.1). If the coverage appears thin relative to the equipment list value, negotiate a minimum dollar amount via a change order or at the next annual review.

## 9. Cancellation of an Engagement Does Not Reduce the Minimum Annual Commitment

**Documents:** MSA § 13.3

**Issue.** Section 13.3 states: "Cancellation of an Engagement does not reduce the Minimum Annual Commitment." This means if Diril cancels a tour or festival (for any reason short of Provider's breach or FM affecting Provider, which trigger the § 4.4 reduction), the fees for the cancelled engagement still count as a gap against the minimum.

**Why it matters.** In a year where Diril's slate is concentrated in one or two large engagements (as with the 2027 Contract Year, where the Ember Season tour represents the bulk of expected fees), cancellation of that engagement creates a large true-up exposure. If the tour is cancelled for artist reasons (e.g., the artist pulls out), Diril would owe cancellation charges under SOW § 6.1 AND still face the minimum commitment true-up for the unmet balance.

**Recommendation.** This is a standard promoter risk but worth flagging to the client. Diril should ensure its artist agreements include robust cancellation and force majeure provisions that shift economic risk away from Diril. In any future MSA amendment, consider negotiating a provision that cancellation due to artist breach or artist-related force majeure reduces the minimum commitment.

## 10. Service Level Chronic Failure Threshold May Be Difficult to Trigger on a 38-Date Tour

**Documents:** Service Level Schedule § 7.2; SOW § 2.2

**Issue.** Section 7.2 provides that failure of the show-readiness or equipment availability service level "on five or more show dates of the same Engagement in any period of 60 consecutive days" constitutes a material breach. The Ember Season tour has 38 show dates over approximately 12 weeks (April 16 through late June/early July 2027). A 60-consecutive-day window captures roughly two-thirds of the tour. Diril would need to document service level failures on five show dates within any rolling 60-day period to establish material breach.

**Why it matters.** While five failures in 60 days is a reasonable threshold, the practical challenge is documentation. Under Service Level Schedule § 5.1, the daily record must be "countersigned or confirmed by email by Client's production manager within two business days after the Service Date. If Client's production manager does not respond within that period, the record is treated as confirmed." If Diril's production manager fails to respond (or responds confirming a record that does not capture a failure), the evidence trail weakens. On a 38-date tour with a small production team, this is a realistic operational risk.

**Recommendation.** Brief Diril's tour production team now on the documentation requirements. Ensure the production manager (or a delegate) is assigned the specific task of reviewing and confirming service level records within two business days of each show date. Consider whether a secondary reviewer should be designated for backup. The September meeting notes (§ 9.2) confirm that Diril will "confirm records by email within two business days"—make sure this is operationalized, not just agreed in principle.

## 11. Data Processing Addendum Retention Period May Conflict with Incident Preservation Obligations

**Documents:** Data Processing Addendum § 8.1; Quality Agreement § 8.4

**Issue.** DPA § 8.1 requires Provider to "delete Credential Data for each Engagement within 90 days after that Engagement's final load-out," but carves out certification records kept under Quality Agreement § 3.2. Quality Agreement § 8.4 requires Provider to "preserve equipment, logs, photographs and other records relating to any incident reportable under Section 8.1 until Client confirms in writing that they may be released."

If a reportable incident occurs late in the tour (e.g., a near-miss on show date 35), and the final load-out is July 3, 2027, the 90-day deletion deadline is approximately October 1, 2027. If Client has not yet confirmed release of incident records (because an investigation is ongoing), Provider faces a conflict: the DPA says delete by day 90; the Quality Agreement says preserve until Client confirms release.

**Why it matters.** DPA § 8.3 provides that Provider "may keep Credential Data that the law requires it to keep, or that is subject to a preservation obligation under Section 8.4 of the production quality and safety agreement." This carve-out should resolve the conflict, but only if the incident records qualify as "Credential Data." If the incident involves, say, a rigging failure and the relevant records are engineering logs rather than credential data, the DPA carve-out may not apply, and the Quality Agreement preservation obligation stands alone. The practical risk is low but the drafting is imprecise.

**Recommendation.** No immediate action required, but flag to Diril's production team that if a reportable incident occurs on the tour, they should promptly issue a written preservation notice to Kevond referencing both Quality Agreement § 8.4 and DPA § 8.3, to eliminate any ambiguity about the deletion deadline.
