## 1. Change of Control: Pellbrook Transaction Triggers Deemed Assignment Under MSA § 16.2

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 16.2, § 16.3
- GC Email § 2.1–2.3

**Issue.** The MSA defines "Change of Control" of Client to include "the acquisition by any person or group of more than 50% of the voting equity of any entity that directly or indirectly Controls Client" (MSA § 1.1). Section 16.2 provides that "Any Change of Control of Client is deemed an assignment" requiring Provider's prior written consent. Section 16.3 states that if Client assigns without consent, Provider may terminate and Client must pay, within 30 days, the Minimum Commitment Shortfall "calculated as of the termination date for the remainder of the then-current term."

The GC Email confirms that Pellbrook Live Group will acquire 100% of Diril Entertainment Holdings, Inc. (Diril's parent), with closing scheduled for December 15, 2026. This squarely triggers the Change of Control definition. The GC Email's consent list (§ 2.3) identifies six contracts requiring consent or notice but does **not** include the Kevond MSA.

**Why it matters.** If Diril closes the Pellbrook transaction without Kevond's consent, Kevond can terminate the MSA and accelerate the entire Minimum Commitment Shortfall for the remainder of the term. Because the MSA auto-renews for three-year periods (see Issue 4 below), the "then-current term" could extend to February 2031. The potential exposure is approximately $4.2 million per Contract Year for each remaining year, less Fees already invoiced — a figure that could exceed $10 million depending on timing. This is carved out from the liability cap (MSA § 14.3(d)).

**Recommendation.** Add the Kevond MSA to the Pellbrook consent list immediately. Request Kevond's written consent to the Change of Control before the December 8, 2026 target. Given the commercial relationship (Ember Season is Kevond's largest 2027 engagement at ~$3.4 million), a consent request framed as routine should be manageable. If Kevond resists, negotiate a side letter confirming that the Pellbrook transaction will not be treated as a breach and that the Minimum Commitment Shortfall will not be accelerated. Do not close without resolving this.

## 2. Reduction Notice Delivered Nine Days Late — Active $1.3 Million Dispute

**Documents and sections:**
- Amendment No. 1 §§ 3.1, 3.2, 3.5
- GC Email §§ 3.1–3.5

**Issue.** Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 requires a Reduction Notice to be delivered "within 60 days after the Amendment Effective Date." The Amendment Effective Date is August 1, 2026. Sixty days after August 1 is September 30, 2026. Diril delivered its Reduction Notice on October 9, 2026 — nine days late. Kevond rejected the notice on October 20, 2026, asserting that the 2027 commitment remains $4,200,000 under § 3.5.

**Why it matters.** The difference is $1,300,000 in 2027 true-up exposure. The Ember Season SOW Fees (~$3,379,000) count toward the 2027 Contract Year (SOW § 1.3). Against a $4.2 million commitment, Diril has a ~$821,000 shortfall before any other 2027 engagements. Against the reduced $2.9 million commitment, the SOW Fees alone exceed the commitment and no true-up is owed.

**Diril's arguments.** The GC Email notes that Kevond continued to engage on the reduction through late September (discussing 2027 slate planning with the CFO's team), did not raise the timing issue until after receiving the October 9 notice, and has continued working on the tour without interruption. Under New York law, a party may waive a contractual condition through conduct that leads the other party reasonably to believe the condition will not be enforced. Kevond's prolonged silence and continued engagement support a waiver or estoppel argument. Additionally, the notice was delivered by both courier and email to Kevond's President, satisfying MSA § 18.2.

**Weakness.** The 60-day deadline is unambiguous. Kevond can argue it reserved its rights by not discussing the merits of the reduction (as opposed to general 2027 planning). The fact that production meetings continued does not necessarily constitute an affirmation of the reduction.

**Recommendation.** Pursue a commercial resolution. Diril's leverage is significant: (a) the Ember Season tour is Kevond's largest 2027 booking; (b) Kevond's own conduct (continued engagement, no timely objection) undermines its position; (c) escalating under MSA § 17.3 would strain the relationship during active pre-production. Propose that Kevond accept the reduction in exchange for a concession on another point (e.g., confirmation that the 2027 commitment reduction does not affect the service level schedule or the SOW fees). If Kevond refuses, prepare a formal dispute notice under § 17.3 and evaluate the waiver/estoppel claim for litigation, but recognize the outcome is uncertain given the clear contractual language.

## 3. SOW No. 7 Contradicts the Quality Agreement on Local Crew Performing High-Risk Tasks

**Documents and sections:**
- SOW No. 7 § 3.2
- Quality Agreement § 3.3
- MSA § 2.3 (Order of Precedence)

**Issue.** SOW § 3.2 states: "All rigging, flying and pyrotechnic work on the Tour will be performed by Provider Personnel, including the Local Crew engaged by Provider as independent contractors." Quality Agreement § 3.3 provides: "Provider shall not assign local labor to performer flying or to pyrotechnic firing."

These provisions directly conflict. Under MSA § 2.3, SOWs rank above schedules and addenda in the order of precedence. The Quality Agreement is an addendum under MSA § 2.2. Therefore, the SOW language technically controls, meaning Local Crew (independent contractors at each Venue) could be assigned to performer flying and pyrotechnic firing.

**Why it matters.** Performer flying and pyrotechnic firing are the highest-risk tasks on the tour. The Quality Agreement's prohibition exists to ensure that only the certified, supervised touring crew performs these tasks. If the SOW overrides that prohibition, Diril faces: (a) increased safety risk at 38 Venues; (b) potential regulatory exposure if a local contractor without proper certification performs pyrotechnic firing; (c) weakened indemnity position — if an incident occurs and the Local Crew member was not properly certified, Kevond may argue the SOW authorized the assignment, complicating a negligence claim under MSA § 10.1.

**Recommendation.** Execute a change order under MSA § 3.4 (or SOW § 9.2) amending SOW § 3.2 to state that Local Crew may perform up-rigging, down-rigging and general rigging support but shall not perform performer flying or pyrotechnic firing, consistent with Quality Agreement § 3.3. This should be done before the first rehearsal day (April 5, 2027) and before Venue labor calls are issued. Flag this to the Production Manager for inclusion in the next pre-production meeting agenda.

## 4. MSA Auto-Renewal for Three-Year Periods Deviates from Term Sheet

**Documents and sections:**
- Term Sheet § 5.2
- MSA § 12.2

**Issue.** Term Sheet § 5.2 provided: "After the Initial Term, the MSA may be extended by one-year renewals only by mutual written agreement." MSA § 12.2 instead provides for automatic renewal "for successive three-year periods" unless a Party gives non-renewal notice in a 90-to-120-day window before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters.** The MSA is materially more restrictive than what the term sheet contemplated. Diril is locked into three-year cycles with a $4.2 million annual commitment (MSA § 12.2: "The terms of this Agreement, including the Minimum Annual Commitment under Section 4.3, apply to each Contract Year of each Renewal Term"). The non-renewal window for the Initial Term (ending February 29, 2028) is approximately October 31 to November 30, 2027 — a 31-day window. Missing it means another three years and roughly $12.6 million in additional commitments.

**Recommendation.** Calendar the non-renewal notice window immediately (October 31 – November 30, 2027). Assign ownership to the GC's office with a reminder at 150 days before the deadline (i.e., by October 1, 2027). In the interim, if the Pellbrook transaction closes and Diril's strategic direction changes under new ownership, the auto-renewal could lock in a vendor relationship the new parent does not want. Consider negotiating an amendment that reverts to the term sheet's mutual-agreement renewal model, using the Pellbrook consent negotiation (Issue 1) as the natural opportunity.

## 5. Service Level Exclusion for Client-Supplied Equipment Creates a Coverage Gap

**Documents and sections:**
- Service Level Schedule § 3.4(b)
- SOW No. 7 § 4.3

**Issue.** SLS § 3.4(b) excludes service credits for "a failure of Client-supplied items, including Client-supplied video surfaces, media servers, audio systems and soft goods." SOW § 4.3 confirms that for the Ember Season tour, "Client or the Artist will supply the video surfaces and media servers, the audio system, the scenic soft goods and all pyrotechnic product."

**Why it matters.** A substantial portion of the show's technical content (video, audio, pyro) is Client/Artist-supplied. If a media server fails mid-show and a cue sequence is omitted, or if the audio system causes a 15-minute delay to show start, no service credit is available under the SLS because the failure is attributed to Client-supplied equipment. Yet from the audience's and artist's perspective, the show was compromised. Diril bears the full commercial consequence (artist penalties, reputation, potential ticket refunds) with no offset from Kevond.

The issue is compounded by MSA § 14.1, which excludes consequential damages (including lost profits). If a Client-supplied equipment failure cascades into a Kevond-supplied system failure (e.g., a video signal loss causes the lighting console to drop cues), Kevond could argue the root cause was the Client-supplied item and deny the service credit.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a clarification in the SLS or a SOW change order stating that if a Client-supplied equipment failure directly causes a failure of a Kevond-supplied system (e.g., loss of video signal causing lighting cue omission), the service credit for the Kevond system failure still applies. Alternatively, confirm that Kevond's crew is responsible for monitoring and protecting its systems from upstream signal losses (e.g., automatic fallback to a default state rather than dropping cues).

## 6. 16-Hour Work Limit in Quality Agreement Conflicts with SOW Tour Schedule

**Documents and sections:**
- Quality Agreement § 3.4
- SOW No. 7 § 2.4

**Issue.** Quality Agreement § 3.4 provides: "Provider shall schedule work so that no member of the touring crew works more than 16 hours in any 24-hour period, except in an emergency affecting safety." SOW § 2.4 sets the daily schedule with rigging call at 7:00 a.m. and load-out complete "4 hours after show end." A typical arena show ends between 10:00 p.m. and 11:00 p.m., putting load-out completion at 2:00 a.m. to 3:00 a.m. Key personnel (Head Rigger, Production Lead, Lighting Director) are present for the full day. A 7:00 a.m. to 3:00 a.m. shift is 20 hours.

**Why it matters.** If Kevond complies with the 16-hour limit, it cannot staff the SOW schedule with the same individuals for both rigging call and load-out completion. This means either: (a) the schedule in SOW § 2.4 is unrealistic and show-ready or load-out times will be missed (triggering service level failures for which Diril would be the beneficiary, but at the cost of operational disruption); or (b) Kevond will staff in violation of the Quality Agreement, creating a compliance issue that could be raised in a post-incident investigation or regulatory inquiry.

Under the MSA precedence order, the SOW controls over the Quality Agreement, so the 16-hour limit yields to the SOW schedule. But the Quality Agreement is also a safety document that Kevond's insurers and regulators may reference.

**Recommendation.** Confirm with Kevond's Production Lead (Darnell Okafor) at the next pre-production meeting how the crew will be scheduled to comply with both the SOW times and the 16-hour limit. If the answer is that different crew members handle rigging call versus load-out, document that in a change order or production schedule addendum. If the answer is that key personnel will work longer shifts, consider amending Quality Agreement § 3.4 to provide a touring-specific exception (e.g., 18 hours with mandatory rest periods) so that the contract reflects operational reality and does not create a technical non-compliance that could be exploited after an incident.

## 7. Side Letter Rate Hold Has Expired; 2027 Festival Rates May Increase

**Documents and sections:**
- Side Letter § 2.4
- MSA § 2.3 (Order of Precedence)

**Issue.** Side Letter § 2.4 fixes rates "through February 28, 2026." After that date, Provider may increase rates once per Contract Year with 90 days' written notice, capped at 4% over the prior Contract Year's rates. The 2027 Contract Year begins March 1, 2027, so Kevond could have given notice as early as approximately December 1, 2026.

Additionally, the side letter sits at the bottom of the MSA's precedence order (§ 2.3). If any SOW for a 2027 festival engagement states different LED or video rates, the SOW controls over the side letter.

**Why it matters.** If Diril has festival engagements in the 2027 Contract Year, equipment rates for LED walls, video processing and IMAG could be up to 4% higher than the rates in the side letter. For a large festival with, say, 5,000 square feet of fine-pitch LED wall over three show days, the base rate is $38.00/sq ft/show day × 5,000 × 3 = $570,000 (before the multi-day discount). A 4% increase adds ~$22,800 per festival. Across multiple festivals, this is material.

**Recommendation.** Confirm with Kevond whether it has issued or plans to issue a rate increase notice for the 2027 Contract Year. If Diril has festival SOWs in draft or planned, ensure they expressly incorporate the side letter rates (or negotiate rates directly in the SOW) to lock in pricing before any increase takes effect. The side letter § 2.4 provides that "an increase does not apply to an SOW signed before the increase takes effect" — so signing festival SOWs before the effective date of any increase preserves the lower rates.

## 8. Data Processing Addendum: Ambiguous Retention Period for Certification Records

**Documents and sections:**
- DPA § 8.1
- Quality Agreement § 3.2
- DPA Schedule 1 (categories of Credential Data)

**Issue.** DPA § 8.1 requires Provider to delete Credential Data within 90 days after an Engagement's final load-out, but carves out "certification records for its own employees as long as Section 3.2 of the production quality and safety agreement between the Parties requires." Quality Agreement § 3.2 states that Provider "shall keep certification and training records for Provider Personnel" but does not specify a retention period.

Credential Data (per DPA Schedule 1) includes "certification type, number and expiry date" for crew members. If the Quality Agreement imposes no time limit, the DPA carve-out effectively permits indefinite retention of personal data (certification numbers are personal identifiers) beyond the 90-day deletion requirement.

**Why it matters.** Under CCPA/CPRA and Canadian PIPEDA, data controllers must limit retention to what is necessary for the stated purpose. Indefinite retention of crew certification data creates regulatory exposure for Diril as the "business" or "controller" (DPA § 2.1). If a data subject (former crew member) requests deletion under their privacy rights, Diril may be unable to confirm that Kevond has deleted the data because the contractual retention period is undefined.

**Recommendation.** Amend DPA § 8.1 (or Quality Agreement § 3.2) to specify a maximum retention period for certification records — for example, "for the duration of the Term plus three years, or until the certification expires, whichever is earlier." This aligns with MSA § 4.7's three-year record retention for invoices and provides a clear endpoint for data subject deletion requests.

## 9. Chronic Service Level Failure: 30-Day Cure Period Is Operationally Useless During a Tour

**Documents and sections:**
- Service Level Schedule § 7.2
- MSA § 13.1

**Issue.** SLS § 7.2 provides that failing show-readiness or equipment availability on five or more show dates in 60 consecutive days constitutes a material breach under MSA § 13.1. MSA § 13.1 requires the non-breaching party to give written notice and allow a 30-day cure period before terminating.

The Ember Season tour runs April 16 through July 2, 2027 (38 show dates over ~11 weeks). If Kevond misses show-readiness on five dates in the first 60 days (by approximately June 14), Diril can declare a material breach. But Kevond then has 30 days to cure — until approximately July 14, after the tour has ended.

**Why it matters.** The termination right is theoretical. By the time the cure period expires, the tour is over and the harm (missed shows, artist penalties, reputation damage) is done. Diril cannot mid-tour replace Kevond's production team for the remaining dates based on this provision. The only practical remedy during the tour is the service credit (capped at 15% of monthly fees under SLS § 4.1) and the § 9.3 re-performance credit.

**Recommendation.** Negotiate a SOW-specific provision (or a change order) that: (a) shortens the cure period for service level material breaches during an active tour to 5 business days; and/or (b) grants Diril the right to engage a replacement provider for remaining show dates if the chronic failure threshold is met, with Kevond responsible for the cost differential up to the liability cap. Without this, Diril's protection during the tour is limited to service credits, which at 15% of monthly fees on a $71,500/show date tour may not approach the actual commercial loss of a compromised show.

## 10. Significant Prepayment Exposure Before Services Begin

**Documents and sections:**
- SOW No. 7 § 5.1, § 5.2(a)–(b)

**Issue.** SOW § 5.2 requires: (a) a 20% deposit on signature (July 13, 2026) and (b) a second 20% deposit invoiced on March 1, 2027. The fixed Fees (excluding Local Crew) total $2,967,000 ($186,000 + $2,717,000 + $64,000). Each deposit is $593,400. Total prepayment before the first rehearsal day (April 5, 2027) is $1,186,800.

**Why it matters.** If the Pellbrook transaction encounters difficulties (e.g., Kevond invokes § 16.3 and terminates in December 2026 or January 2027), Diril has already paid over $1.1 million for services not yet rendered. While MSA § 13.4 requires payment only for "Services performed through the effective date," the deposits are arguably not "Services performed" — they are prepayments. The MSA does not contain an express refund provision for deposits if the Agreement is terminated before the Engagement begins.

Additionally, if Diril exercises its cancellation right under MSA § 13.3 more than 120 days before the first show date, SOW § 6.1 provides that "Deposits paid to date are retained; no further charge." So even a clean cancellation by Diril results in forfeiture of $1,186,800.

**Recommendation.** Confirm that the first deposit has been paid (the GC Email § 8.1 in the meeting notes indicates it has). For the second deposit due March 1, 2027, consider whether Diril can negotiate a later payment date (e.g., 14 days before the first rehearsal) to reduce the window of unearned prepayment. If the Pellbrook transaction closes smoothly and Kevond consents to the Change of Control, this becomes less urgent. But if the § 16.3 risk materializes, Diril should assert that deposits are refundable upon termination before services commence, under general principles of restitution/unjust enrichment, even though the MSA is silent.

## 11. Liability Cap and Consequential Damages Exclusion Limit Recovery for Show Failures

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA §§ 14.1, 14.2, 14.3
- SLS §§ 3.1, 4.1

**Issue.** MSA § 14.1 excludes all indirect, incidental, special, consequential and punitive damages, including lost profits. MSA § 14.2 caps each Party's liability at the greater of Fees paid/payable in the Contract Year or $4,200,000. Service credits are capped at 15% of Monthly Production Fees (SLS § 4.1).

**Why it matters.** If a Kevond equipment failure causes a show to be cancelled or significantly compromised, Diril's actual losses could include: ticket refund obligations, artist contract penalties, venue fees, marketing write-offs, and reputational harm. None of these are recoverable under § 14.1. Diril's maximum recovery is: (a) service credits (15% of that month's production fees); (b) the § 9.3 credit for non-conforming services; and (c) the liability cap amount for direct damages. For a single $71,500 show date, the service credit for a show stop is 10% = $7,150. If the actual loss from a cancelled show is $500,000+ (venue, artist, tickets), Diril absorbs the difference.

**Recommendation.** This is a structural risk that was presumably priced into the Fees. However, Diril should: (a) ensure its artist contracts and venue agreements allocate show-cancellation risk in a way that does not flow through to Diril exclusively; (b) maintain event cancellation insurance that covers production failures; and (c) in any future SOW negotiation, consider whether a super-cap or carve-out is appropriate for total show failures (as distinct from partial service level misses).

## 12. MSA § 4.4 True-Up Reduction Is Ambiguous for Partial Non-Performance

**Documents and sections:**
- MSA § 4.4
- SOW No. 7 § 1.1 (Tour defined as a single Engagement)

**Issue.** MSA § 4.4 reduces the Minimum Annual Commitment by "the Fees for any Engagement that Provider fails to perform for reasons other than Client's breach or a Force Majeure Event affecting Client." The Ember Season Tour is a single Engagement under SOW § 1.1 with Fees of ~$3,379,000.

If Kevond performs 30 of 38 show dates and then abandons the tour (for reasons other than Diril's breach or force majeure), has Kevond "failed to perform" the Engagement? The Engagement is the entire Tour. Kevond partially performed it. The MSA does not address partial performance.

**Why it matters.** If the reduction applies only to a total failure to perform, Diril still owes the full commitment even if Kevond walks after 30 shows. If the reduction applies pro rata, Diril's commitment is reduced by the fees for the 8 unperformed shows (~$572,000). The difference matters for the 2027 true-up calculation.

**Recommendation.** Seek clarification from Kevond (ideally in writing, perhaps as part of the Pellbrook consent negotiation) that "fails to perform" in § 4.4 includes partial non-performance, with the reduction calculated on a per-show-date basis. Alternatively, add a sentence to the SOW or a change order stating that if Provider fails to perform any show date of the Tour for reasons other than Client's breach or Force Majeure, the Minimum Annual Commitment is reduced by the per-show fee for each unperformed date.

## 13. DPA § 2.4: Diril Must Approve Privacy Notice Form — Operational Bottleneck Risk

**Documents and sections:**
- DPA § 2.4
- DPA Schedule 1 (Data Subjects include local crew at 38 Venues)

**Issue.** DPA § 2.4 makes Provider responsible for giving notice at collection to crew members, but "That notice must be in a form Client has approved." The Data Subjects include not just the 22-person touring crew but also "local stagehands, riggers and technicians arranged under Section 3.5 of the Agreement" at each of 38 Venues.

**Why it matters.** If Diril has not pre-approved a notice form, Kevond cannot lawfully collect Credential Data from local crew members. This could delay credentialing at Venues, causing load-in delays and potentially triggering service level failures (for which Diril would be the beneficiary, but only after the operational disruption has occurred). With 38 Venues across the U.S. and Canada, the risk of a bottleneck is real.

**Recommendation.** Confirm that Diril has approved a privacy notice form for Kevond to use with crew members. If not, approve one immediately (a standard CCPA/PIPEDA-compliant notice at collection should suffice). Document the approval in writing so Kevond has a clear record. For Canadian show dates, ensure the notice also satisfies PIPEDA requirements.

## 14. SOW § 3.5: Venue Labor Call Deadline Creates Scheduling Risk

**Documents and sections:**
- SOW No. 7 § 3.5
- Meeting Notes § 7.2

**Issue.** SOW § 3.5 requires the Production Manager to issue a "Venue labor call" at least 21 days before each show date. The Meeting Notes confirm Diril will meet this deadline. However, the SOW also states that "Where a Venue's collective bargaining agreement requires use of house labor for any task, Provider shall use house labor for that task and coordinate it with the Venue."

**Why it matters.** If a Venue's CBA requires house labor for a task that Kevond's Local Crew was scheduled to perform, Kevond must use house labor instead. This could affect: (a) the Local Crew fee estimate ($412,000 in SOW § 5.1) — house labor rates may differ; (b) the service level schedule — if house labor is less experienced, show-readiness could be affected; and (c) the Quality Agreement's certification requirements — house labor may not hold the certifications Kevond's Local Crew would have.

The SOW does not specify who bears the cost differential if house labor rates exceed the Local Crew estimate, or whether a change order is required.

**Recommendation.** Confirm with Kevond how it will handle the house labor requirement at Venues with CBAs. If house labor rates differ from the Local Crew estimate, determine whether the SOW's "billed at actual" language (SOW § 5.3) covers house labor or only Kevond-engaged Local Crew. If there is ambiguity, resolve it in a change order before the first Venue labor call is issued.
