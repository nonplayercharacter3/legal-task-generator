## 1. Pellbrook Transaction Triggers Deemed Assignment Under the MSA — Consent Required Before Closing

**Documents:** MSA §§ 1.1, 16.2, 16.3; GC Email § 2

**Issue.** Section 1.1 of the MSA defines "Change of Control" for Client to include "the acquisition by any person or group of more than 50% of the voting equity of any entity that directly or indirectly Controls Client." Section 16.2 provides that "[a]ny Change of Control of Client is deemed an assignment for purposes of this Section 16.2," and Client may not assign (or be deemed to assign) "without Provider's prior written consent." The Pellbrook acquisition of 100% of Diril Entertainment Holdings, Inc. — the direct parent of Diril Live Presents LLC — is squarely a Change of Control under clause (b) of the definition.

Critically, the Kevond MSA does **not** appear on the consent list in GC Email § 2.3. The list covers the credit facility, ticketing agreement, amphitheater leases, naming rights, F&B concession, and the Harborline Festival license, but omits the Kevond agreement entirely.

**Why it matters.** Under Section 16.3, if Provider determines that a deemed assignment occurred without consent, it may terminate the MSA by written notice within 90 days after learning of the assignment. Upon such termination, Client must pay (a) all Fees for Services performed through the termination date and (b) the **Minimum Commitment Shortfall** for the remainder of the then-current term. With the 2027 Contract Year minimum at $4,200,000 (or $2,900,000 if the reduction is upheld) and the Ember Season tour SOW Fees at $3,379,000, a termination in early 2027 could expose Diril to a seven-figure shortfall payment while simultaneously losing its production vendor mid-tour.

**Recommendation.** Add the Kevond MSA to the consent list immediately. Request Provider's written consent to the Pellbrook transaction before the December 15, 2026 closing. Given that Diril Live Presents LLC remains the contracting entity and no actual assignment of the agreement occurs, a reasonable provider should grant consent, but the contractual language leaves no discretion. If Kevond resists, negotiate a side letter or amendment confirming that the Pellbrook transaction does not trigger Section 16.3, or that Provider waives its termination right in connection with this specific Change of Control. Do not allow the closing to proceed without resolving this.

## 2. MSA Auto-Renewal for Three-Year Terms Deviates Materially from the Term Sheet

**Documents:** MSA § 12.2; Term Sheet § 5.2

**Issue.** Term Sheet § 5.2 provides: "After the Initial Term, the MSA may be extended by **one-year renewals only by mutual written agreement**." MSA § 12.2 instead provides for **automatic renewal for successive three-year periods**, with a non-renewal notice window of only 90–120 days before the end of the then-current term.

**Why it matters.** If Diril misses the narrow non-renewal window (for the Initial Term ending February 29, 2028, the window is approximately October 31 – November 30, 2027), the agreement renews automatically for three more years, each with a $4,200,000 Minimum Annual Commitment. That is a potential $12,600,000 additional commitment on autopilot. The term sheet contemplated a much more controlled renewal process. This appears to be a drafting error in the MSA that favors Provider.

**Recommendation.** Amend MSA § 12.2 to conform to the term sheet: renewal only by mutual written agreement, or at minimum, extend the non-renewal notice window to 180 days and reduce the renewal period to one year. If Provider resists, calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (October 31, 2027) now and assign ownership internally.

## 3. Reduction Notice Delivered Nine Days Late — Active Dispute Over $1.3M

**Documents:** Amendment No. 1 §§ 3.1, 3.2, 3.5; MSA § 18.2; GC Email § 3

**Issue.** Amendment No. 1 § 3.1 required a Reduction Notice "within 60 days after the Amendment Effective Date." The Amendment took effect August 1, 2026, making the deadline **September 30, 2026**. Diril delivered its notice on **October 9, 2026** — nine days late. Kevond rejected the notice on October 20, 2026, asserting the 2027 minimum remains $4,200,000 under § 3.5.

**Why it matters.** The difference is $1,300,000 in the 2027 Contract Year. The Ember Season tour SOW Fees ($3,379,000) exceed the reduced commitment ($2,900,000) but fall short of the original ($4,200,000), so the outcome determines whether Diril owes a true-up of approximately $821,000 at year-end.

**Assessment of Diril's position.** The amendment language is unambiguous: "must be delivered within 60 days." Section 18.3 of the MSA provides that "[n]o waiver is effective unless in writing." Kevond's continued engagement on 2027 slate planning in late September, without raising the timing issue until October 20, supports an argument of implied waiver or estoppel, but it is a weak one against express contractual language and the written-waiver requirement. Kevond could argue it had no obligation to police the deadline and that its silence was not a waiver.

**Recommendation.** Pursue a commercial resolution. Leverage points for Diril: (a) the ongoing relationship and the Ember Season tour, which Kevond has a strong financial interest in protecting; (b) Kevond's failure to object for 10 days after the deadline while continuing to engage on 2027 planning; (c) the fact that Kevond's own account manager (Carla Montrose) was present at the September 16 production meeting and aware of the reduction discussion. Propose that Kevond accept the reduction in exchange for a commitment to the full 2027 slate or a modest fee adjustment on the tour SOW. If negotiation fails, the dispute should be escalated under MSA § 17.3 (senior executive meeting) before considering litigation. Do not sign any document acknowledging the $4,200,000 figure.

## 4. Consequential Damages Waiver Leaves Diril Exposed on Show Cancellation

**Documents:** MSA §§ 14.1, 14.2, 14.3; SOW § 6.3; Service Level Schedule §§ 3, 4, 6.3

**Issue.** MSA § 14.1 excludes all "indirect, incidental, special, consequential or punitive damages, or … lost profits." For a concert promoter, the most significant financial risks of a production failure are: lost ticket revenue, non-refundable artist fees, venue penalty charges, and attendee claims. None of these are recoverable from Provider under § 14.1.

Diril's available remedies for a Provider-caused show failure are limited to: (a) service credits (capped at 15% of Monthly Production Fees per Service Level Schedule § 4.1); (b) re-performance or fee credit under MSA § 9.3; and (c) the liability cap under § 14.2 (greater of annual Fees or $4,200,000). The indemnity in § 10.1 covers third-party bodily injury and property damage claims but does not cover Diril's own commercial losses.

**Why it matters.** On the Ember Season tour, a single show fee is $71,500. If a rigging failure causes a show stop and the show is cancelled, Diril's recovery from Kevond is a 10% service credit ($7,150) plus potentially a fee credit under § 9.3. Diril still owes the artist, the venue, and potentially faces ticket refund obligations. The economic loss far exceeds what the contract recovers.

**Recommendation.** This is a structural risk that is difficult to change now given the MSA is in force and the tour SOW is signed. However, for future SOWs and any renewal negotiation, seek: (a) a carve-out from § 14.1 for direct costs incurred by Client as a result of a show cancellation caused by Provider's breach (venue fees, artist fees already paid, ticket processing costs); or (b) an increase in the service credit for show stops from 10% to a percentage that more closely reflects actual cancellation costs. In the interim, ensure Diril's event cancellation insurance covers production-failure scenarios and that artist contracts allocate cancellation risk appropriately.

## 5. Local Crew Defined to Include Roles Prohibited for Local Labor Under the Quality Agreement

**Documents:** SOW § 3.2; Production Quality and Safety Agreement § 3.3

**Issue.** SOW § 3.2 defines "Local Crew" to include "up riggers, down riggers, **fly operators** and **pyrotechnic technicians**." Quality Agreement § 3.3 provides: "Provider shall not assign local labor to **performer flying** or to **pyrotechnic firing**."

On their face, these provisions are reconcilable — "fly operators" (operating scenic flying) is different from "performer flying" (the artist's flying system), and "pyrotechnic technicians" (wiring, setup) is different from "pyrotechnic firing" (triggering the product). However, the distinction is not made explicit in either document, and in the heat of a load-in, the line between "operating the flying system" and "performer flying" could be blurred. A Venue safety inspector or regulator could read SOW § 3.2 as authorizing local labor to perform tasks that the Quality Agreement prohibits.

**Why it matters.** If local labor is assigned to a prohibited task and an incident occurs, Diril faces: (a) regulatory exposure (OSHA, local fire authority); (b) potential Venue penalty or loss of future access; (c) undermining of the safety case in any subsequent litigation. The MSA § 5.4 makes Provider "solely responsible" for employment obligations, but that does not shield Diril from regulatory or Venue-level consequences.

**Recommendation.** Issue a change order or written clarification to SOW § 3.2 stating that Local Crew shall not perform performer flying or pyrotechnic firing, consistent with Quality Agreement § 3.3. Confirm in the next production meeting that Kevond's Head Rigger and Pyrotechnics Supervisor understand the boundary. Add a line to the daily safety briefing (Quality Agreement § 2.4) confirming which tasks are reserved for touring crew.

## 6. Local Crew Engaged as "Independent Contractors" While Subject to Provider Direction and Supervision

**Documents:** SOW § 3.2; MSA § 5.4

**Issue.** SOW § 3.2 states that Local Crew are engaged "as independent contractors" but also that "Provider shall direct and supervise the Local Crew through the Head Rigger and the Pyrotechnics Supervisor." This is the classic factual pattern that supports employee reclassification under New York and state labor laws.

**Why it matters.** If local stagehands or riggers are reclassified as employees, the exposure includes unpaid wages, overtime, workers' compensation, and payroll taxes. While MSA § 5.4 makes Provider "solely responsible" for employment obligations, Diril could still face: (a) joint-employer claims by workers; (b) regulatory inquiries at the Venue level; (c) reputational harm. The MSA indemnity (§ 10.1) covers third-party claims for bodily injury and property damage but does not expressly cover wage-and-hour or misclassification claims.

**Recommendation.** Confirm that Kevond carries workers' compensation coverage for all Local Crew (the MSA § 11.1(c) requires "workers' compensation as required by law" — verify the certificate names Local Crew or that the policy is a "statutory" policy covering all workers). Consider adding a specific indemnity in a future amendment or SOW for misclassification claims arising from Provider's engagement of local labor. In the short term, ensure Kevond's engagement letters for Local Crew are consistent with independent contractor status (control over methods, provision of own tools where applicable, ability to work for others).

## 7. Festival LED Side Letter Expires with the Initial Term and Permits Rate Increases

**Documents:** Side Letter §§ 1.1, 2.4; MSA § 12.2

**Issue.** Side Letter § 1.1 limits its application to Engagements "where the first show date of that Engagement falls within the Initial Term" (i.e., through February 29, 2028). If the MSA auto-renews under § 12.2 (see Issue 2), the side letter drops away entirely, and festival LED/video pricing reverts to whatever is stated in the applicable SOW — with no rate hold, no multi-day discount formula, and no 4% annual cap.

Additionally, Side Letter § 2.4 permits Provider to increase rates by up to 4% per Contract Year after February 28, 2026, on 90 days' notice. For the 2027 Contract Year (beginning March 1, 2027), Kevond could deliver a rate increase notice as late as **December 1, 2026**.

**Why it matters.** Diril's 2027 festival slate (if any) would be subject to increased LED/video rates with no contractual ceiling beyond the 4% cap. If the MSA renews in 2028, there is no rate protection at all for festival video packages.

**Recommendation.** (a) Monitor for a rate increase notice from Kevond before December 1, 2026. If received, assess the impact on 2027 festival budgets. (b) In connection with any renewal negotiation (see Issue 2), require that the side letter terms be extended into any Renewal Term or that a new rate schedule be agreed. (c) Ensure that any 2027 festival SOW expressly incorporates the side letter rates to lock in pricing for that engagement.

## 8. Non-Renewal Notice Window Is Narrow and Easy to Miss

**Documents:** MSA § 12.2

**Issue.** As noted in Issue 2, the non-renewal notice must be delivered "not more than 120 days nor fewer than 90 days before the end of the then-current term." A notice delivered outside that window "is of no effect." For the Initial Term, the window is approximately **October 31 – November 30, 2027** — a single month.

**Why it matters.** If Diril's legal or business team misses this window by even one day, the MSA auto-renews for three years. There is no cure provision. The all-or-nothing nature of the window creates a significant operational risk.

**Recommendation.** Calendar the deadline now (October 1, 2027 as an internal trigger to begin the renewal/non-renewal decision process; October 31, 2027 as the hard deadline). Assign a named owner. If the auto-renewal is amended (see Issue 2), this risk is eliminated, but until then, treat the window as a critical date.

## 9. Data Processing Addendum Covers Artist Production Staff — Notice and Consent Gaps

**Documents:** DPA Schedule 1; DPA § 2.4; MSA § 5.5

**Issue.** DPA Schedule 1 identifies as Data Subjects not only Provider's touring crew and local labor but also "artist production staff whose credentials Provider handles at Client's request." DPA § 2.4 makes Provider responsible for "giving any notice at collection that Data Protection Laws require when it collects Credential Data from crew members." However, for artist production staff, the personal information may be collected by the artist's production team and provided to Diril or Kevond secondhand. The DPA does not address who gives the notice at collection in that scenario, nor does it require that the artist's production team consent to its staff's data being processed by Kevond.

**Why it matters.** Under CCPA/CPRA, a "business" (Diril) that provides personal information to a "service provider" (Kevond) must have a contractual basis and must have collected the information lawfully. If artist production staff were not notified that their data would be shared with Kevond for credentialing, there is a compliance gap. For Canadian show dates, PIPEDA requires that consent be obtained from the individual (or that an exception applies). The DPA § 6.2 contemplates cross-border transfer but does not address the consent mechanism for artist staff specifically.

**Recommendation.** (a) Confirm with the artist's production team that its staff are aware their credential data will be processed by Kevond and that this is disclosed in the artist's own privacy notice or engagement terms. (b) Add a sentence to the DPA (by amendment or side letter) clarifying that Client is responsible for ensuring artist production staff have been notified, and that Provider's notice-at-collection obligation under § 2.4 applies only to individuals from whom Provider collects data directly. (c) For Canadian dates, confirm that the accreditation portal process includes a privacy notice that covers artist staff.

## 10. SOW Payment Structure Requires 40% of Fixed Fees Before Any Services Are Performed

**Documents:** SOW §§ 5.1, 5.2

**Issue.** The SOW requires: (a) a 20% deposit on signature (July 13, 2026) and (b) a second 20% deposit invoiced on March 1, 2027. Fixed Fees (excluding the Local Crew estimate) total $2,967,000. The two deposits total $1,186,800 — approximately 40% of fixed Fees — paid before the first rehearsal day (April 5, 2027) and before any services are performed.

**Why it matters.** If the tour is cancelled by Diril more than 120 days before the first rehearsal day (i.e., before approximately December 6, 2026), SOW § 6.1 provides that "deposits paid to date are retained; no further charge." Diril would lose $593,400 (the first deposit) with no services rendered. If the tour is cancelled by Kevond (Provider breach), Diril would need to pursue a refund under the MSA warranty and indemnity provisions, subject to the dispute process. There is no escrow or milestone-based release of the deposits.

**Recommendation.** For future SOWs, negotiate a payment structure tied to milestones (e.g., deposit on signature, second payment on delivery of rigging plans, third payment on completion of rehearsals). For the current SOW, the first deposit has already been paid. Ensure the second deposit invoice (due March 1, 2027) is reviewed carefully before payment and that Diril's AP process flags it for legal review given the ongoing commitment dispute (Issue 3). If the reduction is upheld, the second deposit amount is unchanged (it is based on SOW Fees, not the minimum commitment), but the overall cash position improves by $1.3M at year-end true-up.

## 11. Force Majeure Cost Allocation for the Tour Is Asymmetric

**Documents:** SOW § 6.3; MSA § 15; Side Letter § 5.1 (for comparison)

**Issue.** SOW § 6.3 provides that if a Force Majeure Event causes cancellation of a show date **after load-in has begun**, Client pays 50% of the show fee ($35,750) plus Local Crew charges actually incurred. If cancellation is **before load-in**, no show fee is payable. "Each Party otherwise bears its own costs."

Compare the side letter's weather provision for festivals (§ 5.1): if a Venue cancels for severe weather and equipment was "installed and show-ready," Client pays 50% of equipment rates plus crew hours worked. The tour SOW provision is triggered by the less favorable milestone of "load-in has begun" rather than "show-ready," meaning Diril could owe 50% of the show fee even if the production was only partially loaded in.

**Why it matters.** On a 38-show tour spanning April through July in North America, weather-related cancellations (hurricanes, severe thunderstorms, winter weather in northern venues) are a realistic risk. If three shows are cancelled after load-in begins, Diril pays approximately $107,250 in show fees plus Local Crew costs, with no offsetting revenue. The MSA § 15.1 excludes payment obligations for Services "already performed," but the SOW § 6.3 specifically governs FM cancellation and overrides the general provision.

**Recommendation.** For future SOWs, align the FM cost allocation with the side letter standard (50% only if "show-ready") or negotiate a lower percentage (e.g., 25%) for the period between load-in commencement and show-ready. For the current tour, ensure Diril's event cancellation insurance covers FM-related production costs and that venue contracts allocate weather cancellation risk appropriately.

## 12. Service Level Credits Are Capped and Do Not Address the Most Costly Failure Mode

**Documents:** Service Level Schedule §§ 3.1, 4.1, 6.3, 7.2

**Issue.** The maximum service credit for a single show stop is 10% of the per-show fee ($7,150 for the Ember Season tour). The monthly cap is 15% of Monthly Production Fees (§ 4.1). Section 6.3 confirms that credits are "a price adjustment" and are "in addition to" other rights, but they do not compensate for the actual economic loss of a show stop (see Issue 4).

The chronic failure provision (§ 7.2) — five or more show-readiness or equipment availability failures in 60 consecutive days constituting a material breach — provides a termination right but does not provide additional monetary relief.

**Why it matters.** The service level regime is designed to incentivize performance, not to make Diril whole. For a 38-show tour, the maximum theoretical service credit exposure for Kevond in a bad month is 15% of that month's fees. If Kevond has a systemic equipment problem causing multiple show stops, Diril's monetary recovery is modest relative to its actual losses.

**Recommendation.** The service level schedule is a signed addendum and difficult to change mid-term. However, at the annual review required by § 8.2, propose: (a) increasing the show-stop credit from 10% to 25%; (b) raising the monthly cap from 15% to 25%; and (c) adding a specific credit for load-in milestone failures that cascade into a show-ready failure (currently, the single-credit-per-event rule in § 3.3 means Diril gets only the highest applicable credit, not the sum). In the interim, rely on the chronic failure termination right (§ 7.2) and the MSA § 9.3 re-performance/credit remedy as backstops.

## 13. MSA Section 4.4 True-Up Does Not Reduce Commitment for Provider-Side Force Majeure

**Documents:** MSA § 4.4; Term Sheet § 4.3

**Issue.** MSA § 4.4 reduces the Minimum Annual Commitment by Fees for Engagements that "Provider fails to perform for reasons other than Client's breach or a Force Majeure Event **affecting Client**." The qualifier "affecting Client" means that if a Force Majeure Event affects Provider (e.g., a fire at Kevond's equipment warehouse, a labor dispute at Kevond's shop), the commitment is **not** reduced. Diril still owes the true-up.

This tracks the term sheet language (Term Sheet § 4.3), so it was agreed, but it is an asymmetric allocation that favors Provider.

**Why it matters.** If a significant FM event prevents Kevond from performing multiple Engagements in a Contract Year, Diril loses the productions but still owes the minimum commitment. The $4.2M (or $2.9M) is not reduced.

**Recommendation.** In any renewal or amendment negotiation, seek to delete the "affecting Client" qualifier so that any FM event excusing Provider's performance reduces the commitment. This is a lower-priority item given that the current MSA is in force and the term sheet shows this was the agreed position, but it should be on the list for the 2027 renewal discussion.

## 15. DPA Retention Period for Certification Records Is Indefinite

**Documents:** DPA § 8.1; Production Quality and Safety Agreement § 3.2

**Issue.** DPA § 8.1 requires Provider to delete Credential Data within 90 days after final load-out, "However, Provider may keep certification records for its own employees as long as Section 3.2 of the production quality and safety agreement between the Parties requires." Quality Agreement § 3.2 states that Provider "shall keep certification and training records" but does not specify a retention period. The practical effect is that Provider can retain certification records (which include names, certification numbers, and expiry dates — all personal data) indefinitely.

**Why it matters.** This creates a data minimization issue under CCPA/CPRA and PIPEDA. Personal data should be retained only as long as necessary for the stated purpose. An indefinite retention period for certification records of former crew members is difficult to justify and could be flagged in a privacy audit or regulator inquiry.

**Recommendation.** Amend DPA § 8.1 (or Quality Agreement § 3.2) to specify a maximum retention period for certification records (e.g., three years after the individual's last Engagement, or as required by applicable law, whichever is shorter). In the annual DPA review, confirm that Provider's actual retention practice aligns with the contractual terms.
