## A. Issues Requiring Client Decision

### 1. Customer Indemnity to Provider Is Uncapped and Covers Provider's Own Negligence

**Where:** MSA § 10.2 (indemnity by Customer); MSA § 11.3(c) (exclusion from cap); Term Sheet § 8.2 (original deal).

**Why it matters:** The term sheet contemplated that each party indemnifies the other only for its *own* negligence or willful misconduct. The MSA departs materially: § 10.2 requires Nonaisk to indemnify Zouduth for *all* third-party claims "arising from or relating to the Rental Inventory," including bodily injury and property damage at events, **"regardless of whether caused in part by Provider's installation or handling of the Rental Inventory."** Section 11.3(c) then carves this obligation out of the liability cap, making it unlimited.

Practical scenario: a Zouduth crew improperly secures a stage deck; the deck collapses; guests are injured. Under § 10.2, Nonaisk must indemnify Zouduth for the resulting claim even though Zouduth's own handling caused the injury. Nonaisk's only recourse would be a cross-claim against Zouduth under § 10.1(a), which *is* subject to the 12-month-fee cap (§ 11.1). The economics are therefore asymmetric: Nonaisk's exposure is unlimited; Zouduth's is capped.

**Recommended fix:** (a) Delete the "regardless of whether caused in part by Provider's installation or handling" language; (b) limit § 10.2 to claims arising from the Rental Inventory *itself* (defect in the product, pre-existing condition) and not from Provider's acts or omissions; (c) remove § 10.2 from the § 11.3 exclusions so the indemnity is subject to the cap, or at minimum make the cap mutual and uncapped only for the party whose negligence caused the claim. **Flag for client:** this is the single largest financial exposure in the deal and may require a business judgment about how much risk Nonaisk is willing to accept given the $1.85M annual minimum.

### 2. Provider May Assign Unilaterally to Any Affiliate or Successor

**Where:** MSA § 15.2; Term Sheet § 7.1.

**Why it matters:** The term sheet provided that *neither* party may assign without consent, except to a non-Competitor successor. The MSA gives Provider a unilateral right to assign to "any Affiliate or to any successor to all or substantially all of Provider's business or assets … whether by merger, sale of stock, sale of assets or otherwise," with only 30 days' after-the-fact notice. There is no competitor restriction on Provider's side.

This matters because: (a) Zouduth's crews present themselves as working on Nonaisk's behalf (MSA § 3.4(b)), so a change of control could alter the quality, branding, or reliability of the service Nonaisk's event clients experience; (b) Customer Data and Confidential Information would transfer to the assignee; (c) an Affiliate of Zouduth could itself be a Competitor (the definition in § 1.2 covers Affiliates), yet § 15.2 has no competitor carve-out.

**Recommended fix:** Restore the term-sheet structure: no assignment by either party without the other's prior written consent, except to a successor that is not a Competitor. If Provider resists, at minimum add (a) a competitor restriction, (b) a requirement that the assignee assume all obligations in writing, and (c) a termination right for Customer if the assignee is a Competitor or if Customer reasonably objects to the assignee's financial condition.

### 3. Liability Cap Is Not Mutual in Effect

**Where:** MSA § 11.1 (cap), § 11.3(c) (Customer's § 10.2 indemnity excluded); Term Sheet § 8.3 ("will be mutual").

**Why it matters:** The term sheet expressly stated that limitations of liability "will be mutual." As drafted, Provider's maximum exposure is 12 months of fees (~$1.85M), while Nonaisk's exposure under § 10.2 is unlimited. Even setting aside the scope problem in Issue 1, the *structure* contradicts the agreed deal.

**Recommended fix:** Make the cap truly mutual. If the client accepts a broad product-liability-style indemnity (see Issue 1), at minimum cap it at a multiple of annual fees (e.g., 2× or 3×) rather than leaving it uncapped. **Flag for client:** confirm whether the business team is comfortable with any uncapped exposure at all.

## B. Drafting Issues (Markup)

### 4. DPA Governing Law and Forum Conflict with MSA

**Where:** DPA § 12.1–12.2 (New York law; New York County courts); MSA § 16.1–16.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington courts); Term Sheet § 10 (Delaware; Wilmington).

**Why it matters:** The term sheet and MSA both select Delaware. The DPA silently switches to New York. MSA § 2.3 gives the DPA precedence "for Personal Data matters only," so a data-breach dispute would be litigated in New York under New York law while a service-failure dispute arising from the same incident would be in Delaware. This creates forum-shopping risk, inconsistent standards, and added cost.

**Recommended fix:** Conform DPA § 12 to Delaware law and Wilmington forum, matching the MSA and term sheet.

---

### 5. DPA "Customer Data" Definition Is Narrower Than MSA Definition

**Where:** DPA § 1.3 (Personal Data "uploaded by Customer to the Dispatch Platform"); MSA § 1.4 (all data Provider "receives from or collects for Customer," including delivery sign-offs and data "generated by the Dispatch Platform").

**Why it matters:** The DPA's data-protection obligations (security, breach notification, subprocessor controls, return/deletion) attach only to "Customer Data" as defined in § 1.3. Data that Provider's crews *collect* on site (e.g., a venue manager's phone number obtained during a delivery sign-off) or that the platform *generates* (e.g., GPS tracking data, crew notes) may fall outside the DPA definition because it was not "uploaded by Customer." That data would still be "Customer Data" under the MSA (owned by Nonaisk per § 8.1) but would not enjoy the DPA's security, breach, or deletion protections.

**Recommended fix:** Align DPA § 1.3 with MSA § 1.4. The DPA should cover all Personal Data that Provider receives, collects, or generates in performing the Services, not just data Customer uploads.

---

### 6. Customer Not Named as Additional Insured on Cyber Policy

**Where:** MSA § 12.2 (additional insured on GL and auto only); MSA § 12.1(e) ($2M cyber liability required).

**Why it matters:** If a data breach occurs, Nonaisk's contractual recovery from Provider is subject to the 12-month-fee cap (§ 11.1). The $2M cyber policy is the real source of funds for breach costs, but Nonaisk is not an additional insured on it and has no direct claim. Nonaisk would have to sue Provider and hope the cap is sufficient, or negotiate an uncapped carve-out for data-breach liability (which Provider will resist).

**Recommended fix:** Add the cyber liability policy to § 12.2 so Customer is named as additional insured. Alternatively, add a carve-out in § 11.3 for Provider's obligations under the DPA (so data-breach liability is not subject to the cap), mirroring how § 11.3(c) already carves out Customer's indemnity.

### 7. No Termination for Convenience

**Where:** MSA § 14 (no such right); Term Sheet (silent).

**Why it matters:** Nonaisk is locked in for three years plus auto-renewals. The only exit before the end of the Initial Term is for material breach (with 30-day cure) or insolvency. If the event market softens, if Zouduth's service quality degrades below the material-breach threshold, or if Nonaisk's business strategy changes, there is no clean exit. Nonaisk would still owe the Annual Minimum.

**Recommended fix:** Add a termination-for-convenience right for Customer on 180 days' notice, with a buyout of the remaining Annual Minimum at a discount (e.g., 50% of the unexpired portion). If Provider resists, at minimum allow termination for convenience after the first Contract Year with 120 days' notice. **Flag for client:** this is a commercial decision about how much flexibility Nonaisk needs given the $1.85M commitment.

---

### 8. DPA Subprocessor Objection Remedy Is Weak

**Where:** DPA § 5.2 (Customer may terminate "the affected Services without penalty" if objection unresolved).

**Why it matters:** If a new subprocessor is unacceptable, Nonaisk's only remedy is to terminate the affected SOW. It cannot terminate the entire MSA, and "without penalty" is ambiguous (does it waive the Annual Minimum shortfall for the remaining term?). If the unacceptable subprocessor handles core platform hosting, "the affected Services" could be all of them, but the drafting doesn't say that.

**Recommended fix:** Clarify that "the affected Services" means all Services to the extent they rely on the objected-to subprocessor, and that "without penalty" includes a pro-rata reduction of the Annual Minimum for the unexpired portion of the term. Alternatively, give Customer the right to terminate the entire Agreement if the parties cannot resolve the subprocessor objection.

---

### 9. Damage/Loss Recovery Subject to Liability Cap

**Where:** MSA § 3.3 (Provider's obligation to reimburse for lost/damaged Rental Inventory); MSA § 11.1 (cap at 12-month fees).

**Why it matters:** § 3.3 is not listed among the § 11.3 exclusions, so Provider's reimbursement obligation for destroyed or lost inventory is capped at trailing 12-month fees. In a single bad day (e.g., a truck rollover destroying a full load of high-value staging), losses could exceed the cap. The $500K-per-conveyance cargo insurance (§ 12.1(c)) is the first line, but if the loss exceeds the insurance or the insurer denies the claim, Nonaisk's contractual recovery is capped.

**Recommended fix:** Add § 3.3 to the § 11.3 exclusions, or at minimum raise the cap for property-loss claims to 2× annual fees. Confirm that the cargo policy limits are adequate for the highest-value loads Nonaisk ships. **Flag for client:** ask operations what the maximum value of a single truckload is, so we can assess whether the $500K insurance limit plus the fee cap is sufficient.

## C. Lower-Priority / Housekeeping Items

### 10. Annual Minimum Has No Market-Condition Relief

**Where:** MSA § 5.2.

**Why it matters:** The $1.85M commitment is reduced only for Provider SLA failures and force majeure. If event demand drops (economic recession, public-health event), Nonaisk still owes the minimum. The pro-rata reduction for SLA failures helps, but only if Provider is actually missing SLAs.

**Recommended fix:** Consider a "material adverse change in market conditions" reduction or a floor on the reduction (e.g., minimum cannot exceed 120% of actual invoiced fees if the shortfall is due to market decline). **Flag for client:** this is a commercial risk the business team should assess against its revenue forecast.

---

### 11. Auto-Renewal and 90-Day Notice Window

**Where:** MSA § 14.2.

**Why it matters:** Standard, but easy to miss. If Nonaisk wants out at the end of the Initial Term, it must give notice 90 days before the three-year anniversary.

**Recommended fix:** No drafting change needed, but **flag for client** to calendar the non-renewal deadline (approximately December 2, 2028, if the Effective Date is March 2, 2026).

---

### 12. Press Release: Confirm Mutual Approval and Timing

**Where:** Press Release (entire); MSA § 9.3 (mutually approved announcement).

**Why it matters:** The press release is dated March 4, 2026 (two days after the MSA Effective Date). MSA § 9.3 requires mutual approval. Confirm that the release has been approved by both parties' communications teams before it goes out. The release also states "The first statements of work are expected to cover Nonaisk's core metropolitan markets during the 2026 event season" — if SOWs are not yet finalized, this creates a public commitment to scope and timing that could be hard to walk back.

**Recommended fix:** Add a line to the release: "Specific service territories and schedules will be confirmed in statements of work to be issued by the parties." Confirm internal approval workflow.

---

### 13. Service Credit Mechanics Are SOW-Dependent

**Where:** MSA § 6.2 (credits "specified in the applicable SOW"); no SOWs in the file.

**Why it matters:** The only quantified remedy for missed SLAs is the service credit, but the credit amount, calculation method, and whether it is the sole remedy are all deferred to SOWs that don't yet exist. The § 5.2(a) pro-rata reduction of the Annual Minimum for SLA failures is helpful but is a reduction of a *future* obligation, not a direct payment to Nonaisk.

**Recommended fix:** Before the first SOW is signed, negotiate a template that specifies: (a) credit percentage per hour/day of delay; (b) whether credits are the sole remedy for that SLA failure; (c) a mechanism for Nonaisk to claim credits unilaterally (rather than waiting for Provider to "issue" them under § 6.2).
