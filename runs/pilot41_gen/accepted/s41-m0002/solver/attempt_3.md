## Issue 1 – Order Form Redefines "Products" to Include Veterinary Diet Products and All Future Products

**Where:** Order Form, Section 1; Distribution Agreement, Section 1.1 (definition of "Products"); Distribution Agreement, Section 2.3 (Order of Precedence); Distribution Agreement, Recital B; Distribution Agreement, Section 3.1.

**Explanation.** The Agreement defines "Products" as "dry and canned dog and cat food listed in Exhibit A, excluding Veterinary Diet Products" (Agreement § 1.1). The Order Form, however, redefines "Products" as "all pet food manufactured by Supplier and sold under the Fouveirt brand," expressly including Veterinary Diet Products, freeze-dried treats, and any product "as Supplier's product range is updated from time to time" (Order Form § 1). Under the Order of Precedence, the Order Form controls over the Agreement body (Agreement § 2.3). The broader definition therefore governs.

This creates three distinct problems for Fouveirt:

- **Direct veterinary channel conflict.** Recital B states that Fouveirt sells Veterinary Diet Products "through its own veterinary clinic channel, directly to licensed veterinary practices and their affiliated pharmacies." If those products are now "Products" subject to Distributor's exclusive appointment in the Territory (Agreement § 3.1), Fouveirt's continued direct sales to veterinary practices in New York, New Jersey, Connecticut or Pennsylvania may constitute a breach of § 3.1(b) (prohibition on direct sales to Customers in the Territory). The definition of "Customer" in § 1.1 is limited to accounts "that buy Products from Distributor for resale," which may exclude veterinary practices buying directly from Fouveirt, but the exclusivity grant in § 3.1 is broader than the Customer definition and covers all "Products in the Territory." This is at minimum an ambiguity that Pruran could exploit.

- **Open-ended product capture.** The phrase "as Supplier's product range is updated from time to time" means every new Fouveirt-brand pet food product automatically falls within the exclusive distribution. Fouveirt cannot launch a new product in the Territory without routing it through Pruran.

- **Loss of strategic flexibility.** Fouveirt has granted exclusivity over its entire brand in four states for three years (through February 28, 2028), with no right to sell directly except to the three House Accounts in Exhibit B.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Order Form § 1 to conform to the Agreement's definition. Proposed language:

> **"Products"** means the dry and canned dog and cat food listed in Exhibit A to the Agreement, excluding Veterinary Diet Products and excluding any product not specifically listed in Exhibit A or added by written amendment to Exhibit A signed by both Parties.

If the business intent was to include freeze-dried treats, add them to Exhibit A by SKU rather than using an open-ended "all pet food" formulation. In all events, Veterinary Diet Products must be excluded to preserve Fouveirt's direct channel.

**Client flag.** I need to confirm with the client: (a) does Fouveirt currently sell Veterinary Diet Products directly to veterinary practices in the four-state Territory? If so, Fouveirt may already be in breach of § 3.1. (b) Was the Order Form's broad definition intentional or a drafting error? The signatory for both documents is the same person (Helena Marchetti, CCO), which suggests this may have been an oversight, but we need the client's confirmation before we raise it with Pruran.

## Issue 2 – Service Level Schedule Caps Liability for Temperature Excursions and Handling Failures, Overriding the Agreement's Uncapped Food-Safety Protection

**Where:** Service Level Schedule, Section 5.4; Distribution Agreement, Section 12.2; Distribution Agreement, Section 12.3(d); Distribution Agreement, Section 2.3.

**Explanation.** The Agreement was drafted to give Fouveirt uncapped recovery for Distributor-caused food safety failures. Section 12.3(d) expressly excludes from the liability cap "Losses arising from Distributor's breach of Section 8 (Product Handling and Food Safety), including Recall and contamination costs and Customer chargebacks."

However, SLS § 5.4 provides: "Distributor's aggregate liability for Temperature Excursions and Handling Failures, including any indemnity or recall costs, is subject to Section 12.2 of the Agreement, notwithstanding Section 12.3 of the Agreement." Because the Service Level Schedule prevails over the Agreement body under § 2.3, this provision controls.

The practical effect: Fouveirt's maximum recovery for a Distributor-caused temperature excursion or handling failure—including a resulting recall, contamination claims, and customer chargebacks—is capped at the Service Fees paid in the 12 months before the claim. At the current rate of $38,500/month (SOW § 4.1), that cap is **$462,000**. A single recall of canned pet food across four states could easily generate seven- or eight-figure losses in chargebacks, consumer claims, and reputational damage.

**Recommended fix.** Delete SLS § 5.4 in its entirety, or amend it to provide:

> Distributor's aggregate liability for Temperature Excursions and Handling Failures that result in a Recall, contamination, or adulteration of Products shall not be subject to the cap in Section 12.2 of the Agreement, consistent with Section 12.3(d) of the Agreement.

If Pruran resists full deletion, a fallback is to raise the cap for food-safety-related events to a multiple of Service Fees (e.g., 5× or 10×) or to a fixed dollar amount that reflects realistic recall exposure.

**Client flag.** I need to confirm with the client whether this provision was negotiated and accepted knowingly, or whether it was inserted in the "Amended and Restated" SOW (dated February 16, 2026) without sufficient attention. The Original SOW is not in the folder, so I cannot determine whether § 5.4 was added in the restatement or was present originally. See Issue 10 below.

## Issue 3 – 2026 Price Adjustment Notice Was Untimely; Fouveirt Likely Lost Its Price Increase

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 6.4; Statement of Work, Section 1.2; Order Form, Section 6.1.

**Explanation.** Section 6.4 requires Supplier to deliver a price adjustment notice "at least 60 days before each anniversary of the Effective Date." The Effective Date is March 1, 2025. The anniversary for the Contract Year beginning March 1, 2026 is March 1, 2026. Sixty days before that date is **January 1, 2026**. Fouveirt delivered its notice on **January 20, 2026**—20 days late. Pruran rejected the notice as untimely by letter dated January 27, 2026 (SOW § 1.2).

Section 6.4 states: "If Supplier does not deliver a notice within that period, prices for the following Contract Year remain unchanged." On the plain language, the price adjustment failed. Fouveirt is locked at the March 1, 2025 prices (Exhibit A) for the entire Contract Year from March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2027.

The SOW was executed "without prejudice" to either party's position (SOW § 1.2), but a without-prejudice reservation does not cure a missed contractual deadline. Unless Pruran agrees to accept the late notice or the parties amend § 6.4, Fouveirt has no basis to impose the increased prices.

**Recommended fix.** Two options:

- **Short-term:** Request that Pruran accept the 2026 adjustment as a one-time courtesy, in exchange for Fouveirt's concession on another open item (e.g., the SLS § 5.4 cap). Frame it as a commercial resolution rather than a legal entitlement.
- **Structural:** Amend § 6.4 to extend the notice window or to allow a second notice if the first is missed. Proposed language:

> Supplier may adjust prices once per Contract Year by written notice delivered at least sixty (60) days before the applicable anniversary of the Effective Date. If Supplier fails to deliver timely notice for a given Contract Year, Supplier may deliver a late notice at any time before the first Purchase Order for the following Contract Year is accepted, and the adjustment shall apply to Purchase Orders accepted on or after thirty (30) days following delivery of the late notice.

**Client flag.** I need to confirm the magnitude of the missed price increase (the SOW does not state the proposed new prices). This affects the commercial calculus for negotiating a resolution.

## Issue 4 – "Sole Remedies" Language Limits Fouveirt's Recovery for Minimum Purchase Shortfall

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 5.3.

**Explanation.** If Distributor fails to meet the $6,200,000 minimum annual purchase commitment, Fouveirt's remedies are limited to (a) converting the appointment to non-exclusive or (b) terminating the Agreement. The clause states these are "Supplier's sole remedies for that shortfall." Fouveirt cannot recover the difference between the minimum and actual purchases as damages.

This means if Pruran purchases only $4,000,000 in a Contract Year, Fouveirt walks away with $2,200,000 less in revenue and no right to claim that shortfall as a loss. The protection is purely structural (loss of exclusivity or termination), not financial.

**Recommended fix.** Add a shortfall payment obligation. Proposed language to add to § 5.3:

> In addition to the remedies in (a) and (b) above, if Distributor's actual purchases in a Contract Year are less than the minimum annual purchase commitment, Distributor shall pay Supplier the difference within thirty (30) days after the end of that Contract Year. This payment is in lieu of, and not in addition to, any damages claim, and does not limit Supplier's right to terminate under (a) or (b).

If Pruran resists a true-up payment, a fallback is to reduce the minimum commitment or to add a "buy-back" provision requiring Distributor to purchase the shortfall quantity at a discount within 90 days after year-end.

## Issue 5 – Assignment Without Consent Permits Transfer of Exclusive Distribution Rights to a Successor

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 16.4.

**Explanation.** Either party may assign the Agreement "without consent to a successor to all or substantially all of its business to which this Agreement relates, on written notice to the other Party." If Pruran is acquired by a competitor, a private equity firm, or any entity that lacks Pruran's capabilities or goodwill, Fouveirt is locked into the exclusive arrangement with the acquirer for the remainder of the Term (up to February 28, 2028, plus any renewal).

Fouveirt has no right to terminate upon an assignment. The only protection is the general termination-for-breach right in § 14.1, which requires a material breach after a 30-day cure period.

**Recommended fix.** Amend § 16.4 to give Fouveirt a termination right upon assignment by Distributor. Proposed language:

> Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the other Party's prior written consent, except that either Party may assign to a successor to all or substantially all of its business to which this Agreement relates on written notice to the other Party. If Distributor assigns this Agreement pursuant to this Section, Supplier may terminate this Agreement by written notice delivered within thirty (30) days after receipt of the assignment notice, in which case Section 14.3 shall apply.

## Issue 6 – Service Fee Is Uncapped for the Final Year of the Initial Term

**Where:** Statement of Work, Section 4.5; Statement of Work, Section 4.1.

**Explanation.** The Service Fee is fixed at $38,500/month only "through February 28, 2027" (SOW § 4.5). The Initial Term runs through February 28, 2028 (Order Form § 2). For the final 12 months of the Initial Term (March 1, 2027 – February 28, 2028), any change to the Service Fee "requires a written amendment signed by both Parties." There is no cap, no formula, and no most-favored-customer protection. Pruran could propose a substantial increase during that period, and Fouveirt's only leverage is the threat of non-renewal (which is weak because the Term is nearly over) or termination for cause (which requires a material breach).

**Recommended fix.** Extend the fee-fixing period to the end of the Initial Term, or add a cap. Proposed language:

> The Service Fee is fixed through the end of the Initial Term. Any change to the Service Fee during the Initial Term requires a written amendment signed by both Parties, and no such amendment shall increase the Service Fee by more than five percent (5%) per Contract Year.

## Issue 7 – Fouveirt Is Not Expressly an Additional Insured on Distributor's Product Contamination and Recall Policy

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 15(b); Distribution Agreement, Section 15 (final sentence).

**Explanation.** Section 15(b) requires Distributor to maintain "product contamination and recall insurance of at least $5,000,000." The final sentence of § 15 requires each party to name the other "as an additional insured on its general liability policy." The additional-insured requirement is limited to the general liability policy, not the product contamination and recall policy. If a recall is caused by Distributor's handling failure, Fouveirt's direct recovery would come from the indemnity in § 11.2 (subject to the cap in Issue 2) rather than from the insurance policy itself.

**Recommended fix.** Add to § 15:

> Distributor shall name Supplier as an additional insured on its product contamination and recall insurance policy, or shall provide Supplier with a loss-payee endorsement on that policy to the extent of Supplier's insurable interest in the Products.

## Issue 8 – Price Adjustment Window Is Narrow and One-Time Only

**Where:** Distribution Agreement, Section 6.4.

**Explanation.** Beyond the missed 2026 notice (Issue 3), the structure of § 6.4 is inherently risky for Fouveirt. The notice must be delivered in a 60-day window before each March 1 anniversary, and if missed, prices are locked for the entire 12-month Contract Year with no opportunity to cure. There is no mechanism for mid-year adjustments in response to input cost spikes (e.g., grain, protein, fuel).

**Recommended fix.** In addition to the late-notice cure proposed in Issue 3, consider adding a materiality threshold that permits a mid-year adjustment if raw material costs increase by more than a stated percentage (e.g., 10%) during the Contract Year.

## Issue 9 – Email Thread Creates Informal Operational Standards Not Reflected in the Contract Documents

**Where:** Email thread, messages 2 and 4 (Tobias Harrow, February 19 and March 3, 2025); Distribution Agreement, Section 16.7.

**Explanation.** Pruran's VP of Warehouse Operations stated by email that the launch pallet label format (including the "PROTECT FROM FREEZING" banner and 1.25-inch human-readable lot codes) would be "the standard for all future inbound shipments unless you tell us otherwise" (March 3 email). Fouveirt's Director of Supply Chain confirmed the changes but did not expressly adopt the label format as a permanent standard.

Under § 16.7, amendments require a "written instrument signed by both Parties." An email exchange is unlikely to meet that standard. However, a sustained course of dealing could create expectations that are difficult to unwind. If Fouveirt later wishes to change the label format (e.g., to add allergen information or change the SSCC structure), Pruran could argue that the email established a binding operational standard.

Additionally, the "PROTECT FROM FREEZING" banner is a handling instruction that, if relied upon by Pruran's dock team for routing decisions, creates an operational dependency. If Fouveirt removes the banner in a future label revision without notice, and a canned product is misrouted to a non-temperature-controlled zone, Pruran could argue Fouveirt contributed to any resulting damage.

**Recommended fix.** (a) Add the approved label format as an exhibit or annex to the SOW or the Specifications, so it is a formal contract document rather than an email understanding. (b) Add a provision requiring Fouveirt to give 30 days' written notice before changing any label format or handling instruction. (c) Confirm that the Specifications (which are not in the folder) already address the "PROTECT FROM FREEZING" requirement, so that the obligation rests on the formal document rather than the email.

**Client flag.** I do not have the Specifications in the folder. I need to confirm whether they already mandate the freezing-protection labeling and the human-readable lot code requirements.
