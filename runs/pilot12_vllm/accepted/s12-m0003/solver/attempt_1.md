## Issue 1 – Missed Deadline to Object to Price Adjustment Notice (URGENT)

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 6.3; Fubeix letter to Draisath dated September 21, 2026, ¶ 2.

**What happened.** Draisath issued a Price Adjustment Notice dated **July 1, 2026**, increasing CDU list prices by 9%, effective **October 1, 2026** (90 days' notice, satisfying § 6.2). Section 6.3 requires Distributor to object "within **45 days** after the date of a Price Adjustment Notice." Forty-five days after July 1, 2026 is **August 15, 2026**. Fubeix's objection letter is dated **September 21, 2026**—37 days late.

**Why it matters.** Section 6.3 is unambiguous: "If Distributor does not object in writing within that period, Distributor is deemed to have accepted the adjustment, and the adjusted prices apply to all Purchase Orders accepted on or after the effective date stated in the Price Adjustment Notice." On a plain reading, Fubeix is deemed to have accepted the 9% increase, and the higher prices apply to all CDU purchase orders accepted on or after October 1, 2026. The September 21 letter's request to "begin the good-faith negotiation contemplated by Section 6.3" may be contractually ineffective because the precondition (a timely objection) was not met.

**Compounding effect.** Because the Side Letter limits the $6M minimum purchase commitment to CDU purchases only (Side Letter § 1.2, § 2.3), the 9% price increase directly reduces Fubeix's purchasing power against that commitment. If Fubeix's CDU revenue remains flat, it will need to buy roughly 9% more units to hit $6M, or it risks a shortfall under § 13.2 (loss of exclusivity or termination).

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm with the client whether any objection or communication was sent to Draisath on or before August 15, 2026 (e.g., an email that was not included in the folder). If so, the deadline may have been met.
- If no timely objection was made, assess whether Draisath has actually invoiced at the increased prices or accepted any post-October 1 purchase orders at the higher prices. If Draisath has not yet acted on the increase, there is an opportunity to raise the issue informally before it becomes entrenched.
- In the negotiation call proposed in the September 21 letter, Fubeix should acknowledge the timing issue candidly and seek a commercial resolution (e.g., a phased increase, a price hold on existing customer bids, or a credit). Draisath may be amenable to a practical fix given the commercial relationship.
- Document the client's explanation for the missed deadline in case it becomes relevant.

## Issue 2 – Parent Change of Control: Consent Required Before Closing (URGENT)

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), § 13.3, § 17.2; Fubeix letter to Draisath dated September 21, 2026, ¶¶ 1.1–1.4; Term Sheet § 10.2.

**What happened.** Fubeix's parent, Fubeix Industrial Group, Inc., has signed a definitive agreement for Ostrava Climate Holdings to acquire all equity of Fubeix Industrial. Closing is expected **December 2026**. The September 21 letter notifies Draisath of the Transaction and states: "This letter satisfies any notice requirement under the Agreement with respect to the Transaction."

**Why it matters.** The Agreement does not have a mere "notice" requirement for a change of control. It has a **consent** requirement:

- § 17.2: "Distributor shall not assign or transfer this Agreement … without Supplier's prior written consent, which Supplier may grant or withhold in its **sole discretion**. Any Change of Control of Distributor, including a change of control of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Distributor, is **deemed an assignment** … Any attempted assignment in violation of this Section 17.2 is **void**."
- § 13.3: "Supplier may terminate this Agreement **immediately** by written notice if a Change of Control of Distributor occurs without Supplier's prior written consent under Section 17.2."
- The defined term "Change of Control" (§ 1.1(a)) expressly covers "the acquisition … of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting interests of … any entity that directly or indirectly controls that Party." Ostrava's acquisition of Fubeix Industrial (Fubeix's direct parent) is squarely within this definition.

The September 21 letter is a notice, not a consent request. If the Transaction closes in December 2026 without Draisath's prior written consent, Draisath could argue the Agreement is void (§ 17.2) or terminate immediately (§ 13.3).

**Context from the Term Sheet.** Term Sheet § 10.2 stated: "The definitive drafting is to be agreed." The final Agreement ended up far more restrictive than the term sheet contemplated—adding the "sole discretion" standard, the deemed-assignment language, and the immediate termination right. This appears to be a point where Draisath obtained a more favorable position in the definitive drafting.

**Recommended fix.**
- **Before closing**, Fubeix must send a specific written request to Draisath for its consent under § 17.2. The request should emphasize the representations in the September 21 letter (no change in management, staff, operations, or performance).
- Consider offering Draisath a right to negotiate if the Transaction materially changes Fubeix's ability to perform (e.g., if Ostrava directs Fubeix to reduce its Draisath purchases).
- If Draisath withholds consent, Fubeix needs a plan: (a) negotiate a consent with conditions, (b) seek a waiver, or (c) assess the commercial impact of losing the Agreement. Given that Draisath's consent is in its "sole discretion," Fubeix has limited leverage, and the client should understand that Draisath could say no.
- Do **not** allow the Transaction to close before obtaining written consent. The letter's statement that it "satisfies any notice requirement" is insufficient as a matter of contract.

## Issue 3 – Non-Compete Dramatically Broader Than Term Sheet

**Where:** Term Sheet § 4.3; Distribution Agreement § 4.3.

**Comparison:**

| Element | Term Sheet § 4.3 | Final Agreement § 4.3 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | During the Term only | Term + **18 months** |
| Product scope | Competing **coolant distribution units** only | **Any thermal management products** manufactured by any person other than Supplier |
| Geographic scope | **Within the Territory only** | **Anywhere in the world** |
| Restricted activities | Distribute, market, sell | Distribute, market, sell, **promote, solicit orders** |
| Entity scope | Distributor | Distributor, **any Affiliate**, "or otherwise" |
| Carve-out | "Distributor's other product lines are not restricted, including its distribution of third-party air-side cooling equipment." | **None** |

**Why it matters.** The term sheet explicitly preserved Fubeix's ability to distribute third-party air-side cooling equipment and other non-CDU product lines. The final Agreement eliminates that carve-out and extends the restriction to all thermal management products worldwide for 18 months post-termination. If Fubeix currently distributes or plans to distribute any competing thermal management product (air coolers, other liquid cooling, free cooling, etc.), it is in breach of § 4.3 as drafted. Even if Fubeix does not currently compete, the restriction limits its strategic flexibility for the life of the Agreement plus 18 months.

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm with the client what other product lines Fubeix currently distributes or plans to distribute. If any fall within "thermal management products," Fubeix is potentially in breach right now.
- Raise this with Draisath in the next negotiation (ideally tied to the consent request under Issue 2 or the price adjustment discussion under Issue 1). Seek to restore the term sheet's carve-out for air-side cooling and other non-competing product lines, and to narrow the geographic scope to the Territory.
- If Draisath resists, at minimum seek to narrow "any thermal management products" to "competing coolant distribution units" (the term sheet language) and to limit the post-termination tail to the Territory.

## Issue 4 – Automatic Five-Year Renewal with Narrow Non-Renewal Window

**Where:** Term Sheet § 2.2; Distribution Agreement § 2.2.

**Comparison:**
- **Term Sheet:** "one-year renewal terms by mutual written agreement. Neither Party will be obliged to agree to a renewal."
- **Final Agreement:** "automatically renew for successive **five (5) year** renewal terms." Non-renewal requires written notice "during the **fifteen (15) day period ending ninety (90) days before** the expiration of the Initial Term or the then-current Renewal Term."

**Why it matters.** The final Agreement converts what was a mutual-agreement, one-year renewal into an automatic five-year lock-in with a very narrow opt-out window. For the Initial Term (ending January 14, 2029):

- 90 days before expiration = **October 16, 2028**
- 15-day non-renewal window = approximately **October 2 – October 16, 2028**

If Fubeix misses this 15-day window, it is locked in for five additional years (through January 14, 2034), and the non-compete (§ 4.3) runs for the full Term plus 18 months.

**Recommended fix.**
- **Calendar the non-renewal deadline immediately.** Set a reminder for no later than **September 1, 2028** (giving a month's buffer before the October 2 window opens) to decide whether to renew and, if not, to prepare and send the non-renewal notice well within the window.
- Consider whether the automatic renewal is appropriate given the non-compete's breadth (Issue 3). If the non-compete is not narrowed, a five-year auto-renewal compounds the restriction significantly.
- In any renegotiation with Draisath, seek to revert to the term sheet model (mutual agreement, one-year terms) or at minimum extend the non-renewal notice window to 90 days before expiration.

## Issue 5 – Side Letter Redefinition of "Products": Potential Scope Ambiguity

**Where:** Side Letter § 1.2, § 2.1, § 4.1; Distribution Agreement § 1.1 (definition of "Products"), § 18.4 (order of precedence).

**The issue.** Side Letter § 1.2 defines "Products" as "solely the coolant distribution unit models listed in Exhibit A, Part 1." Section 18.4 of the Agreement provides that a signed side letter controls over the Agreement in the event of conflict. Read strictly, the Side Letter's definition could be argued to override the Agreement's definition of "Products" (which includes CDUs, RDHx, and spare parts) throughout the Agreement.

**Why it matters.** If Draisath argued that "Products" throughout the Agreement now means only CDUs, the practical effects would be severe: Fubeix's exclusive distribution right (§ 3.1, § 4.1) would cover only CDUs, not RDHx or spare parts. Draisath could appoint other distributors for RDHx and spare parts in the Territory.

**Likely correct reading.** The Side Letter is expressly limited to the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment. Section 4.1 states: "References in the Agreement to the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment are references to that commitment as amended by this Side Letter." The redefinition in § 1.2 is contextual—it specifies what counts toward the $6M minimum. A court would likely read it that way. But the drafting is not airtight.

**Recommended fix.**
- In the next amendment or side letter with Draisath (e.g., the consent letter under Issue 2), include a clarifying sentence: "For the avoidance of doubt, the definition of 'Products' in Section 1.2 of the Side Letter applies solely for purposes of calculating the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment and does not modify the definition of 'Products' in Section 1.1 of the Agreement for any other purpose."
- Alternatively, if a broader renegotiation occurs, restate the minimum purchase provision in a new side letter with unambiguous drafting.

## Issue 6 – Reserved Accounts List: Confirm Whether Draisath Provided It

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 4.2; Exhibit B, Part 3.

**The issue.** Exhibit B, Part 3 provides: "Supplier's reserved global accounts under Section 4.2 are the hyperscale cloud operators that Supplier identifies to Distributor **in writing on or before the Effective Date** [January 15, 2026]." The list "may be updated only with Distributor's written agreement."

The matter folder does not contain any evidence that Draisath provided this list. If Draisath never identified any reserved accounts in writing by the Effective Date, then arguably there are no reserved accounts, and § 4.2 is inoperative—meaning Draisath cannot sell directly to any hyperscale cloud operator in the Territory without breaching its exclusivity obligation under § 4.1.

**Why it matters.** Hyperscale cloud operators are major buyers of data center cooling. If Draisath is selling directly to them in the Territory without a valid reserved account designation, Fubeix has a breach claim (and potentially a claim for lost profits, subject to the consequential damages exclusion in § 15.1). Conversely, if Draisath did provide the list and Fubeix simply did not file it, Fubeix is subject to the carve-out and receives only a 3% referral fee on those sales.

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm with the client whether Draisath provided a written list of reserved accounts on or before January 15, 2026.
- If no list was provided, consider sending a letter to Draisath requesting confirmation of whether any reserved accounts were designated. If Draisath cannot produce a written list, Fubeix should put Draisath on notice that any direct sales to hyperscale cloud operators in the Territory constitute a breach of § 4.1.
- If a list was provided, review it for scope and confirm Fubeix is receiving the 3% referral fee on all qualifying sales.

## Issue 7 – Compounding Risk: Price Increase + Minimum Purchase + Exclusivity

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§ 5.1, 5.3, 6.2, 6.3, 13.2; Side Letter §§ 1.2, 2.1, 2.3.

**The issue.** Three provisions interact to create a compounding risk for Fubeix:

1. The $6M minimum counts **only CDU purchases** (Side Letter §§ 1.2, 2.3).
2. The 9% CDU price increase (if effective) means Fubeix must purchase approximately 9% more CDU units to reach $6M in net invoice value.
3. If Fubeix misses the minimum, Draisath may convert the appointment to non-exclusive or terminate (§ 13.2), with notice due within 90 days after the Contract Year ends (by approximately **April 14, 2027** for the first Contract Year).

If Fubeix's CDU sales volume is constrained by the price increase (customers push back on higher prices, Fubeix's bids become less competitive), it could miss the minimum and lose exclusivity—precisely when it needs exclusivity most to protect its market position.

**Recommended fix.**
- Model Fubeix's CDU purchasing for the first Contract Year (through January 14, 2027) under both scenarios: (a) 9% price increase effective, and (b) price increase resolved in Fubeix's favor. Identify the gap.
- If the gap is material, consider whether Fubeix can accelerate CDU purchases before October 1, 2026 (at pre-increase prices) to build a cushion. Each dollar of pre-increase CDU purchases counts toward the $6M at a lower cost.
- In negotiations with Draisath, tie the price adjustment discussion to the minimum purchase commitment: if Draisath insists on the 9% increase, Fubeix should seek a corresponding temporary reduction in the minimum or an extension of the first Contract Year.

## Issue 8 – NDA Considerations in Connection with the Ostrava Transaction

**Where:** Mutual NDA §§ 1.2, 1.3, 2.1, 2.2, 7.1, 7.9; Fubeix letter dated September 21, 2026, ¶ 1.

**The issues.**

(a) **Term sheet confidentiality.** The NDA defines "Confidential Information" to include "the terms of any proposal or term sheet exchanged between the Parties" (§ 1.2). The NDA expires **October 5, 2027** (two years from the October 6, 2025 effective date), with a three-year survival period for confidentiality obligations (until **October 5, 2030**). If Fubeix wishes to use the term sheet to support its position on the non-compete (Issue 3) or renewal terms (Issue 4) in a dispute with Draisath, it should be able to do so in litigation between the parties. However, if Fubeix needs to share the term sheet with Ostrava (e.g., for integration planning or in connection with the consent request), Ostrava must be bound by confidentiality obligations "at least as protective as those in this NDA" (§ 2.2). Ostrava is not currently a "Representative" of Fubeix under § 1.3 (it is not yet an Affiliate).

(b) **Non-solicitation.** NDA § 9.1 prohibits either party from directly soliciting the other's employees for **12 months after the NDA term ends** (i.e., until **October 5, 2028**). If Ostrava's integration plan includes hiring Draisath personnel, this restriction applies.

**Recommended fix.**
- Before sharing any Draisath confidential information (including the term sheet) with Ostrava, ensure Ostrava executes a confidentiality undertaking at least as protective as the NDA.
- Flag the non-solicitation restriction to Ostrava's integration team.
- Note that the NDA's "Purpose" (evaluating and negotiating a distribution arrangement) has been fulfilled. The use restriction (§ 2.1) is tied to the Purpose, so one could argue the use restriction no longer applies. However, the non-disclosure restriction (§ 2.2) continues independently.

## Issue 10 – Transit Risk and Insurance Gap

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§ 8.1, 8.2, 16.

**The issue.** Delivery is FCA Draisath's facility in Allentown, PA (Incoterms 2020). Title and risk of loss pass to Fubeix upon delivery at Draisath's dock (§ 8.2). Fubeix bears all transit risk from that point. Section 16 requires each party to maintain $5M CGL including products-completed operations, but does not require cargo or transit insurance.

**Why it matters.** CDUs are high-value, fragile equipment. If a shipment is damaged in transit, Fubeix bears the loss (subject to any carrier insurance it may have separately). The Agreement's insurance requirement does not address this.

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm that Fubeix's existing insurance program includes cargo/transit coverage for inbound product shipments. If not, arrange coverage before the next shipment.
- This is a lower-priority item but should be addressed as part of general risk management.
