## Issue 1 – Change of Control: Notice Given, Consent Not Sought (URGENT)

**Where:**
- Distribution Agreement §1.1 (definition of "Change of Control"), §13.3, §17.2
- Term Sheet §10.2
- Fubeix letter to Draisath, September 21, 2026, §§1.1–1.4

**The problem.** The Agreement defines "Change of Control" to include the acquisition of more than 50% of the voting interests of "any entity that directly or indirectly controls" the Party (Agreement §1.1). Ostrava Climate Holdings' acquisition of Fubeix Industrial Group (Fubeix's direct parent) is squarely within that definition. Section 17.2 deems any such Change of Control to be an assignment that requires Supplier's **prior written consent**, which Supplier "may grant or withhold in its sole discretion." Section 13.3 gives Supplier the right to **terminate immediately** if a Change of Control occurs without that consent. Section 17.2 further provides that any attempted assignment in violation is **void**.

The September 21 letter is structured as a notice. Paragraph 1.4 states: "This letter satisfies any notice requirement under the Agreement with respect to the Transaction." But the Agreement contains no freestanding notice obligation for a Change of Control. The only obligation is to obtain prior written consent. The letter does not request consent; it informs Draisath of the Transaction and asserts that the letter satisfies whatever notice requirement exists.

If the Transaction closes in December 2026 without Draisath's written consent, Draisath has a clean termination right under §13.3 and can additionally argue the deemed assignment is void under §17.2.

**Why it matters.** Loss of the exclusive distribution appointment would be devastating to Fubeix's business in the Territory. The "void" language in §17.2 creates additional uncertainty: it is unclear whether a void deemed assignment would unwind the corporate transaction's effect on the Agreement or simply give Draisath a termination right. Either way, Fubeix is exposed.

**The term sheet context.** Term Sheet §10.2 acknowledged that a Change of Control would be treated as an assignment and that Supplier would have a termination right, but it stated "The definitive drafting is to be agreed." The final drafting is the most restrictive version: sole-discretion consent, deemed-assignment treatment, voidness, and immediate termination. This is a significant departure from the tentative term-sheet language, though not a clear breach of the (non-binding) term sheet.

**Recommended fix.**
1. **Before the Transaction closes**, send Draisath a separate letter or email that expressly requests its prior written consent under §17.2 to the Change of Control, and make clear that Fubeix is not treating the September 21 letter as a substitute for consent.
2. Offer Draisath the assurances already in the September 21 letter (management continuity, continued performance) as the basis for consent.
3. If Draisath withholds or delays consent, assess whether to (a) condition the Transaction's closing on obtaining that consent, (b) negotiate a consent fee or other concession, or (c) seek a side letter confirming that the specific Transaction will not trigger §13.3.
4. Do **not** allow the Transaction to close without resolved consent.

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- Has Fubeix Industrial's definitive agreement with Ostrava included any condition related to Draisath's consent?
- What is the expected closing date, and is there a long-stop date?
- Is Fubeix willing to make the Transaction conditional on Draisath's consent?

## Issue 2 – Price Adjustment Objection Is Late (URGENT)

**Where:**
- Distribution Agreement §6.2, §6.3
- Fubeix letter to Draisath, September 21, 2026, §2

**The problem.** Section 6.3 gives Distributor **45 days** from the date of a Price Adjustment Notice to object in writing. The Price Adjustment Notice is dated **July 1, 2026**. The 45-day deadline therefore expired on **August 15, 2026**. The objection letter is dated **September 21, 2026** – 37 days after the deadline.

Section 6.3 is explicit: "If Distributor does not object in writing within that period, Distributor is **deemed to have accepted** the adjustment, and the adjusted prices apply to all Purchase Orders accepted on or after the effective date stated in the Price Adjustment Notice."

The effective date of the increase is October 1, 2026. As of that date, the 9% CDU price increase is contractually in effect, and Fubeix is deemed to have accepted it.

**Why it matters.** CDUs are the core of Fubeix's revenue. A 9% cost increase on the product category that drives the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment (see Issue 5) directly compresses margins. Fubeix's letter states that it has already submitted customer bids based on pre-increase pricing, creating a margin squeeze on committed projects.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Accept that the objection is likely ineffective as a matter of contract. Do not build the client's strategy on the assumption that the §6.3 negotiation right is available.
2. However, the September 21 letter does serve a practical purpose: it puts Draisath on notice of Fubeix's commercial concern and preserves a basis for a commercial (rather than contractual) discussion. Use it as an opening for a business negotiation – e.g., a temporary price hold on specific POs already bid, a volume rebate, or a most-favored-customer-style concession.
3. If Fubeix wishes to preserve any legal argument (e.g., that Draisath waived the deadline by its conduct, or that the 90-day notice period in §6.2 was the operative timing provision and §6.3's 45-day window should be read in light of it), that argument is weak on the plain text. I would not recommend litigating this point, but it can be held in reserve as leverage in a commercial discussion.
4. Going forward, implement an internal calendar system to track all Price Adjustment Notice deadlines. The Agreement permits only one price adjustment per Product per Contract Year (§6.2), so the next opportunity for Draisath to adjust CDU prices is in Contract Year 2 (beginning January 15, 2027).

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- Were there any communications between the parties between July 1 and September 21 in which Draisath acknowledged the objection period, extended it, or discussed the increase informally? If so, there may be a waiver or course-of-dealing argument.
- What is the dollar value of customer bids that were priced on pre-increase list prices?

## Issue 3 – Non-Compete Drastically Expanded Beyond Term Sheet

**Where:**
- Term Sheet §4.3
- Distribution Agreement §4.3, §13.6 (survival)

**The problem.** The term sheet and the final agreement differ on every material dimension of the non-compete:

| Dimension | Term Sheet §4.3 | Agreement §4.3 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | During the Term only | Term **plus 18 months** after expiration or termination |
| Product scope | "Competing coolant distribution units" | "**Any thermal management products** manufactured by any person other than Supplier" |
| Geographic scope | "Within the Territory only" | "**Anywhere in the world**" |
| Restricted activities | Distribute, market, sell | Distribute, market, sell, **promote or solicit orders** |
| Carve-outs | Explicitly preserves "distribution of third-party air-side cooling equipment" | **None** |

Section 13.6 confirms that §4.3 survives expiration or termination.

**Why it matters.** Fubeix is a thermal management distributor. The Agreement's non-compete, as drafted, would prevent Fubeix from distributing **any** thermal management product from **any** competitor, **worldwide**, for 18 months after the relationship ends. This likely encompasses Fubeix's existing air-side cooling lines, its other product categories, and any new thermal management products it might carry. If the relationship ends (whether by Draisath's termination under §13.3, by non-renewal, or by mutual agreement), Fubeix could be locked out of a significant portion of its business for a year and a half.

The worldwide scope is particularly aggressive for a contract whose Territory is five northeastern states and whose governing law is New York. A New York court might enforce it as written, but the overbreadth creates at least an argument for partial unenforceability under New York's reasonableness standard for restrictive covenants in commercial agreements.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Raise this with Draisath now, while the relationship is intact and the change-of-control consent discussion (Issue 1) provides a natural opening for a broader conversation. Propose conforming §4.3 to the term sheet: territory-limited, CDU-specific, term-only, with the air-side cooling carve-out restored.
2. If Draisath resists, seek at minimum: (a) geographic limitation to the Territory or the United States; (b) product limitation to "coolant distribution units and rear-door heat exchangers" (i.e., the products Fubeix actually distributes); and (c) restoration of the air-side cooling carve-out.
3. If the non-compete cannot be narrowed and the relationship ends, be prepared to argue unenforceability or seek reformation. The term sheet is admissible as evidence of the parties' original intent.

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- What other product lines does Fubeix currently distribute? Which would be captured by "any thermal management products"?
- Is Fubeix planning to expand into any new thermal management product categories?

## Issue 4 – Automatic Five-Year Renewals with Narrow Non-Renewal Window

**Where:**
- Term Sheet §2.2
- Distribution Agreement §2.2

**The problem.** Term Sheet §2.2 provided for optional **one-year** renewal terms "by mutual written agreement," with neither party obliged to agree. Agreement §2.2 provides for **automatic** renewal for successive **five-year** terms. Either party can prevent renewal only by giving written notice "during the fifteen (15) day period ending ninety (90) days before the expiration of the Initial Term or the then-current Renewal Term."

For the Initial Term (ending January 14, 2029), the non-renewal notice window is:
- 90 days before January 14, 2029 = **October 16, 2028**
- 15-day period ending October 16, 2028 = **October 2 through October 16, 2028**

If Fubeix misses that 15-day window, it is locked in for five additional years (through January 2034), with the next non-renewal window not arriving until approximately October 2033.

**Why it matters.** Combined with the $6M minimum purchase commitment (which applies to "each later Contract Year during the Term" per Side Letter §2.2) and the non-compete, an automatic five-year renewal significantly increases Fubeix's long-term exposure. If market conditions change, if Draisath's product line loses competitiveness, or if Fubeix wants to add a competing product line, it cannot easily exit.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Calendar the non-renewal window now: **October 2–16, 2028**. Set multiple internal reminders well in advance.
2. In any renegotiation with Draisath (see Issue 1), propose reverting to the term sheet structure: optional one-year renewals by mutual agreement.
3. If the automatic renewal structure stays, consider negotiating a "for convenience" termination right exercisable on 12 months' notice, or a right to terminate if the minimum purchase commitment is met for two consecutive years (i.e., Fubeix has proven itself and should not be locked in).

## Issue 5 – Side Letter Narrows Minimum Purchase Counting to CDUs Only

**Where:**
- Distribution Agreement §5.1
- Side Letter §§1.2, 2.1, 2.3, 4.1
- Distribution Agreement §18.4 (order of precedence)

**The problem.** The original Agreement §5.1 set a $8M minimum counting **all** Products (CDUs, RDHx, and spare parts). The Side Letter reduces the minimum to $6M (Side Letter §2.1) but redefines "Products" for purposes of the Side Letter to mean "solely the coolant distribution unit models listed in Exhibit A, Part 1" (Side Letter §1.2). Side Letter §2.3 then provides that "Purchases of Products" count toward the minimum, and §4.1 provides that all references in the Agreement to the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment refer to the commitment "as amended by this Side Letter."

Under the Agreement's order of precedence (§18.4), the Side Letter controls. The practical effect: **only CDU purchases count toward the $6M minimum.** RDHx and spare parts purchases do not count.

**Why it matters.** This is not obviously a benefit to Fubeix. Consider an illustrative year:

| Scenario | CDUs | RDHx | Spare Parts | Total | Original $8M (all products) | Side Letter $6M (CDUs only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | $5.0M | $2.0M | $1.0M | $8.0M | Met | **$1.0M short** |
| B | $6.5M | $1.0M | $0.5M | $8.0M | Met | Met |
| C | $4.0M | $3.0M | $1.0M | $8.0M | Met | **$2.0M short** |

If Fubeix's revenue mix includes significant RDHx or spare parts, the Side Letter's narrowing could create a shortfall even though total purchases exceed $6M. A shortfall triggers Draisath's right to convert to non-exclusive or terminate (Agreement §13.2).

**Recommended fix.**
1. Confirm with the client the expected product mix for the current Contract Year (January 15, 2026 – January 14, 2027) and the next. If CDUs are expected to represent less than $6M of purchases, this is a live risk.
2. If the product mix makes the CDU-only counting a problem, raise it with Draisath and propose either (a) restoring all-product counting at the $6M level, or (b) reducing the minimum further to a level that is achievable on CDUs alone.
3. In the interim, ensure that the quarterly statements Draisath provides under Side Letter §3 (and Agreement §5.2) are tracked carefully. If a shortfall is emerging, address it before the Contract Year ends, because Draisath has 90 days after year-end to exercise its §13.2 rights.

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- What is the projected CDU-only purchase value for Contract Year 1 (ending January 14, 2027)?
- Has Draisath provided any quarterly statements to date, and what do they show?
- Was the CDU-only counting a deliberate trade-off negotiated by the client (i.e., the client accepted a lower minimum in exchange for the narrowing), or was this a drafting point that was not fully considered?

## Issue 6 – Reserved Accounts List: Confirm Delivery

**Where:**
- Distribution Agreement §4.2, Exhibit B Part 3
- Term Sheet §4.2

**The problem.** Both the term sheet and the Agreement provide that Supplier's reserved global accounts are "the hyperscale cloud operators that Supplier identifies to Distributor **in writing on or before the Effective Date**" (Exhibit B, Part 3). The Effective Date is January 15, 2026. If Draisath did not deliver a written list by that date, the reserved-accounts carve-out is empty: Draisath has no reserved accounts and cannot sell directly to any customer in the Territory without breaching §4.1.

**Why it matters.** If the list was delivered, Fubeix is entitled to a 3% referral fee on each reserved-account sale for installation in the Territory, and Fubeix should be tracking those sales. If the list was not delivered, Fubeix has stronger exclusivity than it may realize, and Draisath's direct sales to hyperscale customers in the Territory would be breaches.

**Recommended fix.**
1. Confirm with the client whether Draisath provided a written list of reserved accounts on or before January 15, 2026. If so, obtain a copy and calendar the referral-fee tracking.
2. If no list was provided, do not raise the point proactively (it gives Draisath a free opportunity to cure by delivering a list retroactively), but be aware of it as leverage in any negotiation.

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- Did Draisath provide a written list of reserved accounts by January 15, 2026? If so, which accounts are on it?
- Has Fubeix received any referral fees to date?

## Issue 7 – NDA Non-Solicitation and Ongoing Confidentiality Obligations

**Where:**
- Mutual NDA §§7.1, 7.2, 9.1
- Distribution Agreement §12

**The problem.** The NDA (effective October 6, 2025) runs for two years, expiring **October 6, 2027**. Its non-solicitation provision (§9.1) restricts both parties from directly soliciting each other's employees "during the term of this NDA and for twelve (12) months afterwards" – i.e., through **October 6, 2028**. The confidentiality survival period is three years post-expiration (§7.2), running to **October 6, 2030** (or indefinitely for trade secrets).

Separately, the Distribution Agreement has its own confidentiality provisions (§12), which survive for three years after termination (§12.3, §13.6).

**Why it matters.** This is not an acute issue, but it becomes relevant if:
- Fubeix is planning to hire any Draisath engineers or sales people (or vice versa) in connection with the Transaction or otherwise.
- The Transaction involves integration planning that could expose Draisath's confidential information to Ostrava or its advisors.
- Fubeix wants to use information received during the NDA period (e.g., Draisath's cost data, roadmap) for purposes beyond the distribution relationship.

**Recommended fix.**
1. If Fubeix or Ostrava is planning any headcount changes that could involve Draisath personnel, clear it against §9.1 first.
2. Ensure that Ostrava's deal team and advisors are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as the NDA before they receive any Draisath information. The NDA's "Representatives" definition (§1.3) covers Fubeix's own advisors but does not extend to a third-party acquirer's team.
3. Note that the Distribution Agreement's §12 confidentiality obligations are separate from and additional to the NDA. Information shared in performance of the Agreement is protected under §12 regardless of the NDA's term.

**Items to confirm with the client:**
- Is Ostrava's deal team or integration team in possession of any Draisath confidential information?
- Are there any planned hires from Draisath?
