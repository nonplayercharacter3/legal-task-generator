## Issue 1 – Fubeix's Objection to the Price Adjustment Notice Is Untimely

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §6.3; Letter to Draisath (Sep 21, 2026), ¶¶2.1–2.2; Price Adjustment Notice dated Jul 1, 2026 (referenced in the letter).

**Explanation.** Section 6.3 gives Distributor 45 days after the date of a Price Adjustment Notice to object in writing. The Price Adjustment Notice is dated **July 1, 2026**. Forty-five days later is **August 15, 2026**. Fubeix's objection letter is dated **September 21, 2026** — 82 days after the notice, or 37 days past the deadline. Section 6.3 states that if Distributor does not object within the period, it "is deemed to have accepted the adjustment."

**Why it matters.** Draisath has a strong textual argument that the 9% CDU price increase (effective October 1, 2026) is already in force and Fubeix is contractually bound to it. The good-faith negotiation mechanism in §6.3 is triggered only by a timely objection. If Draisath refuses to negotiate, Fubeix's leverage is limited to arguing waiver, estoppel, or that Draisath's conduct (e.g., continuing to accept POs at old prices) constituted a practical waiver — none of which is strong on this record.

**Recommended fix.**
- Immediately assess whether Draisath has responded to the September 21 letter or has begun invoicing at the increased prices. If Draisath has accepted POs and invoiced at pre-increase prices after the effective date, document that conduct as a potential waiver or course of performance.
- If Draisath has not yet responded, send a follow-up letter acknowledging the timing issue and framing the September 21 letter as a request for equitable relief / good-faith negotiation under New York law, while reserving all rights.
- Going forward, calendar all contractual notice and objection deadlines the day they arise. The 45-day objection window is short and unforgiving.

**Needs client confirmation:** Has Draisath responded to the September 21 letter? Has Draisath invoiced any POs at the increased prices since October 1, 2026?

## Issue 2 – Change of Control: Notice Given, But Consent Not Sought or Obtained

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§1.1 (Change of Control definition), 13.3, 17.2; Letter to Draisath (Sep 21, 2026), ¶¶1.1–1.4; Term Sheet §10.2.

**Explanation.** The September 21 letter notifies Draisath that Ostrava Climate Holdings will acquire 100% of Fubeix Industrial Group, Inc. (Fubeix's parent), with closing expected December 2026. The letter states: *"This letter satisfies any notice requirement under the Agreement with respect to the Transaction."*

However, the Agreement does not contain a freestanding "notice of change of control" provision. What it contains is a **consent requirement**:

- **§17.2:** Distributor shall not assign without Supplier's prior written consent, "which Supplier may grant or withhold in its **sole discretion**." A Change of Control of Distributor (including of any entity that directly or indirectly controls Distributor) "is deemed an assignment."
- **§13.3:** If a Change of Control occurs without Supplier's prior written consent under §17.2, Supplier may **terminate immediately** by written notice.

The Change of Control definition (§1.1) is triggered: Ostrava's acquisition of Fubeix Industrial Group (which directly controls Fubeix Thermal Distribution) is "the acquisition … of more than fifty percent (50%) of the voting interests of … any entity that directly or indirectly controls that Party."

The letter is a notice, not a request for consent. It does not ask Draisath to grant or withhold consent. It does not acknowledge that consent is required.

**Why it matters.** If the transaction closes in December 2026 without Draisath's prior written consent, Draisath has an immediate, no-cure-period termination right under §13.3. There is no requirement that Draisath act reasonably, no buyout provision, and no deemed-consent-after-silence mechanism. Draisath holds an absolute veto.

The term sheet (§10.2) acknowledged that "the definitive drafting is to be agreed" on this point, suggesting the parties had not settled the CoC mechanics before signing. The final Agreement is markedly one-sided in Draisath's favor.

**Recommended fix.**
- **Before closing**, send a formal written request to Draisath for its consent under §17.2, accompanied by the factual representations already made in the September 21 letter (management continuity, no change in operations, Fubeix remains a wholly owned subsidiary of Fubeix Industrial).
- If Draisath withholds consent or does not respond before closing, evaluate: (a) whether closing without consent is acceptable given the termination risk; (b) whether to seek a side letter or amendment conditioning the CoC provision (e.g., consent not to be unreasonably withheld, or a most-favored-customer / buyout right); (c) whether the transaction documents can be structured to delay closing until consent is obtained.
- If Draisath consents, obtain the consent in writing and file it with the Agreement.

**Needs client confirmation:**
- Has any consent request been sent to Draisath?
- What is the firmest possible closing date? Is there flexibility to delay closing pending Draisath's response?
- Is Fubeix Industrial Group's definitive agreement with Ostrava Climate Holdings subject to any regulatory conditions that would provide additional time?

## Issue 3 – Non-Compete Is Dramatically Broader Than the Term Sheet

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §4.3; Term Sheet §4.3.

**Explanation.** The term sheet provided:

> "During the term, Distributor will not distribute, market or sell competing **coolant distribution units**, **within the Territory only**. Distributor's other product lines are not restricted, including its distribution of **third-party air-side cooling equipment**."

The final Agreement provides:

> "During the Term and for **eighteen (18) months** after its expiration or termination, Distributor shall not, directly or indirectly, **through any Affiliate or otherwise**, distribute, market, sell, **promote or solicit orders for any thermal management products** manufactured by any person other than Supplier, **anywhere in the world**."

The deviations are:

| Dimension | Term Sheet | Final Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Product scope | Competing CDUs only | **Any** thermal management products |
| Geographic scope | Territory only | **Anywhere in the world** |
| Duration | During term only | Term + **18 months** |
| Affiliate restriction | Not mentioned | Expressly included |
| Conduct restricted | Distribute, market, sell | Distribute, market, sell, **promote, solicit orders** |
| Air-side cooling carve-out | Express | **Absent** |

**Why it matters.** If Fubeix currently distributes third-party air-side cooling equipment (as the term sheet contemplated it would continue to do), Fubeix may be **in breach of §4.3 as of the Effective Date** (January 15, 2026). The non-compete is not limited to CDUs or to the Territory. "Thermal management products" is undefined in the Agreement and is reasonably read to encompass air-side cooling, liquid cooling, and related equipment. The worldwide scope means Fubeix cannot sell thermal management products from any other manufacturer in any market, including markets outside the five-state Territory.

This is also a significant concern in the context of the Ostrava transaction: if Draisath terminates under §13.3 (see Issue 2), the 18-month post-termination non-compete would still apply (§13.6 lists §4.3 as a surviving provision), potentially restricting Fubeix's operations for 18 months after termination.

**Recommended fix.**
- Confirm with the client whether Fubeix is currently distributing or selling any third-party thermal management products (including air-side cooling). If so, assess exposure and consider whether to raise the issue with Draisath as a drafting error or to seek a clarifying side letter.
- In any renegotiation or side letter (e.g., in connection with the CoC consent request), seek to restore the term-sheet scope: limit the non-compete to competing CDUs, limit geography to the Territory, and reinstate the air-side cooling carve-out.
- If the non-compete is enforced as written, evaluate enforceability under New York law. New York courts will enforce reasonable non-competes in the commercial context, but a worldwide restriction on "any thermal management products" for 18 months post-term may be challenged as overbroad, particularly given the term sheet's explicit narrowing.

**Needs client confirmation:**
- Is Fubeix currently distributing or selling any third-party thermal management products (air-side cooling or otherwise)? If so, which products, in which markets, and at what revenue level?
- Did the client flag this deviation during negotiation, or was it a surprise at signing?

## Issue 4 – Automatic Five-Year Renewal Replaces Mutual-Agreement Renewal

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §2.2; Term Sheet §2.2.

**Explanation.** The term sheet provided for "one-year renewal terms **by mutual written agreement**," with neither party obliged to agree. The final Agreement provides for **automatic** renewal for successive **five-year** terms, with the only opt-out being a written notice of non-renewal given during a **15-day window** ending **90 days before** the expiration of the then-current term.

For the Initial Term (ending January 14, 2029), the non-renewal notice window is approximately **October 2–16, 2028** (the 15-day period ending 90 days before January 14, 2029). If Fubeix misses that window, it is locked in for five additional years (through January 14, 2034), subject to the non-compete, minimum purchase, and all other obligations.

**Why it matters.** The narrow opt-out window creates a significant trap. Combined with the broad non-compete (Issue 3) and the minimum purchase commitment, an inadvertent failure to give timely non-renewal notice could commit Fubeix to a five-year extension under terms it may no longer want. The term sheet's mutual-agreement structure gave both parties a clean exit at each renewal; the final Agreement does not.

**Recommended fix.**
- Calendar the non-renewal notice deadline (approximately October 2, 2028) immediately, with internal reminders at 180, 120, and 60 days before the window opens.
- In any renegotiation (e.g., in connection with the CoC consent), consider seeking to amend §2.2 to restore a mutual-agreement renewal or to extend the opt-out window.

## Issue 5 – Side Letter Redefines "Products" to Mean Only CDUs for the Minimum Purchase Commitment

**Where:** Side Letter §§1.2, 2.1–2.3, 4.1; Distribution Agreement §§1.1, 5.1, 18.4.

**Explanation.** The Agreement defines "Products" to include CDUs, RDHx, and spare parts (§1.1). The Side Letter redefines "Products" in §1.2 to mean "solely the coolant distribution unit models listed in Exhibit A, Part 1." Section 2.3 then states that "Purchases of Products under accepted Purchase Orders count toward the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment." Section 4.1 provides that "References in the Agreement to the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment are references to that commitment as amended by this Side Letter."

Under the Agreement's order of precedence (§18.4), the Side Letter controls over the Agreement. The practical effect is that **only CDU purchases** count toward the $6,000,000 minimum. RDHx and spare parts purchases do not.

**Why it matters.** This is **favorable** to Fubeix: CDUs are the highest-value products, so Fubeix can meet the $6M minimum with fewer total dollars of mixed-product purchases. However, the drafting creates an ambiguity that Draisath could exploit:

- Draisath might argue that the Side Letter's redefinition of "Products" was intended only to set the dollar threshold (i.e., "we'll reduce the minimum to $6M, measured by CDU revenue, because CDUs are the main growth driver") and that for all other purposes (e.g., the exclusivity grant, the supply obligation, the warranty), "Products" retains its Agreement definition.
- Conversely, Fubeix should confirm that Draisath's obligation to supply and fill POs is not somehow limited to CDUs by the Side Letter's redefinition.

The more natural reading is that the redefinition applies only to the minimum purchase calculation, but the text is not perfectly clear.

**Recommended fix.**
- In the next side letter or amendment (e.g., in connection with the CoC consent), add a clarifying sentence: "For the avoidance of doubt, the definition of 'Products' in Section 1.2 of the Side Letter applies solely for purposes of calculating the Minimum Annual Purchase Commitment and does not modify the definition of 'Products' in Section 1.1 of the Agreement for any other purpose."
- Track CDU-only purchases separately from total purchases to ensure the $6M CDU threshold is met each Contract Year.

**Needs client confirmation:** What is Fubeix's current CDU-only purchase run rate for Contract Year 1 (Jan 15, 2026 – Jan 14, 2027)? Is Fubeix on track to meet $6M in CDU purchases by January 14, 2027?

## Issue 7 – NDA Term, Survival, and Interaction with the Agreement

**Where:** NDA §§7.1–7.2; Distribution Agreement §12.

**Explanation.** The NDA has a two-year term from October 6, 2025, expiring **October 6, 2027**. Confidentiality obligations survive for three years after expiration (until **October 6, 2030**), or indefinitely for trade secrets. The Agreement's confidentiality provision (§12.3) survives for three years after the Agreement's expiration or termination (at earliest, **January 14, 2032**).

The NDA's non-solicitation provision (§9.1) runs during the NDA term and for 12 months after (until **October 6, 2028**).

**Why it matters.** The NDA will expire while the Agreement is still in force. Information disclosed during the pre-contract negotiation phase (covered by the NDA) will lose NDA protection in October 2027, but may still be protected under the Agreement's §12 if it qualifies as "non-public business, technical and pricing information … received in connection with this Agreement." There is a potential gap: information shared during negotiations that is not shared again during the Agreement term may fall outside §12's scope after the NDA expires.

The non-solicitation restriction (until October 6, 2028) is relevant if Fubeix or Draisath wishes to hire from the other's team during that period.

**Recommended fix.**
- Low priority. Note the NDA expiration date (October 6, 2027) in the contract management file.
- If Fubeix anticipates needing to rely on NDA-protected information after October 2027, confirm that the information also falls within the Agreement's §12 definition.

## Issue 8 – Term Sheet Deviations: Pattern of Unfavorable Drift

**Where:** Term Sheet (various); Distribution Agreement (various).

**Explanation.** Beyond the specific issues identified above (non-compete, renewal), there is a pattern of the final Agreement being more favorable to Draisath than the term sheet:

| Provision | Term Sheet | Final Agreement |
|---|---|---|
| Renewal (§2.2) | Mutual agreement, 1-year terms | Automatic, 5-year terms |
| Non-compete (§4.3) | CDUs only, Territory only, during term | Any thermal management, worldwide, term + 18 months |

The term sheet is non-binding (§13.2), so Fubeix cannot enforce its terms. However, the pattern is relevant to:
- Any argument that the final Agreement's terms represent a material departure from the parties' agreed deal (potentially supporting a claim of misrepresentation or failure of essence, though this would be a difficult claim under New York law given the signed Agreement);
- Leverage in any future renegotiation;
- Context if the non-compete is challenged for overbreadth.

**Recommended fix.**
- Document the deviations in the file. If the CoC consent negotiation (Issue 2) opens a dialogue with Draisath, use the term sheet deviations as context to support requests for more balanced terms.
- Do not rely on the term sheet as a legal basis for any claim, but use it as a negotiating reference point.

## Issue 9 – Minimum Purchase Shortfall Consequences Are Severe

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §§5.1, 5.3, 13.2; Side Letter §§2.1–2.3.

**Explanation.** If Fubeix fails to meet the $6M CDU-only minimum for any Contract Year, Draisath may (within 90 days after year-end) either:
- (a) convert Fubeix's appointment to **non-exclusive** (eliminating the exclusivity protection in §4.1), or
- (b) **terminate** the Agreement on 60 days' notice.

There is no cure right, no grace period, and no requirement that the shortfall be material or that Draisath act reasonably.

**Why it matters.** Loss of exclusivity would be commercially devastating: Draisath could appoint competing distributors in the Territory, undermining Fubeix's customer relationships and investment. Termination would trigger the 18-month non-compete (Issue 3) and the 120-day sell-off period (§13.5(c)).

**Recommended fix.**
- Monitor CDU purchase progress quarterly (Draisath is obligated to provide quarterly statements under §5.2 / Side Letter §3).
- If Fubeix is tracking below $6M in CDU purchases at any quarter, escalate internally and consider whether to request a side letter amendment (e.g., a one-time reduction or a cure period) before year-end.
- Calendar the 90-day post-year-end window in which Draisath can exercise its rights, so Fubeix is prepared to respond if Draisath issues a notice.

**Needs client confirmation:** What is Fubeix's current CDU purchase total for Contract Year 1? What is the pipeline for the remainder of the year?

## Issue 10 – Section 6.2 Limitation: One Price Adjustment Per Product Per Contract Year

**Where:** Distribution Agreement §6.2.

**Explanation.** Section 6.2 limits Draisath to "no more than one Price Adjustment Notice in any Contract Year for the same Product." The July 1, 2026 notice covered all three CDU models. This constitutes the single permitted adjustment for CDUs in Contract Year 1 (Jan 15, 2026 – Jan 14, 2027).

**Why it matters.** This is a **protection** for Fubeix. Even if the current price increase is upheld (see Issue 1), Draisath cannot issue another CDU price increase until Contract Year 2 (on or after January 15, 2027). This gives Fubeix a 12-month window of price stability for CDUs.

**Recommended fix.**
- Note this protection in the contract management file.
- If Fubeix is in good-faith negotiation with Draisath over the current increase (Issue 1), this limitation strengthens Fubeix's position: even if the increase is sustained, it is a one-time event for the year.
