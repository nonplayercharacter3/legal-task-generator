### Issue 3: Unrestricted Transferability of the Seller Note

| | |
|---|---|
| **Document / Section** | Seller Note § 8(a)–(b); SPA § 1.1 ("Seller Note") |
| **Facts** | Note § 8(a): "Holder may sell, assign, transfer, pledge or negotiate this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker." There is no restriction on the identity of the transferee, no right of first refusal, no negative covenants, and no requirement that the transferee be a "permitted transferee." Note § 8(b) requires Maker to reissue notes upon partial transfer. |
| **Why it matters to Stipuv** | Seller (Braveirt) could sell the $7.5M note to any third party – a debt buyer, a fund, a competitor, or an affiliate of a competitor – and that party would become "Holder" with full rights under the Note, including the extensive **monthly information rights** in Note § 7 (financials, contract schedules, default notices). Stipuv has no consent right, no right to approve the transferee, and no ability to terminate the information flow. In the elevator services industry, customer contract data (counterparty, building location, annual value, expiration, renewal dates) is highly sensitive competitive information. |
| **Recommended action** | (1) Raise with Seller's counsel the possibility of amending Note § 8(a) to require Maker's consent (not to be unreasonably withheld) for any transfer, or at minimum to restrict transferees to financial institutions and their affiliates, excluding competitors in the elevator/vertical transportation space. (2) If Seller resists (and the note has already been delivered at Closing), assess whether the SPA's order-of-precedence clause (SPA § 11.9) or any implied covenant of good faith provides leverage. (3) As a practical matter, implement internal information-security protocols so that the monthly contract schedule required by Note § 7(b) is prepared on a need-to-know basis and transmitted only to the designated Holder address. (4) Monitor for any notice of transfer under Note § 8(a) (10 Business Days' notice required). |
| **Further information needed** | Whether the note has been or is being pledged as collateral by Seller; any side agreements between the parties regarding the note. |

### Issue 10: SPA Section 6.8 – Tension Between "Reasonable Efforts" to Release Seller Guarantees and Operational Constraints

| | |
|---|---|
| **Document / Section** | SPA § 6.8; SPA § 1.1 ("Seller Guarantees") |
| **Facts** | SPA § 6.8 requires Buyer to use "reasonable efforts" to cause the Company to obtain release of Seller from the Seller Guarantees, including by offering replacement guarantees or bonds. However, the same section provides: "Buyer shall not, and shall cause the Company not to, extend, renew or amend any contract supported by a Seller Guarantee in a manner that increases Seller's obligations under that Seller Guarantee without Seller's prior written consent." |
| **Why it matters to Stipuv** | This creates an operational constraint: if Lockwen wishes to renew or expand a customer contract that is currently supported by a Seller Guarantee, it cannot do so in a way that increases the guarantee amount without Seller's consent. (2) For contracts coming up for renewal, prepare replacement guarantee or bond proposals in advance. (3) If a contract renewal would increase the guarantee scope, seek Seller's written consent early to avoid delays. (4) Document all "reasonable efforts" to obtain releases (correspondence, proposals, quotes) to create a record that protects against a future claim of covenant breach. |
| **Further information needed** | Schedule 6.8; the terms and renewal dates of the underlying contracts; quotes for replacement guarantees/bonds from surety companies. |
