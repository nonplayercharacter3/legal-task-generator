### 1. CRITICAL – Likely Missed Notice of Disagreement Deadline (SPA § 2.4(b))

**Documents:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 2.4(b); buyer_managers_written_consent.md, Recital C and Resolution 2.

**Facts:** SPA § 2.4(a) required Seller to deliver the Closing Statement within 45 days after the June 30, 2026 Closing. SPA § 2.4(b) gives Buyer **30 days after receipt** of the Closing Statement to deliver a Notice of Disagreement; absent timely notice, "the Closing Statement shall be final and binding on the parties." The Managers' Consent (dated **September 14, 2026**) states in Recital C that the Company **received the Closing Statement on August 4, 2026** and that "The Company has not yet delivered a Notice of Disagreement to Seller." Thirty days from August 4 is **September 3, 2026**. The Consent is dated eleven days after that deadline and confirms no Notice has been sent.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** If the deadline has in fact passed without a Notice being delivered, the Closing Statement is final and binding. Stipuv would be obligated under SPA § 2.4(d) to pay the $1,240,000 surplus to Seller within five Business Days of "final determination"—which, if the statement is deemed final by operation of § 2.4(b), is arguably already triggered. This is a seven-figure exposure on a disputed accounting item (unearned deferred revenue from prepaid maintenance contracts).

**Recommended fix:**
- **Immediately** confirm with the client whether a Notice of Disagreement was in fact delivered on or before September 3, 2026, even if the Consent (dated September 14) says otherwise. The Consent may have been drafted before final confirmation.
- If no Notice was delivered, assess whether Seller has already demanded payment or taken other action. If Seller has not yet demanded payment, there is an argument that the parties can agree to waive the deadline or treat the dispute as preserved, but this is uncertain under New York law and depends on whether Seller asserts the bar.
- If a Notice **was** delivered before September 3, obtain a copy and confirm the delivery method satisfies SPA § 11.2 (email before 5:00 p.m. ET on a Business Day, or overnight courier).
- Regardless of outcome, the client should be advised that the internal governance process (Board Consent dated September 14) created a documentation gap that could undermine Stipuv's position if Seller argues the deadline was missed.

**Client attention flag:** This requires the client's immediate response. If the deadline was missed, we need to decide whether to (a) pay the $1,240,000, (b) negotiate a waiver with Seller, or (c) assert that the deadline was not missed (if there is evidence of earlier delivery). The cost of inaction is high.

### 2. HIGH – Seller Note Freely Transferable Without Maker's Consent (Note § 8(a))

**Document:** seller_promissory_note.md, § 8(a).

**Facts:** "Holder may sell, assign, transfer, pledge or negotiate this Note, in whole or in part, to any Person without the consent of Maker." The only requirement is that Holder give Maker written notice of the transfer within ten Business Days.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** As maker of the $7,500,000 Note, Stipuv cannot control who holds it. Seller could sell the Note to a distressed-debt fund, a competitor, or any other party. The transferee would step into Seller's shoes with all rights under the Note, including the right to declare acceleration upon an Event of Default (Note § 10(a)) and to collect attorneys' fees (Note § 10(c)). Stipuv also loses the benefit of any informal forbearance or flexibility it might have negotiated with Seller directly.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to Note § 8(a) adding a **negative covenants list** (e.g., no transfer to a competitor in the elevator/vertical transportation industry, no transfer to a Person that is a direct competitor of the Company) or a **consent right** for transfers to Affiliates of Seller or to any Person other than a financial institution.
- If Seller will not agree to a consent right, at minimum seek a requirement that any transferee execute a joinder agreeing to be bound by the subordination provisions (Note § 6) and the set-off provisions (Note § 5) and that the transfer not relieve Seller of its indemnification obligations under the SPA.
- Confirm that SPA § 11.9 (order of precedence) preserves Stipuv's set-off rights under SPA § 9.7 even if the Note is transferred, since the set-off right is granted in the SPA in favor of "Buyer Indemnitees."

### 3. HIGH – Short Survival Period for Non-Fundamental Representations (SPA § 9.1)

**Document:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 9.1.

**Facts:** "All other representations and warranties contained in this Agreement shall survive the Closing until **six (6) months** after the Closing Date." The Closing Date is June 30, 2026, so non-Fundamental Representations expire **December 30, 2026**. Only Fundamental Representations (organization, authority, capitalization, brokers) survive until 60 days after the applicable statute of limitations.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** The representations most likely to give rise to post-closing claims in an elevator services business—financial statements (§ 4.5), undisclosed liabilities (§ 4.6), absence of material adverse changes (§ 4.7), elevator code compliance (§ 4.12), litigation (§ 4.13), taxes (§ 4.14), employees and labor (§ 4.15), insurance (§ 4.17), and environmental matters (§ 4.18)—all expire in six months. Elevator code violations, latent mechanical issues, and tax audits often surface after six months. After December 30, 2026, Stipuv's sole remedy for most breaches of Seller's representations is gone (subject to the Fraud and Fundamental Representation carve-outs in § 9.5(c)).

**Recommended fix:**
- If the deal has not yet closed (the documents suggest it has, but if there is any amendment opportunity), negotiate to extend survival for § 4.5 (Financial Statements), § 4.6 (Undisclosed Liabilities), § 4.12 (Elevator Code Compliance), and § 4.14 (Taxes) to **18 months** or the applicable statute of limitations.
- If the deal has closed and the SPA cannot be amended, ensure that all potential claims are identified and Claim Notices delivered **before December 30, 2026**. Build a claims calendar now.
- Note that SPA § 9.4(a) requires a Claim Notice before the survival period expires; failure to give notice is not a complete bar (it only relieves the indemnifying party to the extent of material prejudice), but it is a significant risk.

**Client attention flag:** The client should be aware that the window to assert most indemnification claims closes in approximately three months. A claims identification exercise should begin immediately.

### 4. HIGH – Ongoing Monthly CFO Certification Obligation (Note § 7(a))

**Document:** seller_promissory_note.md, § 7(a).

**Facts:** Until the Note is paid in full (i.e., through December 31, 2029), Maker must deliver, within 30 days after each month-end, the Company's unaudited balance sheet and income/cash flow statements, "certified by the **chief financial officer of Maker** as fairly presenting in all material respects the financial condition of the Company."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** This is a personal certification by Stipuv's CFO (not the Company's CFO—note the distinction: "CFO of Maker"). If Stipuv does not currently have a CFO, or if its CFO is not comfortable making a monthly "fairly presenting" certification on a company they may not know well, this creates (a) a potential ongoing Event of Default under Note § 9(b) (failure to perform any other obligation, continuing 30 days after notice), and (b) personal professional exposure for the individual making the certification.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm that Stipuv has a CFO in place who is willing and able to make the monthly certification.
- If the CFO is also the Company's CFO, clarify in writing that the certification is made in their capacity as Maker's officer.
- Consider whether the standard "fairly presenting in all material respects" is appropriate for monthly unaudited financials or whether "presented in accordance with GAAP, subject to normal year-end adjustments" (the standard used in SPA § 4.5 for interim statements) would be more appropriate. If an amendment is feasible, propose the latter.
- Calendar the monthly delivery obligation and assign internal responsibility.

### 5. HIGH – Event of Default for Sale of Shares or Substantially All Assets (Note § 9(d))

**Document:** seller_promissory_note.md, § 9(d).

**Facts:** "Maker sells or otherwise disposes of all or substantially all of the Shares or of the assets of the Company, unless this Note is paid in full at or before that disposition" is an Event of Default.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** This is a change-of-control / asset-sale restriction that could prevent Stipuv from (a) selling Lockwen to a strategic buyer, (b) merging the Company into another subsidiary, or (c) liquidating the Company's assets in a restructuring, without first paying off the entire $7.5M Note (plus accrued interest). Given that the Note matures in 2029, this could constrain Stipuv's exit strategy for up to three and a half years.

**Recommended fix:**
- Negotiate an amendment to carve out: (i) a sale to an Affiliate of Maker; (ii) a merger in which Maker is the surviving entity; (iii) a sale in which the buyer assumes the Note in writing; or (iv) a sale where the proceeds are sufficient to pay the Note in full (which is already covered, but the "substantially all assets" language is broader than a stock sale).
- If no amendment is possible, the client should factor this constraint into any strategic planning for the Lockwen asset.

### 6. MODERATE – Seller Guarantees: Ongoing Operational Restrictions (SPA § 6.8)

**Document:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 6.8.

**Facts:** Post-Closing, Buyer must use "reasonable efforts" to cause the Company to obtain release of Seller and its Affiliates from the Seller Guarantees (performance bonds, surety bonds, parent guarantees under elevator contracts). Until released, Seller must maintain them. Critically: "Buyer shall not, and shall cause the Company not to, **extend, renew or amend** any contract supported by a Seller Guarantee in a manner that increases Seller's obligations under that Seller Guarantee **without Seller's prior written consent**."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** The Company's revenue is derived from maintenance and service contracts with building owners. Many of these contracts are supported by Seller Guarantees (listed in Schedule 6.8, which I do not have). If the Company wants to renew a contract with increased scope, higher penalties, or a longer term, it needs Seller's consent. This creates an ongoing operational dependency on Seller and could delay or prevent contract renewals. It also means Seller retains leverage over the Company's business decisions for as long as the guarantees remain in place.

**Recommended fix:**
- Obtain Schedule 6.8 (list of Seller Guarantees) and identify which contracts are affected, when they expire, and what the release process will look like.
- Develop a plan to replace Seller Guarantees with Company or Parent guarantees/bonds as quickly as possible. The broker (Pellucid) may be able to arrange surety bonds in the Company's or Stipuv's name.
- If the replacement process will take time, negotiate with Seller for a defined period (e.g., 12 months) after which the consent requirement in § 6.8 falls away, or for a mechanism that deems consent given if Seller does not respond within a specified period.
- Note that SPA § 9.3(b) makes Buyer indemnify Seller for any Seller Guarantee, including any demand or draw. This is an uncapped indemnity (excluded from the cap and basket under § 9.5(c)). Stipuv is fully exposed for any claim under a Seller Guarantee that is not released.

### 8. MODERATE – No Confirmation of Seller's Occurrence-Based Insurance Policies (SPA § 6.9; Broker Letter)

**Documents:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 6.9; broker_insurance_certificate_cover_letter.md, §§ 2–4.

**Facts:** SPA § 6.9 requires Seller to "reasonably cooperate with Buyer and the Company in pursuing claims arising from pre-Closing occurrences under any **occurrence-based insurance policy of Seller** under which the Company was an insured." The broker's cover letter (dated July 10, 2026) addresses only the Company's own policies (CGL, auto, umbrella, workers' comp, professional liability, pollution liability, disability/paid family leave). It does not reference any Seller-level occurrence-based policy under which the Company was an additional insured.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** In the elevator industry, a single serious injury or property damage claim can generate significant liability. If a pre-Closing occurrence gives rise to a claim after the Closing, Stipuv/Company would want to pursue the Seller's occurrence-based policy. Without confirmation that such a policy exists, remains in force, and will honor claims, Stipuv may be relying on an insurance asset that is not there. The SPA representation in § 4.17 covers the Company's own policies, not Seller's.

**Recommended fix:**
- Request from Seller (or confirm in the Disclosure Schedule, § 4.17) a list of all occurrence-based insurance policies of Seller or its Affiliates under which the Company was named as an insured or additional insured.
- Obtain copies of those policies and confirm they remain in force and that the insurer has acknowledged the change of ownership.
- If no such policies exist, note that Stipuv's protection for pre-Closing occurrences is limited to the indemnification provisions of SPA Article 9 (subject to the cap, basket, and survival period).

### 9. MODERATE – Indemnification Basket Is Relatively High (SPA § 9.5(b))

**Document:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 9.5(b).

**Facts:** "Seller shall not be liable under Section 9.2(a) unless and until the aggregate Losses in respect of all claims thereunder exceed **$485,000**, in which event Seller shall be liable for all such Losses from the first dollar."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** The basket equals 1% of the Purchase Price. This is a tipping basket (once crossed, Seller is liable from dollar one), which is favorable, but the threshold itself is high. Stipuv absorbs the first $485,000 of losses from breaches of Seller's representations. In a business with 1,000 shares, union labor, and regulatory compliance obligations, it is not unusual to discover multiple small representation breaches (e.g., a minor code violation, a small tax item, a contract technicality) that individually are below the basket but collectively exceed it. The risk is that Stipuv discovers issues totaling $400,000 and has no recourse.

**Recommended fix:**
- If the SPA can still be amended (unlikely post-closing), negotiate to reduce the basket to 0.5% ($242,500) or convert it to a deductible (where Seller is liable only for excess over the basket, not from dollar one).
- If the SPA is closed, ensure that the claims identification process (see Issue 3) is thorough so that all potential claims are aggregated before the survival period expires.

### 10. MODERATE – Key Employee Agreement Listed as Seller Closing Deliverable (SPA § 3.2(e))

**Document:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 3.2(e).

**Facts:** SPA § 3.2 requires **Seller** to deliver at Closing "the Key Employee Agreement, duly executed by Darnell Okafor-Wyse." However, the Key Employee Agreement is between the **Company** (Lockwen) and Okafor-Wyse, with Stipuv (Parent) joining only for §§ 5(d) and 13. Seller (Braveirt) is not a party to the agreement.

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** This is a drafting anomaly. If the Closing has already occurred (as the Managers' Consent and broker letter confirm), the practical issue is limited. However, if there is any dispute about whether the Closing conditions were satisfied, Seller could argue it fulfilled its obligation by delivering the document, while Stipuv could argue that the proper party to deliver (or to have caused to be delivered) was the Company or Buyer. More importantly, if Okafor-Wyse's signature was not actually obtained at Closing, there could be an argument that a closing condition was not met, which could affect the validity of the transaction or give rise to a claim.

**Recommended fix:**
- Confirm that Okafor-Wyse actually signed the Key Employee Agreement at or before Closing. Obtain the executed copy for the file.
- If there is any question, consider whether a confirmatory signature or ratification is appropriate.
- Note for future transactions: this should have been a Company deliverable or a condition to Closing rather than a Seller deliverable.

### 11. LOWER – Note Subordination Is Broadly Defined (Note § 6(a))

**Document:** seller_promissory_note.md, § 6(a).

**Facts:** "Senior Indebtedness" means "all obligations of Maker or the Company for borrowed money owed to any bank, institutional lender or **other financial institution**, whether outstanding on the date of this Note or incurred later."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** This is actually **favorable** to Stipuv as maker. It means that if Stipuv or the Company obtains bank financing (e.g., a working capital line, term loan, or equipment financing), the Seller Note is automatically subordinated. Stipuv can take on senior debt without triggering a default under the Note or needing Seller's consent. The main risk is that the definition of "other financial institution" is broad and could potentially capture non-traditional lenders (e.g., a venture debt fund or a mezzanine lender). If Stipuv plans to use any non-bank financing, confirm that the lender qualifies as a "financial institution" under this definition.

**Recommended fix:** No action needed unless Stipuv plans to use a non-traditional lender. If so, confirm the subordination language works for that lender's requirements, and note that § 6(c) requires Holder to execute a subordination agreement with the senior lender on "terms customary for seller financing."

### 12. LOWER – Insurance Certificates Are Informational Only (Broker Letter §§ 5.1–5.2)

**Document:** broker_insurance_certificate_cover_letter.md, §§ 5.1–5.2.

**Facts:** "Each certificate is issued as a matter of information only. It confers no rights on the certificate holder." "A certificate does not amend, extend or alter the coverage provided by the policies described in it."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** Standard broker language, but it means Stipuv should not rely on the certificates alone to confirm coverage. The actual policy terms, conditions, and exclusions control. In particular, Stipuv should verify: (a) that the additional insured endorsements are properly worded to cover Stipuv for the Company's operations; (b) that the policy limits are adequate for the Company's risk profile; and (c) that there are no exclusions that would undermine coverage for elevator-related claims (e.g., professional services exclusions, contractual liability exclusions).

**Recommended fix:**
- Request copies of the actual policies and endorsements from Pellucid (the broker letter confirms they are "available on request" per § 5.3).
- Have insurance counsel review the CGL, umbrella, and professional liability policies for coverage gaps relevant to the elevator business.
- Confirm that the workers' compensation coverage meets New York State requirements (the Form C-105.2 and DB-120.1 are enclosed, but verify the coverage is at the statutory level).

### 13. LOWER – Transfer Taxes Split Equally (SPA § 8.2)

**Document:** stock_purchase_agreement.md, § 8.2.

**Facts:** "All transfer, documentary, sales, use, stamp and similar Taxes arising from the transfer of the Shares shall be borne **equally** by Seller and Buyer."

**Why it matters to Stipuv:** In New York, stock transfers generally do not trigger transfer taxes (unlike real property transfers). However, if the Company held any real property interests (the SPA § 4.10 says it does not own real property, only leases), or if there are any documentary stamps on the Note, the tax could be non-trivial. The equal split is standard but worth confirming that no unexpected transfer tax applies.

**Recommended fix:** Confirm with tax counsel that no New York transfer tax or documentary stamp tax applies to the stock transfer or the Note. If any applies, ensure it was paid at Closing and properly allocated.
