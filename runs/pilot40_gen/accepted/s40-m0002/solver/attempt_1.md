## Summary

Review of the Asset Purchase Agreement ("APA"), Bill of Sale, Letter of Intent ("LOI"), and closing logistics email thread reveals three critical issues that should be resolved before we go back to Seller's counsel, several significant gaps, and a number of secondary items. The three critical issues are: (1) the Bill of Sale's redefined "Purchased Assets" term excludes the Kestrin Warehouse inventory and likely other off-site assets; (2) the Pellwyn consent is missing from Schedule 4.4 despite being identified in the LOI as required; and (3) the Bill of Sale's exclusive jurisdiction clause conflicts with the APA's and, under the order-of-precedence provision, would control title disputes.

## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Bill of Sale Excludes Kestrin Warehouse Inventory from the Conveyance

**Where:** Bill of Sale, Section 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA, Section 10.4 (Order of Precedence); APA, Section 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA, Schedule 4.7.

**Issue.** The APA defines "Purchased Assets" broadly: "all assets used in the Business, **wherever located**, including inventory at third-party warehouses" (APA § 1.1, "Purchased Assets" definition, introductory text). Subsection (a) expressly includes "Inventory held at the Seller Facility and at the Kestrin Warehouse." Schedule 4.7 confirms two inventory locations: Fort Collins and Reno.

The Bill of Sale, however, redefines "Purchased Assets" as "all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under the assets used in the Business **that are located at the Seller Facility**" (Bill of Sale § 1.1). This geographic limitation excludes the Kestrin Warehouse inventory (finished test kits and packaged reagents in Reno, Nevada) from the conveyance.

The problem is compounded by APA § 10.4, which provides that "the Bill of Sale shall control the conveyance of title and any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets." Because the Bill of Sale is the operative conveyance instrument for tangible assets and it controls title disputes, the narrower definition in the Bill of Sale likely governs what actually passes to Soulor.

**Why it matters.** The Kestrin Warehouse holds finished-kit inventory that is part of the value Soulor is paying for. If the Bill of Sale does not convey that inventory, Soulor pays $18.4 million but does not receive title to a component of the Purchased Assets. Soulor would be left with an indemnification claim against Seller (subject to the $184,000 threshold and $2,760,000 cap in APA § 8.4) rather than actual title.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Bill of Sale § 1.1 to conform to the APA definition. Proposed language:

> "**Purchased Assets**" means all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under the assets used in the Business, **wherever located**, including:
>
> (a) all Inventory, including raw materials, reagents, work-in-process, finished test kits, packaging and supplies, **whether held at the Seller Facility, at the Kestrin Warehouse, or at any other location identified on Schedule 4.7 to the Purchase Agreement**;
>
> (b)–(d) [unchanged]

Alternatively, if Seller resists a wholesale conforming edit, add a sentence after the list in Bill of Sale § 1.1: "For the avoidance of doubt, the Purchased Assets include all Inventory held at the Kestrin Warehouse (Reno, Nevada) as described in Schedule 4.7 to the Purchase Agreement."

### 2. Pellwyn Consent Missing from Schedule 4.4 (Required Consents)

**Where:** APA, Schedule 4.4; APA, § 4.4 (Consents rep); APA, § 6.3 (Consents covenant); APA, § 7.2(c) (closing condition); LOI, § 5.1; APA, Schedule 1.1(c) (item 1).

**Issue.** The Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement with Pellwyn Reagents, Inc. is an Assigned Contract (Schedule 1.1(c), item 1). The LOI states explicitly: "Under the Pellwyn supply agreement, Pellwyn prior written consent is required for assignment, including by asset sale or change of control" (LOI § 5.1). Seller "has advised that it has not previously sought Pellwyn's consent to any assignment" (LOI § 5.1).

Despite this, Schedule 4.4 lists only one Required Consent: the Fort Collins landlord consent. The Pellwyn consent is absent.

The consequences of this omission are significant:

- **No closing condition.** APA § 7.2(c) makes closing conditional on Seller obtaining "each of the Required Consents." Because Pellwyn consent is not a Required Consent, Soulor has no contractual right to refuse to close if it has not been obtained.
- **No covenant to obtain.** APA § 6.3 requires Seller to use "reasonable best efforts to obtain each of the Required Consents." The Pellwyn consent is not covered.
- **False representation.** APA § 4.4 represents that "[e]xcept for the Required Consents set forth on Schedule 4.4, no consent, approval or notice of or to any Person is required." If Pellwyn consent is in fact required, this representation is false as of the signing date.
- **Not tracked in closing logistics.** The email thread and closing checklist make no reference to Pellwyn consent.

**Why it matters.** According to the LOI, Pellwyn is "the sole source of the reagents used in Seller's flagship CanineScreen panel" (LOI § 5.1). If the assignment of the Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement is ineffective for lack of consent, Soulor loses its reagent supply for its core product line. This goes to the heart of the Business's value.

**Recommended fix.**

(a) Add the following item to Schedule 4.4:

> 2. Consent of Pellwyn Reagents, Inc. to the assignment of the Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement to Buyer, in form and substance reasonably satisfactory to Buyer.

(b) Confirm with the client whether Pellwyn consent has been sought or obtained. The LOI (dated July 22, 2026) states Seller had not previously sought consent. The APA was signed September 14, 2026. The email thread (through December 8, 2026) does not mention it. **Question for client:** Has Seller obtained or committed to obtain Pellwyn consent? If not, this must be a condition to closing and we should not proceed to December 15 without it.

(c) If Pellwyn consent is obtained, add delivery of the executed consent to Seller's closing deliverables in APA § 3.2(d) (which currently covers "copies of the Required Consents" and would pick it up automatically once added to Schedule 4.4).

### 3. Bill of Sale Jurisdiction Clause Conflicts with APA and Controls Title Disputes

**Where:** Bill of Sale, § 7.2; APA, § 10.9; APA, § 10.4.

**Issue.** The APA provides for exclusive jurisdiction in "the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware" (APA § 10.9). The Bill of Sale provides that "[a]ny action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Bill of Sale, or to the conveyance of the Purchased Assets, must be brought exclusively in the District Court for Larimer County, Colorado" (Bill of Sale § 7.2).

Under APA § 10.4, the Bill of Sale "shall control the conveyance of title and any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets." A dispute over whether Soulor received valid title to the Kestrin inventory (see Issue 1 above) or to any other asset would be a "claim concerning title to the Purchased Assets." The Bill of Sale's jurisdiction clause would therefore require Soulor to litigate in a Colorado county court rather than in Delaware Chancery.

**Why it matters.** Delaware Chancery is a sophisticated commercial court with well-developed equitable remedies. Larimer County District Court is a general trial court. For a dispute involving $18.4 million in assets and complex title questions, the forum matters. Additionally, the APA's specific performance provision (§ 10.11) and jury trial waiver (§ 10.10) are in the Delaware clause; the Bill of Sale has no equivalent provisions, creating uncertainty about whether those protections apply in a title dispute.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Bill of Sale § 7.2 to conform to the APA. Proposed language:

> **7.2 Jurisdiction.** Any action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Bill of Sale, or to the conveyance of the Purchased Assets, must be brought exclusively in the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware. Seller, and Buyer by its acceptance of this Bill of Sale, each irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of that court and waives any objection to venue there. Each party irrevocably waives any right to trial by jury in any such action.

Alternatively, add to APA § 10.4 a clarifying sentence: "Notwithstanding the foregoing, the jurisdiction and governing law provisions of this Agreement (Sections 10.8 and 10.9) shall apply to all disputes, including disputes concerning title to the Purchased Assets."

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 4. Bill of Sale Governed by Colorado Law; APA Governed by Delaware Law

**Where:** Bill of Sale, § 7.1; APA, § 10.8.

**Issue.** The Bill of Sale is governed by Colorado law; the APA is governed by Delaware law. Because the Bill of Sale controls title disputes (APA § 10.4), a title claim would be analyzed under Colorado law while the underlying contractual rights (reps, indemnity, survival) are governed by Delaware law. This creates interpretive friction and potential for inconsistent outcomes.

**Recommended fix.** Conform Bill of Sale § 7.1 to Delaware law, consistent with the APA. If Seller insists on Colorado law for the Bill of Sale (perhaps because the real property is in Colorado), at minimum add a savings clause: "To the extent any provision of this Bill of Sale is inconsistent with the Purchase Agreement, the Purchase Agreement shall control except as provided in Section 10.4 thereof."

### 5. No Purchase Price Adjustment for Inventory Normalization

**Where:** LOI, § 3; APA, § 2.5.

**Issue.** The LOI states: "The Purchase Price assumes delivery of inventory at normalized levels consistent with Seller's trailing twelve-month average and the absence of any material adverse change in the Business" (LOI § 3). The APA contains a fixed Purchase Price of $18,400,000 with no adjustment mechanism for inventory levels (APA § 2.5).

The LOI is non-binding (LOI § 12), so its terms do not automatically carry into the APA. However, the inventory normalization concept reflects the commercial basis on which the price was set. If inventory at closing is materially below the trailing twelve-month average, Soulor pays the full price for less inventory.

**Why it matters.** In a diagnostics business, inventory (reagents, finished kits) is a significant component of enterprise value. A shortfall could be material.

**Question for client:** Did the parties intend to include a working capital or inventory adjustment in the APA? If so, it was omitted. If the fixed price was a deliberate negotiation outcome, no action is needed, but the client should confirm it understands there is no adjustment.

**Recommended fix (if client confirms adjustment was intended):** Add a new APA § 2.5(b) or § 2.8:

> **2.8 Inventory Adjustment.** The Purchase Price shall be adjusted on a dollar-for-dollar basis to the extent that the fair market value of Inventory included in the Purchased Assets as of the Closing Date is greater or less than $[X] (the "Target Inventory Level"), which Target Inventory Level shall be consistent with Seller's trailing twelve-month average inventory as of September 30, 2026. The Parties shall agree on the Target Inventory Level within ten (10) Business Days after the Closing Date. Any adjustment shall be paid by the Party owing within thirty (30) days after agreement or, if the Parties cannot agree, determined by an independent appraiser selected by mutual agreement.

## MODERATE ISSUES

### 8. Officer's Certificate Does Not Cover MAE Condition

**Where:** APA, § 3.2(e); APA, § 7.2(d).

**Issue.** The officer's certificate required at closing certifies only the satisfaction of conditions in § 7.2(a) (bring-down of reps) and § 7.2(b) (performance of covenants). It does not certify § 7.2(d) (no Material Adverse Effect). The MAE condition is therefore left to Soulor's own judgment at the closing call.

**Why it matters.** In practice this is manageable because the MAE is also covered by the bring-down of Seller's representation in § 4.13 (no MAE since December 31, 2025), which is certified via § 7.2(a). However, the § 4.13 rep looks back to December 31, 2025, while § 7.2(d) looks back to the signing date (September 14, 2026). There is a gap: an MAE occurring between September 14 and the Closing Date would be captured by § 7.2(d) but not directly by § 4.13 (which covers the period from December 31, 2025 forward). In practice, § 4.13 as brought down to closing does cover the period through closing, so the gap is theoretical. Still, for a clean closing, consider expanding the certificate.

**Recommended fix.** If the client wants additional comfort, amend § 3.2(e) to read: "a certificate of an officer of Seller certifying the satisfaction of the conditions in Sections 7.2(a), 7.2(b), and 7.2(d)."

### 9. Bulk Sales Waiver – Third-Party Creditors May Not Be Bound

**Where:** APA, § 6.9.

**Issue.** The parties waive compliance with bulk sales laws, and Seller indemnifies Buyer for resulting losses. However, bulk sales statutes (e.g., former UCC Article 6, still in effect in some states) exist to protect Seller's creditors. A waiver between Buyer and Seller does not bind Seller's creditors. If a creditor of Diseil asserts a bulk sales claim against the transferred assets, Soulor could be subject to a lien or recovery action, and the indemnity from Seller (a potentially dissolving LLC post-closing) may be of limited practical value.

**Why it matters.** Colorado repealed its bulk sales statute (former C.R.S. § 4-6-401 et seq.) effective 2001. Nevada (where the Kestrin Warehouse is located) also no longer has a bulk sales statute. If the Business operates only in Colorado and Nevada, this risk is minimal. **Question for client:** Does the Business operate in any jurisdiction that still has a bulk sales statute? If not, this item is low risk.

**Recommended fix.** If the client confirms no applicable bulk sales jurisdiction, no change needed. Otherwise, consider requiring Seller to provide a list of its creditors and a covenant that Seller will notify them of the transaction.

### 10. IP Representation Is Not a Fundamental Rep

**Where:** APA, § 4.9; APA, § 8.1.

**Issue.** The IP representation (§ 4.9) survives only 18 months (APA § 8.1). It is not one of the Fundamental Representations (§§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.5). For a diagnostics business whose value is heavily dependent on assay IP (protocols, formulations, validation data, the CanineScreen brand), an 18-month survival may be insufficient if an IP defect or third-party infringement claim surfaces later.

**Why it matters.** Patent and trade secret claims can take years to mature. An 18-month window may expire before Soulor discovers a material IP problem.

**Recommended fix.** Consider adding § 4.9 to the list of Fundamental Representations in § 8.1, or at minimum extending the survival period for § 4.9 to the full statute of limitations for IP infringement. If Seller resists, a compromise is 36 months for the IP rep.
