### 1. Pellwyn Consent Missing from Schedule 4.4 (Required Consents)

**Where:** APA Schedule 4.4; APA § 4.4; APA § 7.2(c); LOI § 5.1; Closing Email Thread (Messages 1–7, no mention of Pellwyn).

**Issue:** Schedule 4.4 lists only one Required Consent: the landlord's consent to the Fort Collins Laboratory Lease. Because the Pellwyn consent is not on Schedule 4.4:

- The covenant in § 6.3 (Seller to use "reasonable best efforts" to obtain Required Consents) does not reach the Pellwyn consent.
- The closing checklist (Email, Message 3) tracks only the landlord consent under item (d).

**Why it matters:** Without a valid assignment of the Pellwyn supply agreement, Soulor may lose its sole source of reagents for the CanineScreen panel—the flagship product. This goes to the heart of the business value. Post-closing, Soulor would have an indemnity claim for breach of § 4.4, but that claim is subject to the $184,000 basket and (because § 4.4 is not a Fundamental Representation) the $2,760,000 cap in § 8.4. More practically, Soulor would be operating the business without its critical supply agreement.

### 2. Bill of Sale Defines "Purchased Assets" More Narrowly Than the APA – Title Gap for Off-Site Assets

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA § 10.4 (Order of Precedence).

**Issue:** The APA defines "Purchased Assets" as "all assets used in the Business, **wherever located**, including inventory at third-party warehouses" (APA § 1.1). The Bill of Sale redefines "Purchased Assets" as "all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under the assets used in the Business that are **located at the Seller Facility**" (Bill of Sale § 1.1). The Seller Facility is defined in both documents as the Fort Collins, Colorado premises.

APA § 10.4 provides: "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Document, this Agreement shall prevail, **except that the Bill of Sale shall control the conveyance of title and any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets**."

The result: for purposes of title, the Bill of Sale's geographically limited definition governs. Inventory at the Kestrin Warehouse in Reno, Nevada (Schedule 4.7, item 2), and any other tangible assets not physically located in Fort Collins at closing, are **not conveyed** by the Bill of Sale. No other closing deliverable specifically conveys that inventory. The Assignment and Assumption Agreement covers the Kestrin *contract* (the warehousing agreement), not the *inventory* stored there.

**Why it matters:** Soulor would pay $18.4 million for assets it does not receive clear title to. The Kestrin Warehouse holds "finished test kits and packaged reagents" (Schedule 4.7). If those kits are material to revenue (and as finished product, they likely are), Soulor has a significant title gap on day one.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend Bill of Sale § 1.1 to conform to the APA definition. Proposed language:

  > "**Purchased Assets**" means all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under all assets used in the Business, **wherever located**, including Inventory held at the Seller Facility and at the Kestrin Warehouse, and including the items described in clauses (a) through (g) of the definition of "Purchased Assets" in Section 1.1 of the Purchase Agreement.

- Alternatively, add a separate schedule to the Bill of Sale listing all locations from which assets are conveyed.
- This should be raised with Seller's counsel immediately, as the Bill of Sale is in "agreed form" (Exhibit A) and is a closing deliverable.

### 3. No Inventory Adjustment Mechanism Despite LOI's Normalized-Levels Assumption

**Where:** LOI § 3; APA § 2.5; APA § 4.7; APA § 6.1(a).

**Issue:** LOI § 3 states: "The Purchase Price assumes delivery of inventory at normalized levels consistent with Seller's trailing twelve-month average and the absence of any material adverse change in the Business." The APA contains no purchase price adjustment mechanism (no working capital target, no inventory true-up, no post-closing adjustment). Meanwhile, § 6.1(a) permits Seller to sell inventory in the ordinary course between signing and closing.

**Why it matters:** Seller can lawfully sell through inventory during the ~3-month pre-closing period. If inventory at closing is materially below trailing twelve-month average, Soulor pays the full $18.4 million for a depleted inventory position. Soulor's only recourse would be:
- A claim under § 4.7 (inventory is "usable and saleable in the ordinary course")—but a reduction in quantity, without quality issues, may not breach this rep.
- A claim under § 4.13 (no MAE since December 31, 2025)—but ordinary-course inventory sales are unlikely to constitute an MAE.
- A claim under § 6.1 (ordinary course)—but selling inventory *is* ordinary course.

In short, the economic assumption in the LOI was not implemented in the APA, leaving Soulor exposed.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add an inventory adjustment provision. Proposed language for a new § 2.5(c):

  > "(c) **Inventory Adjustment.** The Purchase Price shall be adjusted as follows. Within 30 days after the Closing Date, Buyer shall prepare a statement of Inventory on hand as of the Closing Date (the 'Closing Inventory'), valued at the lower of cost or net realizable value, and shall deliver it to Seller together with supporting detail. If the aggregate value of Closing Inventory is less than $[target amount] (the 'Target Inventory Level,' to be agreed by the Parties and set forth on Schedule 2.5), Seller shall pay to Buyer the difference (the 'Inventory Deficiency'). If the aggregate value of Closing Inventory exceeds the Target Inventory Level, Buyer shall pay to Seller the difference. Any such payment shall be made within 15 days after the Parties agree on the amount or, if in dispute, within 15 days after resolution under Section 8.5."

- The Target Inventory Level should be set based on Seller's trailing twelve-month average, which the client should provide from diligence.
- **Question for client:** What is the trailing twelve-month average inventory value? Do we have a physical inventory count or reliable book value as of a recent date?

### 4. Non-Compete Binds Only the Seller Entity, Not Key Individuals

**Where:** APA § 6.6; LOI § 8.

**Issue:** Section 6.6 provides: "For five years after the Closing Date, **Seller** shall not, directly or indirectly, engage in any business that competes with the Business in North America." "Seller" is defined as Diseil Animal Health Laboratories, LLC. In an asset purchase, it is common (and often expected) that the selling entity will dissolve or become a shell after the transaction. If Diseil dissolves, the non-compete binds an entity with no assets, no operations, and no ability to compete—rendering the restriction effectively meaningless.

The restriction does not extend to Seller's members, managers, officers, or employees. The LOI (Section 8) uses the same entity-level language ("Seller will agree not to compete"), so this gap was present from the outset.

**Why it matters:** If the individuals who built and operate the Diseil business (the CEO, CFO, laboratory director, or key scientists) are free to launch a competing diagnostics company the day after closing, Soulor's $18.4 million investment is at risk. The five-year, North America scope is appropriate, but it is only as good as the obligor.

**Recommended fix:**
- Require that key individuals (at minimum the CEO, CFO, and laboratory director identified in the "Knowledge of Seller" definition) execute a separate non-competition and non-solicitation agreement as a condition to closing, or join the APA as additional obligors under § 6.6.
- Alternatively, add § 6.6(b):

  > "(b) Each of the individuals listed on Schedule 6.6 (the 'Key Persons') shall execute and deliver a non-competition and non-solicitation agreement in the form attached as Exhibit B, with terms no less restrictive than those set forth in subsection (a), as a condition to Buyer's obligation to close."

- **Question for client:** Which individuals at Diseil are critical to the business and most likely to compete? Should we require personal non-competes from them, and if so, which ones and on what terms (scope, duration, geographic limitation, liquidated damages)?

## II. SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 5. Conflicting Governing Law and Jurisdiction Between APA and Bill of Sale

**Where:** APA § 10.8 (Delaware law), § 10.9 (Court of Chancery, Delaware); Bill of Sale § 7.1 (Colorado law), § 7.2 (District Court, Larimer County, Colorado); APA § 10.4.

**Issue:** The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery. The Bill of Sale is governed by Colorado law with exclusive jurisdiction in the District Court for Larimer County, Colorado. Section 10.4 provides that the Bill of Sale "shall control... any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets."

**Why it matters:** If a title dispute arises (e.g., regarding the Kestrin Warehouse inventory, or an encumbrance not properly disclosed), Soulor would need to litigate in a Colorado state court under Colorado law, while all other disputes (indemnification, covenant breaches, etc.) are in the Delaware Court of Chancery under Delaware law. This splits the litigation, increases cost and complexity, and subjects Soulor to a less familiar forum for title claims. Colorado also does not have a Court of Chancery equivalent, so the sophisticated equitable remedies available in Delaware (specific performance, etc.) may not be available for title disputes.

**Recommended fix:**
- Amend Bill of Sale § 7 to conform to the APA:

  > "**7.1 Governing Law.** This Bill of Sale is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law rule that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction.
  >
  > **7.2 Jurisdiction.** Any action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Bill of Sale must be brought exclusively in the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware. Each Party irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of that court and waives any objection to venue there."

- If Seller resists (Colorado entities sometimes prefer home-state jurisdiction), a fallback is to at least make the jurisdiction non-exclusive for the Bill of Sale, so Soulor can choose Delaware.

### 6. Product Liability Allocation Does Not Protect Soulor from Third-Party Claims

**Where:** APA § 2.3 (Assumed Liabilities); APA § 2.4 (Excluded Liabilities); APA § 8.2(c).

**Issue:** Section 2.4 provides that "product liability claims for products sold before the Closing" remain Seller's responsibility. Section 2.3 limits Assumed Liabilities to (a) post-closing obligations under Assigned Contracts and (b) listed trade payables. Product liability is not an Assumed Liability.

However, post-closing, Soulor will be the entity selling and distributing the diagnostic products. A patient or veterinarian injured by a defective test kit will sue the entity that sold the product to them—Soulor. The contractual allocation between Soulor and Diseil does not bind third-party plaintiffs. Soulor's protection is solely the indemnity in § 8.2(c) (for Excluded Liabilities), which is **not** subject to the basket or cap in § 8.4 (those limits apply only to § 8.2(a)). This is favorable, but it means Soulor's recovery depends entirely on Diseil's ability to pay.

**Why it matters:** If Diseil dissolves post-closing (likely, given it is selling substantially all assets), Soulor may have an indemnity right against an entity with no assets. Product liability in the diagnostics space, while lower-risk than pharma, is not zero (e.g., misbranded product, contaminated reagent, incorrect result leading to patient harm).

**Recommended fix:**
- This is harder to fix contractually, but consider:
  - Requiring Seller to maintain product liability insurance for a period post-closing (e.g., 3–5 years) with Soulor named as additional insured, as a covenant in Article 6.
  - Requiring Seller to not dissolve for a period post-closing, or to make an irrevocable funding commitment for the product liability tail.
  - Confirming with the client whether the existing product liability policy can be extended or whether a "run-off" policy is available.
- **Question for client:** What is the current product liability insurance coverage, limits, and expiration? Is a tail or run-off policy available and at what cost?

### 7. No Environmental Representation

**Where:** APA Article 4 (no environmental rep); APA § 4.10 (general compliance with Laws).

**Issue:** The Business operates a reference laboratory handling reagents, potentially including hazardous chemicals, biological materials, and laboratory waste. There is no specific representation regarding:
- Compliance with environmental laws (EPA, state equivalent);
- Absence of contamination at the Seller Facility;
- Proper disposal of hazardous waste;
- Pending or threatened environmental claims;
- Permits for discharge or waste handling.

Section 4.10 ("material compliance with all applicable Laws") provides some general coverage, but environmental issues often arise post-closing (e.g., soil contamination discovered during renovation) and a general compliance rep may be insufficient.

**Why it matters:** If the Fort Collins facility has environmental contamination, Soulor as the new owner/operator could face cleanup liability under CERCLA or state analogs. This liability would be an Excluded Liability (indemnifiable under § 8.2(c)), but again depends on Seller's solvency.

**Recommended fix:**
- Add a new § 4.10A:

  > "**4.10A Environmental.** (a) The Business is in compliance in all material respects with all environmental Laws applicable to the Seller Facility and the operation of the Business. (b) No hazardous substance has been released at or from the Seller Facility in violation of Law. (c) There is no pending or, to the Knowledge of Seller, threatened action, claim or assessment relating to environmental contamination at or from the Seller Facility. (d) Seller holds all permits required under environmental Laws for the generation, storage, transport or disposal of hazardous waste at the Seller Facility."

- **Question for client:** Did environmental diligence identify any issues at the Fort Collins facility? Were Phase I/II ESAs conducted?

### 8. Escrow Amount Less Than Indemnity Cap – Recovery Gap

**Where:** APA § 2.5(a) (Escrow Amount: $1,840,000); APA § 8.4 (cap on § 8.2(a) claims: $2,760,000); APA § 8.6 (escrow as first recourse).

**Issue:** The escrow holds $1,840,000 (10% of purchase price). The maximum recovery for breach of representations (other than Fundamental Reps) is $2,760,000 (15%). Section 8.6 requires Soulor to first seek recovery from the escrow. For losses between $1,840,000 and $2,760,000, Soulor must pursue Seller directly for the excess—a collection action against an entity that may have distributed its remaining assets to its members post-closing.

**Why it matters:** The practical recovery for mid-range representation breaches is limited to the escrow unless Seller remains solvent and identifiable post-closing.

**Recommended fix:**
- This is a negotiated economic term and may be difficult to change at this stage. However, consider:
  - Extending the escrow release period (currently not specified in the APA—need to check the escrow agreement) to align with the 18-month survival period.
  - Confirming in the escrow agreement that the escrow is released only after the expiration of the survival period (or earlier by mutual agreement), not on a shorter timeline.
- **Question for client:** What are the terms of the escrow agreement regarding release timing? Has it been finalized?

### 11. Permit Transferability – Factual Gap

**Where:** APA § 1.1 (Purchased Assets include "all permits, licenses and registrations... to the extent transferable"); APA § 4.10.

**Issue:** The APA includes transferable permits in the Purchased Assets but does not identify which permits are transferable and which are not. For a regulated diagnostics laboratory, key permits (e.g., CLIA certificate if human testing is involved, state laboratory license, DEA registration if applicable, local health department permits) may be personal to the holder and require re-application.

**Why it matters:** If critical permits are not transferable, Soulor must obtain them post-closing, which may take weeks or months and is not guaranteed. There is no covenant requiring Seller to cooperate in the re-application process (though § 6.8 Further Assurances provides general support).

**Recommended fix:**
- **Question for client:** From diligence, which permits/licenses are held by Diseil for the Business? Which are transferable by assignment and which require re-application? Is there any gap period risk?
- If re-application is needed, add a covenant requiring Seller to cooperate fully and to remain the permit holder until Soulor's replacement permit is issued.
