## CRITICAL ISSUES

### 1. Pellwyn Consent Omitted from Schedule 4.4 (Required Consents)

**Where:** APA § 4.4 and Schedule 4.4; contrast with LOI § 5.1 and APA Schedule 1.1(c) (item 1).

**Issue.** The Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement with Pellwyn Reagents, Inc. is the sole source of reagents for the CanineScreen panel—the Business's flagship product. It is listed as an Assigned Contract (Schedule 1.1(c), item 1). The LOI expressly states that "Pellwyn prior written consent is required for assignment, including by asset sale or change of control" and that "Seller has advised that it has not previously sought Pellwyn's consent to any assignment" (LOI § 5.1). Yet Schedule 4.4 lists only one Required Consent: the landlord's consent under the Fort Collins lease.

The consequences are threefold:

- **Section 7.2(c)** (Buyer's closing condition) requires only that "Seller has obtained and delivered to Buyer each of the Required Consents"—i.e., only the landlord's. Pellwyn consent is not a condition to closing.
- **Section 6.3** (Consent covenant) obligates Seller to use "reasonable best efforts to obtain each of the Required Consents"—again, only the landlord's.
- **Section 4.4** (Seller's representation) states: "Except for the Required Consents set forth on Schedule 4.4, no consent, approval or notice of or to any Person is required in connection with the execution and performance of this Agreement by Seller." If Pellwyn's consent is in fact required, this representation is false as of the signing date.

**Why it matters for Soulor.** Without Pellwyn's consent, the Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement cannot be validly assigned. The CanineScreen panel would lose its sole reagent source at closing. This goes to the heart of the deal's value. Additionally, if the representation in § 4.4 is breached, Soulor's remedy is limited by the § 8.4 cap ($2.76M) and the 18-month survival period—far less than the value at stake.

**Recommended fix.** Add the Pellwyn consent to Schedule 4.4 as a Required Consent. This will automatically:
- Make it a closing condition under § 7.2(c);
- Obligate Seller to use reasonable best efforts to obtain it under § 6.3; and
- Cure the § 4.4 representation.

Proposed addition to Schedule 4.4:

> 2. Consent of Pellwyn Reagents, Inc. to the assignment of the Exclusive Reagent Supply Agreement to Buyer.

**Question for client:** Has Seller made any progress on obtaining Pellwyn's consent since signing? If Pellwyn has signaled resistance or the consent process will take longer than the time remaining before the December 15 closing, we may need to consider whether to (a) extend the outside date (§ 9.1(b) currently January 31, 2027), (b) add a specific covenant requiring Seller to deliver the consent by a date certain, or (c) negotiate a transition supply arrangement as a fallback.

### 2. Bill of Sale Defines "Purchased Assets" More Narrowly Than the APA—Excludes Kestrin Warehouse Inventory

**Where:** Bill of Sale § 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA § 1.1 (definition of "Purchased Assets"); APA § 10.4 (order of precedence).

**Issue.** The APA defines "Purchased Assets" as "all assets used in the Business, **wherever located**, including inventory at third-party warehouses" (APA § 1.1). The Bill of Sale, however, defines "Purchased Assets" as "all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under the assets used in the Business that are **located at the Seller Facility**" (Bill of Sale § 1.1). The Seller Facility is defined in both documents as the Fort Collins, Colorado premises.

The Bill of Sale's definition therefore excludes:
- All finished test kits and packaged reagents held at the Kestrin Warehouse in Reno, Nevada (APA Schedule 4.7, item 2); and
- Any other tangible personal property located off-site.

APA § 10.4 provides: "In the event of any conflict between this Agreement and any Ancillary Document, this Agreement shall prevail, **except that the Bill of Sale shall control the conveyance of title and any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets**."

**Why it matters for Soulor.** Because the Bill of Sale controls title, the narrower definition creates a real risk that Soulor does not receive valid conveyance of title to the Reno inventory. If a dispute arises (e.g., a creditor of Seller claims the Reno inventory was not properly transferred), Soulor would be in a weak position. The Kestrin Warehouse holds finished kits and packaged reagents—potentially a significant portion of total inventory value.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Bill of Sale § 1.1 to align with the APA. Replace the chapeau "that are located at the Seller Facility" with "wherever located," and add the Kestrin Warehouse inventory expressly.

Proposed revised Bill of Sale § 1.1:

> "**Purchased Assets**" means all of Seller's right, title and interest in, to and under the assets used in the Business, wherever located, including:
>
> (a) all Inventory, including raw materials, reagents, work-in-process, finished test kits, packaging and supplies held at the Seller Facility and at the Kestrin Warehouse;
>
> (b) all equipment, instruments, analyzers, furniture and other tangible personal property, including the items listed on Schedule 1.1(b) to the Purchase Agreement;
>
> (c) all books, records, customer lists, supplier lists, validation data and quality files of the Business; and
>
> (d) all other tangible personal property of every kind used in the Business.

### 3. No Inventory Purchase-Price Adjustment Mechanism

**Where:** LOI § 3; APA § 2.5 (Purchase Price).

**Issue.** The LOI states: "The Purchase Price assumes delivery of inventory at normalized levels consistent with Seller's trailing twelve-month average and the absence of any material adverse change in the Business" (LOI § 3). This language signals that the parties contemplated the Purchase Price being predicated on a particular inventory level. The APA, however, sets a fixed Purchase Price of $18,400,000 with no mechanism to adjust for inventory that is above or below normalized levels at closing.

**Why it matters for Soulor.** If inventory at closing is below the trailing twelve-month average (e.g., because Seller sold through stock during the pre-closing period, or because of spoilage or expiration), Soulor pays the full $18.4M for less inventory than the price was based on. Conversely, if inventory is above normalized levels, Seller benefits. In a diagnostic-reagent business where inventory has shelf life and the CanineScreen panel is the flagship product, even a modest inventory shortfall can represent meaningful value.

**Recommended fix.** Add an inventory true-up provision to APA § 2.5. For example:

> **2.5(d) Inventory Adjustment.** The Purchase Price shall be adjusted as follows. Within 10 Business Days after the Closing Date, Buyer shall deliver to Seller a statement setting forth (i) the value of Inventory at the Closing Date (the "Closing Inventory"), determined at the lower of cost or net realizable value, and (ii) the value of Inventory at the trailing twelve-month average as of the date of this Agreement (the "Target Inventory"), as set forth on Schedule [__]. If Closing Inventory exceeds Target Inventory, Buyer shall pay Seller the excess within 15 Business Days after delivery of the statement. If Closing Inventory is less than Target Inventory, Seller shall pay Buyer the shortfall within 15 Business Days after delivery of the statement, or the shortfall may be deducted from the Escrow Amount if sufficient. The Parties shall resolve any good-faith dispute regarding the statement within 30 days, failing which either Party may submit the dispute to [expert determination / the dispute resolution mechanism in Article 10].

**Question for client:** Do we have the trailing twelve-month inventory figures from diligence? If so, we should prepare a Target Inventory schedule to attach. If not, we need to request them from Seller before closing.

## SIGNIFICANT ISSUES

### 4. Indemnification Cap May Be Inadequate

**Where:** APA § 8.4.

**Issue.** Seller's aggregate liability for breaches of non-fundamental representations is capped at $2,760,000 (15% of the Purchase Price). The cap does not apply to fraud or to breaches of Fundamental Representations (§§ 4.1, 4.2, 4.5). However, it does apply to breaches of the inventory representation (§ 4.7), the material contracts representation (§ 4.8), the IP representation (§ 4.9), the compliance representation (§ 4.10), and the absence-of-changes representation (§ 4.13), among others.

Given the issues identified above (Pellwyn consent, inventory level, potential IP or compliance problems discovered post-closing), a $2.76M cap may not adequately protect Soulor. For example, if the Pellwyn consent issue results in loss of the supply agreement and the CanineScreen panel cannot be produced, the damage could far exceed $2.76M.

**Why it matters for Soulor.** The cap limits recovery for the most likely post-closing claims. Combined with the 18-month survival period (§ 8.1), Soulor's window to discover and claim for many issues is limited.

**Recommended fix.** Consider negotiating:
- An increase in the cap to 25–30% of the Purchase Price ($4.6M–$5.5M); or
- Carve-outs from the cap for specific high-risk representations (e.g., § 4.7 Inventory, § 4.8 Material Contracts, § 4.9 IP); or
- An extension of the survival period for the inventory and IP representations to 24–30 months.

If the cap cannot be increased, ensure the escrow amount is at least equal to the cap (currently the escrow is $1.84M vs. the cap of $2.76M, leaving $920K unsecured).

**Question for client:** What is our estimated exposure if the Pellwyn consent is not obtained? If it exceeds the cap, we should prioritize either (a) making Pellwyn consent a condition to closing (via fix in Issue 1) or (b) increasing the cap.

### 5. Escrow Amount Does Not Cover Full Indemnification Cap

**Where:** APA § 2.5(a) (Escrow Amount: $1,840,000); APA § 8.4 (cap: $2,760,000); APA § 8.6 (recovery from escrow first).

**Issue.** The Escrow Amount is $1.84M (10% of the Purchase Price), but the indemnification cap is $2.76M (15%). Section 8.6 requires Buyer to seek recovery from the escrow first. For claims between $1.84M and $2.76M, Soulor would need to collect the excess directly from Seller post-closing, with no security.

**Why it matters for Soulor.** After an asset sale, Seller (a Colorado LLC) may distribute its remaining assets (including the $16.56M cash proceeds) to its members. Collecting a post-closing judgment from an empty shell is difficult and expensive.

**Recommended fix.** Either:
- Increase the Escrow Amount to $2,760,000 (matching the cap), or
- Add a provision requiring Seller to maintain the excess ($920K) in a separate blocked account or letter of credit until the escrow is released, or
- Add a personal guarantee from Seller's principal(s) for the excess.

If none of these are achievable, at minimum ensure the escrow release terms are tight (e.g., no release before the 18-month survival period expires, absent mutual agreement or a qualified independent accountant's certification).

**Question for client:** What are the escrow release terms in the draft escrow agreement? We need to review that document (not yet in the folder) to confirm the release mechanics align with the survival periods in § 8.1.

### 6. Conflicting Governing Law and Jurisdiction Between APA and Bill of Sale

**Where:** APA § 10.8 (Delaware law), § 10.9 (Delaware Court of Chancery); Bill of Sale § 7.1 (Colorado law), § 7.2 (Larimer County District Court, Colorado).

**Issue.** The APA is governed by Delaware law with exclusive jurisdiction in the Delaware Court of Chancery. The Bill of Sale is governed by Colorado law with exclusive jurisdiction in Larimer County, Colorado. APA § 10.4 provides that the Bill of Sale "shall control the conveyance of title and any claims concerning title to the Purchased Assets."

**Why it matters for Soulor.** If a title dispute arises (e.g., a third party claims an interest in the Kestrin Warehouse inventory, or the equipment lender's lien was not properly released), the dispute would need to be litigated in Colorado under Colorado law, while any related indemnification claim against Seller under the APA would be in Delaware under Delaware law. This creates:
- Split proceedings and potential inconsistent outcomes;
- Increased litigation cost and complexity;
- Uncertainty about which forum has primary jurisdiction over intertwined claims.

**Recommended fix.** Amend Bill of Sale § 7 to conform to the APA:

> **7.1 Governing Law.** This Bill of Sale is governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without giving effect to any choice or conflict of law rule that would cause the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction.
>
> **7.2 Jurisdiction.** Any action or proceeding arising out of or relating to this Bill of Sale, or to the conveyance of the Purchased Assets, must be brought exclusively in the Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware. Seller, and Buyer by its acceptance of this Bill of Sale, each irrevocably submits to the jurisdiction of that court and waives any objection to venue there.

If Seller resists (Colorado sellers sometimes insist on home-state law for real-property-adjacent conveyances), a fallback is to at least make the jurisdiction non-exclusive in Colorado and permit concurrent filing in Delaware.

### 10. Tax Allocation Mechanism Lacks Dispute Resolution

**Where:** APA § 2.6.

**Issue.** Section 2.6 requires Buyer to deliver the § 1060 allocation within 60 days after closing and states that "The Parties shall file all tax returns consistently with that allocation." There is no mechanism for resolving a dispute if Seller disagrees with the allocation.

**Why it matters for Soulor.** The § 1060 allocation affects the tax treatment of the purchase (e.g., amortization of IP vs. depreciation of equipment). If Seller disagrees and files inconsistently, Buyer could face IRS challenges or be forced into a costly dispute. Placing the drafting obligation on Buyer (rather than jointly or on Seller) also gives Seller leverage to insist on changes.

**Recommended fix.** Add a dispute-resolution sentence:

> If the Parties cannot agree on the allocation within 30 days after Buyer's delivery, either Party may engage a nationally recognized accounting firm to determine the allocation, and its determination shall be final and binding. The fees of the accounting firm shall be borne by the Party whose position is furthest from the determination.
