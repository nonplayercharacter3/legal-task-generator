## Summary

I have reviewed the five documents in the matter folder. The relationship is governed by a Distribution Agreement dated March 1, 2024, a Field Engineering SOW No. 1 (same date), a Rebate and Marketing Side Letter (same date), and Amendment No. 1 (January 15, 2026). The two most consequential findings are: (1) the signed Agreement grants Brisosk far broader exclusivity than the prior draft or the Side Letter recital indicate was negotiated, and (2) Rirux's December 10, 2025 non-renewal notice appears to have been ten days late, meaning the Agreement likely auto-renewed for three additional years. ## Issue 1 – Territory and Exclusivity Scope: Signed Agreement Deviates Materially from Prior Draft and Side Letter Recital

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 1.14 (Territory defined as PA, NJ, DE, MD, NY) and § 2.1 (exclusive appointment for "all Products to all Customers in the Territory").
- Prior Draft (Nov 2023), § 1.14 (Territory defined as PA and NJ only) and §§ 2.1–2.3 (exclusivity limited to Healthcare Laundry Accounts; non-exclusive for other Customers; reserved rights to Rirux).
- Side Letter, Recital A: *"In the term sheet negotiated before the Agreement was signed, the Parties agreed that Brisosk's exclusivity would cover Healthcare Laundry Accounts in Pennsylvania and New Jersey only."*

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

The signed Agreement expanded the Territory from two states to five and converted a limited healthcare-only exclusivity into a full exclusive appointment covering every customer type in all five states. Under § 2.1, Rirux "shall not sell, lease, market or distribute any Products to any Customer in the Territory, including Rirux's national accounts," whether acting directly, through an Affiliate, or through any other channel. Rirux must also refer every in-Territory inquiry to Brisosk.

The Side Letter Recital A expressly states the parties' understanding was the narrower deal. However, under § 14.3 (Order of Precedence), the Distribution Agreement ranks above Side Letters, so the Recital does not override the operative text. The Recital is nonetheless powerful evidence of mutual assent to a different bargain, which could support a reformation claim—but reformation requires clear and convincing evidence of a shared mistake, and the signed document is unambiguous on its face.

**Recommended fix:**

- If Rirux wishes to contest the scope, the most viable path is a reformation action in Delaware (per § 14.1), using the prior draft, the Side Letter Recital, and any pre-signing correspondence as evidence. We should request from the client all term sheets, emails, and negotiation drafts between November 2023 and March 2024 to assess the strength of that claim.
- If Rirux accepts the signed text as controlling, it should negotiate a territory or customer-segment reduction as part of any settlement of the renewal dispute (see Issue 2).
- In either case, the orphaned definition of "Healthcare Laundry Account" in § 1.6 (present in both drafts but used in no operative provision of the signed Agreement) should be flagged to the client as further evidence of the drafting deviation.

**Information needed from client:** All pre-signing negotiation materials (term sheets, redlines, emails) between November 2023 and March 2024; identity of any Rirux national accounts with facilities in DE, MD, or NY that are currently being referred to Brisosk; and whether Rirux has made any direct sales in the expanded Territory since March 2024.

## Issue 2 – Non-Renewal Notice Appears Untimely; Agreement Likely Auto-Renewed for Three Years

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 9.1 (Initial Term: two years from March 1, 2024, i.e., through February 28, 2026) and § 9.2 (auto-renewal for three-year Renewal Terms unless non-renewal notice received at least 90 days before expiry).
- Amendment No. 1, Recital B: Rirux gave written notice of non-renewal on **December 10, 2025**.
- Amendment No. 1, Recital C: Brisosk contends the notice was untimely; Rirux disputes; both parties reserve rights under § 4.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Ninety days before February 28, 2026 is **November 30, 2025**. Rirux's notice was delivered December 10, 2025—ten days late. On the face of the documents, Brisosk's position appears correct: the notice did not satisfy § 9.2, and the Agreement auto-renewed for a three-year Renewal Term (March 1, 2026 through approximately February 28, 2029).

If the renewal is upheld, Rirux is bound by the full exclusivity in § 2.1 (see Issue 1) for three more years. The fact that both parties executed Amendment No. 1 on January 15, 2026—before the Initial Term expired—while expressly reserving their positions (§ 4.2) does not cure the lateness but does show the parties continued to operate as if the relationship would persist.

**Recommended fix:**

- Assess whether any equitable doctrine (waiver, estoppel, course of performance) could rescue Rirux's position, though this is uphill given the clear 90-day language.
- If the renewal stands, Rirux's leverage is limited. The practical path is to negotiate a modification (e.g., territory reduction, customer-segment carve-out, or a break clause) as the price of avoiding litigation. Amendment No. 1's reservation-of-rights structure (§ 4) shows the parties are already comfortable operating with a live dispute, which could be a model for a structured resolution.
- If Rirux litigates, the Delaware forum (§ 14.1) and the unambiguous 90-day deadline make this a difficult case. We need to understand why the notice was sent on December 10 rather than by November 30.

**Information needed from client:** Why the non-renewal notice was dated December 10, 2025 rather than on or before November 30, 2025; whether Rirux received any communication from Brisosk acknowledging or contesting the notice before Amendment No. 1 was signed; and whether Rirux has taken any actions since March 1, 2026 that are inconsistent with the Agreement having expired (e.g., selling directly in the Territory, appointing another distributor).

## Issue 3 – "Critical Failure" Defined More Broadly in SOW Than in Agreement, Increasing Rirux's Service-Level Exposure

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 1.4: "Critical Failure" means "a failure rendering a Product **wholly inoperable**."
- SOW No. 1, § 3.1(a): "Critical Failure" means "any malfunction of Covered Equipment that stops the equipment from operating, **or that reduces the rated throughput of the equipment by ten percent (10%) or more**, measured against Rirux's published specifications."
- SOW No. 1, § 3.2: Critical Failure triggers 1-hour remote response and 4-hour on-site response, available 24/7 (§ 3.3), with a 5% monthly fee service credit for missed on-site response.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Under § 14.3, SOWs prevail "as to Services and Service Levels." The SOW's broader definition governs for service-level purposes. A 10% throughput reduction—far less dramatic than complete inoperability—triggers the most stringent response obligations (4-hour on-site, 24/7) and the only category that generates service credits. In a tunnel washer system with 8–16 modules, a single module underperforming by 10% could constitute a Critical Failure under the SOW definition even though the system as a whole is operating.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a revised SOW or SOW No. 2 that aligns the Critical Failure definition with the Agreement's "wholly inoperable" standard, or at least raises the throughput threshold (e.g., 25% or 50%) and limits the 24/7 obligation to complete stoppages.
- In the interim, ensure Rirux's service portal and dispatch team are calibrated to the SOW definition so that classification disputes don't become recurring friction points.
- Confirm that "Rirux's published specifications" referenced in SOW § 3.1(a) are current and unambiguous, since the throughput measurement depends on them.

**Information needed from client:** How many service requests have been classified as Critical Failures under the SOW's broader definition since March 2024; whether any classification disputes have arisen; and what Rirux's current published specifications state for throughput on each product line.

## Issue 4 – No Stated Consequence for Brisosk's Failure to Meet Minimum Purchase Commitment

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 2.5: Brisosk "shall purchase Products with an aggregate net invoice value of at least the minimum purchase amount set out in Exhibit B" ($3,500,000 per contract year, per Exhibit B ¶ 2).
- No other provision of the Agreement, SOW, or Side Letter specifies a remedy for failure to meet the minimum.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

The minimum purchase commitment is the quid pro quo for the exclusivity Rirux granted. Without a stated consequence (e.g., automatic conversion to non-exclusive, territory reduction, or a liquidated damages payment), Rirux's only recourse is to argue that a missed minimum constitutes a "material breach" under § 9.3(a), requiring a written notice and a 30-day cure period before termination. A single year's shortfall might not rise to "material" in a court's view, especially if Brisosk is close to the threshold. Meanwhile, Rirux remains locked out of the Territory.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate an amendment adding a specific consequence: for example, if Brisosk fails to meet 80% of the minimum in any contract year, the exclusive appointment converts to non-exclusive for the following year, or the Territory is reduced.
- If the renewal dispute is being negotiated (see Issue 2), this is a natural item to include in any settlement package.
- At minimum, document Rirux's position that a material shortfall is a material breach to preserve the § 9.3(a) remedy.

**Information needed from client:** Brisosk's actual purchase volumes for each contract year to date (March 2024–February 2025; March 2025–present); whether Rirux has raised the minimum purchase issue with Brisosk; and whether Rirux's production planning has been disrupted by forecast inaccuracies.

## Issue 5 – SOW Monthly Support Fee Has No Deregistration or Sunset Mechanism

**Where it appears:**

- SOW No. 1, § 6.1: Monthly support fee is calculated on "Covered Equipment registered on the first day of each calendar month."
- SOW No. 1, § 1.2: Covered Equipment is defined by installation and registration; no provision addresses decommissioning, relocation, or sale of equipment by the Customer.
- SOW No. 1, § 2.3: Registration occurs within 15 business days after installation; no corresponding deregistration obligation is stated.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Once equipment is registered, there is no mechanism described for removing it from the fee base. If a Customer decommissions a washer-extractor or sells it to a facility outside the Territory, the equipment arguably remains "Covered Equipment" (it was sold/installed by Brisosk and registered), and Rirux would continue billing the monthly fee. This creates two risks: (a) Brisosk disputes the charge and withholds payment, creating a friction point; and (b) if Rirux does not bill, it leaves revenue on the table. Neither outcome is clean.

Conversely, if a Customer's equipment is relocated within the Territory to a new site, the SOW does not address whether re-registration is required or whether the fee continues uninterrupted.

**Recommended fix:**

- Add a deregistration provision to the SOW (via amendment or SOW No. 2) requiring Brisosk to notify Rirux within a specified period after equipment is decommissioned, sold, or relocated outside the Territory, with the fee ceasing on the first day of the following month.
- Clarify that relocation within the Territory does not interrupt the fee.

**Information needed from client:** Whether any equipment has been decommissioned or relocated since the SOW took effect; whether Brisosk has questioned any monthly fee invoices on this basis.

## Issue 6 – Purchase Order Acceptance: No Consequence for Rirux's Failure to Respond Within Five Business Days

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 3.2: "Rirux shall accept or reject each Purchase Order within five (5) business days of receipt and shall not unreasonably reject a Purchase Order that conforms to this Agreement."

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

The provision imposes an affirmative obligation on Rirux to respond within five business days but states no consequence for non-response. It does not say a PO is "deemed accepted" if Rirux is silent. However, in a dispute, Brisosk could argue that Rirux's failure to reject a conforming PO within the stated window constitutes acceptance by conduct, particularly if Rirux subsequently ships or acknowledges the order in any way. The absence of a "deemed rejected" or "no effect" clause leaves the gap open.

**Recommended fix:**

- Amend § 3.2 to state expressly that failure to respond within five business days does not constitute acceptance, and that a PO is binding only upon Rirux's written acceptance. Alternatively, if Rirux prefers the deemed-acceptance approach for operational efficiency, state that clearly and ensure internal processes can handle it.

**Information needed from client:** Whether Rirux has ever failed to respond to a PO within five business days; whether any dispute has arisen over an unresponded PO.

## Issue 7 – Warranty Period Uses "Earlier Of" Formula That May Shorten Customer-Facing Coverage

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 7.1: Warranty lasts "twelve (12) months from the **earlier** of installation at the Customer site and ninety (90) days after delivery to Brisosk."

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

This formula means the warranty clock starts no later than 90 days after delivery to Brisosk, regardless of when installation actually occurs. If Brisosk holds inventory for 60 days before installing, the customer receives the full 12 months from installation. But if Brisosk holds inventory for 120 days, the warranty started 30 days before installation, and the customer receives only 11 months of coverage from the installation date.

This is actually favorable to Rirux (it caps Rirux's warranty exposure), but it creates a practical problem: Brisosk's customers may experience a warranty that appears to have "expired" before they've had 12 months of use, generating complaints that Brisosk directs at Rirux. Section 7.4 permits Brisosk to pass the warranty through, so Brisosk is the one fielding customer calls.

**Recommended fix:**

- No contractual change is strictly necessary—this is a defensible position for Rirux. However, Rirux should ensure its customer-facing warranty documentation (if any) is consistent with the "earlier of" formula so that end-users are not surprised.
- If Brisosk raises this as a commercial issue, Rirux could offer to extend the outer cap from 90 days to 120 days as a concession, without moving to a "later of" formulation.

**Information needed from client:** Whether any warranty disputes have arisen from this formula; typical lead time from delivery to Brisosk to installation at Customer sites.

## Issue 8 – Amendment No. 1 Adds New Products While Renewal Dispute Is Unresolved

**Where it appears:**

- Amendment No. 1, Recitals B–D and §§ 2–4 (dated January 15, 2026).
- Amendment No. 1, § 4.2: Neither execution nor performance is an admission or waiver regarding the timeliness of the Non-Renewal Notice or whether the Agreement renewed.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Rirux is expanding the product portfolio available to Brisosk (adding RX-FF and RX-SP series) at a time when the fundamental question of whether the Agreement is still in effect is unresolved. If the Agreement renewed, the new products are subject to the same full exclusivity in the five-state Territory. If it did not renew, the legal basis for Amendment No. 1 is questionable—though both parties signed it, and § 4.2 preserves the dispute.

The practical effect is that Rirux is deepening its operational entanglement with Brisosk (training obligations under § 3.6, support fee obligations under § 3.4, preventive maintenance under § 3.3) while the relationship's legal status is in limbo.

**Recommended fix:**

- Ensure that all performance under Amendment No. 1 is documented as being provided "without prejudice" to Rirux's position on the renewal question, consistent with § 4.2.
- If Rirux intends to litigate the renewal issue, be aware that a court may view the execution of Amendment No. 1 as evidence of course of performance supporting renewal, notwithstanding the reservation clause.
- Consider whether the new product lines should have been held in abeyance until the renewal question is resolved.

**Information needed from client:** Whether any New Products have been sold or installed since January 15, 2026; whether Rirux has provided the training required by § 3.6; and the business rationale for adding the new products during the dispute.

## Issue 9 – No Dispute Resolution Mechanism for Service-Level or Technical Disputes

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 14.1: Exclusive jurisdiction in Delaware state and federal courts. No arbitration, mediation, or expert-determination clause anywhere in the Agreement or SOW.
- SOW No. 1, § 2.4: Brisosk may ask Rirux to reconsider a fault classification; "Rirux shall consider any such request in good faith." No further mechanism if disagreement persists.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Service-level disputes (was this a Critical Failure or a Major Fault? Did Rirux meet the response time?) are inherently technical and fact-specific. Resolving them in Delaware litigation is expensive and slow. The "good faith" reconsideration language in SOW § 2.4 is aspirational and provides no binding resolution path. For a relationship that may be locked in for three more years (see Issue 2), the absence of a tiered dispute resolution mechanism (e.g., escalation → mediation → binding expert determination for technical questions → litigation for legal questions) is a gap.

**Recommended fix:**

- Negotiate a dispute resolution addendum: (1) good-faith escalation between designated officers within 15 business days; (2) non-binding mediation; (3) for purely technical questions (e.g., whether throughput was reduced by 10% or more), binding determination by an independent industry expert; (4) litigation as a last resort.
- This is a reasonable ask that does not concede the renewal dispute.

## Issue 10 – Side Letter Recital A Creates Evidentiary Risk in Any Future Dispute

**Where it appears:**

- Side Letter, Recital A (quoted in full in Issue 1 above).
- Side Letter, § 5.2: The terms of the Side Letter are Confidential Information under § 10 of the Agreement.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Recital A is a written admission by both parties (it was signed by Brisosk) that the negotiated deal was narrower than the signed Agreement reflects. In any dispute—whether over the scope of exclusivity, over course of performance, or over the parties' expectations—this Recital will be Exhibit A for Brisosk. It undercuts Rirux's ability to argue that the broader exclusivity was a deliberate, understood concession.

The confidentiality designation (§ 5.2) means the Side Letter cannot be freely shared with third parties (e.g., potential new distributors in DE, MD, or NY) without Brisosk's consent or a legal compulsion. This further constrains Rirux's ability to work around the exclusivity even informally.

**Recommended fix:**

- Do not draw attention to Recital A in any communication with Brisosk unless in the context of a structured negotiation or litigation.
- If Rirux pursues reformation (Issue 1), Recital A is a key piece of evidence, but the overall evidentiary record (prior draft, negotiation history) must be assembled first.
- Consider whether the Side Letter should be amended or superseded as part of any global resolution of the renewal and territory disputes, to eliminate the inconsistent recital from the document set.

## Issue 11 – Section 9.4(b) Requires Rirux to Fulfill Accepted POs on Expiry (Not Termination for Cause)

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 9.4(b): "Rirux shall fulfil all Purchase Orders accepted before the date of expiry or termination, unless Rirux terminated under Section 9.3."

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

If the Agreement expires by its terms (as opposed to being terminated for cause), Rirux must still complete all POs it accepted before the expiry date. For capital equipment with long lead times (tunnel washers, for example), this could mean Rirux is manufacturing and delivering products well after the relationship has ended, with no minimum purchase obligation backing the order and no service relationship in place for the installed equipment.

**Recommended fix:**

- If Rirux is planning for a clean exit (contingent on the renewal dispute being resolved in Rirux's favor), ensure that no POs are accepted close to the anticipated expiry date without a specific delivery schedule that completes before that date.
- In any negotiation of a transition or exit, seek to cap the post-expiry fulfillment obligation at a stated number of days or to limit it to POs with delivery dates within 60 days of expiry.

## Issue 12 – No Additional Insured or Liability Insurance Requirements Beyond CGL and Workers' Comp

**Where it appears:**

- Signed Agreement, § 13.1: Each party must maintain CGL (including products/completed operations) at $2M per occurrence and workers' comp as required by law. No requirement to name the other party as additional insured. No requirement for umbrella/excess coverage, professional liability, or cyber insurance.

**Why it matters for Rirux:**

Given that Rirux's indemnification obligation under § 11.1 covers product defects and IP infringement, and that the liability cap in § 12.2 excludes indemnification obligations from the cap, Rirux's exposure on a major product defect claim is uncapped. The $2M CGL requirement is a floor, not a ceiling on Rirux's own insurance program. If a catastrophic defect occurs at a healthcare facility (a high-risk customer segment), $2M may be inadequate, and Rirux would be personally exposed above that.

**Recommended fix:**

- Confirm Rirux's current products liability insurance limits and whether they exceed $2M per occurrence. If not, increase coverage before the next renewal cycle.
- Consider negotiating a mutual additional-insured provision so that Brisosk's CGL policy also covers Rirux for Brisosk-caused claims (e.g., improper installation by Brisosk's personnel triggering a § 7.3(b) warranty exclusion dispute that morphs into a third-party injury claim).

**Information needed from client:** Rirux's current insurance program (carrier, limits, products liability sub-limit, umbrella layer); any claims history under the Agreement.

## Conclusion

The document set reveals a relationship in which Rirux has granted substantially more exclusivity than the negotiation record suggests was intended, and may be locked into that exclusivity for three additional years due to a late non-renewal notice. The SOW's broader "Critical Failure" definition and the absence of a minimum-purchase remedy compound Rirux's operational and commercial constraints. The immediate priority is to determine the legal status of the Agreement (renewed or expired) and, on that basis, decide whether to litigate, negotiate a modification, or accept the current terms and manage within them.
