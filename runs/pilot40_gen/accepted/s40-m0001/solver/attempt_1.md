## Issue 1 – Non-Renewal Notice Likely Untimely; Agreement Probably Renewed

**Where:** Distribution Agreement § 9.1–9.2; Amendment No. 1, Recital C and § 4.

**Explanation.** The Initial Term is two years from the March 1, 2024 Effective Date (§ 9.1), expiring on or about February 28 / March 1, 2026. Section 9.2 requires non-renewal notice to be *received* "no later than ninety (90) days prior to the last day of the then-current term." Ninety days before February 28, 2026 is November 30, 2025; ninety days before March 1, 2026 is December 1, 2025. Rirux's notice was given December 10, 2025 (Amendment No. 1, Recital C) — nine to ten days late under either reading. Section 14.2 confirms a notice is effective only on receipt, so even if dispatched December 10, it was received no earlier than that date.

Brisosk has already asserted the notice was untimely (Recital C). The Agreement contains no grace period, no cure mechanism for a late non-renewal notice, and no provision allowing the parties to "reset" the deadline. On the face of the documents, Rirux is in a weak position.

**Why it matters.** If the notice was untimely, the Agreement auto-renewed for a three-year Renewal Term (§ 9.2), locking Rirux into full exclusivity in five states through approximately February/March 2029. All SOW and Side Letter obligations continue for that period.

**Recommended fix.** This cannot be fixed by amendment at this point without Brisosk's agreement. Options to discuss with the client:

- Negotiate a mutual release or early-termination provision with Brisosk (possibly in exchange for a buyout or transition period).
- Assess whether Brisosk's own conduct (e.g., failure to meet minimum purchase commitments) gives Rirux a termination-for-cause right under § 9.3(a) that could be exercised now.
- If Rirux wishes to preserve its position, it should send a formal letter to Brisosk stating its interpretation and demanding confirmation of the renewal status, to create a record.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- Exact date and method of delivery of the December 10, 2025 notice (hand, courier, email?).
- Whether Brisosk acknowledged receipt and, if so, on what date.
- Whether Rirux has any evidence the parties discussed a later deadline or that Brisosk knew of Rirux's intent not to renew before December 10.
- Brisosk's purchase performance against the $3.5M minimum in Contract Year 1 (March 2024 – February 2025) and Contract Year 2 (March 2025 – February 2026).

## Issue 2 – Exclusivity Scope Dramatically Expanded from Draft to Signed Agreement

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 2.1; Prior Draft §§ 2.1–2.3; Side Letter, Recital A.

**Explanation.** The November 2023 draft gave Brisosk exclusivity only over *Healthcare Laundry Accounts* in the Territory (§ 2.1 of draft), a non-exclusive appointment for all other Customers (§ 2.2 of draft), and an express reserved-rights clause (§ 2.3 of draft: "All rights not expressly granted to Brisosk … are reserved to Rirux").

The signed Agreement § 2.1 grants exclusivity over **all Products to all Customers** in the Territory, expressly including "Rirux's national accounts," and extends the restriction to sales "directly, through an Affiliate, or through any other distributor, dealer, agent or sales representative." The reserved-rights clause is gone.

The Side Letter's Recital A states: "the Parties agreed that Brisosk's exclusivity would cover Healthcare Laundry Accounts in Pennsylvania and New Jersey only." This directly contradicts the signed Agreement.

**Why it matters.** Under § 14.3 (order of precedence), the Distribution Agreement trumps the Side Letter, so the signed text controls. But Recital A is powerful evidence of the parties' original commercial understanding. If Rirux ever disputes the scope of exclusivity (e.g., arguing that a particular customer falls outside the intended grant), the Side Letter recital cuts against Rirux because it was *Brisosk's* understanding that was narrower. Conversely, if Rirux wants to argue the signed Agreement is a scrivener's error or the product of a drafting mistake, the prior draft and Side Letter recital support that argument — but Rirux would bear the burden of proving mistake, and both parties signed the final version.

**Recommended fix.** If the renewal dispute is resolved in Rirux's favor (i.e., the Agreement did not renew), this becomes moot. If the Agreement did renew, Rirux should:

- Avoid any conduct that could be construed as a waiver of the broad exclusivity (e.g., do not sell directly to any customer in the five-state Territory without routing through Brisosk).
- If Rirux believes the signed text does not reflect the deal, evaluate a claim for reformation based on mutual mistake, using the prior draft and Side Letter Recital A as evidence. This is a high-bar argument in Delaware and would likely require litigation.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- Who negotiated the change from healthcare-only to full exclusivity, and when? Was it during the final drafting phase between the November 2023 draft and the March 2024 signing?
- Is there any email or correspondence in which Rirux's deal team confirmed or discussed the expanded scope?
- Does Rirux have any existing national accounts in the five-state Territory that are now blocked?

## Issue 4 – Execution of Amendment No. 1 During the Renewal Dispute Creates Estoppel/Waiver Risk

**Where:** Amendment No. 1, Recitals B–D, §§ 2–4.

**Explanation.** Rirux gave (allegedly untimely) non-renewal notice on December 10, 2025. On January 15, 2026 — still within the Initial Term but only ~6 weeks before its expiry — Rirux negotiated and executed Amendment No. 1, adding two new product lines with pricing, support fees, PM schedules, and a 90-day training obligation. While § 4.2 expressly reserves rights regarding the renewal dispute, continued performance and amendment of the Agreement are facts that Brisosk will argue show Rirux treated the Agreement as ongoing.

**Why it matters.** In a dispute over whether the Agreement renewed, a court or arbitrator will look at the parties' conduct. Rirux's willingness to amend the product schedule, set new pricing, and commit to training within weeks of the (allegedly) expired term undermines the position that Rirux believed the relationship was ending. The reservation-of-rights clause helps but is not a complete shield against an estoppel or course-of-performance argument.

**Recommended fix.**

- Going forward, Rirux should avoid executing any further amendments or SOWs without an express condition that they are effective only if the Agreement is in force, and with a renewed reservation of rights.
- If Rirux wishes to litigate or arbitrate the renewal question, it should be prepared to explain the Amendment No. 1 timing (e.g., "we were finalizing the new product launch schedule independently of the renewal question").
- Document internally the business reasons for executing Amendment No. 1 despite the pending dispute.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- What was the business rationale for executing Amendment No. 1 in January 2026 rather than waiting to resolve the renewal question first?
- Were the new products (RX-FF, RX-SP) already in production or in the pipeline before the non-renewal notice was sent?
- Has Brisosk placed any purchase orders for New Products since the Amendment Date?

## Issue 5 – No Express Remedy for Failure to Meet Minimum Purchase Commitment

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 2.5; Exhibit B ¶ 2.

**Explanation.** Section 2.5 requires Brisosk to purchase at least $3,500,000 in net invoice value per contract year. The Agreement does not specify a consequence for missing the minimum — no liquidated damages, no automatic termination right, no price adjustment, no reduction in exclusivity. The only available remedy is to argue that a missed minimum constitutes a "material breach" under § 9.3(a), triggering the 30-day cure notice.

**Why it matters.** If Brisosk consistently under-purchases (e.g., $2.5M against a $3.5M minimum), Rirux's only lever is a material-breach termination argument, which requires a 30-day cure period and is fact-intensive. "Cure" for a purchase shortfall is ambiguous: can Brisosk cure by rushing orders in the last 30 days? The Agreement is silent.

**Recommended fix.**

- In any renegotiation or amendment, add a specific consequence: e.g., (a) if the minimum is missed by more than 20% in any contract year, Rirux may convert the appointment to non-exclusive; (b) liquidated damages equal to the shortfall; or (c) a right to terminate without cure period if the minimum is missed in two consecutive years.
- In the interim, track Brisosk's purchases quarterly against the annual minimum and send a courtesy notice if the run-rate suggests the minimum will be missed, to build a record.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- What were Brisosk's actual net purchases in Contract Year 1 (March 2024 – February 2025) and Contract Year 2 to date?
- Has Rirux raised the minimum-purchase issue with Brisosk informally?

## Issue 6 – "Critical Failure" Defined Differently in the Agreement and the SOW

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 1.4; SOW § 3.1(a).

**Explanation.** Agreement § 1.4 defines Critical Failure as "a failure rendering a Product wholly inoperable." SOW § 3.1(a) defines it as "any malfunction … that stops the equipment from operating, **or that reduces the rated throughput of the equipment by ten percent (10%) or more**." The SOW definition is materially broader.

Under § 14.3, SOWs have precedence over the Distribution Agreement "as to Services and Service Levels." So for service-response purposes, the broader SOW definition likely controls. But for warranty-priority purposes (Agreement § 7.2: "Rirux shall give priority to warranty claims arising from a Critical Failure"), the Agreement's narrower definition may apply.

**Why it matters.** The broader SOW definition means more incidents trigger the 1-hour remote / 4-hour on-site response requirement and the 5% monthly service credit. A tunnel washer running at 88% throughput is a "Critical Failure" under the SOW but not under the Agreement. This increases Rirux's operational burden and service-credit exposure.

**Recommended fix.**

- Harmonize the definitions. In the next SOW or an amendment, either (a) conform the SOW definition to the Agreement (wholly inoperable only), or (b) conform the Agreement definition to the SOW and accept the broader trigger. Option (a) is preferable for Rirux.
- If the definitions are not changed, Rirux's service team should be trained to apply the SOW definition for response-time purposes (to avoid service-credit disputes) but the Agreement definition for warranty-priority purposes.

## Issue 7 – Four-Hour On-Site Response for Critical Failures Across a Five-State Territory

**Where:** SOW § 3.2 (response-time table); § 3.3 (24/7 availability for Critical Failures).

**Explanation.** Rirux must respond on-site within 4 hours of receiving a complete service request for any Critical Failure, available 24/7/365. The Territory spans PA, NJ, DE, MD, and NY. Depending on the location of Rirux's manufacturing facility and field-engineer housing, a 4-hour on-site response to a customer in, say, western PA or upstate NY may be operationally very difficult, particularly at night or on weekends.

**Why it matters.** Each missed 4-hour on-site response triggers a 5% service credit on the monthly support fee (SOW § 3.2). If Rirux has, say, 50 tunnel washers generating $62,500/month in support fees, a single missed response costs $3,125. Repeated misses could become a material cost. More importantly, chronic inability to meet the SLA could give Brisosk a material-breach termination right.

**Recommended fix.**

- Assess Rirux's current field-engineer coverage across the five states. Identify geographic gaps.
- Consider negotiating a "reasonable travel time" carve-out: e.g., the 4-hour clock starts when a field engineer departs the nearest Rirux service center, not when the request is logged. Or extend to 6 hours for sites beyond a certain radius.
- Ensure the service-portal system accurately timestamps request receipt so that Rirux can defend against disputed "time of receipt" claims.
- Note that SOW § 3.6 excuses delays caused by site-access failures or inaccurate information — make sure Rirux's service team diligently documents any such excuses in real time.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- Where is Rirux's manufacturing facility located?
- How many field engineers does Rirux currently deploy in the Territory, and where are they based?
- Has Rirux missed any 4-hour on-site responses to date? If so, how many and what was the cause?

## Issue 8 – Seven-Year Spare Parts Obligation

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 6.4.

**Explanation.** For at least seven years after Rirux discontinues a Product, Rirux must keep spare parts available for purchase by Brisosk. The product lines in Exhibit A (as amended) include washer-extractors, tunnel washers, dosing systems, flatwork finishers, and small-piece folders.

**Why it matters.** Commercial laundry equipment has long service lives (15–25 years). A seven-year post-discontinuation parts obligation means Rirux must continue manufacturing or stocking components for discontinued lines well after they are no longer in production. This ties up working capital, requires maintaining tooling and supplier relationships, and creates a cost center with no offsetting revenue unless Brisosk actually purchases the parts.

**Recommended fix.**

- In any renegotiation, seek to reduce the period (e.g., to 5 years) or limit it to "reasonably foreseeable" spare parts rather than all parts.
- Add a requirement that Brisosk give annual forecasts of anticipated spare-parts demand so Rirux can manage inventory.
- Confirm that the 20% spare-parts discount in Exhibit B ¶ 1 adequately covers Rirux's cost of maintaining the parts supply chain.

## Issue 9 – Rebate Structure: Tier Rate Applied to All Net Purchases (Cliff Structure)

**Where:** Side Letter § 2.1.

**Explanation.** The rebate table applies a single rate to *all* Net Purchases once a threshold is crossed. For example, if Net Purchases are $5,000,001, the 2.5% rate applies to the entire $5,000,001 (yielding ~$125,000), not just to the $1 above $5,000,000. This is a "cliff" or "full-tier" structure, not a marginal one.

**Why it matters.** The cliff structure creates a disproportionate cost spike at tier boundaries. Rirux pays $75,000 (1.5% × $5M) at the top of Tier 2 but $125,000 (2.5% × $5M) at the bottom of Tier 3 — a $50,000 jump for one additional dollar of purchases. Over the life of a renewed three-year term, this could represent a significant margin erosion.

**Recommended fix.**

- If renegotiating, convert to a marginal structure (each tier's rate applies only to purchases within that tier's band).
- Alternatively, add a "true-up" mechanism so that if purchases fall below a threshold at year-end, the rebate is recalculated at the lower tier.

## Issue 10 – Referral Obligation Covers All Inquiries in the Territory

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 2.1 (last sentence).

**Explanation.** "Rirux shall refer to Brisosk any inquiry for Products that it receives from a Customer located in the Territory." This is not limited to Healthcare Laundry Accounts (as in the prior draft) and covers any product inquiry from any customer in the five-state Territory.

**Why it matters.** Rirux's sales team must route every inbound inquiry from a customer in PA, NJ, DE, MD, or NY to Brisosk, even if the inquiry is for a product Brisosk does not stock or staff to support. This creates an administrative burden and could slow Rirux's response to prospects. It also gives Brisosk visibility into Rirux's pipeline in the Territory.

**Recommended fix.**

- Negotiate a carve-out for inquiries about products not yet in the Territory pipeline or for which Brisosk has no qualified staff.
- Implement a clear internal protocol so Rirux's sales team knows how to route referrals and document compliance.

## Issue 11 – Software and Firmware Updates Included in SOW Without Scope Limitation

**Where:** SOW § 1.3(e).

**Explanation.** The Services include "software and firmware updates for RX-DS Series Chemical Dosing Systems and for tunnel washer controllers." There is no limitation on the frequency, scope, or complexity of updates. The monthly support fee ($60 per dosing system, $1,250 per tunnel washer) is supposed to cover this.

**Why it matters.** If Rirux releases significant firmware updates (e.g., new features, security patches, regulatory compliance updates), the engineering cost of developing, testing, and deploying those updates to all covered sites is borne by Rirux within the fixed monthly fee. As the installed base grows, this cost scales linearly while the fee per unit stays flat.

**Recommended fix.**

- Clarify in a future SOW amendment that "updates" means bug fixes, security patches, and minor version upgrades, and that major version releases or new-feature developments are out-of-scope work billable at the § 6.3 rate ($165/hour).
- Track engineering hours spent on updates to assess whether the fee is adequate.

## Issue 12 – Rirux Must Fulfill Accepted Purchase Orders on Expiry (Unless Terminating for Cause)

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 9.4(b).

**Explanation.** On expiry or termination (other than for cause by Rirux), Rirux must fulfill all Purchase Orders accepted before the effective date of expiry/termination.

**Why it matters.** If the Agreement expires or is terminated by Brisosk (or by Rirux for Brisosk's breach), Rirux is still on the hook for any POs it accepted in the final days. If Brisosk places a large order just before the termination date and Rirux accepts it (or is deemed to have accepted it by failing to reject within 5 business days under § 3.2), Rirux must manufacture and deliver.

**Recommended fix.**

- In the wind-down period (if Rirux is exiting), reject all non-urgent POs or negotiate a mutual release of pending orders as part of any termination agreement.
- Ensure the 5-business-day acceptance/rejection window in § 3.2 is strictly monitored so Rirux does not inadvertently accept orders it does not want to fulfill post-termination.

## Issue 13 – IP Indemnity Not Subject to Liability Cap

**Where:** Signed Agreement §§ 11.1(b), 12.2.

**Explanation.** Rirux's indemnity for IP infringement claims (§ 11.1(b)) is carved out of the liability cap in § 12.2. Rirux's exposure for a patent or trademark infringement claim is theoretically unlimited.

**Why it matters.** Commercial laundry equipment involves complex mechanical and software components. A competitor or NPE (non-practicing entity) could assert a patent claim. Because the indemnity is uncapped, Rirux's exposure is not limited to trailing 12-month revenue from Brisosk.

**Recommended fix.**

- This is a standard provision and difficult to change in a signed agreement. However, Rirux should:
  - Maintain a robust IP clearance file for all product lines.
  - Confirm that its product liability / IP insurance (beyond the $2M CGL in § 13.1) covers IP indemnity obligations. The § 13.1 insurance requirements do not expressly include IP infringement coverage.
  - Consider adding an IP insurance requirement in a future amendment.

**Facts we need from the client:**
- Does Rirux carry intellectual property liability insurance? If so, what are the limits?

## Issue 14 – SOW Monthly Fee Based on First-of-Month Registration; No Deregistration Mechanism

**Where:** SOW § 6.1.

**Explanation.** The monthly support fee is calculated on Covered Equipment "registered on the first day of each calendar month." There is no provision for deregistration (e.g., when a customer sells the equipment, goes out of business, or the equipment is removed from service).

**Why it matters.** (a) Rirux loses one month of fee for newly installed equipment (it is not "registered on the first day" until the following month). (b) If equipment is removed from service but not formally deregistered, Rirux continues to bill for it, creating a dispute risk. Conversely, if Brisosk argues equipment should have been deregistered, Rirux could face a refund claim.

**Recommended fix.**

- Add a deregistration provision: e.g., "Brisosk shall notify Rirux in writing within 15 business days of any Covered Equipment being removed from service, sold, or relocated outside the Territory. Rirux shall remove the equipment from the monthly fee calculation effective the first of the following month."
- Reconcile the registered equipment list quarterly with Brisosk.

## Issue 15 – Order of Precedence Places Side Letter Last; Recital A Effectively Neutralized

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 14.3; Side Letter, Recital A.

**Explanation.** Section 14.3 establishes: Amendments > SOWs (for Services/Service Levels) > Distribution Agreement > Side Letters. The Side Letter is at the bottom. Its Recital A (stating the parties "agreed" exclusivity would cover only Healthcare Laundry Accounts in PA and NJ) is a recital, not an operative term, and in any event is subordinate to the Agreement's § 2.1.

**Why it matters.** If Rirux wishes to argue that the signed Agreement's broad exclusivity is a drafting error, the Side Letter Recital A is the best evidence available. But because it is (a) a recital rather than an operative term, and (b) in a document ranked last in the precedence order, it carries limited legal weight. A Delaware court would likely give it some weight as evidence of background/intent but would not let it override the clear text of § 2.1.

**Recommended fix.** No direct fix available. This is relevant only if Rirux pursues a reformation or mistake argument (see Issue 2).

## Issue 16 – Brisosk's First-Line Support Obligation Is Unenforceable in Practice

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 6.2; SOW § 2.1.

**Explanation.** Brisosk must provide "first-line support" including "intake of service calls, basic troubleshooting, and escalation to Rirux." However, there is no service level, no penalty, and no objective standard for what constitutes adequate first-line support. Rirux has no right to audit Brisosk's troubleshooting or to reject an escalation that should have been resolved at the first-line level.

**Why it matters.** If Brisosk escalates every minor issue to Rirux without performing basic troubleshooting, Rirux's field engineers are tied up on calls that should have been handled locally. This increases Rirux's cost of providing Services and could cause Rirux to miss response-time SLAs for genuine Critical Failures (because engineers are occupied with improperly escalated minor faults).

**Recommended fix.**

- In a future SOW amendment, add: (a) a requirement that Brisosk document troubleshooting steps before escalation (already partially in SOW § 2.2(d)); (b) a right for Rirux to reject a service request that does not include adequate troubleshooting documentation, with the clock on response times not starting until a complete request is received (SOW § 3.6(b) already excuses delays for "inaccurate information" — Rirux should use this); (c) periodic joint review of escalation quality.

## Issue 18 – Assignment Clause Permits Transfer to Acquirer Without Consent

**Where:** Signed Agreement § 14.5.

**Explanation.** Either party may assign the Agreement without consent to "a successor to all or substantially all of its business or assets to which this Agreement relates."

**Why it matters.** If Brisosk is acquired by a competitor of Rirux or by a private equity firm that intends to restructure the distribution network, the Agreement (including the exclusivity grant) transfers automatically. Rirux has no approval right. Conversely, this protects Rirux if it sells its business — the buyer steps into the Agreement without needing Brisosk's consent.

**Recommended fix.**

- If Rirux is concerned about Brisosk's ownership changing, negotiate a "change of control" provision requiring Rirux's consent (or at minimum notice) if Brisosk is acquired by a named competitor.
- Monitor Brisosk's corporate filings for any change-of-control transactions.

## Issue 20 – SOW Service Credit Claim Window Is Short

**Where:** SOW § 3.5.

**Explanation.** Brisosk must claim a Service Credit "in writing within thirty (30) days after the end of the month in which the missed response occurred." If Brisosk misses the deadline, the credit is forfeited.

**Why it matters.** This is actually favorable to Rirux. However, Rirux's own monthly reporting obligation (SOW § 3.7: report within 10 business days after month-end showing "Service Credits accrued") means Rirux is putting Brisosk on notice of any missed responses. If Rirux's report identifies a missed response and Brisosk then fails to claim the credit within 30 days of month-end, Rirux should note the forfeiture in its records to prevent a late claim.

**Recommended fix.** In the monthly report, expressly state: "No Service Credit has been claimed for the above missed response(s). Pursuant to SOW § 3.5, any claim must be received within 30 days after month-end."

## Conclusion

The single most consequential issue is the likely untimeliness of Rirux's non-renewal notice (Issue 1). If the Agreement renewed, Rirux is bound for three additional years with full exclusivity in five states, and the practical questions shift from "how do we exit?" to "how do we manage the relationship and protect ourselves during the Renewal Term?" I recommend the client make a prompt decision on whether to (a) accept the renewal and focus on performance management and protecting margins, or (b) pursue a negotiated early exit with Brisosk, using the minimum-purchase and service-level levers identified above. The estoppel risk from Amendment No. 1 (Issue 4) makes a contested exit more difficult than it would otherwise be.
