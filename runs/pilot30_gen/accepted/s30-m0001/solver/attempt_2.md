## 1. Working Capital Dispute – Apparent Missed Deadline for Notice of Disagreement

**Documents:** Stock Purchase Agreement § 2.4(a)–(b); IP Assignment Agreement, Recital C

**Issue.** Seller delivered the Closing Statement on April 30, 2026 (SPA § 2.4(a) requires delivery within 60 days after the April 1 Closing – timely). SPA § 2.4(b) requires Buyer to deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement." Thirty days from April 30 is May 30, 2026. Buyer's Notice of Disagreement was delivered June 12, 2026 – twelve days late. Seller now asserts the Closing Statement is "final and binding" (IP Assignment, Recital C).

**Why it matters.** If the deadline is enforced, Rilux loses the right to challenge Seller's working capital calculation. Depending on the size of the dispute, this could mean Rilux overpaid at closing with no recourse. The IP Assignment's Recital C and § 8.1 confirm the dispute is live but do not cure the procedural default.

**Recommendation.**

- Immediately confirm the exact date and method of delivery of the Closing Statement. If it was delivered by email without confirmation of receipt, or by courier with a later delivery date, the 30-day clock may not have started on April 30.
- Review whether the parties engaged in any discussion of the Closing Statement between April 30 and June 12 that could support equitable tolling or waiver.
- If no procedural argument exists, assess the dollar amount in dispute. If it is material, consider whether Seller's assertion of finality is itself a breach of the good-faith negotiation covenant in § 2.4(c) (which contemplates that a timely Notice triggers negotiation – Seller's position may be an attempt to short-circuit that process).
- Raise with Glegond's counsel as the top-priority item on the next call. Do not sign off on the IP Assignment (which acknowledges the dispute) until this is resolved or a settlement framework is agreed.

**Open question.** What is the dollar amount of the working capital dispute? We need this to assess materiality and to frame the negotiation.

## 2. Cooperative's Change-of-Control Consent Not a Closing Condition

**Documents:** SPA Schedule 4.5(a), Item 1 (Raw Organic Milk Supply Agreement); SPA Schedule 7.2(d) (Required Consents); SPA § 7.2(d)

**Issue.** The Raw Organic Milk Supply Agreement with Ashbourne Valley Organic Producers Cooperative requires the Cooperative's prior written consent for any change of control of the Company (SPA Schedule 4.5(a), Item 1, consent requirement). The Cooperative may terminate on 60 days' written notice if consent is not obtained. However, Schedule 7.2(d) – which defines the "Required Consents" that are a condition to Buyer's closing obligation under § 7.2(d) – lists only the landlord's consent (Lamoille Industrial Properties). The Cooperative's consent is conspicuously absent.

**Why it matters.** The milk supply agreement is the Company's primary raw material contract. Without the Cooperative's consent, the Cooperative can terminate the supply relationship on 60 days' notice, potentially stranding the St. Albans Facility with no organic milk input. Because this was not a closing condition, Rilux may have closed (or will close) without securing this consent, and the SPA provides no specific remedy if the Cooperative later terminates.

**Recommendation.**

- Confirm whether the Cooperative's consent was actually obtained before or at Closing. If yes, obtain a copy and file it.
- If no: (a) demand immediate delivery of the consent as a post-closing covenant under SPA § 6.4 (Seller covenants to use commercially reasonable efforts to obtain Required Consents – though strictly the Cooperative consent is not on Schedule 7.2(d), so this is a gap); (b) negotiate an amendment adding the Cooperative consent to Schedule 7.2(d) with a specific outside date and a termination right for Buyer if not obtained; or (c) if the deal has already closed, negotiate a specific indemnity from Seller for losses arising from termination of the milk supply agreement.
- Raise with Glegond's counsel. This is a structural gap that should have been caught in drafting.

**Open question.** Has the Cooperative's consent been obtained? If the deal has already closed (the IP Assignment recitals suggest it did on April 1), what is the current status of the milk supply relationship?

## 3. Bill of Sale – One-Way, Uncapped Indemnity from Company to Seller

**Documents:** Bill of Sale § 5.1–5.2

**Issue.** Section 5.1 requires the Company (now Rilux's wholly owned subsidiary) to indemnify Seller for "any and all Liabilities arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or their ownership, operation, maintenance or use," including liabilities "whether arising before, on or after the date hereof." Section 5.2 confirms the indemnity is unlimited in amount. There is no corresponding indemnity from Seller to the Company for pre-closing liabilities related to the equipment or tankers.

**Why it matters.** The Conveyed Assets include an HTST pasteurizer, filler lines, CIP system, and four bulk milk tankers. Pre-closing product contamination events, tanker accidents, or equipment defects that give rise to claims after closing would be the Company's (i.e., Rilux's) problem under this indemnity, with no cap and no basket. This is asymmetric with the SPA's indemnification regime (Article VIII), which caps Seller's liability at $8.45M and imposes an $845K deductible.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a mutual indemnity: Seller should indemnify the Company for Liabilities arising from pre-closing ownership, operation, or condition of the Conveyed Assets.
- At minimum, cap the Company's indemnity to Seller at a stated amount (e.g., the fair market value of the Conveyed Assets) and exclude liabilities arising from Seller's pre-closing acts or omissions.
- Add a knowledge qualifier or a "to Seller's knowledge" standard for pre-closing liabilities.
- If Glegond's counsel resists, propose that the Company's indemnity be subject to the same cap and basket as SPA Article VIII.

## 4. IP License to Seller is Transferable

**Documents:** IP Assignment Agreement § 4.1

**Issue.** The license granted to Seller under § 4.1 is "non-exclusive, **transferable**, royalty-free." This means Seller can assign its right to use the SORRELFIELD marks and yogurt formulations to any third party without the Company's consent.

**Why it matters.** A transferable license allows a competitor (or even a non-dairy entity) to acquire the right to use the SORRELFIELD brand. This undermines: (a) the non-compete in SPA § 6.8 (which restricts Seller and its Affiliates but not a transferee); (b) brand equity that Rilux paid for in the $84.5M purchase price; and (c) consumer trust in the SORRELFIELD organic brand if a third party uses it on products of unknown quality.

**Recommendation.**

- Strike "transferable" from § 4.1. If Seller needs flexibility for its retained operations, permit transfer only to an Affiliate or a successor in interest in a sale of all or substantially all of Seller's retained dairy business, and only with the Company's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld).
- Add a provision that any purported transfer in violation of the restriction is void and constitutes a material breach triggering immediate termination under § 4.6.

## 5. Six-Month Survival Period for All Representations and Warranties

**Documents:** SPA § 8.1

**Issue.** All representations and warranties in Article IV survive only six months after the Closing Date. The parties expressly state this "shall replace any otherwise applicable statute of limitations, as permitted by 10 Del. C. § 8106(c)." This includes the tax representation in § 4.9.

**Why it matters.**

- **Tax:** Vermont and federal tax audits routinely extend beyond six months. If a post-closing audit identifies underpaid Pre-Closing Taxes, Rilux's claim under § 8.2(c) requires a Claim Notice "prior to the expiration of the applicable survival period" (SPA § 8.4). Six months is insufficient for most tax audit cycles.
- **Financial statements:** If the audited financials (rep in § 4.4) are later found to be materially misstated, the six-month window may close before the error is discovered.
- **Organic certification:** If the Company's organic handler certification (rep in § 4.8) is revoked or found to have been obtained improperly, the issue may not surface within six months.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a tiered survival structure: (a) fundamental reps (organization, authority, capitalization) – survive until the expiration of the applicable statute of limitations; (b) tax reps – survive until the later of six months after Closing or 30 days after the applicable statute of limitations expires for the relevant tax period; (c) all other reps – 12 to 18 months.
- If Glegond's counsel insists on six months for general reps, at minimum carve out § 4.9 (Taxes) and § 4.4 (Financial Statements) for a longer period.

## 6. "Retained Product Lines" Definition Creates a Broader Non-Compete Carve-Out Than Intended

**Documents:** SPA § 6.8 (carve-out for "conventional (non-organic) dairy operations, as conducted on the date hereof" [February 16, 2026]); IP Assignment § 1.2 ("Retained Product Lines" defined by reference to products sold "as of the Effective Date" [June 22, 2026] plus "reasonable line extensions")

**Issue.** The SPA non-compete carves out Seller's conventional dairy operations "as conducted on the date hereof" (February 16, 2026). But the IP license is limited to "Retained Product Lines," which are defined by reference to a later date (June 22, 2026) and include "reasonable line extensions." This creates a four-month window (Feb 16 – Jun 22) during which Seller could launch new product lines that would qualify as "Retained Product Lines" for IP license purposes but would not fall within the SPA's non-compete carve-out (which is frozen at Feb 16). Conversely, "reasonable line extensions" is undefined and could allow Seller to creep into adjacent categories (e.g., organic-adjacent products, plant-based dairy alternatives using the SORRELFIELD name).

**Why it matters.** The non-compete is a key value protection for Rilux. If the IP license effectively allows Seller to market products under the SORRELFIELD brand that are outside the non-compete carve-out, the competitive protection is diluted.

**Recommendation.**

- Align the definitions: "Retained Product Lines" should be defined by reference to the SPA date (February 16, 2026), not the IP Assignment Effective Date.
- Delete "reasonable line extensions" or replace with a specific, enumerated list of product categories agreed by both parties.
- Add a provision that the IP license does not authorize use of the Marks on any product that would violate SPA § 6.8.

## 7. Conveyed Assets Delivered "AS IS" with No Condition Warranty

**Documents:** Bill of Sale § 3.2

**Issue.** The bottling-line equipment and bulk milk tankers are conveyed "AS IS, WHERE IS" with an express disclaimer of all implied warranties, including merchantability and fitness for a particular purpose.

**Why it matters.** For a dairy processor, the pasteurizer, homogenizer, CIP system, and tankers must comply with the Pasteurized Milk Ordinance and FDA food-safety requirements. If the equipment is defective or non-compliant, the Company faces production downtime, regulatory action, or product recall. The "AS IS" clause, combined with the one-way indemnity in Bill of Sale § 5 (Issue 3 above), places all equipment condition risk on Rilux.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate a limited warranty from Seller that the Conveyed Assets are in good working order and comply with applicable food-safety and motor-vehicle regulations as of the Closing Date.
- Alternatively, require Seller to deliver a pre-closing equipment inspection report (by a qualified food-processing engineer) as a closing deliverable under SPA § 3.2, and make the absence of material defects a condition to closing.
- At minimum, add the Conveyed Assets to the SPA's indemnification framework (Article VIII) so that pre-closing equipment defects are covered by the capped indemnity rather than the uncapped Bill of Sale indemnity.

## 8. Indemnification Basket is a True Deductible, Not a Tipping Threshold

**Documents:** SPA § 8.6(a)

**Issue.** Section 8.6(a) provides that Seller is not liable "until the aggregate amount of Losses thereunder exceeds $845,000, **after which Seller shall be liable only for Losses in excess of that amount**." This is a true deductible: Rilux absorbs the first $845,000 of losses in all cases.

**Why it matters.** In a deal of this size, a true deductible means that even if Rilux's total losses are $2M, it recovers only $1,155,000. A tipping basket (where Seller becomes liable for 100% of losses once the threshold is crossed) would be more favorable.

**Recommendation.**

- Negotiate to convert the deductible to a tipping threshold: "after which Seller shall be liable for all Losses from the first dollar."
- If Seller resists, at minimum reduce the basket to $500,000 or tie it to a lower percentage (e.g., 0.5% of Base Purchase Price).

## 9. IP Assignment "No Set-Off" Provision May Limit Leverage on Working Capital Dispute

**Documents:** IP Assignment Agreement § 8.2

**Issue.** Section 8.2 states: "No Party shall withhold or delay performance of its obligations under this Agreement by reason of the matters described in Recital C." This prevents either party from using the working capital dispute as a reason to delay or withhold performance under the IP Assignment.

**Why it matters.** If Rilux is considering withholding cooperation on the IP Assignment (e.g., delaying the trademark recordation, or conditioning further-assurances requests on resolution of the working capital dispute), § 8.2 could be cited against it. While the provision is mutual, Seller is the party with more to lose from a delay in the IP Assignment (it needs the license to operate its retained product lines), so the "no set-off" language actually protects Seller more than Buyer.

**Recommendation.**

- Do not agree to § 8.2 in its current form if the working capital dispute remains unresolved. Either (a) delete § 8.2, or (b) add a proviso: "provided that nothing in this Section 8.2 shall limit Buyer's rights under Section 2.4 of the Purchase Agreement or Buyer's right to seek specific performance or injunctive relief under Section 11.8 of the Purchase Agreement."
- Alternatively, make execution of the IP Assignment conditional on resolution of the working capital dispute.

## 10. Brand Dilution Risk – SORRELFIELD Marks on Conventional Products with Limited Quality Control

**Documents:** IP Assignment Agreement § 4.1 (license scope), § 4.4 (quality control), Schedule 1 Part III (Licensed IP includes Marks 1–3 and yogurt Formulations 3–4)

**Issue.** Seller is licensed to use the SORRELFIELD word mark, the SORRELFIELD ORGANIC DAIRY word mark, and the SORRELFIELD and Meadow Design composite mark on its "Retained Product Lines" (conventional dairy). Quality control is limited to sample inspections "not more than twice in any calendar year" (§ 4.4).

**Why it matters.** The SORRELFIELD ORGANIC DAIRY mark (Reg. No. 6,102,958) contains the word "ORGANIC." If Seller uses this mark on conventional (non-organic) products, there is a risk of consumer confusion, FTC enforcement under the organic labeling rules, and dilution of the organic brand that Rilux acquired. Even if Seller uses only the SORRELFIELD word mark (not the "ORGANIC DAIRY" variant), the co-existence of organic and conventional products under the same brand in the same geographic market creates reputational risk.

**Recommendation.**

- Exclude the SORRELFIELD ORGANIC DAIRY mark (Reg. No. 6,102,958) from the Licensed IP in Schedule 1, Part III. This mark should be reserved exclusively for the Company's organic products.
- Add a requirement that Seller's use of the SORRELFIELD marks on conventional products include a clear distinguishing identifier (e.g., "by Glegond" or a separate sub-brand) to avoid consumer confusion.
- Increase the inspection frequency to quarterly, or at minimum permit inspection upon reasonable suspicion of non-compliance (not limited to twice per year).

## 11. SORRELFIELD GREEK Mark is Only a Pending Application

**Documents:** IP Assignment Agreement, Schedule 1, Part I, Item 4

**Issue.** The SORRELFIELD GREEK mark is listed as App. Serial No. 98/662,104 – a pending application, not a registered mark. The Company receives the sole right to prosecute (§ 7.1), but if the USPTO issues an office action or the application is abandoned, the Company may not obtain registration.

**Why it matters.** If the Company offers Greek-style yogurt (which it does – see Schedule 1, Part II, Item 4), the absence of a registered mark for "SORRELFIELD GREEK" leaves that product line vulnerable to third-party challenges or competing registrations.

**Recommendation.**

- Require Seller to represent that the application is in good standing, that no office actions are pending, and that Seller has not received any adverse communication from the USPTO.
- Add a covenant that Seller will cooperate in the prosecution of the application and will not take any action that would impair registration.
- Confirm the status of the application with the USPTO TSDR database before signing off.

## 12. No Specific Closing Condition Tied to Organic Certification

**Documents:** SPA § 4.8 (representation), § 7.2 (conditions to Buyer's obligations)

**Issue.** Section 4.8 represents that the Company holds a valid organic handler certification. However, the conditions to closing in § 7.2 do not specifically require the certification to be in force at Closing. The general bring-down in § 7.2(a) (reps true "in all material respects") would theoretically cover a lapse, but a certification lapse might be argued to be immaterial if it is curable.

**Why it matters.** The entire value proposition of the acquisition is the Company's organic status. If the certification lapses before or at closing, the Company cannot legally market its products as "organic," which would fundamentally impair the business.

**Recommendation.**

- Add a specific closing condition: "The Company's organic handler certification shall be in full force and effect as of the Closing Date."
- Require delivery of a copy of the current certification as a closing deliverable under § 3.2.

## 13. Formulations Delivery – 10 Business Day Post-Effective Date Deadline

**Documents:** IP Assignment Agreement § 2.4

**Issue.** Seller must deliver complete copies of all formulation documents within 10 Business Days after the Effective Date (June 22, 2026), i.e., by approximately July 6, 2026. There is no specific remedy for failure to deliver, and no condition that the Company's right to the IP is contingent on actual delivery.

**Why it matters.** The formulations (culture blends, process parameters, straining ratios) are operational trade secrets essential to product consistency. If Seller delays or withholds complete formulations, the Company may be unable to reproduce its products exactly, leading to quality inconsistencies, customer complaints, or even regulatory issues.

**Recommendation.**

- Add a specific remedy: if Seller fails to deliver complete formulations by the deadline, the Company may (a) engage a third-party food scientist to reconstruct the formulations at Seller's expense, or (b) treat the failure as a material breach of the IP Assignment giving rise to indemnification under SPA § 8.2(b).
- Require Seller to certify in writing that the delivered formulations are complete, with a specific list of documents delivered.

## 14. Insurance – No Confirmation of Pre-Closing Coverage or Gap Analysis

**Documents:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter § 2.1

**Issue.** The broker's letter (dated April 8, 2026) confirms that the Company was added to the Rilux insurance program effective 12:01 a.m. on April 1, 2026 (the Closing Date). There is no information about what insurance was in place for the Company before April 1, 2026, or whether there were any gaps, claims made, or coverage denials in the pre-closing period.

**Why it matters.** If a product contamination event or tanker accident occurred in March 2026 (pre-closing) and a claim is filed post-closing, the question of which policy responds (Seller's pre-closing policy or Rilux's post-closing program) could be disputed. The SPA's indemnification for Pre-Closing Taxes (§ 8.2(c)) does not explicitly address pre-closing insurance claims.

**Recommendation.**

- Request from Seller a copy of the Company's pre-closing insurance policies and a certificate that no claims were made or pending as of the Closing Date.
- Confirm with the broker that the Rilux program includes "prior acts" or "retroactive" coverage for the Company, or that the pre-closing policies have a run-off period that bridges any gap.
- Add to the SPA (or a side letter) a representation from Seller that no insurance claims were made, pending, or threatened as of the Closing Date.

## 15. SPA § 6.9 – IP Assignment is Post-Closing, Creating a Title Gap

**Documents:** SPA § 6.9; IP Assignment § 2.1

**Issue.** SPA § 6.9 requires Seller to execute the IP Assignment "following the Closing." The IP Assignment is dated June 22, 2026 – 82 days after the April 1 Closing. Although § 2.1 provides that the assignment is "effective as of the Closing Date for all purposes," there is a practical gap during which Seller remains the record owner of the trademarks and holder of the formulations.

**Why it matters.** During the gap: (a) if a third party infringes the SORRELFIELD marks, Seller (not the Company) is the record owner and would be the named plaintiff; (b) if Seller files any new trademark applications or makes changes to existing registrations, the Company may not have visibility; (c) if Seller becomes subject to bankruptcy or creditor claims during the gap, the IP could be at risk despite the retroactive effectiveness clause.

**Recommendation.**

- This is largely a timing issue that has now occurred. Going forward, ensure the USPTO assignments are recorded promptly (the Company has the right to do so under IP Assignment § 2.3).
- Confirm that no new trademark filings, assignments, or encumbrances were made by Seller between April 1 and June 22, 2026.
- If the deal has not yet closed (if we are still pre-signing), negotiate to move the IP Assignment to a closing deliverable rather than a post-closing covenant.
