## Summary

I have reviewed the Stock Purchase Agreement (SPA), Bill of Sale, IP Assignment Agreement, and Insurance Certificate Cover Letter. I identify twelve issues, ordered by significance. The three most consequential are: (1) the Raw Milk Supply Agreement consent is missing from the Required Consents schedule, creating an existential termination risk; (2) the Bill of Sale imposes an uncapped, pre-closing indemnity from the Company to Seller that inverts the risk allocation; and (3) Rilux's Notice of Disagreement on the working capital adjustment appears to have been delivered 13 days outside the contractual deadline, and Seller is using the IP Assignment Agreement to pressure Rilux into performing while that dispute remains unresolved.

## Issue 1 – Missing Required Consent: Raw Organic Milk Supply Agreement

**Document / Section:** SPA, Schedule 7.2(d) (Required Consents) read against Schedule 4.5(a), item 1.

**The Problem.** Schedule 4.5(a) discloses that the Raw Organic Milk Supply Agreement with Ashbourne Valley Organic Producers Cooperative contains a change-of-control consent requirement (Section 14.2 of that agreement). A transfer of a majority of the Company's voting stock triggers the consent, and without it the Cooperative may terminate on 60 days' written notice. However, Schedule 7.2(d) lists only the Lamoille Industrial Properties landlord consent. The Cooperative consent is absent from the Required Consents schedule and therefore absent from the closing condition in Section 7.2(d).

**Why It Matters.** The milk supply agreement runs through February 28, 2029 and is the sole disclosed source of certified organic raw milk for the St. Albans Facility. It is, functionally, the company's feedstock. If the Cooperative has not consented, it holds a termination right exercisable at any time on 60 days' notice. Rilux would own a dairy processor with no guaranteed milk supply. This is not a theoretical risk; it is a live, exercisable right that the deal documents have failed to condition on.

**Recommended Fix.**
- **Immediate:** Confirm with Glegond's counsel whether the Cooperative consent was obtained before or at Closing. If it was, request a copy for the file and add it to Schedule 7.2(d) by amendment for cleanliness.
- **If not obtained:** This is a closing condition failure. Either (a) obtain the consent before releasing any further obligations, or (b) negotiate a specific indemnity from Seller for losses arising from termination of the milk supply agreement, with a survival period tied to the remaining term of that agreement (through February 28, 2029), not the general 6-month survival in Section 8.1.
- **Going forward:** Require that the consent be delivered as a post-closing covenant with a hard deadline (e.g., 30 days) and that failure to obtain it constitutes a material breach giving rise to specific performance or damages.

## Issue 2 – Bill of Sale: Uncapped Indemnity from Company to Seller for Pre-Closing Liabilities

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, Sections 5.1 and 5.2.

**The Problem.** Section 5.1 requires the Company (i.e., Rilux's wholly owned subsidiary) to indemnify Seller against "any and all Liabilities arising out of or relating to the Conveyed Assets or their ownership, operation, maintenance or use," expressly including liabilities "whether arising before, on or after the date hereof." Section 5.2 confirms there is no cap. The defined term "Liabilities" (Section 1.3) sweeps in "debts, liabilities, obligations, claims, Losses, fines, penalties and expenses of any kind, whether known or unknown, accrued or unaccrued, absolute or contingent."

**Why It Matters.** This inverts the normal risk allocation. Seller is conveying equipment it owned and operated, yet Rilux's subsidiary assumes all liability connected to that equipment—including liabilities that arose before Closing, when Seller was in control. If a product contamination event is later traced to a defect in the pasteurizer that existed before Closing, or if a tanker was involved in a pre-closing spill, the Company indemnifies Seller. Meanwhile, Section 3.2 of the same Bill of Sale conveys the equipment "AS IS, WHERE IS" with no warranty of merchantability or fitness. Rilux gets no condition protection and gives away all liability protection. The SPA's Section 11.9 (order of precedence) does not save this: the SPA's indemnification article addresses breaches of the SPA's own reps and covenants, not asset-level indemnities in ancillary agreements.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Delete the phrase "whether arising before, on or after the date hereof" from Section 5.1, or replace it with "arising on or after the date hereof." Pre-closing liabilities should remain with Seller under the SPA's indemnification framework.
- Add a cap to Section 5.2, tied to the fair market value of the Conveyed Assets or a stated dollar amount.
- Add a carve-out: the indemnity does not apply to Liabilities to the extent covered by insurance proceeds actually received (mirroring SPA Section 8.6(c)).
- Raise this with Glegond's counsel as a condition to Rilux's execution of the Bill of Sale. If the Bill of Sale has already been executed at Closing, seek an amendment.

## Issue 3 – Working Capital Dispute: Notice of Disagreement Appears Late

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 2.4(a)–(b); IP Assignment Agreement, Recital C and Section 8.

**The Problem.** The Closing occurred April 1, 2026. Seller delivered the Closing Statement on April 30, 2026 (within the 60-day window in Section 2.4(a)). Section 2.4(b) requires Buyer to deliver a Notice of Disagreement "within thirty (30) days after delivery of the Closing Statement." Thirty days after April 30 is May 30, 2026. The Notice of Disagreement was delivered June 12, 2026—13 days late. Seller asserts the Closing Statement is therefore "final and binding." Buyer disputes this and "reserves all of its rights" (Recital C).

**Why It Matters.** If the 30-day deadline is enforced as written, Rilux has lost the right to challenge the working capital number and must pay any shortfall determined by Seller's calculation. The dollar impact depends on the size of the dispute, which we do not have. Compounding the problem, Section 8.2 of the IP Assignment Agreement prohibits either party from withholding performance of the IP Assignment "by reason of" the dispute. In practical terms, Rilux is being asked to grant Seller a perpetual, royalty-free license to the SORRELFIELD marks and yogurt formulations (see Issue 4) before the money question is resolved. This gives Seller leverage: if Rilux refuses to sign the IP Assignment, Seller can claim breach of SPA Section 6.9.

**Recommended Fix.**
- **Assess the strength of Rilux's position.** The 13-day gap is difficult to overcome on the plain language. Possible (weak) arguments: (a) the Closing Statement was not "delivered" in a manner that triggered the 30-day clock if it lacked the "reasonable supporting schedules" required by Section 2.4(a); (b) equitable tolling if Seller's statement was materially incomplete or misleading; (c) mutual mistake as to the delivery date. These are factual arguments that need the actual Closing Statement and delivery records.
- **Negotiate a resolution.** Given the IP Assignment leverage dynamic, I recommend proposing a global settlement: Rilux agrees to execute the IP Assignment (with modifications per Issue 4) in exchange for Seller agreeing to submit the working capital dispute to the Independent Accountant under Section 2.4(c) notwithstanding the missed deadline. Frame it as a waiver by Seller of the deadline, consideration for which is Rilux's execution of the IP Assignment.
- **Open question:** What is the dollar amount in dispute? We need the Closing Statement and the Notice of Disagreement to assess materiality. If the delta is small relative to the $84.5M purchase price, a commercial resolution may be preferable to litigating the deadline.

## Issue 4 – IP License to Seller Is Overly Broad and Effectively Perpetual

**Document / Section:** IP Assignment Agreement, Sections 4.1–4.7; Schedule 1, Part III.

**The Problem.** The Company grants Seller a "non-exclusive, transferable, royalty-free license" to use:
- The SORRELFIELD word mark, SORRELFIELD ORGANIC DAIRY word mark, and the SORRELFIELD and Meadow Design composite mark (Schedule 1, Part I, items 1–3); and
- The plain whole-milk yogurt and Greek-style strained yogurt formulations (Schedule 1, Part II, items 3–4).

The license is limited to "Retained Product Lines," defined as "dairy products manufactured, marketed or sold by Seller or its Affiliates as of the Effective Date, together with reasonable line extensions of those products" (Section 1.2).

**Why It Matters.**
- **"Reasonable line extensions" is undefined and open-ended.** Seller could argue that a new yogurt SKU, a flavored variant, or a adjacent dairy product is a "line extension" of its retained conventional dairy operations. This could erode the Company's product portfolio over time.
- **The license is transferable (Section 4.1) and sublicensable to contract manufacturers (Section 4.2).** If Seller sells or merges its retained dairy business, the license goes with it. Rilux would be granting a competitor access to the SORRELFIELD brand and its proprietary yogurt formulations.
- **Termination is narrowly available (Section 4.6).** Only for uncured material breach of quality control or trademark usage guidelines, or if Seller stops using the marks entirely for 12 consecutive months. There is no termination for convenience, no sunset date, and no materiality threshold on the competitive harm.
- **Royalty-free.** Rilux paid $84.5M for a company whose brand and formulations Seller can use without compensation indefinitely.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Add a **sunset date** to the license (e.g., 3 years from the Effective Date, co-terminus with the non-compete in SPA Section 6.8).
- Replace "reasonable line extensions" with a **closed list** of specific product SKUs or categories, or require that any new product bearing the marks be pre-approved by the Company in writing.
- Make the license **non-transferable** except in connection with a sale of the entire Retained Product Lines business, and only with the Company's prior written consent (not to be unreasonably withheld).
- Add a termination right if Seller or its sublicensee uses the marks in a manner that is disparaging to, or creates consumer confusion with, the Company's products.
- If Seller resists these changes, consider a **royalty** (even a modest one) as a compromise to offset the breadth of the grant.

## Issue 5 – Survival Period Is Only Six Months, Including for Tax Representations

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 8.1.

**The Problem.** All representations and warranties, including the tax rep in Section 4.9, survive only six months post-closing. The section expressly states this replaces the otherwise applicable statute of limitations "as permitted by 10 Del. C. § 8106(c)."

**Why It Matters.** Six months is well below market for a transaction of this size and is particularly inadequate for:
- **Tax (Section 4.9):** The Vermont and federal tax authorities can audit returns for up to three to six years after filing. A pre-closing tax deficiency discovered in year two or three would be unremediable under the SPA.
- **Organic certification (Section 4.8):** A USDA NOP audit finding a pre-closing compliance violation could surface after 6 months, potentially resulting in decertification.
- **Environmental / product liability:** Latent defects in the processing facility or product contamination claims can manifest well beyond 6 months.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Negotiate a **tiered survival structure**: general reps survive 18 months; fundamental reps (organization, authority, capitalization) survive until the applicable statute of limitations; tax reps survive until the later of (a) the expiration of the statute of limitations for the relevant tax period plus 60 days, or (b) 3 years post-closing.
- If Seller resists, at minimum carve out Section 4.9 (Taxes) and Section 4.8 (Organic Certification) from the 6-month period and give them 18-month survival.
- **Note:** This is a post-closing issue. If the SPA has already been signed and the Closing has occurred, the survival period may already be running. Confirm the execution date and assess whether there is room to amend before the 6-month period expires (i.e., by approximately October 1, 2026).

## Issue 6 – Indemnification Cap and Deductible Are Unfavorable

**Document / Section:** SPA, Sections 8.6(a) and 8.6(b).

**The Problem.**
- Section 8.6(a): Seller is not liable for the first $845,000 of losses (a true deductible, not a tipping basket—Rilux recovers only the excess).
- Section 8.6(b): Total recovery under Article VIII is capped at $8,450,000 (10% of the Base Purchase Price).

**Why It Matters.** For an $84.5M acquisition, a 10% cap is at the low end of market (typically 15–30% for middle-market deals of this size). The deductible means that if Rilux's total losses are, say, $1.2M, it recovers only $355K. Combined with the 6-month survival (Issue 5), Rilux's practical protection is thin.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Increase the cap to 20% of the Base Purchase Price ($16.9M) or, if that is not achievable, carve certain claims (fraud, Pre-Closing Taxes, indemnities under the Bill of Sale as amended per Issue 2) out of the cap entirely.
- Convert the deductible to a **tipping basket** (once losses exceed $845K, Seller is liable for the full amount from dollar one). This is a common compromise.
- As with Issue 5, confirm whether the SPA has been executed. If so, this is a negotiation point for the working capital settlement (Issue 3) or a future amendment.

## Issue 7 – Gap in IP Title Between Closing and IP Assignment Effective Date

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 6.9; IP Assignment Agreement, Section 2.1 and Recital B.

**The Problem.** SPA Section 6.9 requires Seller to execute the IP Assignment "following the Closing" but sets no deadline. The IP Assignment has an Effective Date of June 22, 2026—82 days after the April 1 Closing. Section 2.1 provides the assignment is "effective as of the Closing Date for all purposes," but the USPTO records will continue to show Glegond Dairy Group, LLC as the registered owner until the assignment is recorded.

**Why It Matters.** During the 82-day gap:
- The Company is using registered trademarks that are still in Seller's name. A third party could argue the Company lacks authority to enforce the marks.
- If Seller becomes subject to a creditor's claim or bankruptcy filing, the marks could be reached as Seller's assets before the assignment is recorded.
- The Company's rep in SPA Section 4.7 is conditional ("owns, or will own upon the consummation of the IP Assignment Agreement"), which weakens Rilux's position if a dispute arises during the gap.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Record the short-form assignment (Exhibit A to the IP Assignment) with the USPTO immediately upon execution of the IP Assignment. Do not wait for the full agreement to be finalized.
- Require Seller to deliver an **interim assignment** or a **power of attorney** (the IP Assignment includes one in Section 6.2, but it only triggers if Seller fails to act within 10 Business Days of a request) at or before Closing, so the Company can record with the USPTO without waiting for the full IP Assignment.
- Confirm that Seller has no pending or threatened bankruptcy or creditor proceedings that could cloud title to the marks.

## Issue 8 – No Covenant Addressing Organic Certification Continuity

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 4.8 (rep only); no corresponding covenant in Article VI.

**The Problem.** Section 4.8 represents that the Company holds a valid USDA National Organic Program handler certification. However, there is no covenant requiring Seller to maintain the certification through Closing, no condition to Closing that the certification be in good standing, and no post-closing covenant addressing the change-of-control notification that USDA NOP regulations require.

**Why It Matters.** The entire business model—organic fluid milk and yogurt—depends on maintaining the organic certification. If the certification lapses or is revoked due to a pre-closing violation that surfaces post-closing, the Company cannot lawfully market its products as organic. The 6-month survival period (Issue 5) means Rilux may have no indemnity recourse if the problem manifests after that window.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Confirm the certification is currently in good standing and that no USDA audit findings are pending.
- Add a post-closing covenant (or confirm as a closing deliverable) that Seller has notified the certifying agent of the change of control and that the certification has been transferred or reissued in the Company's name.
- If the SPA is already executed, raise this as a diligence item immediately. The cost of losing organic status dwarfs any indemnification recovery.

## Issue 9 – Insurance Policies Not Yet Delivered; Exclusions Unknown

**Document / Section:** Insurance Certificate Cover Letter, Section 5.2.

**The Problem.** The broker confirms that coverage was bound effective April 1, 2026, and provides certificates of insurance. However, Section 5.2 states that complete policy copies (including all endorsements) will be delivered "within approximately thirty (30) days after the date of this letter" (i.e., by approximately May 8, 2026). As of the IP Assignment Effective Date (June 22, 2026), we have no confirmation in the file that the policies were delivered.

**Why It Matters.** Certificates are informational only (Section 5.1). The actual policy language controls. Without the policies, Rilux cannot confirm:
- Exclusions (e.g., is there a contamination exclusion in the product recall policy that carves out certain pathogens? Is there a known-loss exclusion that could be triggered by a pre-closing incident?)
- Conditions (e.g., notice requirements, cooperation duties)
- Whether the equipment breakdown coverage in the property policy actually covers the specific bottling-line equipment listed in Bill of Sale Schedule A
- Whether the auto policy covers hired and non-owned vehicles in a manner adequate for the tanker operations

**Recommended Fix.**
- Confirm with Kestrel & Pruitt whether the full policy copies have been delivered. If not, request them immediately.
- Once received, have our insurance counsel review for gaps, particularly: (a) product recall policy exclusions; (b) equipment breakdown coverage for the specific assets in Bill of Sale Schedule A; (c) auto policy coverage for the four tankers and any hired transport.
- **Open question:** Does the Company have cyber liability coverage? For a food processor with supply chain data, this is increasingly important and is not listed in the cover letter.

## Issue 10 – Non-Compete Carve-Out for "Conventional" Operations

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 6.8.

**The Problem.** The 3-year non-compete prohibits Seller from engaging in "organic fluid milk or organic yogurt" in the United States. However, the last sentence provides: "Seller's retained conventional (non-organic) dairy operations, as conducted on the date hereof, shall not violate this Section 6.8."

**Why It Matters.** The carve-out is limited to operations "as conducted on the date hereof" (February 16, 2026). If Seller modifies its conventional operations—e.g., adds a functional dairy line, a lactose-free product, or a product marketed in the same retail channels—those modifications may fall outside the carve-out but also outside the non-compete (which covers only "organic" products). The gap between "organic" and "conventional" is not always clear in the marketplace. Additionally, the IP license (Issue 4) allows Seller to use SORRELFIELD marks on its "Retained Product Lines," which are defined by reference to products sold "as of the Effective Date" (June 22, 2026)—a different date than the non-compete carve-out. This creates a potential inconsistency: a product might be a "Retained Product Line" for IP license purposes but not covered by the non-compete carve-out, or vice versa.

**Recommended Fix.**
- Align the definitions. The "Retained Product Lines" definition in the IP Assignment should be conformed to the non-compete carve-out in the SPA, or vice versa.
- Add a requirement that Seller provide the Company with an annual list of products sold under the license, so the Company can monitor whether Seller is drifting into organic-adjacent territory.
- Consider adding a covenant that Seller will not market any product under the SORRELFIELD marks in channels or to customers that overlap with the Company's organic product distribution.

## Issue 11 – Equipment Conveyed "AS IS" With No Condition Warranty

**Document / Section:** Bill of Sale, Section 3.2.

**The Problem.** The Conveyed Assets—including the HTST pasteurizer, carton fillers, yogurt line equipment, and CIP system—are conveyed "AS IS, WHERE IS" with no express or implied warranty of merchantability or fitness for a particular purpose.

**Why It Matters.** This is the processing equipment that makes or breaks the business. If the pasteurizer has a latent defect in the plate heat exchanger that causes under-pasteurization, the resulting product recall and liability would fall on the Company (and, under Bill of Sale Section 5.1 as currently drafted, the Company would even indemnify Seller). The "AS IS" clause means Rilux has no claim against Seller for the equipment's condition.

**Recommended Fix.**
- At minimum, add a warranty that the equipment is in good working order as of the Closing Date and free from known defects, with a survival period of 12 months.
- If Seller resists a full warranty, require a pre-closing inspection certificate from a qualified independent engineer confirming the equipment's condition, delivered as a closing condition.
- Confirm that the equipment breakdown coverage under the property policy (Insurance Cover Letter, item 5) actually covers mechanical failure of the specific equipment listed in Schedule A.

## Issue 12 – SPA Section 4.4: Timing of 2025 Audit

**Document / Section:** SPA, Section 4.4.

**The Problem.** The SPA represents that Seller has delivered "audited balance sheets of the Company as of December 31, 2024 and December 31, 2025." The SPA is dated February 16, 2026. An audit of the 2025 fiscal year would need to be completed within approximately seven weeks of year-end, which is aggressive but not impossible for a company of this size.

**Why It Matters.** If the 2025 financial statements were not in fact audited (e.g., they were reviewed or compiled only), the representation in Section 4.4 is inaccurate. This would affect the reliability of the Target Working Capital number and the Closing Working Capital calculation.

**Recommended Fix / Open Question.**
- Confirm whether the December 31, 2025 financial statements were subject to a full audit (opinion issued by an independent CPA firm) or merely a review. If they were not audited, this is a rep breach and should be raised with Glegond's counsel.
- Verify that the Accounting Principles in Schedule 2.4 (which reference the 2025 audited balance sheet as the baseline) are consistent with the actual audit methodology.
