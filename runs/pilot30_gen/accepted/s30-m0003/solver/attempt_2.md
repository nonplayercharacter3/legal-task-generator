## Issue 1 – Definition Conflict on "Authorized User" Drives the $111,600 Audit Demand

**Documents:** Software License Agreement § 1.2; Order Form No. 1 § 1.1; Audit Notice ¶¶ 2.1–2.3, 3; Agreement § 14.2.

**The problem.** The Agreement defines "Authorized Users" broadly: "employees, agents and contractors of Licensee and its Affiliates who are authorized by Licensee to access and use the Software" (Agreement § 1.2). Order Form No. 1 defines "Authorized User" narrowly: "a named employee of Licensee who is located at a Licensed Facility and has been issued unique login credentials" (Order Form § 1.1). Under the precedence rule in Agreement § 14.2, the Order Form definition controls over the Agreement body.

Rivix's audit notice (¶ 2.1) concedes it measured usage against the Order Form § 1.1 definition. It then excludes 62 individuals from the licensed 150: (a) 34 seasonal agency staff and independent contractors at three plants, who are "not employees of Licensee" (¶ 2.2(b)); and (b) 28 personnel of Veilound Wholesale Distribution LLC at the Lehigh Valley Distribution Center, who are "not employees of Licensee" (¶ 2.2(c)). Rivix demands $111,600 (62 × $1,800) under § 4.3(a), payable within 30 days of receipt (i.e., by approximately **November 19, 2026**).

**Why it matters.** If the Order Form definition controls, those 62 individuals were never "Authorized Users" and their access was outside the scope of the § 2.1 license grant. That is not merely an overage—it is unlicensed use, which could support a material-breach termination right under § 10.2 and exposes Veilound to a larger claim than the true-up amount. Even if Veilound prevails on the definition, the 30-day clock is running.

**Veilound's strongest arguments:**

- *Affiliate personnel (28 users).* The Lehigh Valley Distribution Center is expressly listed as a Licensed Facility in Order Form § 4, operated by Veilound Wholesale Distribution LLC. Agreement § 2.2 states that "Licensee may permit its Affiliates to use the Software through Authorized Users" and makes Licensee responsible for Affiliate compliance. It is internally incoherent for the Order Form to define "Authorized User" as only a "named employee of Licensee" while the Agreement's license grant (§ 2.1) extends to "Licensee and its Affiliates" and § 2.2 expressly contemplates Affiliate use. The most reasonable harmonizing reading is that "Authorized User" includes Affiliate personnel at Licensed Facilities.
- *Seasonal contractors (34 users).* Agreement § 1.2 expressly includes "agents and contractors." A commercial bakery's use of seasonal labor is ordinary and was known to Rivix at the time of contracting. The narrow "named employee" language in Order Form § 1.1 appears to be a drafting oversight that cannot have been the commercial intent, particularly because the Licensed Quantity of 150 was presumably negotiated with knowledge of Veilound's workforce model.

**Recommended actions:**

1. **Do not pay the $111,600 demand without first responding in writing.** Send a dispute notice under Agreement § 3.3 (withholding disputed amounts in good faith with written notice of the basis) before the 30-day deadline. State that Veilound disputes the classification of the 62 individuals and explain the definitional conflict.
2. **Propose a negotiated resolution:** (a) accept the 28 Affiliate users as within the license going forward (they are at a Listed Licensed Facility); (b) for the 34 seasonal contractors, offer to pay a true-up for the actual period of excess use at a reduced rate or to prospectively include them in the Licensed Quantity at the § 3.2 additional-user rate; (c) request that Rivix withdraw the "unlicensed use" characterization.
3. **Internally:** Pull the credential log and employment/contractor records for all 212 users. Confirm which individuals were seasonal vs. permanent, their start/end dates, and whether any were at the Lehigh Valley DC. This will sharpen the response and protect against a follow-on claim.

**⚠️ TIME-SENSITIVE:** Response deadline is approximately November 19, 2026.

## Issue 2 – Draft Side Letter § 4 Silently Narrows the Liability Cap Exclusion for Data Security Breaches

**Documents:** Agreement § 9.1, § 9.3(b), § 6.2–6.4; Draft Side Letter § 4.

**The problem.** Current Agreement § 9.3(b) carves out from the liability cap *all* of Section 6—Confidentiality **and** Data Security. This means if Rivix suffers a security breach that exposes Veilound's recipe formulations, allergen data, or supplier information (all defined as Licensee Data in § 1.7 and as Veilound's Confidential Information in § 1.3), Veilound's damages are uncapped.

Draft Side Letter § 4 replaces § 9.3(b) with: *"a Party's breach of Section 6.1 (Confidentiality)."* This strips the uncapped treatment from § 6.2 (Security Program), § 6.3 (Security Incidents), and § 6.4 (Return and Deletion). A data-security breach by Rivix would then be subject to the § 9.1 cap—12 months of fees, or **$270,000**.

**Why it matters.** For a bakery company, recipe formulations and allergen data are core trade secrets. A single data breach exposing that information to competitors or the public could cause damages far exceeding $270,000 (lost exclusivity, rebranding, customer notifications, regulatory exposure). This provision is buried in the Side Letter and is easy to overlook during negotiation.

**Recommended action:** **Strike Draft Side Letter § 4 in its entirety.** If Rivix insists on a modification, the minimum acceptable position is to preserve the current § 9.3(b) language (all of Section 6). Do not accept a carve-out limited to § 6.1 only. Flag this to the client as a non-negotiable item.

## Issue 3 – Draft Side Letter § 2.1 Imposes a Far Broader Exclusivity Lock-In

**Documents:** Agreement § 2.6; Draft Side Letter § 2.1–2.2.

**The problem.** Current § 2.6 prohibits only "competing production-scheduling software" at "Licensed Facilities" "during the Term." The draft replacement prohibits "production planning, inventory management, recipe management or warehouse management software from any provider other than Licensor, whether at the Licensed Facilities or at any facility worldwide, during the Term and for twenty-four (24) months thereafter," and extends the restriction to all Affiliates (¶ 2.2).

The expansion is fourfold:

| Dimension | Current § 2.6 | Draft Side Letter § 2.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Software scope | Production-scheduling only | Production planning **or** inventory management **or** recipe management **or** warehouse management |
| Geographic scope | Licensed Facilities | Any facility worldwide |
| Temporal scope | During the Term | Term **plus 24 months** |
| Entity scope | Licensee | Licensee **and all Affiliates** |

**Why it matters.** With a 3-year initial term plus auto-renewals (§ 10.1), the "Term plus 24 months" tail could lock Veilound out of using any competing software in four categories at any facility it owns or operates anywhere in the world for five or more years. This would prevent Veilound from (a) evaluating alternatives if BakePlan underperforms, (b) using different software at new facilities acquired or opened after Phase 2, and (c) permitting its distribution Affiliate to use a different WMS. The current § 2.6 already carves out ERP, accounting, inventory, warehouse, and maintenance software; the draft eliminates that carve-out.

**Recommended actions:**

1. **Reject the draft language as written.**
2. **Negotiating position:** If Veilound wishes to grant any expanded exclusivity as consideration for the Phase 2 discount (15%) and implementation credit ($40,000), limit it to: (a) production-scheduling and recipe-management software only (the two modules actually licensed); (b) at Licensed Facilities (including Phase 2 facilities); (c) during the Term only (no post-term tail); (d) preserve the existing carve-out for ERP, inventory, warehouse, and maintenance software.
3. **Do not extend the restriction to Affiliates beyond what is necessary** for the Phase 2 facilities actually being onboarded.

## Issue 4 – Governing Law and Venue Conflict Between Agreement and Order Form

**Documents:** Agreement § 15.1–15.2; Order Form No. 1 § 7.

**The problem.** The Agreement specifies Delaware law and exclusive jurisdiction in Wilmington, Delaware. Order Form No. 1 § 7 specifies New York law and exclusive jurisdiction in New York County. Under § 14.2, the Order Form prevails, so disputes "arising out of or relating to" the Order Form would be governed by New York law in New York courts, while disputes relating to the Agreement generally would be in Delaware.

**Why it matters.** A single dispute (e.g., the audit true-up, which implicates both the Agreement's audit provisions and the Order Form's user definitions and pricing) could be subject to a threshold fight over which law and forum applies. This creates litigation-cost risk and uncertainty. The conflict is almost certainly a template error.

**Recommended action:** Raise this with Rivix in the next negotiation (ideally as part of the Phase 2 Side Letter or a cleanup amendment). Propose conforming Order Form § 7 to Agreement § 15 (Delaware law, Wilmington forum). If Rivix resists, at minimum document the issue so Veilound is prepared for a venue challenge if the audit dispute escalates.

## Issue 7 – Side Letter § 1.3: Implementation Credit Has a 12-Month Use-It-or-Lose-It Expiration

**Documents:** Draft Side Letter § 1.3.

**The problem.** The $40,000 credit applies against Phase 2 implementation fees and expires 12 months after the Phase 2 Order Form is signed. If Phase 2 implementation is delayed beyond 12 months (e.g., due to facility construction, regulatory approvals, or Rivix resource constraints), Veilound loses the credit.

**Why it matters.** $40,000 is material. The risk is that Rivix controls the implementation timeline (it must deliver the project plan within 30 days of the Phase 2 Order Form per § 3.1), yet the expiration runs from the Order Form date regardless of whether Rivix has performed.

**Recommended action:** Negotiate the credit expiration to run from the later of (a) 12 months after the Phase 2 Order Form date or (b) 90 days after the last Phase 2 facility go-live. Alternatively, tie expiration to Rivix's delivery of the implementation plan.
