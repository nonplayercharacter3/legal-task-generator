## Issue 1 – Audit True-Up: "Authorized User" Definition Conflict

**Documents:** SLA § 1.2; Order Form No. 1 § 1.1; Audit Notice §§ 2.1–2.3, 3.

**The problem.** The SLA defines "Authorized Users" broadly: "employees, **agents and contractors** of Licensee **and its Affiliates**" (SLA § 1.2). The Order Form narrows this to "a named **employee of Licensee** who is located at a Licensed Facility" (Order Form § 1.1). Under the SLA's own precedence rule (SLA § 14.2), the Order Form controls, so Rivix is technically correct to exclude:

- 34 seasonal agency staff and independent contractors (not "employees"), and
- 28 personnel of Veilound Wholesale Distribution LLC (an Affiliate, not "Licensee").

**Why it matters.** Rivix demands **$111,600** (62 × $1,800) within 30 days of receipt of the October 20, 2026 letter (Audit Notice § 3; SLA § 4.3). The 62 users are presumably still active, so exposure grows each month the issue remains unresolved. Additionally, SLA § 2.2 makes Veilound responsible for its Affiliates' compliance, so the 28 DC personnel are squarely on Veilound's side of the ledger.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Do not pay the full demand without pushing back.** Veilound has a colorable argument that the Order Form definition is a drafting error inconsistent with the parties' evident intent: the SLA expressly contemplates contractor and Affiliate use (§§ 1.2, 2.2), the Order Form's own Licensed Facilities list includes the Affiliate-operated Lehigh Valley DC (Order Form § 4), and the parties priced 150 users knowing the DC would be included. A court or arbitrator could find the narrower definition unenforceable as contrary to the agreement's overall purpose, or construe it harmoniously with the SLA.
2. **Assert the definitional conflict in writing before the 30-day deadline.** Send a response letter (a) identifying the conflict between SLA § 1.2 and Order Form § 1.1, (b) explaining that the Licensed Facilities include an Affiliate site and the SLA contemplates contractor access, and (c) requesting that Rivix either (i) withdraw the demand as to the 28 Affiliate personnel and the 34 contractors, or (ii) agree to a prospective amendment of Order Form § 1.1 to conform to the SLA definition.
3. **If Rivix insists, negotiate a reduced true-up.** A reasonable compromise: pay for the 28 Affiliate personnel (since the Order Form definition clearly excludes them and the facility is listed in the Order Form) but not the 34 contractors (since SLA § 1.2 expressly includes "contractors" and the parties' implementation plan in the email thread contemplates on-site work by Rivix's own engineers). Alternatively, pay a prorated amount for the actual period of excess use (March 2 – October 15 ≈ 7.5/12 of the annual rate) rather than the full $1,800 per user.
4. **Practical step (immediate):** Suspend or reassign credentials for the 34 contractor accounts that are no longer active (seasonal staff) to reduce the going-forward count. Confirm with the Lehigh Valley DC which of the 28 personnel actually need BakePlan access versus those who could be moved to a different tool.
5. **Preserve records.** Under SLA § 4.1, Veilound must keep "reasonably accurate records" of Authorized Users. Ensure the credential log distinguishes employee / contractor / Affiliate and documents the business need for each.

**⚠ TIME-SENSITIVE:** Response deadline is approximately **November 21, 2026** (30 days from receipt of the October 20 letter).

## Issue 2 – Phase 2 Side Letter: Drastically Expanded Exclusivity

**Document:** Draft Side Letter § 2.1 (replacing SLA § 2.6).

**The problem.** The original exclusivity (SLA § 2.6) bars only "competing production-scheduling software" at the "Licensed Facilities" during the Term, and expressly carves out "enterprise resource planning, accounting, inventory, warehouse or maintenance software." The proposed replacement bars:

| Dimension | Original (SLA § 2.6) | Proposed (Side Letter § 2.1) |
|---|---|---|
| Software scope | Production-scheduling only | Production planning, **inventory management, recipe management, warehouse management** |
| Geography | Licensed Facilities | **Any facility worldwide** |
| Entities | Licensee | Licensee **and all Affiliates** |
| Duration | During the Term | Term **plus 24 months** |
| Activities | License and use | License, **develop**, or use |
| Carve-outs | ERP, accounting, inventory, warehouse, maintenance, spreadsheets during transition | **None** |

**Why it matters.** Veilound operates a distribution center (Lehigh Valley) and a wholesale affiliate. The new clause would prohibit Veilound Wholesale Distribution LLC from using *any* warehouse management software from any vendor, anywhere in the world, for the Term plus two years. It would also bar Veilound from developing its own inventory or recipe tools. This could force Veilound to abandon existing or planned systems unrelated to BakePlan's core function, at significant cost and operational disruption.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Reject § 2.1 as drafted.** This is not a negotiation on terms; it is a fundamental change in the commercial bargain.
2. **Counter-propose** a replacement that (a) limits the exclusivity to "production-scheduling software" (the original scope), (b) restores the carve-out for ERP, accounting, inventory, warehouse and maintenance software, (c) limits geography to Licensed Facilities (including Phase 2 facilities), and (d) removes the "develop" restriction and the 24-month tail.
3. **If Rivix insists on broader scope as a condition of the Phase 2 discount**, quantify the cost to Veilound of being locked into Rivix for inventory and warehouse software (switching costs, lost functionality, vendor lock-in) and use that as leverage to either narrow the clause or increase the discount.
4. **Do not sign the Side Letter** until this issue is resolved. The Side Letter is not yet binding (SLA § 14.1; the document is marked "DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION").

## Issue 3 – Phase 2 Side Letter: Narrowing of the Liability Carve-Out for Data Security Breaches

**Documents:** SLA § 9.3(b) (current); Draft Side Letter § 4 (proposed replacement).

**The problem.** Currently, SLA § 9.3(b) removes from the liability cap and indirect-damages exclusion any breach of **all of Section 6** – including § 6.2 (Security Program) and § 6.3 (Security Incidents). The Side Letter would replace this with a carve-out for breach of **§ 6.1 (Confidentiality) only**.

**Why it matters.** Veilound's most sensitive data – recipe formulations, ingredient specifications, and **allergen data** (SLA § 1.7) – is hosted by Rivix. If Rivix fails to maintain its security program (§ 6.2) or mishandles a security incident (§ 6.3), and that failure leads to a data breach causing a product recall, consumer harm, or regulatory action, Veilound's recovery would be capped at 12 months of fees (**$270,000**) and limited to direct damages. All consequential losses (recall costs, lost revenue, regulatory fines, consumer class actions) would be barred. For a food manufacturer, this is an unacceptable risk allocation.

**Recommendations:**

1. **Strike Side Letter § 4 entirely.** Restore SLA § 9.3(b) as originally drafted (breach of all of Section 6 is uncapped).
2. **If Rivix resists**, the minimum acceptable position is to carve out breaches of §§ 6.2 and 6.3 (Security Program and Security Incidents) from the cap, even if § 6.1 (general confidentiality) remains subject to the cap.
3. **Flag to the client** that this provision, combined with the insurance requirements in SLA § 12 ($5M cyber liability), suggests Rivix is trying to shift data-breach risk back onto Veilound while maintaining a high insurance limit for its own protection.

## Issue 4 – Order Form No. 1: Governing Law and Forum Conflict with the SLA

**Documents:** SLA §§ 15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington, DE forum); Order Form No. 1 § 7 (New York law; New York County forum).

**The problem.** The Order Form selects New York law and venue, while the SLA selects Delaware. Under SLA § 14.2, the Order Form prevails. This means disputes "arising out of or relating to" Order Form No. 1 (e.g., the fee true-up, the user count, the implementation scope) are governed by New York law in New York courts, while disputes under the SLA generally (e.g., data security, confidentiality, termination) are governed by Delaware law in Delaware courts.

**Why it matters.** The current audit dispute sits squarely in this gap. Rivix could argue the true-up is an Order Form dispute (New York), while Veilound might prefer Delaware (where the SLA's broader definitions and the overall commercial context are more favorable). The split also increases litigation cost and complexity.

**Recommendations:**

1. **In the response to the audit notice**, note the conflict and reserve Veilound's position on applicable law and forum. Do not concede New York jurisdiction.
2. **In any amendment or Side Letter**, conform Order Form § 7 to the SLA (Delaware law, Wilmington forum) to eliminate the split.
3. **Practical step:** If the matter proceeds to dispute, analyze which forum is more favorable under each state's law before making a strategic choice.

## Issue 7 – Phase 2 Side Letter: Price Hold Is Redundant and Ambiguous

**Document:** Draft Side Letter § 1.4; Order Form No. 1 § 3.3.

**The problem.** Side Letter § 1.4 states Rivix "shall not increase the per-user rate stated in Order Form No. 1 for the remainder of its initial subscription period." Order Form § 3.3 already fixes that rate for the entire 36-month Subscription Period. The Side Letter language adds nothing for Order Form No. 1 and is silent on the Phase 2 Order Form's own rates.

**Why it matters.** If Veilound relies on this as price protection for Phase 2, it has none. The Phase 2 discount (15% off list, Side Letter § 1.2) applies only to "the initial subscription period of the Phase 2 Order Form." After that period, list-price increases would pass through.

**Recommendation:** If the Side Letter is negotiated further, add explicit language that the Phase 2 per-user rate (list less 15%) is fixed for the Phase 2 Subscription Period, mirroring Order Form § 3.3.
