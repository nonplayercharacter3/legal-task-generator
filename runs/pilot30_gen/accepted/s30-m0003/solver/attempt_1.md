## ISSUE 1 – Audit True-Up Demand: Definition Conflict Favors Veilound ⏰ TIME-SENSITIVE (response due ~November 19, 2026)

**Documents:** Order Form No. 1 §1.1; SLA §1.2, §2.1, §14.2; Audit Notice §§2.1–2.3, §3.

**The problem.** Rivix demands $111,600 for 62 "excess" users: 34 seasonal agency staff/contractors at Veilound plants and 28 personnel of Veilound Wholesale Distribution LLC at the Lehigh Valley Distribution Center. Rivix states it is measuring against the Order Form definition of "Authorized User" (Audit Notice §2.1), yet the 62 individuals do not meet that definition.

Order Form No. 1 §1.1 defines "Authorized User" as **"a named employee of Licensee who is located at a Licensed Facility."** The 34 contractors are not employees of Licensee. The 28 Distribution Center personnel are employees of an Affiliate, not of Licensee. Under the definition Rivix itself invokes, none of the 62 are "Authorized Users" and none count against the 150.

Meanwhile, SLA §1.2 defines "Authorized Users" (plural, in the license grant) as **"employees, agents and contractors of Licensee and its Affiliates"** who are authorized to access the Software. The license grant in SLA §2.1 extends to this broader group. Under SLA §14.2, the Order Form prevails over the SLA body in case of conflict, so the narrower definition controls the *quantity* limit, while the broader definition controls the *scope of the license*.

**Why it matters.** Veilound has a strong textual argument that the 62 users are permitted under the license grant but simply do not count toward the 150-user Licensed Quantity. Rivix's demand appears to conflate "people with credentials" with "Authorized Users as defined in the Order Form."

**Recommendation.**
- Do **not** pay the $111,600 within the 30-day window without first responding.
- Send a written response (per SLA §13.1, by email with courier confirmation, to Rivix General Counsel) before the deadline, asserting that the 62 individuals fall outside the Order Form §1.1 definition and are therefore not subject to the Licensed Quantity. Cite SLA §2.1 and §1.2 as the basis for their permitted access.
- Request that Rivix withdraw the demand or, alternatively, reissue a revised calculation limited to any truly excess *named employees of Licensee at Licensed Facilities* (which appears to be zero, per Audit Notice §2.2(a)).
- **Fallback position for negotiation:** If Veilound wishes to preserve the relationship and the Phase 2 discount, it may offer to pay a prorated amount for the contractors only (not the Affiliate personnel, who are clearly within the SLA license grant), or to prospectively include the 34 contractor positions in a future Order Form amendment at the standard per-user rate. The proration point (see Issue 5) applies if any payment is made.

## ISSUE 2 – Draft Phase 2 Side Letter: Exclusivity Expansion Is Unacceptable ⏰ TIME-SENSITIVE (Phase 2 negotiation is active)

**Documents:** SLA §2.6 (original); Side Letter §2.1–2.2 (proposed replacement).

**The problem.** The original exclusivity (SLA §2.6) prohibits only "competing production-scheduling software" at "the Licensed Facilities" "during the Term," and expressly carves out "enterprise resource planning, accounting, inventory, warehouse or maintenance software."

The proposed replacement (Side Letter §2.1) prohibits Veilound and **all Affiliates** from licensing, **developing, or using** "production planning, **inventory management, recipe management or warehouse management** software from **any provider** other than Rivix, at **any facility worldwide**, during the Term **and for 24 months thereafter**.

**Why it matters.**
- Eliminates the ERP/inventory/warehouse carve-out. Veilound's existing ERP likely includes inventory and warehouse modules; using them would become a breach.
- "Any facility worldwide" means Veilound could not use a different WMS at a future acquisition or a plant not on BakePlan.
- "Develop" would prohibit Veilound from building or commissioning its own planning tool, even internally.
- The 24-month tail extends the restriction well beyond the commercial relationship.
- Side Letter §2.2 makes Veilound directly responsible for Affiliate compliance, compounding the risk.

**Recommendation.**
- **Do not sign the Side Letter as drafted.** This is a deal-breaker in its current form.
- Counter-propose to retain the original SLA §2.6 language, or at most extend it to cover the Phase 2 Licensed Facilities (not "any facility worldwide") and add the Phase 2 modules to the "competing software" scope.
- Insist on retaining the carve-out for ERP, accounting, inventory, warehouse, and maintenance software.
- Delete the word "develop."
- Delete the 24-month post-Term tail; exclusivity should run only during the Term.
- If Rivix insists on broader exclusivity as the price of the 15% discount, require a material increase in the discount or a reduction in the per-user rate to compensate.

## ISSUE 3 – Draft Phase 2 Side Letter: Liability Carve-Out for Data Security Breach Is Narrowed

**Documents:** SLA §9.3(b) (original); Side Letter §4 (proposed replacement).

**The problem.** Original SLA §9.3(b) removes the liability cap and indirect-damages exclusion for any breach of **Section 6 (Confidentiality and Data Security)** – i.e., §§6.1 through 6.4, including the security program obligation (§6.2) and the security incident notification/remediation obligation (§6.3).

The Side Letter replaces this with a carve-out for breach of **§6.1 only (Confidentiality)**. The result: if Rivix fails to maintain its security program (§6.2) and a data breach exposes Veilound's recipe formulations, allergen data, and supplier information, Veilound's recovery would be **capped at 12 months of fees (~$270,000)** and **indirect damages (lost profits, customer notification costs, regulatory fines, reputational harm) would be excluded**.

**Why it matters.** Veilound's Licensee Data (SLA §1.7) includes proprietary recipe formulations and allergen records – core competitive assets. A security breach of this data could cause damages orders of magnitude larger than the cap. The §6.3(d) reimbursement obligation for breach-related costs would also become subject to the cap.

**Recommendation.**
- **Strike Side Letter §4 in its entirety.** Restore the original SLA §9.3(b) covering all of Section 6.
- If Rivix resists, accept a compromise that at minimum preserves the uncapped treatment for §6.2 (Security Program) and §6.3 (Security Incidents), even if §6.1 (general confidentiality) is separated out.
- Do not trade this point for the Phase 2 discount; the data-security exposure is existential for a food manufacturer.

## ISSUE 4 – Order Form No. 1: Governing Law and Forum Conflict with the SLA

**Documents:** SLA §15.1–15.2 (Delaware law; Wilmington, DE forum); Order Form No. 1 §7 (New York law; New York County forum).

**The problem.** Under SLA §14.2, the Order Form prevails over the SLA body. So disputes "arising out of or relating to" Order Form No. 1 are governed by New York law in New York courts, while disputes under the SLA generally (e.g., the audit right in §4, the license grant in §2) are governed by Delaware law in Delaware courts. The current audit dispute sits at the intersection: it arises under SLA §4.3 but concerns the Licensed Quantity defined in the Order Form.

**Why it matters.** A split governing law creates uncertainty about which state's contract-interpretation principles apply to the definition conflict at the heart of the audit dispute. It also forces Veilound to potentially litigate in two forums.

**Recommendation.**
- In the response to the audit notice, argue that SLA §15 (Delaware) governs the audit right and its interpretation, because the audit mechanism is a SLA-level provision (§4) that applies across all Order Forms.
- Going forward, if a Phase 2 Order Form is executed, require that its governing-law clause conform to the SLA (Delaware) to eliminate the split.
- If the dispute escalates, be prepared to address the forum question as a threshold issue.

## ISSUE 5 – Audit Notice: Calculation Should Be Prorated, Not Full-Year

**Documents:** SLA §4.3(a)–(b); Audit Notice §3.

**The problem.** Rivix charges 62 × $1,800 = $111,600, which is the full annual rate. The audit period runs March 2 – October 15, 2026 (~227 days, or ~62% of the subscription year). SLA §4.3(b) explicitly references "Fees for the **period of excess use**," indicating proration. Section 4.3(a) is less clear but should be read consistently.

**Why it matters.** Even if Veilound concedes the definition issue (which it should not), the maximum exposure is ~$69,200, not $111,600 – a savings of ~$42,400.

**Recommendation.**
- Include the proration argument in the response letter as a secondary point, in case the primary definition argument is not accepted.
- If Veilound ultimately pays anything, pay only the prorated amount and reserve rights.

## ISSUE 6 – Auto-Renewal: 90-Day Non-Renewal Notice Deadline

**Documents:** SLA §10.1.

**The problem.** The initial 3-year term ends March 1, 2029. The agreement then auto-renews for successive 1-year terms unless either party gives non-renewal notice at least 90 days before the end of the then-current term. The first non-renewal deadline is **December 1, 2028**.

**Why it matters.** If Veilound is unhappy with the relationship (e.g., the audit dispute is resolved adversely, or the Phase 2 Side Letter terms are unacceptable), it must decide by December 1, 2028 whether to renew. Missing the deadline locks Veilound in for another year on the same terms.

**Recommendation.**
- Calendar the December 1, 2028 deadline now.
- Reassess the relationship at the 18-month mark (September 2027) to allow adequate time for a replacement evaluation if needed.
- Note that the exclusivity in SLA §2.6 (or the expanded version in the Side Letter, if signed) runs for the full Term including renewals, so auto-renewal extends the exclusivity as well.

## ISSUE 7 – Side Letter: Implementation Credit Expiration Risk

**Documents:** Side Letter §1.3.

**The problem.** The $40,000 implementation credit expires 12 months after the Phase 2 Order Form date. Phase 1 implementation took approximately 5.5 months (March–August 2026). If Phase 2 involves more facilities or more complex configurations, 12 months may be tight, particularly if go-live dates slip.

**Why it matters.** Losing the credit is a $40,000 cost with no offsetting benefit to Rivix.

**Recommendation.**
- Negotiate to extend the credit expiration to 18 months, or tie it to the later of (a) 12 months after the Phase 2 Order Form date or (b) 6 months after the last Phase 2 facility go-live.
- Alternatively, require that the credit not expire while any Phase 2 facility is still in implementation.

## ISSUE 8 – Side Letter: Price Hold Is Redundant and Misleading

**Documents:** Side Letter §1.4; Order Form No. 1 §3.3.

**The problem.** Side Letter §1.4 promises that Rivix "shall not increase the per-user rate stated in Order Form No. 1 for the remainder of its initial subscription period." But Order Form No. 1 §3.3 already provides that the rate "is fixed for the Subscription Period" (36 months). The Side Letter adds nothing.

**Why it matters.** The redundancy may signal that Rivix intends to argue the price hold does not apply to the Phase 2 Order Form (which would have its own rates). Veilound should not assume the Phase 2 rates are protected.

**Recommendation.**
- In the Phase 2 Order Form, include an explicit price-protection clause (similar to Order Form No. 1 §3.3) fixing the Phase 2 per-user rate for the full Phase 2 subscription period.
- Delete Side Letter §1.4 as unnecessary, or restate it to cover the Phase 2 Order Form rates.
